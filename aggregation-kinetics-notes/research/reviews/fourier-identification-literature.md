# Literature audit: asymptotic Fourier identification with additive coagulation

Date: 2026-09-07. Reviewer: independent literature agent. This is a focused search and comparison, not a certification of priority or an independent proof review.

The plausible new contribution is a quantitative identification theorem for a particular nonlinear coagulation–fragmentation equation. Transform inversion, inference from unlabelled distributions, inference from asymptotic profiles, and cancellation of an unknown factor through two-time characteristic-function ratios all have direct precedents. No inspected source establishes the proposed additive-coagulation remainder bound and its resulting asymptotic quotient identification under only finite number and mass.

## Candidate being compared

For additive coagulation (K(x,y)=\lambda(x+y)), constant parent fragmentation rate (\sigma), conserved mass (m), and a size-independent daughter measure (B) on ((0,1)) with total mass two and first moment one, let (\eta_t=n_t/N(t)). The candidate uses

\[
\phi_t(k)=\int x^{ik}\eta_t(dx),\qquad
\psi(k)=\sigma\int(\theta^{ik}-1)B(d\theta).
\]

An exponentially decaying nonlinear forcing in (\partial_t\phi_t=\psi\phi_t+r_t) gives, near (k=0),

\[
\phi_t(k)=e^{t\psi(k)}C(k)+O(e^{-\omega t}),\qquad C(0)=1,
\]

and hence (\phi_{t+h}(k)/\phi_t(k)\to e^{h\psi(k)}). A continuous logarithm near zero identifies the exponent locally. The negative support of the finite jump measure gives global uniqueness from that local information. Number growth then separates coagulation from fragmentation. These statements concern exact distribution traces at arbitrarily late times.

## Closest precedents inspected

### Two-time Fourier cancellation of an unknown factor is explicit prior work

Guillaume Garnier, *Decompounding with unknown noise through several independent channels*, arXiv:2405.10588 (2024), Section 2.2, Equation (4) and the immediately following displays, considers noisy compound Poisson observations with transform

\[
\varphi_{Z_t}(u)=e^{t\lambda(\varphi_X(u)-1)}\varphi_\varepsilon(u).
\]

Taking a quotient at two times cancels the unknown noise transform. The paper then takes distinguished logarithms and constructs statistical estimators; Section 3 treats multiplicative decompounding through Mellin transforms. This is the closest algebraic precedent. Its process has independent compound Poisson increments and independent observation noise. Our nonlinear correction is generated by coagulation, and the limiting factor (C) need not itself be a characteristic function. Thus the new task is proving the asymptotic factorization and quantitative error, rather than inventing the quotient cancellation. I inspected the model, Section 2.2, and estimator statements, not every statistical proof. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10588v1).

### Constant-rate pure fragmentation already has exact transform separation

Doumic and Escobedo, *Time asymptotics for a critical case in fragmentation and growth-fragmentation equations*, Kinetic and Related Models 9 (2016), 251–297, derive the Mellin evolution (\partial_tU=(K-1)U) and exact solution (U(t,s)=U_0(s)e^{(K(s)-1)t}), Equations (12)–(14). Normalizing by number and setting (s=1+ik) gives the pure-fragmentation characteristic exponent used here. Exact two-time cancellation is an immediate consequence even when the article is read primarily as an asymptotic analysis. Therefore the case (\lambda=0) is not a new inverse mechanism. I inspected the transform derivation and its analytic domains. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.03588).

### Identification from asymptotic fragmentation profiles is established

Doumic, Escobedo, and Tournus, *Estimating the division rate and kernel in the fragmentation equation*, Annales de l'Institut Henri Poincaré C 35 (2018), 1847–1884, recover a power-law division rate and daughter kernel from an asymptotic profile. Their Mellin inversion contains the quotient

\[
K(s)=1+\frac{(2-s)G(s)}{\alpha\gamma G(s+\gamma)}.
\]

Hypothesis 1 imposes (\gamma>0) for the selection rate (\alpha x^\gamma), excluding the constant-selection case of the candidate. Their result nevertheless rules out a broad claim that recovering fragmentation parameters from long-time population distributions is new. I inspected the hypotheses, transform formula, and inversion discussion. [Primary full text](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.anihpc.2018.03.004.pdf).

### Short-time inverse fragmentation also uses two-time Mellin quotients

Doumic, Escobedo, and Tournus, *An inverse problem: recovering the fragmentation kernel from the short-time behaviour of the fragmentation equation*, Annales Henri Lebesgue 7 (2024), 621–671, give in Section 4, Equation (4.2), the estimator

\[
K_{\mathrm{est}}(s)=\frac{U(t+\Delta,s)-W(t,\Delta,s)}{\alpha\Delta U(t,s+\gamma)}.
\]

Here (W) accounts for parent survival over the short interval. The paper develops measure solutions and quantitative approximation estimates, with additional regularity for Mellin inversion estimates. It explicitly concerns population measurements without tracking individual breakages. Its short-lag limit and pure fragmentation differ from our late-time limit with persistent nonlinear coagulation. I inspected the introduction, Section 4, and the hypotheses accompanying Theorem 4.4. This paper is also already in the local literature folder. [Primary full text](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/ahl.207.pdf).

### Unlabelled log-size Fourier inversion has a statistical precedent

Hoang, Pham Ngoc, Rivoirard, and Tran, *Nonparametric estimation of the fragmentation kernel based on a partial differential equation stationary distribution approximation*, Scandinavian Journal of Statistics 49 (2022), 4–43, use unlabelled size observations from a limiting population profile. Their model has constant size growth speed and constant division rate, with no coagulation. Sections 3.1.1–3.1.4 change variables to log size and use the Fourier quotient (g^*=1+\alpha D^*/(2RM^*)), Equation (12). They analyze empirical transforms, regularization of the denominator, consistency, and rates under smoothness and moment assumptions. This is strong prior work for the observation scheme and transform-based reconstruction, but not the nonlinear time-ratio limit. I inspected the model, the stationary approximation, and those estimator sections. [Primary full text](https://www.math.univ-paris13.fr/~phamngoc/HoangPhamRivoirardTran.pdf).

### Inverse daughter-law estimation with actual coagulation is prior work

Mirzaev, Byrne, and Bortz, *An Inverse Problem for a Class of Conditional Probability Measure-Dependent Evolution Equations*, Inverse Problems 32 (2016), 095005, formulate least-squares estimation of a conditional daughter distribution from population histograms at multiple times. Their motivating application is bacterial flocculation with aggregation and fragmentation. The method includes existence and approximation/consistency analysis. Crucially, consistency invokes identifiability as assumption (A2) in Section 2.2.2; it does not provide the candidate's explicit structural uniqueness theorem for additive coagulation. The paper also explicitly allows nonunique minimizers for finite data. I inspected the introduction and theoretical estimation framework; this audit does not endorse every general compactness claim in that framework. [Primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5352987/).

### General decompounding is a mature inverse problem

Lindo, Zuyev, and Sagitov, *Nonparametric estimation for compound Poisson process via variational analysis on measures*, Statistics and Computing 28 (2018), 563–577, estimate a Lévy jump measure from compound Poisson observations using characteristic-function and convolution fitting. The measure formulation accommodates discrete jump laws. This is relevant because the candidate allows atomic daughter fractions: reconstruction of a finite jump measure from a compound Poisson transform should be credited to decompounding theory. I inspected the introductory model and characteristic-function inversion discussion. [Primary full text](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/501881/file/501881_Fulltext.pdf).

## Direct relevance to Ramkrishna and local literature

Ramkrishna's *Population Balances* (2000), Chapter 6, already treats inversion of dynamic size distributions to recover breakage functions and aggregation frequencies. Sections 6.1.2.1–6.1.2.2 describe self-similarity-based recovery, numerical sensitivity, and regularization. The experimental breakage example deliberately uses a lean dispersion with negligible coalescence. Section 6.2 includes inversion of the sum aggregation frequency. Thus a rigorous inference result while both mechanisms remain active connects directly to his established interests, but the general inverse-problem motivation is old. [Local primary text](../../literature/papers/ramkrishna2000-population-balances-theory-and-applications/fulltext.md).

The 2024 Doumic–Escobedo–Tournus introduction traces inverse breakage to Ramkrishna, *Drop-breakage in agitated liquid-liquid dispersions*, Chemical Engineering Science 29 (1974), 987–992, and Narsimhan–Ramkrishna–Gupta (1980). The [1974 publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0009250974800904) confirms use of batch drop-distribution data to estimate breakage probabilities. I did not obtain and inspect the complete 1974 or 1980 articles in this audit; do not attribute a particular transform formula to either on that basis.

Alomari and Dubovski, *Recovery of the integral kernel in the kinetic fragmentation equation* (2013), DOI 10.1080/17415977.2012.677446, is a further inverse-fragmentation precedent. The inspected model restricts the binary kernel through parent size and uses linearization and inverse-source/control methods from partial observations at two times. It does not supply the nonlinear long-time quotient theorem. [Publisher full text](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17415977.2012.677446).

## Limits on novelty and interpretation

1. The plausible result is **asymptotic identification despite additive coagulation, with an explicit nonlinear bias bound and weak assumptions**. Its proof combines a model-specific fractional-moment estimate with standard variation of constants, transform quotients, and one-sided transform uniqueness.
2. Local exponent uniqueness through analytic continuation is classical complex analysis. It must be proved or cited carefully, particularly for finite measures without log moments; it should not be advertised as a new harmonic-analysis theorem.
3. A continuous logarithm anchored at zero resolves the local branch ambiguity. A principal logarithm used globally at a fixed lag need not recover the exponent.
4. Exact-data uniqueness is not stable statistical recovery. At nonzero frequency the denominator can decay exponentially in time. Later data reduce coagulation bias but can amplify sampling error. The existing decompounding and fragmentation estimators make this issue central. A practical estimation claim needs an observation model, denominator control, and a finite-time choice balancing bias and noise.
5. Analytic continuation from a small frequency interval may be severely unstable. The identification theorem alone gives no uniform error bound for the full daughter measure from noisy low-frequency data.
6. If (\sigma=0), the daughter law is unobservable. Separation of (\lambda) from (b=\lambda m) requires the absolute conserved mass (m), not merely normalized size laws and a count growth rate. Excluding a daughter atom at one avoids invisible zero jumps in the finite Lévy measure.

A claim suitable for a draft is: “For this additive coagulation–fragmentation model, we prove that late-time characteristic-function quotients converge to the fragmentation exponent with an explicit error bound. This yields structural identification from exact population distribution traces without knowing the initial distribution.” It should be followed by the precedents above and a clear statement that the statistical problem remains separate.

## Search scope and remaining uncertainty

Searches combined coagulation–fragmentation, inverse/identification, Fourier/Mellin, ratio, long-time/asymptotic, decompounding, and unknown noise. I followed references in the 2018 and 2024 fragmentation papers and inspected the local Ramkrishna chapter. The Inria MERGE 2024 activity report was useful for finding Garnier's preprint; it explicitly lists decompounding as an active approach to fragmentation inference, reinforcing the need for narrow novelty claims. [Primary team report](https://radar.inria.fr/report/2024/merge/index.html).

Some publisher pages and PDFs could not be retrieved completely. This bounded audit cannot exclude an obscure prior theorem, recent preprint, thesis chapter, or an immediate specialization of a more general inverse evolution result. In particular, the full nonlinear asymptotic factorization appears more distinctive than its inverse corollary. Future searches should prioritize perturbative decompounding with dependent errors, asymptotically homogeneous Markov processes, and inverse kinetic equations with decaying nonlinear terms.
