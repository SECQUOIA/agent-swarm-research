Literature audit of the proposed log-size limit for critical additive coagulation and constant per-particle fragmentation

Reviewed 2026-09-06 by an independent literature agent. This is a bounded novelty audit, not a proof certification. No directly matching theorem was found in the openly accessible sources inspected. The strongest potentially new statement is the explicit, uniform-in-time Wasserstein comparison between the nonlinear number distribution and a homogeneous fragmentation compound Poisson law. A Gaussian limit for logarithmic size under pure fragmentation is classical. Any novelty claim should emphasize the nonlinear comparison and the persistence of the same logarithmic limit despite ongoing additive coagulation.

The precise model being compared has coagulation kernel `K(x,y)=λ(x+y)`, constant per-particle binary selection rate `b=λm`, conserved mass `m`, constant number `N`, and a size-independent daughter measure `B` satisfying `B((0,1))=2` and `∫θ B(dθ)=1`. Write `H₀=∫√x n₀(dx)` and `κ=3−2√2`. The proposed result is

```math
W_1\!\left(\rho_t,
 \mathcal L\!\left[Z_0+\sum_{j=1}^{P_{2bt}}\log\Theta_j\right]\right)
\leq \frac{H_0^2}{2\kappa mN},
\qquad \rho_t=(\log)_\# n_t/N,\quad \Theta\sim B/2.
```

The claimed mechanism is an exact generator decomposition with a nonlinear remainder bounded in the dual Lipschitz norm by `λH(t)²/N`, where `H(t)≤H₀exp(−κbt)`. Integrating this bound against the contracting compound Poisson semigroup yields a bounded total logarithmic displacement. This specific mechanism, constant, and conclusion were not found in the sources below.

1. Jean Bertoin, *The asymptotic behavior of fragmentation processes*, Journal of the European Mathematical Society 5 (2003), 395–416, [full primary article](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/31511?nt=1), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10097-003-0055-3). Inspected the introduction, Section 2.2, Theorem 1 and its proof, and the subsequent discussion of stable limits. Logarithmic Gaussian limits for homogeneous fragmentation are explicit. The main theorem concerns a mass-weighted empirical measure and uses the subordinator followed by a tagged mass point. Its jump weighting differs from the number-weighted `B/2` law. The introduction also identifies earlier number-based branching-random-walk central limit theorems, including work by Asmussen–Kaplan and Biggins. This is decisive prior art against presenting a fragmentation log-size CLT itself as new. It does not contain the present nonlinear additive-coagulation comparison.

2. Marie Doumic and Miguel Escobedo, *Time asymptotics for a critical case in fragmentation and growth-fragmentation equations*, Kinetic and Related Models 9 (2016), 251–297, [publisher record](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/krm.2016.9.251), [full open preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.03588). Inspected equation (5), the introductory positioning, main asymptotic statements, and Corollary 1. Constant division rate and zero or linear growth are the critical setting. Mellin methods yield refined asymptotics, and Corollary 1 gives the mass-weighted logarithmic Gaussian limit with drift `K′(2)` and variance coefficient `K″(2)`. The paper explicitly places its work after known fragmentation central limit theorems. Its analysis of discrete daughter laws also warns against equating a weak logarithmic CLT with convergence of an unscaled density to a lognormal density. There is no coagulation operator in this model.

3. Philippe Laurençot, *Stationary solutions to coagulation-fragmentation equations*, Annales de l'Institut Henri Poincaré C, Analyse non linéaire 36 (2019), 1903–1939, [full primary article](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.anihpc.2019.06.003.pdf). Inspected the introduction and formulas (1.1), (1.5), (1.6), and (1.8). The affine-additive example (1.8) has `K(x,y)=k₀+k₁(x+y)`, parent selection `a(x)=A₀x`, and daughter density `2/y`. Thus its selection rate is linear, not constant. The main theorem's sublinear coagulation and positive selection exponent assumptions also exclude the present boundary case. The reference to Dubovskii–Stewart (1996), *Trend to equilibrium for the coagulation-fragmentation equation*, concerns that different affine-additive/linear-selection model; the original 1996 article was not inspected here.

4. Amenda Ngar Fai Chow, *The discrete coagulation-fragmentation model with bilinear coagulation kernel and constant fragmentation kernel*, MSc thesis, University of Alberta (2008), [Library and Archives Canada full thesis](https://www.collectionscanada.gc.ca/obj/thesescanada/vol2/002/MR47191.PDF). Inspected the title page, model definition (2.5), the constant rate specialization, and Chapter 4 on the sum kernel. Despite the close terminology, `L_{j,k}=b` is the binary pair fragmentation rate. Summation over possible splits gives parent loss `b(k−1)/2` for size `k`. It therefore does not study size-independent per-parent fragmentation. This is a concrete reason not to infer overlap from the phrase “sum kernel and constant fragmentation.” An accessible publisher table of contents for Banasiak–Lamb–Laurençot, *Analytic Methods for Coagulation-Fragmentation Models*, Volume II, also contains a similarly named section; its full contents were not inspected and should not be assumed to match either model.

5. Pierre Degond, Jian-Guo Liu, and Robert L. Pego, *Coagulation-fragmentation model for animal group-size statistics*, Journal of Nonlinear Science (2017), [open author manuscript](https://www.math.cmu.edu/cna/Publications/publications2015/papers/15-CNA-017.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00332-016-9336-3). Inspected Section 2.2, equations (2.10)–(2.15), and the equilibrium-oriented scope. Their continuous model has constant coagulation rate and binary pair fragmentation rate proportional to `1/(x+y)`, giving constant total per-parent selection with uniform daughter fractions. This is genuinely the same type of fragmentation, but the coagulation kernel is constant rather than additive. Their stationary theory does not establish the proposed logarithmic escape and Gaussian limit for the critical additive model.

6. Michael Erlihson and Boris L. Granovsky, *Reversible coagulation-fragmentation processes and random combinatorial structures: asymptotics for the number of groups*, [full open preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0212170). Inspected the abstract, model scope, and Theorem 4.6. The Gaussian result concerns the number of groups in equilibrium random integer partitions as total mass tends to infinity. It concerns a different observable, limit parameter, and model. Reversible partition central limit theorems should not be cited as if they settle a time-asymptotic logarithmic size limit for the deterministic nonlinear equation.

7. Alex Ely Kossovsky, *Random Consolidations and Fragmentations Cycles Lead to Benford's Law* (2015), [full open preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.05235). Inspected the abstract and Part 2's model description. A finite collection alternates one randomly selected split and one uniformly selected pair merger, preserving its number and mass after each complete cycle. Simulations and qualitative multiplicative-process arguments discuss digit frequencies and lognormal behavior. Uniform pair choice differs from additive coagulation. The inspected material provides no matching uniform Wasserstein estimate or logarithmic central limit theorem. It is useful contextual precedent for the broad intuition that fragmentation may dominate typical logarithmic sizes despite mergers, not a rigorous substitute for the present statement.

Two additional potentially confusing search results were screened more narrowly. Bertoin–Watson, [*Probabilistic aspects of critical growth-fragmentation equations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.09187), concerns growth and fragmentation rather than binary coagulation; only the abstract record was inspected. Lancelot F. James, [*Coagulation-Fragmentation Duality of Infinitely Exchangeable Partitions from Coupled Mixed Poisson Species Sampling Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18668), has compound Poisson representations for structured partition dualities; only the abstract was inspected. Neither abstract supplies evidence of the current deterministic nonlinear log-size approximation.

The novelty distinction can be made directly from the equations. For pure fragmentation with selection rate `b`, the total number grows as `exp(bt)`. Dividing its number measure by that factor produces exactly the translation generator

```math
A\varphi(z)=b\int_{(0,1)}[\varphi(z+\log\theta)-\varphi(z)]B(d\theta).
```

The compound Poisson representation with rate `2b` follows immediately. Its drift is `2b E log Θ`, and its variance coefficient is `2b E[(log Θ)²]`, including randomness of the jump count. These observations are elementary deductions from the established fragmentation equation, not candidate new theorems. The nonlinear result would show that the critical coagulation equation shares this law up to a uniformly bounded logarithmic transport error. Consequently the error disappears under any diverging spatial normalization, including the `√t` normalization of the CLT.

Some limits on the proposed statement matter for positioning. Ordinary `W₁` on `P₁(R)` requires finite first absolute logarithmic moments initially and for the jump law. Finite number and mass alone do not control `∫_{(0,1)}|log x| n₀(dx)`. One can instead formulate an extended transport distance or a bounded-test comparison, but that needs an explicit argument. A weak Gaussian limit of centered, scaled logarithmic sizes does not imply convergence of original-size moments, a globally accurate lognormal density, or decay of coagulation event counts. In particular, conserved mean size can coexist with number-typical sizes tending to zero, because the size observable is not uniformly integrable under that limit. These are mathematical distinctions, not contradictions.

Searches combined additive/sum kernel, constant selection/fragmentation, logarithmic size, lognormal, compound Poisson, central limit theorem, coagulation-fragmentation, asymptotic behavior, and critical growth-fragmentation. Full articles were inspected where identified above; title or abstract screening is labelled separately. This finite search cannot establish exhaustive novelty. The defensible current assessment is: classical probabilistic limit law, plausibly new explicit nonlinear comparison for a particular critical kernel and its resulting transfer of that law. A publishable claim should retain that precise scope and cite the pure-fragmentation predecessors prominently.
