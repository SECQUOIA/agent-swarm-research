# Final literature audit: sharp daughter-law envelopes

Date: 2026-09-07. Scope: [sharp-daughter-extremality.md](../results/sharp-daughter-extremality.md) and [last-coagulation-exact-equal-split.md](../results/last-coagulation-exact-equal-split.md). This is a bounded novelty audit, not a proof review or certification of publication priority. No further research directions are proposed here.

The inspected literature establishes the exponential-functional benchmarks, convex-order techniques, and generator/martingale verification methods. I did not find the exact sharp optimization over parent- and time-dependent binary daughter laws for the probability of a future **auxiliary number-weighted coagulation**, or its optimal number–mass overlap coefficient. These remain plausible model-specific contributions. Their mechanism is a short application of established comparison methods once the auxiliary representation is available; they should not be presented as a new general theory of convex ordering or fragmentation control.

## Primary sources inspected

1. **Bertoin, Biane, and Yor, *Poissonian exponential functionals, q-series, q-integrals, and the moment problem for log-normal distributions*.** Inspected the exponential sum representation, Theorem 1.1(i), and its proof. Their product transform gives exactly the equal-split critical benchmark after rescaling: (S=I^{(1/2)}/2). Consequently the weighted exponential sum, product, and resulting scalar transform identities are established. The manuscript correctly attributes them. Their result does not itself identify this transform with a future-event probability for a nonlinear coagulation population. [Author-hosted primary article](https://monge.univ-eiffel.fr/~biane/q_poisson.pdf).

2. **Rüschendorf, Schnurr, and Wolf, *Comparison of time-inhomogeneous Markov processes*, Advances in Applied Probability 48 (2016), 1015–1044.** Inspected Section 3.2, Theorem 3.3 and proof. The theorem compares evolution operators from ordered generators and preservation of a test-function class. This is direct precedent for propagation of comparison through time-dependent dynamics. Applying their full abstract theorem here would require checking its domains and regularity; the manuscript's direct bounded killed-submartingale argument avoids claiming those hypotheses automatically. Neither the comparison principle nor the admission of time dependence is novel by itself. I did not find the present daughter constraints, coagulation killing rate, or extremal benchmark pair in that result. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.02925), [publication record](https://doi.org/10.1017/apr.2016.63).

3. **Boudabsa and Simon, *Some properties of the Kilbas-Saigo function*, Mathematics 9 (2021), 217.** Inspected the introduction, convex-order arguments for hyperbolic transform bounds, and preprint Proposition 5.4 with Remark 5.5. The authors use multiplicative martingales and convex ordering of exponential-functional-related random variables to order Laplace transforms; their examples include bounds involving an exponential law. Thus a rational envelope such as (1/(1+q)) emerging from convexity and an exponential benchmark is familiar probability methodology. Their parameter families involve stable subordinators and special functions, rather than optimization over all binary daughter laws or nonlinear auxiliary coagulation. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.05666), [publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/math9030217).

4. **Kyprianou and Pardo, *An optimal stopping problem for fragmentation processes*, Stochastic Processes and their Applications 122 (2012), 1210–1225.** Inspected the model, Sections 3–4, and the verification argument. They reduce optimization over stopping lines of a fragmentation process to a tagged-fragment generalized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck problem, using integrated exponential Lévy processes and a supermartingale verification method. The dislocation law is fixed; the optimization chooses when to stop. Our optimization changes daughter laws under a fixed conditional first moment and concerns a soft killing probability. This is a close methodological comparison, not the same optimization problem. [Author-hosted primary article](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/statistics/staff/academic-research/kyprianou/KP12.pdf).

5. **Knobloch and Kyprianou, *Survival of homogeneous fragmentation processes with killing*, Annales de l'Institut Henri Poincaré B 50 (2014), 476–491.** The earlier focused audit inspected the killing construction and survival results. Killing occurs at an exponential size barrier and concerns survival of a branching fragmentation population. It is distinct from killing a single auxiliary trajectory at intensity (bQ), and does not supply the sharp daughter-law envelopes or overlap identity. [Primary article](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1214/12-AIHP520.pdf); see the recorded inspection in [controlled-last-collision-literature.md](controlled-last-collision-literature.md).

Search screening also found optimal control of growth-fragmentation growth rates and a 2026 coagulation-control preprint. Those optimize different objectives or a scalar coagulation multiplier. Their abstracts do not establish the proposed envelope theorem, and I did not use abstract screening as evidence of a complete non-overlap assessment.

## Precise novelty assessment

The static inequality

\[
w(q/2)\leq E w(q\Theta)\leq\tfrac12[w(0)+w(q)],
\qquad E\Theta=\tfrac12,
\]

is ordinary Jensen and the chord bound. Equal splitting and endpoint splitting are the classical least and most variable distributions on a bounded interval with fixed mean. The novel candidate is the identification of the two appropriate infinite-horizon benchmark value functions and the resulting exact, attainable-or-approachable bounds for this particular coagulation observable. The benchmark functions remain valid against parent- and time-dependent daughter rules because the generator inequalities hold conditionally at every state. This is a useful robust statement, but its proof uses standard verification logic.

The theorem establishes an order of Laplace transforms of accumulated hazards. It does **not**, as written, establish full convex order of those hazards or stochastic ordering of the whole population. Literature about convex order is therefore methodological precedent; a claim of full hazard convex order would exceed the manuscript's conclusion.

The overlap identity (1-\|\eta-\pi\|_{\rm TV}=E_\eta\min(1,Q)) is standard. Likewise, for an increasing concave (g) with (g(0)=0), the coefficient (g(1)) in (g(q)\geq g(1)\min(1,q)) follows immediately from concavity and monotonicity. Thus the new candidate is the **dynamical interpretation and sharp model-specific coefficient**, not a new total-variation inequality. Once the sharp conditional envelopes are proved, the optimal (c_{b,\sigma}), critical (1/2), and equal-split value approximately (0.58058) are short corollaries. Monodisperse initial data establish sharpness; this does not determine the long-time decay exponent.

For the exact equal-split note, the last-event density identity is an application of marking an event by the conditional probability that no later event occurs. Its reduction to the specific nonlinear observable involving the classical product is a plausible application result. The scalar dissipation obstruction and uniform lower time-tail bound were not located in the inspected sources. This audit does not independently certify their priority or repeat their separate proof review.

## Closed status and search limits

The two manuscripts' cautious status statements are appropriate. A defensible description is: “Sharp daughter-law envelopes and optimal sampling-overlap constants for future coagulation in the additive model's number-weighted auxiliary process.” Avoid a broad claim that equal splitting is universally optimal for coagulation–fragmentation, that this optimizes physical tagged-material events, or that the exponential functional itself is new.

Queries covered exponential functionals with convex order, subordinator transform comparisons, fragmentation splitting optimization, time-dependent generator comparison, killed fragmentation, and coagulation/last-event/overlap terminology. Some publisher pages failed, so accessible author manuscripts and arXiv versions were inspected instead. No matching prior theorem was located in this bounded audit. The literature status is **candidate model-specific novelty, with established tools and benchmarks explicitly acknowledged; priority unresolved**.
