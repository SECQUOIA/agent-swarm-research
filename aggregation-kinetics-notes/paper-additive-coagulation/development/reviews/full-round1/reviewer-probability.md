The probability arguments need several distinctions that the draft handles correctly. In Theorem 4.1, the first-moment Lyapunov calculation is performed before marginal identification. The expected stopped rate is bounded using the stopped process, so the nonexplosion proof does not assume its conclusion. The separate prescribed flux is exactly 2 sigma+b and is integrable on every bounded time interval. Restricting the output coordinate in the loss formula retains incoming gain from large parents. Positive substitution then makes the prescribed probability dominate the complete minimal finite-jump law, and nonexplosion gives the latter mass one. This is enough to force equality and avoids an unsupported uniqueness assertion for an unbounded forward operator.

In Theorem 4.3, finiteness of the upward logarithmic sum alone would not imply finitely many jumps. The additional daughter-product argument fills that logical requirement. On every finite horizon the product martingale is bounded by exp(F(T)), and the global maximal estimate gives an almost-sure finite supremum even when F(infinity) is infinite. The intensity bound requires only pathwise finiteness of X_0, exp(A_infinity) and that supremum. It does not take an expectation of their product. Stopping the continuous compensator at deterministic levels then proves a finite count. In contrast, Tonelli and the identified marginals give E K_infinity=B_c(infinity), which may diverge. There is no hidden uniform-integrability assumption.

The transport result is stronger than a comparison of finite means. The clipped-test difference is a length and increases monotonically to the displacement. Thus the lower cost bound remains valid when both separate log means are undefined. Ordinary W1 is invoked later only after the necessary first log moments have been established. The path CLT uses the raw jump second moment, includes the fluctuations of the Poisson count, and transfers by a uniform perturbation bounded by abs(Z_0)+A_infinity. Nothing in that argument establishes convergence of actual variances or a density approximation, and neither is claimed.

The last-event arguments consistently avoid conditioning at the last time itself. The controlled first-event hazard is evaluated along the process with coagulation suppressed; it is not equated to the compensator of the full future path after its first merger. For general critical daughters, the eventual-last-event indicator is projected at each genuine event time, and only that projected mark is integrated against the compensator. Measurability of the survival function follows from the kernel recursion and infinite-horizon limit. The expected finite-window event count provides all needed local summability.

The daughter-envelope proof also remains within its moment assumptions when sigma exceeds b. Normalized size can grow between daughter events and the hazard need not have a finite second moment, but its first moment is one. The transforms have the first derivative and boundedness required for the finite-horizon generator argument. The endpoint benchmark is a comparison process, not an admissible daughter rule. Its approximation by two strictly positive fractions includes an explicit residual expected hazard tending to zero. Both conditional sharpness and the mean-constrained overlap examples therefore use realizable positive populations.

For the count diffusion, the original process and its martingale remain integrable on finite intervals because the event-count domination has finite moments there. After time rescaling, the bracket rate is 2b Z_n-b/n; its stopped upper bound is 2bR. This establishes the needed stopping-time modulus directly. The jumps are uniformly 1/n, so tight limits are continuous. The explicit Gaussian radial construction identifies the limiting Markov transitions and the nonnegative weak SDE solution, including c=0. The diffusion describes physical count on time n; it does not validate continuum size laws at the earlier logarithmic horizon.
