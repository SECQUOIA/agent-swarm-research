**Checks of supporting arguments and integration.** Appendix A is a real proof for the measurable kernel class used in the main text. I checked the output-coordinate loss restriction, weak integration of moving measures, the positive Picard construction for bounded kernels, the third-moment cutoff error of order inverse cutoff, and removal of the third-moment initial assumption using weighted stability. The stability coefficient uses only locally bounded second moments and is uniform in cutoff. No narrow-limit passage through a discontinuous parent kernel is needed. Appendix D supplies the missing type of argument that unbounded observables would otherwise require: tangent truncations differ from a bounded function by a linear mass test, both increments have integrable majorants, and the complete integrated identities pass to the limit. Its right-derivative argument at monodisperse equal-split data is sufficient for sharpness.

I checked the unnumbered inverse-size generator identity directly. Multiplication by `h=1/x`, followed by subtraction of the eigenvalue `sigma-b`, gives the displayed number generator, while the mass process has an extra coagulation rate `b`. Thus the later finite-last-event statements are not being transferred to an ordinary physical mass tag. I also checked that the parent-independent reference is introduced only when its construction is valid. The repeated distinctions between the auxiliary process, a physical finite vessel, and independent continuum observations are scientifically necessary and consistent across the abstract, assumptions table, theorem statements, and discussion.

The pure-coagulation last-event formula follows from the displayed Borel generating equation with argument `exp(-exp(-bt))`. Subtracting the linear tail term leaves `h^2/2` asymptotically equal to `exp(-bt)`, giving the coefficient `sqrt(2)`. The sharper benchmark exponent is not confused with instantaneous sharpness of the robust differential inequality. At criticality the product is a conditional-size survival function; its large-size logarithmic-square asymptotic is not used as a calendar-time asymptotic.

I checked the physical omitted-diagonal correction in `sections/finite-population.tex:158` and following: the diagonal continuum contribution is negative for fractional orders, so removing it adds `lambda(2-2^p)M_(p+1)/n`. At order zero it gives the count drift `b/n`. The mass-CDF theorem does not require a uniform initial second moment, because existence is used separately for each finitely supported preparation. Nor does it establish accuracy at earlier increasing times; the manuscript says so.

For the power-kernel material after Theorem 10.4, I recomputed the diagonal and cross terms in the two-atom fractional drift. The coefficient `2^p+2^(2-p)-4` is positive, the cross term remains bounded when `alpha<=1`, and the growth condition is `p>1-alpha`. The supplied exact expression at `alpha=1,p=1/2,R=64` exceeds `151/500`. These are vector-field examples, with an explicit qualification concerning time-dependent existence. The Tran–Van equilibrium comparison retains its finite-count distinction and uses the correct scaling of mass and time.

For Appendix E, I also checked the unnumbered finite-grid reconstruction formulas, parameter count, source reconstruction, and deterministic error constants `3/c^2`, `5/(2c)`, and `7` for the source estimate. The finite-difference error is `eta/t+Lt/2`; its stated minimizer and required validity interval are correct. Fixed-mass preparation, same-shape dilution, and changing the surrounding medium are not conflated.
