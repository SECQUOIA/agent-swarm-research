**S2-SRC-001 — MINOR — distinguish a log lattice of the reference from atomic support of the coagulation–fragmentation law.**

- Location: `sections/log-limits.tex:269–274`, paragraph following the logarithmic limit theorem.
- Finding: The sentence about equal splitting preserving a lattice in log size follows a discussion of `Z_t`, and therefore reads as a statement about the full coagulation–fragmentation law. Equal splitting preserves a log lattice for the pure-fragmentation reference with appropriate initial data. Coagulation need not preserve that lattice. Atomic support and lattice support are different properties.
- Evidence: Take the allowed data `n_0=delta_1`, positive constant coagulation and fragmentation rates, and equal splitting. At every positive time, positive surviving populations at sizes `1`, `2`, and `3` arise from zero, one, and two coagulation gains. Their logarithms `0`, `log 2`, and `log 3` cannot belong to one arithmetic lattice, since `log 2/log 3` is irrational. Meanwhile the countable set of positive dyadic rationals is closed under addition and halving, so the accepted construction preserves atomicity from these data. This supplies the intended obstruction to a density claim without a log-lattice assertion about the full law.
- Proposed correction: Replace the sentence with, for example: “For equal splitting and monodisperse initial data, the size laws remain atomic. The pure-fragmentation reference is supported on a lattice in log size.” The preceding warning that a weak CLT does not imply a lognormal density is correct.

The mathematical checks below support acceptance of the new theorems.

**Number process and mass tagging.** Dividing by the count balance produces the stated number generator, including the factor `2 sigma` in fragmentation and the absence of the extra `b(t)` in the coagulation rate. The first-moment stopping argument controls both overshoots and the expected stopped jump count. It does not need a second size moment. Marginal identification is not circular: the prescribed curve satisfies the loss-integral equation, its positive substitutions dominate the minimal finite-jump expansion, and the independent nonexplosion argument makes that expansion a probability measure. The output restriction retains the full incoming gain. I also checked the inverse-size generator identity directly; both its potential term and its fragmentation term are correct. The text appropriately limits this calculation to an identity of generators and uses the direct construction for the path law.

**Finite correction and finite total coagulations.** The logarithmic increment inequality gives the advertised integrable bound using the accepted half-moment decay. Both the finite-clock and infinite-clock cases are handled correctly. Conditional daughter mean one half suffices for the product martingale even with measurable parent dependence. Its finite-horizon bound and maximal inequality give a finite pathwise supremum. Combining this with finite `A_infinity` yields finite cumulative coagulation intensity without requiring integrability of its random upper bound. Stopping the compensator at a deterministic level then proves finitely many coagulations almost surely. The possibly infinite expected total count is consistent with that conclusion. The controlled size dichotomy follows from the same product identity.

**Transport and limits.** The selfsimilar assumption is imposed exactly where needed for the independent marked-Poisson reference; the parent-dependent case is not silently treated as independent. The clipped-test proof establishes the exact extended transport cost without subtracting infinite means. The constant-rate factors, including the critical factor two in the decay exponent, agree with the foundation. The functional limit proof reduces to iid increments of variance `r E[Y^2]`; its interpolation error vanishes under precisely the stated second jump moment. The bounded pathwise correction transfers the functional limit uniformly and hence in `J_1`, without an initial log moment. Ordinary Wasserstein conclusions add the required first initial log moment. The first-moment truncation argument, infinite-negative-mean speed, diverging-scale transfer, mean-log and entropy identities, and pure-coagulation endpoint all have the stated hypotheses. No second log moment, density convergence, or variance convergence is inferred from a weaker conclusion.
