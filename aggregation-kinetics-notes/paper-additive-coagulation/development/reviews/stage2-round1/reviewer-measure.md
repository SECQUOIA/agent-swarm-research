## MEASURE-S2-01 — MINOR: distinguish a log lattice from an atomic nonlinear population

**Location:** `sections/log-limits.tex`, lines 269–274, paragraph following Theorem `thm:log-limits`.

**Finding.** The final sentence says that “equal splitting can preserve a lattice in log size.” In this paragraph the object being discussed is the actual nonlinear log-size law, and the standing assumptions include positive additive coagulation. Equal splitting preserves a log lattice for the pure-fragmentation reference with compatible initial data, but addition does not preserve that lattice in the full population.

For instance, start from \(n_0=\delta_1\) with equal splitting and positive constant rates. Sizes \(1\) and \(1/2\) can coagulate to \(3/2\), outside the powers of two. More generally, the full population has positive atoms at sizes \(1,2,3\) at positive times: the initial size has positive survival weight, and successive coagulation gains produce the others. Thus the log support contains \(0,\log2,\log3\), which cannot lie in any single lattice \(a+h\mathbb Z\), since \(\log2/\log3\) is irrational. Nevertheless, starting from size one, addition and halving preserve the countable set of positive dyadic rationals. The nonlinear size law can therefore remain purely atomic, which is sufficient for the intended warning about density conclusions.

**Concrete fix.** Replace the final sentence with, for example: “For monodisperse initial data and equal splitting, the pure-fragmentation reference stays on a log lattice, while the full coagulation–fragmentation law can remain purely atomic.” Alternatively, mention only the atomic nonlinear example. The weak and functional CLTs themselves need no correction.

## Independent checks of the main arguments

**Construction and nonexplosion (`thm:number-process`).** The normalized bounded-test equation has the stated generator, including fragmentation rate \(2\sigma\) and coagulation rate \(\lambda Nx\). The prescribed size law is a measurable probability kernel, so the quantile construction works with arbitrary measurable parent dependence. Both clocks are locally integrable when the state is bounded. For the stopped process, the expected absolute increments are controlled by \((b+\sigma)x\); the added mark has finite conditional mean \(m/N(t)\), including at an overshoot. Consequently the stopped Dynkin estimate does not assume a second size moment. Positivity allows the stopped drift to be bounded using the expectation of the stopped state, giving the stated Gronwall estimate. Removing the size stop for fixed jump number is valid because only finitely many finite states occur before that jump. The resulting uniform expected stopped count bound implies \(k\mathbb P(\tau_k\le T)\le L_T\), and hence nonexplosion.

**Marginal identification and weak kernels.** The prescribed curve has integrated flux \(\int_0^T(2\sigma+b)\,dt<\infty\). Its gain and loss are therefore finite weak measure integrals. The accepted loss lemma applies with weight one and the local bound \(2\sigma+\lambda NR\). Restricting the output variable retains the entire incoming gain, including arrivals from larger parents; this avoids a false closed equation on a bounded size set. Iterating the positive survival identity correctly makes the prescribed law dominate every partial sum of finite-jump contributions. Nonexplosion makes the full sum a probability law, so domination identifies the marginals. This does not invoke unproved uniqueness for an unbounded forward operator or a Bochner derivative. The inverse-size generator calculation is also correct and is appropriately limited to an algebraic identity.

**Finite correction and martingale (`thm:controlled-path`).** Nonnegative compensation gives exactly the displayed rate \(a(t)\). The estimate \(\log(1+r)\le\sqrt r\), together with the accepted half-affinity estimate, makes its full-time integral finite. The integral tail tends to zero even when the coarser exponential clock bound does not. The daughter product has conditional multiplier mean one half despite dependence on the parent and the coagulation history. Its compensated drift vanishes in the full filtration, and its deterministic finite-horizon bound justifies a true mean-one martingale. Doob's inequality then gives a finite global supremum almost surely. The resulting pathwise coagulation-intensity bound uses no expectation of \(e^{A_\infty}\). Stopping the count at a compensator level proves that finite total intensity gives finitely many jumps; the separate identity \(\mathbb E K_t=B_{\mathrm c}(t)\) is consistent with an infinite limiting expectation. The two controlled path limits follow from the same product identity and the fragmentation driver's finite or infinite total activity.

**Moment-free transport (`thm:transport`).** Under selfsimilar daughters, the marked fragmentation driver is independent of the initial state and coagulation driver; it gives the required standalone reference. The manuscript correctly excludes this conclusion for adaptive parent-dependent marks. The ordered coupling yields the upper transport bound. Increasing clipped identity differences give its matching lower bound without subtracting infinite marginal means. The signed remainder is used only through bounded tests and paired increments, so no hidden separate log moment is needed. The constant-rate and critical constants agree with the Stage 1 estimate.

**Probability and functional limits (`thm:log-limits`, `cor:infinite-log-speed`, `prop:general-scale-transfer`).** The compound-Poisson strong law uses only the first daughter log moment. The centered unit-block increments used for the invariance principle are iid with variance \(r\mathbb E Y^2\), as required. The finite-second-moment tail bound makes the largest within-block fluctuation negligible after division by \(\sqrt T\); this justifies replacement of interpolation by the actual path in the uniform metric and hence in \(J_1\). The bound \((|Z_0|+A_\infty)/\sqrt T\) transfers the functional limit without initial log moments. For ordinary \(\mathsf W_1\), the additional initial first absolute log moment is explicit. Uniform integrability is applied to the centered reference, where the second-moment bound is available; the nonlinear correction transfers convergence through its first-moment cost. Truncation proves the first-moment-only transport law of large numbers and the infinite negative speed assertion. The general diverging-scale transfer requires a reference limit and does not manufacture domain-of-attraction assumptions.

**Means, entropy, and pure coagulation.** First log integrability is established before taking expectations. The count-normalization term in the relative-entropy identity has the correct sign, and no mass-weighted log moment is used. The zero-fragmentation endpoint correctly omits a daughter log-moment requirement. Finite total coagulations give eventual constancy of the pure-coagulation path; the clipped-test argument gives both displayed exact transport costs. The Borel formula, limiting probabilities, and moment distinctions are correct.
