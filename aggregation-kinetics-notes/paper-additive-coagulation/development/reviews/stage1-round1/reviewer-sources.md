The following source checks are confirmations and qualifications, not additional defects.

**Cepeda.** I checked the actual [open manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.1934), especially Hypotheses 2.1–2.2, Definition 2.4, and Theorem 2.5, on PDF pages 3–5. The relative-fragment measure is fixed, with no parent dependence; the selection rate can depend on parent size but satisfies boundedness and derivative assumptions. The theorem requires only the homogeneity moment of the initial measure, which is the first moment for the additive kernel. It also imposes a dislocation representation with no eventwise mass increase and an integrability condition. Thus the current comparison in `sections/model.tex:92–97` is reasonable when read with its time-independent selfsimilar qualification. It must not be upgraded to a claim that this theorem directly supplies the entire present daughter class. An optional precision edit is to say “weaker initial-moment assumptions are available for fixed relative-fragment laws satisfying the dislocation hypotheses.” The year, volume, issue, and pages in `Cepeda2014` agree with the [arXiv journal-reference record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.1934): 2014, 27(1–2), 105–136. The linked arXiv version was revised in 2015; that does not make the journal year wrong.

**Norris.** I checked [the original open preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9801145), Section 2, especially conditions (2.4)–(2.5) and Theorem 2.1 on PDF pages 4–5, rather than relying on the repository extraction where formulas are missing. Its kernel is measurable and bounded by a product of a continuous sublinear weight. A finite squared-weight initial moment gives a maximal strong solution; an additional additive bound gives global existence. Taking a suitable multiple of `1+x` covers pure additive coagulation with finite count and second moment. The theorem also gives mass conservation and uniqueness against another admissible local solution when one is strong. It has no fragmentation term, so it is not a direct citation for arbitrary measurable daughters. The current paper makes no contrary claim and explicitly disclaims novelty for existence methods. Adding Norris as background would be useful in the planned literature synthesis, but its current absence is not a demonstrated error in this self-contained proof.
