## Proof decisions and resolved gaps

1. The finite count uses unordered pair rates `lambda*(xi+xj)/n`. Excluding self-pairs gives deaths `b*(L-1)` and normalized count drift `b/n`. The process is dominated in event count by a linear pure-birth chain, which permits unstopping the moment calculations. Concentration is absolute; relative accuracy is qualified when initial normalized count tends to a positive value.
2. The mass-CDF theorem uses the exact same empirical measure for finite and continuum initialization. At `C log n`, a single fixed `p<1` gives a positive exponent whenever `C>1/(b log 2)`. The proof is pathwise through the deterministic physical ceiling `nm`, and also applies to the expected empirical mass law. The threshold is sufficient, with no earlier-time approximation, first-breakdown, or propagation-of-chaos assertion. The moment ratio is explicitly relative, not an absolute-error lower bound.
3. The classical count diffusion is derived rather than imported with an unchecked scaling convention. The PGF yields transition laws uniformly over bounded scaled initial states; stopped bracket increments verify path tightness. The two-dimensional Brownian radius construction identifies the continuous limit, its drift/noise constants, and its moments. This calculation is labeled classical.
4. The old entropy qualification “whenever justified” is removed. For both `x^2` and `x log x`, a tangent extension beyond `R` becomes bounded after subtracting a linear function. The accepted bounded weak equation and exact mass conservation therefore apply. Convexity of both the truncated function and its remainder bounds coagulation and fragmentation increments by integrable majorants requiring only locally bounded M2. Dominated convergence gives the complete absolutely continuous balances.
5. The stronger entropy constant is valid: the accepted sharp fractional pair inequality, divided by `1-p` and passed to `p=1`, gives `(x+y) Delta(x log x) >= 4(log 2)xy`. Together with daughter Jensen, this proves production at least `lambda*m^2*log 2`. Equal splitting and monodisperse initial data attain it. Continuity of M2 and total variation imply weighted `(1+x^2)` variation convergence at zero, justifying the right derivative used for sharpness. There is no M3 or daughter-log assumption.
6. The M2 daughter bound uses only expected daughter count two, expected daughter mass x, and support below x. It does not require a realizable eventwise daughter pair, unlike the physical finite-system theorem. This corrects the unnecessary restriction in the older source note without changing any accepted manuscript result.
7. Count-neutral classification and observable rigidity range over every finitely supported preparation at a fixed mass, allowing count to vary. They are initial vector-field statements for general kernels; full-time count neutrality is stated only for mass-conserving trajectories with integrable event rates.
8. The power-kernel stationary proof first uses count to force `s=m`. Its rate-weighted invariant law has finite inverse-power integral even when the population has no negative moments. Monotone convergence makes the singular stationary test legal, including when daughter inverse moments might otherwise be infinite. The positive drift gap is then finite and strictly positive. No nonexplosive auxiliary path or time-dependent power-kernel solution is assumed.
9. The atomic power counterexample is a vector-field obstruction, with all monodisperse and cross terms displayed. At alpha=1, p=1/2, R=64, exact rational squares certify the positive bound `151/500`; a global trajectory is not asserted. For every alpha>1/2 the half moment can have positive drift at some such atomic preparation.
10. The Tran–Van comparison gives the rate and mass rescaling explicitly. Their stationary Bernstein identity implies finite positive mass but infinite particle count. Those known equilibria do not contradict the finite-count exclusion. The paper does not infer general critical population existence from well-posedness of a transformed equation.
