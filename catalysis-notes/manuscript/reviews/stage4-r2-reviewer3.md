## Quantitative and decision checks

- **Acceptance windows now support the intended accounting.** Lines 34–44 distinguish product acceptance from policy operating requirements. Production and return-assessment windows, maximum intervention durations, and return deadlines are fixed prospectively. Planned low-output intervals retain their time and resource costs; retrospectively relabeling a failure as recovery is prohibited. Usable physical EO does not disappear from the reported balance merely because the policy fails. This resolves the round 1 ambiguity.
- **The cost boundary is dimensionally and economically scoped.** Lines 46–54 compare costs per bed campaign over a common horizon and matched initial Ag inventory. EO value times EO amount, unit resource/disposal costs times their quantities, and labor cost per hour times labor hours all have monetary units. Rearranging campaign value minus preparation, resource and labor costs yields the stated inequality. Preparation is not charged again as an operating resource, and elapsed downtime is not automatically billed as labor. Without valuations, only a symbolic boundary and physical differences are claimed; no unsupported numerical economic optimum is inferred.
- **Untreated aging remains a real competing choice.** Lines 25 and 58–60 require an independently replicated untreated policy through the full horizon, selected from its own measured response within the common operating and tuning limits. Fresh sham qualification alone no longer excludes delayed processing damage. A Ni/sham difference that does not improve upon untreated operation is appropriately labeled processing dependent.
- **Absolute recovery output is correctly distinguished from recovery benefit.** For a common pre-intervention history up to age `a`, the difference between recovery and continuation over the full horizon is exactly the difference integrated over `H − a`. Lines 89–97 state that boundary, preserve each material's own continuation baseline, and require independent validation of both absolute recovery outputs and the decisive ranking. A large sham recovery benefit, or sham recovery beating ordinary Ni, cannot remove a Ni advantage if the tested Ni recovery policy performs better.
- **The comparison includes all relevant tested policies.** The ranking includes admissible Ni and sham ordinary/recovery policies and untreated ordinary operation at the same horizon, acceptance rules and inventory basis, with resources reported. Transfer to a different aging history requires validation. The text explicitly limits the conclusion to the finite tested set rather than an unmeasured global optimum or a periodic-service policy.

No new source-performance quantity was introduced by this revision.
