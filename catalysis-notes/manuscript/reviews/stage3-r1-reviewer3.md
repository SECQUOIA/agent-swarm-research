## Accounting and quantitative checks

- **The isotope equation is correct under the stated assumptions.** Lines 29–39 specify two classified original carbon sources, constant ethylene enrichment throughout the relevant history, calibrated carbon atom fractions, and bounded isotope discrimination. The balance `x_j = f_PE x_PE + (1 − f_PE) x_E` yields the displayed formula. Multiplying by product carbon number and molecular amount gives moles of polymer-origin carbon, the stated demand denominator. The manuscript correctly avoids using a singly labeled molecular abundance as an atom fraction.
- **Retained carbon is handled coherently.** Constant enrichment allows material carried from earlier charges to retain its original PE/ethylene classification without falsely identifying the originating PE charge. Cumulative collection is therefore the appropriate primary endpoint. The text includes beginning/end inventories, catalyst-bound carbon, other carbon inputs, and a prohibition on counting residue disappearance and later collected product twice. Replacing methane and requalifying the reference closes an otherwise consequential ambiguity in two-source attribution. Failure to classify appreciable other carbon closes the saving claim rather than being silently absorbed into PE output.
- **The demand and productivity equations have consistent units.** `D_Na` and `D_W` are catalyst-solid mass per mole of accepted polymer-origin carbon; `J_PE` is that carbon amount per complete time. Supports are included, elemental inventories are separate, and time includes interruption and any policy regeneration. Gross propylene output is not substituted for polymer recovery.
- **The three-charge arithmetic is correct.** All-fresh operation uses 2.4 g solids; no makeup uses 0.8 g; the midpoint uses 1.0 g; the full once-only supplement uses 1.2 g. The total-solid productivity factors are `2.4 f` and `2 f` for midpoint and full supplement. At equal accepted output, the midpoint's 0.6 versus 0.8 g Na/alumina is a 25% reduction; 1.0 versus 1.2 g total solids is a 16.7% reduction. These are correctly presented as conditional arithmetic, not observed savings.

## Experimental inference and practical decisions

- Lines 73–81 require a semibatch-specific reuse deficit and Na-rescue comparison before interpreting pressure as a rescue intervention. Fixed total pressure and flow, measured gas composition, common charge-1 history, fresh comparisons at both conditions, and full-charge collection support an operating comparison without claiming an intrinsic reaction order. Lower inlet ethylene need not imply lower compression demand or lower stoichiometric consumption, as the chapter states.
- The time-resolution and carryover limits are substantive. A short-lived propylene release is not credited as renewed production; pressure switching is conditional on measured response times. The separate workup qualification prevents recovered chain distributions from being assumed to represent the hot reacting mixture.
- Independent sequence replication, pilot-based consequential differences, isotope/balance uncertainty, product specification, and the complete-time ceiling are appropriate decision requirements. The withheld intermediate dose is selected prospectively, tested on independent sequences, and cannot become “validated” through retrospective refitting. Irregular dose response leads to selection among measured policies, not an invented optimum.
- The extension through further PE charges addresses deferred replacement. It counts both catalyst components, actual output, retained carbon, quality, and elapsed time, and compares demand at a common accepted-output target. Three-charge savings alone are not presented as sustained process superiority.

## Source and originality checks

Checked Conk's original PDF pp.4–5: Fig.4B explicitly supports a single 0.4 g Na/alumina addition before charge 2 and PE alone before charge 3. The manuscript uses that schedule and does not adopt the source's stronger inference that Na rescue proves unchanged W activity. Checked original SI S6–S8 and S17–S18: gas charging precedes heating, methane is the standard, semibatch sampling is every 12 minutes, and the optimized example specifies gauge pressure. The chapter preserves these distinctions.

Checked Wang's original PDF p.78, printed p.73, which explicitly describes isomerization and fragment recombination during depressurization and a 2–3-hour cooling period. Checked the final Chen model discussion: finite isomerization, the restricted physical regime, numerical quasi-equilibrium check, saturation/inhibition, omitted deactivation, and neglected ethylene gradients are represented accurately. The prior-work discussion appropriately limits originality to a demonstrated and predicted reduction in fresh-material demand on retained Na/W, rather than claiming pressure effects, Na rescue, or a chain-population model as new.

Instrument access, isotope-method qualification, semibatch reuse behavior, and a positive intervention result remain experimental dependencies. Their absence as measured results is not a defect in this proposed program.
