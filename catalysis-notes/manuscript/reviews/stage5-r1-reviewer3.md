## Numerical and source-dependent conclusions

- **Tungsten:** Checked Li's original article Table 1 and original SI Table S3. Ni/M alone gives 27.1% EG; Ni/M plus the preleachate gives 29.5%. The shortlist correctly rejects assigning the whole latter yield to exported W and does not claim that the difference is statistically resolved or uniquely W-mediated. The retained/soluble reference and export/carbon qualification are proportionate first steps for an exploratory hold, not a new full campaign.
- **Withdrawn mannose premise:** Rendered and inspected El Mohammad's original SI Figure S7. Mannose feeds produce substantial glycolaldehyde under the stated hot conditions. Figure S8 documents epimer formation. The withdrawal is correctly limited: these observations undermine an assumed inactive-mannose reservoir without establishing direct mannose cleavage or excluding an inactive fraction.
- **Liquid Ti-zeolites:** Checked Torres's original thesis pp.58–60, Figure 2.6. Added epoxyhexane changes solvent OH spectral features in Ti-MFI, supporting the cited precedent. The shortlist does not turn that adsorption spectroscopy into a validated reaction-network prediction and explicitly requires qualification in the chosen liquid system rather than transfer of vapor-fed parameters.
- **Zirconia:** Checked the original Artsiusheuski discussion and Scheme 1. Hydrocarbon scavenging of bound water is already proposed; the cleaning products are below detection in the reported regime. The proposed pilot correctly starts with useful net output and chemical accounting, and avoids inferring process savings merely from omitting steam.
- **Zeolite assay history:** Checked Class-Martínez's original methods, which cycle the same sample through aging and NH3-TPD with intervening water and thermal treatments. Checked Luo's original Section 3.4.2/Figure 7: both aging-order samples are assayed before their next aging exposure. The proposed assay/no-assay comparison is therefore not already supplied by that order test. Function before terminal titration and capacity after a common assay remain distinct endpoints; a schedule null is not made into an intermediate-lifetime measurement.
- **Wet methane and Ga:** Checked Ryu's original Figure 6 caption and accompanying methods, which include NO in the sulfur exposure, and Wu's original oxygen-pulse discussion, which assigns O2 consumed minus CO2 formed to catalyst reoxidation. The low-NO application question and the warning about hydrogen/other oxygen-product contributions are sound. The latter identifies an attribution ambiguity rather than asserting that the published performance or redox interpretation has been disproved.

## Ranking and scope

The overview consistently presents four developed programs in the reviewed order: water, cyclic oxides, polymer catalyst demand, and promoted Ag. It labels this an editorial and scientific-development ranking, permits laboratory access to alter first-experiment priority, and does not claim a numerical optimization or readiness for four simultaneous campaigns. The concise summaries preserve the decisive distinctions introduced in the chapters: late additive intervention, ordinary steam comparison before reset, crossed pressure–dose tests, and untreated/ordinary/recovery Ag comparators.

The six remaining entries are genuinely brief, conditional reserve ideas. Each identifies a missing decision or measurement without presenting a mechanism correction as a demonstrated useful intervention. Tungsten's first position among reserves does not contradict its exploratory-hold status. The final withdrawal paragraph separates unsupported inferences from rejection of entire research areas. No material omission warrants expanding these entries into additional experimental chapters.
