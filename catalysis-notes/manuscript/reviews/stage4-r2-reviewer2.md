Measured promoter inventories, processing controls, and Ag morphology help define the intervention, while the manuscript avoids equating bulk matching with identical promoter placement or isolated working Ni atoms. Preserving a functioning promoted reference is a reasonable entry condition, and lack of a fresh Ni advantage does not prematurely close a later retention question.

The ordinary-operation design uses independent charges, common preparation blocks and conditioning clocks, matched horizons, and instrument-drift checks. Useful chloride choices are selected before retention runs. Changes in flow or temperature are treated as parts of the policy and charged through their effects on output and exposure. The chapter does not require an arbitrary severe excursion to manufacture a practical problem.

The revised acceptance rules at lines 42–44 distinguish product qualification from production requirements. Planned conditioning, recovery, and return windows can have low output without automatically invalidating a potentially useful policy; their durations and return deadline are fixed and all costs remain counted. Conversely, an unexpected low-output interval cannot be relabeled afterward to rescue a failed policy. Unfiltered EO and physical balances remain available for interpreting inadmissibility separately from off-spec product.

The preparation-cost inequality at lines 46–54 is dimensionally consistent and explicitly conditional on stated valuations. It compares a named candidate and comparator on a matched initial Ag and complete-campaign basis, reports actual total inventory and volume, and distinguishes initial preparation from operating resources and labor. The symbolic boundary is an appropriate output when defensible unit values are unavailable; no numerical economic optimum is claimed.

The recovery experiment still compares treatment with ordinary continuation on matched-age Ni and sham beds. Its own intervention and return intervals enter the benefit integral, and distinct subsequent trajectories are retained. The identity at lines 89–95 correctly constructs each recovery policy's full-clock output from its own continuation baseline, provided the specified pre-intervention histories and inventory bases match.

The paired validation at lines 95–99 prevents a favorable sham recovery from being compared only against untreated recovery-free Ni operation while a superior tested Ni recovery is omitted. Both absolute recovery predictions and the decisive ranking are validated on independent preparation blocks, and all admissible tested ordinary policies remain comparators. Restricting the conclusion to the finite tested set is appropriate; no global optimization or repeated-service result is inferred from one recovery.

The mechanistic branch remains conditional. A useful empirical recovery prediction can succeed without microscopic identification. Optional isotope-return and composition-series experiments are not required for the initial operating decision.
