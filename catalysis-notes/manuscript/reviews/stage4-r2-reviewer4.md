## Assessment of the revisions

**Untreated aging is now a practical comparator.** `manuscript/sections/04-silver.tex:25`, lines 58–60. Fresh agreement between untreated and sham materials no longer substitutes for checking their later behavior. At least one independently replicated untreated policy is carried through the full horizon, selected from its own chloride response within common operating and tuning bounds. The Ni/sham pair still estimates the complete Ni-addition intervention; the untreated policy checks whether that intervention improves the functioning starting catalyst. A processing-dependent rescue is explicitly distinguished from such an improvement. A full untreated recovery factorial is unnecessary for the stated finite-policy comparison.

**The recovery decision ranks both materials fairly.** Lines 80–97. The model is calibrated and prospectively tested for Ni and sham, with matched operating age and pre-intervention history. The absolute-output identity at lines 89–95 is correct: each recovery benefit adds to its own full-clock continuation baseline. All admissible tested Ni/sham ordinary and recovery policies, plus untreated ordinary operation, enter the same ranking. A sham recovery that beats ordinary Ni cannot remove a useful Ni advantage if tested Ni recovery performs better. History transfer remains conditional on validation, and the manuscript appropriately limits the result to the tested policies.

**Acceptance windows permit a genuine recovery policy without hiding its costs.** Lines 34–44. Product criteria apply to every credited amount, while production and return-assessment windows carry the operating requirements. Planned conditioning, recovery, and return intervals have fixed bounds and a return deadline. Their elapsed time and resource use are charged, and a failed production interval cannot be relabeled after observation. Unfiltered EO and physical balances remain visible. These rules avoid both automatically disqualifying every recovery and allowing recovery downtime to disappear from the comparison.

**The preparation-cost boundary is dimensionally and conceptually coherent.** Lines 46–54. The inequality compares campaign preparation costs with additional accepted-product value after differences in operating resources, disposal, and labor. It specifies matched initial Ag inventory and reports actual bed inventories, avoids charging preparation twice, and distinguishes downtime from labor. Without valuations, the symbolic threshold and physical differences are the appropriate result. It is a finite campaign decision boundary, not a complete industrial economic analysis; the chapter does not claim otherwise.

## Whole-program judgment

The program still progresses from a functioning promoted catalyst and ordinary drift to a useful recovery or persistence prediction. It does not create an artificial excursion to manufacture the need for Ni. Its contribution remains a controlled Ni-dependent relationship that predicts a material or chloride-management choice, beyond historical retention and familiar chloride optimization alone.

The alternative explanations remain properly constrained. Calibrated chlorine/product kinetics, local product gradients, processing effects, and changing morphology or promoter inventories compete with an additional memory term. Neither curve departure nor a bulk assay establishes a particular minority oxygen or promoter population. A useful operating result can stand without that microscopic assignment, while a stronger mechanism requires an independent discriminator.

The withheld test now validates both absolute recovery outputs and their consequential ranking. Failure to resolve a useful contrast, failure of a candidate recovery, or success of the familiar baseline closes the appropriate claim. One successful treatment still cannot support periodic-service or industrial-lifetime extrapolation. The remaining experimental dependencies are explicit and do not constitute invalid design.
