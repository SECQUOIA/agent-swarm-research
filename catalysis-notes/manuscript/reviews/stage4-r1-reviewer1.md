## Prior work and source accuracy

- **`sections/04-silver.tex:10` — historical Ni retention is credited and accurately bounded.** Kemp's original preparation and composition tables support the reported Ni loading and the Cs difference between aged catalysts A and C. Table III reports losses of 4.3 and 6.1 selectivity percentage points after 50 days. The chapter correctly distinguishes these losses from an exact endpoint difference, notes reoptimization and accelerated operation, and identifies that solvent control B was not aged. The approximate 1:835 bulk atomic ratio follows from 13.5 wt% Ag and 88 ppm Ni. This prior disclosure prevents claiming Ni-dependent retention itself as new; the chapter explicitly respects that boundary.
- **`sections/04-silver.tex:12` — the Jalil comparison does not overstate transferability or durability.** The main article supports preferential suppression of combustion and cooperation with chloride on its studied Ni/Ag materials. I also visually inspected supplementary Figure S21, PDF page 30, printed page 29: the NiAg EO rate first rises after chloride introduction and then declines substantially. The chapter describes that trajectory correctly and does not turn it into evidence of durable performance on Cs/Re-promoted Ag.
- **`sections/04-silver.tex:14` and `:21` — mechanistic and reactor-model attribution is appropriately limited.** Hwang's architecture dependence motivates preserving a functioning reference; it does not establish direct Ni-to-Re oxygen transfer. Iyer and Bhan's 2021 Section 3.4 integrates independently assessed kinetics into a forward reactor calculation, so crediting that accomplishment is warranted. The chapter also distinguishes this prior steady-reactor result from a validated temporal recovery model on a new formulation.
- **`sections/04-silver.tex:14` and `:48` — the chloride-management alternative is real prior art.** [US20240279193A1, Example 2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240279193A1/en) reports 28-day tests and fits the days 8–28 results before extrapolating later performance; flow also differs among chloride conditions. [US20240025869A1, Examples 3–6](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240025869A1/en) adjusts temperature to maintain work rate and separately tracks the optimum modifier requirement. The chapter preserves these distinctions and requires an actual Ni-free operating competitor.
- **`sections/04-silver.tex:16` — the intended original contribution is sufficiently distinct and conditional.** A validated relationship showing where chloride operation substitutes for Ni, or where Ni retains a useful advantage, goes beyond either rediscovering the composition or repeating historical endpoint retention. The scoped evidence search also correctly notes that [US20260027557A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20260027557A1/en) contemplates Cs, Re and Cl promoters alongside its Ni/Ag formulation. The chapter makes no exhaustive priority claim.
- **`sections/04-silver.tex:62` — the titration qualification is supported.** Iyer and Bhan's 2023 paper treats total trifluoroethanol uptake as an upper-bound estimate because inactive adsorption cannot be excluded and compares different estimators. The chapter does not mistake that measurement for an exact working-site count on Ni-containing material.

## Scientific design and interpretation

The material controls at lines 23–25 distinguish the complete Ni-addition intervention from processing damage without pretending to isolate every microscopic effect. Common-setting comparisons and separately selected useful policies have distinct roles, with independent charges and preparation blocks specified at lines 30 and 46.

The output accounting at lines 34–42 includes conditioning, downtime, resource consumption, unfiltered EO and additional inventory normalizations. The stoichiometric example at line 8 is correct under its stated two-product assumption. The carbon-balance requirements at line 28 account for ethane and cofed products rather than assigning all carbon dioxide to ethylene combustion.

Ordinary operation precedes a recovery campaign, and the finite-horizon stopping rules prevent creating an artificial practical problem through severe excursions. Recovery and continuation retain separate matched-age trajectories. Lines 56–64 appropriately require familiar chlorine and product effects to be tested before assigning a new functional state or microscopic mechanism.

The recovery integral at lines 70–77 measures a within-material benefit. The explicit additional comparison of absolute, complete-clock output prevents confusing a larger recovery benefit with a better material. The prohibition on transferring recovery predictions across unmatched operating histories is also necessary and present. Training, a second duration check, and an independent withheld test make the predictive claim falsifiable without demanding positive results in advance.
