# Full manuscript review, round 1 — reviewer 1

**Verdict: approve with one minor source-traceability edit. No major scientific revision identified.** The manuscript presents four developed proposed programs, distinguishes published observations from calculations and proposed experiments, and makes defensible, bounded originality claims. Experimental success is appropriately left open. The reserve list is short relative to the main programs and explains what evidence would justify reopening each idea.

I independently read `main.tex`, all six section files, and `references.bib`, and checked the 34-page PDF's extracted text and pagination. I did not read other reviews, contact peer reviewers, or edit the manuscript or literature library.

**Major findings: none.**

The central claims survive the decisive source checks:

- **Water, `sections/01-water.tex:8–22, 26–48`.** Fang 2022 does report the mixing-distance comparison and removal of PDVB from used CoMnC; the manuscript correctly declines to claim these as new. Fang 2026 Fig. 1 and its methods support the low-dose separate-granule formulation, matched nominal bed volume, reaction conditions, initial activity penalty, and long-duration functional effect. The manuscript does not inherit the source's stronger active-site drying interpretation. Its storage/conductance distinction and two-pool counterexample correctly explain why aggregate water decay alone does not identify local exposure. I independently read the retained source XLSX and integrated the products of the linearly interpolated conversion and selectivity series: 116.0352 and 219.8266 h on 25–585 h, agreeing with Eq. (1). The two stated productivity ratios also agree.
- **Cyclic oxides, `sections/02-cyclic-oxides.tex:15–29, 41–48, 82–96`.** Brody 2022 original PDF pp. 4, 6–7 and 13 supports the Ar-purged experimental cycle, steam heat-exchange model, 53.2% and 47.24% C2+ yields, and inconsistent 10/20 wt% loading statements. It does not establish steam damage, as the manuscript explicitly recognizes. Gao 2020 Fig. 4D/E distinguishes the coated-LSF inhibition comparison from the separate molten-carbonate reversible switch experiment; the manuscript preserves that distinction. Chacko's original dissertation, chapter 4.1 (PDF pp. 110–117; printed pp. 95–102), supports the high-loading mixed-carbonate precedent and coupled hydroxide/carrier regeneration. Fereres 2018, section 3.6 and Fig. 9, supports prevention versus incomplete delayed restoration as existing precedent. The proposed ordinary steam/Ar comparison, followed by a separately defined recovery and timing question, is therefore a meaningful remaining experiment.
- **Polymer, `sections/03-polymer.tex:8–16, 20–39, 45–64, 75–87`.** Conk 2024 original PDF Fig. 4B confirms that fresh Na/alumina is added only before the second charge; the manuscript's catalyst-solid accounting is correct. Fig. 4A reports labeled-product evidence, not a universal polymer-carbon fraction. The proposed consistent ethylene labeling and cumulative carbon-origin accounting address that distinction. The source's semibatch comparison changes both flow and pressure, so the manuscript correctly qualifies the new reactor mode and avoids extracting an intrinsic pressure order. Chen 2024's finite-isomerization model, saturation/inhibition predictions, omitted deactivation/gradients, and limited physical applicability are accurately described. The new contribution is the crossed pressure/makeup decision and withheld prediction, not discovery of pressure effects or Na rescue.
- **Silver, `sections/04-silver.tex:9–15, 21–25, 55–63, 89–101`.** Kemp's original patent HTML, preparation parts B–D and Table III, confirms the 4.3 versus 6.1 percentage-point losses, approximate 1:835 bulk Ni:Ag ratio, different Cs contents, and absence of an aged solvent-control result. These limitations are accurately retained. The proposed untreated/sham/Ni comparison and inclusion of ordinary and recovery policies directly address them. Hwang 2026 supplies a proposed coupled promoter mechanism, not evidence of Ni-to-Re transfer. Iyer 2023 explicitly treats total titrant uptake as an upper-bound site estimate; the manuscript correctly avoids an exact site-count claim. A finite material/policy comparison plus a consequential independent prediction is a valid proposed contribution despite the old retention precedent.

**Minor finding M1 — identify the workbook behind the secondary water calculation.**

Location: `sections/01-water.tex:12–18`, Eq. (1), and the `fang2026` entry in `references.bib`.

The numbers reproduce, but the manuscript cites only the article and calls the inputs “their published series.” A reader cannot immediately tell whether these were digitized from the figure or read from the source-data workbook. This is particularly relevant because conversion and selectivity have different observation times and ranges.

Remedy: add a short source note identifying Fang 2026's **Source Data workbook, sheet “Figure 1”**, state that conversion and selectivity were separately interpolated before integrating their product, and retain the stated common interval. A footnote or brief parenthetical is sufficient; no new analytical campaign or numerical revision is needed. The retained authoritative artifact is `literature/papers/data2026-source-workbook-for-hydrophobic-promoter/original.xlsx` (reference conversion A/B, reference C5+ selectivity C/E, promoted conversion G/H, promoted C5+ selectivity I/K). Link to the publisher's public source-data artifact or identify it through the article; do not distribute the supplied library artifact.
