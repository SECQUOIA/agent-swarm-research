## Minor finding — optional precision

**Use the direct timing-contrast interval when closing the timing-benefit branch.** `manuscript/sections/02-cyclic-oxides.tex:126`, referring to lines 70–76.

The sentence about both timing arms recovering within the consequential-loss bound is a useful practical shorthand, but separate recovery bounds are not generally the same as a bound on the difference between policies. Different dry-history baselines, gains above baseline, and uncertainty can matter. The chapter already measures the relevant contrasts.

**Suggested remedy:** state that the persistent concurrent-over-delayed benefit is excluded when the interval for the predeclared direct wet P–R contrast excludes a consequential advantage; use the interaction interval for the narrower steam-dependent timing claim. No additional arm or experiment is needed. This does not prevent approval because the required measurements and uncertainty framework are already present.

## Coherence and contribution of the full revised program

The program now has a clear sequence: test ordinary operating compatibility; assess recovery; diagnose a justified treatment-order effect; compare usable policies on complete-cycle output and burdens; then test whether a measured state predicts an independent operating history. The dilute ten-minute diagnostic and the selected five-minute steam duty are explicitly separate. Treatment cadence during accumulated diagnostic exposures, ordinary operation, and terminal analysis is also distinguished.

The scientific claims remain proportionate to the evidence. Equilibrium direction does not become a five-minute rate law, downstream Li does not identify a volatile molecular species, total Li does not establish coating coverage, and carbonate carbon is not automatically assigned to hydrocarbon combustion or coke. The new NanoSIMS and Fe-state measurements are candidate methods with explicit matrix, sampling, resolution, and interpretation limits. Their failure closes the corresponding mechanism claim without invalidating a direct ethylene-output result.

The contribution extends beyond a diagnostic. The untreated policy comparison can support a bounded compatibility result; the managed-policy comparison can establish whether recovery or prevention earns its gas, time, and heat burden; and the withheld alternating schedule requires predictive value beyond simple aging descriptions. Close precedents are acknowledged. The chapter does not claim novelty for carbonate stabilization or regeneration alone.

The stop rules are credible. They distinguish benign operation from treatment-enabled recovery, constrain nulls to the actual duty and history, and reject a practically inferior intervention even if it preserves a favorable material signal. The absence of demonstrated steam damage, a qualified reset, or adequate state-measurement sensitivity remains an experimental dependency rather than an invalid design.
