## Source fidelity and prior work

- **Tungsten, `sections/05-shortlist.tex:7` and `:28`: supported.** Li's original Table 1 gives 27.1% ethylene-glycol yield for Ni/M, while supplementary Table S3 gives 29.5% for Ni/M plus the prepared solution. The shortlist correctly refuses to call 29.5% a leachate-derived contribution and does not claim that the difference is statistically resolved or zero. Ti–O–W stabilization and reuse are credited as prior work. I visually inspected El Mohammad's supplementary Figure S7: mannose feeds form glycolaldehyde with both tungstate and molybdate under the reported hot conditions. The shortlist retains the distinction between productive feed conversion and identification of a direct cleavage route, and does not exclude an inactive fraction.
- **Liquid Ti-zeolites, `sections/05-shortlist.tex:10`: supported and suitably conditional.** Torres's Figure 2.6 compares solvent spectra with and without epoxyhexane, providing direct precedent for product-induced solvent changes. Kwon's work uses a vapor-fed reactor with condensed pore solvent; its discussion of Figure 5 reports sublinear epoxide dependence and limitations in allocating product inhibition. The shortlist requires kinetics qualified for the chosen liquid system before a frozen prediction and does not treat those vapor-fed results as an established liquid baseline.
- **Zirconia, `sections/05-shortlist.tex:13`: supported.** Artsiusheuski's Section 3.6 attributes the asymptotic behavior to water binding and hydrocarbon-assisted removal, but reports that the proposed cleaning products lie below detection in the tested low-conversion regime. The proposed oxygen balance and assay-perturbation controls address a real interpretation boundary. The text appropriately conditions expansion on useful output and avoids inferring process savings from steam omission.
- **Zeolite assay history, `sections/05-shortlist.tex:16`: supported.** Class-Martínez's methods return the same specimen through repeated hydrothermal aging and NH3-TPD sequences, including a wet purge. Luo's original Section 3.4.2 measures NH3-TPD after the first aging treatment on both specimens before continuing their different aging sequences. That comparison therefore cannot answer assay versus no assay. Measuring function on matched specimens before a terminal titration avoids silently replacing the state of interest with recoverable capacity after the assay.
- **Wet methane, `sections/05-shortlist.tex:19`: supported.** Ryu's Figure 6m discussion introduces NO before adding SO2; it does not supply the proposed sulfur comparison without NO. Auvinen reports different NO effects on fresh and sulfur-aged catalysts. The shortlist asks about a material ranking under a specific duty rather than presenting NO promotion as new or assigning a unique microscopic cause.
- **Ga regeneration, `sections/05-shortlist.tex:22`: supported.** Wu's Figure 1 analysis assigns oxygen equal to formed CO2 to coke combustion and the remainder to reoxidation; the shortlist correctly identifies why that subtraction alone is not a selective Ga inventory. [US20220126281A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20220126281A1/en) explicitly proposes shortening or eliminating later oxygen treatment and reports conversion examples for enriched-oxygen regeneration of a Ga-containing catalyst. The shortlist credits that intervention prior art without treating endpoint conversion as complete-cycle productivity.

These statements establish appropriate limits on the proposed contribution. They do not claim exhaustive novelty, prove that a cited mechanism is false, or use a missing measurement as evidence of a positive new mechanism.
