\section*{Overview and recommended priorities}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Overview and recommended priorities}

This portfolio recommends developing \textbf{four ideas in the main document}, in the order shown below. Each connects an important catalytic limitation to a bounded intervention, a fair material or operating comparator, and a prediction that can be tested on independent experiments. Four is an editorial choice: it gives the two strongest development candidates full treatment while retaining two more immediately bounded studies with concrete resource-use decisions. The remaining ideas are recorded briefly in Section~\ref{sec:shortlist}; their present limitations do not justify equivalent experimental scope.

This ordering concerns scientific development and space in the manuscript, not a commitment to run four campaigns simultaneously. Water management is the first feasibility priority and cyclic oxides the second feasibility priority. Polymer and Ag may offer the best first experiment where their reactors, reference materials and analytical methods already exist. Laboratory access, preparation reproducibility, achievable uncertainty and actual costs have not been established. None of the four currently warrants an unrestricted campaign or a claim of competitive industrial lifetime.

The programs preserve different decisive comparisons. Water management introduces the additive after common catalyst conditioning and tests function as well as water response. Cyclic oxides first compare ordinary steam and Ar operation before using equal-dose \COtwo{} timing and a common analytical reset to investigate a consequence. Polymer work crosses the selected ethylene condition with fresh-Na dose instead of comparing one low-pressure/low-dose choice against an incompletely optimized reference. Ag compares untreated, sham-processed and Ni-treated materials, then the useful ordinary and recovery policies available to both Ni-containing and Ni-free choices. A simpler credible operating alternative is part of each material decision.

Three kinds of statement are kept separate throughout. Published observations are attributed to their sources and retain their preparation, measurement and time boundaries. Calculations are deductions from explicit assumptions or identified published data; they are not new experimental measurements. Proposed experiments specify the information needed for a decision, possible outcomes and limits of interpretation. Their contribution is a testable research design, not advance confirmation of a mechanism. In particular, the document does not establish lower local water exposure at Co sites, steam-induced Li loss, selective loss of polymer initiation, or preservation of a particular Ag--Re oxygen-transfer state.

The common standard for expansion is a consequential prediction: measured behavior should identify a material or operating choice and correctly anticipate its response under a withheld condition or history. A finite, well-resolved null can close that choice within its tested scope. More characterization is justified when it separates explanations that would lead to different decisions. Full elapsed time, inventories, product origin where relevant, and treatment burdens remain part of the comparison even when a mechanistic interpretation is successful.
