\section{When does nickel improve the useful life of promoted silver?}
\label{sec:silver}

\subsection{The opportunity and the claim that survives prior work}

Ethylene epoxidation must favor ethylene oxide (EO) over combustion while maintaining enough reaction rate to produce EO economically. Selectivity alone can reward an almost inactive catalyst. The proposed program therefore asks whether dilute nickel changes the sustained output of a functioning Cs/Re/chloride-promoted Ag catalyst enough to justify a different material or operating policy. It starts with ordinary operation, then tests one prediction about the observed difference. It does not presume that Ni preserves a particular oxygen species or Ag--Re arrangement.

The potential value can be illustrated without predicting a catalyst improvement. If EO and complete combustion are the only carbon outlets, making one mole of EO at fractional ethylene selectivity $S$ consumes $1/S$ moles of ethylene and $0.5+3(1/S-1)$ moles of oxygen, and produces $2(1/S-1)$ moles of \COtwo. Raising $S$ from 0.90 to 0.91 at fixed EO output would reduce these reaction-level requirements by 1.10\% for ethylene, 4.40\% for oxygen, and 10.99\% for \COtwo{} formation. Recycle, separation, utilities and catalyst manufacture are outside this calculation. A useful measured improvement must also survive those costs.

Ni-dependent retention on a fully promoted catalyst is already reported. Kemp's 1995 patent describes Ni added after preparation of an Ag/Cs/Re/S catalyst, with lithium-containing precursors also used. Its Ni-containing catalyst lost 4.3 selectivity percentage points after 50 days of accelerated aging, compared with 6.1 points for its Ni-free parent; measurements followed return to 40\% oxygen conversion and reoptimization. Both started within a reported 86.0--86.5\% selectivity range. The bulk Ni:Ag atomic ratio was approximately 1:835. However, the aged pair differed in Cs content and post-impregnation treatment. A solvent-treated, Cs-matched Ni-free control had poorer initial performance and was not aged. Thus the disclosure establishes relevant prior performance, while leaving the isolated Ni effect and useful integrated output unresolved. The endpoint difference is a difference in losses from separate starting values, not an exactly measured final-selectivity difference or a demonstrated lifetime extension.\citep{kemp1995}

Jalil and colleagues provide a different starting point: dilute Ni suppresses combustion more strongly than EO formation on supported Ag, and organochloride gives a further selectivity increase. Their main materials lack the complete Cs/Re promoter system. Their processing controls, model-surface experiments and Ni spectroscopy support a Ni-associated effect, but nominal loading does not count working surface Ni atoms. The supplementary chloride-response trace shows an initial EO-rate increase followed by substantial decline on NiAg. It demonstrates evolving cooperation, not a stationary, durable output advantage on the proposed formulation.\citep{jalil2025,jalil2025si}

Gas-phase alkyl-chloride modifiers deposit chlorine on Ag, while competing processes, including removal by alkanes such as ethane, reduce the retained chlorine. This balance changes oxidation rates and selectivity, so chloride adjustment can trade selectivity against useful EO rate. Its optimum depends on the material and operating conditions; neither a universal coverage nor a universal modifier setting follows.\citep{iyer2021}

Hwang and colleagues link the distinctive Cs/Re/chloride response to promoter architecture and propose conversion of otherwise unselective oxygen into an additional epoxidizing intermediate. That proposed sequence motivates a compatibility question; it is not direct evidence that Ni supplies oxygen to Re or preserves their contact.\citep{hwang2026} The competing operating explanation is equally serious. Iyer and Bhan already combined independently assessed oxidation, chlorine exchange and support-mediated EO degradation in a forward reactor prediction. Santos and colleagues demonstrate that temperature, oxygen, water and \COtwo{} change the chloride response.\citep{iyer2021,santos2024} Lower-chloride aging policies and materials with a more stable optimum chloride requirement also appear in prior disclosures. The former includes 28-day experiments followed by longer-term extrapolation; the latter maintains work rate by adjusting temperature. Neither a fitted distant crossover nor a stable chloride optimum proves stable activity.\citep{lee2024underchloride,modifier2024}

The original contribution sought here is therefore narrow: a composition- and processing-controlled, Ni-dependent relationship that predicts when changing chloride operation can substitute for Ni, or when a useful Ni advantage survives that alternative. Better controls alone do not establish that contribution. A practical benefit may be established without a unique microscopic explanation; a mechanistic claim requires a discriminator beyond a selectivity curve.

\subsection{Stage A: qualify one material pair and a fair output comparison}

\paragraph{Preserve the functioning promoter architecture.}
Use an existing, reproducible Cs/Re-promoted Ag/alpha-alumina reference and its qualified epoxidation reactor. Access to that platform is an entry requirement. Transferring every promoter to a dilute colloidal-Ag support could remove the reference function before Ni is tested. Hwang's Ag/support area dependence makes preserving the useful reference architecture more defensible than matching nominal particle diameter alone.\citep{hwang2026}

Make three materials from each parent preparation: untreated reference, sham-processed reference, and Ni-treated reference. A first, historically grounded route is post-impregnation with the nickel-nitrate/amine-containing solution and staged drying described by Kemp, adapted to the existing material only after a small compatibility check. Start near a bulk Ni:Ag atomic ratio of 1:800, an illustrative preparation choice close to the historical dilute loading, rather than assuming Jalil's 1:200 optimum transfers. Apply all solvent, ligand, drying and handling steps to the sham, omitting the Ni precursor and documenting any unavoidable solution-composition difference. Measure actual Ag, Ni, Cs and Re contents, other intentional promoters, and Ag size distributions before claiming a matched pair. Matching gross inventories does not establish identical promoter placement.

The untreated reference diagnoses damage caused by the added processing and remains an ordinary-aging policy comparator; the sham is the causal comparator for the complete Ni-addition intervention. If the sham loses the reference's useful response, permit one correction tied to an identified preparation problem, then stop this route if the problem remains. Fresh qualification cannot exclude a processing penalty that emerges later, so untreated operation must also be followed over the complete comparison horizon. A Ni/sham retention difference without a useful advantage over untreated operation is a processing-dependent result, not an improvement of the starting catalyst. The historical route does not establish isolated Ni in Ag. Add an unpromoted Ag/Ni--Ag bridge only if attributing the result specifically to Jalil's fresh-chemistry function; a full Ni-by-Re composition series is a later discriminator, not an entry requirement.

\paragraph{Separate common conditions from useful operating choices.}
Commission gas balances, temperature uniformity, transport sensitivity and feed-to-analyzer response before assessing small selectivity differences. Measure EO, ethylene, oxygen, \COtwo, water and any significant carbon byproducts, with an independently calibrated flow tracer. Distinguish net formation from outlet concentration when \COtwo{} or EO is cofed. Ethane used to remove chlorine is another possible carbon source: measure its conversion and do not assign all \COtwo{} to ethylene combustion. The ratio $r_{\rm EO}/(r_{\rm EO}+r_{\rm CO_2}/2)$ is an ethylene selectivity only when its carbon-source and product assumptions hold. Use consumed ethylene and a closed product balance for the reported selectivity; unresolved cofeed contributions limit interpretation.

Apply a common initial conditioning protocol to untreated, Ni and sham materials, and record their resulting states instead of forcing equal initial selectivity by different treatments. Matching a reproducible history is feasible; identical microscopic starting states are not guaranteed. Use a small overlapping bracket of chloride settings with reference returns, keeping temperature, pressure, ethylene, oxygen, ethane and flow common for the Ni/sham causal comparison. Qualify a useful untreated-reference setting within the same operating and tuning bounds. Specify absolute pressure and the actual chloride feed, not only a nominal chloriding index. Wait according to observed rates and gas holdup, with a bounded maximum dwell. If reference returns remain history dependent, report age-specific trajectories rather than assembling different ages into a fictitious steady curve. Independent preparations of the decisive contrast are required; repeated samples from one bed do not measure synthesis variation.

Use these data for two distinct questions. At overlapping conditions, does Ni change the EO and combustion rates? Under each material's own selected chloride setting, which choice gives more accepted EO? A shifted chloride optimum can answer the second without proving altered oxygen chemistry. Do not require a fresh Ni advantage to proceed: if both materials qualify, a fresh null still permits a later retention difference.

Define the output requirements before the ordinary-operation comparison: an accepted ethylene-conversion interval, a selectivity floor, a minimum EO work rate, and common temperature and feed limits. Set these from the reproduced reference and the intended operating decision, not from whichever endpoint favors Ni. Fix a finite complete observation horizon $H$, starting with initial conditioning, and the smallest useful contrast using commissioning uncertainty and available run time. An unresolvable contrast requires a better measurement or a narrower claim, not an assumed long-term advantage.

For policy $p$, use the complete elapsed-time productivity
\begin{equation}
 P_p(H)=\frac{Q_p(H)}{m_{{\rm Ag},0}H},\qquad
 Q_p(H)=\int_0^H \dot n_{{\rm EO},p}^{\rm accepted}(t)\,dt.
 \label{eq:ag-productivity}
\end{equation}
The accepted rate is zero for unqualified product or downtime; product-acceptance windows must be fixed and compatible with analyzer response. Separately predeclare production windows and a return-assessment window in which conversion, selectivity and work-rate requirements apply. Planned conditioning, recovery and return intervals may have low or zero output, but their maximum durations and return deadline are fixed before the run. All elapsed time and resource use remain charged, and product criteria apply to every credited EO amount. Failure during a required production window or failure to return within the allowed interval makes a policy inadmissible; an inconvenient low-output interval cannot be relabeled afterward as planned recovery. Temperature and feed bounds apply throughout.

Report the unfiltered net EO integral and the timing of every qualification failure alongside $Q_p$. Policy inadmissibility does not make chemically usable EO disappear; distinguish it from actual off-spec product and retain all physical flows in the material balance. Include initial conditioning, chloride searches that recur in the proposed policy, recovery and return to production. Also report output per initial total catalyst mass and occupied bed volume, since Ag normalization alone can hide dilution or additional material. Integrate ethylene and oxygen consumption, net \COtwo{} generation, chloride and ethane use, and temperature history over the same clock. Higher selectivity with lower work rate or higher required temperature remains a tradeoff.

For each admissible candidate policy $p$ versus a named tested comparator $b$, state a preparation-cost boundary per bed campaign over $H$, with matched initial Ag inventory and actual bed masses and volumes reported:
\begin{equation}
 C_{{\rm prep},p}-C_{{\rm prep},b}
 \leq v_{\rm EO}\,[Q_p(H)-Q_b(H)]
 -\sum_k v_k\,[U_{k,p}(H)-U_{k,b}(H)]
 -v_L[L_p(H)-L_b(H)].
 \label{eq:ag-preparation-cost}
\end{equation}
Here $v_{\rm EO}$ is value per mole of accepted EO, $U_k$ are resource or disposal quantities with explicit unit costs $v_k$, and $L$ is operating handling labor with cost $v_L$ per labor-hour. Include measured feed and modifier demands, utilities with a stated measurement or estimation basis, and any valued emissions or waste. Preparation costs include the initial material and its processing; do not count those costs again as operating resources. Downtime is already charged through complete-clock output and is not automatically an extra labor cost. Without defensible valuations, report this symbolic boundary and the physical differences, not a numerical monetary saving or economic optimum.

\subsection{Stage B: ordinary drift before an excursion campaign}

After the exploratory measurements, assign independent charges within each preparation block to the Ni/sham common-setting pair and the useful policies identified above, including at least one independently replicated untreated-reference policy. Start their comparison clocks with the same conditioning protocol and follow all arms over the same elapsed horizon. A common-setting arm can also serve as a policy arm when its entire history is identical; otherwise use separate beds or independent matched-age runs. Randomize reactor positions or run order within preparation blocks, include reference checks for instrument drift, and establish uncertainty from independent runs. The common-setting pair identifies the effect of the Ni intervention under that exposure; separately chosen policies compare what an operator could use. They are not interchangeable estimands.

Select the untreated policy from its own measured chloride response: use one lower-chloride setting if it meets the requirements, otherwise a credible reference setting or scheduled chloride adjustment fixed before aging. Do not copy a fitted percentage of another catalyst's optimum. Ni and sham receive the same operating bounds and opportunity to select a useful policy, with equal limits on tuning effort. Hold the other operating variables common where feasible. If maintaining accepted conversion requires different flow or temperature, that change belongs to the policy, and its rate, residence-time, product-exposure and temperature consequences enter the comparison. At least one credible untreated policy is indispensable even if its fresh response matches the sham. It need not receive the full chloride or recovery factorial. Report the best tested untreated policy explicitly in absolute output/resource rankings.

Compare cumulative EO and reactant loss throughout $H$, not just endpoint selectivity. Lower-chloride operation may pay an ongoing selectivity penalty; it cannot be booked as a one-time startup cost. An unchanged optimum does not imply that the rate or peak selectivity is unchanged. Likewise, a temperature controller can conceal activity loss unless its full trajectory is retained. No accelerating exposure is needed to create the primary result. If neither ordinary drift nor a useful material/policy contrast is resolved within $H$, close the program at that preparation and operating scope. An arbitrary severe chloride pulse or an extrapolated crossover cannot supply the missing practical burden.

If a consequential loss develops, compare one mild recovery intervention with ordinary continuation on matched-age beds of both materials, using their prespecified common-setting aging history. Choose the recovery chloride setting from the established useful bracket, hold other feeds fixed, and return both beds to the common reference setting for a prespecified observation window. This is a test of recoverable conditioning under that intervention, not a general regeneration claim. A failed recovery establishes persistence over its tested duration; it does not establish absolute irreversibility. The recovery and continuation arms must retain separate subsequent trajectories because treatment may alter later drift.

\subsection{Stage C: distinguish useful memory from familiar chlorine response}

The first explanatory baseline is the measured chlorine/product response on each material, informed by established chlorine-exchange and reactor models.\citep{iyer2021,santos2024} Calibrate it on the actual formulation and feed domain, with independent checks of response times and, where conversion requires it, axial product gradients. Lower combustion generates less water and \COtwo{} within the bed, so equal inlet products need not give equal local states. A transferred literature model with uncalibrated parameters is not a fair null model for new chemistry.

Compare the EO--\COtwo{} rate trajectory during recovery with independently measured steady or matched-age chloride-response curves. A trajectory following those curves can still have a useful Ni-dependent recovery speed; curve collapse does not predict the time taken to move along them. Conversely, departure from a whole-bed curve can arise from axial mixtures of chlorine states, temperature changes or product gradients. It does not identify promoter relocation. Add a slower functional state only if a calibrated chlorine/product baseline fails by more than its prediction uncertainty and a consequential unexplained difference remains. The state then denotes reproducible rate memory, not a counted population of selective oxygen sites.

Use at most one additional discriminator selected by the result. If reduced self-generated \COtwo{} appears to explain the Ni benefit, compare the pair under a common, externally controlled product burden and predict an untested burden after accounting for bed gradients. If ordinary loss survives reconditioning and an inventory change is plausible, examine retained elemental amounts and Ag morphology on sister samples. Unchanged total Re cannot exclude redistribution; a changed mean particle size need not explain all lost function. Ordinary EO cofeed measures net product sensitivity because it can change both EO formation and destruction. Contact-time curvature is also insufficient to identify a secondary combustion flux.

A working-state assay is justified only when it resolves the surviving alternatives. Chlorine stripping is an operational inventory with desorption and normalization assumptions. Trifluoroethanol titration is destructive and estimator dependent: Iyer and Bhan explicitly treat total uptake as an upper-bound site estimate because inactive adsorption is possible.\citep{iyer2023} Neither becomes an exact active-site count on Ni-containing material without new validation. Use sister samples, qualify changes during quench and transfer, and require enough sensitivity to detect the minority population invoked by the explanation. An unchanged element-average spectrum cannot exclude such a minority when its abundance is unconstrained.

Oxygen-isotope return measurements and a Ni-by-Re composition series remain optional extensions. They are not needed to decide which tested policy produces more EO. A return experiment would additionally require a justified relation between the labeled population and oxygen actually returning to gas, a complete balance and a resolvable signal. Neither high selectivity nor failure to detect gas return alone proves direct Ni-to-Re oxygen transfer.

\subsection{Stage D: predict one operating decision and its cost}

The principal follow-on, if ordinary drift proves partly recoverable, is a prediction of when a bounded chloride recovery pays for itself. For both Ni and sham, use the matched-age recovery and continuation traces to calibrate the simplest time-dependent description consistent with both EO and \COtwo{} rates. A single relaxation time is permissible only if its approach and return are resolved and repeatable; an unobserved asymptote and relaxation time cannot both be estimated reliably from a short early segment. Use a second training duration to check the description before selecting a withheld duration within the tested range. Rates alone need not uniquely identify surface coverage, and the prediction must not rely on that identification.

At a fixed common remaining horizon $H_r$, define the recovery benefit relative to continuation as
\begin{equation}
 B_m(t_R;H_r)=\int_0^{H_r}
 \left[\dot n_{{\rm EO},m,R}^{\rm accepted}(t;t_R)
 -\dot n_{{\rm EO},m,C}^{\rm accepted}(t)\right]dt,
 \label{eq:ag-recovery-benefit}
\end{equation}
where $m$ specifies Ni or sham, $t_R$ is the recovery duration, and both trajectories start at the same defined operating age $a$, leaving $H_r=H-a$. The integration includes recovery itself and all return time; recovered peak activity is not the benefit. Evaluate resource differences on this same boundary. The model must predict the sign and an uncertainty interval for $B_m$ and the EO/\COtwo{} trajectories for both materials. Construct each recovery policy's absolute campaign output as
\begin{equation}
 Q_{m,R}(H)=Q_{m,C}(H)+B_m(t_R;H-a),
 \qquad m\in\{\mathrm{Ni},\mathrm{sham}\},
 \label{eq:ag-absolute-recovery}
\end{equation}
using its own continuation baseline with the identical pre-intervention history, initial clock and inventory basis. Compare \emph{all admissible tested} Ni and sham ordinary and recovery policies, plus untreated ordinary operation, at the same $H$, with the same acceptance rules and full resource accounting. Report inventory-normalized outputs and the preparation-cost boundary on the stated matched-inventory basis. A sham recovery beating ordinary Ni does not remove a useful Ni advantage if the tested Ni recovery policy is better; a larger within-material benefit also does not determine the best absolute output. A comparison against only ordinary Ni supports that narrower conclusion.

Recovery learned under the common-setting history cannot be assumed to transfer to a different policy history. Lock the withheld duration, return setting, acceptance windows and predictions for both Ni and sham before testing their recovery and matched continuation on independent preparation blocks. Validate both absolute recovery outputs and their decisive ranking, with uncertainty, against each other and the tested ordinary policies, including untreated operation. Do not retune chloride after observing the withheld result, and do not exclude a tested Ni recovery policy simply because sham recovery has already beaten ordinary Ni. This ranks the finite tested policies, not an unmeasured global optimum.

Success requires an observed decision-changing benefit or limit within $H_r$, with predictive accuracy sufficient to resolve the predefined useful contrast. A familiar chlorine model that already predicts the result supports an operating choice but closes the claim of an additional Ni-specific mechanism. If a Ni-dependent memory term is necessary, it must improve withheld prediction beyond that calibrated baseline and identify a useful preparation or operating dependence. One successful recovery does not justify a periodic-service model: repeated returns to the same productive state and measured recurrence costs are prerequisites for that extension.

If the important ordinary difference is persistent rather than recoverable, replace this branch with one prespecified product burden or exposure duration that the surviving explanation predicts will retain or remove the Ni advantage. Do not pursue all branches in parallel. A result that only reproduces historical Ni retention, that disappears under familiar chloride adjustment without a further consequential relationship, or that requires an open-ended synthesis campaign should remain a bounded negative or confirmatory study. A successful program would instead deliver a reproducible formulation/policy comparison and a validated prediction of where its advantage ends. Competitive industrial lifetime remains outside the evidence of a finite laboratory campaign.
