\section{Polymer ethenolysis: maintain useful output with less fresh catalyst}
\label{sec:polymer}

\textbf{Proposed contribution.} Determine whether an operating change can substitute for part of the fresh sodium-on-alumina addition needed when a supported sodium/tungsten catalyst mixture processes successive polyethylene charges. The target is a predicted reduction in fresh catalyst solids per accepted polymer-derived carbon output, at a specified product quality and complete cycle time. The first intervention is one lower ethylene partial pressure on an existing semibatch platform. Selective loss of an initiating function is one possible explanation of reuse behavior, not an established result or a prerequisite for a useful operating rule.

\subsection{Prior work and the remaining opportunity}

Isomerizing ethenolysis combines double-bond migration with metathesis using ethylene to convert unsaturated chains into shorter olefins, ultimately including propylene. A saturated polyethylene (PE) feed additionally needs a route that creates reactive unsaturation. Conk and colleagues established direct conversion of PE and polypropylene with WO\textsubscript{3}/SiO\textsubscript{2} and Na/$\gamma$-Al\textsubscript{2}O\textsubscript{3}. W/silica supplies olefin metathesis and Na/alumina supplies olefin isomerization: double-bond migration and reaction with ethylene repeatedly shorten a reactive chain. These established tandem roles do not determine how saturated PE first becomes reactive or which function changes during reuse. Their standard 1\,g PE experiment uses 0.4\,g of each solid at 320\,$^\circ$C; high fresh-batch output and heterogeneous catalyst reuse are already demonstrated.\cite{conk2024}

The most important benchmark is their three-charge reuse experiment. The starting mixture contains 0.4\,g Na/alumina and 0.4\,g W/silica. In the supplemented sequence, another 0.4\,g Na/alumina is added \emph{only before charge 2}; charge 3 receives PE alone. The mixture is retained, not separated into spent and fresh catalyst components. The unsupplemented sequence loses output, while that single addition restores output over the reported sequence.\cite[Fig.~4B]{conk2024} Fresh-Na rescue is therefore the comparator, not a discovery proposed here. Rescue does not uniquely demonstrate unchanged W function: added Na material can alter unsaturation supply, isomerization, activation, contact, and the balance among tandem functions.

Initiation and regeneration are also active prior territory. Conk's publicly available dissertation abstract proposes transfer dehydrogenation with ethylene as hydrogen acceptor, rather than the original paper's cracking interpretation, and describes modified catalysts and dimethyl-ether-based regeneration. The body remains embargoed, so these disclosures constrain originality without supplying a verified complete protocol or performance comparison.\cite{conk2025thesis} Patent WO2026011187A2 discloses oxidative treatments and subsequent dimethyl ether treatment; its Figure~21 caption names a Na--Fe formulation while adjoining regeneration procedures are not unambiguously consistent about catalyst identity. The disclosure cannot be treated as a demonstrated regeneration benchmark for every unmodified Na/W mixture.\cite{hartwig2026patent}

Theory already connects ethylene and reactive-chain concentrations. Guironnet and Peters developed a chain-population model and described its numerical extension to changing ethylene concentration; their displayed solutions use fixed ethylene concentration. Chen, Sadow, and Peters' final 2024 model resolves finite isomerization kinetics and predicts saturation and possible ethylene inhibition. It concerns a different, molecular catalyst system with initially unsaturated chains, omits deactivation and ethylene gradients, and restricts physical applicability to relatively slow isomerization. The quasi-equilibrium limit is a numerical check, not its general physical assumption. The model does not predict that lowering ethylene improves aged Na/W.\cite{guironnet2020,chen2024}

Pressure reduction and catalyst-supply effects were investigated experimentally in Wang's earlier tandem system. Its pressure comparisons also changed other conditions, and its materials differ from Na/W.\cite[ch.~4]{wang2022thesis} Thus neither a pressure optimum nor a population-balance model alone is original. The remaining question is whether a measured change in operating response with catalyst history can replace a consequential amount of fresh material, and whether that substitution can be predicted. Its importance is reducing catalyst preparation, solids handling, and waste while preserving actual recovery of polymer carbon. No economic superiority is established by three successful batches.

\subsection{Define the decision using carbon origin and complete inventories}

Gross propylene production is an inadequate recycling denominator because ethylene contributes carbon to the products. The source's labeled-polymer experiment shows polymer participation under its tested fresh-batch conditions; it is not a universal carbon allocation for every reused inventory and pressure.\cite[Fig.~4A]{conk2024} Define $Q_{\mathrm{PE}}$ as moles of carbon originating in supplied PE and recovered in the specified accepted product over an entire sequence. For a propylene objective, count polymer-origin carbon in propylene; quantify higher olefins separately rather than silently treating them as equivalent product. Define
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{Na}}=\frac{M_{\mathrm{Na/alumina,fresh}}}{Q_{\mathrm{PE}}},\qquad
 D_{\mathrm{W}}=\frac{M_{\mathrm{W/silica,fresh}}}{Q_{\mathrm{PE}}},\qquad
 J_{\mathrm{PE}}=\frac{Q_{\mathrm{PE}}}{T_{\mathrm{complete}}}.
 \label{eq:polymer-demand}
\end{equation}
These are catalyst-solid demands, not elemental Na or W demands. Report elemental inventories and retained/discarded solids separately. Complete time includes heating, reaction, cooling, gas handling, unloading or recharge, and any regeneration required by the policy.

For decisive carbon attribution, use representative sequences with enriched ethylene at the \emph{same enrichment throughout conditioning and every charge}, paired with unlabeled reference sequences. This avoids assuming that a small labeled-PE fraction represents all chain populations. Replace the source's methane internal standard with He, measured by calibrated GC with a thermal-conductivity detector and Ar carrier gas. The source's flame-ionization detector cannot measure He; retain separately calibrated hydrocarbon detection, with synchronized sampling and tracer recovery checked against known gas flows. Qualify separation from hydrogen, response in the actual gas matrix, and analytical access before decisive runs, then requalify reference behavior after the tracer change. Quantify any initial catalyst carbon and other carbon inputs. If appreciable unclassified carbon remains, two-source attribution is invalid.

Where PE and ethylene are the only contributing carbon reservoirs and isotope discrimination is bounded, the polymer-origin fraction in product $j$ is
\begin{equation}
 f_{\mathrm{PE},j}=\frac{x_E-x_j}{x_E-x_{\mathrm{PE}}},\qquad
 Q_{\mathrm{PE}}=\sum_j N_{C,j}\,n_j\,f_{\mathrm{PE},j},
 \label{eq:polymer-isotope}
\end{equation}
where $x$ denotes \emph{carbon atom fraction} of $^{13}$C, $N_{C,j}$ the number of carbon atoms per product molecule, and the sum covers accepted products collected over the sequence. Derive $x_j$ from calibrated isotopologue distributions with natural-abundance and fragmentation corrections. The published singly labeled propylene abundance is not itself a carbon atom fraction. Validate isotopic response and bound fractionation or unequal enrichment within a source before using Eq.~\ref{eq:polymer-isotope}; report propagated uncertainty rather than forcing fractions into the interval zero to one.

Collect gas, condensates, soluble and insoluble residue, and catalyst-bound carbon. Consistent ethylene enrichment classifies retained carbon from earlier charges by its original source, but does not identify which PE charge produced a later molecule. Use cumulative sequence output for the main decision. Account for beginning and ending retained inventories; count collected products once and do not also credit intermediate residue disappearance as recovered product. Charge-resolved kinetics remain useful, with carryover stated explicitly. A polymer-free blank bounds background reactions but does not reproduce a polymer-conditioned catalyst.

\subsection{Experiment 1: reproduce reuse and bracket the fresh-Na requirement}

Use one characterized clean HDPE lot and reproduce the catalyst preparation, one fresh time course, and the three-charge unsupplemented and once-supplemented sequences. The original sealed procedure charges ethylene and methane before heating; its nominal 15\,bar ethylene is not a controlled 15\,bar hot partial pressure. Record fill temperature, gas quantities, pressure convention, actual hot pressure, and temperature history. The separate-component figure also lists 10\,bar where the associated text and supporting procedure specify 15\,bar; use the detailed method and retain the discrepancy in the replication record.\cite{conk2024,conk2024si}

The finite dose bracket is Table~\ref{tab:polymer-makeup}. The 0.2\,g addition is an analyst-selected midpoint. All reused arms add fresh Na material before charge 2 only; none receives another addition before charge 3. Retain the entire relevant mixture without washing or selective catalyst separation. Use independent sequence replicates, blocked by preparation and run order. The all-fresh arm consists of three fresh catalyst charges and supplies the output reference for material accounting.

If $f=Q_{\mathrm{PE,reuse}}/Q_{\mathrm{PE,allfresh}}$, the published once-supplemented policy gives a total-solid productivity ratio $2f$ relative to all-fresh operation. The midpoint policy gives $2.4f$. At equal output, the midpoint would consume 25\% less fresh Na/alumina and 16.7\% less total catalyst solid than the full supplement. These are conditional arithmetic results, not measured savings. Three doses bracket demand; they do not locate a precise optimum.

The reported restart cools and dismantles the reactor under inert conditions. Include fresh catalyst subjected to a polymer-free thermal/handling cycle before its first PE charge, to determine whether interrupted operation alone changes function. It is a handling/conditioning control, not an exact reproduction of a spent polymer environment. Record solid recovery, retained carbon, exposure duration, and transfer losses. If existing hardware permits closed inert recharging, compare it only after validating delivered PE mass, temperature history, and mixing. Do not presume that avoiding opening changes nothing else.

One added-support control, using the same dried support and the full supplement's solid mass, tests whether increased solids/contact explains part of rescue. It cannot duplicate Na/alumina surface chemistry or rheology. Measure a limited agitation/particle-size sensitivity where the existing apparatus permits it; a stirring plateau does not exclude pore access limitations. If handling dominates the loss, develop a useful handling protocol at that scope rather than calling the decline intrinsic turnover-driven deactivation.

Before decisive runs, fix accepted cumulative polymer-carbon output, allowed product distribution, complete-time ceiling, and the smallest material saving worth detecting. Set balance tolerances and independent replication from pilot uncertainty so that the desired saving is resolvable. Report each charge's output as well as the cumulative value, preventing a policy from concealing a stalled final charge behind early production.

\subsection{Experiment 2: one lower ethylene condition in the same reactor mode}

The pressure intervention requires a semibatch reactor with continuous gas flow and product removal. The published reuse benchmark is sealed batch, so first establish fresh/retained differences and the direction of Na rescue in the semibatch mode actually used. If that mode does not show a consequential reuse deficit, close the pressure-as-rescue branch; changing reactor mode has already changed the question.

Choose one lower ethylene partial pressure against a qualified reference, using inert replacement at fixed total pressure, inlet molar flow, temperature, PE charge, catalyst inventory, and agitation. As a concrete initial contrast, halve the reference inlet ethylene fraction while retaining a constant carbon-free tracer fraction and replacing the balance with Ar. This is a proposed diagnostic amplitude, to be accepted only within the qualified gas-delivery range. Measure reactor/outlet composition and actual molar flow rather than assigning reactor ethylene activity from the cylinder blend. Document absolute versus gauge pressure. The source semibatch procedure's 19\,bar gauge total pressure is not interchangeable with its sealed-batch ethylene fill pressure.\cite{conk2024si}

Use complete-charge comparisons on independently conditioned catalyst inventories. Match all pre-intervention history through charge 1 at the reference condition, then randomize retained inventories across both pressures and the existing 0, 0.2, and 0.4\,g Na/alumina doses: six independently replicated conditions. Each addition occurs once, before charge 2; none occurs before charge 3. Test the nonzero doses regardless of the zero-dose result, because pressure and makeup can interact. Include fresh mixtures at both conditions to establish whether the response is specific to retention. This is an operating intervention on a changing polymer/catalyst mixture, not an isolated intrinsic reaction order. The source itself improved semibatch output while increasing both pressure and flow; that result cannot establish a pressure order, but it is substantial evidence against assuming that less ethylene must help.\cite{conk2024}

Do not begin with rapid pressure waveforms. The published apparatus samples every 12 minutes, and gas holdup plus dissolved-product release may obscure shorter responses.\cite{conk2024si} A reference--lower--reference transient is useful only if measured gas and product response times are short compared with chain evolution and catalyst change. Otherwise, complete-charge output gives the defensible comparison. A temporary propylene spike caused by release of stored product is not restored catalytic production.

A useful reduced-dose policy must satisfy polymer-carbon attribution, full product collection, product specification, and the fixed complete-time ceiling. Compare each reduced dose at both pressures against the full-dose reference-pressure policy, reporting pressure contrasts within each dose with uncertainty. A zero-dose null closes only pressure rescue without makeup; the broader tested saving claim requires informative results at the reduced nonzero dose as well. Test fixed lower ethylene from the start of all three charges if the fresh comparison supports it; a simple fixed condition is the preferred operating rule when it performs as well as a history-dependent switch. Count inert supply, separation, and circulation. Lower ethylene partial pressure at unchanged total pressure does not demonstrate lower compression demand or lower stoichiometric ethylene consumption.

The adverse outcome is equally plausible. Ethylene participates in metathesis, may accept hydrogen during initiation, and changes swelling and gas/polymer transport. Lowering it can slow one function more than it relieves another, change selectivity, or reduce contact. Close the tested partial-substitution branch only if the crossed comparisons exclude a consequential saving that meets the output, specification, and time requirements with informative uncertainty. This conclusion covers the measured doses and two pressures, not every unmeasured intermediate dose. It does not identify which catalyst function decayed, and it does not justify adding a pressure library until an apparent improvement appears.

\subsection{Chain-state evidence and the limit of a selective-initiation claim}

Track product trajectories together with full molecular-weight distributions, recovered fractions, and unsaturation where measurable. Endpoint olefin content reflects formation and consumption simultaneously; low unsaturation does not prove slow initiation. Nor can a bulk C=C fraction establish how many chains contain reactive sites. Heating alone changes molecular weight in the source's component experiments, further limiting mechanistic assignment from a mean molecular weight.\cite{conk2024}

Qualify the quench before treating recovered chains as the reacting state. Wang's dissertation reports that depressurization and two to three hours of cooling permitted isomerization and fragment recombination in its tandem system.\cite[ch.~4, pp.~72--74]{wang2022thesis} On Na/W, compare the fastest technically validated recovery with a deliberately delayed recovery at the same conversion window, accounting for all fractions. If unsaturation or chain distributions depend materially on workup, report them as post-recovery descriptors and close direct hot-state inference. Solvent-soluble material alone cannot represent carbon retained on inaccessible solids.

If the pressure intervention gives a real saving, select one explanation for a further discriminating test. A matched unsaturated PE and hydrogenated counterpart can test whether feed state changes the pressure benefit, provided chain-length/branching distributions, workup residues, and metal contamination are controlled. The prediction must come from the measured Na/W response, not from transferring the molecular Ru model's optimum. This is a conditional mechanistic extension, not a second mandatory first campaign. Different unsaturation also changes the reacting chain distribution and contact, so feed rescue alone does not isolate initiation.

A component-specific claim requires operationally selective probes whose specificity is demonstrated on the separate catalysts and supports, and whose access and conditioning effects are assessed. Small olefins may reach sites inaccessible to polymer or activate the material being measured. If those constraints cannot be met, retain an empirical relation among history, pressure, and fresh-material demand. The useful operating result does not require claiming that initiation alone decays while downstream functions remain intact.

\subsection{Experiment 3: predict a catalyst-use decision, then test whether it persists}

Use the crossed three-charge dose brackets and complete time courses to construct the simplest predictor of accepted output at both pressure conditions, retaining any pressure--dose interaction. Start with direct interpolation over the measured makeup range and measured uncertainty; do not fit separate unobserved initiation and metathesis capacities to the same product trace. If output is not sufficiently regular with dose to support interpolation, select among tested policies rather than claim an unmeasured optimum.

Withhold one intermediate makeup dose from calibration. Choose it prospectively only if the predictor says it can meet the accepted output and time criteria with a resolvable saving; then validate it at \emph{both} pressures on independent three-charge sequences with matched charge-1 histories and the dose supplied before charge 2 only. Freeze both predictions before collecting either result. Compare against the best tested fixed-pressure policy and the published full-supplement schedule in the same reactor mode. Successful validation means that the lower confidence bound on accepted output meets the predeclared requirement, product specification and time are met, and fresh-material demand is lower after attribution uncertainty. To claim that pressure enables this dose saving, require the lower-pressure policy to pass and the same dose at reference pressure to fail an acceptance criterion with informative uncertainty. If both pass, report an adequate lower dose and any separately resolved pressure benefit, without claiming pressure was necessary for the material saving. An unresolved reference result leaves that necessity unestablished. No minimum over unmeasured doses is identified. A failed prediction remains a failure; do not refit it and call it validation.

This first result establishes a bounded inventory saving. To test whether it merely defers catalyst replacement, extend the selected policy and comparator through further 1\,g PE charges. Before the extension, freeze a per-charge intervention trigger based on accepted output, quality, and the charge-time ceiling, a common cumulative accepted polymer-carbon target, and maximum complete-time and charge-count bounds. At the trigger, collect products and remove the entire retained catalyst/residue mixture under the qualified handling protocol; quantify its solids and unprocessed carbon. Replace it with 0.4\,g fresh Na/alumina and 0.4\,g fresh W/silica, then restart the assigned validated pressure schedule and once-only makeup before the new mixture's second charge. Preserve the common reference-condition first charge for policies validated with that history. Do not attempt selective W replacement or unqualified regeneration.

Continue until the common output target is reached or either maximum bound is reached. Charge all fresh solids and complete time, including failed charges, cooling, removal, recharge, reheating, collection, and final inventory closure. Withdrawn and final residues remain in the carbon and waste accounts; unprocessed carbon is not recovered product. Use the common target as the credited output for the target comparison and report any collected excess separately. A policy that fails to reach the target receives no extrapolated credit. This tests sustained demand only over the bounded extension with the specified full-mixture replacement rule; any shorter service interval must pay its measured replacement cost.

The scientific payoff grows when a measured chain or catalyst-state descriptor predicts a new history better than charge count alone and thereby changes a real makeup decision. Without that link, a reproducible fixed-pressure saving remains a useful bounded operating improvement. If no qualified intervention reduces demand at accepted output and time, close this proposal's improvement claim. Neither a negative result nor successful fresh-Na rescue establishes a unique molecular cause of reuse loss.
