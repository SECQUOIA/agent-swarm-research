\section{Cyclic oxides: steam compatibility and recovery of selective oxygen delivery}
\label{sec:cyclic}

\textbf{Summary.} In chemical-looping ethane oxidative dehydrogenation, a cycled oxide acts as an oxygen carrier: it supplies oxygen to ethane and is then reoxidized. Gao and colleagues showed that a Li\textsubscript{2}CO\textsubscript{3} coating makes La\textsubscript{0.8}Sr\textsubscript{0.2}FeO\textsubscript{3} (LSF) a selective carrier.\cite{gao2020} Brody and colleagues cycled coated LSF for more than 1200 elapsed hours; in the first 1000\,h, run with Ar purges, average C\textsubscript{2+} carbon yield (ethylene plus C\textsubscript{3+}) fell from 53.2\% over hours 0--300 to 47.24\% over hours 600--1000. Their process model replaces the Ar purges with steam, which the study did not test on the coating.\cite{brody2022} Carbonate/hydroxide chemistry and \COtwo{} regeneration have close precedents (Barckholtz,\cite{barckholtz2021} Chacko,\cite[ch.~4.1]{chacko2025thesis} Fereres\cite{fereres2018}); another demonstration that \COtwo{} stabilizes carbonate would not suffice, so the question is narrow: does steam in the post-oxidation purge change ethylene output from this nominal 10\,wt\% coating, can a loss be recovered, and does a remedy repay its time and gas? We propose first to compare ordinary cycling with steam-rich and Ar post-oxidation purges; if a difference warrants it, a diagnostic compares \COtwo{} given with steam against \COtwo{} given afterward, and a policy comparison counts complete-cycle output. Success would show whether the substitution is compatible, recoverable or worth managing, and whether a measured state predicts recovery. As an illustration only, fully restoring Brody's Ar-aged C\textsubscript{2+} yield would repay at most about 2.02 extra minutes per 16-minute cycle, before gas and heat are charged (Eq.~\ref{eq:cyclic-threshold}).

\subsection{Prior work and the open question}

The ideal selective reduction of the carrier is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{C_2H_6+[O]_{carrier}\longrightarrow C_2H_4+H_2O};
 \label{eq:cyclic-odh}
\end{equation}
the challenge is to deliver enough oxygen for useful conversion without oxidizing hydrocarbons to CO or \COtwo{}. Gao and colleagues attributed the coated carrier's selectivity to a molten layer that blocks nonselective sites and transports peroxide species associated with Fe\textsuperscript{4+}/Fe\textsuperscript{3+} redox, and report about 0.42\,wt\% oxygen consumption over the first six minutes at 700\,$^\circ$C. The supported coating reportedly melts below bulk Li\textsubscript{2}CO\textsubscript{3}, so hydroxide formation need not be what first makes it liquid.\cite{gao2020}

Brody and colleagues cycled 300\,g of coated LSF. For the first 1000\,h at 700\,$^\circ$C, each 16-minute cycle was 1\,min ethane, 5\,min Ar, 5\,min oxidation and 5\,min Ar. C\textsubscript{2+} carbon yield averaged 53.2\% over hours 0--300 and 47.24\% over hours 600--1000; later temperature and flow changes improved performance, so the later optimum was not the condition used throughout the test. The methods give 10\,wt\% carbonate but the conclusion 20\,wt\%, so loading must be measured. The observed exothermic half-cycles do not show a continuously producing, thermally self-sufficient plant.\cite{brody2022}

Barckholtz and colleagues observed carbonate/hydroxide responses to steam and \COtwo{} in a mixed Li/Na melt, though their Raman signals gave no absolute concentrations.\cite{barckholtz2021} Chacko cycled LSF with 45\,wt\% mixed carbonate at 800\,$^\circ$C and \COtwo{}/O\textsubscript{2} regeneration, a close precedent for coupled coating/carrier regeneration despite the different formulation and temperature: carbonate decomposed during inert purges, and H\textsubscript{2} evolved during regeneration, attributed to reduced iron reacting with water released by hydroxide carbonation.\cite[ch.~4.1]{chacko2025thesis} When Fereres and colleagues switched a bulk Li/Na/K carbonate melt from air to \COtwo{} after 30 days at 640\,$^\circ$C, further mass loss stopped but the initial state was not restored,\cite{fereres2018} a precedent for prevention versus incomplete delayed recovery.

\subsection{Chemical hypothesis and competing explanations}

A candidate component equilibrium is
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{Li_2CO_3+H_2O\rightleftharpoons 2LiOH+CO_2},\qquad
 \frac{a_{\mathrm{LiOH}}^2}{a_{\mathrm{Li_2CO_3}}}
 =K(T)\frac{f_{\mathrm{H_2O}}}{f_{\mathrm{CO_2}}},
 \label{eq:cyclic-hydrolysis}
\end{equation}
where $a$ is condensed-phase activity and $f$ fugacity (approximated by partial pressure in the atmospheric-pressure diagnostic). At fixed temperature a lower steam/\COtwo{} ratio favors carbonate over hydroxide, but this direction gives no composition, equilibration time or Li-loss rate for supported LSF; nonideal melt chemistry, substrate interactions and changing coverage preclude importing a fuel-cell equilibrium constant uncritically. Barckholtz held each condition for hours, so those data do not show a response within a five-minute purge.\cite{barckholtz2021}

Steam could convert the retained salt reversibly, redistribute it away from important interfaces, move Li onto reactor materials or into transported material, or change the carrier's redox state or phase, singly or in sequence. Downstream Li does not identify LiOH vapor, unchanged total Li does not show unchanged coverage, and a smaller carbonate surface signal does not quantify Li loss.

The balanced reverse reaction,
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{2LiOH+CO_2\longrightarrow Li_2CO_3+H_2O},
 \label{eq:cyclic-carbonation}
\end{equation}
releases water even with a dry inlet, and that water can oxidize the carrier\cite[ch.~4.1]{chacko2025thesis} (Chacko's printed reaction omits the factor of two before the hydroxide). Equal fed \COtwo{} or equal mean Fe valence therefore cannot show equivalent coating--carrier interfaces, so the timing comparison measures the total effect of treatment order, interpreted with inventories and intermediate states.

\textbf{Ordinary-cycle comparison.} Prepare one reproducible coating, measure its initial Li content, phases and loading (nominal 10\,wt\% is only the starting recipe), and establish stable short-cycle function under dry Ar. Then run Brody's schedule at 700\,$^\circ$C and atmospheric pressure with the post-oxidation purge either steam-rich or Ar, without supplementary \COtwo{}; the reducing post-ethane purge stays dry Ar throughout, and no result addresses steam there. Set the steam fraction in Ar, flow and temperature trajectory from equipment validation and the intended heat-exchange duty; fix this composition, the initial conditioning and the averaging windows in advance, holding other cycle conditions fixed. Report the steam-minus-Ar difference in cycle-resolved and cumulative ethylene on equal initial mass and feed bases, including a terminal window \emph{before any reset} or extra dry conditioning; it includes any carryover or reversible inhibition, so it is operating performance, not proof of permanent damage. Then apply the common reset (a fixed dry \COtwo{}/O\textsubscript{2} carbonation--oxidation step; see below) once to each history and compare a common block of dry cycles with the matched Ar history: loss followed by recovery supports recoverability under that treatment, not tolerance or a unique repair mechanism. The pre-reset blocks are the untreated comparators for Experiment~2.

\textbf{Timing diagnostic.} If a functional or material difference warrants it, run the arms in Table~\ref{tab:cyclic-timing} on separate specimens at 700\,$^\circ$C and atmospheric pressure. The 10\,mol\% steam and 5\,mol\% \COtwo{} are starting settings, not an optimum or a stand-in for nearly pure steam. Hold total inlet flow, temperature, pressure, specimen mass and elapsed exposure fixed, and match the \emph{integrated inlet} \COtwo{} dose within each timing pair, with switching holdup calibrated in an empty reactor using the actual water and \COtwo{} signals, reporting background \COtwo{}; uptake and release are outcomes.

The dry arms test whether early \COtwo{} protects against a dry exposure regardless of steam. After the common reset, let $q$ be each arm's mean ethylene per cycle and initial catalyst mass over a fixed block, and define
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{steam}}=(q_{\mathrm{wet,P}}-q_{\mathrm{wet,R}})
 -(q_{\mathrm{dry,early}}-q_{\mathrm{dry,late}}).
 \label{eq:cyclic-interaction}
\end{equation}
A positive wet P--R difference shows a timing benefit under steam; a positive $D_{\mathrm{steam}}$ adds evidence that steam changes it. Neither proves hydrolysis or Li volatilization. Also report $q_{\mathrm{wet,P}}-q_{\mathrm{dry,early}}$ and $q_{\mathrm{wet,R}}-q_{\mathrm{dry,late}}$ (steam substitution within each \COtwo{} schedule), and each arm's absolute yield and its difference from Ar, since both wet arms could be equally damaged. A null result here concerns function after dilute steam, this \COtwo{} history and the reset, not untreated compatibility at the selected duty.

Use independently run sister specimens stopped after the first segment, the full exposure, the reset and washout, recording elapsed time and gas history. First-segment samples can locate changes present during steam; final specimens alone cannot separate steam damage from changes in the later Ar/\COtwo{} segment.

\subsection{Common reset, function assay, and inventories}

\textbf{Common reset.} One terminal carbonation/oxidation sequence serves the recovery comparison and all timing arms. In a pilot, choose dry-inlet \COtwo{}/O\textsubscript{2} conditions and a duration long enough for the slowest specimen to reach stable net uptake and a reproducible carbonate-sensitive spectrum, then fix it; show on this formulation that the joint endpoint limits new decarbonation while the carrier reoxidizes. Measure \COtwo{}, O\textsubscript{2}, \HtwoO{}, CO and H\textsubscript{2} throughout. The aim is complete treatment of the remaining carbonatable fraction (not equal carbonate if Li was lost), confirmed by the species/state check because an uptake plateau may reflect slow transport or passivation. If no shared, process-compatible reset reaches interpretable endpoints, report the recovery limit without claiming irreversibility; any further reset is applied comparably and charged to the recovery policy.

\textbf{Washout and function assay.} Before ethane, verify \COtwo{} and O\textsubscript{2} washout against thresholds that exclude an important change in the first pulse: Gao reports \COtwo{} inhibition on coated LSF in Fig.~4D (the reversible switch in Fig.~4E is a separate molten-Li\textsubscript{2}CO\textsubscript{3}/ethane/O\textsubscript{2} experiment),\cite{gao2020} and residual O\textsubscript{2} can change output independently of carrier oxygen. Apply the longest washout needed by any arm to all arms and check whether it alters the contrast; an effect erased by washout is not persistent protection. Then run a pilot-chosen block of dry-Ar-purged ethane/oxidation cycles long enough to separate gas carryover from retained function.

\textbf{Carbon and water accounting.} Directly measured ethylene is the primary outcome, not the source's C\textsubscript{2+}; also measure conversion, CO\textsubscript{x}, other hydrocarbons and retained carbon, with a thermal blank. Include carbonate capture, release and inventory changes in the carbon balance before assigning CO\textsubscript{x} to hydrocarbons; regeneration \COtwo{} alone is not a coke assay. If carbonate and coke cannot be resolved, report total CO\textsubscript{x} and the attribution limit; the regeneration precedent faces the same accounting problem.\cite[ch.~4.1]{chacko2025thesis} No isotope campaign is needed for the ethylene-output decision. Measure water and H\textsubscript{2} directly, since carbonate/hydroxide and carrier-oxygen changes make water alone an ambiguous lattice-oxygen reporter.

\textbf{Lithium and spatial state.} Close the Li inventory over particles, grit, reactor walls, particulates and downstream condensates by complete digestion with blanks and recovery spikes for each matrix, validating sampling and extraction recovery before aging and recording each segment's capture (earlier deposits may be released later); ordinary EDX cannot measure Li coverage. If redistribution matters, cross-sectional NanoSIMS correlated with electron microscopy could map Li-rich patches and rim-to-interior gradients, if its resolution is shown to be finer than those features and matrix-dependent ion yields, sectioning artifacts and beam-induced redistribution are controlled; intensities do not give absolute coverage, and unresolved films are outside the claim. For carrier state, use phase characterization and, if access and validation allow, \textsuperscript{57}Fe M\"ossbauer spectra on state-preserved samples, following Gao;\cite{gao2020} these can show Fe-state or phase changes if reference materials and replicates resolve them above fitting uncertainty; bulk averaging can hide interfacial changes. Carbonate-sensitive vibrational spectra add a chemical indicator. Without access or validation, drop the corresponding NanoSIMS or M\"ossbauer mechanism claim.

\textbf{Required resolution.} On an assumed final-catalyst basis, 1\,g with 10\,wt\% Li\textsubscript{2}CO\textsubscript{3} holds about 18.8\,mg Li, so a 0.1\% change (18.8\,$\mu$g) is below a 1\% bulk-inventory error. Five minutes of 5\% \COtwo{} at 100 standard cm\textsuperscript{3}/min (0\,$^\circ$C convention) supplies about 1.12\,mmol, while converting 0.1\% of the carbonate involves 1.35\,$\mu$mol, about 61\,ppm of the gas dose against a 50\,000\,ppm feed. These are resolution examples, not predicted losses; captures, absolute assays and chemical signatures must complement gas-signal subtraction. If one exposure leaves a change unresolved, repeat the timing history every cycle (with real ethane and ordinary reoxidation steps) over a fixed block and reset once at the end, fixing and reporting this cadence before assigning any loss rate; this ten-minute diagnostic exposure is not the five-minute ordinary purge duty. Raising temperature or extending steam until damage appears would test a different condition.

\subsection{Experiment 2: a usable management policy and its complete-cycle value}

An important ordinary-cycle penalty, recovery benefit or timing contrast justifies comparing four policies at the selected steam-rich duty: concurrent protection, delayed recovery with a demonstrated process-compatible treatment, untreated steam, and dry Ar (the last two from Experiment~1 if conditions and blocks match, otherwise matched controls). The post-ethane purge stays Ar. Fix steam dose, \COtwo{} circulation limit, total flow, temperature trajectory and cadence in advance; unavoidable changes in steam delivery belong to the policy. The diagnostic ratio does not transfer: steam/\COtwo{} $=2$ in a binary purge needs one-third \COtwo{}, which, with the source's five-minute purge and one-minute 81.6\% ethane feed and assuming equal molar flow in the purge and ethane steps, circulates about 2.04\,mol \COtwo{} per mol ethane fed (a composition calculation, not a required dose). Keeping the steam dose instead raises total gas flow by 50\% and needs a new heat and hydrodynamic comparison. If Li export is important and recovery fails, test one measured Li replenishment, counting its interruption, heating, washout and recovery; it may also change coverage, salt composition and carrier structure, and the original synthesis does not imply an online method.

Let $q_i$ be the product per cycle averaged over the whole block including aging, $\tau$ the base cycle time and $h_i$ the average added treatment time. For equal catalyst mass and feed pulses,
\begin{equation}
 J_i=\frac{q_i}{\tau+h_i},\qquad
 J_P>J_R\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 h_P<\frac{q_P}{q_R}(\tau+h_R)-\tau.
 \label{eq:cyclic-value}
\end{equation}
Taking $\tau=16$\,min, the unrecovered Ar-aged state as a comparator with no added time, and the published C\textsubscript{2+} yield ratio as a stand-in for the per-cycle product ratio, fully restoring the Ar-aged yield from 47.24\% to 53.2\% permits at most
\begin{equation}
 16\left(\frac{0.532}{0.4724}-1\right)=2.02\ \text{additional minutes per cycle}
 \label{eq:cyclic-threshold}
\end{equation}
before gas and heat are charged. A five-minute step every cycle would need 31.25\% more output; complete recovery gives 12.62\%. This uses the documented Ar loss, not a predicted steam loss or full-life benefit.\cite{brody2022} Report ethylene and C\textsubscript{2+} productivity with fresh and circulating \COtwo{}/steam, Li handling and heat per product (recycling can reduce fresh \COtwo{} but leaves circulation and separation burdens); a smaller Li signal alone does not show process value.

\subsection{Experiment 3: predicting recoverability under a new operating history}

The goal is a measured state that predicts persistent function under the recovery policy. If Li export is the leading explanation, calibrate per-cycle Li transfer under continuous protected and continuous delayed operation and relate retained Li to later ethylene output. If equal retained Li gives different function, the inventory-only model fails; test an independently measured coating-distribution or carrier-phase indicator rather than fitting a loss constant per history. Withhold one alternating protection/recovery schedule with fixed period and cadence, and predict cumulative Li transfer, cycle-resolved ethylene output, and function after its single terminal reset, which is not inserted between cycles unless it is part of the policy and charged to it. Start with an additive per-cycle loss model; add a measured state only if it explains and predicts a failure under alternation on a further block. The model must beat a carrier-state-only model and a cycle-count aging description by enough to change a recovery or purge decision. Success shows predictive use over the tested range, not that Li inventory causally dominates correlated changes.

\subsection{Outcomes and decisions}

The program becomes substantial if the selected steam duty changes useful function and a measured state predicts recovery.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No important steam-minus-Ar loss:} supports compatibility without supplementary \COtwo{} at the tested duty; no protection claim is needed there.
\item \emph{Loss before the reset, recovery after it:} supports recoverability under that treatment; Experiment~2 decides whether its cost is repaid.
\item \emph{No timing benefit:} drop the persistent concurrent-over-delayed claim only when the interval for the direct contrast $q_{\mathrm{wet,P}}-q_{\mathrm{wet,R}}$ excludes an important advantage; the interval for $D_{\mathrm{steam}}$ assesses whether steam modifies it. Neither shows \COtwo{} treatment is unnecessary; single-arm recovery bounds are separate conclusions.
\item \emph{Protection works but does not repay its complete-cycle cost against untreated steam and Ar:} keep the finding, reject the intervention.
\end{itemize}
Steam cannot explain Brody's Ar-aged decline; explaining it needs representative Ar-aged material and a separate recovery study, which must not silently replace a failed steam hypothesis.
