## Accounting and arithmetic

1. Three all-fresh charges require 3 × (0.4 + 0.4) = 2.4 g catalyst solids. Retained material without makeup requires 0.8 g; one 0.2 g Na/alumina addition gives 1.0 g total; the published once-only 0.4 g addition gives 1.2 g. All reused arms retain 0.4 g initial W/silica and receive no W makeup in the three-charge comparison.
2. For relative accepted polymer-carbon output f versus three all-fresh charges, the published policy's total-solid productivity ratio is 2f. The illustrative midpoint gives 2.4f. At equal accepted output, 0.6/0.8 g fresh Na/alumina represents 25% less Na material; 1.0/1.2 g total solids represents 16.7% less total fresh catalyst. These are arithmetic conditions, not measured savings, elemental Na reductions, or proven long-term makeup rates.
3. Two-source carbon balance: product atom fraction xj = fPE xPE + (1 − fPE) xE, hence fPE = (xE − xj)/(xE − xPE). Product carbon amount is the molecular amount times its carbon number times fPE. This assumes two classified contributing carbon reservoirs and bounded isotope discrimination with representative effective source enrichments. It does not assume a fixed one-third/two-thirds product-carbon split.
4. Enriched ethylene must be used at constant enrichment throughout preparation of the relevant catalyst history and every charge. Changing enrichment after conditioning would leave a distinct retained cofeed-carbon reservoir. Methane internal standard and pre-existing catalyst carbon also invalidate an unqualified two-source analysis. The decisive attribution therefore uses He with calibrated GC-TCD detection and Ar carrier gas, separately calibrated hydrocarbon detection, synchronized sampling, and tracer recovery checked against known gas flows. The source FID cannot detect He. Separation from hydrogen, actual-mixture response, analytical access, and reference requalification are required; other carbon inputs remain measured/bounded. Full isotopologue calibration is required; the source's single-label fragment method is not automatically sufficient.
5. Cumulative accepted output is credited only when collected, not both as residue disappearance and later product. Intermediate inventories support closure and carryover interpretation. Carbon attributed to PE does not automatically identify which PE charge supplied it. Solid demand is normalized to accepted polymer-origin output and includes support mass; complete time includes interruption and restoration operations.

## Design development beyond the existing briefs

- Defined explicit catalyst-solid demands and complete-time productivity, with a separate three-charge inventory table and the correct strongest published makeup comparator.
- Selected pressure as the sole first operating intervention. Required a semibatch-specific reuse and Na-rescue bridge, rather than treating sealed and flowing reactor modes as identical.
- Defined one concrete relative perturbation: halve inlet ethylene fraction by inert replacement, retain constant carbon-free tracer, and hold total pressure/flow/temperature/agitation fixed. The amplitude remains a design choice within a qualified operating range. Complete-charge comparison is preferred to unresolved rapid transients.
- Crossed the existing finite 0/0.2/0.4 g once-only dose bracket with the two pressures in the qualified semibatch mode: six independently replicated conditions, with a common reference charge 1 and randomized assignment for charges 2–3. Nonzero doses are tested regardless of the zero-dose outcome because pressure and makeup can interact. Fresh samples at both conditions distinguish general improvement from an aging-dependent response. Fixed lower pressure for all charges is tested only if the fresh response warrants it.
- Made the isotope method's source history, atom-fraction definition, methane removal/requalification, retained-carbon accounting, and fractionation assumptions explicit.
- Kept a polymer-free thermal/handling control separate from an exact spent-state control; added-support control constrains solids effects without pretending to reproduce Na chemistry.
- Defined a withheld intermediate dose from the crossed brackets, preserving pressure–dose interaction. Freeze predictions at both pressures and validate the dose at both on independent sequences with matched histories and once-only addition. A pressure-enabled dose-saving claim requires the lower-pressure policy to pass while the same reference-pressure dose informatively fails an acceptance criterion. If both pass, the result is an adequate lower dose plus any independently resolved pressure benefit, without claiming pressure was necessary. An unresolved reference result does not establish necessity; no minimum over unmeasured doses is inferred.
- Made the extension executable: predeclare per-charge output/quality/time triggers, a common cumulative accepted polymer-carbon target, and maximum complete-time and charge-count bounds. At the trigger, collect products, remove and account for the full catalyst/residue mixture, add 0.4 g each fresh Na/alumina and W/silica, and restart the validated pressure schedule and once-only second-charge makeup. Preserve the common first-charge history where used in validation. Count failed charges, all replacement solids, handling, reheating, collection, final closure, and all withdrawn/final residues. Use the common target as credited output and report excess separately; an arm that fails to reach it receives no extrapolated credit. Sustained demand is claimed only for this bounded full-mixture replacement policy.
- Kept matched-unsaturated PE and component probes conditional on a successful operational distinction. Neither is a parallel mandatory campaign or a direct assay proving selective initiation loss.

## Remaining experimental dependencies and closure

Actual reactor access, fresh/reused behavior in the chosen mode, quantitative solid recovery, product collection, isotope response, carbon closure, quench fidelity, and a resolvable operating saving require experiments. A zero-dose null closes only pressure rescue without makeup. Closure of the tested partial-substitution claim requires informative crossed reduced-dose results excluding a consequential saving at accepted output, quality, and complete time. This closure covers measured doses and the two pressures, not every unmeasured intermediate dose. An unresolved source balance prevents a polymer-carbon saving claim. Failure of a selective-function interpretation does not invalidate a measured operating improvement, but successful fresh-Na rescue alone cannot supply that interpretation.

[Agilent's detector documentation](https://openlab.help.agilent.com/2.7/en/mergedProjects/GCHelp/mergedProjects/78xxGC/TCD_ThermalConductivityDet.htm) explains TCD response to gases relative to the carrier, and its [site-preparation guidance](https://www.agilent.com/cs/library/support/documents/a15283.pdf) identifies Ar carrier for hydrogen or helium analysis. These support the proposed He/Ar-carrier GC-TCD route as a candidate, not a qualified method on this reactor or evidence of installed equipment. Actual mixture separation, sensitivity, flow closure, and product calibration remain explicit qualifications. No new source-performance number is imported.
