## Arithmetic and accounting checks

For one mole EO, assume only EO and complete combustion, with fractional ethylene selectivity S. Ethylene required is 1/S; ethylene burned is 1/S−1; oxygen is 0.5+3(1/S−1); CO2 is 2(1/S−1). Evaluating `100*(1-f(.91)/f(.90))` gives ethylene 1.0989011%, oxygen 4.3956044%, CO2 10.9890110%. These are reaction-only conditional savings, not predicted process improvements.

The historical bulk Ag/Ni atomic ratio is `(0.135/107.8682)/(88e-6/58.6934) = 834.73`; 1:800 is an explicitly illustrative first preparation target, not an optimum or surface coverage. No unsupported conversion of bulk inventory into surface atoms is used.

Equation for complete-clock productivity has units mol EO/(mass Ag × time). Its integration begins with conditioning; rejected output and downtime contribute zero, with all their consumption still charged. Product criteria apply to all credited output. Separate predeclared production and return-assessment windows carry the conversion/selectivity/work-rate constraints; bounded planned conditioning/recovery/return intervals may have low or zero output but retain every time and resource cost. Missing the return deadline or failing a required production window makes the policy inadmissible, and low output cannot be retrospectively relabeled as planned recovery. Unfiltered net EO output and all physical flows remain reported. Total catalyst mass and occupied volume are additional inventory bases.

The recovery-benefit integral is a within-material difference in accepted EO amount, including treatment and return. It does not rank materials. For each of Ni and sham, Qm,R(H) = Qm,C(H) + Bm(tR; H−a), where a is the matched intervention age and both policies have the same pre-intervention history, full clock, and inventory basis. Rank all admissible tested Ni/sham ordinary and recovery policies plus untreated ordinary operation on the same horizon, acceptance rules, resource boundary and normalization. Sham recovery beating ordinary Ni cannot establish substitution for Ni if Ni recovery is better. Validate both recovery predictions and the decisive absolute ranking on independent preparation blocks. Changing policy histories requires new validation. No periodic-return formula is used because repeated state restoration and recurrence costs are unproven.

The preparation-cost boundary is per bed campaign over H at matched initial Ag inventory, reporting actual total catalyst masses and volumes: added preparation cost ≤ vEO × additional accepted EO − sum(vk × additional resource/disposal quantities) − vL × additional operating labor-hours. Each valuation and unit must be explicit; utilities require a stated measurement or estimation basis. Initial material/processing belongs to preparation cost and is not charged twice as operating resource. Complete-clock output already charges downtime; reactor downtime does not automatically equal labor. With no defensible valuations, report the symbolic boundary and physical differences, not a numerical monetary saving.

## Design developments and resolved ambiguities

1. Choose one historically grounded Ni post-impregnation rather than assuming transfer of the colloidal single-atom-alloy state. Preserve a working promoted reference; untreated/sham/Ni materials separate processing effects from the complete Ni intervention. Fresh sham qualification cannot exclude later processing damage, so at least one independently replicated untreated policy remains in the full-horizon ordinary-aging comparison within each preparation block. A failed fresh sham permits one identified correction and otherwise ends that route. A Ni/sham retention difference without a useful advantage over untreated operation is processing-dependent, not an improvement of the starting catalyst.
2. Freeze policy arms after exploratory calibration. Independent charges share preparation blocks and initial conditioning clocks. Common-setting causal arms may serve as policy arms only when their entire history is identical. Common conditions and individually useful operating choices are not conflated.
3. Select the untreated policy from its own useful chloride response: one lower-chloride setting if admissible, otherwise a credible reference or scheduled adjustment policy fixed before aging. Ni and sham receive the same operating/tuning bounds. Keep the Ni/sham common-setting contrast distinct. Untreated operation does not require a full chloride or recovery factorial but must be explicit in absolute rankings. Flow/temperature changes pay their measured consequences. No invented upset frequency or remote crossover justifies expansion.
4. Recovery versus continuation applies to both materials under a specified common-setting aging history. Full rate trajectories and output during intervention are retained; persistence after one recovery is not absolute irreversibility.
5. The predictive follow-on uses training recovery traces and a second duration check for both Ni and sham, then locks the withheld duration, return setting, acceptance windows and predictions before independent recovery/continuation validation. Both absolute recovery outputs enter the decisive ranking alongside all admissible tested ordinary policies, including untreated operation. No result against ordinary Ni alone closes the broader useful-Ni claim. A single relaxation mode is conditional on identified timescales; it does not count a chemical species. The calibrated familiar chlorine/product baseline must be beaten before claiming additional memory chemistry.
6. EO/CO2 balances explicitly handle cofed products and ethane carbon. Structural probes, a Re factorial and isotope oxygen-return studies are optional discriminators. They cannot substitute for useful output or justify unsupported microscopic claims.

## Remaining experimental dependencies

The reference reactor and reproducible catalyst are not yet established resources. Exact chloride bracket, conditioning maximum, finite horizon, replicate number and minimum useful contrast must be set from commissioning and the operating decision; the manuscript makes no numerical precision promise. The one Ni route may fail, and a finite null cannot exclude other loadings, syntheses or longer service. A useful recovery may already be explained by known chloride kinetics; that closes the extra-mechanism claim. The program remains conditional and experimentally falsifiable, with no new measurements invented.

This explains chloride-policy relevance without asserting universal coverage, optimum, or a Ni-specific mechanism.
