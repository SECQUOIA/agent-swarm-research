# Ni-dependent selective-output retention on promoted Ag

## Recommendation and current status

**2026-09-16 source update:** the [post-upload audit](../reviews/post-upload-ag-audit.md) is incorporated below, including Jalil's preparation/characterization SI and Esposito's ethylene/propylene SI. These sources strengthen the existing baseline and the plausibility of Ni-associated fresh chemistry; they do not demonstrate useful retention on the proposed fully promoted material.

**Retain Ag as a bounded material/operation study on an existing platform.** The [current decision brief](../decision-brief.md) governs portfolio priorities; the [earlier independent portfolio audit](../reviews/portfolio-current-priority-audit.md) no longer ranks the current candidates. Its requirement for a consequential observed contrast and a successful prospective prediction before expansion remains applicable. The question is whether Ni changes useful ethylene oxide (EO) output on promoted Ag in a way that supports a better formulation or operating choice. Access to a functioning platform is unconfirmed.

[US5380885A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5380885A/en) already reports actual dilute-Ni/Cs/Re/S catalyst performance and improved selectivity retention during accelerated aging. Generic compatibility, high-pressure translation and an unspecified durability gain are therefore insufficient contributions. The [independent historical-source review](../reviews/ag-older-ni-prior-art.md) supersedes that part of the earlier positive [value](../reviews/epoxidation-value-review.md) and [portfolio](../reviews/portfolio-second-value-review.md) assessments. No new catalyst improvement has been demonstrated here.

A subsequent [operating-aging review](../reviews/ag-operating-aging-prior-art.md) also finds prior art for lower-chloride aging policies and support-dependent stability of the chloride optimum. **Ordinary operating drift and one credible competing policy now precede a substantial excursion campaign.** Material-versus-operation comparison is established framing; the required contribution is a new predictive Ni-dependent relationship with a consequential observed benefit or limit.

Set the ordinary-operation horizon and the smallest useful contrast after commissioning establishes response times and uncertainty. If no resolved useful difference appears within that horizon, close the comparison rather than extend it indefinitely or commission structural work. If familiar chloride adjustment reproduces the benefit without a new predictive relationship, record the result and demote the program. These stopping rules do not imply a lifetime null beyond the tested horizon.

Structural, isotope and additional-composition measurements remain branches of the final stage when their result would change the decision. A useful null is a measured limit under the stated preparation, feed, policy and horizon, not universal Ni incompatibility or proof of irreversible damage.

## 1. Important problem and specific opportunity

Ethylene combustion consumes feed and oxygen while producing CO2 instead of ethylene oxide (EO). Improvements must retain useful EO output and catalyst life. Shell reports roughly 90% average selectivity over operating cycles of at least three years for its technology; this is supplier evidence, not a matched independent trial. A short laboratory result cannot establish competitive lifetime. [Industrial reference](https://www.shell.com/business-customers/catalysts-technologies/licensed-technologies/petrochemicals/ethylene-oxide-production.html).

The closed reaction balance illustrates the value: raising carbon selectivity from 90% to 91% at fixed EO output reduces reaction ethylene consumption by about 1.1%, reaction CO2 formation by 11%, and reaction O2 consumption by 4.4%. These are calculated reaction-level changes, not predicted performance or plant-wide savings. [Calculation and limits](../calculations/epoxidation-practical-metrics.md).

Three observations supply the starting point:

| Evidence | What is established | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| [Jalil et al., Ni–Ag, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adt1213) | Dilute Ni suppresses CO2 formation more than EO formation on supported Ag; chlorine adds a further selectivity benefit. Model-surface oxygen behavior changes with Ni. | Whether this proposed Ni state and chemical effect persist on the complete promoted formulation. Assigning the supported-catalyst effect to a particular retained oxygen population remains a hypothesis. |
| [Hwang et al., Cs/Re/Cl–Ag, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2026.117021) | Appropriate promoter architectures show a distinct chloride-dependent kinetic/selectivity response, with slow reversible changes after chloride excursions. | Direct identification of the proposed oxygen-transfer sequence and its compatibility with a Ni-conditioned surface. |
| [US5380885A, 1995](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5380885A/en) | A dilute-Ni promoted catalyst reportedly retains selectivity better through the disclosed accelerated-aging cycles, with similar initial selectivity to its Ni-free parent. | The working cause, a composition/processing-matched causal comparison, integrated output and transfer to a current useful reference. |

The [recovered chlorine-kinetics audit](../reviews/ag-chlorine-primary-discriminator.md) establishes coupled chlorine/product reactor modeling as prior art. Chlorine stripping and TFE titration provide operational estimates with state and normalization limits. A transferred model failure is not new chemistry; the proposed contribution must exceed the actual uncertainty of a calibrated baseline and change the useful material/operating choice.

The recent mechanistic interpretations suggest making oxygen less reactive toward combustion or converting it into an additional selective epoxidizing species. Historical combined-catalyst performance supports compatibility in one formulation, but does not show how those proposed functions relate to selectivity retention.

## 2. Consequential hypothesis and alternatives

**Hypothesis:** dilute Ni preserves a selective promoted working state under a consequential operating exposure, and this retention can be distinguished from an immediate change in combustion chemistry or a recoverable conditioning difference. A successful explanation must predict an intervention that improves sustained output or avoids an ineffective material change.

The older patent reports little initial selectivity difference between Ni-containing and Ni-free materials but a smaller subsequent loss. Its approximate Ni:Ag bulk ratio is 1:835, within the dilute regime; its Ni state was not identified. It also hypothesizes ionic Ni association with perrhenate. Neither dilute loading nor generic Ni–Re association is a new claim. In contrast, Jalil's recent Cs/Re-free materials show a substantial fresh selectivity response. Different materials and conditions prevent assigning these observations to different mechanisms without experiment.

| Competing explanation | Observation needed | Consequential intervention |
|---|---|---|
| Ni suppresses combustion in the fresh working material | A reproducible rate/product-split difference at matched conditions before the chosen exposure | Select the formulation and chloride window that retain useful EO output; test the relevant product burden. |
| Ni prevents deterioration that persists through the tested recovery | A persistent difference after equal exposure and demonstrated, bounded reconditioning, linked to a relevant structural or promoter-state change | Avoid the damaging exposure or prepare a material that retains the needed state. Unchanged bulk composition alone is insufficient evidence. |
| Ni mainly changes recoverable conditioning | A specified intervention restores the Ni-free material's useful response, with reproducible recovery and measured output cost | Compare periodic recovery with Ni addition using integrated output and recurring resource costs. |
| Ni changes product inhibition or secondary EO survival | A resolved common-product-state response; a separate flux measurement only when needed to assign loss rather than inhibited formation | Choose product-tolerant material/conditions and predict an untested product concentration. |

These explanations can coexist. Classify an experimentally identifiable contribution rather than forcing a unique mechanism. A failed recovery does not prove absolute irreversibility; it establishes persistence within the tested recovery window. A stationary retained oxygen population can alter chemistry without continuously accumulating oxygen.

## 3. Aim 1 — Establish the useful difference on a matched promoted pair

### Preparation and common-platform gate

Anchor the comparison on a functioning Cs/Re-promoted Ag architecture. Its Ag-to-support surface-area ratio affects promoter contact, so transferring all promoters to a dilute colloidal-Ag material can remove the reference function before Ni is tested. Choose one defensible Ni-addition route and its matched processing blank. Match Cs and other promoters, document their inventories and Ag size distributions, and verify that the blank itself preserves the useful reference response.

The older post-impregnation recipe is relevant preparation prior art, not a validated route to Jalil's proposed working state. Its Ni-free aging comparator had a different Cs amount and lacked the second treatment; the solvent-treated comparator was not aged. Close those confounds for the proposed causal comparison, while recognizing that improved controls alone are not the research contribution.

Jalil's [now available SI](../../literature/papers/jalil2025-supplementary-information-for-nickel-promotes/original.pdf), PDF pp.6–10 and Table S5, already includes Ag controls receiving the NiAg processing without Ni. Its colloidal route is sensitive to ethylene-glycol lot, water, trace impurities and PVP addition, and uses NaOH/hydrazine during Ni incorporation. Reproduce the relevant processing and measure inventories; nominal Ni loading alone does not define a transferable material. Figure 2 includes a 450 °C H2 pretreatment that optimized Figure 3 omits; both use oxidative calcination and an 8 h reaction conditioning step. Do not merge those protocols into one controlled series or assume they preserve a Cs/Re architecture.

The SI's physical mixture of supported Ni nanoparticles and Ag behaves nearly like Ag alone (Figure S19), and its NiAg HERFD-XANES is poorly described by supported Ni/NiO references (Figures S14–S16). These observations support an Ag-associated Ni environment. They neither identify a unique atomic distribution nor establish the working Ni state during promoted EO synthesis. These measurements do not validate transfer to the historical ionic-Ni/perrhenate hypothesis.

Use a no-Cs Ag/Ni–Ag bridge on the chosen platform **if the claim depends on transferring Jalil's combustion-suppression function**. The published Ni parent lacks Cs, so Ni/Cs failure cannot uniquely implicate Re. A full Ni × Re factorial at fixed Cs is a later branch when Re dependence would distinguish the selected explanation, rather than the initial commitment.

For similar particle shapes, nominal Ni per surface Ag scales as `(Ni/Ag_bulk) × d`. Copying 1:200 from approximately 75 nm Ag to 170 nm Ag would increase that nominal density about 2.3-fold; roughly 1:450 would preserve the geometric estimate. This is not measured coverage or an optimum. Use the actual size distribution, and do not infer a distinct Ni state from the difference between this estimate and the older 1:835 bulk ratio. [Source and geometry audit](../working/epoxidation-opportunity.md#follow-up-transferring-the-ni-loading-between-particle-sizes).

That surface-area scaling addresses transfer of the proposed Ni–Ag surface function. For the different hypothesis of Ni association with Re-containing species, record Ni:Re as well: the historical 88 ppmw Ni/280 ppmw Re corresponds to approximately 1:1 atoms. This is a bulk preparation ratio, not association stoichiometry or an optimum. Do not select or interpret the first loading by conflating these hypotheses; neither ratio alone establishes the working Ni state. A later composition contrast should test the surviving dependence, rather than initiate a loading library.

Ni segregation, aggregation, promoter redistribution and preparation-induced changes remain alternatives. Similar bulk contents and Ag sizes remove gross confounds but do not establish identical promoter contact. Permit one reasoned correction for an identified transfer failure; a broad synthesis search changes the program's resource case. Equipment and material access remain unconfirmed. The [core-design review](../reviews/ag-core-experimental-design.md) provides the comparison and measurement limits, with its original full-factorial sequence now conditional on the revised question.

### Site-count reporting after the 2026-09-16 direction search

Any per-site normalization in this program uses Iyer and Bhan's trifluoroethanol (TFE) titration, whose three estimators differ by 2.7-fold (20.7, 29.7 and 56.1 μmol g−1). The [standards](../standards.md#evidence) now require during-reaction titrations to be reported as a titratable total and a bed-average rate per titratable site, with stoichiometry, reversibility and titrant breakthrough stated. The [independent methods audit](../reviews/direction-search-methods-independent-review.md) found that a plug-flow front cannot explain the estimator spread (a front predicts a straight line, and the published curve is convex) and that Iyer's Table 1 already varied loading and TFE flow without trend, so a bed-mass re-test would not decide it. If a per-site rate is required for the Ni/reference comparison, add a support-only TFE blank, a water-cofeed control, breakthrough timing and a fluorine balance; otherwise compare per gram of Ag inventory and treat the titration as an operational estimate. A Ni-dependent difference in rate per titrated site cannot be assigned to per-site chemistry unless the titratable working fraction is shown unchanged.

### Compare useful operating curves

At a temperature, pressure and feed regime demonstrated for the promoted reference:

1. Bracket chloride conditions separately for each formulation at matched ethylene, O2, ethane, CO2 and measured water conditions. Chloride concentration is an operating variable, not a universal surface-state coordinate.
2. Follow observed evolution and return to a reference condition after both lower and higher chloride exposure. Published relaxation over many hours makes short holds unreliable; the Ni/Cl study itself does not demonstrate long-term stationary output. Persistent drift can support a matched time-window productivity comparison, but not a stationary kinetic assignment. Keep preparation differences, analytical uncertainty and drift in the comparison.
3. Measure net EO and CO2 rates, relevant side products, reactant consumption and flow-corrected balances. A resolved low-contact-time limit without inlet EO can identify the primary product split at the inlet state. Finite-contact-time curvature also reflects changing CO2, water and chloride response; it does not uniquely measure secondary EO loss. An ordinary EO cofeed measures net product sensitivity because it can change both EO formation and consumption. Add a discriminating carbon tracer only if that unresolved distinction would change the next intervention and the analytical capability exists.
4. Repeat the decisive material pair using independent preparations. Establish that apparent advantages survive heat/mass-transfer checks and are not caused by different bed temperatures or secondary residence times.

Compare EO output at a specified useful selectivity and selectivity at matched useful output. Report initial total catalyst mass, Ag inventory and promoter inventory. A maximum selectivity obtained by nearly stopping production is not the target.

Fresh chloride optima are mapping results, not assumed best policies through aging. Track ordinary operation long enough to resolve the observed drift or bound it over the tested horizon. Outlet EO concentration alone does not establish matched production: retain the measured molar flow. A stable location of the chloride optimum does not establish stable activity or selectivity.

Keep two comparisons distinct: separately adjusted chloride settings establish the useful catalyst/operating choice; overlapping matched-condition responses test the effect of Ni within the preparation family. Individually optimized endpoints are not a mechanistic factorial interaction. Add a Re-free contrast only if it distinguishes the consequential explanation, and restrict product challenges to the decisive pair.

**First decision:** do both formulations meet the predefined useful-output floor after commissioning and chloride adjustment? If so, complete the predefined ordinary-operation horizon even if their fresh outputs are indistinguishable. **After that horizon**, ask whether a reproducible fresh or selective-output retention difference warrants explanation beyond the historical report. If neither is resolved, stop this material comparison rather than extend the horizon or expand characterization.

## 4. Aim 2 — Distinguish fresh chemistry, persistent deterioration and recovery

Follow the qualified pair through the predefined ordinary-operation horizon, using matched temperature/feed/flow histories for the material comparison. Equal fresh output does not cancel this retention test. Include one useful lower-chloride policy on the Ni-free reference from its measured operating curve, or another documented ordinary policy if lower chloride has no useful production window. Compare that policy with the proposed Ni choice over the same horizon. Policies can use different settings, but those differences and their resource costs must remain explicit; they do not form a matched-condition mechanistic contrast. No upfront full factorial is required. A consequential observed contrast is required before expanding into the mechanistic branches below; no resolved useful difference at the horizon closes the comparison.

The modern underchlorination disclosure supplies a real competing policy, but its long-life benefit is extrapolated from short-run data with different flows. Do not borrow its numerical chloride target or assume that the selectivity penalty is confined to startup. Integrate evolving output and reactant losses; do not justify an observed disadvantage with an unvalidated distant crossover.

Jalil SI Figure S21 supplies a direct reason to measure that evolution: NiAg EO rate initially increases on chloride introduction, then declines substantially through approximately 20 h. Both materials evolve. This supports a different chloride response, not sustained promotion, a stationary endpoint or a demonstrated recovery policy.

Use a further exposure when it distinguishes the remaining explanation for ordinary drift or a documented disturbance burden. A small excursion can establish response times early; a substantial campaign needs that connection. Do not infer normal-operation durability from an arbitrary severe treatment; the historical 85%-conversion/285 °C accelerated cycles do not supply a universal aging protocol.

A [specific chloride-retention branch](../working/ag-chloride-retention-hypothesis.md) asks whether Ni reduces loss of the useful Re-dependent response during a chloride excursion and thereby lowers subsequent recovery cost. Its stronger claim must survive explanations based on fresh Ni chemistry and changed chlorine uptake/removal. Kinetic signatures and total chlorine inventory do not identify productive Ag–Re contact. Preserving function during an excursion and recovering faster after equal loss are distinct hypotheses.

Use that branch only when the exposure addresses ordinary-operation drift or a documented disturbance burden. No industrial excursion frequency is established here. A pulse can probe chemistry without producing a meaningful operating benefit; do not turn a rare or invented upset into an assumed recurring savings opportunity.

After exposure, return both materials to the same reference feed and apply a defined recovery sequence supported by the measured chloride response. Record the time and EO-output cost of recovery. The older patent already reoptimized its endpoint tests, so merely adding a reference return is not new. The contribution must be a specified recovery or persistent-state relationship that predicts a different, useful operating choice.

Include continued ordinary operation or a matched sham return so spontaneous recovery with elapsed time is not credited to the intervention. Chlorine-driven [Ag restructuring is a documented alternative](../reviews/ag-chlorine-structure-alternative.md): restored output does not establish restoration of the original structure or isolate reversible adsorption. Recovery can itself change later deterioration; retain separately followed material when that effect matters instead of treating recovery as a neutral assay. Compare the complete resulting policies over a common horizon using the [recovery-output accounting](../calculations/epoxidation-practical-metrics.md#recovery-versus-ni-stabilization-the-minimum-decision-calculation).

Use characterization only to distinguish the remaining alternatives. For example, an observed Ag-size change can test a coarsening explanation, but correlation alone does not assign the loss to that change. Reversible promoter redistribution, persistent Ni redistribution and chloride-state changes must remain qualified when available measurements cannot distinguish them.

The [working-state measurement audit](../reviews/ag-working-state-measurement.md) supports keeping the initial commitment kinetic. A primary thesis demonstrates Re fluorescence spectra during ethylene oxidation at 500 ppm Re, but does not resolve the required Ag–Re contact assignment. Test absolute Re inventory only for a live loss hypothesis; an unchanged inventory cannot exclude relocation. A later Re-edge measurement needs demonstrated contact-sensitive contrast and a consequential sensitivity bound. An unchanged element-average spectrum cannot exclude an influential minority whose abundance is unconstrained.

If fresh or recovered output depends strongly on products, use the [crossed product-state comparison](../calculations/ag-product-feedback-test.md). Hwang already proposes that suppressing combustion can reduce CO2 inhibition of the promoted channel. The new question is whether this accounts quantitatively for the Ni-dependent response and changes the design decision. Matching inlet CO2 alone does not match local CO2/water throughout a bed. Ordinary EO cofeed measures net product sensitivity, not a unique secondary oxidation rate; [Keijzer's Re/Cl product tests](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4CY00858H) are relevant prior art on different materials and feeds.

The baseline must credit [Iyer 2021](../../literature/papers/iyer2021-interdependencies-among-ethylene-oxidation-and/original.pdf), Section 3.4: separately assessed oxidation, chlorine-exchange and support-degradation kinetics predict local coverage and outlet behavior by integration from inlet conditions. [Santos 2024](../../literature/papers/santos2024-the-complex-chlorination-effects-on/original.pdf), Figures 5–7 and 9, adds elevated-pressure measurements of temperature, O2, water and CO2 effects on the chloride optimum. These are demonstrated prior art, with finite residuals and empirical/model assumptions; an uncalibrated transfer failure is insufficient evidence for a Ni-specific state. Chen 2018's contact-time selectivity loss despite a null EO-cofeed response further confirms that secondary EO loss cannot be read directly from either test alone.

If separately adjusted chloride removes the useful difference after the predefined retention horizon, report the narrower operating result. Collapse of the fresh curves alone does not close the retention test. Collapsing rate/selectivity curves do not uniquely identify chlorine adsorption or prove unchanged oxygen chemistry. Add a mechanism-specific test only when a consequential prediction remains unresolved.

Before that escalation, the [steady-curve consistency check](../working/ag-chloride-retention-hypothesis.md#7-early-operational-gate-does-the-transient-leave-the-steady-operating-curve) compares transient net EO/CO2 pairs with each material's independently measured steady chloride curve. Curve collapse can justify stopping a claim of an additional output relationship, but does not rule out valuable changes in recovery speed. Departure rejects that whole-bed description only after uncertainty and transport checks; it does not prove Re relocation.

### Optional oxygen-return branch

In a restricted stationary cycle, one initial O2 event forms EO and leaves an O atom. That atom can feed combustion, another selective route, or recombination into O2. Let `e` and `z` denote net EO and CO2 formation. The minimal return-only version requires molecular O2 return of `(e−3z)/2` when positive. Independent O2 dissociation and secondary EO combustion preserve a conservative requirement for cross-parent return, but ethane oxidation lowers it. Use the actual product/cofeed balance, not the minimal estimate unchanged. Net selectivity alone cannot choose between these routes. [Derivation and corrected bound](../calculations/epoxidation-oxygen-return.md) and [independent audit](../reviews/epoxidation-balance-review.md).

**Quantitative isotope-return measurements are already prior art.** Esposito and Bhan's recovered propylene SI uses CO2 exchange to label reactive oxygen differently from gas O2 and derives an oxygen-activation reversibility near 0.02. Its common-gas balance already corrects ordinary readsorption. The [fresh independent review](../reviews/ag-isotope-reversibility-prior-art.md) verifies the equations and identifies the conditional ethylene adaptation. This established balance replaces the original mixed-feed proposal as the first measurement approach to assess.

The decisive requirement is a justified isotope contrast between gas oxygen and the oxygen that actually returns to O2. EO oxygen labeling alone need not represent the leftover, recombining population in a sequential mechanism. Assess existing evidence and capability at one stable working state; do not start a new isotope study unless that population connection, the required-return lower bound and analytical precision can support a useful exclusion. Hold chemical cofeeds fixed when changing labels. If CO2 exchange erases original O2-parent ancestry, replace the old mixed-O2 prediction rather than combining incompatible assumptions.

Esposito's [2026 main](../../literature/papers/esposito2026-common-active-sites-and-oxidants/original.pdf) and [SI](../../literature/papers/esposito2026-supporting-information-for-common-active/original.pdf) strengthen shared-oxidant competition evidence for ethylene and propylene on K/NO/Cl–Ag/CaCO3. They also show different ethylene kinetics (approximately 0.6 order in ethylene and 0.45 in O2) and selectivity changing with ethylene pressure. Shared function does not imply identical rate control or transfer of the propylene return bound to ethylene, much less to Ni/Cs/Re/Cl–Ag. The 2024 main explicitly allows a reversible O2-derived precursor upstream of irreversible formation of the CO2-exchanging oxidant; the gas-return result cannot exclude that precursor.

The earlier [mixed-feed feasibility review](../reviews/epoxidation-isotope-feasibility.md) and [transport bound](../calculations/oxygen-escape-bound.md) remain relevant alternatives where their assumptions apply. Pre-mixing recapture and unresolved isotope gradients can still hide return; ordinary readsorption already included in the common-gas balance must not be charged a second escape correction. Hwang's net-rate transport check does not establish the missing gross local-capture bound. If neither approach passes its gates, close this optional branch and judge retention through its separate predictive and practical requirements.

Ruling out bulk return does not identify ClO, prove Ni-to-Re atom transfer, or exclude O2 reformation and reuse on the surface. The useful advance is a bounded chemical conclusion that changes the rationale for catalyst choice. A direct site-specific transfer claim requires stronger evidence.

## 5. Aim 3 — Turn the result into a prediction and a useful intervention

Advance only the material or operating change supported by Aims 1–2. For recoverable loss, predict a recovery interval or procedure and its recurring output cost. For persistent deterioration, predict an exposure that preserves or destroys the relevant advantage after equal reconditioning. For fresh selectivity or product tolerance, predict one untested product condition. A second composition point should test an identified dependence, not start a loading library.

State one prediction before the next experiment. If reduced CO2 inhibition explains the gain, predict the decisive pair's net EO and CO2 rate differences at an untested CO2 concentration. If EO product sensitivity controls it, use an untested EO concentration; if conditioning controls it, use one new chloride history. Keep the selected chloride settings for the first predictive test, rather than retuning them after seeing the result. There is no universal predicted sign: measured sensitivities must support the claimed direction and, when possible, magnitude. Otherwise report a useful product-tolerance relationship rather than mechanistic validation. Successful prediction is more informative than refitting the same operating curve.

Then assess sustained output, conditioning losses and recovery on the timescale of the observed evolution. Quantify penalties in activity, required temperature, Ag inventory, ethane/chloride use and preparation complexity. Include recurring conditioning in integrated productivity; amortize one-time startup only over an explicitly justified service period. No short test certifies industrial catalyst life.

If the mechanism predicts an unfavorable limit, report it with the tested material/condition scope. It can prevent an unproductive promoter strategy without establishing universal Ni–Re incompatibility.

## 6. Novelty boundary

The [Ni patent disclosure](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20260027557A1/en) already contemplates Cs/Re/Cl. Published work also covers promoter placement, chlorine history, oxygen isotope experiments and transient kinetic-model comparisons. None is claimed new here. The uploaded Iyer, Harris, Chen, Santos and Esposito main texts and Jalil/Esposito SIs are incorporated in the [source audit](../reviews/post-upload-ag-audit.md); Iyer2023, Harris2019 and Chen2018 SI remain unavailable in the inspected packages. The [current source status](../literature-status.md) tracks remaining gaps rather than treating recovered main texts as unread. Santos's measured operating-response families, Jalil's processing controls, and Esposito's approximately 14 h promoter-memory and cross-olefin competition results further restrict novelty claims.

Older [Ni preparation prior art](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9018126B2/en) also reports improved Ag dispersion after severe air heating. This reinforces a structural explanation to check; it does not establish durability of the proposed dilute-Ni working state.

The proposed contribution is a causal and predictive account of Ni-dependent selective-output retention that changes a formulation or operating decision beyond the historical Ni/Cs/Re aging result and established chloride policies. Neither a small unexplained gain nor reproducing those results with better controls fully satisfies the ambition. Demote if a familiar operating adjustment accounts for the difference without a new consequential prediction, if the only benefit depends on an unsupported upset burden, or if no useful distinction survives the remaining source checks.

## 7. Confidence and resources

| Judgment | Present assessment |
|---|---|
| Scientific hypothesis | Plausible, with competing fresh-chemistry, structural-retention and conditioning explanations. Historical compatibility is reported; the working cause is unresolved in the inspected evidence. |
| Informative matched comparison | Moderate conditional on a functioning promoted platform and a measurable consequential difference. Preparation and recovery histories are the main burdens; unique microscopic attribution is harder. |
| Informative oxygen-return bound | Conditional on existing analytical capability and demonstrated signal recovery; substantially less certain than the rate/selectivity comparison. |
| Meaningful practical improvement | Low evidentiary support at present, but a clear reaction-level benefit if sustained selective output improves. Modern optimized reference performance is demanding. |
| Broader usefulness | A validated distinction between chemical selectivity, persistent deterioration and recoverable conditioning could guide other promoted oxidation catalysts. Transfer beyond Ag remains a future hypothesis. |

The initial commitment is one matched promoted pair, a bounded fresh/exposure/recovery comparison and one prediction that can change a useful decision. Exact time, cost and equipment requirements need actual laboratory capability; no numerical success probability is assigned.
