# Independent review: the core Ag compatibility experiment

2026-09-15. Scope: Aims 1–2 and the first predictive experiment in [the Ag program](../programs/ag-selective-oxygen-use.md). This review uses the program, the prior value reviews, and the available primary texts of [Hwang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2026.117021) and [Jalil et al.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adt1213). It does not review the optional oxygen-return experiment or establish exhaustive novelty. A targeted read of the newly available [Keijzer et al. Re/Cl study](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4CY00858H) also informs the EO-loss boundary below. No literature files were changed and no new source was identified.

## Judgment

**2026-09-16 implementation update:** the [program's minimum sequence](../programs/ag-selective-oxygen-use.md#minimum-executable-sequence) starts with one promoted Ni/no-Ni pair and ordinary-operation policies. The original full Ni × Re design is a conditional follow-up, not an entry requirement. Uploaded main texts and Jalil/Esposito SI are incorporated through the [post-upload audit](post-upload-ag-audit.md).

**A matched promoted pair can support a useful catalyst choice, but does not itself identify a shared oxygen mechanism or uniquely separate primary formation from secondary EO loss.** A later fixed-Cs Ni × Re comparison is justified only when Re dependence distinguishes the remaining explanation. The bounded experiment requires a functioning common platform. Two controls make its first results easier to interpret:

1. Treat transfer to the common platform as an explicit gate. Reproducing the two papers on their separate native materials is insufficient.
2. Separate measured net product sensitivity from an assigned secondary oxidation rate. Ordinary EO cofeed experiments can change both EO formation and EO consumption.

A full mechanistic decomposition need not precede a performance decision. A reproducible advantage under relevant product concentrations can justify advancing the combination even when the microscopic cause remains unresolved. Conversely, the mechanistic contribution requires a prediction that competing explanations do not all make.

## 1. The common platform is the first consequential uncertainty

Jalil reports Ni-doped colloidal Ag, approximately 70 nm particles, with the main catalytic comparison at 5 wt% Ag on α-Al2O3 and 1 bar. The Ag particles are formed before Ni addition. Hwang's useful Cs/Re response depends on a more specific architecture: its high Ag-to-support surface-area ratio favors promoter contact with Ag. The lower-area-ratio A series lacks the distinctive response even when Cs and Re are present; the higher-area-ratio B series supplies it. The B-CsRe preparation includes Ag and promoter precursors during impregnation and subsequent treatment. These facts appear in Jalil's manuscript pp. 4–6 and Hwang §§2.1 and 3.3, especially pp. 9–10. They are stronger constraints than nominal particle-size matching alone.

Putting all four formulations on the dilute Jalil support could remove the Re function before testing Ni–Re compatibility. Adding Ni to the Hwang platform could instead fail to produce the Ni state responsible for the published benefit. A negative factorial result would then describe an unsuccessful transfer, not competition between two demonstrated working functions.

**Prefer the already functioning Cs/Re architecture as the anchor**, if available. Preserve its Ag/support ratio and preparation as far as the Ni intervention permits. Introduce Ni through one defensible adaptation of the published route, with corresponding processing applied to its Ni-free comparison. Jalil's [uploaded SI](../../literature/papers/jalil2025-supplementary-information-for-nickel-promotes/original.pdf), PDF pp.6–10 and Table S5, already includes Ag controls processed by the NiAg route without Ni. Its ethylene-glycol lot, water, trace impurities, PVP sequence and NaOH/hydrazine reduction matter to preparation; these controls are established practice, not the new contribution. Figure 2 includes 450 °C H2 reduction that optimized Figure 3 omits. Record the reproduced protocol and inventories explicitly. The SI still does not validate applying this colloidal route to an existing supported Cs/Re material.

The first gate requires a functional Cs/Re reference after the processing used in the combined comparison. Add an Ag/Ni–Ag pair **without Cs on the chosen common platform** only if the claim depends on transferring Jalil's fresh-chemistry function. If original parent material is available, it can diagnose transfer failure; reproducing every published preparation is not required by default.

If both that bridge and a later full factorial are justified, the complete set contains six compositions: Ag/Ni–Ag without Cs, then Cs–Ag, Ni/Cs–Ag, Cs/Re–Ag and Ni/Cs/Re–Ag. This is the conditional maximum described here, not the minimum experiment. Existing qualified material can satisfy parent checks. Document actual preparation contrasts; Re-free and Re-containing branches may have different nucleation chemistry.

### What preparation controls can establish

- If the sham processing destroys the original Cs/Re response, repair or abandon the preparation bridge before interpreting the Ni experiment.
- If only Ni addition changes the response, that is a causal effect of the complete Ni-addition intervention. It can still arise from redistribution of Cs/Re, Ni aggregation, or altered Ag structure.
- Similar bulk contents and Ag size distributions remove gross alternatives; they do not prove identical promoter contact or identify a Ni surface coverage.
- If Ni improves the no-Cs bridge but not Ni/Cs, the failure already occurs on adding Cs. Re-specific incompatibility has not been established.

A failed transfer is informative about that preparation, but it is not automatically a substantial general compatibility result. Permit one reasoned correction if a specific cause is found. A broad synthesis search would change the scale and confidence of the program and should trigger a new priority assessment.

## 2. Two distinct comparisons are needed, using largely the same data

**Operating comparison:** each formulation gets its own chloride setting for the same predefined objective, such as maximum net EO output above a specified useful carbon selectivity. This tests the catalyst/operating choice that a user would make.

**Common-condition pair comparison:** compare the Ni-containing and Ni-free versions of the same promoted formulation at overlapping inlet conditions and documented histories. This measures the effect of the Ni-addition intervention without combining it with separately chosen operating settings. It does not assume that the two materials have identical surface chlorine coverage.

These are different quantities. A contrast between two individually optimized endpoints combines composition effects with different operating states. Conversely, forcing both materials to one chloride concentration can penalize a useful catalyst merely because its moderation requirement shifted. **Only if Re dependence would distinguish the surviving explanation**, add the Re-free pair and compare Ni responses with and without Re at overlapping conditions. Four independently optimized endpoints would still not establish a mechanistic factorial interaction.

Use adaptive, overlapping chloride brackets instead of a dense grid. Begin near the reproduced promoted reference's useful region, then extend only enough to distinguish an interior useful region from an activity/selectivity endpoint. Record net EO and CO2 rates separately as well as carbon selectivity; an interaction in a ratio can arise without a corresponding interaction in either elementary branch. Do not require a statistically nonzero Ni × Re coefficient to advance an additive benefit.

Jalil's Ni-plus-chloride parent is a time-dependent result: original SI Figure S21 shows Ni-containing EO rates declining substantially after an initial rise through approximately 20 h; the Ag comparison also loses most of its rate. Reproducing the selectivity trend therefore does not establish a stationary parent state or useful lifetime. Keep two judgments separate: whether the parent intervention is reproduced, and whether the common platform is stable enough for the intended comparison. The source's 8 h conditioning and 2 h waits after conversion changes are recorded protocols, not proof those waits suffice for the proposed promoted material.

Persistent drift need not make every comparison useless. With a reproducible initial treatment and predefined common operating history, compare integrated net EO output and carbon selectivity over matched time windows and inventory bases. Include the drift and conditioning penalty in the result. Do not combine measurements from different ages into an allegedly stationary response curve, or extrapolate a short window into catalyst life. If history cannot be reproduced well enough to distinguish the formulations, stop the mechanistic comparison at that gate.

Reference returns should test whether the chosen operating point is recoverable after both lower and higher chloride exposure. A reference → lower chloride → reference → higher chloride → reference sequence can answer that question without measuring a large arbitrary history matrix. Stabilization must follow observed evolution and balance closure, not a prescribed short dwell. If returns differ materially, report performance conditional on history; a single stationary chloride curve has not been established.

If independently adjusted chloride removes the fresh difference, record that operating result and continue the predefined retention test when both materials meet the useful-output floor. If no consequential practical difference survives the full finite horizon, close the material comparison. Neither result uniquely proves that Ni only changes chlorine adsorption or that oxygen chemistry is unchanged: compensating changes can collapse a rate/selectivity curve. A mechanism-specific investigation still requires a consequential unresolved prediction.

## 3. What residence time and EO cofeed can identify

### Residence-time extrapolation has a useful but narrow meaning

For an isothermal differential bed with no inlet EO, finite local reaction rates and a common inlet working state, let τ be a catalyst-contact coordinate. Near τ = 0,

\[
F_{EO,out}/F_{ref} = r_{EO,primary}(0)\,\tau + O(\tau^2),
\]

\[
\Delta F_{CO_2}/F_{ref} = r_{CO_2,primary}(0)\,\tau + O(\tau^2).
\]

The normalization only fixes the units of the rates. EO made upstream is initially proportional to τ, so its subsequent reaction first contributes at higher order when its consumption rate vanishes at zero EO pressure. The limiting carbon selectivity therefore probes the primary product split at the **inlet working state**. It does not establish the split everywhere in a higher-conversion bed.

Use a small, well-resolved contact-time bracket on the decisive pair. Check that decreasing contact time is not simultaneously changing temperature, external transfer, or a persistent catalyst state. Feed CO2 must be subtracted to obtain net formation. If the inferred intercept depends strongly on the fitted range or its uncertainty is comparable to the material difference, do not report a resolved primary-selectivity change.

Selectivity curvature alone does not measure a secondary EO rate. Along the bed, reactants, CO2, water and effective chloride moderation also change; these can alter primary selectivity. Hwang itself reports that varying residence time and varying chloride give different trajectories. A finite-residence-time loss should remain an observed net loss until a model or additional measurement distinguishes its cause.

Chen2018's [main text](../../literature/papers/chen2018-kinetics-of-ethylene-epoxidation-on/original.pdf), Figures 9–12, provides a concrete precedent: a null EO-cofeed response accompanies a 0.5–1.6 percentage-point selectivity loss with increasing contact time. The 0.6–1.8% of EO combustion discussed by the authors is sufficient to explain the loss; it is not a measured secondary flux. Iyer2021's Section 3.4 subsequently demonstrates a reactor prediction combining independently assessed primary kinetics, local chlorine coverage and support-mediated EO degradation. Use that credited baseline before proposing an additional Ni state.

### Lower self-generated CO2 can improve performance without a direct Ni–Re interaction

Matched inlet CO2 does not imply matched local CO2 at appreciable conversion. A Ni catalyst that burns less ethylene generates less CO2 and water along its bed. Hwang reports strong CO2 inhibition in the intermediate-chloride Cs/Re regime and explicitly proposes that suppressing the combustion channel relieves inhibition of the promoted channel (§§3.4–3.5). This feedback is prior art, not a new mechanism proposed here. It can amplify a Ni benefit without oxygen transfer between Ni and Re. Conversely, loss of the benefit at higher inlet CO2 could reflect stronger product inhibition rather than failure of the Ni state.

The simplest separation is conceptual and experimental: compare low-conversion rates at matched inlet CO2 and measured water to ask about a common local environment, then report finite-bed net performance as including its own product-feedback effects. A feed-matched differential comparison does not remove all structural differences, but minimizes an avoidable concentration-profile confounder. Do not subtract the feedback from the practical benefit; reduced self-poisoning can be a useful property. Withhold a direct shared-oxygen assignment.

If a substantial advantage appears primarily at higher conversion, first test the CO2 explanation with a targeted reversible CO2 challenge on the decisive pair in the useful chloride regime. Keep EO and water accounted for; a CO2 response alone does not exclude their roles. This is preferable to automatically launching a complete EO-loss decomposition. If the small-contact-time advantage is already clear and product feedback does not change the design choice, the extra challenge can wait for the predictive stage.

### An EO cofeed slope is a net response

Write the observable EO rate at fixed other inlet variables as

\[
r_{EO,net}(p_{EO}) = r_{EO,formation}(p_{EO})-r_{EO,loss}(p_{EO}).
\]

Then

\[
\frac{d r_{EO,net}}{d p_{EO}} =
\frac{d r_{EO,formation}}{d p_{EO}}-
\frac{d r_{EO,loss}}{d p_{EO}}.
\]

An ordinary cofeed experiment measures the left-hand side. EO can inhibit primary formation, change moderation, or change the working state as well as undergo oxidation. Extra CO2 does not resolve this by itself because primary ethylene combustion can also change. An EO/O2 test without ethylene probes another chemical environment and cannot automatically supply the secondary rate during epoxidation. Keijzer et al. provide relevant prior art: dedicated tests use 0.15 vol% EO, 0.075 vol% acetaldehyde and 7.5 vol% O2, with no ethylene, and examine Re-dependent EO isomerization/combustion. Their separate epoxidation and product-fate tests are informative, but their Re/Cl results do not establish secondary fluxes on the complete Ni/Cs/Re material. This also argues for measuring relevant carbon side products when needed rather than treating every EO loss as CO2 formation.

**For the first catalyst decision, call this quantity product sensitivity and use it directly.** A catalyst that sustains higher net EO output in a defined EO-containing feed has demonstrated a useful property without assigning every lost turnover. Require a return to the original feed after the challenge to distinguish recoverable product response from persistent change. Limit subtraction claims when cofed EO overwhelms the net EO increment analytically.

Only if primary inhibition versus secondary EO consumption would change the next material intervention should the program add a discriminating flux measurement. A possible option is a carbon-labeled EO tracer within an otherwise unchanged ethylene/EO feed, measuring labeled EO disappearance and labeled carbon products. This would separate the fate of fed EO carbon from newly formed EO carbon, subject to label recovery, relevant side products, tracer effects, and any carbon scrambling or reforming pathways. Replacing part of an existing EO cofeed with labeled EO avoids changing its total concentration. Availability, analytical resolution and informative tracer concentration have not been established. This is a conditional measurement option, not an assumption that the required capability exists or a default addition to the pilot. No oxygen-isotope experiment is needed for the basic net-performance comparison.

Even a successful labeled-EO test measures the fate of gas-fed EO at that state. Inferring the fate of all nascent surface intermediates or converting it into a whole-bed secondary-loss correction requires a defensible transport/reaction model. If that model remains underdetermined, preserve the narrower gas-product survival result.

## 4. Smallest coherent experimental sequence

| Stage | Minimum question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Common-platform gate | Does the processed Cs/Re anchor retain its useful response, and are balances, response times and preparation variation resolved? | Set the finite horizon and useful contrast after commissioning. Stop or correct one specific transfer failure. Add a no-Cs bridge only if transfer of Jalil's function is part of the claim. |
| First useful pair | Do both Ni/Cs/Re and Cs/Re meet the predefined useful-output floor after appropriate chloride adjustment and reference returns, and is there a resolved fresh contrast? | If both qualify, continue through the predefined ordinary-retention horizon even if fresh outputs are indistinguishable. Record a resolved fresh benefit or impairment without requiring it to test retention; a material below the useful-output floor fails this comparison. |
| Ordinary operation and competing policy | Does the pair differ in integrated useful output over the same finite horizon, compared with a credible Ni-free lower-chloride policy? | No resolved useful difference closes the comparison within that domain. Include ordinary continuation as a control before crediting recovery. |
| Conditional Re comparison | Only if Re dependence changes the surviving explanation: do Cs and Ni/Cs show the same, weaker, or opposite response? | Locate the dependence on Re; retain separate rate and selectivity conclusions. Skip this branch when it does not change the decision. |
| Explain the decisive difference | Does a low-contact-time limit preserve the difference? If the advantage grows with conversion, can a targeted CO2 or EO challenge explain the change? | Assign an inlet primary effect only when resolved; otherwise retain a net product-sensitivity or conditioning explanation. Add a flux measurement only if its result changes the design decision. |
| Reproduce and predict | Does an independent preparation reproduce the decisive pair, and does the stated explanation predict an untested product condition or history with fixed parameters? | Expand only beyond a calibrated baseline when the prediction changes a material/policy choice. Otherwise narrow or reject the extra-mechanism claim. |

The exact order of the middle two stages can change with material availability. If the Re-free pair already exists, it need not wait. The economy comes from restricting detailed residence-time, product-response and characterization work to the decisive pair, not omitting the comparisons needed to identify a Re-dependent effect.

Independent preparation replication should precede expensive mechanism-specific measurements when preparation variability could erase the difference. Existing basic transport and analytical checks remain necessary. This sequence adds no default promoter loading library, temperature matrix, full isotope campaign or operando instrument suite.

## 5. Strongest held-out prediction for the core study

**Choose one product challenge from the observed mechanism, with a CO2 challenge preferred if reduced self-inhibition explains the Ni benefit in the Cs/Re working region.** Predict how the net EO and CO2 rate differences between the decisive pair change at one untested inlet CO2 concentration, using the already selected chloride settings and otherwise matched ethylene/O2/ethane/EO/water conditions. State the direction and, where the measurements support it, the magnitude with uncertainty before running the experiment. Use a differential condition when the intended claim concerns common local concentrations; a whole-bed prediction needs concentration profiles or a validated reaction model.

The feedback explanation must predict the result through the measured CO2 sensitivity and reduced combustion, not merely assert that any response is compatible. There is no universal sign: Ni can also alter the promoted branch's CO2 sensitivity or the chloride window. If the available data do not support a distinguishable prediction, call the challenge a measurement of product tolerance rather than a mechanistic validation. Hwang's existing CO2 inhibition observation does not supply the missing Ni-containing response by assumption.

For a resolved EO-loss explanation, use an untested EO cofeed instead. Keep the same selected chloride settings and other feed components, and predict both net EO and CO2 rates. The stronger claims require the following evidence:

- **Resolved primary-selectivity benefit:** the low-contact-time advantage and independently bounded product losses predict a remaining net advantage under the held-out EO burden. Failure challenges the explanation or its state invariance.
- **Resolved reduction in gas-fed EO loss:** the measured survival difference predicts how the net advantage changes with EO cofeed. A widening advantage alone is not proof; its magnitude must agree with the independently assigned contribution within uncertainty.
- **Only net product sensitivity resolved:** predict that net response and describe success as a validated operating relationship. Do not rename it a secondary-oxidation mechanism.

Do not retune chloride or refit the model to make the first held-out prediction pass. Re-optimization after a failure may still find a useful condition, but answers another question. Either product challenge tests whether the reason offered for the advantage survives a relevant product burden. Neither proves Ni-to-Re oxygen transfer, establishes a complete reactor/recycle advantage, or substitutes for sustained operation.

If chloride history rather than product response controls the material choice, replace the product challenge with one predeclared history and a predicted recovery/output trajectory. Do not add both branches automatically. Generic chlorine cycling is established prior art; the contribution must be the Ni-dependent prediction that changes a formulation or operating decision.

## Confidence after review

The chemistry hypothesis remains plausible and unresolved. Confidence in an informative core comparison is **moderate conditional on common-platform transfer**, not merely on reproducing each source in isolation. Confidence in uniquely assigning primary versus secondary contributions from residence time and unlabeled EO cofeed alone is **limited**. Confidence in a useful practical improvement remains **low on existing evidence**, but a fair comparison can decisively establish a bounded advantage or prevent an unproductive promoter strategy. Neither a preparation-specific failure nor a small unexplained gain meets the program's strongest scientific ambition.
