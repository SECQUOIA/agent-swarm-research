# Operating-aging prior art: what remains for Ni-dependent retention?

2026-09-15. Independent bounded challenge to the [Ag program](../programs/ag-selective-oxygen-use.md), [revised value review](ag-revised-value-review.md), and [chloride-retention hypothesis](../working/ag-chloride-retention-hypothesis.md). This is a scientific evidence and research-priority review, not a patentability or freedom-to-operate assessment.

## Decision

**Retain a bounded Ni comparison, but put ordinary-operation drift and competition with an established chloride operating choice before a substantial excursion/recovery program.** Demote a stand-alone claim that Ni improves chloride tolerance or that a material-versus-operation comparison is itself new. The surviving contribution is a Ni-dependent relationship that predicts when useful output cannot be recovered or maintained by a defensible operating policy, or when that policy makes Ni unnecessary.

The two modern disclosures materially narrow the opportunity. They connect catalyst formulation, chloride requirements and aging to operating decisions. Their limitations do not erase that prior knowledge. Conversely, neither establishes the proposed Ni-dependent cause, a demonstrated Ni-versus-recovery decision, or preserved productive Ag–Re contact. A consequential original result remains possible; it is an experimental question rather than an established novelty claim.

## 1. Source and evidence audit

The primary source descriptions, examples and tables were read at [US20240279193A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240279193A1/en) and [US20240025869A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240025869A1/en), with the latter cross-checked against [Justia's application text](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20240025869). Original publication PDFs were downloaded from links on those pages. Relevant experimental and fitted figure sheets were inspected. The original HTML/PDF artifacts and derived text are under `/tmp/ag-aging-prior-art/` and were sent to the sole literature agent for the existing intake requests; this review does not write the literature knowledge base.

### A. Underchloriding: US20240279193A1

**Evidence capsule.** Example 1 is explicitly hypothetical. Example 2 uses six same-batch 500 mg aliquots: duplicate chloriding-index settings of 6, 8 and 10.5, after common multistep startup. The formulation contains 33.9 wt% Ag; listed promoter loadings are Cs 779, Li 45, Na 54, sulfate 103, Re 863 and Mn 115 ppm. No Ni is reported. Tests last 28 days at 250 °C and 1480 kPa. Flow is changed around day 6 to target approximately 2 vol% outlet EO; subsequent fits use days 8–28. Higher chloride receives higher gas hourly space velocity (GHSV). The optimum index 9.23 and the highlighted 80.5%-of-optimum case are fitted, not independently tested arms. Figures 6A–F show evolving selectivity, especially downward drift at lower chloride. The reported 88.41 versus 88.81% comparison comes from fitted period-average selectivity. Long-term activity/work-rate curves are extrapolations; reduced sintering is a proposed explanation. [Example 2, paragraphs 0115–0130, Figures 4–9](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240279193A1/en#p-0113).

**What this means for interpretation:**

- **Outlet concentration does not normalize production.** At similar EO concentration, a reactor with greater molar flow can produce more EO per time. A valid material or policy comparison needs flow-corrected net rates and integrated production per initial inventory. It cannot infer equal work rate from the shared concentration target. Changing flow also changes residence time and internal product exposure, so the cross-setting comparison is not a pure chloride perturbation.
- **The baseline is already conditioned.** The common startup improves comparability, but the later evolution is conditional on that history. It does not isolate virgin-surface aging or prove that slow conditioning has ended. A chosen fit origin earlier than the final operating settings is a mathematical reference, not an independently measured fresh activity at each final setting.
- **Fit quality is not lifetime validation.** A plausible short-window decay fit cannot by itself identify the distant asymptote, preserve the same mechanism during continued operation, or validate its interpolation across chloride and flow. The right conclusion is that underchloriding is an existing candidate policy with short-run evidence and a modeled long-run rationale. A commercial-life advantage remains unestablished by these experiments.
- **The selectivity charge must remain time dependent.** Period averages cannot become a one-time startup penalty in the proposed productivity accounting. Charge the measured evolving EO/CO2 split throughout the comparison. Likewise, a short-run apparent production disadvantage cannot be forgiven using an unvalidated later crossover.
- **Replication has a defined scope.** Parallel aliquots help assess reactor/run variation. They do not establish preparation reproducibility. Neither replicate agreement nor a kinetic fit identifies a structural mechanism.

This source therefore removes generic originality from “slightly lower chloride may trade instantaneous selectivity for better aging.” It does not establish the best policy for the proposed material. Copying its relative chloride target would be unjustified because both the reference optimum and the useful production constraint must be measured on the reproduced catalyst.

### B. Stable modifier requirement: US20240025869A1

**Evidence capsule.** Examples compare conventional carriers with fluoride-mineralized alumina (FMA); all listed catalysts contain Ag/Re/W/S/Li/K/Cs, without reported Ni. Two test regimes maintain EO work rates of 200 or 280 kg m−3 h−1 by adjusting temperature and periodically optimizing ethyl chloride. Reported cumulative-production spans reach 1.6–3.0 and 2.1–4.5 kt EO m−3, respectively. FMA optimum chloriding indices stay within 0.8–1.2 of their values at 0.2 kt m−3; conventional optima increase markedly. Catalyst J has two runs. Composition controls C/D/E are approximate: relative Re is 1/1.1/1.1 and Cs 1/0.9/1.1, with equal listed relative W/S/Li/K. Absolute matched loadings and matched Ag distributions are not supplied by that table. There is no separate prospective fixed-modifier arm, recovery comparison, or direct contact measurement. Figures 2 and 5 show temperature histories, not matched end-of-life criteria or selectivity trajectories. [Examples 1–7, Tables 1–3, Figures 2–8](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20240025869A1/en).

**What this means for interpretation:**

- **Treat the aging evidence seriously.** This is a sustained operating record of the location of an optimum, including a repeat-run check. It is stronger evidence for that particular behavior than a fresh operating curve or a proposed composition alone.
- **A stable optimum is not preserved activity.** The operating controller can compensate for activity loss by increasing temperature. Stability of the chloride setting cannot independently establish stable active-site population, low deterioration, or preserved Ag–Re contact.
- **A stable optimum is not constant selectivity.** Its location can remain unchanged while the height of the selectivity curve falls. Without the corresponding selectivity and consumption record, the reported optimum trajectory does not quantify an integrated resource advantage.
- **Policy inference differs from a policy trial.** Periodically finding nearly the same optimum supports a simpler operating choice. It is not a direct comparison of a preselected constant setting against ongoing optimization, including time spent searching and lost production.
- **The carrier relationship is credible within the tested families, but the causal contrast is incomplete.** Approximate promoter matching helps. It does not isolate every consequence of carrier preparation, morphology, impurities and promoter/Ag distribution. A Ni study must not treat carrier-dependent chloride drift as an unexplained universal Re property.
- **Run length is not comparative service life.** An experiment ending later does not demonstrate later failure. Without a common termination criterion and full performance record, cumulative-production endpoints cannot establish a lifetime ranking.

The useful prior knowledge is that a material choice can simplify chloride control through aging. Repeating that broad relationship with Ni would require a substantial additional prediction or benefit to earn expansion.

## 2. The contribution that survives

The [older Ni patent review](ag-older-ni-prior-art.md) already establishes reported Ni-containing promoted-catalyst retention with endpoint reoptimization. The [chloride-retention note](../working/ag-chloride-retention-hypothesis.md) already recognizes reversible working-state changes and nonunique kinetic markers. With the modern operating disclosures added, three weak claims should be removed from the program's justification:

1. Ni works with a complete promoter system and can improve retention.
2. Chloride history affects useful output, so operation should be optimized during aging.
3. Material stabilization can compete with changing the chloride operating policy.

The proposed work has to supply a **new answer within those established questions**. A strong result would determine, on one qualified preparation family, when a measured Ni-dependent change produces a useful benefit after charging the best tested alternative operating policy. It must predict an untested exposure or intervention. A structurally supported explanation would strengthen that result; a spectrum or kinetic marker alone would not substitute for it.

Two outcomes could warrant expansion:

- A defined ordinary-operation policy preserves or restores the Ni-free material sufficiently that Ni no longer improves useful output, and a measured chemical or functional relationship predicts this substitution under a new condition.
- Ni retains a useful advantage under the competing policy, and the distinguishing state/exposure relationship predicts where that advantage persists or fails.

Neither outcome requires proving an elementary Ag-to-Re oxygen-transfer step. Neither is established merely by correcting historical controls. A useful negative result must resolve an actual material/operation choice, not repackage a null comparison as a general principle.

## 3. Smallest revised sequence

**1. Qualify the pair and establish ordinary drift.** Retain one functioning promoted reference, one justified Ni treatment and its processing control. Measure output and the chloride operating curve with preparation reproducibility where decisive. Document support type and treatment, promoter inventories and Ag size. Use continued ordinary operation to establish whether useful performance actually drifts, how much it costs, and on what observed timescale. Do not create a carrier library to answer this first question.

**2. Test the cheapest credible operating alternative.** If drift or a Ni-dependent advantage is consequential, use the measured curve to choose one useful lower-chloride policy on the Ni-free reference, or another documented ordinary policy if lower chloride has no usable production window. Compare it with the proposed Ni choice over a common observed horizon. Record actual EO production, selectivity/consumption, temperature, feed and intervention costs. Do not require a full material × chloride × support factorial at the outset.

Keep causal and practical comparisons distinct. Matched temperature/feed/flow histories test the material effect under that exposure. Separately selected policies compare useful choices; temperature or flow differences are then part of the policy and must be reported. If the policy result creates an attribution ambiguity that matters, add only the contrast that resolves it.

**3. Use recovery to diagnose a demonstrated burden.** A small excursion can calibrate response times early when needed, but a substantial excursion campaign should follow a connection to ordinary drift or a documented recurring disturbance. Compare a proposed recovery with ordinary continuation. Preserve separate trajectories if recovery changes later aging. A rare or invented pulse cannot supply the economic burden that makes its cure valuable.

**4. Predict one held-out decision.** Choose one new duration, product condition or recovery history from the observed distinction. Lock the intended operating settings and prediction before testing. Require a practically consequential difference in observed integrated output or resource use, not a benefit appearing only beyond a fitted lifetime extrapolation. Add Re-free material or state-specific measurements only when they discriminate the explanation needed for that decision.

## 4. Priority and stopping rule

**Ag can remain the next bounded investigation; a substantial Ni-retention program is not yet earned.** These sources narrow originality while improving the comparator: the program can now ask whether Ni adds value beyond a concrete operating alternative. They do not justify declaring the remaining mechanism original, nor automatically promote another portfolio candidate that has not met its own evidence gates.

Stop or demote if the pair has no consequential ordinary-operation difference, a familiar chloride adjustment accounts for it without a new useful prediction, or the only advantage appears under an unsupported upset frequency. Also demote if distinguishing the surviving alternatives requires an open-ended preparation or characterization campaign. Reproducible excursion tolerance can remain a bounded operating observation without becoming the central research program.

Confidence is moderate that the revised comparison can be informative if the material and reactor are available, low that Ni specifically preserves productive Ag–Re proximity, and low that the present record establishes a competitive practical gain. The source search here is deliberately bounded; it does not establish exhaustive scientific novelty.
