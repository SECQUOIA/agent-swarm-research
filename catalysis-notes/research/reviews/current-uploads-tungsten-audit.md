# Tungsten program audit after the newly supplied originals

The tungsten program needs substantive corrections, but the recommendation remains **exploratory hold**. The new originals strengthen experimental definition and some comparators; they do not select a causal intervention that links sugar coordination, W retention and useful glycol output. The reason for holding is now scientific and practical, rather than waiting for the Li, El Mohammad, Qiao, Liu or Zhang main papers.

This audit covers the tungsten-related sources in the two September 16 inbox-promotion runs, including the newly supplied neighboring support, sugar-network and coordination papers. It compares them with the current [program](../working/program-development/oxygenate-program-alternative.md), [mannose probe](../working/program-development/tungsten-causal-intervention.md), [process accounting](../calculations/tungsten-process-burden.md), [carbon comparator review](tungsten-carbon-comparator-review.md) and [ash comparator](../calculations/tungsten-ash-recovery-comparator.md). It does not initiate new ideas or claim a new literature-wide novelty search. Original PDFs and extracted full text were used; decisive Li main/SI tables, Li Figure 4, Zhang Figure 1 and El Mohammad Table 1 were also inspected visually. No experiments were performed.

## Corrections that change the scientific record

**Li's preleachate is not demonstrated to supply 29.5% EG yield.** The previously accessible SI gives 29.5% EG for Ni/M plus catalyst-derived preleachate. The newly available main Table 1 gives **27.1% for Ni/M alone**. The liquid by itself gives trace EG. The observed increment is only 2.4 percentage points; a paired yield uncertainty is not established here. Thus the earlier description of an “active” or “productive” preleachate was too strong. The experiment remains prior art for testing a preleachate, but cannot establish a substantial homogeneous contribution or its absence during the hot sugar reaction. Preleaching uses water/H2 without cellulose. [Li main](../../literature/papers/li2020-enhanced-ni-w-ti-catalyst/original.pdf), PDF pp. 2, 4, 6; [Li SI](../../literature/papers/li2020-supporting-information-for-enhanced-ni/original.pdf), p. 9/Table S3.

**Li's ppm values do not establish negligible cumulative W export.** Figure 4b labels seven postreaction W concentrations: 28, 22, 19, 21, 18, 20 and 19 ppm. The methods and Table 1 use 0.15 g catalyst, nominal 20 wt% W, and 40 mL liquid. Applying that volume to each run gives a conditional 5.88 mg W exported, or about 20% of the nominal 30 mg starting inventory, before wash and handling losses. This is a scale check, not a measured balance. Figure 3 instead says 100 mL, which would produce a 49% conditional estimate. The 40 mL basis has support in both methods and Table 1, but the inconsistency cannot be silently erased. Seven stable-yield batches with filtration/water washing/363 K air drying are useful evidence, not continuous lifetime or zero-makeup operation. [Li main](../../literature/papers/li2020-enhanced-ni-w-ti-catalyst/original.pdf), pp. 2, 4–6. The [process note](../calculations/tungsten-process-burden.md) preserves the assumptions and both branches.

**Li does not isolate Ti–O–W bond strength as a causal variable.** XPS/Raman and W dispersion support the proposed linkage, but the preparations also change carbon, area/pore structure, W domains and Ni state. Recovered-solid characterization does not establish the claim that HxWO3 stays attached throughout every turnover. The paper supplies a useful retained-catalyst comparator; it does not furnish a clean anchoring intervention to copy. [Li main](../../literature/papers/li2020-enhanced-ni-w-ti-catalyst/original.pdf), pp. 2–7.

**Zhang's W20O58 yield is more stable than WO3, but it is not invariant.** The main text reports combined EG+PG yield decreasing **70.4→61.4 C%** over five runs, a 9-point decline, despite the abstract's “stable” language. Initial EG alone is 55.6 C%. The reported cumulative W loss is about 7% versus 27% for WO3. Conditions are 1.4 mmol glucose in 100 mL water, 0.25 g oxide plus 0.25 g 2% Ru/AC, 518 K, 4 MPa H2 and 10 minutes per run. This is a dilute, catalyst-intensive comparison. The cooled fifth-cycle filtrate gives 7.8% EG and 78.5% sorbitol with fresh Ru/AC, which does not quantify hot mobile turnover. Constant bulk phase also coexists with changed surface oxidation-state populations after use. [Zhang main](../../literature/papers/zhang2024-durable-w20o58-species-catalyzed-cc/original.pdf), pp. 2–5/Figure 1. The prior stable-yield wording is corrected in the current program.

**The homogeneous mechanistic evidence is stronger, but still conditional.** El Mohammad's main article combines concentration orders, pH dependence, deoxy-sugar comparisons and XANES. It supports an O1,O3 mononuclear cleavage complex and predominantly W(VI) under the studied homogeneous conditions. It is not a direct count of the turnover intermediate. At 150 °C, 5 mM sugar, 1.3 mM W and pH 5, Table 1 gives initial net GA formation rates of 0.048 and 0.086 mmol L−1 min−1 from glucose and mannose, respectively. This reinforces rejection of an inactive-mannose premise. The methods state uncertainties arise from tests repeated up to three times; the former SI-only assessment should not imply that the whole study lacks reaction replication. H2/N2 switching has little effect without a hydrogenation catalyst, and the authors expressly leave reduced species with such a function open. [El Mohammad main](../../literature/papers/mohammad2024-identification-of-key-active-species/original.pdf), pp. 3, 5, 10–14.

The main protocol now makes the limited soluble reference reproducible: 10 mL sugar solution is injected into 50 mL preheated catalyst solution, followed by 1100 rpm mixing. Its HPLC method coelutes fructose and threose. Missing carbon and secondary GA chemistry remain analytical constraints, particularly for a mixed-feed probe. Net GA accumulation and feed ancestry do not uniquely identify direct mannose cleavage.

**The bronze-state literature has substantive controls, not only a phase-warning anecdote.** Liu reports immediate wet-transfer XRD and reversible air oxidation, size/activity contrasts, 0.05% postreaction W loss, weak filtrate function, and water-only conductivity evidence for heterogeneous activity. These observations deserve weight. They still do not uniquely exclude sugar-stabilized or short-lived mobile W: size changes dissolution kinetics, recovered filtrate follows cooling, and the conductivity experiment omits sugar and cannot universally detect nonionic species. The 205 °C cellulose comparison and Zhang's 245 °C glucose comparison also differ materially. Neither establishes universal bronze solubility or insolubility. [Liu main](../../literature/papers/liu2023-unraveling-the-active-states-of/original.pdf), pp. 2–6. Qiao's explicit-water DFT/AIMD study supplies a proposed low-coordinate W(V) homolytic pathway, not an experimental resolution of this disagreement. [Qiao main](../../literature/papers/qiao2023-breaking-the-c-c-bond/original.pdf), model construction and pathway analysis, pp. 2–7.

## What should happen next

1. Keep the broad tungsten program on hold. Do not launch an anchor/support library or a spectroscopy campaign simply because main articles are now available.
2. If an existing platform warrants a small feasibility study, reproduce one soluble reference and one retained comparator with validated carbonyl recovery, full liquid-volume records and W/Ni/support-element accounting. Li's cumulative concentration series gives a concrete retention quantity to check. Resolve its volume inconsistency before treating the published protocol as exact.
3. Keep the mannose replacement probe optional. Its adverse export direction remains weakly supported; productive mannose is now quantitatively better established. Relevant feed/recycle mannose and an informative carbon-recovery window remain necessary.
4. Preserve the fair comparison against soluble W management and selective hydrogenation. Demonstrating mobile W, a changed ex situ W state or a modest yield increase alone does not establish a consequential improvement.

**Confidence in the hypothesis:** moderate that substrate coordination, support chemistry and W mobility can interact; low for the specified adverse mannose-export direction and for a selected anchoring intervention. **Confidence in experimental informativeness:** moderate for inventory/export and useful-output accounting once analytical recovery is demonstrated; lower for unique surface-versus-mobile turnover attribution. **Confidence in practical improvement:** still unestablished. The new originals sharpen these limits without making the program uninteresting or automatically competitive with the higher-priority candidates.

## Availability corrections and edits

The current program, conditional intervention and process-accounting documents were updated. Historical reviews retain their earlier source states and should be read through this audit. The important source-dependent reading tasks for Li 2020, ACS Catalysis 2024, Qiao 2023, Liu 2023, Zhang 2024, Ni-MFI 2025 and the two Fuel interface papers are now resolved for the claims above. More detailed future reproduction may still require SI or clarification; this is distinct from missing main originals.

The Zhang 2015 W/Ru glucose-hydrogenation inhibition paper, DOI 10.1016/S2095-4956(15)60278-9, remains locally metadata-only and unread. Its earlier abstract-level caveat remains. No stronger claim was added from it. KB corrections were routed to the single literature worker through root: AMT identity/model-validation pooling; milled-feed wording; W20O58 decline; Carrier authors; and the Li preleachate baseline. The program audit itself did not modify the KB.
