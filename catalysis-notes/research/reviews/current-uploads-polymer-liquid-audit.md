# Uploaded-literature audit: polymer and liquid Ti-zeolite proposals

2026-09-16. Scope: the current polymer clean-feed/makeup proposal, its alternative ethylene-pressure intervention, and the liquid Ti-BEA model-transfer proposal. This audit uses the retained originals promoted in `2026-09-16-inbox-65-promotions` and `2026-09-16-inbox-remaining-promotions`, plus the already retained pressure-comparator thesis whose package still had an unread placeholder. No new research direction was developed. No knowledge-base files were edited by this reviewer.

**The recommendations survive, but “nothing needs changing” would be wrong.** The final polymer model supersedes an unfinished thesis model; the pressure-comparator thesis is available and imposes an additional recovery control; direct product-induced solvent spectral changes already exist; and the liquid baseline must accommodate known unresolved secondary-rate behavior. These corrections narrow interpretation and improve experiments without making either proposal a flagship program.

## Polymer evidence

**Chen, Sadow and Peters, ACS Catalysis 2024, DOI 10.1021/acscatal.4c00465.** The [main article](../../literature/papers/chen2024-a-microkinetic-model-for-isomerizing/original.pdf) is now available. Its pp.4–8 do more than repeat Chapter 6 of Chen's 2023 dissertation. The final model solves finite nonequilibrium isomerization rates; the quasi-equilibrium solution is a numerical check in a limit the authors explicitly call nonphysical for their restricted alpha/beta-olefin representation (p.6). Physical applicability is limited to isomerization slower than metathesis, avoiding migration far down a chain before cleavage. Deactivation and ethylene diffusion are omitted; catalyst-specific rate parameters and solubility assumptions remain necessary. Thus, criticizing the published model merely for the thesis's unfinished quasi-equilibrium construction is incorrect.

Figures 5–6, pp.6–7, and Conclusions p.8 place the modeled Conk 2022 conditions near maximal propylene generation and approximately saturated chain-end/ethylene kinetics. Figure 6 still places the marked concentration before the far-right inhibited region; the revision is not evidence that lowering ethylene helps at that point. High ethylene can inhibit at lower chain-end concentrations in the model. The proposed inhibitory metallacyclobutane population is a model interpretation, not measured W speciation in Na/W. The final article explicitly proposes varying ethylene pressure, initial dehydrogenation and initial molecular weight (p.8), which further closes generic intervention claims. Catalyst retention and replacement are not modeled.

**Guironnet and Peters, J. Phys. Chem. A 2020, DOI 10.1021/acs.jpca.0c01363.** The [main article](../../literature/papers/guironnet2020-tandem-catalysts-for-polyethylene-upcycling/original.pdf), pp.1–6, already treats evolving chain populations, relative metathesis/isomerization rates and product formation. Page 5 explicitly allows time-dependent ethylene concentration by numerical solution with updated coefficients. A pressure waveform or polymer population-balance model is therefore not an originality claim by itself. Its first-order chain-end treatment also cannot replace the concentration-dependent final Chen model indiscriminately.

**Nicholas Mason Wang, 2022 dissertation, _Homogeneous Flow Catalysis Strategies for Lab-Scale Kinetic Investigation and Reaction Development_.** The [original](../../literature/papers/chapter0000-chapter-4-tandem-polyethylene-ethenolysis/original.pdf) is valid despite the package's stale unidentified/unread status and 403 retrieval wording at audit start. The title and author appear on PDF p.1. Chapter 4 contains relevant source evidence:

- PDF pp.72–76 (printed pp.67–71) compare continuously supplied Ru metathesis catalyst, added Pt–Re/SiO2 or molecular Ru isomerization catalyst, and fresh catalyst cofeeds. Declining output does not automatically imply metathesis deactivation; changing isomerization supply changes output. These are different materials and unsaturated low-molecular-weight chains, not the Na/W system.
- PDF p.75 reports little effect when reducing ethylene from 60 to 14.7 PSI, interpreted as isomerization limitation. Its cited comparisons, Figures 4.9, 4.10 and C7, change catalyst dose/feed and/or temperature as well (PDF pp.74, 76, 135). This establishes that pressure reduction was studied, but is not a clean isolated pressure-order determination. Do not export a flat order or quantitative pressure optimum to Na/W.
- PDF pp.77–79 report chain-end enrichment and lower peak output on reusing polymer; Appendix C, PDF p.136, shows the polymer/catalyst solution was retained for two days, so this is not a clean fresh-versus-aged-feed causal separation. More importantly, PDF p.78 explicitly states that depressurization and 2–3 h cooling permit isomerization and fragment recombination. A recovered unsaturation distribution may therefore differ from the reacting distribution. The current proposal now requires a recovery/quench control and limits mechanistic assignments if preservation fails.

These originals offer a stronger mechanistic vocabulary and a more demanding prior-art comparison. They do not establish selective loss of Na-mediated initiation, a recoverable ethylene mismatch after Na/W aging, or practical savings at matched accepted polymer-carbon output and full cycle time. The original three-charge fresh-Na benchmark, carbon attribution requirement and finite first stage remain appropriate.

## Liquid Ti-zeolite evidence

**Ayla et al., ACS Catalysis 2021, 11, 139–154, DOI 10.1021/acscatal.0c03394** ([original](../../literature/papers/ayla2020-alkene-epoxidations-with-h2o2-over/original.pdf)). The stable package says `ayla2020`; the PDF's journal citation is 2021. Pages 8–9, Scheme 2 and Figure 6 establish peroxide-derived versus epoxide-rich surface regimes, inverse epoxide dependence in the latter regime, and the competition between productive oxidation and peroxide loss. Page 10 discusses solvent displacement/reorganization and adsorption-based correlations. This reinforces, rather than weakens, the need for a known-network baseline. A resting-state switch or product inhibition is not the proposed advance.

**Kwon et al., Angewandte Chemie 2024, DOI 10.1002/anie.202405950** ([original](../../literature/papers/kwon2024-influence-of-ti-incorporated-zeolite/original.pdf)). Pages 2–3 and Scheme 1 establish epoxidation, ring opening, diol oxidation and aldehyde oxidation in vapor-fed pores containing acetonitrile. Pages 6–7 show that ring-opening kinetics are strongly affected by peroxide and exhibit approximately half-order PO dependence over part of the range. The authors explicitly state that their proposed elementary scheme does not fully explain that dependence. They also cannot assign all inhibition uniquely to PO rather than later products (p.6). This is a concrete model limitation, not a discovered Ti-BEA liquid anomaly.

Consequently, the baseline must first explain the separately measured component data. An independently estimated empirical sublinear rate relation can be frozen for integral prediction if needed; refusing known behavior and then interpreting the resulting residual as product-induced solvent organization would be invalid. This does not require adding an unconstrained fit to the validation trajectory. The source's reported 80–110% carbon balance (p.2) also cannot set an automatically adequate tolerance for our smaller proposed effect.

**Torres et al., J. Catal. 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.jcat.2024.115288** ([original](../../literature/papers/torres2024-solvent-stabilization-of-alkene-epoxidation/original.pdf)). The article reports Ti-MFI aqueous-methanol effects over 0.005–5 M water, with nonmonotonic adsorption and activation enthalpies. Figures 8–9 and p.12 explicitly establish that adsorption enthalpy is an incomplete descriptor: solvent environments during product calorimetry differ from the Ti–OOH environment during catalysis. The weak negative relation differs from the positive BEA correlation. Adsorption enthalpy is neither a universal rate predictor nor a direct measure of adsorption free energy.

**Chris Torres, 2023 dissertation, _Solvent and Surface Effects on Alkene Oxidation Catalysis over Transition Metal Incorporated Zeolites_** ([original](../../literature/papers/record2023-torres-thesis-on-liquid-zeolite/original.pdf)). PDF p.2 resolves the title and author. Chapter 2, PDF pp.58–60 (printed pp.42–44), Figure 2.6, already contains direct ATR-IR evidence that adding 0.1 M epoxyhexane changes hydrogen-bond features in Ti-MFI containing aqueous methanol or acetonitrile (1 M water, 313 K). The response differs with solvent and defect density. Thus, product-induced solvent reorganization cannot be described only as speculation without a published experimental precedent. However, these nonreacting adsorption spectra do not establish that product accumulation changes useful integral reaction outcomes beyond the known baseline in Ti-BEA. The proposal's remaining hypothesis must retain this narrower scope.

The thesis's Chapter 4, especially PDF pp.122–124, also identifies combined product inhibition and transport for larger C7/C8 alkenes in MFI. This reinforces the existing restriction to one tested catalyst/solvent window and the empirical transport controls. It does not justify expanding to new topologies or alkenes. Chapter 5's preliminary ring-opening work uses Sn zeolites (PDF p.146), not direct validation of the proposed Ti-BEA system.

**Leonhardt, Head-Gordon and Bell, ACS Catalysis 2024, DOI 10.1021/acscatal.3c06073** ([original](../../literature/papers/leonhardt2024-the-effects-of-tioh-site/original.pdf)). The simpler model in Figure 8 gives strong product inhibition and distortion-sensitive activity. The expanded mechanism allows epoxide to remain bound while another accessible facet reacts. Pages 12–14 and Figures 11–12 show that coadsorption narrows available geometries and largely removes the predicted dependence of activity on initial distortion. It agrees with measured reaction orders and barriers; direct observation of this gas-phase coadsorbed pathway is still proposed, not reported (p.13). Sites with only one accessible facet remain an explicit exception. The existing competing-mechanism warning is strengthened, but this gas-phase Ti/SiO2 model must not be represented as experimental Ti-zeolite restructuring.

**Wang et al., Catalysts 2020, DOI 10.3390/catal10010015** ([original](../../literature/papers/wang2019-influence-of-impurities-in-a/original.pdf)). Page 9 establishes that an actual HPPO pilot-plant recycle solvent was tested, alongside individual impurity simulations. Its reported composition includes 3.33 wt% fusel alcohols, 0.01 wt% aldehydes/ketones, 0.02 wt% esters, 0.66 wt% acetals, 6 ppm total N and 3.4 wt% water. The powder tests use 48 h solvent preadsorption followed by drying before reaction; spectroscopy/desorption is a separate protocol (pp.9–10). Competitive adsorption is the authors' interpretation, supported by uptake/spectroscopy, rather than a fitted reversible in-reactor inhibition law for the current liquid proposal. Generic recycle-solvent impurity testing remains preempted, but these data should not be borrowed as quantitative Ti-BEA component parameters.

**Thiele and Roland 1997, DOI 10.1016/S1381-1169(96)00266-X**, and **Wang et al. 2007, DOI 10.1016/j.molcata.2007.03.068** ([1997 original](../../literature/papers/thiele1997-propylene-epoxidation-with-hydrogen-peroxide/original.pdf), [2007 original](../../literature/papers/wang2007-deactivation-and-regeneration-of-titanium/original.pdf)). The earlier work documents salt treatment, pore blockage and peroxide regeneration (pp.2–6). The later isopropanol study analyzes recovered deposits and compares regeneration (pp.2–7), distinguishing oligomer/ether deposits from reversible competition. Its Ti-loss claim is inferred from structure and incomplete activity recovery, not a direct dissolved-Ti inventory. The proposal correctly retains direct Ti analysis and carbon-retention controls. Neither original demonstrates that a short washout establishes long-term stability.

## Source integrity and reading limits

This audit consulted extracted source text, not only the short package summaries. Reading was targeted to decisive sections: Chen main pp.1–8; Guironnet main pp.1–6; Wang dissertation Chapter 4 and cited Appendix C experiments; Kwon main pp.1–8; Ayla pp.1, 7–12; Torres article pp.1–3, 8–13 and thesis Chapters 2–4 selections/associated figures; Leonhardt pp.1, 9–14; impurity paper pp.2–10; 1997 main pp.1–6; 2007 main pp.1–7. This is not a claim to have read the complete dissertations or each Supporting Information file. Decisive original PDF pages were rendered and visually checked: Chen p.7; Wang dissertation pp.75, 78; Torres dissertation p.59; Torres article p.12; Kwon p.7; Leonhardt p.13; impurity paper p.9; Wang2007 p.7. The spectral and model interpretations remain those of the original authors except where this audit explicitly draws a methodological consequence.

The following KB corrections were sent to root for the single literature worker: Wang pressure-thesis identity and stale unread/403 status; Torres dissertation identity; Ayla journal year versus stable slug/online year; Leonhardt's abbreviated note omitting the expanded-model removal of distortion dependence; and Wang2007's inferred, rather than directly measured, Ti loss. The pressure thesis was already retained, so no new retrieval is needed to use the reviewed sections. Conk's embargoed dissertation body and the unresolved original patent regeneration figure remain outside this audit's evidence; neither is silently treated as resolved.

The safe conclusion is narrower than either blanket approval or rejection: correct the source-version and experimental-control details, retain the bounded proposals, and demand actual informative measurements before promoting either program.
