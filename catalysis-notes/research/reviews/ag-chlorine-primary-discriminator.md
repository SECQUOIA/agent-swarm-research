# What existing chlorine kinetics leaves unresolved in the Ag decision

2026-09-15. Bounded primary-source audit supporting the [portfolio selection review](portfolio-current-priority-audit.md). No experiments were performed. Literature ingestion remains with the sole literature worker.

**2026-09-16 update:** the uploaded Harris2019, Chen2018, Iyer2021, Santos2024 and Iyer2023 main texts have now been checked in the [post-upload audit](post-upload-ag-audit.md). The evidence and current access status below incorporate those checks; the dated retrieval table preserves the original search history.

## Decision

**The inspected sources already establish the main chlorine/product framework. They do not, in the inspected material, predict whether Ni changes the finite production cost of recovery or the residual loss after an ordinary operating history.** That missing application is not yet an original substantial research opportunity. First establish a consequential Ni contrast that survives a credible operating alternative, as the [program](../programs/ag-selective-oxygen-use.md) now requires.

The surviving quantitative question is narrow:

> After calibrating each material's fresh chlorine/product response, can those fixed relationships predict its net EO and CO2 trajectories, recovery cost and subsequent output under one withheld, practically relevant history? If not, does one independently supported additional relationship predict the residual well enough to change the preferred material or operating policy?

Failure of a literature model transferred without appropriate calibration would not establish new chemistry. Conversely, successful prediction by established chlorine/product relationships could close the proposed retention program even if Ni changes a fitted coefficient.

## Primary evidence and limits

### Harris and Bhan 2019: chlorine exchange already has quantitative kinetics

The institutional primary abstract reports steady propane oxychlorination during ethylene oxidation, with chlorine-exchange turnover rates around `10^-4 s^-1`, versus ethylene-oxidation rates around `10^-1 s^-1` on the stated surface-Ag basis. Both deposition and removal are kinetically relevant. A derived isotherm predicts chlorine coverages after steady ethyl-chloride/ethane and ethyl-chloride/propane operation. Different ethylene/CO2 sensitivities suggest different site requirements for chlorine exchange and epoxidation. The uploaded [main article](../../literature/papers/harris2019-kinetics-of-chlorine-deposition-and/original.pdf), Sections 3.3–3.4 and conclusions, confirms these findings; the SI remains unretrieved. These findings already defeat a novelty claim based merely on slow chlorine adjustment or separate chlorine and oxidation cycles. They do not by themselves give an arbitrary-history recovery prediction for the proposed Ni pair. [Primary abstract](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/kinetics-of-chlorine-deposition-and-removal-over-promoted-silver-/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2019.09.037).

### Chen, Harris and Bhan 2018: product effects are material- and condition-dependent

The primary abstract describes a highly promoted 35 wt% Ag catalyst. Increasing the empirical chloriding factor changes ethylene and oxygen orders. At 1–5 mol% CO2, CO2 inhibits combustion more strongly than EO formation, giving higher EO selectivity; EO at 0–0.5 mol% and acetaldehyde at 0–1.7 ppm do not change the reported rates. This establishes relevant product/chloride coupling, not a universal beneficial direction for lowering product concentrations. Its empirical chloriding factor does not guarantee constant actual chlorine coverage when other feed variables change. The uploaded [main article](../../literature/papers/chen2018-kinetics-of-ethylene-epoxidation-on/original.pdf) confirms these findings; the SI remains unretrieved. Its Figures 9–12 also show a 0.5–1.6 percentage-point EO-selectivity decrease with increasing contact time despite the null EO-cofeed result. The authors retain both sequential loss and changing primary branching as explanations. Their 0.6–1.8% EO-combustion fraction is the amount sufficient to explain the loss, not a directly measured flux. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29710392/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/chem.201801356).

**Inference for the program:** a product-feedback explanation must use the actual pair's measured responses. Neither CO2 inhibition nor a change in outlet selectivity alone predicts that Ni's lower combustion improves the selective channel.

### Iyer and Bhan 2021: reactor prediction is already prior art; the recovered SI bounds its demonstrated scope

The primary abstract explicitly combines ethylene oxidation, chlorine deposition/removal and support-mediated EO degradation, using kinetics measured at fixed chlorine coverage to predict packed-bed rates and selectivity. A new packed-bed coupling calculation is not the missing advance. The uploaded [main text](../../literature/papers/iyer2021-interdependencies-among-ethylene-oxidation-and/original.pdf), especially Section 3.4, has now been checked alongside the complete 17-page SI and its original plots. [Main DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.1c03493), [original SI](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/31609847).

**Three source observations matter:**

1. **The source already reports slow startup.** SI S3, PDF pp.5–6, shows EO rates evolving for about 18 h before the steady measurement. The proposed explanation is restructuring, citing van Hoof. A delayed EO response is therefore insufficient evidence for a new Ni/Re retention state.
2. **Chlorine coverage is operationally inferred.** SI S4, PDF pp.6–7, first establishes steady reaction, then switches to ethane/O2 recirculation and measures ethyl chloride. Four removal cycles support a power-law tail; ordinary measurements use the first removal cycle plus extrapolation to infinite cycles. The amount is divided by surface silver determined through N2O uptake after H2 reduction. This is not a nondestructive snapshot of every working chlorine population. The tail law and denominator require validation before transferring to a different Ni formulation or aged state. The method remains useful; its intervention and normalization must be represented honestly.
3. **The demonstrated reactor predictions have a finite error scale.** S9.3/Figure S10, PDF p.16 (printed p.13), compares nine integral steady conditions after 18 h, at 513 K, 5.3 bar and 4–6% ethylene conversion. The printed measured/predicted selectivity pairs are `(88.5,85.9)`, `(89.7,85.8)`, `(90.7,89.2)`, `(90.3,88.8)`, `(89.5,84.5)`, `(90.2,88.8)`, `(89.0,88.8)`, `(90.2,88.4)`, `(90.5,89.2)`, in percent. Their differences are 0.2–5.0 percentage points, all with lower predicted selectivity. These are point residuals, not uncertainty limits. The main discusses curve-fitting and measurement errors as possible sources of deviations but does not establish their unique cause. This does not negate the model's useful broad prediction, but prevents treating it as a demonstrated sub-percentage-point reference for a small Ni gain.

The SI shows independently measured coverage versus a steady coverage expression for vinyl chloride and mixed alkyl chlorides (S7), and derives a **steady spatial** plug-flow material balance (S9). The main Section 3.4, equations 5–10 and Figures 8–9 resolve the earlier uncertainty about validation: separately assessed oxidation, chlorine-exchange and support-degradation kinetics compute local chlorine coverage and rates while integrating from inlet conditions; predicted outlet flows and average coverage are compared with integral-reactor measurements, including a catalyst-loading series. Credit this demonstrated steady reactor prediction. The source does not establish blind preregistration or arbitrary temporal recovery prediction, and no pointwise refitting is inferred.

### Santos et al. 2024: industrial chlorine dynamics are the intended framework

The primary perspective abstract explicitly includes formulation, catalyst age, temperature, O2, water and CO2 in addition to alkyl chloride/alkane concentrations. It says customary gas-phase chlorination metrics are incomplete and describes using chlorine chemistry in kinetics/reactor models. Thus, adding water, temperature, age or gradients to a generic chlorine-state story is not an original premise. The retrieved eight-page SI contains DFT settings, reaction thermochemistry and Ag cluster models; it supplies no Ni/no-Ni aging or finite-recovery experiment. The uploaded [main perspective](../../literature/papers/santos2024-the-complex-chlorination-effects-on/original.pdf) contains measured promoter comparisons at 11 bara (Figure 1) and operating-response families at 19 barg (Figures 5–7 and 9). Temperature, O2, water and CO2 change the chloride response. These experiments belong in the established baseline. The framework retains empirical corrections; its approximately invariant optimum coverage follows stated assumptions, including negligible secondary EO loss and common functional forms for primary pathways, and is not a universal measured rule. The inspected main supplies no prospective Ni/no-Ni aging or recovery prediction. [Main DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.4c01764), [original SI](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/47419939).

### Iyer and Bhan 2023: titration supplies bounds, not a unique intact-state census

The full publisher HTML, including methods and tables, was read. TFE uptake inhibits EO synthesis under working feed. The reported 52–69 μmol/g range uses total uptake, described as an upper estimate because inactive adsorption cannot be excluded. Linear-region and initial-slope estimates differ substantially. Table 1 averages are 56.1, 29.7 and 20.7 μmol/g for the three methods. Chlorine coverages are calculated from previous models; they are not independently measured during every titration. Main methods use a Cs/Li/Na/Re/Mn/sulfate-promoted 35 wt% Ag material without listed Ni, at 513 K and 5.3 atm. This supports testing function-linked accessibility when needed, not transferring one universal TFE:site stoichiometry to aged Ni/Re. The destructive titration is unsuitable as an innocuous serial readout. The [original main PDF](../../literature/papers/iyer2023-chemical-titration-of-promoted-ag/original.pdf) is now retained and its bounds and Table 1 were checked; only the SI remains unretrieved. [Publisher full text](https://chemistry-europe.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cctc.202300329).

## The minimum predictive comparison

This is a proposed analysis structure, not a model claimed validated by the sources above.

For a fixed material and a locally defined reactor state, a chlorine balance has the form

`dN_Cl/dt = R_deposition − R_removal`,

with EO and CO2 rates depending on chlorine state, temperature, reactants and products. A steady isotherm determines where the difference vanishes. It does not by itself determine the approach time: multiplying both opposing rates by the same factor preserves the isotherm while changing the relaxation. Existing absolute exchange kinetics can constrain that time on the original material; they cannot be assumed unchanged by Ni, aging or a changed site inventory.

Use the simplest demonstrated baseline adequate to the actual pair. A measured fresh response and one local relaxation may suffice to ask whether a useful transient lies on the known operating relation. Building a complete elementary-step or spatial model is not an entry requirement. If gradients matter, incorporate them before attributing a residual to an extra catalyst state. Do not add a new hidden-state parameter merely to absorb every mismatch.

A consequential prospective result would predict, before retuning:

- the sign and resolved magnitude of the **integrated net EO difference** between the chosen Ni and Ni-free policies over a stated observed horizon;
- the production cost of any prescribed recovery, including ordinary continuation as its control;
- one withheld duration, product burden or recovery history that would change the material/policy choice.

A persistent endpoint after one recovery is only persistence under that protocol. A different response at the same inferred chlorine coverage could reflect the inference itself, accessibility, spatial distributions, product effects or structure; it does not uniquely prove Ni–Re contact loss.

## What would close the source gap—and what would earn new work

The most decision-relevant source observation would be a **prospective Ni/no-Ni or genuinely comparable formulation-dependent recovery prediction**, with measured history, fixed parameters and subsequent productive output. A validated ordinary chlorine/product model reproducing that contrast would demote the proposed extra-state explanation. An existing formulation-dependent aging/recovery law tied to useful output could also occupy the proposed contribution even without Ni.

A source that merely omits Ni does not establish novelty. Neither does a failure to retrieve its full text. The current positive case remains an experimental opportunity: determine whether a consequential contrast survives existing operating choices, and only then ask which missing quantitative relationship would change that decision. No substantial mechanism campaign is earned by this audit alone.
