# Ag program after the literature uploads

2026-09-16. Independent, bounded audit of the existing Ag retention program. No new research direction, experiment, or knowledge-base mutation. This report supplements the earlier reviews rather than silently changing their historical reading record.

**Implementation status, 2026-09-16:** all research-file corrections below are incorporated in the Ag program, core-design, chlorine, isotope and working-state reviews and their working/calculation references. The program now gives one minimal sequence with explicit baseline, policy comparison, null interpretation and expansion/stopping gates. Knowledge-base note corrections and shared status/index updates remain assigned to the sole literature worker and root agent; this reviewer has not edited those files.

**Independent design check incorporated:** the required causal comparison is the Ni/no-Ni pair on one promoted formulation; Re dependence remains a conditional extension. Equal fresh output does not prevent the predefined retention test when both formulations meet the useful-output floor. Absence of a consequential difference after that finite horizon closes the comparison.

## Decision

**Make targeted corrections and strengthen the existing baseline; retain the bounded Ni-dependent output-retention comparison.** The newly available main texts strengthen established chlorine/product and isotope prior art. They do not establish a consequential Ni-dependent gain, a predictive Ni-aging relationship, or grounds to promote this comparison into a substantial research program. Confidence that the initial matched comparison can be informative remains conditional on a functioning platform; confidence in practical improvement remains low.

The most important substantive changes are to credit the demonstrated Iyer reactor prediction fully, include the experimental content of the Santos perspective, and prevent the new TFE literature note from presenting an upper-bound uptake as an exact working-site census. The program's existing caution about microscopic attribution, recovery, product effects, and isotope transfer is largely confirmed.

## 1. Iyer 2021 resolves the reactor-validation uncertainty

The [earlier chlorine review](ag-chlorine-primary-discriminator.md) says the unread main article is needed to determine whether local chlorine coverage was predicted or separately measured for reactor validation. The [now available original](../../literature/papers/iyer2021-interdependencies-among-ethylene-oxidation-and/original.pdf), Section 3.4, PDF pp.9–11, resolves this point:

- The authors combine separately examined oxidation, chlorine-exchange, and support-mediated EO-degradation kinetics.
- Equations 5–10 compute local chlorine coverage and reaction rates from local partial pressures while integrating forward from the inlet.
- Figures 8–9 compare predicted outlet flows and average chlorine coverage with integral-reactor measurements, including a catalyst-loading series.

**Required correction:** remove the current unresolved statement that validation may have used separately measured coverage at each condition. Describe this as a demonstrated, independently assembled steady reactor prediction within its tested domain. The paper does not report blind preregistration, nor does its steady spatial calculation establish arbitrary temporal recovery prediction.

Retain the earlier SI audit's 0.2–5.0 percentage-point selectivity residuals as point residuals, not confidence intervals. The main discusses curve-fitting and measurement errors as possible sources of deviations; it does not establish their unique cause. This is stronger prior art than the earlier SI-only description could confirm, but it does not invalidate a consequential Ni-dependent prediction beyond a calibrated baseline.

Original PDF p.9 was rendered and inspected to verify the forward-integration description, equations, and validation scope.

## 2. Santos 2024 contains relevant experiments, not just a proposed framework

The old review describes the [Santos perspective](../../literature/papers/santos2024-the-complex-chlorination-effects-on/original.pdf) mainly from its abstract and computational SI. Its main text contains promoted/unpromoted comparisons and chlorine-response experiments under elevated-pressure conditions: Figure 1 at 11 bara, and Figures 5–7 and 9 at 19 barg. Figures 5–7 and Sections 3.1–3.3 examine temperature, oxygen, and water; Figure 9 and Section 3.4 examine CO2. In particular, water changes the location and sharpness of the optimum chlorine response, while CO2's effect changes with its partial pressure. The paper retains empirical corrections and alternative mechanistic explanations.

**Required improvement:** cite these measured response families when defining the baseline for the Ni comparison. A failure at fixed gas-phase chloriding factor can reflect familiar changes in water, CO2, or temperature. Measuring actual water and matching the relevant product state is already required by the program and is now better supported by direct industrially relevant evidence.

Do not transfer a universal optimum chlorine coverage from this source. Its Sections 2–3 derive that simplification under stated assumptions, including negligible secondary EO loss and matching functional forms for the primary pathways. These assumptions must be checked for the actual Ni pair. No inspected section supplies a prospective Ni/no-Ni recovery or aging prediction.

The [Harris 2019 main article](../../literature/papers/harris2019-kinetics-of-chlorine-deposition-and/original.pdf), Sections 3.3–3.4 and its conclusions, also confirms the earlier abstract-based account: independently assessed exchange kinetics support steady coverage isotherms; the measured exchange and oxidation rate scales differ markedly; differing reaction sensitivities suggest distinct site requirements without uniquely identifying site geometries. Close its main-text access gap without upgrading a steady isotherm into a demonstrated temporal Ni-recovery model.

## 3. Chen 2018 supports the existing secondary-loss caution

The [full paper](../../literature/papers/chen2018-kinetics-of-ethylene-epoxidation-on/original.pdf), “Kinetics for ethylene epoxidation” and Figures 9–12, confirms the earlier abstract-based account of null EO/acetaldehyde cofeed effects in the tested ranges. It also reports a **0.5–1.6 percentage-point decrease in EO selectivity** with increasing contact time, despite that null EO-cofeed result.

The authors explicitly offer both sequential EO combustion and a changed branching ratio as possible explanations. Their statement that combustion of only 0.6–1.8% of formed EO would explain the observed loss is a stoichiometric sufficiency calculation, not a directly measured secondary combustion fraction.

**Improvement:** add this example to the core design's existing warning that contact-time curvature and ordinary cofeeds do not uniquely identify secondary loss. Preserve the distinction if summarizing Santos, which cites this result more assertively. It strengthens the present measurement logic; it does not warrant adding an unconditional tracer campaign.

## 4. Correct the TFE package note's strength of inference

The [Iyer 2023 original](../../literature/papers/iyer2023-chemical-titration-of-promoted-ag/original.pdf), PDF pp.1–4, explicitly treats total TFE uptake as an **upper-bound estimate**, because adsorption on inactive sites cannot be excluded. Table 1 gives means of 56.1, 29.7, and 20.7 μmol TFE/g for total-uptake, linear-region, and initial-slope estimates, respectively. The much-cited 52–69 μmol/g range uses the total-uptake method. Its approximately fivefold comparison with 12.3 μmol N2O/g is therefore a comparison between operational probes and chosen estimators, not proof of a fivefold increase in the true number of working active sites.

The [current package note](../../literature/papers/iyer2023-chemical-titration-of-promoted-ag/paper.md) leads with an in-situ count and the fivefold contrast but omits this explicit bound. **Ask the sole literature worker to add that qualification and the alternative estimates.** The research review already handles these limitations correctly and should retain them. The now archived main PDF closes its artifact-access gap; the SI package still lacks a full text in the inspected folder.

## 5. Esposito 2024/2026 strengthens prior art without validating Ni transfer

The [2024 main article](../../literature/papers/esposito2024-isotopic-studies-of-reaction-pathways/original.pdf), Section 3.3/Figure 4, PDF pp.7–8, confirms the earlier SI reading: CO2 labels the reactive oxygen pool, the authors estimate a reverse/forward activation ratio near 0.02, and substantial isotope exchange does not imply substantial return to gas O2. Importantly, the text allows an irreversibly generated active oxidant downstream of a precursor reversibly derived from O2 that does not exchange with CO2. Thus the result concerns the probed oxidant and gas-return boundary; it does not prove every preceding O2 adsorption event irreversible. No new statistical confidence bound was established by this audit.

The [2026 main article](../../literature/papers/esposito2026-common-active-sites-and-oxidants/original.pdf), Sections 3.1–3.3/Figures 1–4, adds direct ethylene/propylene competition experiments on K/NO/Cl-promoted Ag/CaCO3. It reports approximately 14 h of selective ethylene oxidation after propylene conditioning without continued NO or organochloride cofeed, and competition consistent with a shared oxidant. These are relevant prior art for retained promoter function and cross-olefin mechanistic similarity. They do not identify a unique molecular oxidant or establish the same chemistry on Ni/Cs/Re/Cl–Ag.

Keep rate bases separate: the Figure 4 effective **epoxidation** rate-constant ratio is approximately 4–6 (fitted line 6.395); the approximately 20–30-fold **oxygen abstraction** contrast additionally accounts for selectivity and combustion stoichiometry. Original PDF p.6 was rendered and checked for this distinction. The current generic package summary would benefit from these experimental details, formulation limits, and the distinction between functional evidence and the proposed K–NOx molecular assignment.

**Improvement to the optional isotope branch:** cite the now available main articles and remove the old request for the 2024 main as missing. Retain all existing gates for return-population labeling, gas balance, and consequential discrimination. The 2026 study increases the plausibility of related oxidant functions on its own formulation; it does not make the proposed Ni isotope experiment automatically informative.

## 6. Later upload: Jalil 2025 and Esposito 2026 supplements

The [fourth inbox run](../../literature/runs/2026-09-16-inbox-additions-4/run.md) arrived during this audit. Both supplements are now retained and read. This section supersedes their missing-source status in the initial audit.

### Jalil: a better specified preparation and stronger proximity controls

The [Jalil supplement](../../literature/papers/jalil2025-supplementary-information-for-nickel-promotes/original.pdf) supports the physical plausibility of a Ni-associated Ag function while making its preparation burden more concrete:

- **A meaningful processing control is already prior art.** PDF pp.6–8 describe preparation of the Ag control identically to NiAg except for Ni addition. Table S5 distinguishes those controls from samples made with the plain Ag synthesis protocol. Do not imply that the proposed matched blank itself is an original contribution. Continue requiring a corresponding blank when transferring Ni to the fully promoted platform.
- **Preparation details are consequential.** Ethylene-glycol source, trace impurities and water affected particle shape and NiAg stability; larger particles required a second PVP addition. The Ni-reduction step used NaOH and hydrazine. Transferring this route requires reproducing its processing and checking the resulting promoter inventory; simply matching nominal Ni loading is insufficient. This does not establish contamination as the cause of the reported effect.
- **Pretreatments differ between the published figures.** PDF p.10 and Figure S18 specify 400 °C oxidative calcination for 4 h, followed by an 8 h ethylene/O2 conditioning period at 250 °C. Figure 2 additionally uses a 450 °C H2 treatment; optimized Figure 3 omits that reduction. Record which reference state is reproduced. Do not combine their data as one preparation/pretreatment-controlled series.
- **The physical-mixture control is informative but not atomically definitive.** Figure S19 (PDF p.26) compares Ag with a physical mixture containing supported Ni nanoparticles at 0.022 wt% Ni. Their selectivity–conversion curves are nearly the same. This argues against the tested separate Ni phase reproducing the NiAg effect; it does not establish that every possible support-bound Ni species is inactive, or prove isolated substitutional Ni under reaction conditions.
- **Spectroscopy narrows the alternatives.** Figures S14–S16 (PDF pp.21–23) show that calcined/reduced NiAg spectra are not represented well by linear combinations of supported Ni/NiO reference spectra. This supports a distinct Ni environment associated with Ag. Measurements in He after calcination/reduction are not an operando census during promoted epoxidation, and the fit failure does not uniquely distinguish isolated Ni from small clusters or all plausible interfacial species.
- **The chlorine result contains substantial evolution.** Figure S21 (PDF p.30) plots EO and CO2 rates per gram Ag after adding 0.5 ppm ethyl chloride. NiAg shows an initial EO-rate increase followed by a substantial decline through approximately 20 h; the Ag response also evolves strongly. The plot supports a different chloride response, not sustained rate enhancement, a stationary endpoint, or a measured recovery policy. Different catalyst amounts/loadings in Table S5 further mean this is not a complete comparison of local product states or integrated useful output on a fully matched promoted pair.

Figures S19 and S21 were rendered and inspected directly. Figure S7 also explicitly defines the particle-size/Ni-coverage calculation as a **maximum** assuming all Ni moves to the surface, consistent with the program's current geometric qualification. The local DFT calculation explicitly disclaims quantitative prediction of supported-catalyst combustion rates without a fuller working-surface description (PDF pp.4–5).

**Effect on the existing program:** the Ni-associated fresh-chemistry hypothesis has better supporting controls, and the preparation route is more reproducible in principle. The transfer to an optimized Cs/Re-promoted architecture remains unvalidated. Practical confidence does not increase: the SI's response history reinforces the need to distinguish fresh chemistry, ordinary conditioning, and retention. Keep the matched-pair gate, prospective useful-output prediction, and stopping rule; add the documented pretreatment and processing distinctions to the preparation record.

### Esposito: shared oxidant does not mean identical rate control

The [Esposito supplement](../../literature/papers/esposito2026-supporting-information-for-common-active/original.pdf), S2/Figure S2, reports EO orders of approximately 0.6 in ethylene and 0.45 in O2. Selectivity is approximately 88.8% across its O2 series but rises from 83.6% to 91.0% across its ethylene series. S4/Figure S4 shows propylene epoxidation decreasing as ethylene increases, strengthening the competition interpretation. Original PDF p.6 was rendered and checked.

These observations support shared accessible sites/oxidant on that formulation while explicitly distinguishing ethylene kinetics from the prior propylene regime with sole kinetic control by oxygen activation. Thus neither the propylene rate law nor its reversibility result should be transferred unchanged to ethylene. S1 also documents different timescales for NO-derived promotion and inhibition and warns of differences between preconditioned beds; its two EO series additionally use different feed conditions, so their offset is not an estimate of repeatability at identical conditions.

**Effect on the existing isotope branch:** stronger mechanistic context and direct competition evidence, with a clearer limit on transfer. The SI supplies no new ethylene oxygen-isotope return measurement or Ni/Cs/Re test. Keep the branch conditional and preserve the requirement to establish the actual returning oxygen population.
