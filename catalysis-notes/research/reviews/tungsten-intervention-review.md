**Recommendation: retain the epimer substitution as a conditional, small feasibility experiment; do not select it as the program's first decisive mechanistic intervention.** The revised hypothesis is testable as a feed-composition effect on net W export and useful C2 output. Its unfavorable direction is weakly supported, and the proposed soluble baseline does not supply the missing extraction chemistry. A positive result could justify further work. At present it does not carry the main program's claim to predict how W–support interactions control productive retention.

The original inactive-mannose-reservoir lead should be withdrawn, not preserved as the default interpretation behind revised language. Strongly populated ambient complexes do not establish an inactive hot pool. Mannose-fed solutions make glycolaldehyde under the proposed conditions; an inactive fraction could coexist, but requires independent evidence.

## What the inspected originals establish

The primary [ACS Catalysis 2024 SI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.4c02791.s001) was read from `/tmp/tungsten-process-audit/elmohammad2024-si.pdf`; Figures S7–S9 and S30–S31 were independently rendered and inspected.

- **S7, PDF p. 6:** both glucose and mannose feeds produce glycolaldehyde at 150 °C, 5 mM sugar and 1.3 mM W, under 40 bar N2. Mannose-fed early accumulation is at least competitive with glucose-fed accumulation in the displayed sampling window. These are net product concentrations, not independently resolved intrinsic cleavage rates. Error bars are calculated using 8% relative error on glycolaldehyde concentration; the caption does not establish independent reaction repeatability.
- **S8, p. 7:** mannose feeds also rapidly form glucose, especially with AMT at pH 5. Therefore S7 establishes productive conversion of a mannose feed; by itself it does not separate direct mannose cleavage from cleavage following epimerization. “Competent intrinsic soluble mannose cleavage” is stronger than these figures warrant.
- **S9, p. 8:** reported glucose carbon closure falls early, not merely at the long endpoint. At roughly 10–15 min with AMT, the missing fraction is already comparable to or larger than the carbon accumulated as glycolaldehyde in S7. Approximate visual comparison, not digitized data. An informative short-contact window must be demonstrated; shortening time alone does not establish it. S9 does not provide mannose carbon closure.
- **S29–S31, pp. 20–21:** the two-reference fits use octahedral AMT and tetrahedral sodium tungstate. Mannose reaction spectra have a substantial octahedral-like contribution. Edge energies remain near the measured W(VI) references and far from WO2. These observations do not count inactive W–mannose complexes, uniquely identify the reactive species, or exclude a minor active population.

The [Inorganic Chemistry author manuscript](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.inorgchem.3c04236), local `/tmp/oxygenate-program-alternative/complexes.pdf`, specifies 300 K NMR, usually within two hours of preparation (p. 3). Its abundant mannose complexes and barely detectable glucose complexes concern concentrated solutions, illustrated at 2 M metal/1 M sugar (pp. 4–8). XANES uses 0.1 M metal/0.05 M sugar (pp. 11–13). This is a real stereochemical coordination contrast, but not a hot supported-W extraction measurement or a quantitative extraction equilibrium.

[Li et al. 2020 SI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.0c00836.s001), local `/tmp/tungsten-process-audit/li2020-si.pdf`, Table S2, p. S8, reports 68.7% first-run and 66.9% seventh-run EG yields for Ni-W/M. Table S3, p. S9, reports productive leachate plus Ni/M and soluble-AMT controls. Leachate was prepared in water/H2 without cellulose, at 518 K. Neither result selects mannose as an extraction perturbation; both constrain the novelty and practical benchmark. The main article remains necessary for cycle handling and linkage evidence.

The ACS SI gives chromatographic retention/response information and the XAS vessel drawing (pp. 2–3), but does not supply a complete kinetic reactor, heat-up and sampling protocol. Its main article remains a prerequisite for faithful reproduction and assessing overlap with this experiment. No absence of mixed-feed controls is inferred from incomplete main-text access.

## What the proposed experiment can identify

Constant-total-hexose substitution on the same catalyst formulation is a useful intervention. With adequate balances and reproducibility, it can establish that changing feed composition changes **net hot W export**, useful C2 output, or both. It does not independently change a W–support bond, identify an elementary extraction step, or measure gross microscopic detachment and return.

The uniform-carbon-13 glucose reporter separates feed ancestry. Once labelled glucose epimerizes, labelled glycolaldehyde can arise through labelled mannose; the reporter does not retain the identity of the epimer at the cleavage event. Time-resolved labelled glucose, mannose and fragments can constrain this network. They cannot automatically separate every pathway. Inhibition of reporter conversion can reflect competition with another productive substrate, while total output increases. Label dilution controls and endpoint product analysis alone are insufficient for a molecular inhibition claim.

Four requirements make the narrower causal test interpretable:

1. **Establish the analytical window first.** Calibrate recovery in both feed matrices, including labelled products; distinguish recovered useful carbonyl from inferred gross cleavage. Quantify epimerization and product disappearance. Carbon unassigned to identifiable species must be small enough, relative to the claimed effect, that degradation products cannot plausibly explain it. Bulk total-carbon closure alone does not identify an organic ligand pool. If the window fails, stop this cleavage-only experiment or validate a different analytical scheme before adding supported-catalyst complexity.
2. **Match the soluble comparison to the relevant solution.** An AMT run at 1.3 mM W is a reproduction reference. A supported bed may export much less W, and salts/species released by the support can change the reaction. Use measured exported-W concentrations, residence histories and matrix controls, including W-free-support effluent where needed. Independently measured soluble rates constrain plausible contributions; they are not a quantity that can simply be subtracted from supported rates to obtain surface turnover.
3. **Separate feed effects from catalyst history.** Sequentially feeding glucose then mannose to one bed confounds composition with depletion, conditioning and aging. Use independent matched beds or an order-balanced design with measured inventories. Predefine the observation horizon, repeatability and minimum resolvable difference. A glucose return alone cannot reverse W already lost. Include support-element export, matched solution chemistry and transport/recovery controls already specified in the main program.
4. **Report both absolute quantities before their ratio.** Define cumulative recovered C2 carbon and W export over the same interval. A rise in W/C2 caused solely by lower C2 output is not evidence that mannose increased extraction. Conversely, increased export with proportionally increased output falsifies the proposed additional W burden. Freeze decisions using the two measured changes and their uncertainty, with W/C2 as the practical comparison. Positive downstream reporter conversion establishes surviving exported catalytic capacity, not the fraction of upstream turnovers made in solution.

## The missing chemical and program-level link

Soluble-complex stabilization can favor extraction only if it changes the relevant hot chemical potentials and detachment/return kinetics relative to the bound state. The ambient population contrast does not establish those quantities. Mannose might stabilize mobile W, stabilize adsorbed W, block dissolution, or alter local acidity through its reaction products. Similar homogeneous rates would remove one confounder; they would not favor any one of these extraction directions. No result from a solution without a support can establish that direction on its own.

A held-out intermediate mannose fraction tests a local response prediction if the response rule is frozen first. It is a useful validation, but interpolation between feeds on one support is not yet a prediction of anchor chemistry or a validated choice between catalyst architectures. Selecting separation or anchoring changes still requires a consequential burden under a relevant feed and a chemical intervention aimed at that burden.

**Judgments:** hypothesis plausibility is reasonable for *some* ligand-dependent export effect and weak for the specified adverse direction; experimental informativeness is conditional on recovery, transient resolution and relevant W concentrations; practical value is unestablished because neither consequential mannose abundance nor excess W burden has been shown for the selected architecture.

The broader coordination/retention program remains scientifically possible, but this revision supplies only a conditional supporting experiment. It does not yet satisfy its promised central intervention. Keep initial investment at soluble-method reproduction and a small matched export pilot if those prerequisites pass. Select a core intervention only after the working-state map and fully read anchoring comparator identify a manipulable interaction with a consequential predicted response. If the epimer pilot shows no useful differential effect, retire this lead; do not turn its null result into evidence for the original inactive reservoir or preserve it by expanding the measurement list.
