# Independent reassessment: Ni promotion after the older Ag durability result

2026-09-15. This review reassesses priority after the [older Ni prior-art review](ag-older-ni-prior-art.md). It reads that review, the primary patent's experimental example and Table III in the retained source text, the current [Ag program](../programs/ag-selective-oxygen-use.md), [core-design review](ag-core-experimental-design.md), [previous portfolio review](portfolio-second-value-review.md), and the lower-priority pilots in the [decision brief](../decision-brief.md). It does not establish exhaustive novelty. The main program was being revised concurrently; the recommendation below applies to its research question, not a particular intermediate draft.

## Decision

**Retain Ag as the first-priority bounded investigation, but withdraw generic Ni/Cs/Re compatibility as the justification.** The stronger question is whether Ni changes the loss and recoverability of the useful promoted state enough to support a better catalyst or operating policy. Developing that question is justified; a large promoter, aging or spectroscopy campaign is not yet justified.

This is a narrower conditional recommendation than the previous portfolio review. A substantial program would have to connect a chemical distinction to a prediction that changes a useful choice. Merely reproducing better selectivity retention, correcting the old controls, or adding structural observations would fall short. If the initial comparison cannot expose such a distinction, demote this direction rather than broaden the screen.

Ag retains a positive case: actual promoted-catalyst aging data, recent evidence for a large fresh Ni effect on another Ag formulation, a measurable reversible promoted state, and a direct connection between selectivity retention and consumption of valuable reactants. The older experiment increases confidence that Ni can function in this chemical environment while reducing the novelty of demonstrating it. It does not establish the modern preparation's working state or competitive performance.

## 1. What has changed

[US5380885A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5380885A/en) reports a Ni-postdoped Ag/Cs/Re/S formulation, with lithium-containing preparation, operating at 210 psig with ethyl chloride. Initial selectivity is similar to the Ni-free untreated parent. After repeated severe-aging intervals, the Ni formulation loses less selectivity through 50 days. Crucially, the catalysts return to the standard conversion and are **reoptimized after each aging interval**. Recovery to a reference operating point is therefore already part of the disclosed test.

The example has real control and interpretation limits: different listed Cs contents, no long-term solvent-treated control, no replicated uncertainties, and incomplete temperature/chloride histories. Nevertheless, it is actual combined-catalyst working and aging evidence. It cannot be reduced to an untested composition suggestion. Its proposed ionic Ni–perrhenate association also prevents treating a generic Ni–Re contact hypothesis as new.

The pattern is compatible with delayed onset of loss, among other possibilities; it does not establish a universally smaller deterioration rate. Nor does reoptimization establish that every reversible change was exhausted. The observation to explain is thus **retention of useful promoted behavior under a particular exposure and recovery history**, without presupposing what was preserved.

[Jalil et al.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adt1213) emphasize fresh combustion suppression on a different material and a much shorter evolving chloride experiment. [Hwang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2026.117021) provide a specific Cs/Re-dependent working response with slow reversible chloride effects. Together they motivate competing explanations; they do not prove that one explanation connects all three studies. Nominal dilute Ni, a different preparation, or a single-atom description cannot establish a uniquely new working state.

## 2. The strongest surviving question

**Does Ni preserve selective output by changing fresh reaction branching, by making an inhibited promoted state easier to recover, or by preventing persistent loss of the promoted function—and can that distinction predict when a recovery intervention substitutes for Ni or when Ni remains necessary?**

These possibilities can coexist. Their relative importance should be established only to the extent needed for the decision; a complete elementary mechanism is unnecessary.

| Explanation to distinguish | Observable requirement | Consequential prediction |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh reaction effect | A reproducible difference in net EO and combustion behavior before the damaging history, under a documented working state | The useful advantage persists under a held-out product burden or reference state consistent with the measured rate response. |
| Easier recovery of a reversible operating state | A defined intervention restores useful function differently on the decisive pair, with an ordinary-operation control that allows for spontaneous recovery | An intervention selected from that response changes cumulative useful output under a new exposure history. Ni may become unnecessary, remain beneficial because recovery is faster, or become harmful. |
| Preservation of promoted function through exposure | After the same stated recovery protocol, one catalyst retains more useful function than the other, beyond the fresh difference | A selected exposure changes the persistent performance gap as predicted, and an independently supported chemical change explains why the material or operating choice differs. |

Failure to recover during a finite test establishes **persistent loss under that protocol**, not irreversible structural deterioration. A structural claim needs evidence that distinguishes the relevant change from slowly recovering chloride or promoter states. Conversely, restoration of performance does not prove that structure was unchanged. The program should preserve these boundaries instead of forcing every outcome into a binary reversible/irreversible label.

The chemical hypothesis worth testing is that Ni alters the stability or restoration of the promoter arrangement and oxygen/chloride chemistry responsible for useful selective behavior. Specific Ni–Re binding, suppression of Ag restructuring, altered chlorine uptake/removal, and changes in primary or secondary reaction rates remain alternatives. Existing evidence does not yet favor one sufficiently to make it the required mechanism.

## 3. Smallest investigation that could earn expansion

1. **Use a functioning promoted pair.** Start with a Ni-free promoted anchor and one justified Ni-addition route, with matched composition and processing as far as practicable. Establish useful fresh behavior and preparation reproducibility. The no-Cs bridge and Re-free contrasts are valuable when needed to interpret fresh selectivity, but reproducing every parent and completing six compositions need not precede a focused retention question. A failed treatment transfer is a preparation result, not a substantial incompatibility finding.
2. **Measure a recovery response before calling an aging result structural.** A bounded chloride excursion on qualified material can define the accessible reversible response and its timescale. Ordinary operation or a matched sham return is necessary because time alone may restore activity. This calibration supports interpretation; it is not itself the novel result.
3. **Apply one relevant, documented exposure and one defined recovery comparison.** Record actual temperature, feed, conversion, chloride history and net output. For causal interpretation, avoid unexplained adaptive stress differences between samples. For practical performance, separately adjusted operation can be fair; describe those histories rather than treating them as equal chemical exposure. Do not assume that the patent's 85% O2 conversion or 285 °C stress accelerates the same loss mechanism as useful operation.
4. **Select one discriminating chemical measurement only after the kinetic difference appears.** Choose it to distinguish the remaining explanations for that difference. A spectrum correlated with age does not by itself establish causation. Add Re-free material, a product tracer or a structural intervention only when the result would change the proposed action. The optional oxygen-return test is not an admission gate for this program.
5. **Predict a held-out decision.** State beforehand how a changed exposure duration, recovery history or product condition should alter the decisive pair's useful output. Choose one. Test the proposed intervention against continued ordinary operation and the competing material choice over the same stated horizon. Do not count a prediction made after retuning chloride or refitting the outcome as validation.

The recovery experiment can itself change later deterioration. If that happens, it is part of the policy being tested, not a neutral measurement that can be subtracted without evidence. Preserve untreated or separately followed comparison material when necessary to determine the effect. This is a reason to keep the initial history set small, not to create a large exposure matrix.

## 4. What would make the result substantial?

The clearest success would be a validated choice between a material modification and an operating intervention. Examples are:

- A Ni-free catalyst recovers the relevant function through a defined intervention and delivers more cumulative useful EO than adding Ni, after charging the intervention's lost production and resource use.
- Ni preserves a chemically supported promoted function that the tested recovery cannot restore; the explanation correctly predicts an exposure limit and a practical way to retain output.
- Ni's apparent retention advantage is mainly a fresh branching or product-sensitivity effect. Separating it from recovery allows an intervention to be chosen or rejected correctly under a new operating burden.

These are possible outcomes, not expected gains. A useful negative finding must resolve a consequential choice within its tested scope. A null at one loading, an inability to reproduce a preparation, or a corrected historical control does not become a broad design principle by being negative.

Report integrated net EO output per initial catalyst or Ag inventory over an explicit common period, together with carbon selectivity, reactant consumption, required temperature and conditioning costs. Endpoint selectivity alone can reward a treatment that loses too much output during recovery. Improved cumulative output in an accelerated test still does not establish improved commercial life; a relevant normal-operation comparison is required before that inference.

The principal route to broad usefulness is a demonstrated chemical rule for when promoter-state recovery can replace stabilization, or when stabilization is necessary. A generic procedure for separating reversible and persistent loss is established experimental reasoning. Its application must reveal a new, consequential relationship in this catalyst; transfer to other systems would require subsequent evidence.

## 5. Portfolio and confidence

Ag remains ahead of the zirconia oxygen-fate and zeolite assay-history pilots for the next bounded investment because its target is already a reported loss of selective function in an important process. Zirconia still requires product-rich net-output and chemically consequential cleaning results; zeolite assay history still requires a persistent effect that changes prediction or material choice. This comparison does not strengthen Ag merely because alternatives are weak. Its affirmative case is the conjunction of observed retention, observed recoverable promoted behavior, and a decision between material stabilization and operating recovery.

| Judgment | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Scientific hypothesis | Plausible, with no established dominant cause. The cross-study connection remains a hypothesis. |
| Informative first comparison | Moderate confidence conditional on a functioning promoted pair, reproducible histories and suitable reactor/analytics. Slow evolution and trace-promoter preparation are material burdens. |
| Originality of the narrowed question | Credible but provisional. The historical source narrows it sharply; the source record is not complete enough to claim an unreported mechanism. |
| Meaningful practical improvement | Low evidentiary confidence. The old retention effect supports plausibility, but no modern competitive advantage or new intervention has been demonstrated. |
| Substantial research outcome | Attainable only if a chemical distinction predicts a useful intervention or limit. An additional aging curve is insufficient. |

**Continuation rule:** advance beyond the matched-pair stage only when it reveals a reproducible, consequential difference and a discriminating prediction beyond the historical aging result. If the difference is fully accounted for by already known conditioning behavior with no new useful intervention, or if informative testing requires a broad synthesis/facility campaign, close or demote this bounded program. The current task ends with the existing ideas; this rule does not authorize starting another direction.
