# Independent review: CO2 timing during a proposed steam purge

2026-09-16. Review of [the timing intervention](../working/program-development/cyclic-oxide-causal-intervention.md) and [the broader cyclic-oxide program](../working/program-development/cyclic-oxides.md). No experiments were performed. Main proposals and literature packages were not edited.

**Verdict: retain the timing experiment as a bounded, scientifically credible process-compatibility test within the broader program. It is not yet a substitute for studying the documented operational decline.** Equal supplied CO2 doses, different timing, and a common final treatment can establish whether timing changes retained function after a defined recovery procedure. They cannot alone establish that hydrolysis caused Li volatilization during the steam interval. The proposal largely recognizes this distinction; its remaining weaknesses are an unspecified measurement budget, incomplete localization of damage, and an unresolved connection between diagnostic conditions and the proposed process.

The broader question—when useful selective oxygen delivery stops responding to process-compatible regeneration—is substantial experimental catalysis. Its importance comes from a measured functional loss and an actionable operating decision. Neither an unknown molecular mechanism nor existing carbonate chemistry disqualifies it. Conversely, known carbonate stabilization does not become a substantial program merely by moving a CO2 pulse. The program must link a consequential loss, its material cause, and a durable intervention or useful compatibility limit.

## What the comparison identifies

The two sequences estimate a **total effect of treatment order at equal fed doses**, followed by a common recovery procedure. Equal fed doses are a fair operational starting point; equal uptake, oxygen exchange, or thermodynamic work should not be presumed. Those differences are outcomes worth measuring.

There are two limits to the proposed causal interpretation:

1. A difference after the entire sequence does not locate damage exclusively in its first five minutes. The protection arm then experiences dry Ar; the delayed arm experiences CO2. Loss or redistribution can occur in either segment, during the reset, or through their interaction. Segment-specific outlet capture helps, but deposition and subsequent release can lag the segment that produced the mobile material. Use sacrificial sister specimens at the end of the steam segment, in addition to the final endpoint, when claiming a steam-created material-loss interval. This is a small addition to the already proposed sacrificial measurements.
2. The common reset defines recoverability operationally. A persistent difference means that this stated reset failed to erase the history effect. It does not establish absolute irreversibility. An equal final result means timing has no resolved persistent benefit under this reset and exposure budget; it does not prove that neither arm lost Li or that all reversible chemistry was directly observed.

Restoring carbonate must mean restoring the carbonatable fraction of retained material to an established endpoint, not forcing equal absolute carbonate inventories after unequal Li loss. A uptake plateau plus a phase-sensitive carbonate measurement is more informative than the treatment label. Li incorporated into a ferrite or support phase may remain in the reactor while becoming unavailable for this reset. A longer reset should be investigated only if incomplete restoration prevents interpretation, and its extra time belongs in the recovery cost.

Do not demand a proportional relation between fractional Li loss and fractional product loss without evidence: loss of a small amount from a critical interface can have a large effect. Quantitative consistency requires a defensible inventory change and a credible coverage/function relationship, with uncertainty; it does not require an assumed linear calibration.

## The analytical budget is the first feasibility gate

The note correctly calls for a Li balance, but does not state the smallest loss it could resolve. The following are **illustrative calculations, not expected loss rates or specifications for an instrument**. Assume 10 wt% Li2CO3 in the final catalyst, with the loading convention subsequently verified by digestion.

| Quantity | 1 g catalyst | 100 mg catalyst |
|---|---:|---:|
| Initial Li inventory | 18.8 mg | 1.88 mg |
| 1% of initial Li | 188 µg | 18.8 µg |
| 0.1% of initial Li | 18.8 µg | 1.88 µg |

For scale, 300 elapsed hours at 16 minutes per cycle is 1,125 cycles. If an entirely hypothetical 10% Li loss accumulated uniformly over that period, the average would be about 1.7 µg per cycle per gram of catalyst. Assigning all of that to one post-oxidation purge would still yield a difficult single-purge bulk-inventory difference. This is not an inference from the observed selectivity decline.

Trace analysis of a clean trap can resolve a much smaller transfer than subtraction of two large catalyst inventories. Nevertheless, the relevant limit is the uncertainty of **recovered transfer and unclosed inventory**, including initial sample heterogeneity, blanks, digestion, support/wall extraction, trap efficiency, and deposits outside the recovered region. A 1% uncertainty on each 18.8 mg catalyst inventory already exceeds a 0.1% loss. An instrument detection limit alone cannot validate the balance. Specify the expected uncertainty and recovery checks before choosing the number of exposures. Independent reactor runs, rather than repeated readings of one digest or successive cycles from one bed, establish treatment reproducibility.

Gas balances face a comparable subtraction problem. At a stated standard molar volume of 22.414 L/mol, 100 sccm for five minutes supplies 22.3 mmol total gas: 1.12 mmol CO2 at 5%, and 2.23 mmol water at 10%. One gram of the illustrative catalyst contains 1.35 mmol carbonate. Conversion of 0.1% of that carbonate corresponds to only 1.35 µmol net CO2, about 61 ppm averaged over the full gas dose, superimposed on a 50,000 ppm CO2 feed in the protected arm. Water holdup and switching transients can similarly obscure small net uptake. Actual flow, catalyst mass, calibration uncertainty, and residence-time response must replace these examples. Gas balance cannot be the sole proof of trace shell conversion.

Accumulating captures over a fixed number of realistic cycles is therefore sensible. Repeating steam/CO2 treatments without intervening ethane reduction and oxidation constitutes a different aging history; label it as such. Return to the actual cycle before inferring an operating lifetime benefit.

## Important source checks and confounds

**The steam translation is real, but distinct from the original aging cause.** Brody used a 300 g bed, an alumina tube and alumina grit, and Ar purges in the measured longevity experiment. Steam enters the model as heat-exchange purge steps with flow linked to the ethane feed. The manuscript places the stronger initial selectivity decline around 300–400 hours and acknowledges possible promoter vaporization, redistribution, or pore migration. Thus steam compatibility is an unresolved process dependency; added steam cannot explain the decline under Ar. Water made during ethane reaction remains a separate possible exposure. The first post-oxidation experiment does not test that reducing, product-containing environment. [Brody et al., manuscript §§2.2, 2.3, 3.1–3.2](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/2001472), independently read from the local PDF text.

**The speciation rationale is supported, its rate here is not.** Barckholtz directly observes carbonate/hydroxide Raman responses in 52/48 Li/Na carbonate at 923 K with 10% water and 0–15% CO2. Conditions were held for hours, and uncalibrated response factors prevented absolute concentrations. This supports the direction of the proposed equilibrium perturbation, not a quantitative LiOH fraction, Li escape rate, or equilibration within a five-minute LSF exposure. Absence of an OH signal is informative only with a stated sensitivity on this coating. [Primary full article, experimental evidence section](https://doi.org/10.3389/fenrg.2021.669761).

**There is an exact-material CO2 confound already in the foundational paper.** Gao reports reversible suppression of ethane conversion by a 10% CO2 cofeed at 700 °C on coated LSF (Fig. 4D). Residual CO2 after the common reset could therefore bias the functional probe without irreversible damage. Demonstrate washout at the probe inlet/outlet and follow several cycles. Gao also reports supported-shell melting between 600 and 700 °C: a hydroxide-induced solid-to-liquid switch is not an appropriate premise. [Gao et al., full article, Figs. 1 and 4](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaz9339). Main text independently read through Europe PMC; original DSC supplement not inspected.

**Bare LSF is necessary but not sufficient to control the carrier.** It detects direct oxide responses but lacks the coating/core interaction. A null bare-LSF result does not exclude a ferrite change mediated by the coating. CO2 changes phase/redox behavior in methane-reduced LSF in primary work at 900 °C; this is supporting evidence for caution, not a measured effect at the proposed oxidized 700 °C state. Measure relevant oxygen uptake, phase changes, and CO/H2 during exposures where oxide reoxidation by CO2/water is possible. [Dahan et al., primary indexed methods/results](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsengineeringau.3c00008); complete original was not retrieved in this review.

**Support consumption is a specific alternative to volatilization.** Primary molten-carbonate work reports alumina reacting with Li2CO3 to form LiAlO2. Its precursor-derived alumina is not equivalent to Brody's dense α-alumina tube and grit, so its kinetics cannot be imported. Nevertheless, Li recovered on supports may be chemically immobilized rather than merely deposited. Recover supports separately and characterize Li-rich deposits when substantial; verify contact-material compatibility rather than assuming all ceramic packing is inert. [Primary abstract and conclusions](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-7753(01)00626-7), full original not retrieved.

**Closest chemistry is genuine prior art.** Vogt-Lowell's full text explicitly cycles carbonates through hydroxides and regenerates them using CO2; its 800 °C bulk ternary-melt experiments differ from the thin coating here. It also reports carbonate decomposition and CO2 release during purge periods, reinforcing the need to observe both timing segments. Its 25-cycle quantitative result and 500-cycle run with a leak preventing accurate yields should not be combined into an unqualified quantified durability demonstration. [Primary full article](https://doi.org/10.1002/cssc.202401473), independently read through Europe PMC. The timing-specific loss question remains open in the sources inspected, but the unresolved Chacko dissertation and Mohn/Wendt original prevent a priority claim or numerical volatility prediction.

## Smallest changes needed and investment decision

Keep the two-arm timing comparison, Ar reference, and bare-LSF control. Add an explicit uncertainty/exposure budget; make the existing sacrificial measurements include the end of the steam segment; verify carbonate restoration and CO2 washout before probing; and distinguish catalyst Li, support-bound Li, and transported Li. These changes strengthen the stated experiment without creating another screening matrix. A follow-up control is justified only by an observed ambiguity—for example, delayed CO2 itself causing damage rather than failing to repair steam damage.

State two linked but distinct decisions in the broader proposal: **recover documented loss under the measured Ar protocol**, and **qualify a steam-based process translation**. The timing experiment can start without first reproducing hundreds of hours of Ar aging because it addresses the second decision. It should not silently replace the first campaign or claim to explain its loss.

The 10% steam/5% CO2 test is a practical diagnostic. A null result there supplies a bounded dilute-steam compatibility result, not validation of a nearly pure steam purge. A positive result earns testing at the intended steam composition, gas-to-solid throughput, and realistic temperature trajectory, followed by the post-ethane state. Determine whether CO2 can be supplied during an existing purge or regeneration segment: the two extra five-minute diagnostic segments and final reset cannot be treated as free in an operating policy. Compare retained cumulative useful product per catalyst mass and elapsed time with added gas, downtime, separation, and makeup burdens. A positive Li-retention result with negligible product benefit is materials information, not yet a practical catalysis improvement.

**Proceed with bounded development.** The experiment can reveal a preventable failure introduced by process translation, or rule out a consequential loss within a useful measured bound. Promotion to a substantial operating strategy requires that result to survive representative conditions and matter to cumulative production. Neither presumed novelty nor a pre-existing positive pilot is the selection criterion.
