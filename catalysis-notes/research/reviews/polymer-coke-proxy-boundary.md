# Oxygenate tolerance and coke are separate questions

2026-09-15. Source qualification for the [bounded polymer exposure study](polymer-exposure-falsifiability.md), not a proposal to add oxygenates to the polymer reactor.

## Additional primary evidence

Moodley et al., [*Coke formation on WO3/SiO2 metathesis catalysts*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcata.2006.10.053), studied 8 wt% WO3/SiO2. The initial review used exposed original methods/results on the [author-upload page](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254787291_Coke_formation_on_WO3SiO2_metathesis_catalysts). The subsequently uploaded original is now local; the [2026-09-16 audit](post-upload-polymer-audit.md) read its relevant methods/results and visually checked p.158, Figures 5–6. A [fresh independent review](polymer-makeup-accounting-review.md) checked the exposed primary passages and these source limits.

In 72 h, once-through 1-octene tests at 460 °C and LHSV 5 h−1, continuous 100 ppm oxygenate addition caused no significant reported performance loss. 2-Pentanone reduced deposited coke by roughly half; water and butanol had smaller effects. Suppression diminished after returning to pure feed. The authors discuss competing adsorption and oxygenate transformations; they do not uniquely establish one pathway. A separate long recycle experiment retained high conversion despite substantial coke. Carbon maps were interpreted as deposition mainly around, rather than over, oxide crystallites. That observation is not an active-site census.

These high-conversion traces and their unreported uncertainty do not establish equal intrinsic rates. The findings support low-dose tolerance and distinguish total coke from lost function. They do not establish a useful lifetime gain, a 320 °C tolerance threshold, or compatibility with the Na-containing partner.

## Consequence for the proposed experiment

The existing proposal already allows W tolerance as well as recoverable inhibition. This source strengthens that choice; it does not change the portfolio ranking. A contaminant could reduce one deposit-forming route while impairing another component or changing the polymer substrate trajectory. Measuring less coke cannot decide whether the tandem benefits.

Use integrated accepted product and later clean-feed function as the first decision variables. Measure retained carbon when it resolves a specific discrepancy between these responses. A coke-normalized activity or an oxygenate-induced color change is not a substitute for that comparison. Conversely, unchanged output with less coke may still merit a bounded longer test if deposits approach a measured transport or handling limit; the short test alone supplies no such limit.

Generic oxygenate cofeeding to suppress W-associated coke is therefore prior art. The paper also explicitly discusses relaxing oxygenate feed specifications and reducing extraction intensity; that broad practical motivation is not new. The remaining proposed contribution is the already defined transfer test: whether measured unequal component responses predict a useful feed or catalyst-makeup choice in the actual polymer mixture.
