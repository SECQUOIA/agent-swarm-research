**Independent mathematical checks.** The [water two-pool counterexample](../calculations/water-local-exposure-identifiability.md) is correct under its stated assumptions. Summing the two pool equations with `G_i = λ B_i` removes the capacity and source split. Adding the aggregate and gas balances gives `d(W + V c_g)/dt = F(c_in − c_g) + q_T`, so conservation is respected. The steady relations `c_i − c_g = q_i/(λ B_i)` and `c_g − c_in = q_T/F` follow directly. Assigning all production to pool 1 and changing its capacity from one quarter to three quarters of the fixed total divides its local excess by three while preserving aggregate responses and total excess inventory. The same linear summation applies to an ideal conserved isotope.

This is a counterexample to guaranteed identification, not proof that all possible measurements fail or that indistinguishable pools describe the real catalyst. Equal relaxation rates, specified source histories and constant coefficients are material assumptions. The example nevertheless defeats the proposed inference from even complete aggregate inlet/source responses to a unique source-region exposure. Changing flow cannot repair that inference within this model. A valid local rate law or source-selective measurement could add information, but neither has been demonstrated for the proposed working packing. The correct investment consequence is the explicit claim boundary now in the water notes.

The [preloading assessment](../working/program-development/water-working-liquid-intervention.md) reaches the correct narrower disposition. Changing liquid occupancy also changes wetting, reactant access and the putative water-transfer interface. Equal hydrocarbon mass elsewhere does not isolate a finite wax sink. A persistent benefit with independently verified occupied capacity could reject a restricted empty-capacity explanation; losing the benefit would remain ambiguous. The finite-inventory argument excludes indefinite absorption of all product, not small redistributed liquid inventories or lasting startup effects. This rejected discriminator should stay closed rather than become an additional first-stage experiment.

The [cyclic value calculation](../calculations/cyclic-oxide-prevention-value.md) is also correct. From `J_i = q_i/(τ + h_i)`, the stated prevention-versus-recovery threshold follows for the defined common cycle and block-average outputs. Independently recomputing the illustrative restoration gives `0.532/0.4724 = 1.12616` and `16(1.12616 − 1) = 2.0186 min/cycle`. The 33.3% CO2 composition gives 2.0425 mol CO2/mol ethane for one five-minute purge, or 4.085 for two, under the explicitly assumed equal total molar flows. The carbonate/Li and oxygen-equivalent scales also reproduce.

Brody's retained primary manuscript, Methods 2.1–2.3 and Table 1, supports the 300 g bed, nominal 10 wt% methods loading, 16-minute Ar-purge cycle and 53.2% versus 47.24% C2+ yields. Its conclusion instead says 20 wt%, correctly retained as a discrepancy. The process-model passage introduces steam as heat-exchange blocks; it does not report a steam durability experiment. Accordingly, the 2.02-minute allowance is an illustrative limit for fully restoring the specified Ar-aged yield, not the available headroom for unmeasured steam damage. Maintaining a steam/CO2 ratio is not a measured protective-dose requirement. These boundaries prevent a favorable arithmetic result from becoming a process-benefit claim.
