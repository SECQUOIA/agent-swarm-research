# Independent review of the developing program portfolio

**Historical review sequence:** the [final independent closeout](final-current-programs-review.md) supersedes the rankings and search recommendations below. Water has first priority for limited feasibility, cyclic oxides remain the reserve, and no new idea search is scheduled. Earlier assessments are retained to show how new evidence changed the judgment. Use the [current decision brief](../decision-brief.md) for present priorities.

2026-09-16. This review compares the five [development candidates](../working/program-development/README.md), their fresh reviews, the revised [selection standards](../standards.md), and the Ag/polymer baseline in the [decision brief](../decision-brief.md). It evaluates proposed research; no experiments or practical improvements have been demonstrated. The tungsten candidate was undergoing revision, so the [tungsten review](tungsten-sugar-program-review.md), including its Ti–O–W prior-art correction and limits on surface attribution, governs this assessment where it differs from the draft.

**Recommendation: develop tungsten coordination/retention as the first substantial-program candidate, with physical water management as the strongest alternative.** Both now have a defensible scientific question, a consequential possible outcome beyond a diagnostic, and a credible first experimental stage. Cyclic-oxide recovery deserves focused development as a reserve, especially if representative aged material and high-temperature cycling capability are available. Clean-feed polymer development is preferable to starting with ketone poisoning, but presently has a less distinct central chemical contribution and greater uncertainty about overlap. Keep liquid-zeolite work at a conditional test on an existing platform.

This is a change from the baseline statement that no substantial direction merits selection: there are now candidates worth selecting **for substantial program development**. It is not a recommendation to commission every proposed apparatus or execute every downstream campaign. Select the scientific direction now; size the initial experimental commitment around its first informative result and actual laboratory capabilities. A positive pilot is not a prerequisite for this development decision.

## Ranking and the reasons it is conditional

The order below ranks potential research programs under broadly comparable access, rather than estimating commercial returns or ranking the cheapest next measurements. None of the files verifies current group equipment, staff experience, materials access, or appetite for the relevant chemistry.

|---|---|---|---|

Tungsten coordination, sugar cleavage and retention** | Explain and predict when productive substrate coordination can be preserved while reducing W export; use that relation to choose an anchor or operating composition. | The active coordination environment and metal retention are chemically coupled. The result can change the catalyst architecture, rather than merely classify its post-run state. | The best anchored comparator already removes the relevant burden; hot carbon/W analysis cannot distinguish useful alternatives; or available conditions do not support interpretable cleavage. |

Physical water management** | Predict when physical mixing protects useful catalytic function through stationary transport, exposure history, or changed liquid access, and when it does not. | A substantial reported effect supports the premise. W first and water second is a judgment about scientific depth and the path to consequential knowledge, not a resolved numerical comparison. The first two deserve concentrated development; the current evidence does not justify spreading equal effort across all five. Cyclic oxides and polymer remain credible alternatives, not rejected ideas.

## Why tungsten is the strongest scientific lead

The most interesting question is whether the ligand changes required for selective sugar cleavage can be separated from the ligand changes that export or sequester W. That connects elementary chemistry, support coordination and catalyst retention. It fits Gounder's quantitative Lewis-acid/sugar chemistry and Iglesia's supported-oxide and kinetic reasoning particularly well. This assessment concerns scientific fit, not either group's present project commitments.

**The intended advance must start beyond successful anchoring.** Li et al. already report a Ni/W/Ti material with improved recycling and reduced W loss attributed to Ti–O–W bonding. Its publisher abstract is sufficient to invalidate a claim that productive, stabilizing anchoring is unprecedented; it does not settle the hot mobile-W contribution or establish a causal ligand-exchange rule. The full article and SI remain necessary for that more detailed comparison. [Li et al., DOI 10.1021/acssuschemeng.0c00836](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.0c00836).

The paired-W and mononuclear-W pictures also should not be sold as a contradiction until their solution composition, temperature, hydrogenation function and structural evidence are reconciled. The [fresh review](tungsten-sugar-program-review.md) correctly distinguishes dried or concentrated structural reference samples from hot working populations. Choosing one picture in advance would make the program weaker. A productive outcome could instead identify the operating domains in which different ensembles matter, and show which support or ligand perturbation moves the system between them.

The first investment should establish one reliable soluble glucose-cleavage reference, one supported material and its W-free support, and calibrated carbon/W recovery in a solid-retaining reactor. A short hot downstream test with newly supplied, distinguishable substrate can establish exported catalytic capacity. Its response to transfer delay and a feed–water–feed change can constrain short-lived mobile chemistry. These are attainable first decisions before a large spectroscopy or multizone-reactor commitment. A negative downstream signal does not prove a surface mechanism; a continuously dissolving reservoir can sustain upstream reaction while exported species decay before the assay.

The first deliverable therefore remains a **bounded mass/function result**, not proof of a permanently attached active atom. The substantial program is the next chemically specified intervention: change an anchoring or ligand variable, predict its effect on both cleavage and W export, and test an unmeasured condition. Low net W export can be operationally valuable even if rapid local release/return remains unresolved. Conversely, assigning a surface population has little value if it does not affect a catalytic or recovery decision.

Two work streams should stay connected. Tractable W/ZrO2 can develop the measurement, while the Ti-linked catalyst and low-W soluble architecture determine whether a worthwhile practical burden remains. The latter cannot be deferred until after a long mechanistic campaign. Deleting Ni or H2 from the Ti-linked preparation is not automatically a faithful comparator because it can change the working W state. If initial measurements require a cleavage-only domain, return explicitly to the tandem domain before claiming a glycol-process improvement.

The subsequent [process audit](../calculations/tungsten-process-burden.md) sharpens this comparison: the inspected low-W and integrated-recovery patent descriptions do not contain working-example datasets supporting their stated duration, conversion or recovery ranges. They establish relevant disclosed alternatives, not demonstrated long-run benchmarks or verified recovery economics. Feed-W cycling, reservoir release and pH adjustment are already disclosed strategies; a useful feed–water transient cannot itself claim their invention. The strongest immediate literature dependency remains the Ti-linked original and SI. Measure gross export, fresh makeup, recovered catalytic function and recovery duty separately, because reducing one need not reduce the others.

Confidence is strongest in the existence of substantive coordination chemistry, moderate in obtaining useful initial bounds, and uncertain in achieving a practical improvement. A rigorous result that rejects a particular anchoring strategy can justify the next chemical change. Repeated support substitution without a causal prediction cannot.

## Why water management is a strong alternative, with a different risk

Here the phenomenon is better established than the proposed explanation. The source reports a durable physically mixed cobalt catalyst, alongside lower initial activity than the unpromoted reference. This creates a serious question about the origin and useful transfer of protection. It does not establish that more hydrophobic material or another catalyst family will supply an original advance. [Fang et al., DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-76571-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76571-8).

The distinction between storage and conductance is physically sound: in the candidate's simple model, washout measures B/G while stationary source-generated excess depends on q/G. But this textbook distinction is the basis of a test, not the program's discovery. The [fresh transport review](ft-hydrophobic-transport-review.md) shows why spatial water images, faster desorption and proximity effects are already disclosed prior art. A useful contribution would predict the sign and meaningful size of a functional effect when geometry or exposure history changes, using independently constrained quantities.

The first experiment should combine reproduction of one packing contrast with absolute water balances, inert residence-time measurements and a reversible water-response interval on a stable catalyst state. Compare step-up and step-down behavior before selecting a storage/transport model. Then use one stationary imposed-water challenge and one history challenge to distinguish the operational consequences. This is a coherent first campaign; a hydrophobe library is not needed.

Its main risk is **measurement identifiability at the reacting catalyst**. Total bed water need not be the water pool coupled to cobalt. Packing and wax can also change H2/CO delivery, so a fitted water conductance may be compensating for several changes. A long series of outlet curves does not automatically solve that problem. Require an independent constraint when different physical descriptions fit the calibration but predict different protection. Useful empirical prediction remains possible, but the mechanistic claim must match its evidence.

This direction can become the first experimental choice if a suitable FT reactor, quantitative water handling and conditioned-material protocol already exist. It ranks behind W for scientific development because its broad intervention is established and its distinctive outcome currently depends more heavily on resolving coupled local transport. That is a real comparative risk, not a reason to demand direct imaging of every active site before starting. A validated startup rule could be consequential without proving stationary drying or working in another reaction.

## Why cyclic oxides and polymer deserve development, with smaller initial claims

The [cyclic-oxide proposal](../working/program-development/cyclic-oxides.md) has improved by centering the transition from recoverable to persistent loss rather than requiring an independently variable carbonate state. Chemical conversion, wetting, migration and ferrite changes may occur in sequence. A coupled mechanism is a legitimate chemical result when interventions and balances establish how it controls useful oxygen delivery.

The [source audit](cyclic-oxide-program-review.md) documents loss after substantial elapsed aging and corrects the benchmark's contact-time and product definitions. The first material comparison is therefore not a quick regeneration screen on arbitrarily damaged powder. It needs representative loss, followed by a small CO2/water regeneration comparison at common O2 pressure, with fresh treatment controls, direct water measurements, Li inventory and persistent subsequent performance. Characterization should discriminate the mechanism suggested by that result.

A convincing program would predict which subsequent exposure crosses a recoverability boundary and alter operation to avoid it. A finite unsuccessful regeneration establishes a bound for the tested treatments, not absolute irreversibility. Likewise, a recovered selectivity peak does not establish beneficial regeneration after downtime and gas use. The chemistry is substantial, but obtaining relevant aged specimens and resolving coupled shell/core changes makes its first stage less immediately tractable than the top two under equal platform access. Access to suitable aged material would change that assessment.

For [polymer](../working/program-development/polymer-clean-feed.md), the published clean-feed reuse loss is a better starting limitation than presumed differential ketone poisoning. The revised proposal properly distinguishes the response to feed unsaturation from unique loss of initiation. The same unsaturation change can alter activation, substrate supply, adsorption and evolving contact. Failed rescue can reject the intervention without showing that initiation remained intact. These limits survive even with carefully matched initial polymers.

The most valuable first work is to reproduce the reuse/handling effect with time-resolved output and residual polymer, bracket fresh-Na demand, and then test a small matched feed response. A useful handling fix may emerge before molecular attribution. For greater program depth, the result must connect catalyst history and chain populations to a prospective catalyst-use or cycle-time prediction and one meaningful intervention. Preparation of unsaturated probes is justified as a diagnostic; it is not yet an economical treatment recommendation.

The [fresh polymer review](polymer-clean-feed-program-review.md) identifies substantial initiation, activation, regeneration and kinetic prior art. The embargoed dissertation leaves a genuine overlap uncertainty, but does not imply that every remaining question has an unpublished answer. This uncertainty favors a distinct operational deliverable and bounded first stage. It does not justify either categorical rejection or immediate commitment to selective initiation loss as the program thesis.

## Relationship to the existing Ag/polymer decisions

Ag remains a sensible experiment when the promoted material pair and reactor are already functioning. Its application is unquestionably consequential within the portfolio's stated aims, and a small selectivity-retention gain can matter. It should not be demoted because its possible effect is numerically small. The limitation is that the current evidence leaves a relatively narrow additional contribution after historical Ni retention and modern chloride policies: a chemically supported material-versus-operation choice that predicts integrated useful output under a new history. The [decision brief](../decision-brief.md) already states this boundary clearly.

The distinction between **next experiment** and **program-development lead** resolves the apparent conflict. Existing Ag or polymer capability may make one of their bounded tests the best immediate use of a functioning laboratory. That does not establish that their present theses are stronger foundations for a new substantial program than W coordination or physical water management. Nor should the top-ranked scientific candidate automatically trigger construction of an unfamiliar high-pressure platform while useful existing capability is idle.

The clean-feed polymer question should supersede the ketone branch as the preferred route to developing polymer work. Retain the ketone assay only when the observed function loss and a relevant feed make its exposure limit consequential. For liquid-zeolite epoxidation, an existing platform can cheaply challenge the fixed-environment prediction, but there is currently less evidence of a distinct unmet scientific or practical problem than in the top candidates. The reason to hold it is overlap and weakly established residual headroom, not the absence of an exotic new mechanism. A useful advance in prediction or process operation could eventually suffice.

## What should happen before another broad search

**Targeted development now has greater decision value than adding another broad slate of ideas.** This portfolio has already identified several important problems; the unresolved ordering comes from specific prior-art and feasibility questions. More titles would not resolve those questions.

1. **Complete the consequential comparator reading.** For W, prioritize the Ti-linked material, durable W oxide and active-species paper; seek experimental low-W/recovery evidence beyond the audited patent descriptions and preserve its absence where none is found. For water, resolve the Cu comparator and distinguish the already disclosed imaging from a genuinely new prediction. For cyclic oxides, resolve carbonate/LSF and dissertation overlap. For polymer, preserve the inaccessible dissertation and original regeneration figure as explicit scope limits rather than repeatedly searching around them.
2. **Establish actual laboratory constraints.** For W: pressure-rated aqueous flow and sampling, carbonyl recovery, ICP access, and eventual wet-state structural measurements. For water: pressurized FT, calibrated humidity and wax/product collection, reproducible packing and catalyst-state stability. For cyclic oxides: sustained high-temperature cycling, representative aged material, direct water balance, Li/Fe measurements and controlled sample transfer. For polymer: inert pressure operation, time-resolved gases, complete polymer recovery, and sufficiently matched chain probes. Ag access requires a functioning promoted pair and an appropriate EO/chloride reactor. These are unanswered access questions, not claims that the groups lack those tools.
3. **Specify one first scientific decision for the lead.** Define which observations support exported W activity and which leave a reservoir mechanism unresolved, or which independent measurements allow a water-history/geometry prediction. Set useful contrasts and observation horizons from reproduced variability and the practical decision, not invented universal cutoffs.
4. **Expand for a consequential result or a discriminating negative.** A failed first material can still identify a productive next intervention. An uninformative assay, an already established effect, or an unidentifiable model does not justify enlarging a campaign simply by adding characterization.

Broader idea generation becomes valuable again if targeted audits close the remaining contribution of both leading candidates, or actual platform constraints make their first informative tests inaccessible at reasonable scope. There is no current basis for claiming that the scientific opportunity space has been exhausted. There is enough basis to stop treating the lack of a guaranteed successful pilot as evidence that no substantial program can be selected.

## Evidence and review limits

This is an independent portfolio judgment built from the named candidates and their source-specific reviews. It does not repeat every original-paper audit. I independently checked the publisher-indexed Ti–O–W abstract and the relevant primary Fang results; direct publisher opens were blocked, so these checks do not count as a fresh full-text/SI reading. Detailed structural-method and benchmark corrections elsewhere are attributed to the linked reviews. No new source identifiers were found that require an additional literature handoff; the sole literature agent's newer retrieval reports supersede the access states in the proposals.

Only this review file was written. The candidates, decision brief and literature knowledge base were not edited. The recommendation would be weakened by newly established close prior art or inaccessible decisive measurements, and strengthened by a precise informative first result. Its present basis is already sufficient for focused program development, while full experimental investment remains conditional on the actual capabilities and staged decisions above.

## Reconsideration after the recovered tungsten supplements, 2026-09-16

**The updated order is physical water management first for substantial program development, with tungsten coordination/retention second.** Cyclic oxides and clean-feed polymer retain their earlier conditional positions. This addendum supersedes the first-place W recommendation above; the original assessment remains as the record of how the judgment changed. Laboratory access can still change the order of actual experiments.

The new evidence changes more than bibliographic completeness. The [Li SI audit](tungsten-sugar-program-review.md) establishes seven reported Ni–W/M cycles with EG yield changing from 68.7% to 66.9%, and existing leachate-plus-Ni and soluble-AMT controls. Those results do not establish continuous lifetime, negligible hot W circulation, or the fraction of turnover occurring in solution. They do establish a stronger retained-catalyst benchmark and closer experimental precedent than an abstract-only anchoring claim. The main article is still needed to resolve recovery handling and the linkage evidence. A better hot-transfer assay is valuable, but cannot itself be the substantial contribution.

The attempted [glucose–mannose intervention](../working/program-development/tungsten-causal-intervention.md) also fails to provide the chemical selection rule that my original recommendation anticipated. The [fresh intervention review](tungsten-intervention-review.md) shows that hot mannose feeds produce glycolaldehyde, with substantial epimerization. The original inactive-mannose-reservoir premise should therefore be withdrawn. Similar soluble-feed output would not predict whether mannose increases extraction from a support, suppresses dissolution, stabilizes retained W, or changes the solution through degradation. The early unassigned carbon in the recovered SI further makes an informative cleavage-only window a demonstrated requirement rather than an assumed consequence of shorter sampling. These are substantive changes to hypothesis support and experimental informativeness, not penalties for missing a successful pilot.

W still merits focused scientific development. Productive coordination and retention can be coupled, and the existing preleach does not resolve substrate-dependent export during turnover. But the current package has not selected a manipulable support/ligand interaction with a sufficiently grounded chemical prediction to carry its central claim. Its immediate defensible work is reference reproduction, analytical qualification and a small substrate-present export contrast. That work may reveal the needed intervention; it is not itself a complete program thesis. Do not keep W first simply because the eventual coordination question sounds deeper or because a comprehensive inventory audit is possible. Equally, do not demand a uniquely assigned atomic structure or guaranteed improvement before supporting its development.

Physical water management now has the clearer connection between the remaining question and an experimentally distinct consequence. Separating storage from conductance and damage history leads to different responses to a water step, imposed background, geometry change and recovery history. These distinctions can guide a packing or operating choice on the same catalyst. The program remains conditional on identifying parameters relevant to the reacting catalyst and accounting for coupled H2/CO and wax effects. Its new first-place status does not establish water-only transport, novelty of physical promotion, or a practical advantage over the published mixture. The substantial target is a validated rule for protection and its limits; correcting a diffusion interpretation or collecting another washout curve remains insufficient.

I would therefore concentrate the next development effort on making that water-history/geometry prediction experimentally identifiable, while completing the W main-article comparison and qualifying one small chemical contrast. Reconsider W for first place if those steps identify a consequential, controllable coordination/export relation. This does not require that its preferred catalyst already work. No further broad idea search is warranted by these corrections alone: the top alternative already offers a serious research direction, and the remaining ranking questions are specific.

This reconsideration read the new intervention and both source-specific reviews; it did not independently re-audit the supplement figures. Their exact conditions and interpretation limits are preserved in the linked audits. No literature files or other proposals were edited.

## Reconsideration after the concrete first tests and comparator audits, 2026-09-16

**Water management remains the strongest candidate for substantial program development, but cyclic oxides now rank ahead of W as the reserve.** W should return to exploratory development until it has a chemically grounded intervention aimed at a consequential remaining limitation. Polymer pressure adjustment remains a modest experiment within an existing campaign. These rankings do not justify treating a succession of increasingly qualified diagnostics as an already established research program.

The [published-output calculation](../calculations/water-published-output.md) strengthens water management's importance: the integrated C5+ output proxy favors the reported promoted formulation substantially over its experimental reference after charging the initial penalty. Its normalization and uncertainty limits matter, and it is not a comparison with an optimized industrial catalyst. Nevertheless, the existing effect is large enough to explain why learning its cause and limits could be consequential. The problem is not an imagined benefit.

The [new first test](../working/program-development/water-management-first-test.md) is a credible operating-boundary experiment. Common conditioning followed by late additive introduction can challenge an explanation restricted to original startup. Imposed-water dominance constrains a whole-bed source explanation. But neither identifies local transport or intrinsic cobalt preservation. The [size analysis](../calculations/water-size-discriminator.md) further removes an attractive shortcut: radius-squared scaling can arise from external or internal resistance, and functional retention cannot be treated as a diffusion coefficient. Smaller granules replacing PDVB could be useful, but an empirical size substitution alone would not fulfill the proposed causal program.

**The deciding water uncertainty is whether accessible measurements can independently constrain at least one relevant transport/exposure contribution sufficiently to predict a consequential history or geometry response.** The program need not resolve every microscopic water pool. It does need a measured constraint beyond fitting the same output that it later explains. A defensible route could combine absolute storage, independently bounded transport and a qualified exposure-response interval; its practicality in the conditioned, wax-bearing material remains unverified. This is now the development task. Repeating cofeed, size and additive-removal comparisons without resolving that connection would reduce the work to a useful formulation study. Do not commission a broad campaign on the promise that enough outlet curves will eventually identify it. The unusual imposed-water challenge also needs a real operating use before it supports a process application claim; its immediate justification is mechanistic discrimination.

The [cyclic timing intervention](../working/program-development/cyclic-oxide-causal-intervention.md) has gained comparative strength. It connects a real steam-purge translation to a plausible chemical sequence: concurrent CO2 may prevent a change that subsequent carbonation cannot repair. This provides a concrete intervention with competing outcomes before a successful pilot exists. The [timing review](cyclic-timing-intervention-review.md) correctly keeps this distinct from explaining the documented Ar-protocol decline. **Its deciding uncertainty is whether process-relevant exposures create a measurable, consequential coating change that prevention can avoid more economically than recovery or makeup.** Trace Li recovery, intermediate specimens and realistic cycling can test that question. The dilute-steam diagnostic and potentially large CO2 requirement prevent presenting it as a ready operating strategy. It deserves reserve program development because the chemistry and process decision are connected, not because moving a pulse is inherently original.

For W, the [carbon-catalyst audit](tungsten-carbon-comparator-review.md) finds useful reuse but does not establish extraction as the controlling loss. The [ash-recovery audit](../calculations/tungsten-ash-recovery-comparator.md), confirmed by its [independent review](tungsten-ash-recovery-review.md), adds working recovery followed by product-rich continuous reaction. Its model-waste origin, short test, loading inconsistency and incomplete whole-process balance remain limitations. They do not erase that capability. The proposal cannot claim its advantage from immobilization, leachate activity or active recycling alone. **The deciding W uncertainty is which measured limitation of a credible reference is actually controlled by a manipulable coordination/export relationship.** No such core intervention is currently selected. Completing that identification can be worthwhile, but source auditing and hotter W accounting are supporting work, not substitutes for the program's chemical contribution. W's intellectual appeal no longer justifies second place on the present package.

The [polymer pressure test](../working/program-development/polymer-causal-intervention.md) and [review](polymer-pressure-intervention-review.md) are appropriately modest. The prior model does not predict that lowering ethylene improves the Na/W reference, and reactor-mode transfer, slow gas response and polymer-carbon attribution matter. A fixed-condition improvement may be useful. Program depth would require a measured history/feed-dependent pressure response that predicts a durable change in makeup demand. The new test does not yet supply that connection.

**A small search for stronger alternatives now has value alongside targeted development.** This revises my earlier recommendation against further searching. The additional work has exposed genuine novelty and identifiability limits; further prose refinement cannot resolve them. Concentrate development on the water measurement connection and the cyclic sensitivity/process window, while comparing a small number of alternatives against concrete, important catalytic limitations. Do not launch another large inventory of ideas or hold these candidates to a requirement for guaranteed success. Reopen the ranking if an alternative offers a clearer experimentally testable chemical intervention with consequential outcomes, or if actual capability checks show that the leaders cannot reach their decisive measurements at reasonable scope.
