# Cyclic-oxide audit after the literature uploads

2026-09-16. Independent reassessment of the existing coated-LSF program, timing experiment and prevention-value calculation. No new direction or material search. Source basis: original-derived `fulltext.md` from the two inbox-promotion runs and retained originals. Decisive PDF pages were visually checked as specified below. No KB files were edited.

**Recommendation: retain only a conditional, system-specific reserve experiment on steam-purge compatibility. The new papers do require corrections.** They improve the chemical basis for asking the question, but reduce the breadth of a defensible originality claim. They do not demonstrate that the proposed LSF coating suffers consequential steam damage or that concurrent CO2 offers useful protection. The calculation remains correct as a conditional decision threshold, not an improvement prediction. This audit does not independently compare the water, tungsten, polymer or liquid-zeolite programs.

The most consequential change is that **failed delayed restoration is already demonstrated in adjacent carbonate work**. The original documentation mentioned CO2 cover-gas stabilization but missed this closer overlap. The question still not answered by the reviewed sources is whether the modeled steam purge creates a consequential, preventable loss of catalytic function in this specific coating, with a useful full-cycle benefit after gas and time burdens.

## Findings that change the documentation

### Fereres: prevention and failed subsequent recovery are already adjacent prior art

[Fereres et al. 2018 original](../../literature/papers/fereres2018-molten-carbonate-salts-for-advanced/original.pdf), §3.6 and Fig. 9, printed p. 125/PDF p. 7, reports replacing air with 100% CO2 after 30 days at 640 °C. Further loss stops, but the sample does not recover its initial state under that treatment. The figure and text were visually checked. Its legend/caption disagree on the temperature of the separate higher-temperature air curve; the switched 640 °C trajectory is consistent and is the only one needed for this conclusion. The measured response is mass; it is not proof of a specific permanently lost Li species or complete speciation recovery.

This is more than generic CO2 stabilization. A broad proposal to discover that prevention can outperform recarbonation would be overstated. The existing equal-dose steam timing comparison is more specific: different material, steam exposure, catalyst function and burden-accounted operating result. That narrower question survives, subject to an actual meaningful effect.

Sections 3.4–3.5/Figs. 7–8 also strengthen an existing caution. Small flowing TGA samples and larger static-atmosphere tests have different loss histories, including long induction periods. Cold deposits have carbonate diffraction signatures. This supports explicit wall/trap inventory recovery and argues against treating a short TGA rate or absence of five-minute damage as a lifetime result. It does not supply a rate for supported Li2CO3 under steam.

### Chacko dissertation: the nearest carbonate/LSF overlap is no longer missing

[Chacko 2025 dissertation](../../literature/papers/chacko2025-molten-salt-promoted-namgwo-promoter/original.pdf), Chapter 4.1, printed pp. 95–102/PDF pp. 110–117, was read. Printed p. 101/PDF p. 116 was visually checked against its extracted equations and interpretation.

The chapter contains actual experiments with 45 wt% Li/Na/K carbonate on porous LSF, at 800 °C, using 20% ethane and approximately 3% O2/15% CO2 regeneration. It reports carbonate decomposition during inert purges and combined carbonate capture/oxide oxygen uptake. Thus coupled inventories and CO2-containing regeneration are established experiments, not merely an abstract-level proposal. These conditions differ materially from the present lower-loading Li2CO3/LSF question at 700 °C.

It also reports H2 during regeneration, attributed to reduced Fe reacting with water generated by carbonation of hydroxide. This makes the proposed restoration step more coupled than a gas label suggests: **dry inlet CO2 can generate water internally and affect the core**. The timing protocol already measures CO/H2 and carrier state; those measurements should explicitly include the entire final carbonation/reset. A common uptake plateau alone cannot establish equivalent core states.

The chapter carefully states that COx allocation assumes steady cyclic behavior, negligible reforming and no carbonate decomposition during reduction. Do not import its yield or allocation as a matched benchmark. Equation 7 as printed omits the factor of two before XOH; the balanced reaction is `2 XOH + CO2 → X2CO3 + H2O`. This source typo does not alter the qualitative water-generation point.

### Steam chemistry is better supported, but a coating loss rate remains unknown

- [Berkowitz et al. 1960](../../literature/papers/berkowitz1960-heterogeneous-reactions-studied-by-mass/original.pdf), printed pp. 533–540; species-identification and experimental sections checked. Printed p. 533/PDF p. 2 was visually checked. Li2O with water vapor at 1100–1400 K and approximately 0.1 mm Hg water produces predominantly LiOH vapor, with smaller hydroxide oligomers. The previously unusable source is now readable. This is direct evidence for possible vapor species, at a different condensed phase, temperature and pressure. It neither identifies species collected from LSF nor predicts their flux at 973 K.
- [Young et al. 2020](../../literature/papers/al2020-molecular-equilibrium-and-activity-limitations/original.pdf), Results Figs. 2–4 and Conclusions, supports nonideal carbonate/hydroxide equilibrium and dissolved-water effects in mixed melts. Most calculations are at 923.15 K, with 0.1 bar water; the Na/K case is at 1023.15 K. No supported-LSF rate law follows.
- [Kussainova and Panagiotopoulos 2025](../../literature/papers/kussainova2025-chemical-reactions-in-molten-lithium/original.pdf), results and conclusions, especially printed pp. 7289–7290, directly treat reactive mixtures and vapor–liquid equilibria. Printed p. 7289/PDF p. 8 was visually checked. A high-CO2 mixture test at 900 K collapses unphysically because training coverage is insufficient. The model supports relevant equilibrium chemistry but is not validated for the present supported coating or low-temperature mixture response. No practical Li-loss rate is supplied.
- [Ochoa-Fernández et al. 2009](../../literature/papers/fernandez2009-effects-of-steam-addition-on/original.pdf), stability discussion/Figs. 6–8 and Conclusions, shows faster carbonation/regeneration but poorer cycling stability with steam in alkali zirconate/silicate acceptors. The authors leave sintering, phase segregation and alkali vaporization unresolved. Their quotation of Mohn/Wendt's vapor dependence is a secondary report; the unavailable original still must not be treated as read or its law transferred to this coating.
- [Lee et al. 2001](../../literature/papers/lee2001-influence-of-aluminum-salt-addition/original.pdf), pp. 93–95, confirms precursor-derived alumina reacting with carbonate to form LiAlO2 during matrix sintering. The current warning about container/support consumption remains sound; dense alumina tube/grit kinetics are not established by this experiment.

These additions increase confidence in the *plausibility of perturbing salt chemistry*. They do not increase confidence to the same degree in irreversible Li loss, a measurable five-minute response, or a practical benefit from protection.

## Findings that confirm or sharpen existing limits

[Liu et al. 2021](../../literature/papers/liu2021-molten-salt-mediated-carbon-dioxide/original.pdf), Figs. 2–3 and reaction-pathway discussion, directly establishes molten carbonate/hydroxide cycling and CO2 capture. The retained [Vogt-Lowell full text](../../literature/papers/lowell2024-molten-salt-mediated-chemical-looping/fulltext.md), Results and Experimental Section, extends this chemistry at 800 °C with a 10-minute ethane step, 3-minute carbonation and intervening 6-minute Ar purges. Neither is the proposed steam-protection experiment. Neither carbonate chemistry nor combined carbon capture should be claimed as original here.

[Chacko et al. 2025](../../literature/papers/chacko2025-effective-cox-suppression-by-a/original.pdf), Results and Conclusions, links promoter coverage and restricted oxygen release to suppressed COx in NaMgW/MgMn oxides. [Chacko et al. 2026](../../literature/papers/chacko2026-unveiling-the-role-of-oxygen/original.pdf), Figs. 4–6, changes post-oxidation purge time, oxidation duration and oxygen partial pressure to alter Fe–Mn phases and selectivity. This is direct prior art for regeneration-history control of selective function on promoted oxides. A generic dynamic oxygen-state project would add too little.

[Morales et al. 2026](../../literature/papers/morales2026-bridging-steady-state-and-chemical/original.pdf), Figs. 7–10 and Conclusions, supports the existing concern that dynamic selectivity must be evaluated with conversion and cycle-average yield. Its frequency optimization is partly a model prediction, not experimental validation of each optimum. It does not examine steam or carbonate loss.

[Luongo et al. 2022](../../literature/papers/luongo2022-oxygen-uncoupling-and-chemical-looping/original.pdf), Fig. 5 and cyclic Methods, provides an especially useful negative boundary: stable function and measured Na inventory over 200 cycles in a different carbonate-coated oxygen carrier/M1 system at 500 °C. Coating loss is not inevitable. Its solid Na2CO3 shell and dilute-feed/two-bed arrangement cannot validate molten Li2CO3/LSF steam compatibility.

[Chan et al. 2018](../../literature/papers/chan2018-enhancing-the-capacity-of-oxygen/original.pdf), Figs. 5–9 and Discussion, and [Song et al. 2023](../../literature/papers/song2023-chemical-looping-oxidative-propane-dehydrogenation/original.pdf), results on the diffusion-controlled pellet stage, confirm existing prior art on selective capacity, bulk delivery and phase cooperation. [Dahan et al. 2023](../../literature/papers/dahan2023-selective-partial-oxidation-of-methane/original.pdf), Methods and §3.1, shows CO2 changing LSF oxygen/redox behavior during methane conversion at 900–950 °C. This supports the core-state confound but does not quantify it at 700 °C.

[Liu et al. 2023 perspective](../../literature/papers/liu2023-mixed-oxides-as-multi-functional/original.pdf), §§2.1.1–2.1.2 and concluding discussion, was also screened directly. Its active/inert shell taxonomy, requirement for core–shell compatibility, and explicit concern about high-temperature salt vaporization reinforce existing scope limits. This is contextual synthesis rather than new primary evidence of the proposed steam-loss mechanism; it does not require another program change.

## Recommended commitment and changes made

Retain the existing one-pair timing test, its intermediate specimens, dry reference, full inventories, common endpoint and washout. Explicitly track water generated during the reset. Proceed to a representative steam-rich comparison only if the initial work resolves a consequential response or useful compatibility bound. Do not promote the reserve to a broad aging or materials campaign on the strength of these uploads. Stop the practical protection branch if ordinary restoration is sufficient or the relevant steam exposure is benign within a meaningful uncertainty bound.

Scientific confidence is moderate for altered carbonate chemistry, lower for irreversible coating loss under the selected short exposure, moderate and platform-dependent for an informative comparison, and low/uncertain for practical improvement. The updates favor *narrower scope*, not abandonment: the specific process translation remains unresolved and could still matter.
