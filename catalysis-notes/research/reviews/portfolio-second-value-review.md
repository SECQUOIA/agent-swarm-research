## Decision

**Develop Ag epoxidation as the first-priority conditional research program. Retain zirconia and zeolite assay history as narrower, lower-priority pilots.** The core Ag question is whether combustion suppression by dilute Ni remains useful in the working Cs/Re/Cl-promoted catalyst. Oxygen return is a specific mechanistic branch, not the program's defining experiment or admission requirement.

The evidence is sufficient to justify a program with explicit continuation decisions. It is insufficient to claim superior performance, a new promoter combination, or a proven oxygen-transfer mechanism. Those are outcomes to establish. Requiring them before selecting research would confuse evidence that a question is worth pursuing with evidence that its answer is already known.

This recommendation does not arise from eliminating the other candidates. Ag has its own positive case: two experimentally demonstrated interventions in the same important reaction, a concrete compatibility question, and a direct connection between the targeted loss and useful output. Its first comparison addresses that connection. Zirconia and assay history require additional discoveries before their local unknowns become equally consequential.

## Why Ag has a stronger starting point

Hwang et al. report a distinct Cs/Re-dependent chloride response and interpret it through an additional selective oxygen-use pathway. Jalil et al. show that dilute Ni suppresses combustion more strongly than EO formation on supported Ag, with further chlorine promotion. The two studies therefore supply actual catalytic behaviors whose coexistence can be tested. They do not establish that the same oxygen population mediates both effects. See Hwang, pp.9–13 and 16–17, [article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2026.117021), [local text](../../literature/papers/hwang2026-mechanism-and-site-requirements-for/fulltext.md); Jalil, manuscript pp.6–9, [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adt1213), [local manuscript](../working/source-artifacts/jalil2025-nickel.txt).

The mechanistic tension is useful: making an oxygen population less effective at combustion could preserve selective chemistry, inhibit a useful route, or alter the working surface without that retained oxygen turning over. Each possibility changes how one should combine or condition the promoters. A nonzero statistical interaction is unnecessary. Independent benefits that survive appropriate conditioning can be valuable; apparent synergy at an unfair chloride setting is not.

The practical target is also direct. At fixed EO output, suppressing combustion reduces reaction consumption of ethylene and oxygen. The exact relation follows from the measured carbon selectivity, rather than from a speculative downstream mechanism. The [practical-metrics calculation](../calculations/epoxidation-practical-metrics.md) supplies this accounting. It does not establish plant economics, available headroom over the best commercial formulation, or attainable improvement.

The [patent disclosure](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20260027557A1/en) prevents presenting Ni/Cs/Re/Cl itself as a new combination. It does not resolve a quantitative compatibility mechanism or establish an optimized, independently verified performance comparison. Conversely, using an already disclosed combination places greater weight on explaining its working behavior and demonstrating a consequential result. A nominal composition series alone would be a weak program.

The zirconia and zeolite questions remain legitimate. Their downside is not that their hypotheses are unproved. It is that a positive initial result has a comparatively weak connection to the requested advance: an oxygenated product or a changed acid assay can be real while adding little to catalyst choice or mechanism. The Ag comparison measures useful branching and output from its beginning, although explanation and durability still require further work.

Ease of a first experiment does not determine the ranking. Zeolite history may prove easier to compare on available material; the record does not establish actual laboratory access for any candidate. A convenient measurement should not displace the stronger scientific question without considering what it can decide.

## The Ag program should have one central deliverable

**Determine whether Ni's suppression of combustion can be retained alongside the useful Re-dependent response, and establish the chemical reason for the resulting sustained EO performance.**

This deliverable allows a favorable, adverse, or additive result. It does not require a uniquely assigned Ni-to-Re oxygen transfer. A defensible separation between primary selectivity, secondary EO destruction, and chloride-controlled working-state changes already provides meaningful mechanistic knowledge when it explains a consequential catalyst choice. Merely fitting different apparent rate constants to each formulation does not.

A strong positive result would show a reproducible improvement in the attainable selectivity/output tradeoff, establish which reaction contribution changed, and predict a relevant additional condition from that account. A strong adverse result would show that an otherwise effective Ni modification compromises the Re-dependent working state, identify the responsible chemical limitation sufficiently to guide formulation or operation, and define where it applies. Neither result must establish universal behavior for all Ag architectures.

If the only finding is an unresolved difference between preparations, stop at a formulation report. If the benefit disappears after each catalyst receives appropriate chloride conditioning, establish whether Ni offers any remaining operational benefit before pursuing an oxygen-sharing explanation. Saving trace Re is secondary to maintained output, selectivity, and catalyst service; it should not become the default justification when those gains fail to appear.

## Resource priority and first decision

Allocate the next substantive research effort to **establishing the two parent Ag behaviors and then comparing their combination**, with the existing published methods as the starting point. First determine whether the necessary catalysts, synthesis procedures, reacting-state analysis, and conditioning capability are already available or can be reproduced within the intended project's scope. This is a capability and scientific-feasibility decision, not an assumption that particular instruments exist.

The first scientific decision is: **does adding Ni improve or materially alter the attainable EO-output/selectivity relationship of the reproduced Cs/Re catalyst after separate chloride adjustment?** Use the proposed Ni × Re comparison at fixed Cs to interpret that answer. Jalil's Ni parent lacks Cs, so failure of Ni/Cs to outperform Cs–Ag must not be mistaken for failure to reproduce the original Ni effect or uniquely blamed on Re.

Compare sustained net output and resolved combustion rates, not a transient selectivity maximum. Treat slow conditioning as part of obtaining the working state, rather than repeatedly adding shorter experiments that never reach it. Select the decisive output range from the reproduced reference and a stated catalytic operating objective. Analytical uncertainty, repeatability, and the practical output constraint should set the required resolution; an arbitrary percentage improvement should not.

If both parent behaviors cannot be reproduced, stop the compatibility comparison and report the specific obstacle. A broad synthesis search is a different investment requiring its own rationale. If they reproduce and the combination provides a useful difference, concentrate further characterization on explaining that difference. Do not automatically execute every oxygen, chloride, spectroscopy, and lower-Re branch.

Keep zirconia behind this program in the resource queue, with its existing bounded stopping rule. Keep zeolite history available when it can resolve a concrete durability-method decision using relevant material. There is no scientific reason to launch all three extensive campaigns to preserve portfolio breadth.

## Oxygen return: valuable when identifiable, insufficient as the whole program

The return calculation identifies a real ambiguity: in its restricted stationary cycle, recombination and return of leftover oxygen can reproduce the net balance attributed to additional selective oxygen transfer. The required return is `(e − 3z)/2`, using the specified EO and CO2 rates and the model's boundary. This provides a quantitative target, not evidence that return occurs.

The [feasibility review](epoxidation-isotope-feasibility.md) materially limits the interpretation. A mixed-O2 measurement can constrain return that enters the bulk gas only with defensible isotope-pairing and recovery bounds. It cannot generally bound oxygen that reforms and reacts locally before reaching that gas. A positive scrambling signal also includes exchange unrelated to productive turnovers. Consequently, a small signal can reject a specified bulk-return explanation under its assumptions; it cannot establish a Re-bound intermediate or exclude every recombination route.

This makes the isotope branch a conditional source of additional fundamental knowledge. It is worth developing when a reproduced working state, product-balance precision, and a defensible measurement model permit a useful exclusion or prediction. It does not warrant a new instrument campaign merely because the nominal mixed-O2 signal appears detectable. Its practical importance should be stated in terms of the design inference it changes, rather than equating closure of one mechanistic loophole with a catalyst improvement.

Conversely, inability to identify that flux does not demote a successful compatibility program to unimportant work. Meaningful reaction decomposition and a reproducible sustained performance benefit can justify development without assigning every oxygen atom. A useful fundamental result can also survive without a performance gain if it distinguishes competing explanations strongly enough to change the catalyst-design rationale. These are different evidentiary routes to value, not interchangeable claims.

## Final recommendation

Replace “no substantial research lead established” with **“Ag selective oxygen use is the selected conditional research program; its performance and molecular mechanism remain unestablished.”** Develop the program around Ni/Cs/Re/Cl compatibility and the useful reaction branches. Make the first commitment parent reproduction and a fair output/selectivity comparison. Reserve oxygen-return measurements for a specific identifiable question that the initial results make worth answering.

This is a stronger opportunity than the other two because its known interventions, mechanistic question, and useful output are already connected. Its conditions protect that rationale; they should not become an indefinite sequence of controls that prevents the proposed research from receiving a clear decision.
