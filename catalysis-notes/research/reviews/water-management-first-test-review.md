# Independent review of the imposed-water first test

2026-09-16. Reviewed the [first-test proposal](../working/program-development/water-management-first-test.md), [main program](../working/program-development/physical-water-management.md), [earlier transport review](ft-hydrophobic-transport-review.md), and [program standards](../standards.md). No experiments are available. Only this review was written.

**Recommendation: develop this bounded first experiment, with a handling pilot and a more precise endpoint.** It is an informative extension of the published additive effect, and does not need to identify every transport pathway before proceeding. It can test whether late PDVB addition improves the response of a conditioned formulation to a specified water challenge. It cannot yet establish preservation of intrinsic cobalt activity, stationary local drying, or a useful humid-feed process. These distinctions should govern the decision before the paired runs.

The proposal correctly distinguishes the headline low dose from the much larger dose in Fang's changed-flow and humid-pretreatment controls. Fang's main methods specify 0.025 g PDVB per 0.5 g catalyst; SI Figs. 30–31 explicitly use 1.0 g. Fig. 3a reports the 78 mg water/(g catalyst h) cofeed without repeating its additive dose. The proposal's generated-water estimates and its approximately 2.5% generated/supplied ratio for the new high-water plateau are arithmetically consistent with their stated assumptions. Those balances support a different experimental regime, not proof of a different local water activity. [Fang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76571-8), main Figs. 1 and 3, Methods “Catalytic tests,” SI pp. 31–32.

## Common conditioning is useful, but transfer must be qualified

A common parent followed by randomized splitting is a credible way to remove differences in the original activation and wax-forming history. It controls the distribution of initial states; it does not make every granule identical. Conditioning a larger inventory can itself produce axial differences. Recover the full relevant inventory and split representative portions rather than assigning the upstream portion to one arm. Document retained liquid and catalyst recovery on a catalyst-mass basis; wet recovered mass is not catalyst mass.

The practical uncertainty is recovering and mixing wax-bearing granules without appreciable oxidation, liquid loss, segregation, or attrition. A small parent-only trial should establish whether matched cooling, inert transfer, repacking and restart produce reproducible function and a sufficiently stable diagnostic interval. The present statement that *any* resolvable handling-induced change invalidates the split is too strict: a reproducible common shift can define a new conditioned starting state. Uncontrolled shifts, selective loss, or damage that destroys the intended comparison are the failures. Do not condition until an already damaged reference merely appears stable.

Preserve the source's distinction between synthesis reduction/passivation and analytical pretreatment. The FT methods do not explicitly specify the 300 °C hydrogen treatment used for TKA. SI Note 10 also refers to a dry-hydrogen “standard test” without resolving the full restart sequence. This is a documentation issue to record during platform qualification, not permission to silently choose a favorable activation. Paterson's activation study supports the need for this care, but its serial beds were disconnected and tested separately; it does **not** validate unpacking and homogenizing a conditioned wax-bearing bed. [Paterson et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cattod.2024.114559), [local primary text](../../literature/papers/xu2024-fischer-tropsch-cobalt-activations-the/fulltext.md), pp. 8–11 and Fig. 9.

Adding PDVB after splitting still permits an effect during restart. Thus a positive result excludes an effect confined to the **original** conditioning, not every startup mechanism. That limitation is already acknowledged and is acceptable for this first stage.

## Define recovered function without calling it intrinsic survival

Keep both quantities already proposed: each formulation's post/pre productivity ratio, and its absolute useful productivity before and after the challenge. For example, a reference falling from 100 to 80 and a promoted arm remaining at 60 gives better fractional retention with PDVB but lower useful output. The ratio is informative about stability; it does not charge the initial penalty by itself. Cumulative useful output over the fixed observation window supplies the practical comparison without extrapolating lifetime.

The stronger interpretive issue is that the two recovery assays retain different formulations. A constant multiplicative PDVB inhibition would cancel in its post/pre ratio. An inhibition or promotion that changes with water history, liquid distribution, or catalyst state would not. Conversely, inhibition during the assay could mask genuine preservation. Common gas conditions therefore establish **recovered operating function of each formulation**, not an additive-independent count of surviving cobalt sites. No need to remove PDVB for this first operational claim. If the result warrants an intrinsic-preservation claim, a later matched additive-removal assay with sham handling, or an independent catalyst-state constraint, must address that ambiguity; removal is itself a perturbation.

Predefine the recovery endpoint and require water and product signals to settle sufficiently for that endpoint. A persistent loss over that interval is not proof of permanent damage. Also establish background drift over the **whole challenge-plus-recovery horizon**, rather than only a short prechallenge window. If it is material, include a time-matched low-water sham for each formulation before attributing differential loss specifically to the high-water step.

## Supplied-water dominance narrows one explanation

The high-water balance strongly suppresses the whole-bed production difference as an explanation. It does not categorically exclude it without a bound on water sensitivity: a small bulk-water difference could matter near a sharp damage threshold. A parent-only check around the measured plateau can determine whether the residual bulk difference is consequential. Otherwise say the explanation is constrained, not rejected.

There is also a useful asymmetry: at 2% conversion, the proposed **0.5 bar diagnostic** has generated/supplied water of approximately `0.25 × 0.02 / 0.025 = 20%`, compared with 2.5% at 4 bar. Its recovery assay need not have matched bulk or local water exposure between arms. Report the actual balances there as well. Raising flow does not necessarily lower generation per granule; neither the flow check nor a matched outlet proves negligible intragranular gradients or equal local H2O/H2.

The high-flow requirement is feasible in principle but needs an equipment check. Merely preserving the source's promoted CO consumption while lowering conversion to 2% at inlet CO fraction 0.25 would require about 68 L/(g catalyst h), on the source's volume basis, versus 2.4 L/(g catalyst h). This is a planning illustration, not a predicted rate at the new partial pressures. Water delivery, heated collection, pressure drop and dilute-product quantification need qualification at the selected flow. A generated-water signal only a few percent of supplied water should be constrained through the product oxygen balance and measurement uncertainty, not asserted from subtracting two large, noisy water signals.

## What the outcome should enable

| Outcome | Defensible decision |
|---|---|
| Better recovered fraction and useful absolute output | Continue low-dose development for this defined disturbance and state. Investigate the responsible transport/interface change next. |
| Better recovered fraction but lower useful output | Retain a mechanistic stability result; no process advantage has been shown over this interval. |
| Only ingress or recovery transients improve | Develop a specific operating-history prediction if the measured output benefit warrants it; do not claim stationary drying. |
| No benefit, with source effect reproduced and handling/challenge qualified | Establish a boundary for late addition in this state. Startup intervention or internally generated water remains viable, rather than becoming the proven explanation. |
| No reference loss or unstable handling | Redesign or stop this particular challenge. It has not falsified the additive effect or the broader program. |

A useful next geometry intervention, conditional on a positive contrast, is a paired catalyst-granule-size comparison with and without the same low PDVB dose, preserving promoter granule size and documenting packing. Use matched conditioning and measured per-catalyst generation under the imposed-water challenge. Smaller catalyst granules change the internal diffusion length; increasing flow alone does not. Predefine the expected change in promotion under the candidate resistance model, and compare it with the alternative of ordinary size improvement in the unpromoted arm. Fang already varies size and mixing, so the contribution must be a tested prediction under the defined source/history boundary, not another size screen. Changes in contacts or liquid state remain possible competing explanations.

Finally, remove the immediate inference to humid-feed or water-recycle application. An externally supplied challenge is a useful diagnostic even if no process desires that inlet. Such an application needs an actual operating envelope and a reason that accepting this water burden creates value. The first result can justify mechanistic and formulation development without inventing that process case.
