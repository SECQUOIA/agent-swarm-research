# Current uploads: closed alternatives and earlier retained studies

Audit date: 2026-09-16. This review checks the two latest supplied-file promotion runs against the existing formaldehyde, lactate and fluorochemical screens and the earlier retained studies. It does not generate a new direction. No experiments were performed and no knowledge-base files were changed.

**Recommendation: keep all three alternative searches closed.** The uploaded originals require source-status corrections and more precise comparisons. They strengthen several reasons for closure; they do not establish a new intervention worth promoting. The earlier Ag, CHA, zirconia, Ga-PDH, ammonia and wet-carbonylation decisions do not acquire a direct contrary result from these uploads. That last conclusion is a source-to-question mapping, not a fresh exhaustive review of every historical citation.

### Lactate: material scientific qualifications

[Murphy, Letterio and Xu, 2017, DOI 10.1021/acscatal.6b03166](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.6b03166), [original](../../literature/papers/murphy2017-catalyst-deactivation-in-pyridine-assisted/original.pdf), PDF pp. 4–5 and 15–17, confirms the proposed salt-blocking chemistry for pyridine/NaY. However, the headline near-90% dehydration selectivity is not sustained near-90% yield. Table 2 combines three 10 h reaction segments separated by regeneration: cumulative ML conversion is 31%, dehydration yield about 27.5%, and carbon balance 97%. Regeneration only partially and temporarily restores activity; the 30 h denotes reaction time, not total elapsed processing time including regeneration. The authors themselves call pyridine a mechanistic model rather than a likely practical solution. A future comparator must charge regeneration and modifier handling. This finding supports the existing conservative decision rather than rescuing the proposed campaign.

[Pang et al., 2021, DOI 10.1021/acscatal.1c00573](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.1c00573), [accepted manuscript](../../literature/papers/pang2021-on-the-spatial-design-of/original.pdf), pp. 5–8, 13–14 and 21–22, demonstrates coupled basicity, pore access and local steric limits. Tetramethylguanidine reaches 89 C% selectivity initially but falls to 80 C% by 10 h with carbon balance below 80 C%. The previously available 2023 diamine study remains distinct: pyridine salt accumulation cannot simply be assigned to every diamine without evidence.

[Kim et al., 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.jcat.2026.116949](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2026.116949), [original](../../literature/papers/kim2026-critical-role-of-silanol-defects/original.pdf), pp. 3 and 8–13, supports an amine-free K/silanol comparison much more firmly than the old abstract-only entry. The main text reports about 81% AA selectivity at full LA conversion over 16 h, and >99% conversion with about 79% mean AA selectivity over 100 h. The latter references SI Figure S14, which is not retained in this package and was not independently inspected. Main methods specify 10 wt% aqueous LA, 325 °C, and LHSV 0.64 h−1; Figure 4 gives 0.67 h−1. Preserve this small source discrepancy when building a quantitative comparison. Neither condition is automatically equivalent to an ML/amine feed.

The same paper explicitly states on p. 13 that its calculated reaction energies are **not kinetic barriers**. Thus the stronger wording in its conclusion about lowering a barrier should not be recast as a calculated activation-energy result or a uniquely established operating pathway. It supports a defect/cation hypothesis and measured performance; it does not remove the need to distinguish changes in acidity, accessibility, composition and local chemistry. No new K/silanol program is proposed here.

### Formaldehyde: stronger prior art with explicit modeling limits

[Raun et al., pellet model, DOI 10.1016/j.cej.2018.12.142](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2018.12.142), [original](../../literature/papers/raun2019-modeling-of-the-molybdenum-loss/original.pdf), pp. 5–10, establishes water suppression of Mo loss, methanol/temperature acceleration, internal redeposition and the pellet-scale transport mechanism. The measured mass-loss data fit the model; depletion profiles provide an additional comparison. The proposed volatile molecular identity remains model-based, not a directly identified universal gas species. High-temperature deviations and FeMoO4 formation are explicitly acknowledged.

[Raun et al., reactor model, DOI 10.1016/j.ces.2019.03.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ces.2019.03.020), [original](../../literature/papers/raun2019-modeling-of-molybdenum-transport-and/original.pdf), pp. 6–9, fits pressure-drop parameters to 20 days of pilot operation, then simulates two years. The 0.44 bar base case versus 0.22 or 0.37 bar front-bed alternatives is a model result. The authors identify fixed axial profiles and the absence of selectivity prediction as limitations; reducing pressure drop alone may sacrifice useful selectivity. This is consequential prior design work, not a validated prediction of whole-process lifetime.

[Pitchers et al., DOI 10.1039/D5CY00211G](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5CY00211G), [original](../../literature/papers/pitchers2025-the-selective-oxidation-of-methanol/original.pdf), Table 2 and pp. 3, 6–8, reports its best 42.5 mmol HCHO gcat−1 h−1 at 250 °C, 15.8% conversion and 80.5% selectivity. That preparation/productivity comparison does not establish long-term retention under the proposed feed intervention.

[Liu, Cheung and Iglesia, DOI 10.1039/B302776G](https://doi.org/10.1039/B302776G), [original](../../literature/papers/liu2003-effects-of-al2o3-support-modifications/original.pdf), pp. 1 and 5–6, directly establishes supported MoOx/VOx DME oxidation and support modification. Table 2 gives Mo-normalized rates 4.6 versus 12.2 mol (g-atom Mo h)−1, with 98.1 versus 97.7% primary HCHO selectivity, for unmodified versus SnOx-modified supports. These are primary, methanol-free selectivities; direct comparison with an integral-feed selectivity would be unfair. No matched DME/methanol Mo-export result was identified in the inspected text.

[Zhang et al., preprint DOI 10.2139/ssrn.6126583](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6126583), [original](../../literature/papers/zhang2026-tungsten-modification-promotes-the-selective/original.pdf), pp. 6–16, reports 80.0% DME conversion/80.1% HCHO selectivity on W-FeMo versus 75.0%/79.1% on FeMo at 290 °C and GHSV 4000 h−1. Its inspected evidence addresses adsorption/activation and composition; it does not establish the proposed matched Mo-export benefit. It remains a preprint. The original screen's stronger powder/pellet comparisons remain the key practical constraint.

### CF4: access gap closed; benchmark interpretation confirmed

[Chi et al., DOI 10.1002/anie.6752036](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.6752036), [original](../../literature/papers/chi2026-entropy-stabilized-aluminate-catalysts-that/original.pdf), pp. 4 and 7–9, verifies water orders −0.40, −0.22 and −0.13 for γ-Al2O3, ZnAl2O4@γ-Al2O3 and the multication aluminate, respectively. Its sequential/isotope evidence already addresses lattice-oxygen consumption and replenishment. The authors still leave the precise short-lived CFx oxidation sequence unresolved.

Figure 5 uses 1073 K, 0.67 kPa CF4, 33 kPa water and CF4-based WHSV 0.2 h−1. The aluminate falls from 98.6% to 91.6% conversion over roughly 100 h and then stays approximately constant to 150 h. The authors deliberately accelerated deactivation by increasing WHSV twentyfold and CF4/water concentrations 2.7-fold relative to earlier tests. This strengthens the material comparator, but is not an optimized equal-resource process comparison with the lower-temperature Ga/SO2 papers. The missing Chi SI and native pulse-patent figures remain distinct gaps; their absence is not evidence that reported controls do not exist.

## Documentation and source actions

The three current closed-screen documents were corrected in place: [formaldehyde](../working/program-development/alternative-oxidation-search.md), [lactate](../working/program-development/alternative-acidbase-search.md), and [CF4](../working/program-development/fluorochemical-program-search.md). Historical artifact descriptions remain labeled as such. No aggregate ranking was edited by this reviewer.

Remaining source limits within this audit are Murphy SI, Kim SI (especially the 100 h trace), Chi SI, the native pulse-patent figures, and the already listed plasma full-text gap. These are existing-source gaps, not newly proposed literature directions. They do not justify reopening a rejected intervention on the present evidence.

Two metadata corrections were sent to root for the sole literature worker: the Raun pellet-paper author list omits Sina Baier, present on original p. 1; `names1997-transient-and-steady-state-studies` still lists unknown authors despite retaining its original. The latter belongs to the water audit. No literature metadata was edited here.

The practical recommendation is therefore to retain the current limited scopes, remove stale access claims, and use the more exact comparators above. There is no basis in this subset for claiming either that everything was already correct or that any closed program should now be restarted.
