# Independent review: water-dependent deactivation of m-ZrO2 ketonization (direction-search candidate)

2026-09-16. Fresh reviewer; did not write the [screen](../working/direction-search/liquid-phase-oxygenate.md). Standard applied: [standards.md](../standards.md) and the bar used in the [program value challenge](program-value-challenge.md). No experiments; nothing under `literature/` changed. Sources were re-read from open copies: Miller et al. 2022 accepted manuscript (NREL/OSTI copy, docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy22osti/81353.pdf), the NREL deck (docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy22osti/80795.pdf), Huq et al. 2021 (docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy21osti/78354.pdf), Shylesh et al. 2020 accepted manuscript (escholarship qt6nf4v5bw), Lin et al. 2023 (OSTI 2552922). Wang & Iglesia 2017 (J. Catal. 345, 183; JPCC 121, 18030) could not be retrieved (Iglesia site and Elsevier manuscript host unreachable; abstracts withheld by publisher); their water statements remain unverified by both the screen and this review. Web search budget ran out before the last prior-art queries; Crossref, Europe PMC and OSTI API queries were used instead.

## Verdict

**Do not schedule. Keep as a documented conditional check, one rung below the screen's "bounded diagnostic".** The mechanistic boundary is real and the design is competent, but three findings lower the stake and the originality below what the screen states: (1) NREL's own TEA shows ketonization catalyst lifetime is economically almost irrelevant down to three months; (2) NREL's 2022 deck shows the route owners were already measuring deactivation at 0 versus 7–11 kPa water; (3) the closest published water/recovery data on the sister oxide (anatase) show full reversibility and negligible deactivation. The screen's "retain as bounded diagnostic" is not wrong on the science; it is too generous on the stake. Disagreement preserved below.

**Has anyone measured k_d of m-ZrO2 ketonization versus water pressure at matched acid pressure over ~100 h?** Not in any retrieved open source. But the NREL deck shows the measurement in progress by the group best placed to do it, and the group has said it will publish. This is a "someone is measuring it" situation, not "no one has".

## 2. Discriminability of H1–H4 (judgment)

- **k_d precision.** The only published m-ZrO2 loss trajectory on a neat feed is 6% in 48 h then 2% in 24 h (Huq Fig. S4): small amplitude and decelerating, not first-order. A "first-order k_d" is then a fit to a few percent of rate change over 100 h. Resolving a 2-fold contrast in k_d requires rate precision of about 1–2% sustained over 100 h and between two independent beds per arm; typical GC rate scatter and bed-to-bed variation are larger. The screen's own "reset thresholds from measured scatter" is an admission that the resolvable contrast is unknown; a likely outcome is "unresolved", which the standards say is not evidence of equivalence.
- **Reference returns confound the wet arms.** The Bhan-type correction returns the bed to a reference condition every ~10 h. For the wet arm, that return is a water-off purge, which is the very intervention (dry purge at reaction temperature) whose recovery is under test. Either the wet reference is itself wet (then k_d values across arms are comparable only through the inhibition model) or the "continuous wet" arm is actually a cyclic wet/dry history. The screen does not address this.
- **Hydroxylation versus carboxylate accumulation.** DRIFTS can separate OH from carboxylate bands, but at 350 °C and 40 kPa acid the surface is near-saturated with reversible carboxylates; the persistent fraction of interest is a small perturbation on a large reversible population. TPO carbon does not distinguish persistent carboxylates from condensation deposits. The one clean discriminator is the H4 water-only pretreatment arm (no acid, then dry rate); it is cheap and should be run first, not alongside.
- **Frozen early-recovery prediction.** Predicting the 100-h recovery ladder from a 2-h dry purge at 20 h is formally prospective, but at 20 h the recoverable signal is dominated by reversible inhibition, and a failed prediction cannot be separated from precision limits. It satisfies the letter of the frozen-prediction rule without much discriminating power.
- **Carbon-support inference.** As chemistry the inference is sound: a carbon support cannot survive 500 °C air burn-off, and none of the ZrO2/C or TiO2/C papers report oxidative regeneration. It is not decision-relevant to this campaign: measuring bulk m-ZrO2's recovery ladder says nothing about whether ZrO2/C accumulates the same species or needs any regeneration; and the carbon hybrids target dilute aqueous feeds (15 wt% acid in water), a different process configuration from NREL's neat vapor feed. Mild (lower-temperature, CO2 or steam) regeneration of carbon is also conceivable and untested. The "carbon supports viable at all?" decision requires testing a carbon-supported catalyst, which the screen rightly declines to synthesize.

## 3. Practical stake (judgment, using NREL's own numbers)

The route is pre-commercial. Of the three decisions the screen lists: (1) interstage water removal is already specified by a >97% reactor-size argument; a lifetime term could only reinforce it, and only H3 (water-protective) would create a trade-off, which the reactor-size number would still dominate. (2) Non-oxidative regeneration: air regeneration of a $50 kg⁻¹ fixed-bed oxide is demonstrated to restore rate within error; the value of a dry/H2 purge is regeneration downtime, which NREL's TEA does not price but which is small for a swing-bed of a cheap catalyst. (3) ZrO2 versus TiO2 is deferred by the screen itself. NREL's TEA sensitivity (lifetime 3 y / 2 y / 0.25 y) puts a 3-month ketonization lifetime within 1% of baseline cost. A design rule for a route that does not yet operate, whose owner's economics are insensitive to the parameter measured, is a weak stake for this portfolio. An informative negative (H2) validates an assumption the TEA already shows to be non-critical.

## 4. Overlap with the zirconia styrene pilot

Shared: monoclinic ZrO2 (one parent preparation could serve both), H2O-pulse site titration, XRD/N2 physisorption, a flow reactor with online GC/MS. Not shared: temperature (350 versus ~450 °C), water regime (kPa cofeed versus ppm-level trace), the site population (near-saturation carboxylate-covered pairs versus the 0.56 nm⁻² minority population titrated in dehydrogenation), and the question (slow loss under a water-rich feed versus oxygen fate of hydrocarbon cleaning). Merging into one "Zr–O pair water tolerance" study would dilute both: neither result predicts the other, and the styrene pilot's gate (net styrene output, oxygen balance) is orthogonal. The one genuinely shared, cheap experiment is the H4 arm: expose m-ZrO2 to water only at 350–450 °C, then measure recovery under dry feed and count sites by titration. If the styrene pilot commissions, run that arm on the same specimens as a materials experiment; do not merge the programs.

## 5. Standing relative to the Ag Ni-retention study

**Below Ag, and below the zirconia styrene pilot.** Ag concerns an operating commercial process with a quantified selectivity-to-feedstock lever and a specific historical claim to test; its weakness is platform access. The ketonization diagnostic is easier to run and has cleaner kinetics, but its stake is smaller (Section 3), its originality is at risk from the route owner's in-progress study (Section 1), and its most likely outcome is either an unresolved contrast or a water-independent, air-recoverable loss that changes no decision. It belongs with the portfolio's retained conditional checks (carbonylation wet-feed versus drying, Ga regeneration endpoints): documented, not scheduled.

**Result that would justify promotion:** a replicated, several-fold water-dependent loss at 25 kPa versus dry over ~100 h on m-ZrO2, not recovered by dry purge but recovered by H2 or air, and shown to change cycle-integrated ketone output per catalyst mass relative to interstage water removal for a defined wet-feed configuration (for example dilute aqueous VFA without upstream drying). Water-only restructuring (H4) confirmed in the vapor phase at 25 kPa would also be publishable, but it would strengthen the case for the drying already specified rather than change a decision.

**Result that closes it:** k_d water-independent within replicate precision with air recovering the fresh rate (H2); an unresolvable contrast at attainable precision; or publication of NREL's deferred deactivation study covering the water contrast, whatever its outcome.

## Disagreements with the screen, preserved

1. The screen calls the assumed lifetimes "the point of attack"; NREL's own sensitivity shows a 3-month lifetime is within 1% of baseline cost. I read this as removing most of the practical stake; the screen did not weigh it.
2. The screen states the recovered rate after air regeneration was "not verified here"; Huq Fig. 3C verifies it within experimental error. The benchmark for "non-oxidative regeneration viable" is therefore stricter than the screen implies.
3. The screen lists Lin 2023 only as mechanism; its full-reversibility and negligible-deactivation statements on anatase (≤3.8 kPa, ≤553 K) are direct evidence against H1 on the sister oxide and should be in the table.
4. The screen treats the absence of an NREL follow-up as an open gap; the 2022 deck shows the gap was being measured by the route owners. I weigh this as an originality risk; the screen may reasonably reply that an unpublished measurement leaves the literature boundary open.
5. The screen's wet-arm design with periodic dry reference returns confounds continuous wet history with the purge intervention under test; this needs a redesign before any campaign.
6. Minor: η is 0.94, not 0.96; the ketone non-inhibition test used isopentanoic acid.
