# Working-state measurement gate for dilute Ni/Re-promoted Ag

2026-09-15. Independent feasibility and identifiability audit of the [Ag program](../programs/ag-selective-oxygen-use.md) and [chloride-retention branch](../working/ag-chloride-retention-hypothesis.md). No measurement or equipment access has been established.

**2026-09-16 source correction:** Jalil SI and Iyer2023 main are now retained and incorporated below; the [post-upload audit](post-upload-ag-audit.md) documents original-PDF checks. The 2026-09-15 scope at the end remains a historical reading record.

## Decision

**Keep the committed experiment kinetic. A structural-retention claim needs a separate, passed feasibility gate.** The sources inspected do not establish that a measurement on the proposed dilute, fully promoted catalyst can distinguish its consequential minority Re population from chlorine conditioning or Ag coarsening.

If a useful, reproducible Ni-dependent retention difference survives the existing kinetic and recovery gates, select the cheapest measurement that addresses the remaining explanation. A validated **total-Re inventory comparison** can test gross promoter loss if that is the live alternative; the existing **Ag-size comparison** can test gross coarsening. Neither identifies retained interfacial Re. For that stronger question, the single structural candidate examined here is **one Re-edge differential X-ray absorption experiment**, comparing defined working states of the matched pair. It is conditional on resolving a contact-sensitive signal at the actual loading and under the relevant feed, pressure and temperature. An observable Re absorption edge or oxidation-state difference alone does not pass this gate. Do not commit to a Ni/Re/Ag multi-edge campaign or a microscopy/spectroscopy suite to compensate for an unidentified observable.

The immediate scientific result can be a predictive relation between chloride history, Ni addition and useful output. Calling that relation preservation of productive Ag–Re contact would exceed the present evidence.

## 1. What the primary measurements establish

### Jalil 2025: dilute Ni can produce a spectrum, but this is not the required working-state measurement

The [retained author manuscript](../working/source-artifacts/jalil2025-nickel.txt), manuscript p. 5, reports Ni K-edge high-energy-resolution fluorescence-detected XANES on an approximately 9 wt% NiAg200/α-Al2O3 catalyst, with supported approximately 5 nm NiO as a comparison. **The uploaded SI clarifies the states:** Figures S14–S16 compare calcined and reduced NiAg spectra collected in He with supported-Ni spectra obtained across a temperature-programmed reduction series. NiO and reduced Ni references fit the supported-Ni reduction series but fit the NiAg spectra poorly. The authors interpret the distinct environment as evidence for Ni incorporation in Ag. This excludes adequacy of that particular two-reference description; it does not uniquely count isolated surface Ni, identify Ni–Re association, or establish the state during chloride-containing EO synthesis. [Original SI, PDF pp.21–23](../../literature/papers/jalil2025-supplementary-information-for-nickel-promotes/original.pdf).

For nominal Ni:Ag = 1:200 and Ag mass fraction 0.09:

`w_Ni = 0.09 × 58.6934 / (200 × 107.8682) = 0.00024485`,

or **approximately 245 ppmw Ni per total catalyst mass**. Here 9 wt% refers to the supported Ag formulation; it is not 9 wt% Ni. The calculation is nominal and does not replace the uninspected SI's measured composition table. The historical catalyst contains **88 ppmw Ni and 280 ppmw Re at 13.5 wt% Ag**, corresponding to Ag:Ni ≈ 835:1 and Ag:Re ≈ 832:1 atom ratios. Its Ni concentration is approximately 2.8-fold lower than the nominal HERFD sample. These ratios say nothing by themselves about which promoter atoms occupy active interfaces. [Historical preparation and source audit](ag-older-ni-prior-art.md).

Jalil supplies a useful precedent for obtaining an element-specific spectrum at a dilute loading. It supplies no calibrated lower bound on an interface fraction distinguishable in the historical or proposed catalyst, no demonstrated EO-operando sensitivity, and no proof that a smaller change in a minority population is resolvable. A published Ni near-edge measurement also does not establish usable Re extended fine structure under a different gas-cell geometry.

### Hwang 2026: strong functional motivation, no calibrated structural marker

[Hwang's main text](../../literature/papers/hwang2026-mechanism-and-site-requirements-for/fulltext.md), Sections 2, 3.1 and 3.3–3.4, uses composition, Ag size, kinetics and chloride transients to infer promoter-associated ensembles. The proposed Re retreat into Cs-containing domains and restoration of Ag–Re contact are mechanistic interpretations. The [SI](../../literature/papers/hwang2026-supplementary-information-for-mechanism-and/fulltext.md), Sections S1–S5, supplies transport checks, Ag size distributions and kinetic analysis; it does not supply a Re/Ni working-state spectrum or an interface-population calibration. Section S4 also explicitly shows that distinct kinetic descriptions fit the existing data comparably. Consequently, neither a kinetic derivative nor a fitted ensemble fraction can be used as a measured count of Re contacts. [Hwang et al., DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2026.117021).

The reported approximately 10–50 h transients make repeated spectra during well-defined windows conceivable, but they do not prove that sufficient counts can be collected before the state changes or that a spectroscopic cell will reproduce the reactor behavior.

### Keijzer 2025: the loading and state gap is material

[Keijzer's main text](../../literature/papers/keijzer2025-insight-into-the-influence-of/fulltext.md), Characterization, Table 1 and the AgReO4 discussion around Figures 3–4, deliberately raises Re loading to facilitate characterization. Calculating from its ICP Ag loading and Re/(Re + Ag) atom fraction gives approximately **936, 5801 and 9086 ppmw Re** for 0.4Re–Ag, 2Re–Ag and 4Re–Ag, respectively. For example, the first is `0.146 × [0.0037/(1−0.0037)] × (186.207/107.8682)`. Even this lowest loading is approximately 3.3-fold above the historical 280 ppmw.

Its conspicuous Ag/Re STEM-EDX maps in Figure 4 concern the separately prepared **15 wt% AgReO4 model**. They do not validate dilute working-interface mapping. XPS finds predominantly Re(VII), but that oxidation state can occur in different Re-containing phases. The study also reports Ag particle growth from approximately 70–80 nm to 90 nm after reaction. Thus size evolution is a real confound, while neither the maps nor oxidation-state assignment closes the proposed contact-retention question. [Keijzer et al., DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4CY00858H).

### Marsh 2024: working Re spectra are feasible; contact assignment remains unresolved

[Amy Ruby Marsh's Durham thesis](https://etheses.durham.ac.uk/id/eprint/15818/1/Marsh000892601.pdf), printed pp. 59–61, 129, 143–150 and 193–197, provides a closer precedent: fluorescence Re L3-edge XAS at **500 ppmw Re and approximately 10 wt% Ag**, including Cs/Re materials with approximately 600 ppmw Cs. B18 capillary experiments used 5% ethylene/10% O2 at 225/275 °C; at least ten spectra were averaged. Transmission sensitivity was inadequate. Methods p. 60 calls the edge K; results consistently label L3.

The author could not fit the Re/Ag or Cs/Re/Ag α-alumina spectra satisfactorily using the attempted structural models. Assignment of Re–O–Ag versus Re–O–Al requires a better model. Changes at 275 °C accompany Ag sintering. These are real working-state spectra at a relevant loading scale, **not validated contact counts**. The stated feeds contain no Ni/chloride comparison and do not demonstrate the required pressurized chloride experiment. STEM-EDX also suggests Ag/Re colocation (p. 134), without a calibrated minority-contact population.

This source materially improves collection feasibility, while directly illustrating the assignment gap.

## 2. Why an element-average spectrum may miss the decisive change

This is a measurement-model argument, not a claimed instrumental detection limit. For two spectroscopically distinct Re populations, an ideal normalized absorption signal is

`μ(E) = f μ_contact(E) + (1−f) μ_other(E)`.

If only their fraction changes, then

`Δμ(E) = Δf [μ_contact(E) − μ_other(E)]`.

Obtaining a clear Re edge establishes sensitivity to total Re. Resolving redistribution requires sensitivity to the much smaller difference on the second line. A small population with high turnover frequency can control much of the output. A large kinetic change therefore supplies no lower bound on `Δf` without an independently justified bound on productivity per relevant site.

For a predefined scalar spectral contrast with endmember separation `D`, let `a` be the measured before/after difference and `u` its justified uncertainty bound, including drift and model uncertainty. Under this two-population model, a null can exclude a proposed change of at least `f_min` only if `|a| + u < f_min |D|`. Neither `D`, `u` nor `f_min` is established here. This inequality makes the missing information explicit; it is not a numerical sensitivity claim.

There is a second limitation even with excellent counting precision: two populations can have similar spectra. Re oxo species moving between support/Cs domains and an Ag-associated region may retain similar oxidation state and first-shell Re–O coordination. Functional proximity need not create a unique, readily resolved Re–Ag scattering path. Conversely, a Re–Ag contribution could belong to an inactive AgReO4-like phase. A Ni–Re association would not establish that Re remains in productive contact with Ag.

Extended fine structure could help only if a proposed Ag-neighbor contribution is identifiable against alternative neighbors, disorder and other plausible phases over the actual measured data range. Apparent coordination changes can trade off with disorder or mixture fractions. A fitted path chosen because it matches the desired mechanism is not a demonstrated contact measurement.

High-resolution near-edge detection can sharpen spectral contrast; it does not remove population averaging or turn an oxidation-state marker into a spatial-contact count. Collecting another element edge without a specific discriminating prediction adds cost without automatically removing these ambiguities.

## 3. Choose the smallest informative contrast first

**If the remaining hypothesis is total promoter loss**, compare absolute Re inventories before exposure and after the defined return, using a representative starting aliquot and quantitative recovery/digestion of the exposed catalyst. Use recovery checks and the same assay on the processing/sham control. Concentration alone can mislead if catalyst/support mass changes, sampling is unrepresentative or material is lost during handling. A detected loss beyond the validated recovery uncertainty can support promoter depletion; it does not specify which Re sites disappeared or prove that the loss caused the output change. Quantitative retention can exclude gross loss down to the reported bound, but cannot exclude redistribution or loss of a decisive minority below that bound. Do not add effluent traps or assume a volatile Re species without evidence that escape is plausible under the chosen exposure. The starting paper's discussion of possible volatility is not a measured Re flux in this experiment.

This inventory comparison is worthwhile only when distinguishing loss from retention changes the next material or recovery decision. If the live question is relocation at constant inventory, repeating elemental analysis cannot answer it. A spatial map could answer a resolved location question only with independently demonstrated loading sensitivity, representative sampling and state preservation; Keijzer's concentrated model maps do not establish these for the proposed working catalyst.

A reaction-state chemical titration might measure an accessible population that changes with history, but its interpretation also needs a validated stoichiometry on the Ni/Re pair. The now retained [Iyer/Bhan chemical-titration main](../../literature/papers/iyer2023-chemical-titration-of-promoted-ag/original.pdf) explicitly treats total TFE uptake as an upper-bound estimate because inactive adsorption remains possible. Table 1 gives means of 56.1, 29.7 and 20.7 μmol/g for total-uptake, linear-region and initial-slope estimates. The roughly fivefold comparison with N2O is probe- and estimator-dependent, not a calibrated fivefold count of true working sites. Its SI remains unavailable. Do not treat an adsorbate capacity as a count of Ag–Re contacts or assume that a potentially state-changing titration preserves the state whose retention is in question. This is not an additional required experiment.

Keijzer supplies a direct caution against using ordinary oxygen uptake as that shortcut: its high-Re materials store oxygen through Re redox chemistry, and the hydrogen pretreatment used for chemisorption changes the AgReO4 model's phase inventory and subsequent uptake. Those observations do not invalidate a separately qualified working-state titration, but they prevent equating generic uptake with retained Ag area or productive Re contacts in this promoter family.

## 4. The one conditional structural gate

**Candidate:** fluorescence Re L3-edge absorption, including extended fine structure only if the required contact contrast is supported. Marsh establishes a relevant collection precedent; the proposed fully promoted comparison still needs qualification. This is not a claim of available access or established discriminating performance. It targets Re because the selected hypothesis concerns retention of a Re-containing contribution; Ni K-edge alone is less direct for that question.

Before scheduling a full history experiment, the provider and investigator must demonstrate all of the following in one bounded feasibility effort:

1. **A relevant working baseline.** Use the actual dilute promoted catalyst and an attainable cell geometry at the required conditions. Simultaneous outlet analysis must reproduce its useful EO/CO2 response and the selected chloride history. A higher-loading surrogate or a reducing treatment can help interpret references but cannot substitute for this baseline. Beam exposure and repeated scans must not themselves change the spectrum or functional response beyond the comparison uncertainty.
2. **A predefined discriminating contrast.** State which spectral feature or identifiable coordination contribution separates the proposed contact-bearing and alternative environments. Candidate references/calculations must address those alternatives; metal Re, ReO2 and a perrhenate reference alone do not establish a productive-contact marker. If the only resolvable observable is average valence, close the contact branch.
3. **A sensitivity bound for that contrast.** Repeated measurements of the same working state, including a return/sham where appropriate, must bound drift, normalization/background errors and fit uncertainty. Reference-mixture or signal-injection recovery using the actual noise can test fraction sensitivity **under the stated spectral model**; it cannot validate that model or prove that its components exist in the catalyst. Report the smallest fraction change that the demonstrated contrast could exclude, rather than borrowing a generic ppm limit from an instrument specification.
4. **A consequential fraction scale.** Explain why the resolvable fraction change addresses the hypothesis. If no independently justified minimum affected fraction exists, a null remains non-discriminating even when the edge is clean. Allow a positive exploratory structural observation, but do not sell this as an experiment that can rule out all microscopic retention mechanisms.

These requirements are deliberately a gate. If useful signal, interpretable contrast, realistic state reproduction or available access fails, retain kinetics-only scope. Do not repair a failed dilute measurement by increasing promoter loading until a different catalyst becomes measurable.

### Minimal state comparison after the gate passes

Reuse the consequential matched pair and exposure already selected by kinetics. For each material, compare its reference working state with the state after the same excursion and bounded return to the common reference feed. Add a recovered state only if recovery actually changes the functional conclusion. Make each contrast relative to its own baseline and its existing ordinary-drift control; an initial Ni-induced spectral difference is not evidence of retention.

Acquire spectra within measured, sufficiently stable windows or explicitly average the simultaneously measured output over each acquisition. Do not interpret a scan spanning a changing state as an instantaneous intermediate. Preserve the original chloride/product/temperature histories. A room-temperature ex situ comparison is not equivalent: quenching, depressurization, air exposure and sample preparation can alter the mobile promoter state. Without state-preservation validation it answers only what survives that handling.

This is one measurement modality on a small set of already justified states. It is not permission for a characterization survey.

## 5. What the outcomes would mean

| Result | Defensible conclusion | Remaining limit |
|---|---|---|
| Ni-free catalyst loses an independently identified contact-sensitive Re contribution; Ni catalyst retains it; the change follows functional loss/recovery | Evidence that Ni-dependent output retention accompanies preservation/restoration of that measured Re environment | Does not alone prove that the environment causes output, or that Ni acts independently of chlorine. Require the resulting state relation to predict the held-out exposure/recovery response. |
| Re spectrum changes, but only average valence/first-shell coordination is identified | Ni/chloride history changes an average Re environment | No assignment of Ag–Re proximity or productive contact. Chlorine ligation/redox and inactive-phase changes remain alternatives. |
| Spectrum is unchanged and sensitivity excludes the proposed population change under a validated contrast model | Reject that specified sufficiently large redistribution model within the tested states | Does not exclude a smaller, spectrally similar, or highly active minority; report the actual bound. |
| Spectrum is unchanged with unknown minority sensitivity or nonunique contrast | Measurement is non-discriminating | Neither retention nor absence of retention is established. Stop structural escalation. |
| Contact-like signal changes but output does not, or it also changes in the unexposed control | The observed structural change is not a specific marker of the consequential history-dependent effect | It may describe a spectator, ordinary drift or measurement-induced change. |

**Chlorine conditioning:** common inlet chloride, a restored inlet feed or equal total chlorine inventory does not establish equal active chlorine populations. Even a genuine Re-location change can be downstream of changed chlorine deposition/removal. A structural signal would distinguish a literally unchanged-Re conditioning model; it would not establish a chlorine-independent action of Ni. Use the existing operational history/steady-curve and recovery comparisons first, and keep the stronger causal attribution conditional when site-specific chlorine remains unresolved.

**Generic Ag coarsening:** reuse the program's required pre/post Ag-size-distribution comparison. It is the smallest check on gross particle growth, but an unchanged distribution only excludes changes within its sampling/resolution; it does not exclude atomic surface reconstruction or interface loss below that scale. If differential coarsening accompanies the effect, a simultaneous Re-contact difference may be a consequence of lost Ag surface area. The one-edge experiment then cannot uniquely assign direct Ni–Re stabilization. If no representative Ag-size comparison is available, retain this alternative rather than asserting that Re spectroscopy has eliminated it.

## 6. Scope and stopping decision

The structural hypothesis is plausible but currently underidentified. The inspected studies establish useful functional and spectroscopic precedents, but none establishes the required sensitivity and assignment for the decisive minority interface in the proposed catalyst.

Proceed now with the matched kinetic history/recovery experiment only if its operating burden and output difference justify it. Treat the Re-edge measurement as a conditional future probe with one explicit contrast and a measured sensitivity bound. Until that gate passes, describe any result as **Ni-dependent retention of useful Re-dependent function**, when the Re-dependence control supports that wording. Do not call it direct observation of Ni/Re working-state retention or preserved productive Ag–Re contact.
