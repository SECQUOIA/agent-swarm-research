## Decision

**The patent materially strengthens the reason to investigate polymer catalyst use. It does not establish a substantial original program, or a stronger mechanistic opportunity than the bounded Ag comparison.** Polymer now has a documented practical burden rather than an abstract concern about dirty feeds. A limited study that determines whether a useful feed specification must control instantaneous exposure or cumulative damage is justified now. Its value includes rejecting a proposed protection mechanism or showing that the required feed purity makes this route unsuitable for a specified feed.

The earlier portfolio statement that polymer first needs a verified catalyst/feed baseline should be updated: much of that baseline is now available. The remaining uncertainty concerns what controls useful catalyst throughput and whether resolving it changes a defensible process choice. Successful new experiments are not a prerequisite to proposing this investigation. They are required before presenting a particular intervention as effective or expanding its scope.

I would retain polymer alongside Ag as a serious bounded decision study, while keeping Ag provisionally first if its platform already works. I cannot rank their absolute experimental cost without facility information. Polymer has the clearer demonstrated inventory and feed-preparation problem; Ag has a reported material-retention association and a concrete operating competitor. Neither has an established new chemical rule likely to deliver the substantial outcome sought here. A functioning polymer platform and an unavailable Ag platform could reasonably reverse this practical ordering.

## What the new source changes

The [patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2026011187A2/en) makes four facts consequential:

- The standard experiment uses 0.8 g combined catalyst per gram PE (§0165). This creates a strong reason to study cumulative use; it does not establish the minimum required inventory or prove stoichiometric operation.
- Output falls on repeated clean HDPE charges, and adding fresh Na/alumina reportedly restores output (§0135). Supplementation is already known; the subsequently uploaded Science Figure 4B specifies 400 mg before charge 2 only, with only PE added before charge 3. Contaminants cannot be assumed to explain this clean-feed decline.
- PET, PVC and DEHP additions depress output, but each is a 50 mg addition to 1 g HDPE (§0168). These tests establish vulnerability at a large controlled dose, not a realistic trace specification or a measured poison capacity.
- Postconsumer samples undergo dissolution, precipitation, drying and grinding (§0198). These operations remove some contaminants and alter polymer morphology. A benefit from this treatment cannot be assigned uniquely to chemical cleaning, and laboratory solvent charge is not net industrial solvent consumption.

The substantial practical question is therefore **how much accepted polymer carbon a retained catalyst inventory can process, under a specified feed preparation**, rather than whether a fresh batch can give a high endpoint yield. This question has value even if the mechanism is familiar. However, a series of impurity-versus-yield curves would mainly characterize a particular recipe; it would not by itself supply the deeper contribution requested.

## Additional prior art changes the chemical starting point

The original screen places considerable weight on cumulative W poisoning. That is a hypothesis, not a general property of supported tungsten metathesis catalysts.

Van Schalkwyk et al., [*Factors that could influence the activity of a WO3/SiO2 catalyst: Part III*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0926-860X(03)00536-2), studied 8% WO3/SiO2 and 1-octene at 460 °C. Their 200 ppm 2-pentanone experiment showed little activity/selectivity effect; switching back changed selectivity and product color (pp.149–150, Figure 6). A distinct recycle experiment tolerated roughly 300 ppm but lost conversion at 400 ppm and regained initial activity on clean feed (pp.147–149, Figure 4). These are different conditions, not a universal threshold. The initial review used exposed main-text passages; the 2026-09-16 audit read the uploaded original and visually checked Figures 4 and 6. The recycle experiment also reports transformed carbonyl-containing products (p.149), so parent-ketone delivery cannot alone identify the effective exposure. Neither response supplies a tolerance limit or recovery guarantee for the 320 °C Na/W polymer mixture. Post-exposure selectivity changes also preclude assuming an identical recovered catalyst state.

Maksasithorn et al., [*NaOH modified WO3/SiO2 catalysts for propylene production from 2-butene and ethylene metathesis*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1872-2067(12)60760-8), provide a second caution. The uploaded full text confirms loading-dependent W leaching and activity loss, but retained metathesis performance for higher W loadings, with altered acidity, induction and selectivity. Its aqueous NaOH treatment, drying and calcination (§2.1) and 400 °C gas-phase test (§2.3) differ materially from polymer operation. This is an aqueous preparation treatment, not demonstrated Na migration during polymer conversion. It rules out treating detected Na near W as sufficient evidence of destructive poisoning.

These sources narrow the opportunity: neither reversible oxygenate inhibition nor alkali modification of W is new. The potentially useful knowledge is whether **different exposure laws for the two operating components explain and predict the lifetime of their coupled polymer conversion**, including when a cumulative impurity budget fails.

## One specific prediction worth testing

**Proposed hypothesis:** for one delivered ketone, operating W metathesis is predominantly inhibited while the ketone is present, whereas Na/alumina loses a fraction of its useful initiation/isomerization function that persists after the ketone has been removed. Under those conditions, early rate suppression and later loss of polymer throughput will be governed by different variables: the concentration history reaching W and the cumulative chemically effective exposure of Na/alumina.

Use 2-pentanone initially because the older W study tested that molecule, giving a concrete chemical comparison across conditions. Its reported tolerance at lower concentrations is as relevant as its recoverable inhibition in the higher-concentration recycle test. At 320 °C, **W tolerance, recoverable inhibition and persistent loss must all remain live outcomes**. It is a mechanistic probe, not a claim that this compound dominates waste contamination. Coordination of a carbonyl to W provides a plausible inhibition route. Reaction or persistent retention of carbonyl-derived species on the basic Na/alumina component provides a plausible persistent-loss route. Condensation products, water formation, and changes in Na distribution are alternatives to simple ketone adsorption; none is established here. Do not declare a stoichiometric Na–oxygen capacity from bulk elemental amounts.

The distinguishing prediction is operational: **after measured ketone removal, W-alone metathesis should recover substantially more than Na-alone function, and the remaining polymer-output loss should be predicted by the surviving functions without assigning permanent W damage merely from the original yield decline.** Recovery means the measured response under the stated conditions, not proof that an identical molecular site population returned.

This is more specific than “the bottleneck moves,” but its originality remains provisional. The Science main article/SI are now available and the inspected methods/results contain no identified ketone exposure/recovery test of the operating mixture. The unavailable dissertation body and patent regeneration figures still limit the novelty assessment. The study is justified by the decision at stake and the conflicting plausible exposure responses, not by silence in unread sources.

### A small informative campaign

1. **Establish clean reuse and handling controls.** Use the disclosed recipe and one clean PE. Include three clean charges on retained solids and the published companion control with 0.4 g fresh Na solid added before charge 2 only; measure polymer-derived carbon output and full cycle time for each. Quantify an ordinary uninterrupted run and the actual repeated-charge handling sequence, with the same total polymer, elapsed time and catalyst inventory where the apparatus permits. A cooled, handled catalyst and an operating catalyst are different histories. Keep this baseline separate from the added-ketone effect.
2. **Measure operating-component responses to one ketone exposure.** Use W with a defined internal alkene/ethylene reaction and Na/alumina with validated operational isomerization and initiation readouts. Include support controls and a clean interruption. Observe the initial response and recovery directly; do not use a long fresh-substrate assay that silently activates a previously inactive W sample. Measure ketone delivery, transformed products, water and removal. A missing inlet molecule is not automatically captured poison.
3. **Challenge the proposed exposure laws.** Compare the same total delivered ketone in two concentration histories at a common total observation time, substrate supply and temperature. Choose histories after measuring mixing and removal times. Use the component responses to predict the tandem response prospectively. Account for changing polymer access and gas–melt transport before interpreting a history effect. No universal rule says dilution improves integrated output: even reversible inhibition can give a nonlinear response to concentration.
4. **Test one feed connection.** Characterize one relevant feed and its existing preparation contrast. Determine whether it actually delivers the tested carbonyl class or the same damaging products at relevant exposure. Use the measured exposure and remaining functions to predict repeated-charge polymer-carbon recovery. A ketone-only result cannot specify acceptable PET, ester, PVC, filler or total-oxygen levels.

An absolute W site count, spectroscopy campaign, novel adsorbent and new regeneration procedure are unnecessary for this decision. If assays cannot discriminate operating loss from assay-induced recovery, report empirical exposure/output limits and narrow the mechanistic claim. Component recombination remains a supporting function test, with the confounders identified in the earlier independent review; it is not the unique proof of protection.

## What each outcome would mean

| Outcome | Valuable conclusion and practical boundary |
|---|---|
| W response is mostly reversible; Na function and polymer output retain an exposure-dependent loss | A transient low rate need not justify discarding W. A component-specific feed specification and the disclosed Na supplementation policy can be assessed quantitatively. Neither selective recovery of powders nor profitable W reuse is assumed. Their physical implementation remains a process constraint. |
| Both components recover under normal clean feed | A fixed cumulative poison capacity is the wrong model in this domain. Characterizing exposure concentration and residence time may prevent unnecessarily stringent cleanup. This does not establish a particular feed-blending policy. |
| W or both components retain substantial damage at feed-relevant doses | Establish the cleanup burden and catalyst-use limit. If required purification is disproportionate to useful output, the negative result argues against this route for that feed, rather than for another speculative protective material. |
| Clean-feed decline or transport dominates, and the controlled contaminant contributes little additional useful-output loss | Close the contaminant-budget branch for the selected domain. The patent's contamination stress tests were real, but they did not identify the dominant throughput limitation. |
| The molecular contrast is clear but the characterized feed does not generate the relevant exposure | Retain the chemical result with its scope. Do not convert it into a waste-feed improvement claim. |

This campaign could replace the broad cross-protection hypothesis with a simpler, useful account. A negative cross-protection result is not a failed program if it establishes a reliable boundary for feed acceptance or prevents unnecessary catalyst disposal. Conversely, identifying a damaged component already rescued by fresh Na would add little unless the exposure relationship predicts a decision beyond that existing observation.

## Practical comparison and confidence

Report accepted polymer-derived carbon per initial and added catalyst mass, full cycle time, net ethylene consumption, feed preparation losses, and solids handling. Charge the disclosed supplementation/regeneration baseline and the actual preparation alternative. Keep total product carbon separate from polymer carbon; most propylene carbon need not originate in PE. A lower reaction temperature and high batch yield do not establish superiority over cracking or other ethenolysis routes. Such comparisons need matched product boundaries, catalyst use, ethylene supply and separation demands.

**Hypothesis confidence: low to moderate.** Reversible W oxygenate effects have a direct precedent at different conditions, and unequal component susceptibility is plausible. Persistent ketone damage to the relevant Na functions and its dominance in polymer operation remain unverified.

**Feasibility and information value: moderate, conditional on the platform.** Controlled component exposures and product balances are tractable. Polymer contact, initiation assays, feed transfer and working-state recovery are meaningful difficulties. A useful result need not identify every surface species.

**Practical-improvement confidence: low.** The known burden gives real headroom, but does not show that contamination controls it or that useful components can be retained economically. Better feed acceptance and rejection criteria are more credible near-term outcomes than a large catalyst-lifetime improvement.

**Substantial-program judgment:** not selected. The chemically specific exposure distinction warrants a finite investigation, not an open-ended contaminant atlas. Promotion would require evidence that the distinction controls a consequential feed or catalyst-use choice and adds knowledge beyond the full prior record. This is a standard for expanding investment, not a requirement to know the experimental answer before undertaking research.
