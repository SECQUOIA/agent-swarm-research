# Independent review: methods-advance screen (front-regime titration claim)

2026-09-16. Prior-art and correctness audit of `research/working/direction-search/methods-advance.md` and Section 5 of `research/reviews/direction-search-portfolio-review.md`. Scope: the claim that a strongly bound titrant in a packed bed gives a linear rate-versus-uptake line with intercept equal to the titratable total regardless of activity distribution, that linearity is therefore not evidence of uniform per-site activity, and that no source states or calibrates this. Labels: **E** established evidence (read in the source), **I** inference, **J** judgment, **R** recollection of a source I could not open today. Web search was unavailable (session budget exhausted); Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Google Scholar snippets, publisher pages, and local read notes were used instead.

## 1. Prior art

**Reactor-engineering front (E for existence, R for detail).** A fast, irreversible poison in a fixed bed moves as a Bohart–Adams-type adsorption wave; this is the subject of Wheeler & Robell 1969, *J. Catal.* 13, 299 (DOI 10.1016/0021-9517(69)90404-7), whose Scholar snippet describes a Bohart–Adams breakthrough model for the poisoning wave and predicts conversion versus time for varied velocity, bed length and poison concentration. The Bohart–Adams equation itself is *JACS* 1920, 42, 523 (DOI 10.1021/ja01448a018). Later fixed-bed poisoning literature routinely speaks of a "poison front" (e.g., Cho, Hegedus, Aris 1983; Birtill 2011; Eigenberger & Ruppel in Ullmann's). Wheeler 1951, *Adv. Catal.* 3, 249 (DOI 10.1016/S0360-0564(08)60109-1) is the pellet-scale analogue (pore-mouth versus uniform poisoning changes the activity-versus-poison curve shape) (R). None of these treats the front as a problem for *site counting*; they treat it as a reactor performance problem. **J:** the plug-flow front is textbook physics; the linearity consequence at differential conversion follows in one line and is "obvious" to anyone who has seen a Bohart–Adams breakthrough curve, but I found no place where that line is written down.

**Wrasman et al. 2024, *J. Catal.* (DOI 10.1016/j.jcat.2024.115451) — the strongest hit (E, local fulltext lines 203–207).** Beyond the sentence the screen quotes, the paper explains that, during transient titration of a bed with finite depth, a concentration front can expose upstream sites before sites with greater titrant affinity. Varying titrant partial pressure can help distinguish site populations if their binding occurs in different pressure ranges, provided binding reversibility is understood (paraphrase). That is (a) the front, (b) upstream-first coverage irrespective of affinity, (c) the titrant-pressure diagnostic, and (d) the reversibility caveat. What Wrasman does *not* say: that the resulting curve is linear whatever the activity distribution, that its intercept is the titratable total, or that a linear curve is therefore uninformative. The screen's sentence "Wrasman et al. say only that a nonlinear curve may reflect ... concentration fronts" understates this prior art and should be corrected.

**Kumar et al. 2021, *ACS Catal.* 11, 9933 (DOI 10.1021/acscatal.1c01588; Wrasman ref. 40) (E from publisher page).** In-situ pyridine titration during isopropanol dehydration; on Al-SPP the rate fell linearly to >96 % suppression and varying pyridine partial pressure left both measured STY and BAS counts unchanged; on P-SPP the count and the degree of quenching depended on pyridine pressure and rates recovered within minutes after pyridine removal (reversible binding). So the titrant-pressure invariance test the screen proposes has already been run and interpreted in the literature, in both the invariant and the non-invariant case.

**Iglesia-group titration papers (E from publisher pages and local notes).** Macht, Carr, Iglesia 2009 *JACS* (DOI 10.1021/ja900829x): n-hexane isomerization showed an approximately linear rate decline as 2,6-di-tert-butylpyridine removed accessible Brønsted acid sites. The authors interpret full suppression as consistent either with DTBP access to every reactant-accessible site or with irreversible adsorption blocking or deactivating the remaining protons (paraphrase) — an accessibility statement, not a uniform-activity claim. Knaeble & Iglesia 2016 *J. Phys. Chem. C* (DOI 10.1021/acs.jpcc.5b11127): the authors report complete rate suppression after proton titration and interpret DTBP uptake as counting every proton in POM/SiO2 (paraphrase); the intercept (1.9–2.9 H+/POM) is used as the proton count. Jones et al. 2014 *J. Catal.* (local note, fulltext line 139): the CH3OH dehydration rate declined linearly with pyridine uptake and did not recover after pyridine removal; the authors infer irreversible titration of every site responsible for CH3OH dehydration (paraphrase). Baertsch et al. 2002 *J. Catal.* 205, 44 (DOI 10.1006/jcat.2001.3426, resolved via Crossref; content not accessible today) and Macht, Carr, Iglesia 2009 *J. Catal.* 264, 54 (DOI 10.1016/j.jcat.2009.03.005) could not be read. **Finding:** none of the accessible papers claims uniform per-site activity *from* linearity; they use the intercept as the proton count, which is exactly the "titratable total" the screen says it is. The screen's target inference is therefore weaker in the primary literature than the screen implies.

**Davis 2018, *AIChE J.* 64, 3778 (DOI 10.1002/aic.16385; OSTI 1610280, 28-page accepted manuscript read in full text) (E).** The manuscript contains no "Zn", "zinc", "equally active" or "identical site activity" statement, and the word "linear" does not occur in the DTBP discussion. It reports complete activity suppression by DTBP in a differential flow reactor, assigns an active-acid-site density of 1.9 H+ per H4SiW12O40, and recommends reaction-time selective poisoning for estimating that density (paraphrase). (Its Figure 1 is reproduced from Knaeble & Iglesia 2016 and its caption says H4PW12O40; the text says H4SiW12O40 — an internal discrepancy in Davis.) The other poisoning examples (SCN− on Fe–N–C; benzoic acid on Co2+/N-C) argue the *opposite* of uniform activity: a small fraction of sites carries most of the rate. **The screen's Davis attribution ("linear rate decrease ... evidence that nearly every Zn(II) site is equally active") is not supported by the OSTI copy and must be corrected or given a page locator from another Davis source.** Davis does equate the intercept with the *active* site density, which is the leap the screen targets, so the benchmark survives in weakened form.

**Older poisoning-curve literature (R; DOIs verified, content not read).** Herington & Rideal 1944 *Trans. Faraday Soc.* 40, 505 (DOI 10.1039/tf9444000505) and Maxted 1951 *Adv. Catal.* 3, 129 (DOI 10.1016/S0360-0564(08)60106-6) analyse the *shape* of activity-versus-poison curves for ensembles versus isolated centres on metals; Bartholomew 2001 *Appl. Catal. A* 212, 17 (DOI 10.1016/S0926-860X(00)00843-7) gives the selective/nonselective/antiselective classification of activity-versus-coverage curves. All of these are surface-level treatments in batch or well-mixed systems and do not address a bed front. Boudart & Djéga-Mariadassou 1984 could not be checked; from recollection its selective-poisoning discussion concerns surface nonuniformity, not reactor geometry. Bhan-group and Gorte sources: no explicit front statement located (Scholar snippets only).

**Summary on originality (J).** The physics is known (Wheeler–Robell); the qualitative application to in-situ titration and the pressure diagnostic are published (Wrasman 2024, Kumar 2021). What I could not find anywhere is the explicit statement that *a linear curve with the "right" intercept is what the front regime produces for any activity distribution*, or a calibration on mixtures of known spectator fraction. That is a restatement with a sharper consequence, not a new method.

## 2. Arithmetic and extensions

**Screen derivation (E as a limiting case).** Assumptions: sharp front, 1:1 stoichiometry, axially uniform N_A/N_S, differential conversion, rate ∝ uncovered active sites, no titrant-induced change. Then rate(U) = a·N_A·[1 − U/(N_A+N_S)]: linear, intercept N_A+N_S, slope −a·N_A/(N_A+N_S). Correct. (The screen omits the sign; the portfolio review includes it.)

**Extension (i), front sufficient not necessary (E).** Linearity requires only θ_A(U) = θ_S(U) at every U, i.e., reactivity-blind coverage. A front gives this; so does a reversible titrant with equal affinities on all sites, or a kinetically blurred front in which active and spectator sites share the same adsorption rate constant. Correct, and the sharper statement is: the curve bends only when coverage ordering is *selective* (by affinity or by adsorption rate) *and* the selectivity is correlated with activity.

**Extension (ii), identifiability (E).** A line has two parameters, rate(0) = a·N_A and intercept N_A+N_S; a, N_A, N_S cannot be separated. Correct. Add: a convex curve does not fix them either without an independent affinity–activity model; it adds at most an ordering.

**Non-ideal cases (I).**
- *Finite adsorption rate.* The Bohart–Adams/Thomas front has finite width, independent of bed length for constant-pattern behaviour, so the blurred fraction of the bed shrinks with bed mass at fixed velocity. Within the blurred zone coverage is ordered by adsorption rate constant, not by binding strength. If active and spectator sites adsorb at the same rate the line survives; if active sites adsorb faster the curve is convex (steep early slope, shallow tail), if slower, an initial plateau. Early titrant breakthrough (outlet titrant before the rate has fallen substantially) is the direct signature.
- *Partial reversibility.* The titrant redistributes toward stronger binders on the desorption time scale; curve shape and the count then depend on titrant pressure and on time (Kumar 2021 P-SPP), and the inlet–outlet uptake balance no longer equals bound titrant.
- *Spectator with different affinity.* Under a sharp front the spectator is covered in lockstep and is invisible. Under blurred or reversible conditions a weaker-binding spectator produces a tail (rate reaches ~0 before uptake ends: total-uptake and linear-extrapolation estimators diverge), a stronger-binding spectator an initial plateau.
- *Other non-site causes of curvature (often omitted):* integral conversion; axial gradients in temperature or promoter coverage; ensemble or co-adsorbate effects so that rate is not proportional to uncovered sites; stoichiometry that drifts with coverage; titrant carrier (e.g., water) altering rates; titrant-induced restructuring (screen's A3).

**When shape is informative (J).** Never about site heterogeneity in the sharp-front regime; in that regime curvature indicts one of the non-site causes above. Only when coverage is affinity- or rate-ordered (gradientless reactor, reversible titrant at set pressure, deliberately blurred front) *and* the ordering is independently linked to activity (known mixtures) does the shape bound the working fraction. Linearity and nonlinearity are both uninformative on their own.

**Diagnostics (J).** Bed-mass scaling and titrant-pressure variation are correct but weak: both are invariant in the sharp-front case, which is the case that hides heterogeneity, and a "no change" result is expected in either the front or the equal-affinity case. Two cheaper, more direct checks are missing: (1) time the titrant breakthrough at the outlet against the rate decline (already implicit in the Iyer–Bhan pulse data), and (2) a *layered* bed (spectator upstream of catalyst) versus the same mixture intimately mixed. In the front regime the layered bed gives an uptake plateau at unchanged rate followed by the same line; in the equilibrating regime layered and mixed beds coincide. This decides H1 versus A1 with the same materials in one day. Known-spectator mixtures remain the right ground truth for the intercept and slope predictions; the strong-affinity spectator must have its own DME rate measured at 415 K, as the screen notes, and should also be checked for pyridine reversibility so that A1 is not confounded by the spectator rather than the catalyst.

## 3. Value and the Iyer–Bhan case

**Value (J).** A known point that deserves an explicit, quotable restatement with the arithmetic and the identifiability consequence; that justifies a short standards addition. A pilot is justified only as the bounded calibration the screen already limits itself to (mixtures plus the layered-bed test), not as a program. The turnover-linked-inactivator idea (Section 6 of the screen) is correctly parked.

**Iyer & Bhan 2023, *ChemCatChem* (DOI 10.1002/cctc.202300329; local fulltext) (E).** The EO rate falls *non-linearly* (convex) with TFE uptake; Table 1 already varies catalyst loading 51.8–190 mg (3.7×) and TFE flow 0.08–0.31 μmol min−1 (3.9×): Method 1 gives 47.5/56.8/58.4/61.8, Method 2 29.6/40.1/27.3/21.7, Method 3 21.3/23.0/21.4/17.2 μmol g−1 with no monotonic dependence on either variable; in pulse mode the titrant breaks through only when the rate has fallen to 0.05–0.1× of its initial value; the titrant is fed as 3 mol % TFE in water. **I:** a sharp front predicts a *straight* line, so the front effect cannot explain the convexity that generates the 2.7-fold spread; the late breakthrough suggests the bed is close to the front regime, which points the curvature toward non-site causes (water co-feed, drifting stoichiometry of trifluoroethoxy/CF3 deposits, axial Cl-promoter gradients, uptake on support or promoter phases with slower adsorption) or toward the authors' own site-heterogeneity reading, which requires a blurred front with affinity–activity correlation. The screen's proposed re-test (bed mass ×2, titrant pressure ×3) has essentially been run already and did not resolve the spread; repeating it would not decide the question. A decisive version needs a TFE-uptake blank on the promoted support without Ag, a water-only co-feed control, outlet-TFE breakthrough timing on the continuous-cofeed runs, and a post-titration fluorine balance. Whether the Bhan group did some of these in the SI could not be checked (the SI note in `literature/` is metadata-only).

## 4. Proposed standards paragraph

The portfolio-review text is correct in substance but overstated in two places and missing three qualifiers: "binds strongly" is not sufficient for a front (adsorption must also be fast relative to the bed residence time); "curve shape carries information ... only when" omits that a *nonlinear* curve is equally not evidence of heterogeneity; and the assumptions (1:1 stoichiometry, differential conversion, axially uniform bed, rate proportional to uncovered sites) belong in the paragraph, not outside it. It should also credit Wrasman 2024 so the standard does not present a published point as ours. Suggested replacement, shorter and in the register of `standards.md`:

> A during-reaction titration in a packed bed counts titratable sites, not working sites. When the titrant is retained quantitatively as it enters the bed (no breakthrough before the rate falls) or covers sites without regard to their reactivity, the rate-versus-uptake curve is a straight line for any distribution of activity among titratable sites: its uptake intercept is the titratable total and its slope is the bed-average rate per titratable site (assuming 1:1 stoichiometry, differential conversion, an axially uniform bed, and rate proportional to uncovered active sites). A straight line is therefore consistent with, but not evidence of, uniform activity, and a curved line is not evidence of heterogeneous activity until integral conversion, a blurred front, drifting stoichiometry, and titrant-induced restructuring are excluded (Wrasman et al. 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.jcat.2024.115451, note the upstream-first coverage). Report such titrations as "titratable total and bed-average rate per titratable site"; state titrant stoichiometry and reversibility; do not assign a per-site rate difference between samples to per-site chemistry unless the working fraction is shown unchanged. Support any working-site claim with an affinity contrast, a layered-versus-mixed bed or titrant-pressure invariance check, or a calibration on mixtures of known spectator fraction.

**Disagreements preserved.** The screen and the portfolio review treat the front regime as the default for "ordinary" titrations; I regard that as plausible but unmeasured, and the Iyer–Bhan pulse breakthrough is the only direct evidence either way in the local corpus. The screen frames Davis 2018 as the benchmark misuse; I find no such statement in Davis and locate the weaker leap (intercept = active-site density) there instead. The screen calls Wrasman's coverage of the front a gap; I read it as the qualitative half of the claim already published, leaving only the linearity/intercept consequence and the calibration as the screen's own contribution.
