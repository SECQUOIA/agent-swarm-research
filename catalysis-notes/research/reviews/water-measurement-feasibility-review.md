# Independent review of the water-measurement pilot

2026-09-16. Review of [measurement feasibility](../working/program-development/water-measurement-feasibility.md), the [program](../working/program-development/physical-water-management.md), [first test](../working/program-development/water-management-first-test.md), [size discriminator](../calculations/water-size-discriminator.md), and [transport limits](../calculations/water-transport-limits.md). No experiment has been performed. Numerical granule counts below are calculations under stated assumptions, not measured sample properties.

**Proceed with a small apparatus qualification if an existing FT system can support it. Do not yet treat it as a route to a scientifically distinctive transport program.** The draft correctly prevents several misleading interpretations. Its proposed held-out outlet-water response can establish a useful formulation transfer function, but does not establish the source-to-cobalt exposure needed for a stationary drying or granule-size prediction. This is a limitation of the observable, not a problem cured by a better fit.

The useful next distinction is between three deliverables: a qualified measurement, a predictive operating-history result, and an identified stationary transport mechanism. The present pilot plausibly reaches the first, may reach the second with one added functional prediction, and has no demonstrated route to the third. It should state this hierarchy in its investment decision.

**Primary-source check.** The Energy demonstration uses a 4.3 mg silica-gel fragment with estimated effective diameter 1.84 mm, at 10, 25 and 40 °C. Its coefficient is defined by `d n̄/dt = ka(nEq − n̄)`, so it is a loading-referenced relaxation coefficient with units s−1. The authors determine the equilibrium loading relation at low flow and evaluate kinetics at higher flow. Adsorption and desorption differ. Their nitrogen/helium comparison also reveals early thermal sensitivity; they recommend 1–2 mg for nitrogen in that particular silica-gel system. Their flow expression is presented for water mole fractions below 0.1. These details support the draft's caution and do not establish operando applicability at 220 °C, 20 bar and substantial water content. [Brandani and Mangano, accepted manuscript, pp. 3–5 and 10–13](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/files/225421831/MassTransportCoeffWaterZLC_revised.pdf), [Energy DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.energy.2021.121945).

The open ZLC review gives a stronger useful test than simply adding a third nearby flow: partial loading can distinguish an ideal internal-diffusion response from an ideal surface-barrier response. It recommends two flows in kinetic control plus a partial-loading experiment for the ideal linear problem, and explains why matching a blank can mean either very fast exchange or too little detectable signal. Those conclusions assume the underlying passive ZLC model; they are not a ready-made identification theorem for reacting, wax-bearing cobalt. Its heat, leak and equilibrium-control checks support the draft's qualification sequence. [Brandani and Mangano, Sections 2.2 and 3.4–3.9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10450-020-00273-w).

The low-dose geometry is independently confirmed in the locally retained original Fang article: Fig. 1a and the methods specify 0.5 g Co/SiO2 plus 0.025 g PDVB, both 40–60 mesh, as separate mixed granules. The article describes irregular PDVB morphology. The sphere-based sampling calculation below therefore uses an explicit approximation. [Fang et al., Fig. 1 and methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76571-8), local original `/tmp/catalysis-program-development/ft-hydrophobic.txt`.

**What the pilot identifies.** The draft's distinctions among λ, G and K are correct. For a passive linear pool, `λ = G/B`. For a continuum, the transient diffusivity is `K/s`, with differential storage s. Neither a decay rate nor a molecular self-diffusivity supplies the stationary gas-referenced K required by the sphere calculation.

On a live catalyst, even the observed pole need not equal G/B. For the illustrative single-pool balance `B dx/dt = Q(x) + G(cb − x)`, linearization gives `λ = (G − ∂Q/∂x)/B`. This is an algebraic warning, not an asserted model of cobalt kinetics: water inhibition can alter relaxation even when physical G is unchanged. A source-corrected integrated inventory and a transient kinetic model must therefore use consistent source dynamics. A small mean generated/supplied-water ratio is insufficient; the uncertainty in the *integrated perturbation* of Q must be small relative to the sought storage contrast. External-concentration derivatives measured along a reacting steady-state curve also need not equal B with respect to the internal coordinate x.

Wax creates a second identification problem. An externally imposed step can mostly sample accessible silica, polymer-associated liquid or other storage, while a small cobalt-adjacent pool controls damage. Different spatial arrangements can produce similar boundary responses and different source-to-site exposures. Absolute uptake, several flows and a successful held-out water curve do not alone remove this ambiguity. The held-out test is valuable because it can reject a model; passing it establishes the specified input/output prediction, not every internal parameter interpretation.

The most promising limited advance is therefore **predicting the response to a new water history at the same geometry and working state**. If the water-storage/exchange model independently predicts a consequential catalytic response to a held-out pulse or purge, it connects the measurement to use. If it predicts only effluent clearing, it is useful apparatus/formulation characterization with limited novelty. Neither result by itself justifies β and κ in the shaping inequalities.

**Minimum informative experiment.** Retain the draft's hot, pressurized empty-cell and water-specific packing blanks, inert tracer, source balance, state-preserving returns, and representative mixed cartridge. These are necessary to interpret a signal and should not become an open-ended apparatus-development project. Specify in advance the inventory and response-time contrast that would change the intended decision. Measure the actual uncertainty before promising that contrast.

Use a stable wax-bearing parent and a matched low-dose PDVB packing at one qualified operating state. Preserve the working liquid and verify reversible reference function across the measurement sequence. Run a small up/down water step at two flows with fixed H2/CO partial pressures. Do not obtain an apparent equilibrium calibration by moving to a different reaction or liquid state. Deliberately use one reduced-amplitude repeat to check the proposed local linearity, and repeat the baseline to bound drift. Independently prepared packings are needed before reporting a formulation contrast; repeated valve switches in one cartridge do not test packing reproducibility.

If capacity and a relaxation interval are identifiable, select the held-out perturbation by discrimination power. A partial-loading pulse with duration near or below the inferred exchange time is generally more informative than a third nearby flow. Choose the duration from the competing models' predictions and the measured switching limit; if they predict the same response within uncertainty, this pulse does not test the mechanism. Freeze the predicted absolute retained/released amount and the entire outlet response before acquiring it. Failure rejects the proposed predictive model in this state; adding a new pool afterward restarts, rather than completes, validation.

To cross from measurement feasibility into useful science, add **one independently specified functional consequence** to that same held-out pulse. Calibrate the local reversible rate response to a few nearby steady water levels on each stable formulation, while bounding H2/CO-transfer and thermal changes. Combine that calibration with the proposed water-response model to predict a time to recover diagnostic productivity, or cumulative hydrocarbon production during the pulse and return. Select an endpoint the apparatus can measure rapidly enough; product collection delays need their own response correction. If the water-response time is too short for a useful kinetic readout, do not infer site exposure from a delayed product signal.

This functional prediction remains conditional: a steady rate calibration is not a direct local-water reporter, and intrinsic surface relaxation can also delay rate. Agreement would support a bounded operating-history rule after those alternatives are constrained; it would not prove a unique microscopic pool. A null calibrated water sensitivity means this readout cannot test the proposed exposure contrast in that interval. A damaging challenge can follow only after a reproducible exposure-history prediction exists; it is not needed to qualify the apparatus.

**Concrete decisions.** Use prediction intervals that include blank, source, repeated-packing and state-drift uncertainty, not just fit uncertainty. Set the useful effect size from the planned output or operating decision before observing the comparison.

| Result | Decision and defensible conclusion |
|---|---|
| Reversible absolute inventory and an additional sample response are resolved; one fixed model predicts the held-out water pulse and a resolvable functional consequence. | Continue the operating-history branch. Test one useful pulse/purge choice. This can be scientifically distinctive if the independently measured storage/exchange quantities predict the new function rather than merely correlate with it. |
| Capacity and relaxation are resolved, but predicted catalytic histories remain unconstrained or indistinguishable. | Retain storage/response measurements; no-go for an identified exposure mechanism. Continue only if an explicit additional measurement can separate the remaining decision-relevant predictions. |
| No kinetic delay is resolved, but positive capacity and a validated model yield a useful lower bound on G. | A potentially informative negative: if `q/G_lower` is below an independently calibrated consequential exposure change, exclude sufficient drying through that *identified pool*. This does not bound an unseen cobalt pool. |
| The PDVB contrast is statistically compatible with zero and its entire uncertainty interval lies inside a predeclared operationally negligible range. | Informative negative for that observable, state and timescale. Do not extend it to all water exposure or all promotion. |
| Water response is indistinguishable from the blank, capacity is near zero or uncertain, or Q/state uncertainty can explain the contrast. | Failure to identify. It does not show that exchange is fast, storage is absent, or water management is unimportant. Stop this measurement route unless one concrete instrument/state correction can recover the required resolution. |
| Multiple equally adequate models predict conflicting shaping or stationary-exposure outcomes. | No-go for the two-size mechanistic campaign. A direct formulation substitution test remains possible on its own practical merits. |

Thus a useful negative needs an exclusion bound on a quantity tied to the decision. Inability to measure that quantity is a measurement limitation, even when it sensibly stops further investment. If the intended claim remains stationary source-generated drying, the pilot still needs an independently justified source-to-site mapping and relevant resistance partition. No result from the proposed boundary-only qualification guarantees those constraints.

**Are there too few promoter granules?** Potentially yes at milligram scale, but the draft should quantify the concern and not assume that every small sample is unrepresentative. For catalyst mass M in mg, promoter/catalyst mass ratio r = 0.05, sphere-equivalent promoter diameter d in cm, and promoter envelope density ρ in g/cm³,

`N_PDVB = 0.05 M / [1000 ρ (π/6) d³]`.

Assume d = 250–425 µm, using the draft's conversion of 40–60 mesh, and **assume** ρ = 0.5–1.2 g/cm³. These densities are illustrative and are not measured bounds; use envelope density, including granule porosity, rather than polymer skeletal density. Irregular shape and the size distribution can change the result. A central scenario uses d = 335 µm and ρ = 0.8 g/cm³, giving 0.01575 mg per promoter granule.

For a random aliquot with independently distributed, similar-sized granules, count fluctuations have approximate relative standard deviation `1/√N`: about 27% at N = 14, versus 8% at N = 159. This simple sampling model is not a measured error bar. Weighing/counting the additive separately removes much composition sampling error, but not variation in its spatial placement and contacts. Quartz dilution, cartridge walls and wax bridges further affect those neighborhoods. Count alone cannot validate the geometry or require direct catalyst–polymer contact for the mechanism.

A planning target of 100 promoter granules would require about 8–96 mg catalyst across these assumptions, or 31.5 mg centrally. One hundred is an illustrative counting-precision target, not a universal representative-volume criterion. At the first-test planning flow of roughly 68 L/(g catalyst h), that mass range requires about 9–109 mL/min on the same gas-volume basis. Increasing inventory therefore has tangible consequences for the high-pressure water feed and instrument response, but does not inherently demand a large bed. The actual apparatus limits remain unknown.

The practical remedy is to count and weigh a representative PDVB fraction, record packing dimensions, and test independent packings. Select the smallest cartridge whose relevant contrast is reproducible. The silica-gel paper's 1–2 mg recommendation concerns thermal behavior of its single larger fragment; it is not a universal mass ceiling for many smaller mixed granules. Conversely, using 4.3 mg merely because that paper did would leave only about fourteen promoter granules in the central scenario and needs explicit validation.

**Disposition.** Accept the pilot as a bounded feasibility expense. Strengthen it with the quantitative sampling estimate, explicit reacting-source feedback, a discrimination-selected partial-loading prediction, and a functional endpoint before calling the program scientifically distinctive. Keep stationary transport and shaping claims gated on the missing source-to-site constraints. An informative operating-history result is a worthwhile narrower outcome; an unidentifiable water tail is not evidence for or against protection.
