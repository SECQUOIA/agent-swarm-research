# Independent review: value of the Ni × Re epoxidation question

Date: 2026-09-15. This review reassesses the [working opportunity](../working/epoxidation-opportunity.md) against the [program standards](../standards.md), [practical metrics](../calculations/epoxidation-practical-metrics.md), and the original Hwang and Jalil articles. It does not establish exhaustive novelty or commercial performance.

**2026-09-16 superseding assessment:** this original compatibility-oriented review predates the historical Ni/Cs/Re aging audit and recovered primary texts/SIs. The [current program](../programs/ag-selective-oxygen-use.md) and [post-upload audit](post-upload-ag-audit.md) now require a finite useful-retention comparison beyond demonstrated baseline kinetics. Retain the mechanistic possibility below as a hypothesis, not the current novelty case or permission for a full factorial.

## Historical judgment

**Retain a useful conditional pilot, with a credible route to substantial mechanistic value.** The present evidence does not justify calling the idea an established major opportunity, but it also does not justify rejecting it as generic promoter screening. The distinction depends on the question and the evidence obtained.

The scientifically consequential question is whether a surface modification that makes oxygen less reactive toward combustion remains compatible with a catalytic function proposed to convert that oxygen into an epoxidizing species. Resolving that question could guide the choice between suppressing an undesired reaction and productively redirecting its intermediate. This is a meaningful extension of existing research even though Ni promotion and Ni-containing multicomponent formulations are already known.

The working assessment gives patent disclosure too much weight in its opening recommendation. Such disclosure constrains claims of a new combination; it does not establish the oxygen mechanism, eliminate the value of a causal experiment, or independently demonstrate optimized performance. Conversely, a mechanistic story assembled from two papers does not establish that the corresponding surface states coexist on one working catalyst.

**A multiweek experiment is warranted if the group can reproduce both parent behaviors and can resolve a practically consequential rate/selectivity difference.** Building both platforms from scratch is a separate capability investment. The supplied record does not establish that investment's cost or likelihood of success, so an unconditional new program is premature. This fits Iglesia's promoted-Ag kinetics directly; quantitative control of site populations and secondary reactions also fits Gounder's expertise. No new support concept is needed.

## One strongest causal and design question

**At a stabilized working state, does Ni preserve or improve the sustained conversion of oxygen into EO by the Cs/Re/Cl-promoted catalyst, or does the Ni-conditioned surface diminish the useful Re-dependent contribution enough to change which formulation should be used?**

This question admits beneficial, adverse, and independent behavior. It does not presume oxygen competition. Its narrow mechanistic form asks whether Ni alters the accessibility and reaction fate of oxygen relevant to the Re-dependent response. Its design form asks whether adding Ni improves the attainable selectivity/productivity tradeoff after each formulation receives appropriate chloride conditioning.

### Retained inventory is not a continuing sink

A finite population of retained oxygen can establish a surface state that persistently changes adsorption, site availability, or reaction barriers. Once that population becomes stationary, it need not continue accumulating oxygen to suppress combustion. Conversely, a finite store cannot indefinitely absorb one otherwise unwanted oxygen atom per catalytic turnover. Sustained operation requires closure of the oxygen flux through products, oxygen exchange/release, or other measured outlets.

Thus, “Ni removes oxygen from the reactive pool” is ambiguous. It may mean transient storage, continual interception with subsequent release, or formation of a comparatively inert surface state that changes subsequent chemistry. These possibilities predict different transients and different steady-state effects. Rejecting perpetual accumulation does not reject the stationary-state mechanism proposed by Jalil.

Similarly, a sustained selectivity above the ideal monooxygenase limit challenges the assumptions of that particular cycle and its oxygen-disposal route. It does not uniquely identify a Re intermediate, establish oxygen transfer from Ni to Re, or prove that stored oxygen is being consumed. The balance and the mechanism are different evidentiary claims.

## What result would change catalyst choice?

Compare separately conditioned formulations on **net EO output versus carbon selectivity**, with secondary EO loss distinguished from primary selectivity. “Optimal chlorine” must refer to an explicit operating objective; maximizing selectivity at negligible production is not a useful optimum.

| Reproducible outcome after conditioning and chloride adjustment | Design consequence |
|---|---|
| Ni/Cs/Re produces more net EO at the same selectivity, or higher selectivity at matched useful EO output, than Cs/Re; the advantage persists beyond initial inventory changes | Advance the combined catalyst. An additive improvement can justify this choice even if the statistical Ni × Re interaction is zero. |
| Ni improves Cs–Ag but worsens the useful performance of Cs/Re–Ag specifically in the promoted operating region; this survives rate decomposition and reasonable controls | Do not add Ni to that Re formulation. This establishes a consequential compatibility limit; it does not yet prove oxygen interception. |
| A Re-free or lower-Re Ni formulation preserves useful output and selectivity with demonstrably better conditioning, durability, or preparation/resource burden | Advance that simpler formulation. Saving trace Re alone is insufficient justification for a measurable loss in output or selectivity. |
| Ni's effect disappears after chloride adjustment | The apparent interaction reflected operating-state differences. Retain any measured operational benefit, but drop the shared-oxygen interpretation unless independent evidence supports it. |
| No resolved performance difference and no discriminating oxygen-pathway result | End this formulation pilot. This bounds the tested loading, architecture, and operating region; it does not exclude all Ni–Re mechanisms. |

Uncertainty must include independent preparation differences, analytical error, and drift. A selectivity difference should be translated through the existing stoichiometric framework at matched net output. No universal numerical threshold for economic significance follows from the two papers.

## Factorial interaction and a direct oxygen-pathway test serve different purposes

The proposed four formulations at fixed Cs provide a sensible first comparison of the conditional effects of Ni and Re. They establish whether their combination changes observable catalyst performance. Their interaction coefficient depends on the response scale, residence time, and operating point; selectivity is a nonlinear ratio. Analyse net EO and combustion rates as well as selectivity, and compare the useful performance curves after chloride adjustment.

**A nonzero interaction is neither necessary nor sufficient evidence of shared oxygen.** Chlorine chemistry, promoter placement, oxygen activation, site obstruction, and secondary oxidation can all produce an interaction. Conversely, offsetting effects can conceal one. The current requirement to observe a consequential interaction before any mechanistic extension is therefore too restrictive. A substantial additive benefit can justify development, and a specific unresolved oxygen-fate prediction can justify a mechanistic test independently.

A direct test would perturb the oxygen isotope feed at an established catalytic state and measure time-resolved oxygen isotopologues in EO, O2, CO2, and water, with reactor residence time and exchange/storage contributions independently constrained. The useful observable is how the exchangeable oxygen inventory contributes to EO versus other oxygen outlets, and how that relationship changes with Ni and the Re-dependent state. It should address a prediction stated before fitting the transient.

However, an isotope switch alone labels all accessible oxygen populations. A delayed EO isotope response does not identify Ni-bound or Re-bound oxygen; multiple reservoirs and fast exchange can reproduce it. An oxygen preload followed by an oxygen-free ethylene pulse measures a stoichiometric capability in a perturbed state, not necessarily the operating catalytic pathway. A site-specific transfer claim needs an independently defensible assignment or selective perturbation of the relevant reservoir, plus quantitative recovery and exchange constraints.

**Stationary Ni-bound oxygen might have a structural role without measurable turnover of those particular atoms.** A small or absent label flux through retained Ni-associated oxygen therefore cannot rule out its causal effect on the reacting surface. Conversely, observing oxygen exchange does not establish that the exchanging oxygen controls selectivity. Flux tracing and a causal perturbation of the surface state answer different questions.

### A sharper prediction beyond the factorial

An experimentally meaningful fork is whether the Ni-containing catalyst can avoid combustion by returning unwanted oxygen to O2, rather than requiring its conversion into EO by a second selective route. This is an established type of mechanistic alternative, not a claim of a newly discovered oxygen cycle. Jalil's model-surface O2 desorption at about 520 K, versus approximately 590 K on Ag, supplies a reason to test its accessibility; vacuum temperature-programmed desorption does not establish that return occurs at a sufficient rate in an oxygen-containing catalytic feed (manuscript p. 4, Fig. 1).

For the deliberately restricted cycle in which each initial O2 activation forms one EO and one residual oxygen atom, and the latter either feeds combustion or recombines to O2, the required oxygen-return flux is

\[
D_{\rm required}=(r_{\rm EO}-3r_{\rm CO_2})/2.
\]

Here rates are molar, primary product rates are used, and the expression applies only when its right-hand side is positive and there is no second selective oxygen-transfer route. It follows from six oxygen atoms consumed per ethylene combustion event and two CO2 molecules produced. Other oxidation products, cofeed oxidation, secondary EO combustion, changing surface inventories, or a different initial cycle require their explicit balances rather than this expression.

**Discriminating prediction:** if a validated transient/exchange analysis places the return flux from the relevant reactive oxygen pool below this required value during sustained high-selectivity operation, the return-only version of the cycle fails. A second selective oxygen-use pathway or another change to the assumed cycle is needed. If the return-only alternative remains quantitatively viable, Re need not be necessary merely to provide an outlet for that residual oxygen. Re must then justify its presence by additional useful production, selectivity, or stability. This changes the design rationale for adding Re, even if the optimized factorial shows a small interaction.

The qualifier “validated” matters. Gross oxygen isotope scrambling is not automatically the net return flux of oxygen left after EO formation. Exchange that never contributes to EO, incomplete label mixing, and inactive reservoirs can confound the inference. A large exchange signal establishes capacity at most; it does not prove that recombination closes the selective cycle. A flux bound is decisive only if the measurement model and controls support its application to the relevant pool. These assumptions must be specified before numerical interpretation.

Finite-inventory diagnostics are still useful: continuing selectivity over cumulative turnovers far beyond a measured conservative storage capacity excludes a storage-only explanation. It leaves a stationary structural role intact. Together, an inventory bound and a defensible return-flux bound can eliminate specific explanations and make the program informative. If the available measurements cannot bound the relevant flux and cannot perturb the surface state without uncontrolled changes, retain the formulation pilot but do not describe it as a direct oxygen-pathway test.

**Prefer the factorial comparison first when the immediate decision is catalyst choice.** Prefer a direct oxygen-fate experiment first only if the necessary transient capability already exists and a preliminary identifiability analysis shows that plausible alternative mechanisms predict distinguishable results. Adding isotope analysis by default does not turn an underdetermined screen into a causal study. Neither source supplied here establishes that a uniquely site-specific oxygen-pathway experiment is presently feasible.

## Is the proposed campaign proportionate?

Yes, conditionally. Hwang reports relaxation over approximately 10–50 h, so repeated chloride settings across four formulations reasonably imply several weeks. Compressing the schedule would sacrifice the main distinction between a working state and an evolving inventory.

Keep the existing controls for heat/mass transfer, net EO formation, primary versus secondary oxidation, promoter contents, and independent repeats of the decisive pair. The most important preliminary gate is reproduction of the promoted Cs/Re response and the Ni parent effect on available material. Jalil's parent Ni catalyst lacks Cs: failure of Ni/Cs to improve Cs–Ag would not, by itself, show failure to reproduce Jalil or uniquely implicate Re. This distinction should be checked before interpreting the factorial.

One chosen Ni loading is appropriate for a pilot, but is not a general test of every Ni–Re architecture. A negative result at that loading supports stopping the bounded effort if it offers no consequential learning; it cannot establish universal promoter incompatibility. Add a lower-Re dose only if it discriminates a stated mechanism or tests a useful resource/performance tradeoff.

## Untested assumptions that limit practical significance

1. **Coexisting working structures:** Ni remains suitably dispersed and does not disrupt the Ag–Cs–Re contact required for Hwang's response. Nominal composition and particle size do not establish this.
2. **Transfer between operating regimes:** Ni's behavior at 1 bar and on model surfaces persists at the promoted catalyst's pressure, product concentrations, chlorine potential, and particle size.
3. **Available performance headroom:** Suppressing combustion still improves an optimized promoted reference. Benefits measured against bare Ag do not quantify this headroom.
4. **A useful activity/selectivity balance:** Lower combustion is not purchased through excessive loss of EO output, higher required temperature, more Ag, or greater recycle.
5. **Durability:** The relevant surface state survives sustained reaction and chloride changes without adverse Ni aggregation, promoter relocation, or irreversible activity loss. The present papers do not establish commercial lifetime for the combination.
6. **Economic weight:** Any reduction in Re or chlorine has enough value to offset synthesis complexity, conditioning losses, catalyst inventory, and lifetime changes. The source record does not quantify that balance.
7. **A transferable conclusion:** The result reflects a defensible chemical relationship rather than one preparation-specific redistribution of promoters. A clean operational limit is useful, but its generality must be stated accurately.

## Recommended revision to the opportunity assessment

Replace the categorical opening rejection with: **“A bounded Ni × Re compatibility pilot is justified where both parent platforms are available. Its present value is a consequential catalyst-design question; establishing the oxygen mechanism could make it a substantial research contribution. Practical superiority remains unproved.”**

Separate three confidence judgments: the shared-oxygen mechanism is plausible but uncertain; an informative formulation comparison is feasible conditional on reproducible working states; practical improvement has low evidentiary support. Preserve both favorable and adverse outcomes as useful, and allow substantial additive improvement to count as success. The current evidence supports disciplined investigation, without presuming either a positive breakthrough or a negative decision.
