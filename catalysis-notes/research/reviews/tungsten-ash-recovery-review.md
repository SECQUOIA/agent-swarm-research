# Independent review of the ash-recovery comparator

2026-09-16. Bounded audit of [the comparator note](../calculations/tungsten-ash-recovery-comparator.md) against [WO2022064039A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2022064039A1/en) and its [scanned original](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/dd/8e/4e/7b2978dad9907f/WO2022064039A1.pdf). Independently inspected printed pp. 8–13, Tables 1–7 and all six figures on drawing sheets 1/3–3/3. Local originals are `/tmp/tungsten-process-audit/WO2022064039A1.*`. No KB or proposal files were edited.

**The note's central conclusion is supported:** this patent reports working recovery experiments followed by catalytic use of recovered W in a continuous, EG-containing sucrose hydrogenolysis reactor. It is stronger evidence than an untested recovery disclosure. The earlier absence-of-examples finding for two other patents must remain limited to those documents. Two qualifications need sharpening: the source's W-loading numbers conflict internally, and its reported mass-balance metric is not a carbon balance.

## Confirmed experimental boundary

Printed p. 8 explicitly describes a **prepared model waste**, containing glycerol, sorbitol, erythritol, tungstic acid and salts. It is designed to resemble a waste stream after product removal; it is not an actual bleed collected from the subsequent reaction trial. The source note's page range should begin at p. 8 to cover this statement and Table 1.

Printed pp. 9–11 report burning near 1200 °C, collecting seven ash samples from three runs, combining two samples from one run, and taking 9 g of that combination through acid treatment. Treatment uses 90 g of 2 M HCl near 80 °C for 90 minutes, followed by six water washes and drying near 80 °C for 14 hours. A 6.7 g portion of treated ash is subsequently mixed with EG and NaOH near 150 °C for two hours, cooled and filtered. Thus “selected ash” and the stated sequence are accurate. Table 5 additionally reports removal of Na, Ca and Mg while largely retaining W. Contaminant removal is part of the demonstrated recovery function, not just dissolution of a pure W reference.

The source reports **84.2% dissolution “as percentage of the boiler ash”** and 1.26 wt% elemental W in the resulting solution (p. 11). This is an ash-referenced assay result. Neither the original waste-to-collected-ash W capture fraction nor a closed overall recovery balance is provided. The combined ash sample and the later treated-ash portion also prevent reconstructing an unambiguous complete recovery from the stated masses alone. Do not promote 84.2% to overall return efficiency, or multiply it by a separate treatment-retention percentage without establishing compatible denominators. Table 5's reported total W loss is 3.5%; the narrative specifically identifies the 0.17% wash measurement as the **last wash**, so it should not be independently represented as a measured sum of all six washes.

Printed pp. 12–13 specify a 200 mL CSTR with 148 mL liquid volume, a 20 µm outlet filter intended to retain Ru/C, nominal liquid flow 5–7 mL/min, 65–80 bar pressure and 900 rpm. Table 7 gives initial reactor concentrations of 20 wt% sucrose, 19.0/19.1 wt% EG and 60.7/60.6 wt% water. Recovered-W trial P101 lasts 300 minutes; fresh-W trial P039 lasts 360 minutes. This is a meaningful continuous laboratory test over several liquid residence times. It is not repeated closed-loop catalyst recovery or a lifetime comparison. The exact reaction temperature is absent from this experimental account; neither the 150 °C dissolution treatment nor the description's broad reactor range supplies it.

## Meaningful source inconsistency: W loading

The note correctly transcribes Table 7's **0.34 versus 0.30 wt% H2WO4** and NaOH/H2WO4 molar ratio of 0.73 in both cases. These concentrations are H2WO4-equivalent values, not elemental-W weight percentages. However, Table 6 does not reproduce the recovered trial's 0.34%:

- P101 lists 340.3 g sucrose, 1033.5 g water, 294.4 g recovered solution and 33.2 g additional EG: total 1701.4 g.
- The recovered solution's stated 1.26 wt% elemental W gives 3.709 g W, equivalent to approximately 5.041 g H2WO4, or **0.296 wt%** of that total.
- P039 lists 117 g of 5 wt% H2WO4 solution in approximately 1950 g total mixture, giving **0.300 wt%**, consistent with Table 7. Its small additional NaOH-solution amount does not explain the recovered-trial discrepancy.

This arithmetic uses the tabulated masses and standard formula masses; it is not a correction of the original experiment. Preserve both reported values and flag the mismatch. Replace an unqualified statement that the recovered trial *used more W* with “Table 7 reports more W, but this conflicts with Table 6 and the stated ICP concentration.” No matched intrinsic activity, nor a precise 13% W excess, is established by the document.

## Product response and comparison consequence

Figure 2 supports the note's approximate EG readings near 40–42% at 300 minutes. Figure 1 gives a reported mass-balance metric in the mid-to-high 80% range late in the trials. The patent defines these quantities relative to sugar mass with cosolvent subtraction; they are not conventional carbon yields or carbon closure. The generic “compound selectivity” equation actually names cosolvent output and input, leaving its application to other plotted products unclear. Preserve the authors' metric labels and do not convert the plotted values into carbon yields without a justified species balance. Broadly similar trajectories are supported, but the curves are not identical and no repeat uncertainty is shown.

These limitations do not erase the demonstrated capability: recovered W from a contaminated model ash works with Ru/C in an EG-containing continuous reaction. A retained-W program must offer a consequential advantage over such a recovery option; simply demonstrating active recovered W or compatibility with added EG is insufficient novelty. Conversely, the patent does not establish complete recovery from an actual process bleed, equal activity per W, long-term recycle stability or optimized recovery duties. The comparator note appropriately identifies the concrete treatment operations without assuming either prohibitive cost or industrial optimization. Full plant economics is unnecessary to credit this prior art or define the next comparative experiment.
