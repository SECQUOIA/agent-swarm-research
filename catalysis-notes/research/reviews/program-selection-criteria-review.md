# Independent review of program selection criteria

2026-09-16. Scope: [selection standards](../standards.md), [direction-search outcome](direction-search-outcome.md), and the [ethane ODH screen](../working/direction-search/ethane-odh.md), assessed against the user's current research goal. This is a review of reasoning and selection criteria, not a new literature audit. Statements about what these documents contain are verified against the documents; judgments below are the reviewer's. Their underlying scientific claims have not been independently reverified here. No candidate is promoted by this review.

The evidence and experimental-design requirements are valuable. The promotion threshold is too restrictive in ways that do not reliably identify important research. It conflates selecting an ambitious program for development with authorizing its full experimental investment. It also treats the number of affected decisions, incumbent activity, and a particular validation format as substitutes for judging the contribution itself. These rules create a credible risk of false negatives. The files reviewed do not establish how many rejected candidates would survive a better assessment.

## What should remain

Keep the requirements to identify consequential uncertainty; examine competing prior art; state alternatives; demonstrate why proposed measurements discriminate among them; compare fairly; disclose material and apparatus dependencies; and distinguish confidence in the hypothesis, experiment, and practical benefit. Keep independent review and the requirement to freeze a prediction before its validation outcome is observed. These protect against vague mechanism stories, undiscriminating characterization, and reinterpretation of results after the fact.

The direction-search record also contains substantive reasons for declining candidates: prior demonstrations, misattributed claims, confounded comparisons, weak links to process value, and uncertain access to materials. Revising the criteria does not remove those objections. A stronger program needs a stronger scientific and practical case, not a new label for the same pilot.

## Where the current threshold misleads

| Current requirement or recurring reasoning | Useful purpose | Problem and minimal correction |
|---|---|---|
| Change two decision classes, or one across multiple catalyst families | Discourage narrow observations without consequences | Counting classes does not measure significance. One decisive advance in a consequential system can matter more than weak effects across several classes. Require a consequential contribution, with breadth as supporting evidence when justified. |
| Resolve the central uncertainty usefully “either way” | Avoid experiments whose negative result says little | The value and likelihood of outcome branches can differ. A program need not make every possible outcome valuable. State which outcomes justify continuation, which reject an important claim, and which remain inconclusive. Control exposure to uninformative failure through staged investment. |
| A first campaign on standard apparatus decides the central alternatives | Avoid expensive, vague commitments | A first campaign may need to establish detectability, reproduce a phenomenon, or validate a necessary method. Require a feasible and informative first stage, with a credible route to testing the central alternatives. Specialized apparatus is a cost and dependency to justify, not an automatic disqualification. |
| Knowledge already being measured by a field's “owners” weighs against promotion | Account for duplication and competitive disadvantage | Active groups are evidence of competition and potential collaborators, not evidence that unpublished answers exist. Require a distinct contribution relative to accessible results and known ongoing work; assess access and execution advantage explicitly. Do not infer closure from authorship or a stated intention to investigate. |
| A frozen prospective prediction is the expansion gate | Protect against overfitting and retrospective explanation | Retain this for predictive or transfer claims. Other advances may be established by an independently reproduced intervention, a discriminating isotope experiment, a validated measurement capability, or a mechanistic exclusion. Prespecify a suitable decision test before its outcome, without requiring every program to take the form of model transfer. |
| Candidates failing any threshold disappear from the priority table | Keep attention on credible options | This can hide an important opportunity whose immediate uncertainty is access or feasibility. Record its status and the unresolved dependency. Do not give it equal execution priority until that dependency is resolved. |

The current sentence about the first campaign does not explicitly require proven success before promotion. However, its conjunction with the breadth requirement and mandatory prediction gate favors candidates whose scope and likely answers are already unusually clear. For high-value but uncertain ideas, that is a selection bias toward familiar diagnostic work. Program selection should state a justified investment hypothesis, not certify an eventual result.

## Examples from the reviewed screens

The direction-search synthesis concludes that “every checkable stated problem is already being pursued by its authors” and treats a stated open problem as a declaration of intent. An intent statement can inform competitive positioning. It cannot by itself establish that the problem is answered, that an independent approach lacks value, or that collaboration is unavailable. The phrase “owned” should be replaced with the concrete overlap, advantage, or missing access that matters.

The ethane ODH screen marks several references as title-only or otherwise unavailable, while drawing broad conclusions about the territory's saturation. Those limits justify provisional novelty assessments and targeted source retrieval. They do not support declaring an entire research direction exhausted. This criticism does not establish an unoccupied opportunity in ODH.

The same screen treats “a few selectivity points” as a low practical ceiling. Small absolute gains can still be consequential, depending on output, feed and separation costs, heat management, durability, and the cost of obtaining the gain. Conversely, a higher selectivity number alone can be worthless if it sacrifices productivity or increases other costs. The screen needs an explicit consequence calculation or a defensible qualitative bound before using the percentage change to settle importance. Its combined ethylene-plus-acetic-acid benchmark also does not, by itself, establish the remaining opportunity for an ethylene-focused process; the product split and fair process comparison matter.

There is also an inferential gap in the proposed stop rule: low ethylene-derived isotope incorporation in CO2 is followed by “no transport or steam lever exists.” Route assignment alone does not establish that lack of sensitivity. Similarly, observing the proposed secondary-combustion pattern does not establish that existing pellet models quantitatively cover the tested system and conditions. These implications require supporting evidence or should become separate questions. This is a logical criticism of the screen's inference, not a claim that either intervention will work.

The unverified material access, demanding reactor conditions, and uncertain experimental discrimination remain valid reasons to withhold an ODH execution recommendation. Removing inappropriate selection rules does not resolve them.

## Proposed replacement promotion paragraph

> Promote a candidate for substantial program development when it addresses an important catalytic limitation or scientific uncertainty, seeks a consequential contribution beyond established work, and offers a credible experimental route to that contribution. Document the closest prior art and competing efforts, the distinct knowledge or capability sought, and why the available or obtainable materials, methods, and expertise make pursuit credible. Identify a feasible, informative first stage and the evidence that would justify expansion, redirection, or stopping. Assess hypothesis plausibility, experimental feasibility and informativeness, and practical benefit separately. Breadth across systems strengthens a case when supported but is not mandatory; an important advance in one system can suffice. Neither guaranteed improvement nor useful outcomes in every branch are required. Before each decisive test, specify the claims and decision criteria it can support; freeze prospective predictions before validation when predictive or transfer claims are central. Unresolved dependencies limit the initial investment and must remain visible in the recommendation.

This paragraph preserves the existing seven selection requirements rather than creating another scoring framework. Its central change is to replace arbitrary categorical gates with a documented argument about significance, distinct contribution, testability, and proportionate commitment.

## Recommended disposition

Adopt the revised paragraph, then reconsider only candidates whose recorded rejection materially depends on breadth counts, claimed ownership, or mandatory predictive transfer. Preserve specific prior-art and experimental objections. For each reconsidered candidate, require a concise account of what would change the assessment and why obtaining that evidence is worth the effort. Do not restart every closed direction or promote any on this review alone.

The selection process should remain demanding. Its aim is to select the most valuable uncertainty to investigate with a credible experimental strategy, while controlling the cost of being wrong.
