# Independent review: selective sugar cleavage and tungsten retention

**Correction after the main-article upload:** The [current tungsten audit](current-uploads-tungsten-audit.md) supersedes the earlier productive-leachate interpretation. Li reports 29.5% EG with Ni/M plus preleachate versus 27.1% with Ni/M alone; the former is not a leachate-derived yield. Source-availability statements below record the earlier review.


2026-09-16. Fresh review of [the proposed program](../working/program-development/oxygenate-program-alternative.md), using the revised [selection standards](../standards.md). No experiments were performed. This review did not edit the literature knowledge base. Reading limits and source handoffs appear below.

**Recommendation: develop the program, with a narrower and more demanding scientific claim.** The relationship between productive sugar coordination, support anchoring and W extraction has enough chemical depth and potential process value to justify a substantial research program. Its initial commitment should be a bounded experimental campaign. These judgments are compatible: a program can be worth developing before its preferred catalyst architecture is demonstrated. I would not yet describe it as the strongest available program without the same examination of the other finalists and actual laboratory capabilities.

The proposed program is stronger than a routine support screen. However, demonstrating that an anchor reduces W loss while preserving glycol production is already established prior art at the level of a published catalyst claim. The distinctive advance must be a causal account of **which ligand exchanges preserve cleavage while changing W export**, followed by a prospective catalyst or operating intervention. Establishing an operationally useful retained catalyst does not require proving that no individual W atom ever detaches. Conversely, analytical evidence for a surface population does not establish that this population performs most turnovers.

**The strongest fair case** is that the reactant may simultaneously create the active coordination environment and remove the metal from its intended location. A support interaction that suppresses extraction could suppress productive coordination for the same chemical reason. Learning when these effects can be separated would inform catalyst architecture, substrate concentration, product recycle and regeneration. This question connects supported-oxide chemistry with quantitative sugar kinetics and is a sound fit to the interests described for Iglesia and Gounder. A successful rule in this important reaction is sufficient; multiple unrelated applications are not required.

**The strongest potential rejection** is more substantive than uncertainty about homogeneous contributions: existing catalysts may already combine adequate retention and activity, while existing dissolved-W recovery is sufficient at useful throughput. In that case, distinguishing additional W populations without changing a catalyst or operating decision could become an elaborate attribution exercise. The program should establish the remaining performance or recovery limitation of its best comparator early. It must not choose a weak, highly leaching oxide merely because that makes retention improvements easy to demonstrate.

The Ti-linked catalyst is not proof that the entire mechanistic problem is solved. An empirical durability result and a predictive coordination–extraction relationship are different contributions. But the proposal should not imply that productive anchoring remains wholly unprecedented. Whether that paper quantitatively attributes turnover to retained sites remains unverified here; absence of that verification is an unresolved reading task, not evidence that the paper lacks such experiments.

**The mononuclear versus paired-W contrast is a useful hypothesis generator, not yet a controlled contradiction.** The [PNAS 2022 study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2206399119) combines several forms of evidence for a paired-W mechanism. Its solid NMR samples were prepared by impregnation and vacuum drying at 60 °C, then thermally treated at 120 °C; spectra were recorded at 25 °C. This is not direct observation of an aqueous active complex under the 205 °C tandem reaction benchmark. That boundary limits transfer of the structural assignment without negating the authors' mechanistic evidence.

The locally available [Inorganic Chemistry manuscript](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.inorgchem.3c04236), figures 1–3 and associated discussion, uses 2 mol/L metal and 1 mol/L sugar in the illustrated NMR experiments. These are valuable structural references. They are not automatically the dominant complexes in a much more dilute, hot flowing catalyst effluent. The [ACS Catalysis 2024 abstract](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.4c02791) assigns different mononuclear bidentate tungstate species to epimerization and cleavage, probably with W(VI). Its full kinetic and spectroscopic conditions must be read before choosing a motif-specific experiment.

Accordingly, do not order activity as “isolated < paired” or the reverse across differences in pH, W concentration, sugar/W ratio, hydrogenation metal, H2, water content and temperature. In the supported series, total loading changes domain size, support exposure, reduction and transport together. A W–W EXAFS contribution need not count active pairs, and its absence need not exclude a small active paired population. Wet-state XAS should establish which populations are distinguishable at the actual concentration before the proposal promises atomic attribution. Support coordination and sugar coordination can also be difficult to separate through their oxygen shells. This is a measurement limitation, not a reason to abandon useful kinetics.

**The current mass/function experiments are valuable, but their interpretations need asymmetric decision rules.** Positive exported-liquid activity is strong evidence of mobile catalytic capacity. Negative exported-liquid activity is substantially weaker evidence for a retained active site. The proposal already recognizes an empirical homogeneous-rate bound; the same caution must be incorporated in the alternative-outcome table.

| Experiment | Strongest attainable claim | Main failure of a stronger claim and required correction |
|---|---|---|
| Hot filtration or solid-retaining reactor | Material passing the stated separation can retain catalytic capacity at specified temperature, delay and composition. | A negative result can reflect cooling, adsorption, species decay or removal of a mobile colloid. Quantify filter and tubing W recovery and test the complete apparatus with a soluble reference. |
| Inject fresh substrate into an isothermal downstream section | Exported W-containing material can catalyze cleavage of newly supplied substrate, if the matrix blank is inactive. | It does not directly quantify its upstream turnover fraction. Added glucose can create a different W complex or change product inhibition. Use measured matrix composition and, where needed, an isotopically distinguishable substrate spike; resolve new product against transported intermediates. |
| Persistent activity after liquid washout | A retained inventory sustains activity despite export of the original fluid inventory. | The remaining solid may continuously regenerate a short-lived dissolved catalyst. This observation alone cannot distinguish a surface from a dissolving reservoir. Report both alternatives unless other evidence resolves them. |
| Rate and W concentration rise/fall at different times | There is a reproducible dynamical response after residence-time and sampling corrections. | Lags can arise from reactor mixing, adsorption, pore transport, analytical dispersion, speciation and intermediate storage. Calibrate hydraulic response and measured-species recovery. Fit a minimal mass balance before calling a lag a chemical sequence. |
| Recapture and return of activity | W can be recovered into a material that supplies activity in a subsequent test. | Capture may cause precipitation or create a different active complex; it does not demonstrate microscopic reversal into the original anchor. Compare hot capture with cooling-induced capture and measure both capture yield and subsequent productivity. |
| Low effluent W concentration | Low net W export within an analytical bound. | Rapid local release and readsorption can sustain a mobile active pool while producing little net export. Distinguish process retention from a claim that turnover occurs on permanently attached W. |

A minimal counterexample explains the washout problem. Let a solid release a reactive mobile species at rate J, with washout rate constant Q/V and chemical loss rate constant k_d. At steady state its concentration is J/[V(k_d + Q/V)]. Cleavage can persist as long as J persists, even after every molecule in the initial liquid has left. If the mobile species decays rapidly, a downstream section can appear inactive while the upstream chemistry is mainly mobile. This is a proposed identifiability model, not a claim that this mechanism actually operates. The practical result could still be a useful retained catalyst system if local return keeps net W export small.

The outcome table therefore needs an explicit **unresolved compartment**: “A solid inventory is necessary, net export is bounded, but fast release/return and interfacial or retained-site turnover remain indistinguishable.” It should also allow simultaneous pathways. Scientific value is not lost merely because one population remains ambiguous; prospective predictions about W export and cleavage can still be tested. The evidence would not support a unique bound-site mechanism.

**A feasible first experiment should ask whether this distinction matters under conditions where chemistry and analysis both work.** I recommend the following limited sequence, rather than constructing the entire proposed multizone apparatus immediately:

1. Reproduce one soluble-W glucose-cleavage reference and establish carbonyl recovery through the actual sampling train. Choose temperature and concentration from a fully read experiment. Use glucose first; erythrose can validate recovery or kinetics if needed, but success on the easier C4 substrate must not substitute for glucose cleavage. Determine the smallest reaction interval with measurable products and acceptable carbon recovery. If no cleavage-only analytical window exists, explicitly redesign around validated capture or the tandem domain.
2. Use one reproducible supported-W material, its W-free support and a soluble reference in a solid-retaining flow reactor. W/ZrO2 is defensible as a tractable chemical contrast, provided the project does not present it as the best practical catalyst. Establish blank reactions, W recovery, residence-time distribution and measurable steady behavior before interpreting transients.
3. Split freshly generated effluent between immediate quench and a heated section supplied with a distinguishable glucose pulse. Use a small number of independently known hot transfer delays. Repeat with the soluble-W control and a matrix blank. A positive signal establishes export of useful function; dependence on delay tests whether transit destroys that function. A null signal only bounds exported activity for those times and conditions.
4. Make one feed-to-water-to-feed change and measure W and carbon outputs through the return to steady operation. Do not equate early recovery with structural regeneration. Freeze the competing mass-balance predictions before interpreting the transient.

The first campaign should deliver calibrated upper or lower bounds on exported activity and net W loss, plus an explicit accounting of what remains unidentifiable. It is feasible in principle with pressure-rated aqueous flow equipment, reliable carbon analysis and ICP; access to these capabilities is not established in the repo. Synchrotron measurements are not prerequisites for that first result. They become important before asserting an anchoring geometry. A separately located hydrogenation trap also introduces residence time during which fragments can react, and should be added only after direct analytical failure is established and its recovery is calibrated.

In parallel with preparing this campaign, finish the full-text audit of the Ti-linked catalyst and reproduce its retention/performance baseline early under its published tandem conditions if those conditions are accessible. Do not infer its behavior by simply deleting Ni or H2: that can change the W working state. A common hydrogenation function is desirable for later architecture comparisons, but synthesizing a nominally analogous W/Ti material without Ni is a new preparation requiring characterization, not automatically a faithful control.

**The program becomes substantial through what follows the first experiment.** The next step should perturb a chemically specified anchoring or ligand variable, measure the same cleavage/export responses, and predict an unmeasured condition. A useful attainable claim would be: “Within this support and aqueous composition range, changing a defined W–support interaction shifts W export by a predictable amount while preserving the measured cleavage pathway and productivity.” This remains stronger than an activity/leaching correlation even if rapid local exchange cannot be eliminated. A different valuable claim would be a demonstrated ligand-induced inactive sink whose reversal improves recycle performance without additional W. Both require causality and prospective validation, not just fitting a deactivation trace.

Do not elevate the proposed cleavage-only domain into a process recommendation without returning to H2, hydrogenation catalyst and realistic product accumulation. These conditions can change reduction, support chemistry and fragment lifetime. Likewise, regeneration may restore nominal W content while changing the ensemble or consuming enough reagents and time to erase a retention advantage. Measure productivity after recovery, purge and downtime as well as elemental recovery.

The practical comparison should start with a simple material and energy balance, not a precise economic forecast. At matched EG output and yield, calculate gross W export, recycle inventory, fresh makeup, water added by recovery and the fraction of the separation train that actually changes. A reduction in W concentration may not reduce total recovery duty if a large organic stream still requires processing. Conversely, preventing W deposition on the hydrogenation catalyst could matter even when W purchase cost is small. The patent descriptions make these plausible burdens; their magnitudes require measurement. [Ooms et al.](https://doi.org/10.1039/C3GC41431K) remains a useful productivity reference, but its maximum-yield and maximum-productivity points are separate, and its cold-filtrate measurements cannot quantify hot W circulation.

My confidence judgments are:

| Dimension | Judgment |
|---|---|
| Existence of consequential coordination/retention chemistry | Strong rationale; the specific rule remains a hypothesis. Prior anchoring success makes the preferred architecture plausible while narrowing novelty. |
| Ability to produce informative initial experiments | Moderate, conditional on analytical recovery and pressure-flow access. Operational bounds are more attainable than unique assignment of every active population. |
| Ability to establish a causal anchoring rule | Moderate to uncertain. Working-state convergence and ensemble-averaged spectroscopy are genuine risks; a well-defined material intervention can still be informative. |
| Meaningful practical improvement | Uncertain. Good anchored catalysts and soluble-W recovery are serious competitors. Improvement requires a demonstrated remaining burden and a matched process comparison. |
| Program-level recommendation | Develop now, with the first campaign and prior-art audit limiting the initial investment. Do not promote the original title as a novel demonstration of immobilization, or promise complete surface attribution. |

The specific proposal fixes are to add the Ti-linked and durable-oxide comparators; describe the structural evidence under its actual conditions; make the transient/transfer decision rules asymmetric; add unresolved and mixed compartments; distinguish net process retention from permanent atomic attachment; and attach the second-stage intervention to a causal prediction. If the best existing anchored catalyst already meets the relevant retention/output requirement and no consequential coordination question remains experimentally distinguishable, redirect or stop this program. If the initial catalyst fails but provides a discriminating chemical result, that is a justified basis for the next material change.

**Follow-up review of process accounting, 2026-09-16.** I independently checked [the process-burden note](../calculations/tungsten-process-burden.md) against the complete public HTML descriptions saved in `/tmp/tungsten-process-audit/`. Its balance equations, dimensional normalization and distinction between W inventory, elemental recovery and restored catalytic function are sound. The illustrative counterexample is correct: export of 0.1 times the reference with no recovery gives ten times the fresh makeup of the reference with 99% recovery. Neither number is evidence of achievable performance.

One terminology correction was requested from the author: `Q C_W/P` is **gross outward W flow in the selected stream**, not net W loss from a reactor that also receives returned W. The latter requires the return and other streams in the full balance. A direct product recycle can cross a reactor boundary repeatedly without being lost or entering the recovery equipment. For `M_W = (1−η) E_W`, η must mean the fraction actually returned after all recovery losses and purges; a separator's elemental recovery alone may overstate it. Accumulated inactive W belongs in inventory even if it cannot sustain reaction. The note correctly avoids converting recovered mass into recovered activity by assumption.

The patent-evidence caveat is supported. Restricting inspection to each HTML description section, rather than website citation/legal tables, found no tables and no labeled working-example series in either document. References to examples in the low-W description concern earlier disclosures; the remaining example language is generic. This does not establish that the inventors performed no experiments, or that experimental support is absent from every related filing or incorporated reference. The note explicitly restricts its conclusion to the inspected descriptions, which is appropriate.

Paragraphs 073–074 of [WO2022066160A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2022066160A1/en) already disclose varying or ceasing W addition to mobilize deposited W, with optional pH adjustment while maintaining conversion. Feed interruption, reservoir depletion and pH-assisted remobilization therefore cannot carry novelty by themselves. The new contribution can still be an experimentally validated chemical relationship that predicts when such operation preserves useful cleavage or restores the hydrogenation function. Exact economics are not required before investigating that relationship. Process advantages should follow measured changes to a real burden, using the accounting note's boundaries.

**Follow-up audit of Li 2020 supporting information, 2026-09-16.** The [lawful SI](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/23354060), DOI [10.1021/acssuschemeng.0c00836.s001](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.0c00836.s001), is now available locally as `/tmp/tungsten-process-audit/li2020-si.pdf`. I read its extracted text and independently inspected the rendered original tables on S8–S9. This supersedes the earlier SI access limitation; the main article remains unread.

Table S2, S8, reports Ni–W/M with 68.7% EG yield initially and 66.9% in the last of seven reported effective cycles. This provides a concrete reuse benchmark. The table defines effective cycles by a yield-loss threshold, rather than establishing continuous lifetime, full W retention or absence of treatment between runs. A re-calcination/re-reduction footnote marks a different catalyst row; its absence from Ni–W/M is not sufficient documentation of that material's recovery procedure.

Table S3, S9, already tests a solution prepared by exposing Ni–W/M to water/H2, and combines that solution with Ni/M for fresh cellulose conversion. The combined condition reports 29.5% EG carbon yield; the solution alone gives only trace EG. Separate Ni/M plus AMT conditions at the table's 200–800 ppm loadings give 33.2–44.5% EG yields. Thus leachate activity tests and comparison with soluble W are already part of this catalyst's evidentiary record.

The preleach uses 0.15 g Ni–W/M, 40 mL deionized water, 518 K, 4 MPa H2, 120 min and 800 rpm. Cellulose is absent from that preparation. The later catalytic test uses 0.5 g cellulose under the same stated temperature, pressure, duration and agitation. There is a real textual inconsistency: entry 2 is labeled Ni/M plus solution, while the shared footnote for entries 2–6 specifies deionized water and AMT. Preserve that discrepancy pending the main article; do not silently choose a mixing volume. The SI does not supply the leachate W concentration, speciation, hot separation or transfer delay. It also does not show a Ni/M-only comparator in S3. Trace EG without added hydrogenation catalyst cannot independently establish absence of cleavage.

These observations strengthen the practical comparator and narrow the experimental novelty. A fresh-substrate leachate experiment remains a useful reproducibility/control measurement; it cannot be presented as the new method. The program's additional contribution must resolve substrate-dependent release during actual turnover, controlled changes in anchoring or coordination, and consequent cleavage/export behavior. The existing preleach is not a time-resolved measurement under reacting sugar, so it does not settle that question. Nor may the 29.5/68.7 yield ratio be interpreted as the homogeneous fraction of activity: the catalyst inventories, species histories, hydrogenation functions and reaction networks are not equivalent, and endpoint yield is not a rate contribution.

The SI therefore does not close the program, but it raises the standard for the first campaign. Reproduce the published leachate/soluble-W contrast as a baseline where its preparation can be resolved, then test one defined chemical difference between water preleaching and substrate-present turnover. The important new result is a causal explanation and prediction, not a more elaborate confirmation that some leached W is catalytically useful. Seven-cycle performance also prevents treating supported-W retention as an entirely unrealized practical objective. A meaningful improvement must confront this baseline, with its recovery protocol and hot W balance still to be established from the main article or experiment.
