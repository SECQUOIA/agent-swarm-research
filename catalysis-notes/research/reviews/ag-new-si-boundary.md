# Ag chlorine evidence: independent check of the recovered Artiglia SI

2026-09-16. Bounded independent review of the [structural-alternative assessment](ag-chlorine-structure-alternative.md) and its [earlier independent check](ag-structure-alternative-independent-check.md). No experiments performed or knowledge-base files changed.

## Decision

**The SI preserves the existing experimental recommendation but requires two evidence updates:** there is direct chlorine-localization evidence on spent supported powder, and the TEM treatment caption conflicts with the main text. Neither change establishes that restructuring controls Ni-dependent retention or warrants a separate structural campaign.

The distinction between functional recovery and restoration of an identical catalyst state remains sound. Ordinary continuation, sham histories and conditional structural measurements remain appropriate.

## Sources and reading scope

Read the [SI reading copy](../../literature/papers/artiglia2025-supplementary-information-for-reaction-mechanism/fulltext.md), its package note and relevant main-text passages. Inspected original embedded images for Figures S9, S14, S16, S23, S33, S37 and S38 from the [archived DOCX](../../literature/runs/2026-09-15-ag-chlorine-xps/round-1/source/artiglia2025-si.docx). The [publisher-hosted original SI](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-7141633/v1/fddc51a4cc15743df1d7576d.docx) supplements the [Research Square v1 preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-7141633/v1). The figures were extracted without changing their scientific content; TIFF files were converted to PNG for display. No spectra were refitted or microscopy images quantified. A journal version was not established.

### 1. Powder chlorine localization is supported, with a different measurement limit

**Observed:** Figure S23 shows an Ag-rich region and a spatially overlapping Cl map on spent Ag/Al2O3. A selected Ag-particle spectrum contains a small Cl signal absent from the displayed alumina-region spectrum. The caption specifies 48 h at 230 °C, 15% ethylene, 3% oxygen, 0.5 ppm VC, nitrogen balance and a reported space velocity of 10,000 mL min−1 g−1. This is supported powder, not foil.

**Limit:** this is ex situ elemental localization in selected regions after reaction. It does not establish the surface coverage, chemical state, atomic site, spatial uniformity across the catalyst or persistence of the working Cl distribution. Main-text microscopy methods disperse powder in ethanol and deposit it on a grid. This measurement is not equivalent to the 1 mbar powder AP-XPS experiment with 2 ppm VC.

Figure S14 independently confirms the very low signal-to-noise ratio of the powder AP-XPS chlorine feature. Thus the earlier statement that the stronger *AP-XPS* chlorine characterization used foil and 10 ppm DCE remains correct. Add the spent-powder EDX evidence so the reader does not infer that all localization evidence comes from foil. Do not upgrade it to a calibrated working Cl census.

### 2. The TEM supports the reported qualitative comparison, but its caption has an unresolved condition error

**Observed:** Figure S37 contains panels **a–p**, arranged in four groups. It displays void-like features marked by red circles, with fewer and smaller conspicuous features in the last group than in the preceding group. Even the last group contains circled features; suppression must not become complete elimination.

**Source conflict:** the original DOCX caption labels only **a–d** as four conditions and calls the 200 °C treatment **with 0.5 ppm VC**. The main text assigns a–d to fresh material, e–h to 200 °C **without VC**, followed by 230 °C without VC and 230 °C with VC. This mismatch is present in the original XML, not introduced by text conversion. The intended four-group mapping is plausible from the main text and layout, but the SI does not independently resolve the 200 °C chlorine condition.

Retain the authors' reported 230 °C suppression with VC, explicitly recognizing the caption problem. Do not claim that this SI independently verifies a clean chlorine-free temperature series. The displayed fields provide no population-wide void distribution, sampling uncertainty or controlled morphology-only perturbation. They therefore cannot establish that voids caused the CO2 difference. The stronger, separately established van Hoof result remains unaffected.

### 3. Carbon and oxygen assignments gain supporting detail, not unique mechanistic identification

Figure S9 visibly supports suppression of the low-binding-energy powder carbon component with VC, especially at 150–200 °C. Table S4 assigns 282.9 eV to CHy, 284.0 eV to C2Hx and 285.0 eV to a combined category including adventitious carbon, longer carbon species and weakly adsorbed ethylene. The last category is explicitly nonunique. Tables give peak positions, widths and line-shape choices; these are not uncertainty estimates or evidence that all alternative decompositions were excluded. Lower abundance still does not measure a species' reaction flux.

Figure S16 shows broad powder O1s difference features attributed to hydroxyls and adsorbed water using two normalization choices. Area normalization introduces a negative contribution near the support's 531.4 eV lattice-oxygen feature. That negative difference is not direct evidence of removal of alumina lattice oxygen. The main text acknowledges overlap between hydroxyl and oxygenated-carbon binding energies and uses accompanying C1s behavior to motivate its assignment.

Table S7 and Figure S33 give the more detailed lattice/dioxygen oxygen decomposition **on foil with 10 ppm DCE**. They must remain separate from the powder difference spectra. The lattice-oxygen component is lower with DCE at higher temperatures in the plotted foil series; the plotted trend is not uniform suppression at every temperature. The selected reconstructed-surface assignments remain an interpretation supported by calculations, not a uniquely measured inventory of active oxygen on the proposed promoted powder.

### 4. The TPSR control does not exclude every common-intermediate mechanism

Figure S38 shows additional CO2, without a visible corresponding EO increase, when oxygen heats a previously ethylene-exposed surface after gas-phase ethylene removal. This supports a retained carbon population capable of yielding CO2 under that sequence. The authors infer separate mechanisms. The narrower inference is warranted: purging, room-temperature adsorption and subsequent oxygen heating can select different retained species than steady catalytic turnover. The control does not establish that all EO and CO2 pathways lack a common earlier intermediate under working conditions. No calibrated EO detection limit or complete transient carbon balance is given in the inspected SI passage.

## Required integration

Replace the earlier SI-unread statements with this reading scope; add the spent-powder EDX observation; flag the Figure S37 caption discrepancy. Preserve the existing limits on causal attribution, formulation transfer and quantitative surface inventories. No new missing source is required for this bounded check: the SI's Guo et al. 2024 oxygen-species reference is already present in the local knowledge base.
