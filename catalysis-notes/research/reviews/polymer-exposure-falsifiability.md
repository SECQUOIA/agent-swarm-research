# Can unequal component recovery predict polymer catalyst use?

2026-09-15. Fresh independent assessment of the single 2-pentanone hypothesis in [Polymer impurity research after recovery of the patent benchmark](polymer-value-after-patent.md), against the [protocol review](polymer-patent-protocol-review.md), [benchmark accounting](polymer-patent-benchmark-accounting.md), and [earlier independent review](polymer-independent-review.md). No experiments were performed.

**2026-09-16 source update:** the [post-upload audit](post-upload-polymer-audit.md) checked the Science main article/SI and the original W-oxygenate figures. The published three-charge makeup control below supersedes the earlier missing-dose limit. No experiments were performed.

## Decision

**Retain a small, chemically motivated study, with a narrower claim than two established exposure laws.** The useful question is whether ketone exposure leaves a persistent loss in the Na-containing component while W function is retained or recovers on a useful operating timescale, and whether that distinction predicts a different feed-acceptance or catalyst-makeup decision. This is sufficiently specific to justify testing. It is not yet a substantial program, and molecular component assays alone cannot answer the operating tandem question.

Two corrections matter. First, the old W result establishes condition-dependent tolerance and recoverable inhibition, not one response at every ketone concentration. Root and the value reviewer checked exposed primary text: a recycle test tolerated approximately 300 ppm 2-pentanone, lost conversion at 400 ppm, and regained initial activity on clean feed (pp.147–149/Fig.4). A distinct 200 ppm test at 460 °C showed little activity/selectivity change, with changes in heavier-product selectivity and color after switching back (pp.149–150/Fig.6). The uploaded original Figures 4 and 6 were visually checked in the 2026-09-16 audit. The full text also reports transformed carbonyl-containing material in recycle product purges (p.149), strengthening the need to measure delivered and transformed species. The defensible proposed contrast is **W tolerance or recovery versus persistent Na-function loss**. This is a direct same-molecule motivation for testing concentration dependence, but neither threshold nor recovery transfers automatically to 320 °C polymer operation.

Second, a persistent Na response is not yet a cumulative-dose law. Equal delivered ketone doses can produce different transformed species, local concentrations, and damage. A study that finds unequal recovery but does not predict the mixture's behavior remains useful component chemistry; it has not established a feed specification or the claimed explanation of polymer lifetime.

## Chemical motivation and its limits

The patent's Na material is made by heating sodium metal with extensively dried alumina under inert conditions (§§0144–0146). It is not simply a conventional oxide support containing a sodium salt. This gives a more concrete reason to test susceptibility to a carbonyl than the generic statement that a basic support adsorbs oxygenates. A carbonyl could interact with reactive Na-derived ensembles or participate in reactions that leave persistent species. **The synthesis does not identify the working Na state, prove residual metallic Na, or establish a stoichiometric ketone-reduction mechanism.** Coordination, catalytic transformation, retention, and water-mediated changes remain alternatives.

Na-associated functions must also be separated conceptually. Olefin isomerization can survive while the ability to initiate conversion of saturated chains declines, or conversely. One molecular isomerization rate cannot represent both. The patent itself reports chain cleavage/olefin formation with both components and substrate-dependent differences (§§0126–0127). Assigning every initiation event to Na and every W contribution to metathesis would impose a stronger division of labor than the evidence warrants.

The current practical motivation is sound: substantial initial solid inventory, clean-feed reuse loss, a known fresh-Na rescue, and substantial preparation of postconsumer feed. These observations create a consequential catalyst-use problem. They do not establish that ketones cause it. The ketone experiment earns practical relevance only if its exposure overlaps a characterized feed or a chemically justified transformation product of that feed.

## What the proposed experiment can actually identify

### Recovery contrast is the first claim

For each operating component, compare its response after exposure with an age- and handling-matched clean trajectory under the **same assay feed**. Record the initial transient and later response separately. Define recovery over a predeclared operating interval long enough to exceed measured apparatus washout, but relevant to the available cycle time. A residual loss at its end is persistence under that protocol, not irreversible destruction.

An internal-alkene/ethylene assay may maintain or create W sites differently from a polymer mixture. Returning to that assay can itself reactivate W. Likewise, an Na assay can remove or transform retained species. A fast recovery under the assay establishes recoverable function under that assay; it does not prove recovery during polymer operation. Clean interruptions reveal handling and restart effects, but cannot by themselves prove that the assay preserves the operating state.

The smallest informative design therefore measures the exposure and recovery in the operating component reaction where possible, with no added regeneration or long conditioning step. Component tests should match temperature, ethylene activity and the relevant hydrocarbon environment as closely as practicable. Differences that remain must be stated as limits of transfer, then tested in the mixture. There is no need to demand an exact microscopic site census before proceeding.

### A dose law is a stronger, separately testable claim

An illustrative model would describe the W rate as a clean-state rate multiplied by a reversible response to measured ketone concentration, while the surviving Na function follows

`a_Na(t) / a_Na,clean(t) = exp[−k ∫ C_eff(t) dt]`.

This equation is a **candidate model**, not an established mechanism. It assumes separable clean decay and damage proportional to one defined effective concentration. If water or a condensation product causes the loss, parent-ketone input is not automatically `C_eff`. Defining `C_eff` after every failed prediction so that the model fits would make the hypothesis unfalsifiable. A measured parent concentration can still serve as an empirical predictor over a restricted domain, provided the domain and transfer limits are explicit.

Under this simple model, equal effective dose gives equal final surviving Na function at a common observation time. It does **not** give equal integrated polymer output: one history may suppress W more strongly, and the timing of Na loss changes useful conversion before the endpoint. Reversible inhibition alone can also give history-dependent integrated output because its concentration response can be nonlinear. Thus, different yields at equal input dose do not identify cumulative damage, and equal final yields do not prove it.

One exposure/recovery history cannot establish a general concentration law and a general damage law. Use it to select between a small number of explicit response models or to set bounded predictions. Test a second, substantially different history without refitting its parameters. If the first history cannot constrain the alternatives enough to predict a decision, add only the component measurement that resolves that ambiguity; do not launch a contaminant atlas.

### Polymer rate memory is not automatically catalyst memory

During a batch, molecular weight, unsaturation, ethylene inventory, viscosity, solid contact and product concentrations evolve. After a pulse, the exposed batch need not have the same substrate state as an unexposed batch at the same clock time. Its post-pulse rate should not be judged solely against its own pre-pulse rate or an uncorrected clean trace. The pulse may have changed the polymer trajectory while every catalyst function recovered.

A common subsequent clean-PE charge on the retained mixture is a useful practical persistence test, with matched charge and handling controls. It does not perfectly reset the system: residual polymer, retained carbonyl products and solids histories must be bounded. If these inventories prevent a clean interpretation, the result remains a useful repeated-charge output measurement, with no unique assignment to catalyst damage. There is no initial need to physically separate the intimately mixed powders.

## Smallest useful sequence

1. **Establish one clean reference and one delivered exposure.** Use one PE and the disclosed formulation: 1 g PE, 0.4 g W-containing solid and 0.4 g Na-containing solid per initial charge. Establish three clean repeated charges on the retained mixture with handling controls, and the published companion control that adds 0.4 g fresh Na solid before charge 2 only, with only PE added before charge 3 (Science Figure 4B). Measure the ordinary clean time course and each full cycle, including cooling and recharge. Select one ketone dose that is analytically resolvable and does not simply stop all conversion. Measure delivery, disappearance, major transformed products and water sufficiently to bound the operative exposure. A gas-phase washout curve alone does not show removal from the melt or pores. Do not escalate to extreme concentrations merely to force a W response if the feed connection is already lost.
2. **Test the component contrast once.** Expose already functioning W and Na samples, each with its clean interruption control, to that history. Measure W metathesis and the Na function that can plausibly limit the selected PE reaction. If initiation and isomerization cannot be distinguished by a validated readout, retain that uncertainty instead of calling one rate total Na activity. Supports and cross-component contributions need checking only to the extent required to assign those readouts. Reject the specific contrast if it fails here; do not repair it by adding new materials.
3. **Predict one operating-mixture challenge.** Compare a short, higher exposure with a longer, lower exposure at equal delivered dose and common total observation time, alongside the clean reference. Choose timing using measured mixing/removal times. Predeclare the predicted transient ordering and the residual loss on the subsequent clean charge, including uncertainty from exposure and substrate evolution. Keep catalyst inventory, polymer supply, ethylene conditions and handling common. The component responses must predict something more specific than “less activity gives less product.”
4. **Choose one practical follow-through only if a signal survives.** Test the resulting prediction on one characterized feed/preparation contrast, or test a timed fresh-Na makeup decision if this is the decision supported by the result. Retaining the whole mixture and adding Na is physically credible and already disclosed. Selective recovery of W from mixed powders is not an assumed process option. Compare accepted polymer-carbon output per initial and added solid, full cycle time, ethylene consumption and preparation losses against the published three-charge makeup control under matched conditions. Its conditional material-productivity factor is `B = 2f`, where `f` is mean accepted polymer-derived output relative to three fresh charges; this is not a measured economic benefit. Count carried-over polymer carbon once and do not substitute W TON or total ethylene-derived product for polymer-carbon recovery.

These are sequential gates, not a full factorial study. Independent preparations and repeats sufficient to resolve the proposed decision are needed for the decisive comparison; a universal replicate count cannot be justified before its variability is known. If the reactor cannot impose and measure two distinct exposure histories, use measured repeated-feed histories and narrow the model accordingly. Do not portray an unmeasured dosing waveform as a controlled concentration intervention.

## Falsification and the decision each result changes

| Observation | What is rejected or supported | Consequential decision |
|---|---|---|
| W shows no appreciable suppression at the relevant exposure, while Na retains loss | Reject transient W inhibition as the explanation in this domain; retain unequal susceptibility | Do not infer W replacement from the original output loss. Test whether retaining the mixture with measured Na makeup meets the throughput target. |
| W remains impaired beyond the selected clean operating interval | Reject predominantly recoverable W loss **within that interval** | Include W impairment or longer recovery downtime in catalyst-use and feed-acceptance limits; do not call it permanently destroyed. |
| Na returns to its clean trajectory after verified washout | Reject persistent Na loss under this exposure and recovery | A cumulative Na poison allowance is unsupported; assess the cost of transient lost production before imposing additional cleanup. |
| Equal measured effective doses give different final Na-function retention beyond resolved uncertainty | Reject the simple dose-only model | A cumulative impurity budget alone cannot specify the feed. Peak concentration, transformations or state-dependent exposure require attention only if they change the decision. |
| Components show the proposed contrast, but it fails to predict tandem persistence or a subsequent clean charge | Reject transfer of the component model to polymer operation | Do not use the molecular assay to set a waste-feed specification or a Na makeup schedule. Preserve the narrower chemical result. |
| The controlled exposure causes little incremental output loss beside clean decay or transport | Reject the selected contaminant as the controlling throughput limitation | Close this impurity branch for the tested domain and address the documented dominant burden. |
| The measured feed does not deliver the ketone class or the relevant damaging products | Reject the proposed feed connection | Do not require costly removal of total oxygen or unrelated additives on this evidence. |

A result can change a decision without improving the catalyst: rejecting an unnecessary cleanup target, showing that dilution cannot avoid persistent loss, or excluding a feed whose measured preparation/makeup burden is disproportionate are all useful. Conversely, rediscovering fresh-Na rescue adds little unless the exposure relationship predicts **when or how much** makeup is worthwhile beyond clean-feed decay. An economically optimal schedule cannot be claimed without prices and process data; a clear material/output comparison can be made first.

## Originality, confidence and scope

Two subsequent root checks bound the practical interpretation: [oxygenate/coke prior art](polymer-coke-proxy-boundary.md) separates reduced deposits from useful performance, and [makeup accounting](../calculations/polymer-makeup-burden.md) shows why retained W and higher W turnover do not by themselves establish efficient total catalyst use. Neither is a measured result for the proposed mixture.

**Chemical-hypothesis confidence: low to moderate.** Different responses of a sodium-derived material and supported W are plausible, but neither persistent ketone-induced Na loss nor transient W inhibition under this recipe is established. The old W evidence supports concentration-dependent alternatives and makes tolerance an especially important null result.

**Feasibility and information value: moderate, conditional on the platform.** Controlled component exposure is tractable. Distinguishing retained poison, assay reactivation, changed polymer state and genuine operating persistence is harder, but can be tested with a small sequence rather than resolved by exhaustive characterization. The useful claim can remain operational if molecular site assignments are unavailable.

**Practical-improvement confidence: low.** The inventory and preparation burden is real; contaminant control of that burden remains unproved. Feed selection or a better supported makeup decision is a more credible first contribution than a major lifetime gain.

**Originality: provisional and bounded.** Different susceptibility, oxygenate effects on W, and fresh-Na rescue have prior art. What could add meaningful knowledge is a validated exposure relationship that predicts useful throughput and a feed or makeup choice in this tandem reaction. The Science main article/SI now provide a concrete reuse benchmark; the audit found no ketone exposure/recovery study of the coupled mixture in the inspected methods/results. The unavailable dissertation body and unresolved patent regeneration figures still limit the novelty assessment. They are not grounds to demand that the experiment already succeed before proposing it. Retain this study because its competing chemical responses and practical consequence are testable; expand it only if the operating prediction survives.
