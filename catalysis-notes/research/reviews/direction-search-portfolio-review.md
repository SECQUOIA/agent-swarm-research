## 1. Consistency of standards: are the candidates weak, or is the bar unreachable?

**J: both, in a specific way.** The candidates are not weak. Five of the six define a real decision that a finite campaign would change (deSOx dosing; ketonization water removal and regeneration; front-regime reporting; Zn/MFI synthesis recipe; alloy-versus-pressure choice), and four have an informative negative. They were nevertheless all declined, and the same decline would have met any other candidate written to this template, for three reasons.

1. **The template manufactures diagnostics.** "One reaction, one consequential uncertainty, two live alternatives, smallest first campaign" produces, by construction, a single binary question whose answer is one decision on one process. The screens then read "one decision, one process" as "narrow", and narrow maps to "bounded diagnostic". The methods screen is explicit that the stronger idea in its territory (a turnover-linked inactivator) was excluded because it is a hypothesis without a validated reagent; that is exactly the kind of idea a program, rather than a diagnostic, exists to develop.
2. **The prior-art test conflates intervention novelty with knowledge novelty.** The Cu-SCR screen declines mainly because NH3-assisted deSOx is a 2016 paper and a 2024 patent claim, while acknowledging that the sign and size of its effect on the irreversible Cu pathway are unknown and unpublished. `standards.md` §Selection does not require a new intervention; it asks for "the narrow additional knowledge sought" and says an informative negative can justify a program. The decision brief's hardening ("not occupied by direct prior art") is being applied to interventions.
3. **No promotion threshold exists.** `standards.md` lists what a program must explain (items 1–7) but never states what distinguishes "promote" from "retain as diagnostic". With "rejection is a valid outcome" in the brief and no positive criterion, the safe reviewer output is the diagnostic. Six screens by one template on one day, all reaching the same verdict, is evidence of the template's bias, not of the field's emptiness.

**A reachable but still substantial bar (J).** Keep every evidence rule unchanged (verification, labelling, matched controls, frozen predictions). Define a program as substantial by *breadth of consequence*, not by campaign size or intervention novelty: the central uncertainty, resolved either way, must change (a) a decision in at least two of the classes material / operating policy / regeneration / measurement-reporting, or (b) one decision class across more than one process or catalyst family; the first campaign must be decisive on standard apparatus within a stated horizon; the *knowledge* (not necessarily the intervention) must be absent from the literature after a documented search; and the expansion gate must be a frozen prospective prediction. Under that bar the Cu-SCR diagnostic (dosing policy + aging-protocol design + formulation interaction) and a cross-reaction Zr–O program (Section 3) are candidates for promotion; the Ru and Zn/MFI diagnostics are not.

## 3. Cross-cutting opportunity: one m-ZrO2, three reactions, one water rule

**What is established (E).** DME-treated m-ZrO2 exposes a minority population of Zr–O pairs (0.55–0.56 nm⁻², H2O pulse titration) that mediates alkane dehydrogenation and alkene hydrogenation through the same kinetically relevant step, with site-normalized constants unchanged by sintering ([Jaegers 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.4c07766); [Jaegers 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5c03523); local note jaegers2025 p.7, p.13–15). Trace water titrates those pairs during ethylbenzene dehydrogenation and ethylbenzene scavenges bound water; periodic chemical dehydroxylation restores rates ([Artsiusheuski 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5c04904)). On commercial m-ZrO2 at 350 °C, ketonization is inhibited by water with a small Langmuir constant (K_H2O ≈ 0.0038 kPa⁻¹) and deactivation was explicitly deferred (Miller 2022, [10.1021/acs.iecr.1c04548](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.1c04548), as read by the liquid-phase screen). Ammal and Heyden compute a hydroxylation equilibrium and a β-H barrier that rises with water pressure for one site model ([10.1021/acscatal.5c08338](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5c08338)).

**Prior art on a cross-reaction rule (web-checked).** The Iglesia/UC patent [WO2024177986A2](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2024177986A2/en) claims LAB-pair catalysis for dehydrogenation, hydrogenation, alkylation, CO/H2 reactions, oligomerization, metathesis, dehydrocyclization and others; it quantifies H2O/CO2/O2 titration (each O2 titrates two sites via H2O), sets feed impurity limits (order of 0.2–5 ppm O2, <2 ppm impurity), claims DME and alternative cleaning reagents, and explicitly transfers the water-management principle from dehydrogenation to the reverse hydrogenation because both share the rate-limiting transition state. It does not mention ketonization or carboxylic acids. The group's current list ([LSAC publications](https://sites.google.com/view/iglesia-lsac/publications)) shows the program still expanding within dehydrogenation/hydrogenation: submitted 2026 papers on dehydrogenation–cyclization cascades on ZrO2-based LAB pairs, compositional tuning of Y–Zr oxides, and heterolytic H–H and H–OH activation at LAB pairs (theory), plus a 2025 pretreatment patent (US20250050322A1). Kondratenko's 2025 Account ([10.1021/accountsmr.4c00395](https://doi.org/10.1021/accountsmr.4c00395)) treats water as a poison within the Zr_cus model and does not offer a titration rule. Lercher/PNNL's water work is on anatase ketonization and finds water associating with carboxylates rather than competing for the Lewis site ([Lin 2023](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.2c08511)). Bell's closest result is partial irreversibility of water on site-isolated Zr/SiO2 ([10.1021/acscatal.9b05176](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.9b05176)). A 2026 reactive-MD study of hydroxylation equilibrium at the water/m-ZrO2(-111) interface exists ([PubMed 42434960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42434960/), search-level). **No publication by any of these groups states a quantitative water-titration or water-tolerance rule that spans ketonization and dehydrogenation on one material.** This is a search-limited negative made with the tools available today.

**Two substantive problems with the "one rule" premise (I).** First, the site pools probably differ: ketonization runs at near-saturation carboxylate coverage on Zr–O pairs generally (Wang and Iglesia 2017, abstract-level via the screen; full text not retrieved here), whereas the dehydrogenation count is a minority of ~0.55 nm⁻² among 8–12 nm⁻² pairs. Second, the water behaviour already differs qualitatively: in dehydrogenation at 723–773 K water is a stoichiometric titrant that needs chemical removal, while in ketonization at 623 K it is a weak reversible inhibitor (K_H2O·P ≈ 0.1 at 25 kPa). Either the ketonization sites are not the C–H-activation pairs, or carboxylates displace water from them. A cross-reaction program would therefore most likely *falsify* transfer at the outset. That is still informative: it would bound where DME-type chemical dehydroxylation and dehydrogenation feed-water specifications apply, and it would tell the ketonization process whether the LAB-pair concept has anything to offer. It is not the transferable rule the proposal hopes for.

**Selection effect (J).** The three screens converge on water titration partly because the local literature is dominated by Iglesia's ZrO2 papers; convergence inside one corpus is not independent evidence of a fertile territory.

**Verdict (J).** The dehydrogenation legs (ethylbenzene at ~773 K, propane at ~823 K) would restate Iglesia's own published and patented program and its active 2026 pipeline; running them as a new direction is not original. The ketonization leg plus a frozen cross-reaction prediction is original and cheap, and a failed prediction is a usable result. The program is therefore *not* substantial as proposed, but a bounded version is worth doing: on the styrene pilot's m-ZrO2 batch, (i) take the H2O/DME site census once; (ii) run the ketonization water-dependent-loss diagnostic of the liquid-phase screen on the same batch; (iii) before the ketonization runs, freeze a prediction of the ketonization water-inhibition constant, the fraction of rate recoverable by dry purge, and the effect of a DME pulse, derived from the dehydrogenation-side site count and water binding energy; (iv) promote to a program only if the prediction succeeds in two reactions (then the rule is transferable and the practical consequence, non-oxidative regeneration and a common feed-water specification, is real) or fails in a way that identifies a distinct ketonization site pool with its own water chemistry. Do not add the propane leg; Jaegers 2024 already supplies the 723 K water titration and the PDH site-identity add-on covers the reduction question at marginal cost.

1. **Bifunctional intimacy as inhibitor scavenging, applied to hydroisomerization/hydrocracking under real inhibitor loads.** Fischer and Iglesia's reinterpretation of "spillover" as scavenging of gaseous inhibitors (local note fischer2023) and Chen et al.'s bifunctional scavenging on Pt (local note chen2025) predict that metal–acid proximity matters through impurity removal rather than H transport, which would change catalyst layout and feed-pretreatment decisions in a deployed process; the intimacy-criterion literature (de Jong, Iglesia's own 2018 work) is the prior art to clear.
2. **Retained polymethylated aromatics and dry thermal recovery in methanol/DME conversion cycles.** Gounder's 2026 result that occluded polymethylated aromatics, not coke, deactivate intracrystalline MFI sites and can be removed below 673 K (local note ezenwa2026), together with the Cao–Iglesia–Gounder 2026 diffusion-enhanced secondary-reaction framework, poses a regeneration decision (dry thermal swing versus air burn) on a commercial process; MTO regeneration practice is the prior art to clear.
3. **Oxygen-carrier design for chemical-looping ethane ODH on Zr-based oxides.** Iglesia's ceria–zirconia lattice-diffusion and non-ideal-reduction kinetics (local notes hwang2024, hwang2025) predict usable oxygen per cycle far below thermodynamic capacity, while Kondratenko's 2025 LaZrOx looping result ([10.1038/s41467-025-64246-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-64246-9), as cited by the oxide-pair screen) defines the process; carrier composition and cycle length are the decisions, and neither the screens nor the priority table touch it.

## 5. Standards proposal: the front-regime derivation

**Arithmetic check (E for the limiting case).** With N_A active sites of per-site rate a and N_S titratable spectators mixed uniformly along a plug-flow bed, a strongly bound titrant forms a sharp front; after cumulative uptake U the covered fraction of the bed is U/(N_A + N_S), so the remaining active sites are N_A[1 − U/(N_A + N_S)] and, at differential conversion, rate(U) = a·N_A·[1 − U/(N_A + N_S)]. The derivation is correct: the curve is linear, the uptake intercept is N_A + N_S, and the slope is −a·N_A/(N_A + N_S). Two points sharpen it. (i) The front is a sufficient, not necessary, condition: the same line results whenever titrant coverage is uncorrelated with site reactivity (equal affinities with an equilibrated titrant give it too), so the discriminating property is *affinity selectivity*, and the screen's bed-mass and titrant-pressure invariance tests are the right checks. (ii) A straight line has only two parameters, rate(0) = a·N_A and the intercept; a and N_A are therefore not separately identifiable from any linear curve. Linearity is consistent with uniform activity but is not evidence of it. The result assumes 1:1 stoichiometry, no titrant uptake on non-titratable surfaces, and differential conversion; at integral conversion the same physics gives a nonlinear conversion-versus-uptake curve.

**Proposed addition to `research/standards.md`, under "Evidence" (not applied).**

> A rate-versus-titrant-uptake curve measured during reaction in a packed bed identifies the titratable total, not the working population. When the titrant binds strongly, or binds all titratable sites with similar affinity, the curve is linear regardless of how activity is distributed among those sites: its uptake intercept is the titratable total and its slope is the bed-average rate per titratable site. A straight line therefore cannot distinguish uniform activity from a mixture of active and inactive sites, and a per-site rate difference between two samples cannot be assigned to per-site chemistry unless the working fraction is shown to be unchanged. Report during-reaction titrations as "titratable total and bed-average rate per titratable site", state titrant stoichiometry and reversibility, and support any working-site claim with an affinity contrast, a bed-mass and titrant-pressure invariance check, or a calibration on mixtures of known spectator fraction. Curve shape carries information about site heterogeneity only when the titrant distributes by affinity and affinity is shown to correlate with activity.
