# MDA: challenge to a retained-carbon research program

**Source and decision update, 2026-09-16:** This is a historical screen of an existing idea, not an active campaign. The [decision brief](../decision-brief.md) controls current priorities. Access statements and source-request lists below describe the original review unless explicitly updated; current access is recorded in the [literature status](../literature-status.md). Availability of a full text does not mean that every claim or benchmark in it has been rechecked. No new idea or experimental campaign is started by this update.

Peng's C1-intermediate article and SI are now available; the original-PDF checks below replace the unresolved-identity and abstract-only boundary for that work. The short-H2-switch, Haben, Zhang, Gu, Wei and Zhu main texts are also retained and marked read. Hossain's external-Mo/BAS paper and Sun's long periodic-operation article remain unread; their quantitative claims remain provisional. This screen is not promoted.

Independent assessment, 2026-09-15. **Decision: do not promote a new retained-carbon/low-damage regeneration program on the evidence reviewed here.** Carbon participation is well established; its quantitative contribution to productive flux remains unresolved, but resolving that distinction has not yet been connected to a new, experimentally identifiable catalyst-design choice. Existing hydrogen regeneration is a much stronger comparator than a methane-only baseline.

## The consequential question that survives

**Can a substantial fraction of retained carbon be recovered as useful aromatics, with a positive full-cycle hydrogen balance, before it becomes inaccessible—rather than being returned mainly to methane or oxidized?**

This is a sharper practical question than whether carbon is a catalyst, a precursor, or a spectator. If recovery is predominantly methanation, faster carbon turnover can preserve catalyst activity but does not make carbon deposition a useful hydrogen-producing pathway. If selective aromatic recovery is possible, the reservoir could become a deliberately managed intermediate inventory, and regeneration would recover product as well as access. This connects Iglesia-style reaction-network and isotope accounting with Gounder's demonstrated concern for zeolite durability. It is a **speculative alternative worth a small screening experiment only**, not a recommendation for a substantial program.

## What the primary evidence actually establishes

### Carbon participation and Mo rate control are compatible

- Kosinov's pulse experiment used 5 mL methane pulses separated by 200 s in an Ar carrier at 700 °C. After induction with labeled methane, more than 70% of benzene molecules in the first unlabeled pulse contained at least one labeled carbon. This is a molecular incidence, not the fraction of benzene carbon supplied by the reservoir. Hydrogenating the reservoir restored benzene induction without restoring the initial CO-producing activation period. That is meaningful evidence for a functional carbon requirement; it should not be dismissed merely by proposing an unmeasured change in Mo. The experiment still does not quantify steady-flow flux through that reservoir. [Kosinov et al., DOI 10.1002/anie.201711098](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.201711098), local `kosinov2017-confined-carbon-mediating-dehydroaromatization-of/fulltext.md`, original artifact pp.3–5, read.
- The follow-up paper measured 20.3% labeled carbon atoms in the first benzene pulse and a nearly statistical isotopologue distribution. The authors explicitly allow equilibrium between formed benzene and surface carbon. The much smaller methane labeling argues against bulk methane recycling as the source of the aromatic label. It does not by itself distinguish a mandatory forward precursor from exchange between newly formed aromatics and the reservoir. The same paper shows why combustion temperature does not uniquely identify carbon chemistry: location, Mo-catalyzed oxidation, and restricted access affect the peaks. [Kosinov et al., DOI 10.1021/acscatal.8b02491](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.8b02491), local `kosinov2018-structure-and-evolution-of-confined/fulltext.md`, pp.4–7, read.
- Razdan and Bhan's “sole kinetically relevant” Mo conclusion concerns rates and selectivity in their measured regime. It permits acid-catalyzed equilibrated or tangential steps and does not preclude a carbon intermediate. Their paired study identifies a change from methane-activation control at low conversion to downstream C2 chemistry after methane/ethane/ethylene approach equilibrium. The continuous-flow experiments therefore cannot simply be set against strongly transient induction pulses as incompatible mechanisms. [DOI 10.1016/j.jcat.2020.06.035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2020.06.035), local `razdan2020-carbidic-mo-is-the-sole/paper.md`, pp.15–27; [DOI 10.1016/j.jcat.2019.11.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2019.11.004), local `razdan2020-influence-of-ethylene-and-acetylene/paper.md`, pp.20–28. These local read notes were inspected here; the parent independently checked the first full text.

Consequently, **“prove carbon matters despite the sole-Mo paper” is not an adequate novelty claim**. Reciprocal methane/benzene isotope switches could constrain exchange, but fitting a reservoir residence time is not equivalent to measuring its degree of rate control. A causal intervention would have to alter the relevant carbon independently of Mo reactivity and pore access. Neither hydrogen removal nor oxidation has yet supplied that independence.

### Recent work does not close this gap, but limits the opportunity

Hiennadi et al. find a common initial benzene-rate regime above a corrected Mo-RI threshold and no measurable suppression by excess Mo-RII in that regime. Mo-RI is a precursor reducibility descriptor, not a direct census of working carbidic sites or proof that every carbon reservoir is equivalent. Extending that descriptor across regeneration is useful, but is a separate characterization program. [DOI 10.1021/acscatal.6c02024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.6c02024), local `hiennadi2026-quantifying-the-impact-of-metal/paper.md`, pp.8–12, read note inspected.

Santiago-Colón et al. already demonstrate a durable small-pore route with repeated recovery of initial rates. CHA also retains more carbon and exports less aromatic product; hydrogen cofeed decreases cumulative coke selectivity while decreasing total turnovers. Thus equal initial H2 or C2 rates and structural regeneration do not establish attractive full-cycle carbon efficiency. Deliberately recovering retained carbon would address a remaining limitation, but simply shrinking CHA or adding H2 is already in the paper. [DOI 10.1021/acscatal.5c04775](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5c04775), local `colon2025-hydrothermally-stable-and-regenerable-molybdenum/fulltext.md`, §§3.4–3.5 and conclusions, relevant source passages read.

### Peng2025: resolved source and limits of the new controls

The [main article](../../literature/papers/peng2025-elucidating-the-formation-mechanism-and/original.pdf) and [SI](../../literature/papers/peng2025-supporting-information-for-elucidating-the/original.pdf), DOI `10.1021/acscatal.5c01349`, now provide explicit C1-intermediate evidence. The SI combines photon-energy assignments, a catalyst-free control and temporal comparisons to support the CH2 assignment. These observations establish a detected species under the measurement conditions; they do not measure the fraction of steady aromatic production that must pass through it, or show that manipulating retained carbon improves cycle output.

The reactor comparison is material: the quartz experiment is at 2 Torr, whereas the SiC microreactor feed is about 400 Torr; both differ from the 1 bar GC performance tests (SI pp.2–6). At nominally similar methane conversion, the low-Mo/high-Mo comparison uses **0.5 wt% Mo at 800 °C versus 2 wt% Mo at 700 °C** (original SI pp.21–22, Figures S15–S16). It does not independently vary site density at fixed temperature, and therefore does not by itself exclude temperature-dependent radical formation or establish an adjacent-Mo elementary requirement. The reported changing Mo inventory further prevents treating the initial loading as a constant census of active sites. These controls strengthen the evidence for the species assignment while leaving the flux and causal-intervention questions distinct. The nonselection decision and full-cycle carbon/H2 gates are unchanged.

## A hard bound before a mechanism campaign

For carbon deposited with average composition CHx:

`CH4 → CHx(retained) + (4 − x)/2 H2`

Complete regeneration to methane consumes exactly the hydrogen generated by that carbon deposition:

`CHx(retained) + (4 − x)/2 H2 → CH4`.

Therefore, the deposition–methanation loop contributes **zero net H2** before any losses or compression. Recovering that carbon as benzene instead requires `(1 − x)/2 H2` per carbon for x < 1 and leaves the expected overall `1.5 mol H2/mol C` of methane-to-benzene chemistry. These are elemental balances, not predictions of selectivity or thermodynamic feasibility. Coke combustion can supply heat but consumes carbon feedstock and must enter the carbon/energy balance.

A cycle must report net H2 leaving its system boundary, all feed and recovered methane, useful carbon products, retained carbon change, and elapsed time including purge/regeneration. H2 generated during methane exposure alone is insufficient. A coke-derived aromatic pulse is also insufficient if its advantage is offset by downtime or the lost activity of the subsequently depleted reservoir.

## Smallest informative experiment, if this alternative is pursued

**Question:** Is there enough selective recovery of nonvolatile retained carbon as BTX to justify any new mechanism campaign?

Start with one well-characterized, low-loading Mo/MFI batch at 700 °C. Use MFI first because export of aromatics through CHA windows would confound a negative recovery result. Prepare matched samples at the end of induction and after a measured decline in activity. Generate labeled retained carbon using 13CH4, then purge at the reaction temperature until gas-phase aromatic tails are quantitatively bounded. Compare short dilute-H2 treatments with identical inert treatments. Use two H2 chemical potentials chosen from existing regeneration conditions; this is a screening matrix, not a large temperature/pressure optimization.

Measure time-resolved CH4, C2, BTX, heavier condensables, H2 uptake, and retained-carbon inventory before/after on sacrificial matched aliquots. Obtain isotope-resolved gas measurements and end-of-test combustion isotope balances. Independently measure the aromatic washout tail using the same apparatus and temperature; H2-induced displacement of adsorbed benzene must not be credited as cracking a polyaromatic reservoir. Close the carbon balance and distinguish uncertainty in uncondensed products from an inferred disappearing solid inventory. Heated sampling lines and calibrated condensate recovery are necessary.

The first campaign does not need a fitted hydrocarbon-pool mechanism, a synchrotron campaign, or a new Mo titration. If it shows selective aromatic recovery, repeat the decisive condition on independent catalyst aliquots and determine whether the next methane period recovers its useful productivity. Only then examine whether the treatment changed Mo or merely opened pores. A negative test is bounded by the screened condition range; it does not disprove all possible hydrocracking catalysts.

**Predeclared gate:** choose a practically consequential required increment in full-cycle BTX productivity before testing. Set it from the reproducibility of the reference cycle and the added purge time, regeneration time, hydrogen handling, and equipment burden; record that rationale instead of adopting a default percentage. Use the measured removable inventory and recovered-BTX fraction to calculate an optimistic upper bound that initially ignores all downtime. If even that bound falls below the chosen increment, stop. If recovery is predominantly methane, or BTX is explained by the volatile or adsorbed-benzene tail, stop this carbon-harvesting branch. A labeled BTX signal establishes recovery from the nonvolatile reservoir only after those alternative inventories are bounded. Do not rescue the branch by adding a promoter or an extensive spectroscopy program. A positive result advances only if net H2 export and productivity remain favorable after downtime and methane re-induction are included.

To claim a practical advance subsequently requires comparison with the published short-H2-switch protocol, optimized conventional regeneration, and pressure-managed operation at matched feed throughput and full-cycle time. Material durability must be evaluated over enough cycles to expose framework/Mo loss. Ten cycles would be an exploratory check, not a substitute for the long-duration prior art.

## Separate confidence judgments

- **Carbon participates in MDA:** high confidence within the studied materials and conditions. The remaining controversy concerns the path, fraction, and kinetic importance, not the existence of exchange.
- **An independently controllable pool accelerates useful flux and should deliberately be preserved:** plausible, but currently underdetermined. The needed causal intervention is missing.
- **The proposed screening experiment is feasible and informative:** reasonably strong for bounding recoverable aromatic carbon with a suitable isotope-capable transient reactor. It cannot by itself assign the mechanism or distinguish every adsorbed aromatic from an extended pool.
- **Meaningful selective carbon harvesting and process improvement:** low confidence. Published regeneration emphasizes methanation; confined carbon is difficult to access, and hydrogen suppresses net aromatization. A selective recovery window has not been established by the sources read.

## Final recommendation and source handoff

Do not present “active carbon versus spectator coke” as a new flagship opportunity. Do not infer that identifying isotope exchange will deliver better regeneration. Retain the carbon-to-BTX screen only if existing experimental capability makes it inexpensive and full reading of the competing work leaves meaningful room for a contribution. Otherwise reject this branch in favor of a question with a better intervention-to-value connection.
