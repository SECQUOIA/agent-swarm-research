## Decision

**Do not promote a peroxide-generator/zeolite program above bounded Ag.** Direct tandem operation, electrolyte adaptation, peroxide interception near its source, and suppression of catalyst-mediated peroxide destruction all have strong prior art. The inspected evidence does not establish a large remaining peroxide loss on the best relevant coupled reference that a new zeolite intervention would remedy. This is a negative selection result, not a claim that every coupled process is efficient or that all Ti-site chemistry is understood.

The closest defensible materials question is whether an identified TS-1 modification changes **primary oxidant partition into epoxide**, **subsequent epoxide survival**, or **retention of activity during repeated use**. Those effects can lead to different catalyst and operating choices. However, current papers already separate some of them, and no specific unresolved component has yet been connected to a consequential practical penalty. An unexplained site assignment is insufficient to justify the program.

## What the strongest inspected sources already establish

### 1. The starting paper already decouples incompatible environments

The verified title of DOI `10.1002/aic.18930` is **A tandem electro-thermocatalysis platform for practical hydrogen peroxide-mediated oxygenation reactions at high rates**, Chen et al., AIChE Journal, first published 11 June 2025. Its abstract describes solid-electrolyte peroxide electrosynthesis, spatially separated nonaqueous zeolite catalysis, and a microfluidic packed bed. The reported 300 mA cm−2 and 7.07 mmol cm−3 h−1 headline belongs to sulfoxide synthesis, not a demonstrated epoxidation operating point. TS-1 olefin epoxidation and oxime synthesis are additional demonstrations. Full article/SI inspection remains pending here; the abstract cannot support a detailed peroxide-loss or lifetime claim. [Primary abstract](https://aiche.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aic.18930).

### 2. A direct PO comparator reports little unexplained oxidant loss, with an important denominator caveat

Chandra et al. combine anodic peroxide generation with TS-1 immobilized on a gas-diffusion layer. At 10 vol% acetonitrile in 2 M bicarbonate/carbonate, they report 62 μmol PO cm−2 h−1, >97% PO selectivity, approximately 60% peroxide Faradaic efficiency, and a maximum 58% electron efficiency for **PO plus propylene glycol**. Their 98% peroxide-utilization statement compares **PO + glycol + residual peroxide** with peroxide generated in a separate no-TS-1 test. It is not a direct target-product yield or closed coupled oxygen balance. The reported 2.4 V versus RHE is an electrode potential, not cell voltage.

Adding acetonitrile suppresses observed PO hydrolysis; excessive acetonitrile reduces peroxide generation. Repeated runs replace the electrolyte. Activity eventually falls and is largely restored by calcination. The article therefore supplies real solvent/product-survival and activity-loss observations, but no demonstrated large internal-peroxide-destruction burden at its selected operating point. [Primary full HTML, Results and Discussion, Figures 2–4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/anie.202521921). SI not inspected in this screen.

### 3. Alkaline tolerance and crossover management already have material solutions

Kim et al. modify TS-1 with tetrapropylammonium bromide/ethanolamine and attribute its alkaline activity to dinuclear Ti sites. They observe substantial glycol formation with modified TS-1 in deionized water; alkali-containing media improve PO survival. Their integrated formaldehyde-oxidation/O2-reduction system produces 1657 μmol PO cm−2 over 24 h. Formaldehyde crossover consumes peroxide and poisons the zeolite; a Na-A filter addresses it. These are measured incompatibilities and tested interventions, not hypothetical opportunities.

Zero applied bias is paid for by chemical feed conversion: formaldehyde, formate/formic acid and hydrogen belong in the resource and carbon boundary. The demonstrated duration does not establish a long catalyst or filter service life. [Primary full HTML, Results and Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-63828-x). SI not inspected here. Neither generic salt tolerance, a Ti-nuclearity modification, nor a crossover guard is an original program proposal.

### 4. Local peroxide interception and ambiguous utilization were explicitly studied in 2023

Zhang et al.'s solid-electrolyte/TS-1 reactor targets ethylene glycol. It compares interfacial integration with separate tandem reactors and reports approximately threefold greater glycol production and twofold greater inferred peroxide utilization. Its approximately 98.7% overall Faradaic efficiency includes residual peroxide; maximum glycol Faradaic efficiency is approximately 63%. The paper explicitly obtains different peroxide-utilization estimates from different generator references, including an unphysical value above 100%, and discusses activation/inventory effects. Adding TS-1 also raises the measured cell-voltage requirement.

This is strong prior art against presenting either local peroxide interception or improved accounting as the research contribution. It also shows why a high combined efficiency does not establish high useful-product throughput per charge. [Original PDF, pp. 3–7](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/blogs.rice.edu/dist/c/9358/files/2023/07/1-s2.0-S2542435123002726-main.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2023.06.022). The extracted PDF renders some μmol symbols incorrectly; no absolute production rate from that extraction is used here.

### 5. Internal peroxide destruction and solvent control are established catalyst questions

Hammond and Tarantino suppress peroxide decomposition through post-synthetic TS-1 modification. Crucially, their dramatic substrate-free comparison is not evidence of equally large loss during epoxidation: at 50 °C neither catalyst loses more than 6% peroxide in the first 90 min even without olefin, and olefin further suppresses decomposition. The proposed connection to altered Ti speciation remains qualified. [Original PDF, pp. 5–6 and 10](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/84373/1/catalysts-05-02309.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/catal5042309).

The 2023 study *Engineering intraporous solvent environments* already compares hydrophilic/hydrophobic Ti-BEA, three aqueous-organic solvents, epoxidation and peroxide destruction under controlled kinetic conditions. Water-rich mixtures increase epoxidation and decrease decomposition in its tested domain; generic hydrophobicity is therefore not a universally correct intervention. Transfer from Ti-BEA/1-hexene to TS-1/propylene/electrolyte is unproven, but measuring that transfer alone would be incremental without a demonstrated useful penalty. This screen read indexed primary-text methods/results, not an acquired original PDF. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10034100/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1039/D2SC06473A).

### 6. The practical benchmark is larger than a coupled laboratory demonstration

For commodity PO, compare against operating HPPO technology. The 2016 hollow-TS-1 pilot report uses 30 wt% peroxide, reports more than 6000 h operation, 96–99% PO selectivity, and 99.97% final PO purity. These abstract-level claims are an important performance floor, not a matched independent trial against any electrochemical system. Full-paper acquisition is now complete; see the updated primary basis below. [Primary abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1385894716301735); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2016.02.072).

For a mechanistic direct-use comparison, Chandra's PO system is the closest inspected reference; Kim's alkaline system tests a different chemical-reductant boundary; Zhang's glycol system is the stronger local-integration/accounting comparator. Their area-normalized rates and product objectives are not interchangeable, and this screen does not rank them as universal records.

### Updated primary basis for the HPPO comparator

The [Lin2016 original](../../literature/papers/lin2016-green-and-efficient-epoxidation-of/original.pdf) is now retained. Its Figure 7 and conclusions (PDF p.6) report a 1.0 kt/year pilot, a time course extending beyond 6000 h, 96–99% peroxide conversion, 96–99% PO selectivity and purification to 99.97% PO. The reported rate of about 31–32 mmol gcat−1 h−1 is **peroxide conversion per catalyst mass**; despite the paper's “TOF” label it is not a measured turnover rate per Ti active site. The paper gives optimized operating ranges of 40–50 °C, 0.5–2 MPa, methanol/peroxide molar ratio 5–15 and peroxide WHSV at least 1.2 h−1. A range of tested/optimized conditions must not be presented as one fully specified 6000 h operating point. These are source-reported pilot results, not an independently audited comparison of total energy and resources against the electrochemical route. The update replaces the historical acquisition gap without claiming a newly demonstrated advantage for either route.

## Smallest experiment worth considering, only after a real penalty is identified

No new catalyst library is justified. The minimum reopening gate is a **target-product and peroxide balance on one reproduced, relevant reference at its actual coupled feed composition**. Measure the delivered liquid, charge, product collection, retained inventories, residual peroxide, relevant oxygen-containing carbon products and gas flows over a common interval. Use measured full-cell energy. Include solvent and chemical-reductant carbon separately; a high PO/(PO + glycol) ratio is not complete carbon recovery. A generator-only reference is useful but cannot prove its unchanged behavior during coupling.

Only if this exposes a consequential catalyst-associated loss should the closest materials question become an experiment. Compare one established modified TS-1 with its processing-matched parent at the actual liquid composition. Use low-contact-time formation measurements and a separate PO-survival experiment at relevant peroxide/substrate conditions to distinguish primary formation from subsequent loss. A tracer PO cofeed is warranted only if ordinary balances cannot distinguish inhibition from destruction and that distinction changes the choice. Include catalyst-free/blank-support controls and recover retained PO before calling it destroyed. For an observed time-dependent decline, a solvent/feed reset with a matched elapsed-time control tests recoverable occupation before structural characterization.

This comparison changes a useful decision only if it selects a material or bounded recovery policy that improves integrated net PO output per charge and recurring feed use. Merely improving the balance, reproducing known hydrolysis suppression, or observing a different Ti spectrum fails the contribution gate. If the dominant loss lies in peroxide generation, the proposed zeolite program is the wrong intervention. If no material penalty survives measurement uncertainty, stop.

The [accounting note](../calculations/peroxide-coupling-accounting.md) supplies the common boundary. Its stoichiometry and revised distinction between gross peroxide formation, net PO and generator losses were independently checked for this screen. Dilute liquid throughput is not automatically evaporation duty; actual recycle, purge and product recovery determine the burden. No process saving is inferred here.

## Comparison with bounded Ag

| Criterion | Peroxide tandem screen | Current bounded Ag |
|---|---|---|
| Importance | PO and peroxide efficiency matter; a remaining zeolite-controlled burden is not established on the best relevant reference. | EO versus combustion has a direct reaction-level resource consequence. |
| Originality | Generic integration, site modification, electrolyte adaptation and solvent control are heavily preempted. No consequential new intervention identified. | Also historically preempted in generic form; narrower predictive Ni-dependent retention question remains. |
| Feasibility | Basic liquid kinetics may be accessible; decisive coupled accounting, gas/liquid collection and generator-reference validation add substantial work. | Demanding promoted-catalyst preparation and controlled chloride histories; established bounded comparison. |
| Hypothesis confidence | Moderate that site/solvent changes alter rates and survival, because already demonstrated; low that an unaddressed change fixes a consequential current loss. | Plausible retention/conditioning alternatives; actual useful difference still a gate. |
| Practical confidence | Low. Neither net advantage nor a required new materials solution is established. | Also low evidence of improvement, but a clearer target and narrower path to a useful decision. |

The peroxide direction offers a natural connection to confinement/solvation interests and mechanistic kinetics. Intellectual fit alone does not overcome its weak current selection case. Retain Ag's conditional priority; do not weaken its own historical-prior-art or practical-value gates.
