# Light-olefin oligomerization: can selective escape improve useful output?

**Source and decision update, 2026-09-16:** This is a historical screen of an existing idea, not an active campaign. The [decision brief](../decision-brief.md) controls current priorities. Access statements and source-request lists below describe the original review unless explicitly updated; current access is recorded in the [literature status](../literature-status.md). Availability of a full text does not mean that every claim or benchmark in it has been rechecked. No new idea or experimental campaign is started by this update.

The formerly abstract-only Guo directed-siting, Tabernilla water/coke, Li tandem and Li steam-modification main texts are now retained and marked read. Access alone does not validate their extrapolation to useful full-cycle fuel output. The newly supplied Nozik and Koninckx SI are incorporated in the [benchmark review](../reviews/oligomerization-benchmark-accounting.md#newly-available-mechanism-comparators); their kinetic controls and model assumptions must be credited. The narrow external-activity diagnostic remains optional and nonselected.

**Assessment date: 2026-09-15. Decision: do not promote a new catalyst-development campaign.** There is a consequential tradeoff between useful chain growth, branching, product escape and persistent deposits. However, most proposed solutions—Al zoning, external-site passivation, occupied-pore transport control, mild removal of retained oligomers, water addition and tandem oligomerization—already have direct precedents. The evidence supports a narrow diagnostic question about the *minimum external acid activity needed to avoid a deposit-induced surface barrier*. It does not yet support a claim that answering this question would improve integrated liquid-fuel output over strong alternatives.

This is an independent value challenge, not a new selected program. No experiments have been performed or sample access confirmed.

## Practical question and specific hypothesis

For ethanol-derived ethylene, ethylene disappearance is an inadequate objective. The desired outcome is recovered, suitably branched distillate after the necessary hydrogenation and separation, maintained across operation and regeneration. Escaping C4–C6 oligomers are useful intermediates, but their escape can reduce single-pass liquid production; retaining them can accelerate ethylene incorporation while also increasing cracking, hydrogen transfer and deposits. Extensive branching can improve cold flow yet introduce other product constraints. Neither maximum branching nor maximum C9+ selectivity is a universal target.

**Strongest specific hypothesis worth preserving, but not yet promoting:** at fixed internal MFI acid inventory and working transport properties, a small accessible external acid population prevents formation of a refractory surface deposit, while extensive external activity increases secondary conversion of escaping oligomers. An intermediate external activity could therefore maximize integrated output of a predeclared branched distillate cut. This predicts a nonmonotonic output-versus-external-activity relationship, accompanied by different deposit location and purge recoverability.

The hypothesis does **not** specify that external sites crack or clean deposit precursors: that chemical step is not established. The observed shell effect may instead arise from altered adsorption, defects, carbon nucleation or morphology. A nonmonotonic yield alone would not identify the mechanism. Transferring a propene observation at 503 K to ethylene at 623 K is also an untested inference.

Gounder's site-distribution synthesis and titration methods provide the material contrast. Iglesia's reaction-network kinetics and occupied-pore transport framework provide the test of whether the contrast represents local chemistry, residence time or changing access. Combining these capabilities is appropriate, but is already characteristic of the published work below.

The MFI toluene-methylation placement result ([Ezenwa 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.4c00373)) establishes that T-site environments can change transition-state selectivity. It does not establish a site placement that preserves useful olefin branching while rejecting cyclization or coke formation. Likewise, the favorable Si-rich rim in methanol-to-hydrocarbons ([Le 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41929-023-00927-2)) cannot override the unfavorable oligomerization shell result.

## Direct competing work and negative search findings

Several recent original papers further reduce the novelty space. The following are **publisher abstract/metadata evidence only in this assessment**, pending the sole literature agent's full-text work. Their reported mechanisms and quantitative advantages are not independently validated here.

- **September 2026, directed Al siting:** [10.1016/j.jcat.2026.116995](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2026.116995) already combines Al redistribution toward MFI intersections, external passivation and hierarchical transport to improve propene-derived C12 selectivity and durability. The abstract assigns the dominant benefit to transport and treats the extra siting contribution as tentative. A new claim to “decouple stability and selectivity through siting” would need to distinguish itself from this exact competitor.
- **Water operating state:** [10.1016/j.fuel.2025.137698](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fuel.2025.137698), published in Fuel 2026, studies water, secondary reactions and coke in low-pressure ethylene oligomerization. “Retain some water to limit coke” is already directly studied.
- **Coke identity:** [10.1021/acs.energyfuels.5c01009](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.5c01009) explicitly distinguishes soft retained oligomers and harder deposits during ethylene oligomerization.
- **Tandem growth:** [10.1021/acs.iecr.4c04755](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.4c04755) compares FER/MFI physical mixtures and dual beds. Separating initial ethylene conversion from higher-olefin growth is established; the abstract reports a stability advantage for the closer physical mixture.
- **Practical modified-MFI reference:** [10.1039/D5RA01413A](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5RA01413A) reports 78.1% liquid yield for steam-modified MFI. This is a candidate experimental benchmark, not a directly comparable cycle yield or established fuel specification.
- **Historical surface selectivation:** Arroyo 2025, p. 2, cites Mobil patent US5026933 for bulky-base cofeed changing oligomer size and branching, and older surface dealumination and zoning studies with opposite durability trends. The old patent's original examples remain unread here. The field already recognizes that external acidity can be beneficial or harmful depending on conditions; discovering an optimum alone would be incremental.

No source located establishes the specific intermediate-external-activity hypothesis above *together with* improved recovered branched-distillate output across regeneration. That absence does not establish novelty or practical headroom. It may simply reflect that an industrially relevant optimization uses different catalysts and operating conditions.

## Fair practical benchmark

Fuchs, Arnold and Sauer, [Fuel 2025, 133680](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fuel.2024.133680), supply a much stronger comparator than fresh differential MFI conversion: mixed light olefins over amorphous silica–alumina, optionally Ni-modified, with a 222 h experiment and collected liquid. The original artifact is openly available from [KIT](https://publikationen.bibliothek.kit.edu/1000177148/156087948). Original text checked here at pp. 9–11 agrees with the root agent's audit: initial kerosene-fraction selectivity exceeds 77 wt%, ends at 47 wt%, and averages 63 wt% of collected liquid. The 222 h history includes regeneration after 102 h; it is not an uninterrupted stable run. The collected, unhydrogenated kerosene fraction has a −38.2 °C freezing point and 304.1 °C final boiling point, outside the respective comparison limits quoted in that paper. Narrower fractionation and subsequent upgrading remain necessary. Thus this is a substantial, imperfect process benchmark rather than qualified SAF.

LanzaJet's [own process description](https://www.lanzajet.com/news-insights/next-generation-saf-by-lanzajet-and-iag) includes dehydration, oligomerization, hydrogenation and fractionation. Its maximum-jet product slate is about 90% SAF/10% renewable diesel by weight. That is a product split, **not** 90% carbon yield from ethanol; it reveals neither a catalyst composition nor a cycle-normalized productivity. A new MFI result must therefore show an advantage in measured output, cleanup duty or feed tolerance, not claim novelty for making a jet-range carbon distribution.

## Smallest informative program, if existing samples justify one

**Gate before new synthesis:** inspect the new 2026 directed-siting paper and obtain a working-condition benchmark from the existing homogeneous MFI. If no meaningful desired-cut output is measurable, or the competitor already tests the same external-activity continuum, stop this branch.

If suitable samples already exist, compare three verified external-acid activities—unpassivated, intermediate and nearly passivated—at one ethylene operating condition. They must have sufficiently similar internal acid inventory, geometry and initial uptake for the contrast to be interpretable. Use external probe rates plus framework/total acid characterization; bulk Si/Al alone is inadequate. A bulky titrant can be a reversible diagnostic, but changes in adsorption, blockage, residence time and titrant decomposition prevent treating its dose as a clean material variable without controls. Do not launch a broad shell-synthesis campaign simply to produce this comparison.

Measure time-resolved gas and recovered liquid, branched versus linear products within the selected cut, retained carbon, and recovery after the same inert and oxidative treatment. Establish bed thermal and external-transfer independence by dilution/flow checks; use the same feed throughput and catalyst mass for the practical comparison, with a separate matched-conversion contrast for mechanistic selectivity. Repeat only the decisive contrast with independently prepared beds.

The discriminating observation is **an intermediate state that outperforms both endpoints in integrated desired output, avoids the additional purge-resistant surface barrier, and retains the desired branching distribution**. A better fresh selectivity with lower mass productivity does not pass. A difference in total coke mass alone does not locate the transport barrier. Selectivity trajectories must distinguish reduced formation of branched molecules from slower escape or preferential secondary consumption.

Use a simple model with primary growth, secondary consumption and a changing transport resistance only if needed to predict one held-out time or operating condition. Do not fit an unconstrained network to outlet carbon numbers and interpret fitted constants as unique elementary chemistry.

### Accounting and decision rule

Report both carbon yield and mass productivity:

\[
Y_{C,D}=\frac{\text{carbon recovered in the accepted product cut}}{\text{fresh-feed carbon}},\qquad
P_D=\frac{\text{accepted product mass}}{m_{cat}(t_{reaction}+t_{purge}+t_{regeneration}+t_{transition})}.
\]

Count recycle once at the fresh-feed boundary. Report inventory accumulated in the catalyst and collection lines, gaseous and heavy coproducts, burned carbon, and losses during fractionation. Quantify hydrogen consumed in upgrading; hydrogenation adds mass, so carbon yield and mass productivity answer different questions. For mixed feeds, net C4 disappearance is not incorporation because ethylene can also produce C4. Feed-resolved attribution requires additional information and should not be invented from net conversion.

The accepted cut initially means a declared composition/boiling-range target, not certified fuel. Before claiming a practical fuel advantage, hydrogenate representative samples and measure relevant cold-flow, distillation and composition properties on the recovered cut; account for narrowed-cut yield losses and any blend requirement. Scale-up assessment must also include pressure/compression, purge utilities, heat removal, pellet transport, regeneration duty and catalyst recovery over repeated cycles.

Before a test, predeclare a minimum useful increase in cycle mass productivity at matched accepted-cut quality, with carbon yield no worse within uncertainty. Set that gate from reference-cycle reproducibility, the added preparation and regeneration burden, and a stated practical output requirement; a generic 10% threshold has no established basis here. Stop if the benefit disappears after matching conversion, adding downtime, recovering held-up liquid or enforcing the product cut. No numerical commercial advantage is presently justified.

## Separate confidence judgments

- **Established tradeoff and relevance: high.** Reaction-state retention, branching-sensitive transport and deposit-mediated barriers are directly observed, with substantial overlap with both groups' expertise.
- **Specific intermediate-external-activity mechanism: low.** The published endpoint comparison motivates it, but does not identify how exterior acid suppresses deposits or show the effect for ethylene under the proposed conditions.
- **Experimental informativeness: moderate, conditional on existing matched materials.** A cycle-output comparison is feasible with ordinary catalytic and product-analysis equipment; assigning deposit location and matching working transport is harder. The absence of access to suitable samples can make this an expensive synthesis campaign.
- **Remaining originality: low for generic zoning, water or purge proposals; unresolved for the narrow coupled output criterion.** Fresh September 2026 work is particularly close and must be read before promotion.
- **Chance of meaningful practical improvement: unestablished.** Strong mixed-olefin and industrial routes exist, while the proposed contrast has no measured useful-product advantage.

**Final selection:** reject a substantial new program on current evidence. Preserve the limited external-activity diagnostic as an optional use of existing materials, contingent on the new prior-art review and a credible desired-output baseline. Do not convert an unanswered mechanistic detail into a catalyst-development recommendation without that headroom.
