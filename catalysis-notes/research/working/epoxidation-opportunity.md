## Historical decision — superseded as described above

**Develop a conditional program, beginning with a bounded Ni × Re compatibility pilot.** The subsequent [portfolio review](../reviews/portfolio-second-value-review.md) selects it as the current priority; the [developed program](../programs/ag-selective-oxygen-use.md) consolidates that decision. A [fresh value review](../reviews/epoxidation-value-review.md) found a credible route to substantial mechanistic knowledge, while preserving the low evidentiary support for practical improvement. The question is whether Ni-conditioned oxygen states preserve, improve or impair the useful Cs/Re/Cl response after each formulation is appropriately conditioned. Ni promotion and Ni with Cs/Re/Cl are already disclosed; those facts constrain novelty of the combination but do not answer the mechanistic question.

The additional knowledge sought is narrower than combining promoters: **how does Ni change sustained selective oxygen use when the Re-dependent, chloride-promoted state is present, after separately adjusting chlorine and distinguishing primary from secondary oxidation?** A reproducible incompatibility could change catalyst choice; a substantial additive benefit could also justify development. A statistical interaction alone is neither required nor sufficient evidence of shared oxygen. Stop if neither a useful performance difference nor a consequential mechanistic distinction can be resolved.

The [oxygen-return calculation](../calculations/epoxidation-oxygen-return.md) provides one specific mechanistic fork. Recombination of leftover oxygen into O2 can, in a restricted cycle, mimic the net balance of direct second-oxygen transfer to EO. A quantitatively validated bound on that return flux would discriminate these alternatives more strongly than selectivity or an oxygen spectrum alone. This is established mechanistic territory applied to a specific unresolved working state, not a newly invented reaction.

### Why the obvious alternatives were rejected

- **History-dependent chlorine optimization:** Hwang already follows reversible promoted-state loss and recovery. Chen/Harris/Bhan studies quantify deposition/removal and product effects, and the 2026 Cs/co-promoter paper is particularly close prior art. The existing topic's proposed broad working-state campaign also covers this territory.
- **Water or steam conditioning:** water dependence of industrial chlorine response is already discussed in DOI [10.1021/acscatal.4c01764](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.4c01764). The patent [US20070225511A1](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20070225511) specifically discloses oxygen/steam activation of Ag/Re/alkali catalysts. A new water-pulse protocol needs a demonstrated unresolved failure mode; none was established here.
- **Better promoter placement by loading sequence:** DOI [10.1007/s10562-017-2211-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10562-017-2211-5) directly compares Re loading sequences. Hwang already explains promoter placement/retention and cites earlier successful deposition routes. These papers constrain novelty even where only the older paper's abstract was accessible.
- **Simply transfer Ni to higher pressure or optimize particle size:** the Science manuscript explicitly identifies higher-pressure and scalable-synthesis work as ongoing; the associated patent discusses particle-size/coverage optimization. This is an important validation task but not an original intervention established by this search.

## Conditional hypothesis and alternatives

**Hypothesis, not established:** the Ni-conditioned surface changes the accessibility or reaction fate of oxygen relevant to the Cs/Re/Cl response. It could suppress combustion while preserving selective transfer, or impair a useful Re-dependent contribution. A comparatively inert, stationary oxygen population can change rates indefinitely without continuously storing another oxygen atom per turnover. Neither the hypothesis nor the published spectator-oxygen interpretation requires a perpetual oxygen sink. A lower-Re formulation might preserve useful output, but that practical outcome remains unvalidated.

Plausible alternatives:

1. **Complementary functions:** Ni mainly suppresses combustion while Re retains access to a distinct oxygen pool. Benefits remain additive after chlorine optimization.
2. **Chlorine mediation:** Ni changes chlorine deposition/removal. An apparent Ni–Re interaction vanishes after conditioning and re-optimizing chlorine.
3. **Ordinary blocking or morphology:** Ni changes exposed Ag area, Ag size, promoter distribution, or support EO conversion. EO and CO2 rates change without identifying oxygen transfer between Ni and Re.

A rate interaction alone cannot identify an O atom moving from one named site to another. The first experiment can test the catalyst-design consequence. A mechanistic oxygen-transfer claim would require a subsequent isotope experiment with independent constraints on oxygen exchange, water, carbonate, and surface inventory; it should not be promised from one O 1s peak or one transient.

## Smallest discriminating experiment

### Gate before new synthesis

Use an existing, reproducible supported-Ag synthesis that gives the Re-dependent chloride response and a proven dilute-Ni synthesis. Establish that adding Ni does not erase the ability to reproduce the reference morphology and promoted state. If the available laboratory cannot reproduce both parent behaviors, stop: a broad four-component synthesis search is not the proposed small experiment. This project best fits Iglesia's expertise in rate/selectivity decomposition and catalytic cycles; Gounder's relevant contribution would be quantitative control of site populations and support-mediated secondary reactions, not an assumed zeolite advantage.

### Four formulations, one selected Ni loading

Prepare a two-by-two Ni × Re comparison with Cs fixed:

| | No added Re | Reference Re loading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No added Ni | Cs–Ag | Cs/Re–Ag |
| Dilute Ni | Ni/Cs–Ag | Ni/Cs/Re–Ag |

Use the same support batch, Ag inventory, particle-size distribution, and deposition/thermal history. Apply sham deposition to Ni-free samples. Define Ni loading against measured Ag size and exposed-area estimates; the published optimum on approximately 75 nm particles is not automatically transferable. Verify bulk metal contents and the retained working particle-size distribution. Do not label these matched merely because nominal weights agree. If incorporating Ni alters Re/Cs placement beyond the available measurements' ability to distinguish it, report a formulation interaction and withhold the oxygen-pool assignment.

1. At one pressure/temperature already demonstrated for the promoted reference, map each sample over three bracketed chlorine levels at fixed ethylene, O2, ethane, and CO2 partial pressures. Extend the bracket only if it misses the useful operating region. Define the objective as EO output at a specified selectivity or selectivity at matched useful output; maximizing selectivity at negligible production is not a useful optimum. Match feed water and measure outlet water; a nominally dry inlet does not eliminate product water.
2. Require stable product rates and revisit a reference point from both chlorine directions. Given the published 10–50 h relaxation, a two-hour hold is insufficient evidence of steady state. This is four formulations and a small operating matrix, but it is a multiweek campaign.
3. At the sample-specific chlorine optimum, vary residence time at fixed temperature to distinguish near-zero-conversion primary selectivity from secondary EO loss. Run a small EO cofeed perturbation in the reacting mixture; separate the additional EO consumed from the EO formed. Include a support blank and verify that the response is small enough to extrapolate without materially changing the working state.
4. Repeat the decisive pair using independent synthesis batches. First repeat the interaction and parent reference behavior; do not multiply every screening point.

Use calibrated EO, CO2, ethylene and O2 analyses, with acetaldehyde/other carbon products checked rather than assumed absent. Close carbon balances with uncertainty, and include accumulated/desorbing carbon during transients. Carbon-based EO selectivity is EO formed divided by ethylene consumed, with full carbon accounting. Keep O2 conversion, ethylene conversion, and Hwang's special selectivity ratio distinct. Report EO per catalyst volume, per total Ag, and per time; exposed-site normalization is secondary because promoters change site accessibility.

Dilute the bed and demonstrate negligible heat/mass-transfer effects by changing dilution, particle/granule size, and flow at matched contact time as appropriate. Measure bed temperature. Use an inert tracer for feed-switch residence time. These checks address false interactions from heat release, secondary residence time and chlorine fronts; they do not require a new operando instrument suite.

### Observations and decisions

- **Supports an operational conflict:** Ni suppresses combustion on Cs–Ag, but the gain disappears or EO output falls on Cs/Re–Ag in the promoted chlorine window, reproducibly after separate chlorine optimization. The effect must exceed synthesis/measurement uncertainty and survive primary/secondary rate separation.
- **Rejects the proposed conflict:** Ni gives comparable improvement with and without Re after conditioning, or the interaction vanishes after chlorine re-optimization. A consequential additive benefit remains a valid reason to advance the catalyst. Do not call it proof of oxygen competition.
- **Uninformative:** the baseline promoted state is absent; particles, Re retention, chlorine equilibration, or transport differ enough to explain the effect. Resolve that single demonstrated confounder only if feasible within the small campaign; otherwise stop.
- **Stops the practical direction:** the measured selectivity, sustained output, temperature, conditioning losses and expected lifetime trade-offs provide no defensible net benefit over the optimized reference. Higher temperature or lower output is a penalty to quantify, not an automatic rejection by itself. A transient selectivity maximum is not a success.

Add **one half-Re formulation** only if it tests a consequential mechanistic prediction or a useful resource/performance tradeoff. A nonzero factorial interaction is not a prerequisite. Small selectivity or productivity penalties may overwhelm savings of trace Re; no arbitrary tolerance establishes acceptable economics. Resolve differences against measurement and synthesis uncertainty, and include sustained operation and repeated conditioning in any practical claim. The duration required must follow the observed deactivation and relaxation times; a short stable run does not establish commercial catalyst life.

## Practical consequences and confidence

Use the shared [epoxidation practical-metrics framework](../calculations/epoxidation-practical-metrics.md) for stoichiometry, fair throughput comparisons, resource costs, and process boundaries. Its supplier-reported long-run benchmark is a demanding comparison target, not an independent matched trial.

The dominant possible value remains reduced ethylene combustion at sustained EO throughput. Saving ppm-level Re alone may be economically small compared with the Ag inventory, synthesis cost, energy, and lost production. Nickel addition does not automatically reduce chlorine demand; any reduction must be measured at equal output/selectivity. Increased ethane consumption, organochloride discharge, downtime, catalyst replacement, metal recovery, and added preparation steps belong in the comparison. A lower-temperature or higher-selectivity claim must carry its effect on recycle, separation and heat recovery; reactor-only selectivity cannot establish net process savings.

- **Confidence in the mechanistic hypothesis: low to moderate.** The two oxygen-management interpretations create a credible interaction question, but they come from different formulations/pressures and both contain inferred steps. They do not establish that Ni and Re share one kinetically accessible oxygen pool.
- **Confidence in a feasible, informative experiment: moderate, conditional on reproducing both parent catalysts.** The observable interaction and its chlorine confounder can be tested with conventional kinetic equipment. Trace-promoter placement and long equilibration are serious limitations; direct atomistic assignment would be harder.
- **Confidence in a meaningful practical improvement: low.** No durable advantage over an optimized modern catalyst is demonstrated, and the nearest prior art already pursues Ni-containing formulations. Even a good diagnostic result may not justify a new program.

**Overall recommendation:** develop the conditional program through the bounded parent/compatibility comparison. Reproduce both parent behaviors before combining them; the published Ni parent lacks Cs, so a negative Ni/Cs versus Cs result alone does not establish failure to reproduce Ni promotion or uniquely implicate Re. Expand for a useful performance tradeoff with a defensible chemical explanation, or a consequential mechanism-discrimination result. Establishing both platforms from scratch requires a separate capability assessment. An oxygen-return measurement is optional and is not a prerequisite for a successful compatibility program.

## Follow-up: transferring the Ni loading between particle sizes

A later portfolio assessment requested a bounded material-transfer check; the arithmetic below does not change the novelty limits above.

For similarly shaped Ag particles, geometric surface Ag / total Ag scales as `1/d32`, where `d32 = Σ(n_i d_i³)/Σ(n_i d_i²)`. Thus nominal total Ni / geometric surface Ag scales as `(Ni/Ag_bulk) × d32`. Copying Ni/Ag = 1/200 from 75 nm to 170 nm particles raises this nominal density by `170/75 = 2.27`. Equal nominal density would instead give `(1/200) × (75/170) = 1/453`. This is an initial geometric estimate, **not measured Ni coverage or a predicted optimum**.

Hwang's PDF p. 3, Eq. 1, defines the surface-averaged diameter above and assumes hemispherical particles. Table 1 gives 170 nm for B-CsRe, 170 nm for B, and 190 nm for B-LiCsRe. However, p. 9 prose gives 150 nm for B-CsRe and 170 nm for B-LiCsRe, inconsistent with Table 1. Use the table provisionally and the reproduced catalyst's actual distribution for experimental design. Jalil's author manuscript p. 4 describes approximately 70 nm particles, whereas p. 9 describes the optimum on approximately 75 nm particles. The SI was unavailable for the initial calculation. Its now-retained Figure S7 explicitly treats Ni surface coverage as a maximum assuming complete surface segregation, and Figure S8 presents post-reaction size distributions; neither makes the rounded 75 nm value a measured working Ni coverage. Use the actual distribution and inventory for the reproduced material. Using 70 nm gives about 1/486 instead of 1/453 for a 170 nm target; this further rules out presenting 1/453 as an exact prescription.

Jalil p. 5 explicitly assumes all Ni occupies the Ag surface when estimating nominal surface coverage and identifies that estimate as an upper limit dependent on particle size and environment. An ideal sphere and a hemisphere with an inaccessible flat base both have exposed area/volume `6/d`, so those ideal geometries alone do not invalidate the ratio. Real contact angles, facets, size distributions, Ni surface/bulk partitioning, Ni on the support or in oxide clusters, and Cs/Re/Cl-dependent blocking or reconstruction do. Match the estimated geometric loading only as a starting point; do not infer that the accessible Ni population has been matched.

**Reference gate:** first compare matched Ag and NiAg without Cs under the intended transferred conditions. The fixed-Cs Ni × Re factorial does not contain this parent comparison. If Ni/Cs–Ag shows no benefit, that factorial alone cannot distinguish failed Ni-platform transfer from an interaction with Cs. Once the parent effect is reproduced, the fixed-Cs factorial tests a Re interaction conditional on Cs; it does not establish independence from Cs.
