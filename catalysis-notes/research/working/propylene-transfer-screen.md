# Dilute Ni–Ag for direct propylene epoxidation: bounded opportunity screen

**Source and decision update, 2026-09-16:** This is a historical screen of an existing idea, not an active campaign. The [decision brief](../decision-brief.md) controls current priorities. Access statements and source-request lists below describe the original review unless explicitly updated; current access is recorded in the [literature status](../literature-status.md). Availability of a full text does not mean that every claim or benchmark in it has been rechecked. No new idea or experimental campaign is started by this update.

The previously pending Takahashi, Esposito2024 isotope, Esposito2024 chlorine and Esposito2025 co-promoter main texts are now retained and marked read. Esposito2026 main and SI are also available; see the [Ag audit](../reviews/post-upload-ag-audit.md) for the updated kinetic-transfer boundary. The 2023 chlorinated-hydrocarbon main article is also retained and marked read. Their shared oxidant and promoter results do not establish a useful dilute-Ni PO gain. The older Ni-containing Ag precedents continue to rule out a generic Ni-transfer novelty claim.

## Decision

**Do not promote simple transfer of Ni–Ag from ethylene to propylene into a main research program.** The problem is consequential and a dilute-Ni experiment could be informative, but Ni promotion of Ag for direct propylene epoxidation is established prior art. The remaining hypothesis concerns a particular working state and selective reaction branching, with low evidence for a useful process advance. This screen does not establish that the transfer is chemically impossible.

A small comparison is defensible if reproduced Ag/Ni–Ag parent materials and suitable product analysis already exist. It does not presently justify a new catalyst platform, broad promoter library, or displacement of a better-defined program. Neither this conclusion nor the greater potential value of direct propylene oxidation rescues the EO program from its separate prior-art reassessment.

Yu's selectivity/conversion tradeoff deserves careful separation by material: the **Ni0.4Ag1** sample fell from 70.6% to 41.4% selectivity as conversion rose from 0.03% to 0.62% through increased contact time. These are not the conversion coordinates of the **Ni1Ag0.4** sample's 70.7% result. Product loss is a possible explanation, but contact-time dependence alone does not uniquely assign it.

Additional primary sources found but not fully read include [Ag facet experiments/theory](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2012.05.006), [oxygen-species and particle-size calculations with experimental tests](https://doi.org/10.1039/C4CP02198C), and an [older allylic-abstraction model](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0039-6028(98)00025-9). They support treating allylic chemistry as a major competitor, not a proof of universal impossibility. A [2025 AgCl–AgCu study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2025.115981) reports approximately 100% PO selectivity at 80 °C in its abstract; the corresponding conversion/productivity has not been established in this screen. It must not be omitted from later benchmarking or treated as demonstrated process superiority.

## Remaining hypothesis

**Proposed, not established:** a dilute Ni-conditioned Ag state can decrease the propensity of the working oxygen population to initiate allylic chemistry while retaining productive oxygen transfer to propylene. Alternatively, Ni could sustain a selective oxygen-containing state against reduction by propylene. These are distinguishable ideas: suppressing an unselective branch is not equivalent to increasing oxygen uptake.

Jalil's ethylene result provides a concrete reason to test the first idea, but no propylene result. Stabilizing oxygen near Ni does not prove that the oxygen which attacks propylene becomes less basic, that enough selective oxygen remains accessible, or that PO survives the surface. Ni-assisted O2 activation could instead accelerate the same unfavorable branch. A stationary retained-oxygen population is not a continuing sink for unwanted oxygen.

The connection to Gounder/Iglesia is catalytic selectivity governed by working adsorbates, promoter interactions and quantitative kinetics. Neither professor's involvement in a specific proposed experiment is assumed.

## Smallest informative experiment and advancement rule

1. Use an independently reproduced Ag/Ni–Ag pair with matched Ag size and processing; verify the reported EO response on aliquots as the material positive control. Measure propylene oxidation with a resolved low-contact-time product split and more than one contact time. Establish calibrated PO, acrolein, carbonyl and COx analysis and carbon closure; CO2 suppression alone is not selective epoxidation.
2. Compare PO formation and undesired-product formation at matched conditions and at matched conversion. A positive result requires increased PO output at comparable selectivity, or improved selectivity at comparable output, beyond preparation and analytical uncertainty. A weak Ag-only comparator does not establish competitiveness with promoted PO catalysts.
3. If a useful difference survives, test one consequential alternative: primary branching versus secondary PO loss, or selective-state persistence versus changed oxygen supply. PO cofeed gives net product sensitivity and requires additional discrimination if it changes formation as well as destruction. Carbon/deuterium tracers are optional only when that ambiguity changes the next decision; their use alone is not new.
4. Advance only if the Ni-conditioned state changes a useful selectivity–output relationship and produces a testable prediction beyond generic Ni promotion. Reproduce the decisive pair and compare against an appropriate promoted PO reference before expanding into K/NO/CO2/Cl combinations. Stop if Ni merely increases combustion, lowers all rates, or gives a small unexplained improvement at negligible output.

Failure on one reproduced pair would reject this transfer on that material and condition range. It would not establish that all dilute-Ni, support or co-promoter arrangements fail. This limited negative result is useful mainly when inexpensive; it is insufficient by itself to justify a major research program.

## Confidence and source handling

- **Scientific hypothesis:** plausible, weakly supported for propylene. Allylic competition and selective-state depletion add difficulties absent or weaker in ethylene oxidation.
- **Feasible, informative initial test:** moderate confidence conditional on existing parent materials and quantitative PO/byproduct analysis. Unique site-level interpretation is substantially harder.
- **Meaningful practical improvement:** low confidence. Existing Ni–Ag results, low productivity and competing promoted catalysts create a demanding baseline; no process benefit is demonstrated here.
- **Originality:** low for the composition transfer; unresolved for a specific causal working-state relationship. No “first” claim is supported.

## Later cross-olefin prior-art check

Esposito, Parekh and Bhan's [2026 primary abstract](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5c08223) reports competitive ethylene/propylene epoxidation on a common promoted Ag surface and attributes a reversible selectivity effect to NO-derived promotion. Its full text and SI remain unread in this check. This directly narrows any generic claim to discover shared epoxidation chemistry or transferable promoter function across the two olefins. It does not establish how dilute Ni would affect that state or whether a useful PO-output gain would result. The prior [2023 chlorinated-hydrocarbon paper](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.3c00915) was also identified from the authors' official bibliography; its body has not been examined here. Both requests are preserved in the [central registry](../literature-queue.md#further-ag-site-counting-and-cross-olefin-prior-art) and were sent to the sole literature agent. The negative promotion decision remains unchanged.
