# Cu-zeolite SCR durability: does the desulfation atmosphere set the irreversible Cu loss per event?

> **Corrections after independent review (2026-09-16).** The [independent review](../../reviews/direction-search-cu-desox-independent-review.md) found three errors that narrow this screen. (1) He/Ding 2025 (local full text, DOI 10.1021/acs.est.5c02926, Section 2.1) *did* regenerate with and without NO + NH3 and reports a minor atmosphere effect on conversion recovery (its Fig. S5); the statement below that NH3-containing ramps were not compared is wrong at the conversion level. (2) Cummins patents [US9657665B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9657665B2/en) and [US9080524B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9080524B2/en) (2013 priority) already show matched temperature-only versus NO+NH3 sulfur removal and conversion recovery; BASF [US11097264B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11097264B2/en) is a third reductant-assisted deSOx claim. The open boundary is therefore only the per-cycle Cu-speciation accounting versus atmosphere, and unread papers (Shen 2019, 10.1007/s11814-019-0307-x; Gao/Mossin/Vennestrøm 2025, 10.1002/cctc.202500597) may narrow it further. (3) DOI 10.1021/acs.iecr.8b04543 is Wei, Yao et al. 2019, not Chen/Epling. Design changes required by the review: the practical comparator is lean + NO with dosing off, not lean only; the primary sulfation should form Cu sulfate (400 °C SO2 or SO3 exposure), because 250 °C SCR-feed sulfation yields mainly ammonium sulfate; the EPR resolution target should be 10 percentage points, not 5; the campaign is about 50 specimens, not small. Deka 2025 shows a one-event loss after the first calcination of a heavily impregnated sample, not per-event accumulation. The review ranks this candidate below Ag and polymer, alongside the wet-methane diagnostic, and best housed as a protocol-knowledge option under the zeolite-durability program. The screen text below is retained as written.


Direction-search candidate screen, 2026-09-16. Territory: Cu-zeolite NH3-SCR and related Pd/Cu-zeolite emissions catalysts. This is a screen, not a selected program; no experiments were performed, no facility access is assumed, and nothing under `literature/` was modified. Web sources were read as open abstracts, open PDFs or publisher landing pages unless a local read note is cited; each entry in the prior-art table states what was actually read.

**Recommendation in one line:** retain as a **bounded diagnostic**, not a full program. The question is consequential and its mechanism is fresh (2024–2025), but the tempting intervention (NH3 dosing during desulfation) is already claimed by industry, and the practical magnitude of the effect is unproven.

## 1. Practical problem, scale, and fit

Cu-SSZ-13 (Cu-CHA) is the standard heavy-duty and light-duty diesel NH3-SCR catalyst. Post-mortem studies of field-returned commercial bricks now agree that **sulfur aging, not hydrothermal aging, dominates real-world loss**: sulfur poisons isolated CuII to CuSO4-like species, these agglomerate to multinuclear CuSO4 clusters, and the clusters convert to SCR-inactive CuOx during desulfation. Laboratory hydrothermal aging alone reproduces field aging poorly; hydrothermal aging plus SOx reproduces it partially ([Wu 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcatb.2022.121807), abstract and OSTI summary read; [Deka 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2025.171436), open proof read). Field aging selectively slows the oxidation half-cycle (OHC) because sulfur-coordinated Cu is less mobile and forms fewer transient Cu pairs ([Deka 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcatb.2022.121233), open manuscript introduction and abstract read).

The stake is a regulatory lifetime. The Deka 2022 introduction cites the US EPA/CARB heavy-duty useful-life requirement of 435,000 miles, rising under CARB's Omnibus rule to 600,000 miles by 2027 and 800,000 miles by 2031 (as stated in that paper; not independently verified here). Over that life a brick sees on the order of 10^2–10^3 desulfation or DPF-regeneration thermal events. If each event converts a small fraction of active Cu irreversibly to CuOx, the cumulative loss sets catalyst oversizing and end-of-life. Cummins' own useful-life estimate of about 10,000 h appears in [Hammershøi 2018b](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcatb.2018.06.039) (abstract read).

Fit: this is Gounder's core area (Cu-CHA speciation, Al-pair control of Z2Cu versus ZCuOH, NH3-solvated [Cu(NH3)2]+ mobility and transient pairing, CO titration of Cu dimers) and uses Iglesia-style redox-half-cycle and transient kinetics. Gounder co-authored the 2025 aged-Cu-CHA site-selection paper ([[schmithorst2025-origins-of-the-hydrothermal-stability]], local read note) and the 2017 post-aging SCR-exposure study ([[caballero2017-structural-and-kinetic-changes-to]]). These are intellectual connections, not statements about facilities or faculty priorities.

## 2. The one consequential uncertainty

**Does the gas atmosphere during a desulfation event—lean-only versus lean with NH3 (and NO) present—change the fraction of active Cu that is irreversibly converted to multinuclear CuOx per event, at a practical peak temperature (about 550 °C) and matched sulfur inventory?**

Why this and not "characterize sulfation more": the reversible chemistry is settled (Section 3). The irreversible pathway has just been identified (sulfated Cu pairs cannot split; CuSO4 clusters decompose oxidatively via CuO·CuSO4 to CuOx), but every study that established it used NH3-free oxidative desulfation (static air, or lean ramps). Meanwhile the industrial deSOx policy already puts NH3 on the catalyst at ANR > 1 during 500–650 °C events, without published evidence on whether that helps or harms the irreversible fraction. Two chemically motivated predictions point in opposite directions (Section 4). The answer changes a **dosing decision** (NH3 on/off and ANR during deSOx), possibly a **formulation decision** (Al-rich/high-Cu versus standard Si/Al, which He 2025 shows changes which sulfate forms), and the **design of accelerated aging protocols**, which Deka 2025 explicitly lists as needed.

What this screen does not claim: that NH3 dosing during deSOx is a new intervention (it is not), or that the effect is large.

**Open boundary (stated precisely):** no retrieved study measures, on the same sulfated material and with the same sulfur inventory and peak temperature, the irreversible isolated-Cu loss (EPR-active Cu, CO-titratable dimers, H2-TPR CuOx, low-T turnover rate) after desulfation in lean gas versus lean gas with NH3 or NH3 + NO, over repeated cycles. Reductant-assisted deSOx (2016) and the ANR > 1 policy (2024 patent) are established as sulfur-removal and conversion-recovery methods, so the intervention is not new; what is unknown is the sign and size of its effect on the irreversible pathway identified in 2024–2025.

**Negative and constraining results found:** He 2025 reports that poisoning under SCR gas is *more* severe and needs a higher recovery temperature than poisoning without NO/NH3, and Hammershøi 2018c shows SO2 binds reduced Cu(I) more strongly. Both warn that NH3 presence can worsen, not only relieve, Cu–S chemistry. He 2025 also found regeneration atmosphere less important than temperature within its O2/H2O-only set.

## 4. Falsifiable hypothesis and live alternatives

**H1 (proposed).** During a desulfation ramp through 350–550 °C, NH3 (with or without NO) keeps sulfated Cu as reducible, NH3-solvated mononuclear Cu–HSO4/Cu(I) species that release SO2 (the chemical-deSOx route) before CuSO4 clusters form, so the per-event irreversible loss of isolated Cu is **smaller** than under lean-only desulfation at equal peak temperature and sulfur inventory.

**H2 (opposite sign).** NH3 solvation increases Cu mobility and transient pairing during the ramp; sulfate traps those pairs (Deka 2025, Mandal 2024), so NH3-on desulfation produces **more** multinuclear sulfated Cu and more CuOx per event. Supporting priors: Cu(I) binds SO2 more strongly (Hammershøi 2018c); SCR-gas poisoning is more severe (He 2025).

**H3 (null).** At practical peak temperature the irreversible fraction is set by sulfur inventory and thermal history only; NH3 changes only the temperature at which the *reversible* fraction leaves. This would confirm existing chemical-deSOx knowledge and remove the dosing question from the durability agenda.

**H4 (artifacts).** Apparent differences arise from (a) incomplete sulfur removal in one arm (residual S lowers EPR/TOR without any CuOx), (b) the NH3-oxidation exotherm at 550 °C raising local bed temperature in NH3-on arms, or (c) NH3 protecting or damaging the framework independently of sulfur (NH3 protection during steaming is prior art in this repository).

**Discriminating observations.**

| Observation | H1 | H2 | H3 | H4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Irreversible EPR-active Cu loss per cycle, NH3-on vs lean-only, at matched residual S | lower | higher | equal within precision | tracks residual S or bed ΔT, not atmosphere |
| CO-titratable dimer count after desulfation | lower | higher | equal | – |
| H2-TPR CuOx feature (~240–260 °C) growth per cycle | slower | faster | equal | – |
| SCR TOR at 200 °C and its Ea/pre-exponential (OHC-limited regime) | higher TOR, larger A | lower TOR, smaller A | equal | – |
| Sulfur-free NH3/thermal sham cycles | no CuOx growth | no CuOx growth | no CuOx growth | CuOx or Al loss appears without sulfur → framework/thermal effect |
| 27Al NMR / Brønsted NH3-TPD | unchanged across arms | unchanged | unchanged | changes with NH3 arm → (c) |

A clean H3 result is informative: it would tell calibrators that deSOx dosing can be chosen on NH3-slip/N2O grounds alone.

## 5. Smallest informative first campaign

**Materials.** One commercial-type Cu-SSZ-13 (Si/Al ≈ 12, Cu/Al ≈ 0.3, degreened 550 °C/10% H2O) as the primary specimen. One contrast only if the primary result is consequential: an Al-rich, higher-Cu Cu-SSZ-13 near the He 2025 composition, or a paired-Al-enriched CHA (Gounder synthesis), to test whether Z2Cu-rich material changes the sign. No synthesis library.

**Sulfation.** Gas-phase SO2 (30–100 ppm) in standard SCR feed at 250 °C to a fixed measured S/Cu (choice: ~0.3–0.5, in the field-sample range 0.18–0.46 of Deka 2025). Dose by integrated inlet SO2 and verify by ICP on sacrificial aliquots; do not rely on nominal dose. Impregnated H2SO4 is a mechanistic reference, not the main arm.

**Desulfation arms (each a ramp to 550 °C, 30 min hold, 10% H2O, 8–10% O2).** A: lean only. B: lean + NH3 at a realistic ANR > 1 without NO. C: lean + NO + NH3 (SCR feed). D: inert ramp then lean hold (mechanistic reference from Mesilov 2021; not an on-vehicle policy). Cycle sulfation/desulfation 6 times; take sacrificial specimens after cycles 1, 3, 6.

**Matched controls.** Sulfur-free sham cycling for A–C (separates NH3/thermal aging from sulfur chemistry). Lean-only arm with a deliberately raised setpoint matching the measured NH3-oxidation exotherm of arm B/C (separates temperature from chemistry). Fresh and degreened references run through the same final assay.

**Balances.** Sulfur: inlet SO2 integral, outlet SO2/H2S/SO3-related MS or FTIR during each ramp, ICP on aliquots; report closure. Cu: ICP before/after (Cu volatilization is not expected but must be excluded). Isolated Cu: EPR at −150 °C double integration on hydrated samples. Dimers: CO titration (CO2 evolved). CuOx: H2-TPR feature at ~240–260 °C. Framework: 27Al MAS NMR and Brønsted NH3-TPD.

**Transport and thermal checks.** Powder diluted with cordierite/SiC, sieved fraction, differential conversion (<15%) for TOR at 200 °C; internal thermocouple during ramps; Mears/Weisz estimates recorded. Keep NH3-oxidation heat release explicit: at ANR > 1 and 550 °C the NH3 oxidation is fast, and the bed can run hotter than the setpoint.

**Replication.** Independent sulfation/desulfation runs on separate aliquots for each arm (at least three at cycle 6), and one repeat on a second catalyst batch for the decisive contrast. Freeze the minimum consequential difference before unblinding cycle-6 data. Numerical target (a design choice, not a prediction): resolve an absolute difference of ≥5 percentage points in irreversible EPR-active Cu, or ≥15% in 200 °C TOR, between arms at cycle 6 against replicate scatter.

**Decision informed.** If H1 or H2 is supported with useful precision: specify NH3-on or NH3-off (and ANR) for deSOx events in the calibration comparator, and test one held-out prediction (a different peak temperature or a DOC-generated SO3-rich exposure) before any application claim. If H3: close the durability branch; deSOx dosing is then a slip/N2O question only. If H4(b): the real lever is bed temperature, already known.

## 6. Confidence (in words)

- **Hypothesis (H1 as stated):** low to moderate. The mechanism (mobile NH3-solvated Cu; sulfate-trapped pairs) is established, but the priors cut both ways; H2 is at least as plausible as H1.
- **Feasibility and informativeness:** moderate to high. All measurements are standard (EPR, CO titration, H2-TPR, NMR, differential kinetics) and the cycle count is small. Main risks are reproducible gas-phase sulfation to a matched S/Cu, exotherm control, and the sensitivity of EPR/CO titration to few-percent changes. A null result is informative.
- **Practical improvement:** low to moderate. The dosing policy is already claimed by one OEM; the value is in knowing its sign and magnitude for the irreversible pathway and in fixing accelerated-aging protocols. A field-relevant answer also needs SO3 (DOC-generated) and real thermal histories, which this first campaign does not include.

## 7. Fair benchmark and stop/pivot rule

**Benchmark.** Lean-only 550 °C deSOx at equal peak temperature, hold time and sulfur inventory (the documented practice in Brookshear 2015 and the He 2025 O2/H2O arm), and the patented ANR > 1 policy as the industrial comparator. A per-cycle difference must be reported per Cu atom and per gram, with the sulfur balance closed and the sham arms shown alongside.

**Stop.** If after cycle 3 the arms differ by less than replicate scatter and the sulfur balance closes, conclude H3 and stop; do not add cycles or spectroscopy to rescue the question. Stop also if sulfation cannot be reproduced to matched S/Cu within a modest effort.

**Pivot.** If arm B/C shows *more* irreversible loss (H2), pivot to testing whether a lower ANR or NH3-off window during the 350–500 °C part of the ramp removes the penalty. If the exotherm-matched lean arm reproduces the B/C result, report a temperature effect and close the chemistry question.

## 8. Recommendation

**Retain as a bounded diagnostic; do not promote to a full program.** Reasons: (i) the intervention itself (NH3/reductant during deSOx) is 2016 prior art and a 2024 patent claim, so only the irreversible-loss accounting and its sign are new; (ii) the practical magnitude is unknown and could be small relative to the peak-temperature effect that is already known; (iii) a deployment claim needs SO3-containing exposures and real thermal histories beyond this screen. In its favor: the question is sharply defined by 2024–2025 mechanistic work, the decision it informs (dosing during deSOx; aging-protocol design; Al-rich formulation interaction) is concrete, a null result is useful, and the campaign is small. It is comparable in standing to the retained wet-methane NO-dependence diagnostic, with a clearer operating decision but weaker originality of the intervention.

### Other candidates in this territory, and why they were not chosen

- **O2-activation bottleneck in aged catalysts; can NO2 or O2 bypass it?** [[kamasamudram2026-low-temperature-standard-scr-over]] already shows mild aging selectively slows OHC and that raising O2 from 4.0 to 5.8 vol% restores degreened performance at 200 °C; [[paolucci2017-dynamic-multinuclear-sites-formed-by]] shows NO2 oxidizes Cu(I) with density-independent first-order kinetics; Shan et al. 2022 ([10.1038/s41467-022-32136-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-32136-z), abstract) show NO2 *inhibits* low-T SCR on fresh Cu-SSZ-13 and that aging alleviates it. "Use more NO2 on aged bricks" is a DOC-sizing question within established fast-SCR practice. Not original enough.
- **Early Cu-migration marker that predicts end-of-life.** Wu 2022, Rani 2025 and Deka 2025 already identify the markers (EPR-silent Cu, CO-titratable dimers, H2-TPR CuOx, reducible Cu) and show lab HTA + SOx cycling partly reproduces field aging. The remaining task, prediction across protocols, overlaps the repository's existing zeolite-durability assay-history pilot. Not a distinct program.
- **Pd/zeolite passive NOx adsorbers.** CO-induced Pd reduction/sintering, the H2O requirement for CO reduction, and hydrocarbon effects are established (Kunal/Toops 2021, [10.1016/j.apcatb.2021.120591](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcatb.2021.120591), abstract; Morandi/Lietti 2025, [10.1016/j.saa.2024.124945](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.saa.2024.124945), metadata; Khivantsev/Gao/Szanyi series). Heavily worked by PNNL; weaker Gounder fit. Not chosen.
- **Formaldehyde/HCN poisoning.** Already screened in [emissions-challenge](../emissions-challenge.md); the warm ZCuOH cleanup route is prior art. Not re-proposed.
- **NH3 dosing policy during sulfur *exposure*** (as opposed to desulfation). He 2025 shows SCR-gas exposure is more severe but produces the more removable ammonium sulfate; since sulfur is present whenever the engine runs, dosing cannot be withheld during exposure except during DOC-driven SO3 spikes. Folded into the desulfation question rather than pursued separately.
