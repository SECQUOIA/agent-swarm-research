Direction search, 2026-09-16. Screen only; not a selected program. No experiments performed. Follows the shared brief in [README.md](README.md). The earlier [liquid-phase challenge](../liquid-phase-practical-challenge.md) already declined concentrated tandem sugar-to-lactate conversion; that idea is not re-proposed here.

## Decision in one paragraph

Of the candidates in this territory, the one with a real practical decision and an open mechanistic boundary is **water-dependent deactivation of ZrO2 (and TiO2) acid–base pairs during ketonization of wet, waste-derived carboxylic acids for sustainable aviation fuel (SAF)**. Reversible water inhibition of the rate is established and already drives reactor design; what is not established is whether water also changes the *slow, non-kinetic* loss of ketonization activity, and therefore whether feed drying or interstage water removal buys catalyst lifetime in addition to reactor size, and whether regeneration must be oxidative. **Recommendation: retain as a bounded diagnostic with an explicit promotion gate; do not promote to a full program yet.** The Sn-Beta lactate deactivation candidate is judged weaker because the loss modes and regenerations are already densely mapped by Hammond, Gounder, Pérez-Ramírez and Iglesias (Jose) groups; glucose isomerization site-environment work and aqueous extracrystalline H3O+ have weak practical stakes here. Reasons are given below.

## 1. Practical problem, scale, and connection to the professors

**Problem.** Ketonization couples two carboxylic acids to a ketone, CO2 and water over ZrO2 or TiO2 at roughly 300–400 °C. It is the first catalytic step in NREL's wet-waste volatile fatty acid (VFA) to SAF route and, per its authors, the only unit operation in that route not yet practiced industrially [Miller et al. 2022, p. 2998]. The route matters because wet-waste VFAs could supply a material fraction of jet demand with net-negative life-cycle CO2, and NREL's techno-economic analysis is capital-driven, with the ketonization and hydrodeoxygenation reactors as the largest equipment cost drivers and catalyst cost minor "due to low requirements and long assumed lifetimes" [Huq et al. 2021, PMC full text]. Those assumed lifetimes are the point of attack.

**Why water.** Every ketone formed releases one water; a wet feed adds more. On the NREL commercial ZrO2 at 350 °C the fitted rate law contains a water adsorption term (K_H2O,0 = 0.0038 ± 0.0016 kPa−1, ΔH_ads,H2O = −34 ± 17 kJ mol−1) and no CO2 or ketone inhibition; the authors identify water inhibition as "the principal driver of reactor size and pressure drop" and recommend recycle, loop or series reactors with interstage water separation, which cuts simulated reactor size by more than 97% in a nine-stage example [Miller et al. 2022, abstract, Table of parameters, Sections 3.3–3.4, Conclusions; NREL deck PR-5100-80795, slides 9–11]. The same paper states that its kinetic model "does not quantify deactivation over time, as this is a separate area of active study that we will address in a future publication" [Miller et al. 2022, Section 2.1]. My searches (see Section 3) found no such publication through 2026; this is a search-limited negative, not proof of absence.

**Connection.** Iglesia's group established the acid–base pair mechanism of ketonization and aldol chemistry on TiO2 and ZrO2 (1-hydroxy-enolate + coadsorbed acid C–C coupling on Ti–O/Zr–O pairs; near-saturation carboxylate coverages; bidentate carboxylates as unreactive spectators removable by a Cu hydrogenation function) and the kinetic methods (site titration, isotope effects, pressure-order analysis) needed to separate reversible inhibition from site loss [Wang & Iglesia 2017; Wang & Iglesia 2017 JPCC; Wang, Goulas & Iglesia 2016]. The same group's recent ZrO2 work in this repository shows trace water titrating exposed Zr–O acid–base pairs during ethylbenzene dehydrogenation and hydrocarbon-assisted removal of bound water [[artsiusheuski2025-selective-ethylbenzene-dehydrogenation-to-styrene]] p.1, p.5-8. Ketonization is the water-rich counterpart of that problem on the same oxide. Gounder's contribution is methodological: hydrophobicity control and quantitative titration of working-site populations in wet environments [[vila2020-quantification-of-intraporous-hydrophilic-binding]] p.7-12, [[harris2016-titration-and-quantification-of-open]] p.1-13. The connection is primarily to Iglesia.

## 2. The consequential uncertainty

**Question.** On the industrially relevant monoclinic ZrO2, does water change the rate constant of slow activity loss (site loss or persistent carboxylate/carbon accumulation), independent of its reversible kinetic inhibition, and is that loss recoverable by a dry purge or H2 treatment rather than by air oxidation?

**Decisions it changes.**

1. *Design/operating:* whether feed drying and interstage water removal are justified only by reactor size (as now) or also by lifetime, and therefore how many stages or how deep a drying step to specify.
2. *Regeneration:* whether periodic dry inert or H2 purges at reaction temperature suffice, or whether air burn-off is required. This determines whether hydrophobic carbon-supported oxides (TiO2/C, ZrO2/C), the published remedy for water inhibition, are viable at all, because carbon supports cannot survive oxidative regeneration. That incompatibility is my inference; the carbon-support papers below do not report oxidative regeneration.
3. *Material:* whether ZrO2 (NREL's choice) or TiO2 (reported to accumulate carboxylates more slowly) is preferable once the wet-feed loss mode is known.

A "characterize more" program is not proposed: the deliverable is a measured dependence of the loss rate constant and recovery fraction on water pressure, tested by a frozen prediction.

**Status labels.** Established: reversible water inhibition on ZrO2/TiO2 and its reactor-size consequence; carboxylate/coke accumulation on dry feeds and air regeneration; hydrophobic-support mitigation. Inference: that the water-associated bidentate complex (anatase) or hydroxylated Zr–O pairs could be precursors of persistent loss; that carbon supports preclude air regeneration. Hypothesis: water-dependence of the slow loss on m-ZrO2 (Section 4). Unvalidated expectation: that a short early recovery test predicts long-horizon recoverability.

**Open boundary.** No retrieved study measures the *deactivation rate constant* of m-ZrO2 ketonization as a function of water pressure at matched acid pressure and conversion, or the fraction of that loss recovered by dry purge, H2 and air in sequence, over the 100-h horizon NREL used. The closest evidence points in opposite directions: Shylesh et al. (partial irreversibility of water on isolated Zr) versus Fufachev et al. (water suppresses secondary condensation products, which are plausible coke precursors).

## 4. Falsifiable hypothesis, live alternatives, discriminating observations

**H1 (water-accelerated persistent loss).** After removing reversible inhibition, the first-order loss rate constant k_d of ketonization turnover on m-ZrO2 at 350 °C increases with water pressure, because water-associated bridging carboxylates or hydroxylated Zr–O pairs form a persistent, non-desorbing population. Prediction: k_d(25 kPa H2O) > k_d(0 kPa) at matched acid pressure and conversion; a dry He purge at 350 °C recovers less than the reversible-inhibition fraction; TPO carbon or persistent carboxylate IR bands scale with cumulative water exposure, not only with cumulative acid turnover.

**H2 (water-independent carbon accumulation).** k_d is independent of water pressure; loss tracks cumulative acid/ketone turnover through secondary condensation; dry purge and H2 recover little; air recovers the fresh rate. Water removal helps reactor size only.

**H3 (water-protective).** k_d decreases with water pressure because water suppresses condensation-derived deposits (consistent with Fufachev's olefin suppression); a wet bed loses activity more slowly, so interstage water removal trades reactor size against lifetime. Prediction: TPO carbon per turnover lower in the wet arm.

**H4 (water-only restructuring, acid-independent).** Water without acid at 350 °C already lowers the subsequent dry rate (surface hydroxylation or loss of coordinatively unsaturated pairs), with slow or no recovery on dry purge. Discriminated by a water-only pretreatment arm, as Padovan et al. did with solvent-only pretreatment of Sn-Beta [[padovan2018-active-site-hydration-governs-the]] p.5-6.

**Discriminators.** (i) k_d versus P_H2O with periodic reference-condition returns (the Bhan-type correction Miller et al. used). (ii) Sequential recovery ladder on the same bed: dry He at 350 °C → 10% H2 at 350 °C → air at 500 °C, each followed by the reference rate. (iii) TPO carbon and DRIFTS carboxylate bands versus cumulative water exposure and cumulative turnover, separately varied by changing acid pressure and time. (iv) Site counts before/after by acid or water TPD, normalized per surface area (N2 physisorption, XRD phase) to separate site loss from sintering or t→m transformation.

## 5. Smallest informative first campaign

**Materials.** One commercial monoclinic ZrO2 (same class as NREL's), crushed and sieved; one anatase TiO2 as a secondary comparator only if the ZrO2 result is water-dependent. Report phase, surface area, and impurities; biogenic VFAs carry alkali and sulfur [Huq et al. 2021], so use model hexanoic acid first.

**Conditions.** 350 °C, 40 kPa hexanoic acid in He, differential conversion (<10%), water cofeed at 0, 10 and 25 kPa (the NREL range), each run for ~100 h with reference-condition returns every ~10 h. Arms: dry; wet-10; wet-25; water-only pretreatment (25 kPa H2O, 24 h, then dry acid). Two independent bed loadings per arm; replicate scatter in k_d sets the resolvable contrast.

**Balances and checks.** Carbon balance by GC on ketone, CO2, anhydride and secondary products; TPO carbon on spent beds; water balance from fed water plus stoichiometric product water versus outlet (Karl Fischer or calibrated TCD). Transport: two particle sizes and a Weisz–Prater estimate; NREL's pellet calculation shows η ≈ 0.6 with water inhibition, so crushed particles are required. Heat: diluted bed, thermocouple in bed.

**Recovery ladder and frozen prediction.** After 100 h, run the He → H2 → air ladder on each bed. Before the ladder, freeze a prediction of which step recovers the rate for the wet-25 bed from the first 20 h of the same bed (initial recovery on a 2-h dry purge at 20 h). The held-out test is whether that early measurement predicts the 100-h recovery within the replicate scatter. A second prospective test, if warranted, transfers the rule to butyric acid or to a 4-stage water-removal profile.

**Decision informed.** Whether water removal is specified for lifetime as well as size; whether dry/H2 purges can replace air regeneration; whether carbon-supported hydrophobic oxides remain candidates.

**Numerical choices, not predictions.** Treat a ≥2-fold difference in k_d between dry and wet-25 arms, resolved against replicate scatter, as consequential; treat ≥80% recovery by dry purge or H2 as "non-oxidative regeneration viable". These thresholds are design choices for interpretation and should be reset from measured scatter.

## 6. Confidence judgments (in words)

- **Hypothesis H1.** Plausible but unresolved; low-to-moderate. Support is indirect (Shylesh's partial irreversibility on Zr/SiO2; Lin's water-bidentate complex on anatase; water titration of Zr–O pairs in Iglesia's ZrO2 work). H2 and H3 are equally live; H3 has one supportive short-run observation.
- **Feasibility and informativeness.** Moderate-to-high. Gas-phase flow kinetics with standard analytics; the design mirrors NREL's published procedure, so the reference can be reproduced first. Main risks: precision of k_d over 100 h, impurity carryover from acids, and distinguishing carboxylate accumulation from hydroxylation with bulk probes. A clean negative (H2) is still decision-relevant.
- **Practical improvement.** Low-to-moderate and conditional. The outcome is a design or regeneration rule for a pre-commercial process, not a new catalyst. If H2 holds, the result validates current assumptions and excludes carbon supports unless air-free regeneration is shown; if H1 holds, it adds a lifetime term to the interstage-water-removal decision; if H3 holds, it argues against aggressive drying. None is a demonstrated productivity gain.

## 7. Fair benchmark and stop/pivot rule

**Benchmark.** NREL's commercial ZrO2 at 350 °C with its published rate law, the >100-h dry-feed run at full conversion with 1.8 wt% carbon and air regeneration at 500 °C, and the interstage water-removal design [Miller et al. 2022; Huq et al. 2021]. Material comparators: TiO2 (rutile/anatase per Fufachev 2021; stability per Tingelstad 2025) and TiO2/C or ZrO2/C hydrophobic hybrids. Compare on ketone output per catalyst mass integrated over a full cycle including regeneration downtime and water-removal duty, not on initial rate.

**Stop.** Close the branch if k_d is water-independent within replicate precision and air recovers the fresh rate (H2), or if the recovery outcome cannot be predicted from the early test. Report the bounded negative; it is a usable validation of NREL's lifetime assumption. **Pivot** only if a water-dependent, non-oxidatively-recoverable loss appears: then test one operating policy (periodic dry/H2 purge) against continued wet operation and against water removal over the same period, and only afterwards consider a matched ZrO2/TiO2 material comparison. Do not pivot to a generic hydrophobic-support synthesis program; that remedy is prior art.

## 8. Recommendation

**Retain as a bounded diagnostic; do not promote to a full program now.** The practical decision is concrete and the mechanistic boundary is open, but the evidence for a water-dependent persistent loss is indirect, the process is pre-commercial, and hydrophobic mitigation and air regeneration are established. Promotion gate: a replicated, water-dependent loss whose recoverability is predicted by an early measurement and which changes the interstage-water-removal or regeneration specification relative to the NREL benchmark. Against the portfolio's current lead (Ag) this candidate is comparable in structure (persistent versus recoverable state, prospective prediction) and easier to run, but its practical stake is a design rule for a route that does not yet operate at scale.

## 9. Candidates weighed and set aside

**Sn/Zr/Hf-Beta deactivation and regeneration during sugar-to-lactate (Topsøe context).** The loss modes are already mapped in the relevant solvents: methanol at 110 °C removes cooperating proximal Sn–OH/Si–OH hydration, not pore fouling, and a water/methanol wash restores activity [[padovan2018-active-site-hydration-governs-the]] p.2-10 ([10.1021/acscatal.8b01759](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.8b01759)); hot water creates silanol networks and deactivates hydrophobic Sn-Beta without Sn leaching [[cordon2019-deactivation-of-sn-beta-zeolites]] p.8-13 ([10.1039/c8cy02589d](https://doi.org/10.1039/c8cy02589d)); across topologies water erodes the framework while methanol promotes Sn leaching and carbon deposition, with xylose chelating Sn [[lari2016-deactivation-mechanisms-of-tin-zeolites]] p.5-13 ([10.1039/c5gc02147b](https://doi.org/10.1039/c5gc02147b)); thermal regeneration at 550 °C restores and even raises activity through permanent solvent-induced changes, reproducible by a "preactivation" [Botti et al. 2020, [10.1021/acscatal.0c02346](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.0c02346)]; retro-aldol operation at 160 °C needs water plus alkali and plateaus [[botti2022-high-productivity-continuous-conversion-of]] p.6-10; K leaching and furanic deposits dominate on Sn-USY [[martin2025-continuous-production-of-methyl-lactate]] p.3-6; a 434-h Sn-Beta run at 165 °C attributed loss to carbon deposition, framework damage and Sn leaching [Zhang et al. 2020, [10.1021/acs.iecr.0c01770](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.0c01770), search-level]. What remains is whether an early measurable state predicts the number of regeneration cycles a bed survives; that is a real but narrow question inside a crowded, expert-owned field, and the earlier screen already excluded hydration descriptors and Sn leaching as novelty. Not promoted.

**Glucose isomerization site environment (open/closed Sn, hydrophobic pockets).** Mechanistically rich but the productivity stake is weak: enzymatic glucose isomerization is mature, and the open/closed Sn and silanol-density descriptors are already quantified [[harris2016-titration-and-quantification-of-open]] p.1-13, [[vila2020-quantification-of-intraporous-hydrophilic-binding]] p.7-12, with the open/closed band assignment itself contested [[abidi2025-challenging-the-distinction-between-open]] p.2-7. Not promoted.

**Aqueous Brønsted acid catalysis with extracrystalline H3O+.** The cellulose-hydrolysis case is resolved in favour of bulk hydronium [[liu2024-acid-catalysis-mediated-by-aqueous]] p.3-14, and the hydronium-density volcano in H-BEA is established [[kim2024-confined-ionic-environments-tailoring-the]] p.2-6. A practical decision that a further resolution would change was not identified. Not promoted.

**Aldol condensation of ketones on TiO2/ZrO2 for SAF.** Enolate mechanism and water effects are established (table above); in the NREL route the aldol/cyclization step uses H/ZSM-5, which deactivates quickly and is air-regenerated [Miller et al. 2024]. A ketonization-first diagnostic is the better entry; the aldol variant is not proposed.
