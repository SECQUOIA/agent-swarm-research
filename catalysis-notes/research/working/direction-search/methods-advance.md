# Screen: a methodological advance in working-site counting or kinetic diagnosis

> **Corrections after independent review (2026-09-16).** The [independent prior-art audit](../../reviews/direction-search-methods-independent-review.md) finds the plug-flow poison front itself to be textbook physics (Wheeler and Robell 1969, 10.1016/0021-9517(69)90404-7; Bohart–Adams wave) and already stated qualitatively by Wrasman et al. 2024 (10.1016/j.jcat.2024.115451, upstream-first coverage "rather than those that bind the titrant strongest", with a titrant-pressure diagnostic that Kumar et al. 2021, 10.1021/acscatal.1c01588, already ran). What remains unpublished, as far as found, is the explicit consequence that a linear curve with intercept equal to the titratable total arises for any activity distribution, the two-parameter identifiability point, and calibration on mixtures of known spectator fraction. The Davis 2018 attribution below ("linear decrease cited as evidence that nearly every Zn(II) site is equally active") is not supported by the OSTI manuscript and is withdrawn; Davis equates the intercept with active-site density, a weaker step. The Iyer–Bhan estimator spread cannot be explained by the front (a sharp front predicts a straight line; their curve is convex) and their Table 1 already varied loading and TFE flow without trend, so the proposed re-test would not decide it. The [plug-flow model](../../calculations/titration-front-regime.md) added after the screen shows that the hiding condition is complete per-pass uptake on every titratable class (or equal uptake kinetics), not the sharp front as such, and that outlet-titrant breakthrough timing and a layered-versus-mixed bed test are the informative diagnostics. Status: a known point restated with a small unpublished consequence; a bounded calibration only, not a program. The screen text below is retained as written.

## Decision

**Do not promote a substantial program. Retain one bounded diagnostic: a regime test for in-situ (during-reaction) site titration in packed beds, calibrated on physical mixtures of known spectator fraction.** The diagnostic answers a question many groups face and cannot currently settle from their own data: whether a during-reaction titration count is a census of turnover-competent sites or a bed-average over all titratable sites. It is cheap and informative, but it is a reporting and interpretation correction, not a new method that yields working-site counts on its own. The stronger idea in this territory, a turnover-linked inactivator that labels only sites that complete the kinetically relevant step, is a hypothesis without a validated reagent; it is recorded as a conditional follow-on, not a program.

The other candidate methods in the assignment (feed/flow deconvolution of deactivation, intracrystalline concentration measurement, a Zr–O pair-fraction protocol, Bayesian identifiability reporting) are covered by direct prior art or are integrations of established tools; reasons are given in Section 9.

## 1. Practical problem, scale, and connection to Gounder/Iglesia expertise

A turnover rate is a rate divided by a defensible count of participating sites. Both groups built their kinetic practice on that requirement: Boudart's TOF definition, DTBP titration during reaction (Iglesia group since 2001), NH3 titration protocols that exclude inactive protons (Kester/Gounder 2018), open/closed Sn counts (Harris/Gounder 2016), and the 2026 Bates–Gounder review, which lists "reliable lower bounds for site-specific turnover frequencies when only a subset of measured sites is active" as an open problem. [[boudart1995-turnover-rates-in-heterogeneous-catalysis]] p.2-6 [[kester2018-ammonia-titration-methods-to-quantify]] p.1-3 [[harris2016-titration-and-quantification-of-open]] p.10-12 [[bates2026-chemical-kinetics-of-heterogeneous-catalysis]] p.34-36

The scale is the whole field: every site-normalized comparison between two catalysts, two pretreatments, or fresh and aged states rests on this denominator. Wrasman et al. note that changing an assumed probe stoichiometry from one to two molecules per site halves the count and doubles the reported turnover rate. [[wrasman2024-recommendations-for-improving-rigor-and]] p.15-17 Within this repository, the Ag program depends on Iyer and Bhan's TFE titration, whose three estimators differ by 2.7-fold (20.7, 29.7, 56.1 μmol g−1); the zirconia program depends on a Zr–O pair inventory whose participating fraction is unknown (Jaegers et al. report measured active-site densities far below the density of ideal pairs); and the zeolite program asks whether an assay changes the site population. [[iyer2023-chemical-titration-of-promoted-ag]] p.3-4 [[jaegers2024-heterolytic-ch-activation-routes-in]] p.8-10

## 2. The consequential uncertainty

**Uncertainty:** when a during-reaction titration in a packed bed gives a linear rate-versus-uptake curve, does the uptake at zero rate count turnover-competent sites, or all sites that bind the titrant?

**Why it is consequential.** If the count is a titratable total, then a rate-per-site difference between two samples cannot be assigned to a per-site (mechanistic or environmental) change versus a change in the fraction of titratable sites that work. That assignment is precisely the decision at stake in aging studies (site loss versus site slowing), promoter studies (more sites versus faster sites), and pretreatment studies. Davis (2018) describes linear rate decrease with titrant as evidence used for "identical site activity" on a Zn catalyst; that inference is the one this screen questions. [Davis 2018, AIChE J., DOI 10.1002/aic.16385; open copy at OSTI 1610280]

**Inference (derivation, not a literature result).** In a plug-flow bed at differential conversion, with a titrant that binds strongly and irreversibly, the titrant forms a sharp adsorption front. Behind the front every titratable site is covered regardless of its reactivity; ahead of it none is. Let the bed contain N_A active sites with per-site rate a and N_S titratable spectators (rate zero), uniformly mixed along the bed. When cumulative uptake is U, the fraction of the bed behind the front is U/(N_A + N_S) and

    rate(U) = a · N_A · [1 − U/(N_A + N_S)].

The curve is linear whatever the values of N_A and N_S. Its uptake intercept is N_A + N_S (the titratable total), and its slope is a·N_A/(N_A + N_S), the bed-average rate per titratable site. Heterogeneity of activity among titratable sites is therefore invisible in the front regime; linearity is a property of the reactor, not the surface. Only if the titrant redistributes according to site affinity (reversible or mobile titrant, gradientless reactor, dilute pulses that equilibrate along the bed) does the curve shape carry information about which sites work, and then only when affinity and activity are correlated. Sites that are indistinguishable to the titrant but differ in reactivity are not countable by any titration; this is a limit, not a design problem.

**Status of the two halves.** The arithmetic is established as a limiting case. Whether real during-reaction titrations operate in that limit is not established; Wrasman et al. say only that a *nonlinear* curve "may reflect multiple site activities or concentration fronts," and neither Davis 2018 nor the Iglesia-group titration papers we could access states that a front makes linear curves uninformative about heterogeneity. [[wrasman2024-recommendations-for-improving-rigor-and]] p.7 That gap, and the absence of any ground-truth calibration with known spectator fractions, is the open boundary.

## 4. Hypothesis, alternatives, and discriminating observations

**H1 (falsifiable).** Under ordinary during-reaction titration conditions (strongly bound organic base, packed bed, differential conversion), the rate-versus-uptake curve is linear with an uptake intercept equal to the total titratable site count and a slope equal to the bed-average rate per titratable site, independent of the spectator fraction. Prediction on mixtures with spectator fraction f_s: intercept invariant to f_s (per gram of titratable sites), apparent per-site rate scales as (1 − f_s), and the curve stays linear.

**A1 (redistribution).** The titrant equilibrates along the bed (reversible or mobile), so it covers sites in order of affinity. If active sites bind more strongly, the curve is convex and the early slope reports the active population; if spectators bind more strongly, an initial plateau appears. Prediction: curve shape changes with f_s and with titrant partial pressure; intercept still equals the titratable total.

**A2 (intracrystalline front).** The titrant is bed-uniform but crystal-shell-limited, so outer sites are poisoned first. Prediction: curve shape depends on crystal size at fixed f_s; convexity appears only if the reaction itself has intracrystalline gradients (a shell-poisoning transport diagnostic), otherwise linear.

**A3 (titrant-induced state change).** The titrant creates or removes sites (as DME does on ZrO2). Prediction: intercept depends on titrant exposure history and on return-to-reference after mild desorption; the count is not a property of the pre-titration state.

Discriminators: vary bed mass by 2× at fixed titrant molar flow (H1: time to zero rate scales with mass, curve unchanged when plotted against uptake per titratable site); vary titrant partial pressure by 3× (A1: shape changes; H1: no change); vary crystal size at fixed f_s (A2); return-to-reference after titrant removal and a closing titrant balance by TPD (A3). Mixtures with known f_s are the ground truth for the intercept and slope predictions.

## 5. Smallest informative first campaign

**Reaction and materials.** Methanol dehydration to DME on H-MFI at 415 K in the zero-order regime, where the rate is proportional to the working H+ count and product inhibition is weak; Kester et al. report zero-order DME rates of (20–26) × 10−3 mol (mol H+Al s)−1 there, with concentration gradients <15%. [[kester2018-ammonia-titration-methods-to-quantify]] p.7-8 Titrant: pyridine (enters MFI pores; DTBP does not). Ground-truth mixtures: H-MFI (Si/Al ≈ 40, crystallites ≤0.5 μm) blended with a titratable-but-inactive component at f_s = 0, 0.3, 0.6. Two spectator types test the affinity cases: B-MFI (weak sites, <1% of rate) for the weak-affinity case, and a Lewis-acid solid that binds pyridine strongly but gives negligible DME rate at 415 K (candidate: steamed MFI rich in extraframework Al, or γ-Al2O3; its DME rate must be measured first) for the strong-affinity case. Na-MFI and silica are bed-dilution controls with no titratable sites.

**Measurements.** Rate versus cumulative pyridine uptake from the inlet/outlet pyridine balance, closed by post-run TPD; DME carbon balance; the ln(conversion)–ln(space time) slope test for product inhibition [[harris2020-consequences-of-product-inhibition-in]] p.17-20; flow doubling at fixed conversion. [[flaherty2024-improving-the-rigor-and-reproducibility]] p.5-6

**Matched controls.** Bed mass 0.5×, 1×, 2× at fixed titrant flow; titrant pressure ×3; one larger-crystal H-MFI (≈2 μm) at f_s = 0.3; return-to-reference after mild purge; titrant-free time-on-stream drift on every bed.

**Replication.** Three independently loaded beds per mixture composition; independent mixtures prepared twice.

**Numerical targets (choices, not predictions).** Intercept precision within ±10% of the titratable total; ability to resolve an apparent per-site rate change of 30% (the f_s = 0.3 case) at three replicates.

**Decision informed.** If H1 holds, a during-reaction titration in a packed bed must be reported as "titratable total and bed-average rate," and a bed-mass/titrant-pressure invariance check becomes a reporting requirement; working fractions then require an affinity contrast (Kester-type) or a turnover-linked probe. If A1 holds under ordinary conditions, the curve shape can be used to bound the working fraction, and the protocol to extract it is the useful product.

**Transfer tests (second stage only if the first is decisive).** Re-run the Iyer–Bhan TFE titration with the bed-mass and titrant-pressure variations to test whether the 2.7-fold estimator spread is a front/adsorption-kinetics artifact [[iyer2023-chemical-titration-of-promoted-ag]] p.3-4; and apply the regime test to CO2/H2O titration of Zr–O pairs during propane dehydrogenation to learn whether the low measured active-site density is a titratable total or a working subset. [[jaegers2024-heterolytic-ch-activation-routes-in]] p.8-10

## 6. Conditional follow-on: a turnover-linked inactivator (hypothesis only)

**H2 (proposed, not established).** A reagent whose irreversible binding requires the same kinetically relevant elementary step as turnover labels only turnover-competent sites, in proportion to their per-site rate. Then rate versus inactivator uptake is linear for one site class with intercept N_A, and convex for a distribution of activities, because faster sites are labeled first. This is the enzymology active-site titration by mechanism-based inactivators; we found no heterogeneous-catalysis implementation that validates the matched-step requirement (web search 2026-09-16). Iyer–Bhan's TFE is the nearest case, but TFE deprotonation is not the epoxidation cycle. [[iyer2023-chemical-titration-of-promoted-ag]] p.2-5

**Tests that would qualify a reagent.** Inactivation rate constant per site proportional to the turnover rate across temperature and pressure; the same kinetic isotope effect on inactivation as on turnover; count at complete inactivation independent of inactivator pressure; on known mixtures, count = (1 − f_s)·N. **Reagent availability is the obstacle:** for Brønsted-acid methanol dehydration no candidate is evident; for heterolytic C–H activation on Zr–O pairs a terminal alkyne would give a stable acetylide, but its much higher C–H acidity would let weaker pairs react and overcount. Do not schedule this without a reagent that passes the proportionality and KIE tests.

## 7. Confidence judgments

- **Hypothesis H1.** High in the ideal limit (it is arithmetic). Moderate that ordinary titrations sit in that limit; adsorption kinetics, axial dispersion, and partial reversibility can blur the front, and the real regime must be measured.
- **Feasibility and informativeness.** High. One flow reactor, existing materials (B-MFI from the Kester synthesis or a commercial equivalent), and a closed titrant balance decide between H1 and A1–A3 in a few weeks. The result is informative in either direction.
- **Breadth of use and decision enabled.** Moderate. The regime test lets any group state what its in-situ count means and prevents assigning a rate difference to per-site chemistry when the spectator fraction may have changed. It does not by itself yield a working-site count; that requires an affinity contrast or a validated turnover-linked reagent.
- **Practical improvement for any process.** Low and indirect; this is measurement hygiene.
- **Originality.** The front limit is a simple consequence of plug-flow titration that may be known informally; no source found states it or calibrates it with known spectator mixtures. A "first" claim is not supported beyond that narrow point.

## 8. Benchmark and stop/pivot rule

**Benchmark.** Current practice: a linear during-reaction titration read as uniform activity with intercept = active sites (Davis 2018 usage), and the Harris 2016 pyridine-extrapolation versus CD3CN open-site agreement (within about 1.4×) as the best existing cross-check. [[harris2016-titration-and-quantification-of-open]] p.11-12

**Stop.** If the mixtures show, under ordinary titrant pressures and bed masses, that the intercept tracks working sites and the curve shape follows f_s (A1 holds), the front concern is moot for typical practice; record the result and stop. If H1 holds, adopt the bed-mass and titrant-pressure invariance check as a reporting requirement in `research/standards.md` (proposed change, not made here) and stop; do not expand into a synthesis or spectroscopy campaign.

**Pivot.** Only a reagent that passes the H2 qualification tests on the mixture ground truth justifies a working-site counting program. A new titrant, a new spectroscopic average, or a better fit to the same curves does not.

## 9. Candidates not promoted, with reasons

- **Feed/flow deconvolution of intrinsic deactivation from product inhibition, secondary reaction, and transport.** The toolkit exists piecewise: the ln X–ln τ slope and cofeed correction [[harris2020-consequences-of-product-inhibition-in]] p.17-20; Ar-purge recovery to 1.05× the initial rate and crystal-size series at fixed acid density [[bickel2023-influence-of-brnsted-acid-site]] p.4-6; occupied-pore Thiele descriptors that rank rates, secondary selectivity, and deactivation together [[cao2026-consequences-of-diffusion-enhanced-secondary]] p.8-14; qH+R2/D for lifetime [[hwang2019-effects-of-diffusional-constraints-on]] p.5-9; thermal desorption below 673 K versus oxidation for retained aromatics [[ezenwa2026-polymethylated-aromatics-deactivate-intracrystalline-acid]] p.8-10; flow, dilution, and pellet tests [[flaherty2024-improving-the-rigor-and-reproducibility]] p.5-10; the Thiele/effectiveness formalism for zeolites under diffusion and deactivation (Hullfish et al. 2023, ACS Catal., 10.1021/acscatal.3c03559); and reversible/irreversible MDA deactivation separated by pretreatment history (J. Catal. 2023, ScienceDirect PII S0021951723002804). A unified protocol would be a standards contribution, not a research result; the identifiability caveats in [aging-identifiability](../../calculations/aging-identifiability.md) show that endpoint-only data cannot separate these models without measured relaxation and recovery controls.
- **Intracrystalline concentrations during reaction.** IR microimaging of reactant and product profiles during catalytic conversion in a single porous particle is established (Titze et al. 2015, Angew. Chem. 54, 5060; PubMed 25720828), as are single-crystal fluorescence and MRI of pellet liquids [[zheng2023-operando-magnetic-resonance-imaging-of]] p.4-11. These need large crystals and specialist instruments; not broadly usable, and not a decision the ordinary kinetics laboratory can make.
- **A Zr–O pair-fraction protocol transferred to other oxides.** The pair inventory and its perturbation by titrants is the Iglesia group's own active line [[jaegers2024-heterolytic-ch-activation-routes-in]] p.13-15; the transferable question is exactly the working-fraction problem above, so it is subsumed by the retained diagnostic rather than a separate program.
- **Bayesian identifiability reporting for microkinetic fits.** Constrained estimation with SVD truncation [[ahmed2026-parameter-estimation-of-microkinetic-models]] p.5-15, Bayesian site identification and design [[walker2018-identifying-active-sites-of-the]] p.5-15 [[walker2020-chekipeuq-intro-2-harnessing-uncertainties]] p.1-10, model-based transient design with its mechanism-discrimination caveat [[yonge2024-model-based-design-of-experiments]] p.5-16, PRBS transients for practical identifiability (ACS Catal., 10.1021/acscatal.4c05157), and optimal design for identifiability and discrimination (arXiv 2506.11311) already cover the method; the remaining work is adoption, not discovery.
