# Ethane (and propane) oxidative dehydrogenation: is there a consequential open question left for an Iglesia-heritage kinetics program?

Candidate screen, 2026-09-16, under the [shared brief](README.md). No experiments were performed; nothing under `literature/` was changed. This is a screen, not a selected program.

## Correction after independent review

The original screen below is a historical assessment, not the current portfolio decision. The [selection-criteria review](../../reviews/program-selection-criteria-review.md) identified unsupported closure arguments: competing groups do not establish that a question is solved; a few selectivity points can be consequential; isotope provenance alone cannot rule out steam or transport effects. The [cyclic-oxide program](../program-development/cyclic-oxides.md) now reopens one part of this territory after a [primary-source audit](../../reviews/cyclic-oxide-program-review.md).

Brody's long-duration test used a heated cyclic packed bed. Autothermal operation was modeled and supported by measured exotherms; it was not demonstrated as a continuously producing heat-self-sufficient plant. The first 1,000 elapsed hours used one minute of ethane per 16-minute cycle and declining C2+ selectivity. Later optimized performance must not be assigned to the entire run. Remaining original qualitative saturation and small-gain judgments below are not accepted current selection rules.

## Original decision in one paragraph

**Do not promote a substantial program. Retain, at most, one bounded and conditional diagnostic.** The territory's headline uncertainties are either already answered in the open literature or are being worked by dedicated expert groups with better access to the relevant materials and reactors: boron ODH is established as surface-initiated, gas-phase-propagated radical chemistry whose reactor-volume dependence Hermans and the Lu/Xu groups have already quantified and modeled; Te loss from the M1 phase under oxygen-lean conditions is established (Mishanin 2016), and Shell's 2026 patent already stages a Te-free second bed to avoid it; chemical-looping ODH has a >1200 h heated cyclic test and an autothermal process model (Li) and a ZrO2 variant (Kondratenko); and pellet-scale transport, autothermal scale-up, forced dynamic operation and even homogeneous high-temperature ODH on M1 are the subject of a continuous 2021–2026 series from the Houston group (Bollini, Harold, Balakotaiah). The one question this screen finds genuinely open and decision-relevant is narrow: on the M1 phase at industrially relevant conversion, pressure and steam, does CO2 arise only through an ethylene-derived acetate intermediate (as the Clariant/Lercher kinetic model asserts by fit) rather than by direct ethane or ethylene combustion, and is that partition changed by intrapellet water and ethylene gradients? Resolving it with 13C tracers on a pellet-size series would decide whether steam cofeed level or pellet geometry is the right lever against the last few points of COx. That is an Iglesia-heritage measurement, but its practical upside is a few selectivity points on a process whose developers already hold proprietary pellet and steam data, and it requires a phase-pure M1 sample the repository has no verified access to. It does not justify a full program.

## 1. Practical problem, scale, and connection to the professors

Ethylene is the largest-volume organic chemical; steam cracking supplies it from an endothermic furnace at about 850–900 °C. Ethane ODH on the MoVTeNbOx M1 phase converts ethane and O2 to ethylene, acetic acid, CO and CO2 exothermically at 300–400 °C in a salt-cooled multitubular reactor; Linde's EDHOX page reports combined ethylene plus acetic acid selectivity above 93 % in a demonstration plant, CO2 recovered as a pure stream, and a claimed >60 % reduction in CO2 emissions versus an ethane cracker ([Linde EDHOX page](https://www.linde-engineering.com/products-and-services/process-plants/petrochemical-technologies/edhox-technology); [Clariant press release, 2021-10-07](https://www.clariant.com/en/Corporate/News/2021/10/Clariant-unveils-groundbreaking-catalysts-developed-jointly-with-Linde-Engineering-for-novel-ethylen)). Trade-press coverage of the same announcement quoted capacities from about 0.2 to 1.5 Mt/a per plant and commercialization around 2024–2025 ([ICIS, 2021](https://www.icis.com/explore/resources/news/2021/10/05/10691746/clariant-odh-catalysts-enabling-low-carbon-ethylene-production-expected-to-be-in-use-by-2024-2025-execs); the article body could not be retrieved, so the capacity and timing are unverified). Shell holds a 2026 US patent on a staged two-zone M1 process at 3–10 bara with a Te-free second zone ([US12540109B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12540109B2/en)). No commercial ODH plant start-up was verified in this screen.

What limits the process is not activity but the last several selectivity points: CO must be oxidized or separated, CO2 removed by amine wash, and every carbon atom lost to COx also consumes O2 and releases heat in a reactor whose thermal stability is the main scale-up hazard (Chen, Bollini, Balakotaiah 2021, [10.1002/aic.17168](https://doi.org/10.1002/aic.17168)). Melzer et al. state the target as single-pass ethylene yields above 60 % with minimal COx (Nat. Commun. 2019, [10.1038/s41467-019-11940-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11940-0), introduction).

Connection to Iglesia: the kinetic and isotopic framework for alkane ODH on dispersed VOx and MoOx — primary dehydrogenation (k1), primary alkane combustion (k2), secondary alkene combustion (k3), irreversible C–H activation, Mars–van Krevelen lattice-oxygen participation, and 13C-alkene cofeed tracers to partition COx — is Iglesia/Bell heritage (Chen, Iglesia, Bell 2000, [10.1021/jp002100x](https://doi.org/10.1021/jp002100x); Argyle, Chen, Bell, Iglesia 2002, [10.1021/jp0144552](https://doi.org/10.1021/jp0144552) and [10.1006/jcat.2002.3570](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcat.2002.3570)). Deshlahra, an Iglesia alumnus, applied that framework to M1 micropores (Annamalai et al. 2018, [10.1021/acscatal.8b01586](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.8b01586)). Connection to Gounder: methodological only — the transport-first diagnosis of "diffusion-enhanced secondary reactions" and bed heat-flux criteria in the local [Cao, Iglesia, Gounder 2026 note](../../../literature/papers/cao2026-consequences-of-diffusion-enhanced-secondary/paper.md) (p.3–5, p.13–14) transfers to ODH pellets; no Gounder material or reaction is involved. The local corpus holds one ODH paper (VOx/In2O3 CO2-ODH of propane, [jiang2022](../../../literature/papers/jiang2022-co2-assisted-oxidative-dehydrogenation-of/paper.md)), which is adjacent but not this chemistry.

## 2. The consequential uncertainty

Four candidate uncertainties from the brief were weighed. Three are closed or saturated (section 3 and section 9). The one retained:

**On the M1 phase at industrially relevant ethane conversion (≥50 %), pressure (3–10 bar) and steam, is CO2 formed only through an ethylene-derived surface acetate that decarboxylates (so that CO2 selectivity is set by the competition between acetate desorption as acetic acid and acetate decarboxylation), or does a direct ethane- or ethylene-combustion route contribute — and does intrapellet transport in industrial-size pellets change that partition?**

Why it is decision-relevant. The Clariant/TUM intrinsic model (Donaubauer et al. 2020, [10.1016/j.cej.2019.123195](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2019.123195)) states, by regression, that "carbon dioxide exclusively originates from decarboxylation of acetate species, while ethene selectively reacts to CO", with lattice oxygen cleaving C–H bonds and electrophilic surface oxygen forming C–O bonds. If that assignment holds at high conversion in pellets, then:

| Lever | Acetate-route model predicts | Classical secondary-combustion model (k3 route) predicts | Primary-combustion model (k2 route) predicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raising steam partial pressure | Shifts acetate to desorb as acetic acid: CO2 falls, acetic acid rises, CO roughly unchanged | CO and CO2 change together through site blocking/water inhibition (Argyle 2002 reports water inhibition of k1 on VOx) | Little effect on the CO2/CO ratio |
| Increasing pellet size at matched conversion | Interior is ethylene- and water-rich: CO (from ethylene) rises; CO2 rises less or not at all | Both CO and CO2 rise with pellet size (diffusion-enhanced secondary combustion) | Neither depends on pellet size |
| 13C2H4 cofeed at 50 % conversion | Label in CO2 equals label in acetic acid; label in CO higher | Label in CO and CO2 similar and high | Label in CO2 low (CO2 from ethane) |
| 13C-acetic acid cofeed | 13CO2 appears at a rate consistent with acetate readsorption/decarboxylation | Small | Small |
| Design consequence | Steam level and acetic-acid coproduction set CO2; thin-shell pellets cut CO only | Thin-shell pellets or O2 staging cut both COx | Only the catalyst composition (Te/Nb, V5+ fraction) helps |

The table is an unvalidated set of illustrative hypotheses, not consequences uniquely implied by route identity. Rate orders, coupled reaction networks, local fields and isotope-pool turnover can change these responses; the categorical statements that primary combustion has no pellet or steam lever are not justified.

Evidence labels: the Donaubauer assignment is an established model claim, not a tracer result; the predictions in the table are this screen's inferences from the respective models; whether the models diverge measurably at 3–10 bar in pellets is the open boundary.

### 3.4 Established, inferred, hypothesized, expected

- Established: irreversible C–H activation, MvK lattice oxygen, and coexisting primary and secondary COx routes on dispersed VOx/MoOx (Iglesia/Bell); Te loss under O2-lean conditions and its mitigation by pressure or Te-free staging; boron ODH as surface-initiated gas-phase radical chemistry with void-volume-dependent rates; M1 micropore confinement of ethane activation.
- Inference (this screen): the Donaubauer acetate-only CO2 assignment implies a steam lever that is distinct from the pellet-size lever; that distinction has not been tested with tracers on M1 at high conversion.
- Hypothesis: see section 4.
- Expectation (unvalidated): even if resolved, the practical gain is bounded by the few COx selectivity points that remain above ~93 % combined selectivity.

## 4. Falsifiable hypothesis, live alternatives, discriminating observations

**H1 (acetate route).** On Te-containing M1 at 3–6 bar and 340–400 °C, CO2 forms predominantly by decarboxylation of ethylene-derived surface acetate; CO forms from ethylene by a separate route. Predictions: (i) with 13C2H4 cofeed at ≥50 % ethane conversion, the 13C fraction in CO2 equals that in acetic acid within error and is lower than that in CO; (ii) raising steam from 0 to ~30 kPa lowers CO2 selectivity and raises acetic-acid selectivity at matched conversion while CO selectivity changes by less; (iii) at matched conversion, increasing particle size from powder (<0.1 mm) to 1–3 mm raises CO selectivity but changes CO2 by less than the steam effect.

**H2 (classical diffusion-enhanced secondary combustion).** CO and CO2 both arise from readsorbed ethylene by the k3-type route. Predictions: 13C fractions in CO and CO2 are equal; both rise together with particle size; steam affects both through inhibition.

**H3 (primary combustion at high conversion).** A substantial CO2 fraction forms directly from ethane (k2-type), so 13C label in CO2 is low, and neither steam nor particle size moves CO2 much.

Discriminating observations: label fractions in CO, CO2 and acetic acid from 13C2H4 and 13CH3-13COOH cofeeds at matched conversion; the CO2 versus CO response to steam and to particle size; selectivity–conversion extrapolation to zero conversion; measured Weisz–Prater and heat-flux criteria for each particle size. H1 and H2 diverge in the CO2 label and the particle-size response; H3 diverges in the label alone.

## 5. Smallest informative first campaign

- Material: one phase-pure Te-containing M1 batch (either from a collaborator or reproduced by the 190 °C hydrothermal route of Melzer 2019, with XRD phase purity and Rietveld M1 fraction as acceptance criteria) plus a matched Te-free MoVNb batch as the composition control (Melzer 2020 attributes selectivity loss without Te to ethene → COx on VOx). Bind or extrude the same powder into 1 and 3 mm particles; keep the powder fraction (<0.1 mm) as the transport-free reference. Material access is unverified.
- Conditions: 340–400 °C, 3–6 bar total, C2H6/O2 ≈ 2–3 with O2 conversion kept ≤90 % in every run to avoid the Mishanin Te-loss regime; 0–30 kPa steam. Quartz-diluted beds, measured axial temperature, heat-flux criterion after Cao et al. 2026 (local note p.4–5), Weisz–Prater estimate from measured rates and a 22DMB- or ethane-uptake diffusivity.
- Balances: carbon balance ≥97 %, oxygen balance, acetic-acid trapping and GC/MS quantification; label fractions by MS of CO, CO2, C2H4 and acetic acid.
- Replication: two independent M1 preparations; each condition in duplicate on separate bed loadings; reference run repeated at start and end of each series to detect drift (Te loss would show as a step change in selectivity and V-surface state).
- Decision informed: whether steam level (with acetic-acid coproduction) or pellet geometry is the lever against the residual CO2, and whether a Te-free second bed (Shell) trades CO2 for CO.
- Numerical targets are choices, not predictions: treat a ≥2-point CO2 selectivity change at matched conversion, reproduced across preparations, as consequential; smaller differences are within typical GC scatter and would not change a design.

## 6. Confidence judgments

- Hypothesis H1: plausible; it is the published Clariant/Lercher model assignment, but it rests on regression rather than tracers, and Argyle/Chen-style data on dispersed VOx show both primary and secondary COx routes coexisting. Moderate-to-low confidence that H1 survives unchanged at 50 % conversion in pellets.
- Feasibility and informativeness: moderate if a phase-pure M1 sample exists; low otherwise, because M1 synthesis, phase purity and Te retention are known failure points and a two-batch replication doubles that risk. The tracer design itself is well precedented and informative in any outcome (a low CO2 label would also falsify the current industrial model).
- Practical improvement: low. The process already exceeds 93 % combined selectivity in a demonstration plant; the achievable gain is a few points, the developers hold pellet and steam data, and Houston's reactor models would absorb any new rate constants without a new program.

## 7. Fair benchmark and stop/pivot rule

Benchmark: the EDHOX demonstration figure (combined ethylene plus acetic acid selectivity >93 %, 300–400 °C, multitubular) and the Donaubauer 2020 model as the published kinetic reference; Mishanin 2016 (85 % at 37 % conversion, 1 atm) as the open-literature lab baseline for a Te-containing M1.

The original stop rules are withdrawn. Low ethylene-derived label in CO2 constrains carbon provenance but does not rule out steam or transport effects. Similar CO/CO2 labeling does not establish that an existing pellet model quantitatively predicts the tested conditions. A universal two-percentage-point threshold has no demonstrated process basis here. Before executing this diagnostic, define the relevant model differences, isotope-pool controls, material stability and useful contrast from the actual reaction and process comparison. If Te loss changes the material during the experiment, resolve or bound that change before interpreting it as a pathway test. No current execution recommendation follows from correcting these logical errors.

## 8. Recommendation

**Do not promote.** Retain the section 5 tracer/pellet diagnostic as a documented, conditional option that is only worth executing if a phase-pure M1 sample becomes available at no synthesis cost to the group and an available reactor already supports pressurized, steam-cofed, isotope-labeled operation. Its practical ceiling is a few selectivity points on a process whose owners have proprietary data, and its scientific novelty is a tracer verification of an existing model rather than a new mechanism. The Gounder connection is methodological only; the Iglesia connection is heritage rather than current work.

## 9. Candidates weighed and set aside

- **Boron ODH: surface-initiated gas-phase radical chemistry vs. surface catalysis, and B–OH census as a selectivity predictor.** Closed to first order. Hermans (reactor parameters 2018; radical mechanism 2020; operando XRS 2023 showing rate ∝ void volume; microkinetic-to-process 2024; surface contribution 2025), Lu/Xu (Sci. Adv. 2019; Nat. Commun. 2023 orders 1 → 2; 2024 review), Zhang/Chen/Luo (2026 defect tuning of the gas-phase share) and the PEPICO radical detection (JACS 2023) already establish the hybrid mechanism and its reactor-volume dependence; boron leaching/volatility and its confinement fix are also published (Dorn 2020; Zhou 2021 Science, 210 h; Cai/Lu 2024). Post-bed volume and quench design are engineering consequences these groups are already drawing. No Gounder/Iglesia lever.
- **M1 surface reconstruction and Te loss under steam and lean O2.** Established mechanism (Mishanin 2016), established mitigations (pressure, Mishanin 2019; Te-free second zone, Shell 2026 patent), and an active Wachs/Trunschke surface-science line. Reversibility would be a materials question with an industrial answer already in practice.
- **Chemical-looping ODH advantage.** The original screen treated Li's long cyclic test/process model, Wachs's promoter work and Kondratenko's ZrO2 variant as closing the question. That conclusion is superseded by the recoverability program linked above; the ZrO2 lattice-oxygen aspect is already in the [oxide-pair screen](oxide-pair-dehydrogenation.md).
- **NiO/NiNbO (Lemonidou).** Mechanism, oxygen speciation and reactor design are published; the 2021 dual-site NiO/HY result shows the selectivity concept has moved on.
- **Supported VOx.** The Iglesia/Bell heritage is complete for dispersed VOx at atmospheric pressure; supported VOx is not the industrial candidate.
