# Candidate screen: one water-titration/cleaning rule for m-ZrO2 Zr–O pairs across ketonization, ethylbenzene dehydrogenation and propane dehydrogenation

Direction search, 2026-09-16. Screen only; not a selected program. No experiments performed; nothing under `literature/` changed. Follows the [shared brief](README.md). Companion screens: [oxide-pair dehydrogenation](oxide-pair-dehydrogenation.md) (site-identity dispute) and [liquid-phase oxygenate](liquid-phase-oxygenate.md) (ketonization water and lifetime). The existing [zirconia styrene pilot](../../programs/zirconia-styrene-oxygen-fate.md) owns the ethylbenzene cleaning question.

## Decision in one paragraph

**Do not promote. Retain at most one bounded transfer test inside the existing zirconia pilot, and drop the ketonization leg.** The proposed "single rule" has three parts: a site census, a titration/cleaning kinetic model, and a claim that its parameters transfer across reactions. The first two already exist and have already been transferred by Iglesia's group across propane, C2–C4 alkane, alkene, styrene and ethylbenzene chemistry on one material (one H2O-pulse census taken during propane dehydrogenation is used to normalize all of them). The third part fails on the ketonization leg by existing evidence: at 600–650 K with 1–25 kPa water the pairs would be fully hydroxylated under any binding constant compatible with the dehydrogenation data, yet ketonization runs at near-saturation carboxylate coverage with a water inhibition constant five to ten orders of magnitude weaker than any pair-water constant, and on TiO2 water is reported not to compete for Lewis sites at all. The one open, decision-relevant fragment is whether the ethylbenzene-fitted titration constant plus an independently measured propene cleaning constant predict the propane-dehydrogenation asymptote and the post-air-regeneration induction at 823–873 K. That is a confirmation test of Iglesia's own published model. Either outcome is worth recording; neither constitutes a substantial original program.

## 1. Practical problem, scale, connection

**Problem.** Chemically dehydroxylated m-ZrO2 exposes a minority population of low-coordination Zr–O Lewis acid–base pairs (0.55–0.56 nm⁻², against 8–12 nm⁻² total Zr–O pairs on low-index planes) that dehydrogenate alkanes and ethylbenzene and hydrogenate alkenes at rates comparable to Pt or Cr catalysts. Trace water titrates these pairs one-to-one; hydrocarbons and DME remove the bound water. [[jaegers2024-heterolytic-ch-activation-routes-in]] p.4-6, p.13-14 ([10.1021/jacs.4c07766](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.4c07766)); [[jaegers2025-hydrogenation-of-alkenes-cycloalkenes-and]] p.7 ([10.1021/acscatal.5c03523](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5c03523)); [[artsiusheuski2025-selective-ethylbenzene-dehydrogenation-to-styrene]] p.5-8 ([10.1021/acscatal.5c04904](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5c04904)). If the same oxide is to serve several processes, each needs a feed-water specification and a regeneration protocol. The three candidate processes differ by four orders of magnitude in water burden: ketonization of carboxylic acids at 600–650 K produces one water per ketone (1–25 kPa in NREL's kinetics); ethylbenzene dehydrogenation at 773 K is sensitive to 1–40 ppm; propane dehydrogenation at 823–873 K is regenerated in air, which forms water from coke, and Kondratenko's cyclic operation relies on in-situ activation by propane/propene rather than any drying step.

**Scale.** On-purpose propylene (Oleflex/Catofin), styrene (Fe–K, steam-diluted) and NREL's wet-waste ketonization step (pre-commercial) are all real. The stake here is not a new catalyst but a shared operating specification for an abundant-oxide family; the [oxide-pair screen](oxide-pair-dehydrogenation.md) §7 records that ZrO2-family productivity remains 1–3.5 kg propene kg⁻¹ h⁻¹, an order of magnitude below Pt systems, and the [liquid-phase screen](liquid-phase-oxygenate.md) §1 records that ketonization is capital-driven with catalyst cost minor.

**Connection.** The site census, the H2O-pulse titration during reaction, the DME desiccation and the titration/cleaning plug-flow model are all Iglesia's. Gounder's contribution would be methodological (titrant-based counting of Lewis sites and treatment of water as a reversible, countable poison), as in the [oxide-pair screen](oxide-pair-dehydrogenation.md) §1. Both connections are strong; that is also the originality problem.

## 2. The consequential uncertainty

**Question.** Is the working Zr–O pair population in each reaction set by one pair-specific water binding/removal chemistry whose parameters (site count, titration rate constant, cleaning rate constant and their temperature dependence) transfer across reactions, so that a rule measured in one reaction predicts water tolerance and non-oxidative regeneration in another? Or is water's effect reaction-specific: different titrant identity (hydroxyl pair, carboxylate, carbonate), different cleaning reagent, or restructuring at high temperature?

**Decisions it would change.** (i) Whether one feed-water specification and one scrubber sizing can be reused between ethylbenzene and propane dehydrogenation on the same oxide. (ii) Whether a dry purge or a product-alkene soak after air regeneration suffices, or a DME/CO pulse is required, and how long the induction period after regeneration will be at a given effluent-water tail. (iii) Whether the water census used for dehydrogenation counts anything relevant in ketonization (it is inferred below that it does not).

This is not a "characterize more" proposal only if the deliverable is a frozen quantitative prediction across reactions. That is the test applied in §5.

## 4. Hypothesis, alternatives, discriminating observations

**H1 (single transferable rule).** One site population (N ≈ 0.55 nm⁻² on this batch), one titration rate constant k_titr (near unit sticking, weakly temperature-dependent) and one water-removal chemistry with reagent-specific but independently measurable k_clean(reagent, T) govern the working pair fraction in all three reactions. Prediction: the ethylbenzene-fitted k_titr and an independently measured propene k_clean at 773 K, Arrhenius-extrapolated, predict the propane-dehydrogenation asymptote at 823–873 K for measured inlet water, and the post-air-regeneration induction from the measured effluent-water tail. In ketonization, H2O pulses of 0.55 nm⁻² scale would suppress the rate in proportion.

**H2 (reaction-specific titrant identity).** In ketonization the pairs are carboxylate-saturated; water acts on carboxylate speciation (Lin 2023) with K ≈ 0.4 bar⁻¹ (Miller 2022). H2O pulses at the dehydrogenation census scale have no resolvable effect on ketonization rate; the census is irrelevant there. In PDH after air regeneration, CO2 (carbonate) rather than water is the dominant residual titrant, so the water-only model under-predicts the induction. This screen already judges H2 established for the ketonization leg (inference from Miller 2022, Lin 2023, Wang and Iglesia 2017).

**H3 (kinetic control, non-transferable cleaning).** The site count and k_titr transfer, but k_clean(propene, 823–873 K) cannot be predicted from k_clean(ethylbenzene, 773 K) by any shared descriptor; each reagent needs its own measurement. This is Iglesia's own stated position (Jaegers 2024 §3.4) and is consistent with the 2026 PCCP finding that unique scaling relations are precluded for H–OH heterolysis at pairs. Under H3 the "rule" reduces to: measure k_clean for each reagent, then everything else transfers.

**H4 (thermal restructuring).** Above ~823 K the site count itself changes with history (sintering: 0.55 → 0.35 nm⁻² from 723 to 1108 K He treatment, Jaegers 2025 §3.2), so a census taken at 723 K does not transfer to PDH at 873 K after repeated air regenerations at 873 K. Prediction: re-titration after each regeneration cycle shows monotonic loss unrelated to water flux.

**Discriminators.** (i) Frozen prediction of the PDH asymptote versus measured inlet water (H1/H3 vs failure). (ii) Re-titrated site count after each regeneration (H4). (iii) Effluent H2O and CO2 during the post-regeneration purge and induction, with the model run on both titrants (H2 in PDH). (iv) H2O-pulse response during ketonization at matched pulse size (H2 in ketonization; expected null).

## 5. Smallest informative first campaign

If the transfer test is run at all, it should be an appendix to the styrene pilot on the same batch and apparatus (differential quartz reactor, 1:20–1:50 quartz dilution, 5 ppb O2/H2O scrubber, sample-loop H2O pulsing, online MS/GC, independent inlet/outlet water measurement already required by the [pilot](../../programs/zirconia-styrene-oxygen-fate.md) Aim 1).

Materials: one hydrothermal m-ZrO2 batch (~130 m² g⁻¹), two independent bed loadings per condition. No new synthesis.

Sequence:
1. Census: H2O-pulse titration during propane dehydrogenation at 723 K after DME (N per gram, replicate scatter).
2. Ethylbenzene leg (already in the pilot): asymptotic rate versus independently measured inlet water at 773 K; fit k_titr and k_clean(EB). Report the water balance, not the SI's inventory-derived ppm.
3. Propene cleaning constant at 773 K: partially titrate with a measured H2O dose, then expose to propene/H2 at fixed pressure without propane; follow rate recovery through short propane assays, with the assay-validity check the pilot already prescribes (the assay reagent can itself clean). Repeat at 823 K for the Arrhenius slope.
4. **Freeze** the prediction: asymptotic PDH rate fraction at 823 K for two measured inlet-water levels (e.g., ~1 and ~10 ppm by measured flux), using N, k_titr(EB-fitted) and k_clean(propene) only. Then measure.
5. Regeneration leg: coke under 40 vol% propane at 823 K for one cycle, burn in 4% O2/He at 873 K (patent protocol), purge in He while measuring effluent H2O and CO2, then restart propane. Freeze the predicted induction time from the measured water/CO2 tail and k_clean(propene); measure the induction; re-titrate N afterwards.
6. Ketonization control (one experiment, not a leg): hexanoic acid at 623 K on the same batch, H2O pulses of the census scale. Expected null response at the 0.55 nm⁻² scale (H2). If instead a proportional suppression appears, the working-state inference in §3 is wrong and the ketonization leg reopens.

Controls and balances: quartz-only blanks for every pulse; interleaved return-to-DME references; carbon balance by GC; water balance from measured inlet/outlet; transport checks by dilution and particle size at the highest rate; Chin criterion as in Jaegers 2024 SI S4. Numerical thresholds (for example "prediction within 2× replicate scatter") are choices to be set from the measured scatter, not predictions.

Decision informed: whether one feed-water specification can be reused between the two dehydrogenations, and whether a dry purge or propene soak after air burn replaces a DME pulse. The ketonization decision is already made by inference (§3); the control only guards that inference.

## 6. Thermodynamic plausibility and an illustrative bound

**Assumptions (illustrative, not a prediction).** One-to-one dissociative water binding at a pair, Langmuir form θ_w = KP/(1+KP), K = exp[−(ΔH − TΔS)/RT] with standard state 1 bar; ΔS = −170 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ (loss of most gas-phase entropy of water; the actual value for a dissociated H⁺/OH⁻ pair is not measured); no competing adsorbate; no kinetic term. Four ΔH values bracket the sources: A = −132 kJ mol⁻¹ (DFT strongest pair, Jaegers 2025 §2.4); B = −165 (upper end of the −140 to −165 range cited in Jaegers 2024 introduction; also the upper end of Ushakov–Navrotsky's −110 to −170 integral calorimetry); C = −210 (Ammal–Heyden ΔE = −2.27 eV less an assumed ~10 kJ mol⁻¹ zero-point/thermal correction); D = −235, back-calculated so that ΔG(773 K) = 0 at 10⁻⁷ bar, Ammal–Heyden's stated hydroxylation threshold.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ketonization 623 K, 1 kPa H2O | 0.61 | >0.99 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| Ketonization 623 K, 25 kPa H2O | 0.97 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| EB DH 773 K, 0.1 ppm (scrubber residual) | <10⁻⁴ | <10⁻⁴ | 0.02 | 0.50 |
| EB DH 773 K, 1 ppm | <10⁻⁴ | 2×10⁻⁴ | 0.17 | 0.91 |
| EB DH 773 K, 4 ppm | <10⁻⁴ | 7×10⁻⁴ | 0.45 | 0.98 |
| EB DH 773 K, 43 ppm | <10⁻⁴ | 0.008 | 0.90 | >0.99 |
| PDH 823 K, 1 ppm | <10⁻⁴ | <10⁻⁴ | 0.03 | 0.52 |
| PDH 873 K, 1 ppm | <10⁻⁴ | <10⁻⁴ | 0.005 | 0.13 |
| PDH 873 K, 100 ppm (purge tail after air burn) | <10⁻⁴ | 0.001 | 0.33 | 0.94 |
| PDH 873 K, 1% (early regeneration effluent) | 0.001 | 0.09 | 0.98 | >0.99 |

Values are equilibrium titrated fractions θ_w. Computed in this session; the arithmetic is reproducible from the stated formula.

**What the bound shows.**

1. *The equilibrium parameters are unknown to within ~100 kJ mol⁻¹, and that range spans every answer.* At the experimentally studied condition (773 K, 4 ppm) θ_w runs from below 10⁻⁴ (A, B) to 0.98 (D). Any "single rule" written as an equilibrium isotherm is therefore unconstrained by the available thermochemistry.

2. *The experimental facts are inconsistent with equilibrium control of the dehydrogenation population, whichever ΔH is right.* Sets A and B predict no titration at ppm water, yet rates fall in proportion to water dosed with roughly unit sticking (full titration of the 2.4 μmol inventory in ~5 h at 4 ppm and 50 mL min⁻¹, matching the SI's linear-decay times of 1.7 and 5.9 ks at 40 and 10 ppm). Sets C and D predict near-complete titration at 4 ppm, yet 70% of the initial rate persists. The TPD shows the relevant water leaving only between 723 and 1108 K at 0.167 K s⁻¹; a first-order Redhead estimate for a feature peaking near 900 K gives a desorption barrier of ~260 kJ mol⁻¹ (assumption: ν = 10¹³ s⁻¹; hydroxyl recombination is second order and can be diffusion-limited, so this is indicative only). By contrast Ammal–Heyden's G_act = 1.03 eV for water removal from the adatom gives k_des ~ 3×10⁶ s⁻¹ at 773 K, which would equilibrate in microseconds. So either the adatom is not the titrated site, or its desorption barrier is far too low. Either way, the working population is a kinetic balance of flux-limited titration against reagent cleaning, as Artsiusheuski's model states (χ ≫ 1, asymptote set by inlet water conversion, no equilibrium constant appears). *The transferable object, if any, is a kinetic parameter set, not a binding equilibrium.*

3. *Ketonization cannot share the rule.* With any K above ~10² bar⁻¹ (every set except A at the lowest water) the pairs would be fully hydroxylated at 1–25 kPa water. Ketonization proceeds at those pressures with a measured water constant of 0.0038 kPa⁻¹ (0.38 bar⁻¹, ΔH −34 kJ mol⁻¹), five to ten orders of magnitude below any pair-water constant in the table, and on TiO2 water is reported not to compete for Lewis sites at all. The working pair in ketonization is carboxylate-covered; water competes with or modifies carboxylates, not bare pairs. A water census of 0.55 nm⁻² has no reason to count the ketonization-relevant population. This is inference from three independent sources, not a measured result on this batch.

4. *Between the two dehydrogenations the rule is plausible but is the published model.* Sets C/D predict that at 823–873 K a purge tail of ~100 ppm keeps a large fraction titrated until propene cleaning restores it; that is precisely Kondratenko's in-situ activation and the BASF patent's 6 min rise, both already observed. The predictive content that remains is k_clean(propene, T), which Jaegers 2024 states is reagent- and temperature-specific.

**Site density note.** The 0.55–0.62 nm⁻² population is ~5–8% of the 8–12 nm⁻² Zr–O pairs. The bound above applies to that minority; the majority pairs bind water more weakly (−30 to −132 kJ mol⁻¹ range in the DFT survey) and are irrelevant to the census. A single rule would have to hold for the minority population specifically, which is the population whose structure remains inferred (edge/corner sites; Jaegers 2024 §3.7).

## 7. Confidence judgments

- **Hypothesis H1 (one transferable rule across all three reactions):** low. The ketonization leg is contradicted by established kinetics and spectroscopy on ZrO2 and TiO2; the thermochemistry cannot constrain an equilibrium rule; the group's own theory reports that unique scaling relations for H–OH heterolysis at pairs are precluded. Restricted to the two dehydrogenations with a reagent-specific cleaning constant (H3), the hypothesis is plausible and is already the published framework.
- **Feasibility and informativeness:** high for the two-dehydrogenation transfer and the regeneration leg, because the apparatus, census and model exist and the pilot already requires measured water; the frozen prediction is cheap. The ketonization control is feasible but expected to be null. Informativeness is bounded: a pass confirms Iglesia's model; a fail records that k_clean must be measured per reagent, which the authors already state.
- **Practical improvement:** low. Sub-ppm feed water and 5 ppb scrubbers are already practiced; Kondratenko already cycles 70 times with no drying step; the BASF patent already specifies air regeneration plus purge. A shared specification between two dehydrogenations is a convenience, not a demonstrated gain in output, lifetime or cost.

## 8. Fair benchmark and stop/pivot rule

**Benchmark.** For water tolerance: Artsiusheuski's model prediction (95% of initial rate at 1 ppm, 70% at 4 ppm, 773 K) and the ~100 ppb residual titrant level behind a 5 ppb scrubber. For regeneration: Kondratenko's 70-cycle operation with in-situ activation and no drying, and the patent's 4% O2/He at 873 K plus purge with a 6 min rise to peak rate. For ketonization: Miller's rate law with K_H2O = 0.0038 kPa⁻¹ and NREL's >100 h dry-feed run with air regeneration at 500 °C. Any claim must be compared on complete-cycle output including purge and induction time, not on initial rate.

**Stop.** Stop the cross-reaction framing now for ketonization. For the dehydrogenation pair, stop after step 4–5 of §5 whatever the result: if the frozen prediction holds within replicate scatter, record the confirmation and reuse the specification; if it fails, record that k_clean is reaction-specific and that a census taken at 723 K does not survive 873 K regeneration (re-titration result). Neither outcome opens a program. **Pivot** only if the regeneration leg shows a resolved CO2-dominated induction that the water-only model cannot describe and that a purge-length change removes; that would be a bounded operating result inside the [PDH regeneration diagnostic](../../reviews/dehydrogenation-fresh-value.md), not a new direction.

## 9. Recommendation

**Do not promote.** Assessed against the four questions in the brief:

(a) *Genuine cross-reaction program or restatement?* Mostly restatement. The census, the pulse-titration method, the titration/cleaning model and the reuse of one census across reactions are all published by Iglesia's group (Jaegers 2024, 2025; Artsiusheuski 2025). The group also states the reagent- and temperature-specificity of cleaning and, in 2026, that a unique scaling relation for H–OH heterolysis at pairs is precluded. No prior work performs a frozen cross-reaction prediction, but the missing step is a confirmation experiment, not an open scientific contest.

(b) *Is a single rule thermodynamically plausible?* No, as an equilibrium rule: the binding enthalpy is uncertain by ~100 kJ mol⁻¹, which spans full and negligible titration at the studied condition; the experiment shows flux-limited titration balanced by reagent cleaning, so the working population is kinetic. Across the ketonization boundary it is implausible on established evidence: water there is a weak, non-competitive inhibitor of carboxylate-saturated pairs.

(c) *Smallest decisive campaign.* One batch, one census, ethylbenzene-fitted k_titr, propene k_clean measured at 773–823 K, a frozen PDH asymptote and post-regeneration induction prediction, re-titration after regeneration, one ketonization pulse control. Falsifiers: prediction outside replicate scatter; site count drift after regeneration; a CO2-dominated induction; or (unexpectedly) a proportional water-pulse response in ketonization.

(d) *Substantial original program by the repository's standard?* No. It should remain, at most, a diagnostic appendix to the [zirconia pilot](../../programs/zirconia-styrene-oxygen-fate.md) and the [oxide-pair screen](oxide-pair-dehydrogenation.md) regeneration branch, run only on already-commissioned apparatus, and only after the pilot's measured-water reference exists. The ketonization leg should not be run as part of this idea; its own water/lifetime question is handled by the [liquid-phase screen](liquid-phase-oxygenate.md).
