# Direction screen: retained polymethylated aromatics versus coke as the deactivating population in methanol/DME-to-hydrocarbons, and the dry-desorption versus oxidative-burn regeneration decision

Date: 2026-09-16 (third wave). Candidate territory: item 2 of Section 4 of the [portfolio review](../../reviews/direction-search-portfolio-review.md). Written to that review's proposed "breadth of consequence" bar (Section 1): a program is substantial only if its central uncertainty, resolved either way, changes decisions in at least two classes (material / operating policy / regeneration / measurement-reporting) or one class across more than one process; the first campaign is decisive on standard apparatus; the *knowledge* is absent from the literature after a documented search; and the expansion gate is a frozen prospective prediction. This is a screen, not a selected program.

**Decision: do not promote to a substantial program.** The qualitative knowledge is occupied: a desorbable, non-oxidatively removable population of retained aromatics is established on MFI at low temperature (Schulz 1999/2018; Ezenwa/Gounder 2026; Bickel/Gounder 2023), an H2-removable "internal soft coke" fraction is established on MFI at MTH temperature (Lee and Choi 2019), and on CHA/SAPO-34 the non-oxidative recovery options (high-pressure H2, high-pressure steam, steam partial regeneration) are published and, for steam, patented and piloted by DICP. The one negative that matters most for the proposed dry swing is also published: an inert sweep of coked SAPO-34 at 953 K leaves a catalyst that converts methanol poorly (DICP 2021). What remains open is quantitative (the recoverable fraction of lost activity, with site accounting, at industrially relevant conversion and time on stream, and its dependence on crystal size and zoning). That question changes a regeneration decision only for fixed-bed MTP/MTG/ethanol-to-hydrocarbons units, and the expected answer at the end of a fixed-bed cycle is "small", because the population that ends a cycle at 673–773 K is external and polycyclic, not polymethylbenzene. **Retain, at most, a bounded measurement diagnostic**: a sequential recovery ladder (inert, H2, O2) with site-count accounting on one MFI series, run as a frozen-prediction test of the Cao–Iglesia–Gounder occupancy-transport framework. Do not build a program on it.

Labels: **E** established by a cited source as read here; **I** inference; **H** hypothesis; **X** unvalidated expectation. Search-status caveats are in the last section.

## 1. Practical problem, scale, and connection to Gounder/Iglesia

**Process class.** Methanol- and DME-to-hydrocarbons on acid zeolites: fluidized-bed MTO on SAPO-34 (UOP/Hydro; DICP DMTO, first commercial unit 2010 and seven DMTO units by 2015 per [Tian 2015](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5b00007), accessible text; "more than 20 MTO units" by 2021 per the introduction of [Nat. Commun. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-24403-2), open text), fixed-bed MTP on ZSM-5 (Lurgi/Air Liquide), fixed-bed MTG on ZSM-5 (ExxonMobil, practiced in China by JAMG per Wikipedia, page-level), ethanol-to-hydrocarbons and DME-to-olefins at smaller scale. All deactivate by carbon retention and are regenerated by burning it. **E** for the fluidized MTO: continuous circulation between reactor and regenerator with coke removal by air combustion or steam gasification ([DICP Nat. Commun. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-20193-1), abstract and introduction, open text via Europe PMC). **E** for fixed-bed MTP at laboratory scale: in situ air regeneration at 723–748 K for 50 h between cycles; lifetime per cycle up to 658 h; 29.3% of framework Al converted to extra-framework Al by the fifth cycle ([Liao 2018](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.8b02114), abstract). Industrial Lurgi MTP cycle practice (number of parallel reactors, cycle hours, burn conditions) could not be verified this session (all search engines and the Air Liquide page refused the fetch); the statement that it is a fixed-bed process with periodic in situ burn is from memory and is **unverified**.

**Why regeneration matters.** Two costs. (i) Carbon: coke combustion returns feed carbon as CO2; one 2026 review-style introduction puts this at up to 8% of total feedstock ([Angew. 2026 in situ XPS paper](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC13411610), introduction, citing its ref. 27; the underlying number is not verified here). (ii) Catalyst life: every burn produces steam at high temperature and dealuminates the zeolite. **E**: commercial ZSM-5 loses about 22% of framework Al and about 50% of acid sites after 10 reaction/O2-regeneration cycles at 673 K, and 29%/75% after 6 cycles at 773 K, with reaction-generated steam the main driver and retained coke partly protective (local note [wang2024](../../../literature/papers/wang2024-structural-changes-of-zsm-5/paper.md) p.7–13). A regeneration step that removed the reversible part of the deactivating inventory below ~673 K without oxygen would, if that part were large, lengthen fixed-bed cycles, cut the number of burns per catalyst life, and return the removed carbon as product rather than CO2. That is the practical stake. It applies to fixed-bed units; the fluidized MTO units already regenerate continuously and DICP has already moved the regeneration chemistry from air to steam (Section 3), so a dry swing has no obvious place there.

**Connection to the two groups.** Gounder's toluene-methylation deactivation study supplies the cleanest in-reactor accounting of a retained aromatic population (external-site poisoning with DTBP, in-bed TPD with product identification, full rate recovery after inert treatment; local note [ezenwa2026](../../../literature/papers/ezenwa2026-polymethylated-aromatics-deactivate-intracrystalline-acid/paper.md) p.6–10). Cao, Iglesia and Gounder supply the occupancy-corrected transport framework that predicts how retained-product gradients scale with H+ density and crystal size and couple to deactivation constants (local note [cao2026](../../../literature/papers/cao2026-consequences-of-diffusion-enhanced-secondary/paper.md) p.8–14). Bickel and Gounder established inert-purge recovery and its crystal-size dependence in propene oligomerization ([bickel2023](../../../literature/papers/bickel2023-influence-of-brnsted-acid-site/paper.md) p.4–6). Diaz Arroyo and Gounder showed that a passivated rim shifts the deposit to the exterior and makes inert recovery fail ([arroyo2025](../../../literature/papers/arroyo2025-passivation-of-brnsted-acid-sites/paper.md) p.10–11). Bhan's SAPO-34 transport parameter ([hwang2019](../../../literature/papers/hwang2019-effects-of-diffusional-constraints-on/paper.md)) is the CHA counterpart.

## 2. The consequential uncertainty

**Question.** At industrially relevant MTH conditions (673–773 K, near-complete conversion, hundreds of hours), what fraction of the lost activity, expressed per remaining Brønsted site, is recoverable by (a) an inert sweep at or below 673 K and (b) a dilute-H2 treatment at or below 673 K, before oxidation is required; and does that fraction depend on framework (MFI vs CHA), crystal size, Al zoning, or external passivation in the way the occupancy-transport framework predicts?

**Decisions it would change (breadth-bar test).**

| Class | Decision | Changed by which outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Regeneration | Fixed-bed MTP/MTG/ETH: insert an inert or H2 swing at reaction temperature before the burn, or lengthen the cycle by a mid-cycle swing, versus burn only | Only if the swing-recoverable fraction at the cycle end-point is large; if small, burn-only stands |
| Measurement-reporting | Report deactivation as reversible occlusion plus irreversible site loss, with a recovery ladder and site count, rather than as conversion-versus-time or total turnovers | Either outcome changes reporting, because current MTH metrics (total turnovers, site-loss yield) do not separate the two |
| Material | Crystal size and zoning chosen to shift the deposit from external polycyclic to internal desorbable | Only if internal desorbable inventory is the cycle-limiting population, which the evidence below argues against at 673–773 K |
| Operating | H2 co-feed pressure | Already decided by Bhan/DICP prior art (prevention); not changed by this question |

Nominally two classes across three fixed-bed processes: the breadth criterion is met on paper. The next two sections test whether the knowledge is absent and whether the answer is genuinely uncertain.

### 3a. What Gounder 2026 and Cao 2026 establish, and what they do not

- **E** (Ezenwa 2026, original text checked at fulltext lines 257–289; note p.4–9): at 403 K, 4 kPa toluene/66 kPa DME, below 2% conversion, MFI rates fall 80–93% in 7.5 h; DTBP shows intracrystalline sites carry the loss; in-bed He TPD releases 1.9 C2–3 and 2.0 C6–10 aromatics per unit cell (1.4 aromatics per H+), all eluting below 673 K, in a 1:1 ratio consistent with one dealkylation of an average C10–C11 polymethylbenzene; re-introducing the feed after the He ramp to 853 K gives initial rates within 5% of fresh, and 5% O2/He also restores them. The authors explicitly contrast this with MTH at 573–773 K, where coke-producing reaction pathways are active and polycyclic species require O2 or H2 at high temperature (fulltext lines 289, 307).
- **I**: the result therefore establishes the mechanism *where dealkylation is off* (403 K). It does not measure the desorbable fraction under MTH conditions, at any conversion above 2%, on CHA, or as a function of crystal size or zoning. The paper's own MTH statement is an inference from literature, not a measurement.
- **E** (Cao 2026, note p.6–14): first-order deactivation constants rise from 2.6 to 23 × 10−3 ks−1 with the occupied-pore structural Thiele parameter Ξ2* across four H-MFI (Si/Al 18–140) in ethene oligomerization at 623 K; the framework predicts that retained heavier products are faster co-reactants and H-donors, so gradients raise both rate and deactivation. It contains no regeneration or purge experiment (fulltext grep for regenerat/purge/recover returned only the deactivation-constant discussion).
- **E** (Bickel 2023, fulltext lines 169–171): Ar purge at 503 K for 47 ks restores propene dimerization rates to about 1.05× initial on MFI with 1.3 and 5.7 H+ per unit cell; recovery attributed to reversible alkene occlusion, not polyaromatics.
- **E** (Arroyo 2025, note p.10–11): an inert 503 K treatment restores the bare core to about 1.1× initial but the Si-shell particle only about 1.5× its deactivated rate; 823 K oxidation is needed for the shell, attributed to refractory external carbon.
- **I**: together the Gounder/Iglesia work establishes that *below the dealkylation threshold* the deactivating population is desorbable and its size is set by site count and occupancy-transport, and that moving sites inward (passivation) moves the deposit outward and defeats inert recovery. None of it is at MTH temperature or conversion.

## 4. Hypothesis, alternatives, discriminating observations

**H1 (falsifiable, the transport-framework hypothesis).** At 673 K and near-complete conversion, the fraction of lost site-normalized activity recovered by an inert sweep at reaction temperature scales with the occupied-pore structural Thiele parameter (H+ density × R²/De under reaction occupancy) across an MFI series, because larger gradients retain more slow-diffusing but still dealkylatable methylated aromatics; the additional fraction recovered by dilute H2 at ≤673 K is small and independent of that parameter; and the O2-only remainder grows with time on stream. On CHA/SAPO-34 the inert-recoverable fraction is near zero at any industrially relevant coke loading because the cage-trapped population is methylnaphthalene and larger.

**A1 (external-deposit control).** At the end of a fixed-bed cycle the activity loss is dominated by external polycyclic carbon that blocks pore mouths (Wan 2018; Angew. 2026 XPS introduction; Arroyo 2025 for the passivated case). Then the inert- and H2-recoverable fractions are small for every sample regardless of Ξ2*, and the only material lever is crystal size (already established). Discriminator: recovery fraction independent of crystal size at fixed H+ density; TPO shows the removed carbon is predominantly the low-temperature (external, accessible) peak; DTBP or bulky-probe external-site titration before/after the sweep.

**A2 (self-cleaning at reaction temperature).** Above ~573 K, dealkylation keeps the polymethylbenzene inventory at steady state (Schulz 2018), so an inert sweep at reaction temperature removes only what continuous operation already removes; the "recoverable" fraction is an artifact of transient re-equilibration of the hydrocarbon pool rather than removal of a deactivating population. Discriminator: the sweep-recovered activity decays back to the pre-sweep value within the induction time of the pool (minutes to tens of minutes), and the carbon released during the sweep is much smaller than the retained inventory by TPO.

**A3 (irreversible site loss dominates).** Reaction-generated steam dealuminates during the cycle itself (Wang 2024), so the site count, not occlusion, falls; no recovery step short of nothing restores it. Discriminator: NH3 titration or pyridine IR of the site count before and after each ladder step; recovered rate per remaining site equals fresh per-site rate.

**A4 (H2 works only at pressure).** H2 removal of formed polycyclics requires >1 MPa (Zhao 2019; DICP 2021 barrier estimate). Discriminator: compare the H2 step at 1 bar and at the highest pressure the standard rig allows; if only the latter recovers, the "dry swing" collapses into the established high-pressure H2 co-feed operating policy.

H1 is **H**; A1–A4 are all live and each has published support (**E** for their ingredients). The expected outcome (**X**) is that A1 and A2 together explain most of the observation at cycle end, leaving H1 true but small.

## 5. Smallest informative first campaign

**Materials.** One existing H-MFI series with independently varied H+ density and crystal size (the Bickel 2023 or Cao 2026 sets, if in hand; otherwise two commercial ZSM-5 at Si/Al ~15 and ~40 plus one large-crystal sample) and one commercial SAPO-34 (or H-SSZ-13) batch. No new synthesis. Controls: an Si-passivated core-shell MFI (Arroyo 2025 type) as the external-deposit reference, and a sham sequence (feed cut, reaction-temperature hold, no gas change) for the A2 test.

**Reaction.** Methanol (or DME, to test transfer) at 673 K, ~16–20 kPa oxygenate, space velocity set for >95% initial conversion, in an isothermal diluted bed with internal thermocouple; run to a predeclared conversion end-point (for example 80%) and, on a second aliquot, to half that time on stream, so the recoverable fraction is measured at two points in the cycle.

**Recovery ladder, in the bed, in sequence.** (i) Inert sweep at 673 K until effluent hydrocarbons fall below a stated bound, with heated-line GC quantification of everything released; (ii) 5% H2 at 673 K, same accounting (CH4 and light alkanes distinguished from desorbed aromatics); (iii) if the standard rig permits, the H2 step repeated at elevated pressure (A4); (iv) TPO in 5% O2 to 823 K with CO/CO2 integration. After steps (i), (ii) and (iv), return to reaction conditions and measure initial site-normalized rate and selectivity for one hour; after (iv) also NH3 titration of the site count (A3). Carbon balance: released carbon per step plus TPO carbon must equal the retained inventory of a sacrificial matched aliquot by TGA within a stated tolerance.

**Transport checks.** Bed dilution and flow-rate invariance at the initial condition; Weisz–Prater on the fresh sample; crystal-size series at fixed H+ density is itself the transport variable.

**Replication.** Independent beds (not repeats of one bed) for the decisive contrast (largest versus smallest Ξ2* at the cycle end-point).

**Frozen prediction (expansion gate).** Before the CHA and the second-time-point runs, freeze the predicted inert-recoverable fraction for an untested crystal size (or the CHA batch) from the fitted Ξ2* dependence on the first three MFI samples. Promote only if the prediction holds and the fraction at the cycle end-point exceeds a predeclared threshold that would justify a swing in a fixed-bed schedule (a design choice, set from the burn-count reduction needed to offset the added downtime and the dealumination per burn from Liao 2018/Wang 2024; not a literature number).

**Decision informed.** Whether a fixed-bed MTP/MTG/ETH schedule should include a non-oxidative swing; whether MTH deactivation reports should separate recoverable occlusion from site loss; whether crystal size has any additional lever through the recoverable fraction beyond its established lifetime effect.

Duration: weeks on one flow reactor with online GC, TPO and NH3 titration; standard apparatus.

## 6. Confidence judgments

- **Hypothesis (H1).** Low-to-moderate that the inert-recoverable fraction scales with Ξ2* on MFI early in a cycle (it follows from Bickel/Cao and Schulz); low that it is large at the cycle end-point, because Wan 2018, Lee and Choi 2019, Borodina 2017 and the 2026 XPS work all place the end-of-cycle population in polycyclic or external carbon. Near zero for CHA at industrial coke loading, given the DICP N2-sweep negative.
- **Feasibility and informativeness.** High: everything is standard, and every alternative (A1–A4) has a clean discriminator in the same run. The informative negative ("recoverable fraction at cycle end is below threshold; deactivation reports should still separate occlusion from site loss") is nearly certain to be obtained and is worth recording as a reporting standard, but it is not a program.
- **Practical improvement.** Low. Fluidized MTO does not need it and has already adopted steam partial regeneration. For fixed-bed MTP/MTG the benefit exists only if the recoverable fraction at cycle end is large, which the prior art argues against; and the operating alternative (H2 co-feed) is established and patented territory (Bhan; DICP). No commercial fixed-bed unit's cycle practice could be verified this session.

## 7. Benchmark and stop/pivot rule

**Benchmark.** Burn-only cycling on the same catalyst at the same end-point conversion (Liao 2018 protocol), scored as cumulative product per gram over a fixed number of cycles including regeneration time, framework-Al retained (27Al NMR or NH3 site count), and feed carbon lost as CO2. A swing earns its place only if it reduces burns per unit product at equal or better site retention.

**Stop.** Close the diagnostic if the inert- plus 1-bar-H2-recoverable fraction of lost site-normalized activity at the cycle end-point is below the predeclared threshold on every MFI sample; or if the sweep-recovered activity relaxes back within the pool induction time (A2); or if the site count after the ladder equals the site count before it and explains the loss (A3).

**Pivot.** If only high-pressure H2 recovers (A4), record the result as confirmation of the established high-pressure co-feed policy and do not open a regeneration program. If the fraction is large early but small at cycle end, the useful output is a reporting rule (partition occlusion from site loss in MTH deactivation metrics) and a note that mid-cycle swings, not end-of-cycle swings, would be the only design worth costing; hand that to process-engineering literature rather than to a mechanism program.

## 8. Recommendation

**Do not promote for full development.** Against the breadth bar: the consequence classes (regeneration + measurement, across MTP/MTG/ETH) are present, the first campaign is decisive and cheap, and a frozen prediction exists; but the *knowledge* is not absent. The desorbable-polymethylbenzene mechanism is Schulz's, the H2-removable internal-coke split on MFI at MTH temperature is Lee and Choi's, H2 and steam removal on CHA is DICP's (published, patented, piloted), the crystal-size and zoning decisions are Wan's and Le's, and the inert-sweep negative on CHA is DICP's. What is genuinely new is quantitative and its expected magnitude at the decision point is small. The Ezenwa 2026 result is a clean low-temperature mechanism whose own authors bound it away from MTH conditions.

**Retain as a bounded measurement diagnostic only**, and only if an MFI series with independently varied H+ density and crystal size is already in hand: run the recovery ladder once at the cycle end-point on three MFI samples and one CHA batch, freeze the Ξ2* prediction, and record the partitioned result as a reporting standard for MTH deactivation. Do not add a synthesis library, a spectroscopy program, or the CHA/AEI/other-framework expansion. Rejection of the swing after that single run is the likely and useful outcome.

**Relation to earlier screens.** This is distinct from the declined oligomerization product-escape screen ([oligomerization-value-challenge](../oligomerization-value-challenge.md)), which concerned external-activity thresholds and integrated liquid output, and from the MDA carbon-recovery screen ([mda-consequential-uncertainty](../mda-consequential-uncertainty.md)), which concerned recovering retained carbon as BTX with an H2 balance. Neither is re-proposed; both reached the same conclusion for the same reason (removal and purge interventions are prior art; the remaining question is quantitative and its value is bounded).
