# Direction search — 2026-09-16

Purpose: the portfolio has screened eighteen directions and selected no substantial original program. This search looks for a stronger direction in territory the repository has not screened. Each candidate note is a screen, not a selected program. Selection happens in `research/decision-brief.md` only after independent review.

## Shared brief for candidate screens

Read first: `research/standards.md`, `research/decision-brief.md`, the priority table in `research/README.md` (the list of screened directions), and the relevant `literature/topics/*.md` files. `literature/index.md` lists local papers; `literature/papers/<slug>/paper.md` holds read notes. Do not modify anything under `literature/`.

A candidate note must:

1. State the practical problem, its scale, and the connection to Gounder and/or Iglesia expertise.
2. Identify one consequential scientific uncertainty whose resolution would change a material, operating, regeneration, or measurement decision. Generic "characterize more" proposals are insufficient.
3. Search the open literature (web) for direct prior art, including competing groups and negative results. Table the closest works with DOI, what they establish, and the boundary that remains open. Distinguish established evidence, inference, hypothesis, and expectation.
4. State a falsifiable hypothesis, at least two live alternatives, and the observations that discriminate them.
5. Sketch the smallest informative first campaign: materials, matched controls, balances, transport checks, replication, and the decision it informs. Numerical targets are choices, not predictions.
6. Give separate confidence judgments (hypothesis; feasibility and informativeness; practical improvement) in words, without invented probabilities.
7. State a fair benchmark and a stop/pivot rule.
8. Give an honest recommendation: promote for full development, retain as a bounded diagnostic, or do not promote. Rejection with reasons is a valid outcome.

Web sources must be openly accessible. Cite DOIs or stable URLs. Do not represent simulations as experiments. Keep claims traceable to a source or mark them as inference.
