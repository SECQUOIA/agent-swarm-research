# Stated open problems in the leaders' 2025–2026 papers — mined screen (2026-09-16)

Fourth-wave direction screen. Instead of choosing a territory, this screen mines the open problems that Gounder, Iglesia and their closest collaborators state in their own most recent papers, on the premise that a problem a leader names but has not started is more likely to be original and consequential. Written against the [promotion threshold](../../standards.md#promotion-threshold). No experiments were performed; nothing under `literature/` was changed.

**Result in one sentence.** Twenty-two explicitly stated open problems were inventoried from twenty local 2025–2026 papers and the Bates–Gounder review; the three best fits were checked against the open literature; the strongest (which quantity hydrothermal aging changes in the Cu-CHA oxidation half-cycle, and whether O2 compensation survives severe aging) is a good bounded diagnostic but **does not meet the promotion threshold**, because the field's owners are measuring the oxidation half-cycle now and the operating decision it would inform is robust to the answer in the regime where it is used. The premise of the search is also diagnosed: authors state the problems they intend to solve next, so a stated open problem is a map of where the owners are going, not vacant ground.

## Method and source budget

- Sources: `literature/papers/<slug>/paper.md` for every 2025–2026 slug with Gounder or Iglesia in the author line (30 notes), plus the named collaborator papers (Hullfish/Sarazen 2026, Usberti/Collier 2026, Gjetja/Kamasamudram 2026, Lebrón-Rodríguez/Hermans 2025, Xing/Zheng 2026, Sun/Chen 2025, Wang/Valtchev 2026). For each, the authors' own conclusion or outlook was read in `fulltext.md`, and the full texts were swept for "remain", "unresolved", "future", "warrant", "not yet", "little is known", "beyond the scope". Page locators below are from the `<!-- page N -->` markers in the local full text (PDF page, not journal page).
- A statement is listed only if the authors write it; where the local note's "Limitations" paragraph is the literature worker's paraphrase rather than the authors' words, it is marked "note paraphrase".
- Web check: the session's WebSearch budget was exhausted before this screen started, and OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar rate-limited every call (HTTP 429). Crossref (keyword and author queries, abstracts where deposited) and Europe PMC were used instead; SSRN and Elsevier landing pages refused fetches, so two competing 2026 papers are listed by title and DOI only. This is a documented but incomplete search.

## 3. Full screen of the strongest candidate: what hydrothermal aging changes in the Cu-CHA oxidation half-cycle, and whether O2 compensation survives severe aging

### 3.1 Practical problem, scale, and fit

Cu-CHA is the SCR catalyst in essentially every heavy-duty diesel aftertreatment system, and the compliance-limiting regime is low temperature (150–200 °C, cold start and low load) after field aging. Gjetja et al. showed on one formulation that mild aging slows only the oxidation half-cycle and that raising the O2 partial pressure in proportion to k_OHC,fresh/k_OHC,aged restores both NO conversion and the bed-average Cu2+ fraction, and they propose oxygen management as a model-based aging compensation (`kamasamudram2026-low-temperature-standard-scr-over` p.9–11). Schmithorst et al. showed on a different formulation, aged more severely, that per SCR-active Cu the oxidation-limited constant rises while the active count falls (`schmithorst2025-origins-of-the-hydrothermal-stability` p.2–3, p.9). Whether the control strategy can be calibrated from one aging descriptor, and over what aging range it stays valid, depends on which of these pictures holds. Gounder's Cu-CHA redox kinetics and site counting (Paolucci 2016/2017, Jones, Krishna 2023) and Iglesia's transient-kinetics discipline fit the campaign directly.

### 3.2 The consequential uncertainty

When aging lowers the low-temperature OHC rate, is the loss (a) a lower per-site oxidation constant of a preserved redox-active pool, (b) a smaller redox-active pool with an unchanged or higher per-site constant, or (c) a Cu+ mobility change (fewer encounters of NH3-solvated Cu(I) pairs) at constant pool? Established: Gjetja's per-total-Cu k_OHC decrease and E_a increase with unchanged RHC (mild aging); Schmithorst's per-active-Cu k_first increase and active-count decrease (severe aging); Deka 2026's demonstration that an OHC-only inhibition can be a mobility effect (NH3, fresh catalysts). Inference: Gjetja's fit normalizes σ to total Cu (13.0 µmol), so any Cu rendered SCR-inactive by aging would be absorbed into a lower k_OHC. Hypothesis: the two published results are the same phenomenon seen through two normalizations, and the O2-compensation rule is valid only while the reduction-titratable pool is preserved.

Decisions changed: (O) whether O2 management for aged bricks is calibrated from k_OHC alone or needs a pool-size descriptor, and up to which aging severity it works, since RHC per total Cu also falls when the pool shrinks; (X) which aging descriptor to report and normalize to (NO2-TPD ZCuOH fraction, NO+NH3-titratable Cu, k_OHC per titratable Cu, N2O yield), and the rule that a second-order-in-σ fit must state its Cu basis; (M) whether paired-Al-rich (Schmithorst) or ZCuOH-rich (Gjetja) compositions are the better aging-tolerant choice, though this third class is weaker because both groups' formulations differ in Si/Al and Cu/Al as well as aging.

### 3.4 Hypothesis, alternatives, discriminating observations

- H1 (per-site slowdown, Gjetja): titratable Cu unchanged; k_OHC per titratable Cu falls with E_a up; O2 compensation restores X_NO and σ_ss at both mild and severe aging.
- H2 (pool loss, Schmithorst): titratable Cu falls; k_OHC per titratable Cu constant or higher; O2 compensation restores σ_ss but not X_NO at severe aging, because RHC per total Cu falls with the pool.
- H3 (mobility, by analogy with Deka 2026): titratable Cu unchanged; k_OHC per titratable Cu falls, but the fall shrinks as Cu density rises (encounter-limited, as in Krishna 2023) and is partly reversed by lower NH3 pressure; O2 compensation works but the required ratio depends on Cu loading and NH3.
- Discriminators: (i) NO+NH3 reduction titration (µmol Cu2+ reduced per total Cu) fresh versus aged; (ii) OHC transient constants renormalized to titratable Cu; (iii) Cu-density and NH3-pressure dependence of k_OHC on aged samples; (iv) O2 compensation at 200 °C at both aging severities, judged on X_NO and σ_ss together.

### 3.5 Smallest informative first campaign

Materials: one Cu-SSZ-13 near the industrial range (2–2.6 wt% Cu, SAR 25–30, matching Gjetja) prepared as one batch, split into three states: degreened (550 °C, 4 h), mild (650 °C, 50 h), severe (750 °C, 16 h), all in 10% H2O/10% O2/N2; two independent aging replicates per state. Optional fourth material at Schmithorst's composition (Si/Al 11, Cu/Al 0.21) only if the first three states disagree with both published pictures.

Measurements per state: ICP Cu and Al; NH3-TPD (H+ count); NO2 adsorption/TPD (ZCuOH fraction, Gjetja protocol); NO+NH3 reduction titration of the pre-oxidized, NH3-saturated bed (titratable Cu2+); RHC and OHC transients at 150, 175, 200 °C (Gjetja protocol: 500 ppm NO/NH3, 2% H2O, O2 step at 1%); steady-state standard SCR (8% O2) followed by O2 cut-off for σ_ss; O2 order 1–8% at 200 °C; N2O by FTIR; O2-compensation run at 4.0% versus the predicted O2 on aged samples. Balances: N balance (NO, NH3, N2O, NO2) within 5%; Cu balance by ICP before and after; titration stoichiometry stated (1 NO per Cu2+).

Transport and artefact checks: two dilution ratios and two particle sizes at the highest rate; differential conversion (<15%) for orders; bed temperature monitored; H2O and NH3 breakthrough recorded during titration per the standards' titration rule (the titration counts titratable, not working, Cu).

Replication and horizon: two aged replicates times two beds per state; about 24 bed loadings; four to six months on a standard transient reactor with FTIR.

Frozen prediction (numerical values are choices, fixed before data): mild aging leaves titratable Cu within 10% of degreened and O2 compensation restores X_NO and σ_ss within 3 percentage points (reproducing Gjetja); severe aging lowers titratable Cu by more than 25%, leaves k_OHC per titratable Cu within a factor of 1.5 of degreened, and O2 compensation restores σ_ss within 3 points but leaves X_NO more than 5 points short. If instead k_OHC per titratable Cu falls by more than a factor of 2 at both severities with titratable Cu preserved, H1 or H3 holds and O2 management is the general rule; H3 is then separated from H1 by the NH3-pressure and Cu-density dependence.

### 3.6 Confidence, in words

- Hypothesis (H2-with-H1-in-mild-regime): moderate. The two publications are internally consistent with it, but the formulations and aging severities differ in more than one variable, so the reconciliation might be a composition effect rather than a normalization effect.
- Feasibility and informativeness: high. Every measurement is a published protocol on standard apparatus; the discriminators are sign tests, not small effects.
- Practical improvement: low to moderate. In the mild-aging regime, where oxygen management would actually be applied, the operating decision (raise O2 by the k_OHC ratio) is the same under H1, H2 and H3; the campaign mainly bounds where the rule stops working and fixes the reporting basis.

### 3.7 Benchmark and stop/pivot rule

Benchmark: Gjetja's O2-recovery result on mild aging, and Schmithorst's per-active-site trend on severe aging, each reproduced on the same batch. Stop if titratable Cu and k_OHC per titratable Cu both change by less than 15% across all three states (no separable effect), or if the Tronconi/Cummins or Chmelka/BASF groups publish the two-normalization comparison first (both have the materials and methods). Pivot to a materials question only if the severe-aging pool loss correlates with the paired-Al fraction measured by Co2+/Ba2+ titration (Ge 2026) on split batches.

### 3.8 Recommendation

Do not promote. Retain as a bounded diagnostic, most naturally as a protocol-knowledge option under the [zeolite-durability program](../../programs/zeolite-durability-and-measurement.md) beside the Cu desulfation diagnostic, with the frozen prediction above as its gate.

## 5. Why the leaders' stated open problems are diagnostics, not programs

1. **A stated open problem is a declaration of intent.** In this inventory, every problem stated by a leader has either a follow-up already in press (rows 1, 5, 6, 7, 13, 15) or is the explicit next application of a method the same group built (rows 1, 16, 17). Authors state the problems they are equipped to solve; the "not yet started" premise of this search was false for all twenty-two rows where the pursuit could be checked.
2. **Stated problems live inside the authors' framing.** The consequential questions outside the framing are not stated: Hu 2025 asserts CO recycle without discussing Ni carbonyl at 483 K; Gjetja and Schmithorst each normalize to a different Cu basis without noting that the other's result could be the same phenomenon; the Bates–Gounder review lists methods, not gaps. Those unstated cross-paper tensions are where this repository can add value, and they produce diagnostics of the size found here, not programs.
3. **They are still useful.** As a negative filter: any future screen that lands on a row above should expect the owner's paper within a year. As cheap frozen predictions attachable to retained pilots: Saxena 2026's "more non-Cu-compensated Al per cage raises turnover" is a testable owner-supplied prediction for any Cu-CHA series the zeolite-durability program prepares; Gjetja's O2-ratio rule is a one-run check on any aged Cu-CHA sample; the standards' titration-reporting rule applies directly to the σ normalization in the transient SCR literature.
