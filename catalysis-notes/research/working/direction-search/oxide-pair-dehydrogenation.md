# Oxide Lewis acid–base pairs for light-alkane dehydrogenation: is the site-identity dispute consequential?

Candidate screen, 2026-09-16. Follows the [shared brief](README.md). No experiments were performed; nothing under `literature/` was changed. This is a screen, not a selected program.

## Recommendation in one paragraph

**Do not promote to a full program. Retain, at most, a bounded diagnostic that can share the apparatus and materials of the existing [zirconia styrene pilot](../../programs/zirconia-styrene-oxygen-fate.md).** The "oxygen-vacancy / coordinatively unsaturated Zr" model (Kondratenko, Jiang and co-workers) and the "intrinsic dehydroxylated Zr–O pair" model (Iglesia and co-workers; Ammal and Heyden) are genuinely different site models, and they diverge on a few operating predictions: whether a reductive step is required after oxidative regeneration, what CO or H2 treatments actually do, whether dry O2 destroys the active state, and whether H2 inhibits turnover. But most of the cheap discriminating measurements have already been done by Jaegers et al. (2024) for DME-treated m-ZrO2, the Kondratenko school's own doped materials have already moved away from Zr vacancies as the site, and the practical stake is second-order: ZrO2-family productivity is roughly 1–3.5 kg propene kg⁻¹ h⁻¹, an order of magnitude or more below the best Pt systems, and the dominant practical limits (coke half-life below an hour without H2 at 873 K, 85–90% selectivity at industrially relevant conversion) are not what the site dispute decides. What remains open is a narrow, well-posed question with an informative negative outcome; it does not justify a substantial campaign.

## 1. Practical problem, scale, and connection to the professors

On-purpose propylene is made by nonoxidative propane dehydrogenation (PDH) on Pt–Sn/Al2O3 (Oleflex, continuous regeneration) or CrOx/Al2O3 (Catofin, cyclic fixed beds); the same catalyst families serve isobutane and n-butane dehydrogenation. Pt cost and Cr toxicity motivate abundant-oxide alternatives. Bulk ZrO2 and promoted ZrO2 are the most developed Pt- and Cr-free oxide candidates: the Kondratenko group has reported cyclic operation for 240 h over 60 cycles (Otroshchenko et al. 2015, [10.1002/anie.201508731](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.201508731)) and 70 cycles over 13 days (Zhang et al. 2018, [10.1038/s41467-018-06174-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06174-5)), and BASF has patented Cr- and precious-metal-free ZrO2 PDH catalysts with Iglesia as co-inventor ([US12330140B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12330140B2/en)).

Connection: Iglesia is the principal author of the Lewis acid–base (LAB) pair model for ZrO2 dehydrogenation and hydrogenation ([jaegers2024](../../../literature/papers/jaegers2024-heterolytic-ch-activation-routes-in/paper.md); [jaegers2025](../../../literature/papers/jaegers2025-hydrogenation-of-alkenes-cycloalkenes-and/paper.md); [artsiusheuski2025](../../../literature/papers/artsiusheuski2025-selective-ethylbenzene-dehydrogenation-to-styrene/paper.md)). Gounder's expertise is directly relevant in method rather than material: titrant-based counting of Lewis acid sites and the treatment of water as a reversible, quantifiable poison at Lewis acid centers (Harris et al. 2016, [10.1016/j.jcat.2015.12.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2015.12.024); Cordon et al. 2018, [10.1021/jacs.8b08336](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.8b08336)).

## 2. The consequential uncertainty

**Is the catalytically competent Zr site on m-ZrO2 created by removing lattice oxygen (a reduction event that leaves Zr³⁺/Zr_cus at an anion vacancy), or is it an intrinsic Zr–O pair at low-coordination surface positions that merely has to be freed of bound H2O/CO2?**

Why it is consequential: the two answers prescribe different pretreatment and regeneration chemistry and different promoter logic.

| Operating variable | Vacancy / Zr_cus model predicts | Dehydroxylated LAB-pair model predicts | Already tested? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reductive pretreatment (H2, CO) | Required or beneficial; CO better than H2 because it removes lattice O more readily (Zhang 2018: CO 30 min → 7×, H2 6 h → 2×) | Works only insofar as the gas reacts with bound OH/H2O (CO + OH pairs → CO2 + H2); creates a weaker titrant (CO2); DME or alkenes are better desiccants at lower T | Partly: Jaegers 2024 He/H2/DME at 723 K; patent O2 vs H2 at 873 K (see §3) |
| Dry O2 exposure of an active, hydrocarbon-free surface at reaction T | Reoxidizes vacancies; activity lost; O2 uptake ≈ vacancy count | No effect unless H2O/CO2 is formed; no O2 uptake at the pair | **No clean test found** |
| Stoichiometry of CO "reduction" | CO2 evolved without H2; net lattice-O loss (¹⁸O exchangeable) | CO2 and H2 evolved 1:1 from hydroxyl pairs; no net lattice-O loss | **Not found** |
| H2 cofeed kinetics | H binds strongly at Zr_cus; H2 desorption can be rate-limiting on defective surfaces (Kondratenko DFT as summarized by Ammal and Heyden 2026) → H2 inhibition | Zero order in H2 at differential conversion (Jaegers 2024, 0–30 kPa) | Yes on DME-treated m-ZrO2 (zero order); patent reports 2× initial-rate loss with 12.3 kPa H2 on O2-pretreated ZrO2 at 823–873 K (confounded, §3) |
| Feed water | Reoxidizes/hydroxylates; poison | Titrates pairs 1:1; poison quantified at 0.56 pairs nm⁻²; alkenes scavenge | Both agree; not discriminating |
| Regeneration | Air burn-off, then in-situ re-reduction by propane/propene (no reductive step used by Kondratenko) | Air burn-off forms H2O which titrates pairs; dry purge, DME or product alkenes restore | Both predict an induction period; not discriminating without effluent accounting |
| Dopants | Choose promoters that lower vacancy formation energy (Bi, La, Y, Cr; trace Rh/Ru/Cu) | Choose for pair density and OH lability; reducibility irrelevant | Kondratenko's GaZrOx (2023) and Zn/TiZrOx (2020) already attribute activity to non-vacancy sites |
| Crystallite size / facets | Smaller crystallites → more vacancies (70× drop 7→45 nm) | Smaller crystallites → more low-coordination edge/corner pairs | Both fit; not discriminating |

Evidence labels: the predictions in columns 2–3 are the authors' stated models (established as claims, not as facts); the "already tested" column is this screen's inference from the sources in §3.

### 3.3 What is established, inferred, hypothesized, expected

- **Established:** on DME-treated m-ZrO2 at 723 K, H2 treatment does not enhance rates, H2 is not consumed to 893 K within 0.04 μmol against ~27 μmol titrated sites, rates are zero order in H2 and first order in alkane, and water titrates a minority site population 1:1 (Jaegers 2024). On Kondratenko's small-crystallite ZrO2 at 550–625 °C, CO treatment raises rates several-fold, rates correlate with probe-counted Lewis sites and crystallite size, and no reductive pretreatment is needed for cyclic operation because activity develops in situ (Zhang 2018).
- **Inference (this screen):** the two schools' baselines differ (He at 723 K vs thermal/oxidative at 823–873 K), so the ">100-fold DME vs <10-fold CO" contrast has not been made on one material with one baseline. The BASF patent's O2 ≈ H2 pretreatment equivalence at 873 K weakens "reduction is required" but does not test "reduction occurs".
- **Hypothesis (Iglesia):** CO acts as a desiccant (CO + hydroxyl pair → CO2 + H2). **Hypothesis (Kondratenko):** CO removes lattice O. These make opposite predictions for effluent H2 during CO treatment and for O2 uptake on reoxidation; neither prediction has been tested in the retrieved sources.
- **Expectation, unvalidated:** that on bare m-ZrO2 the LAB explanation will hold at 723 K and the vacancy explanation may become material-dependent above ~800 °C or on Bi/La/Y-promoted oxides where removable lattice oxygen is demonstrated (Nat. Commun. 2025 chemical looping).

## 4. Falsifiable hypothesis, alternatives, discriminating observations

**H0 (test target).** On bare m-ZrO2, the alkane-dehydrogenation-competent sites are dehydroxylated Zr–O pairs; reductive gases act only by removing bound H2O/CO2. Consequences: (i) exposure of an active, hydrocarbon-free surface to dry O2 at reaction temperature consumes no O2 beyond blank and does not lower the rate; (ii) during CO treatment, H2 is evolved in ≈1:1 ratio to CO2 and ¹⁸O from labeled CO2/O2 does not enter the lattice beyond blank exchange; (iii) the per-site rate constant (per H2O-titrated site) is the same after DME, CO and H2 treatments, and the treatments differ only in how many sites they free.

**H1 (vacancy model).** Lattice-O removal creates the sites. Consequences: dry O2 uptake ≥ a fixed fraction of the titrated site count with proportional rate loss; CO treatment evolves CO2 without stoichiometric H2; per-site k after CO exceeds per-site k after DME, or the site populations counted by H2O and by O2 uptake do not coincide.

**H2 (material-dependent mixture).** H0 holds at ≤723 K on hydrothermal m-ZrO2, H1 contributes at ≥823 K or on promoted (Bi, La, Y) ZrO2 where lattice oxygen is demonstrably removable. Consequences: the H0 tests pass on one material and fail on the other; O2 uptake and rate loss scale with promoter reducibility.

**H3 (adatom/restructuring).** The competent minority sites are formed by adsorbate-induced surface restructuring (Ammal and Heyden's Zr(O)2 picture); neither reduction nor mere dehydroxylation is sufficient, and site counts change with thermal/chemical history irrespective of oxygen balance. Consequence: titrated site count changes with treatment history while both O2 uptake and H2 evolution stay at blank.

Discriminators are the three effluent/uptake stoichiometries plus the per-site rate constants. A single dry-O2 pulse experiment with mass-spectrometric O2/H2O accounting and a subsequent rate measurement separates H0 from H1 on a given material; the CO-treatment H2/CO2 balance and ¹⁸O exchange separate "desiccation" from "reduction"; H2O titration before and after each treatment tests whether treatments change site number or per-site rate.

## 5. Smallest informative first campaign

Materials: (a) hydrothermal m-ZrO2 (~130 m² g⁻¹) as in Jaegers 2024; (b) small-crystallite ZrO2 from ZrO(NO3)2 decomposition as in Zhang 2018/Otroshchenko 2019; optionally (c) one promoted sample (Y- or Bi-ZrO2). Two independent preparations of (a) and (b).

Apparatus: the differential quartz reactor with 1:20 quartz dilution, O2/H2O scrubbers to <0.1 ppm, sample-loop pulsing and online MS/GC already specified for the styrene pilot ([program](../../programs/zirconia-styrene-oxygen-fate.md)). Add ¹⁸O2 and C¹⁸O2 as pulse gases.

Sequence per material, with the predictions frozen before the runs:

1. Treatments at matched temperature (723 K, and 823 K where the vacancy school's CO data were taken): He, DME (1.5 kPa, 0.25 h), CO (5 kPa), H2 (5 kPa). Record effluent CO2, H2, H2O, CH3OH, CO continuously; close the O and H balances on each treatment. The H2/CO2 ratio during CO treatment is the first discriminator.
2. After each treatment, H2O-pulse titration during C3H8 dehydrogenation at 723 K (15 kPa C3H8, 5 kPa H2) to count competent sites and obtain per-site k; measure H2 order at 0–30 kPa and apparent Ea on a descending temperature ramp.
3. Dry-O2 pulse test: after DME (and after CO), purge in He, pulse dry ¹⁸O2 at reaction temperature with no hydrocarbon present; quantify ¹⁸O2 uptake, ¹⁶O¹⁸O and H2¹⁸O release against a quartz-only blank; then re-measure the rate. Rate loss with O2 uptake ≈ site count supports H1; no uptake and no loss supports H0.
4. Regeneration branch (only if 1–3 give a clear answer): coke a sample under 40 vol% C3H8 at 823 K, burn in dilute O2, then compare (i) dry He purge at reaction T, (ii) DME pulse, (iii) CO 30 min, on the time to reach the asymptotic rate and on effluent H2O/CO2/H2 during the induction period.

Controls: quartz-only trains for every pulse gas; He-only sham treatments between steps; a return-to-DME reference after each sequence to check that the site inventory has not drifted. Transport: rate independence of dilution and particle size at the highest rate condition; Chin criterion for bed-scale gradients as in Jaegers 2024 SI S4. Replication: two preparations per material, each sequence repeated once. Detection: the TPR limit of 0.04 μmol against ~27 μmol titrated sites per 0.2 g (Jaegers SI S6) sets the required sensitivity for O2 uptake; state the resolvable fraction before running.

Decision informed: whether a reductive step should ever be specified after oxidative regeneration of bare or promoted ZrO2 (versus a dry purge or a desiccant pulse), and whether promoter selection should target reducibility or pair density. Numerical thresholds (e.g., "O2 uptake below 0.1 per titrated site") are choices to be fixed from the measured blank variance, not predictions.

## 6. Confidence judgments

- **Hypothesis H0 on hydrothermal m-ZrO2 at 723 K:** moderate to high. The He/H2/DME comparison, absent H2 uptake to 893 K, zero H2 order, Hf control and computed vacancy energetics all point the same way; what is missing is the direct reoxidation and CO-stoichiometry tests. On small-crystallite or promoted ZrO2 at ≥823 K: low to moderate; removable lattice oxygen is demonstrated for LaZrOx at 700 °C, and the patent's 84 mol kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ after CO is not explained by either school's published data.
- **Feasibility and informativeness:** high for the specific site question; the measurements are pulse, titration and effluent balances on standard equipment, and a negative result (no O2 uptake, H2 ≈ CO2) is as informative as a positive one. The main risk is analytical: micromole-level O2 uptake against apparatus and quartz exchange, and the hydroxyl-desorption background during heating.
- **Practical improvement:** low. Resolving the site does not change the productivity gap to Pt systems, the coke half-life or the selectivity at integral conversion. It can remove an unnecessary reduction step or justify a desiccant pulse in a cyclic unit; that is a process-detail decision, not a material breakthrough.

## 7. Fair benchmark and stop/pivot rule

Benchmarks. For the Cr-replacement stake: a commercial-like K-CrOx/Al2O3 under Kondratenko's cyclic conditions (550–600 °C, 40 vol% C3H8, 30 min segments, air regeneration), against which Zn/TiZrOx reached 1.25 kg kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ at 30% conversion (Han 2020) and Kondratenko describes bare ZrO2 as "comparable" (Account 2025; no number given). For the productivity ceiling: PtSn/S-1 at ~1.17 mol propylene g⁻¹ h⁻¹ (≈49 kg kg⁻¹ h⁻¹) at 550 °C and WHSV 165 h⁻¹ (Lu et al. 2026, [10.1038/s41929-026-01538-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41929-026-01538-3)), Pt/zeolite at 4500 h (Xu et al. 2025, [10.1038/s41586-025-09168-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09168-8)) and PtGaOx at 8.7 kg kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ (Wu et al. 2025, [10.1002/anie.202506704](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202506704)), all as recorded in the [PDH screen](../dehydrogenation-consequential-screen.md). ZrO2-family numbers are 1–3.5 kg kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ (28 mol kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ at 823 K in WO2022132843A1; ≈84 mol kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ after CO in US12330140B2, conditions unstated; 1.25 kg kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ for Zn/TiZrOx; 2.7 kg isobutene kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ for Rh/YZrOx). Lifetime evidence is 60–70 cycles over 240 h/13 days; no thousand-hour data exist. Selectivity is 85–90% at >25% conversion for bare ZrO2 and 95% for Zn/TiZrOx, versus >98% for the Pt benchmarks. Comparisons must match feed dilution, conversion and regeneration accounting; Iglesia's >99% selectivity is a dilute, differential value.

Stop/pivot. Stop the vacancy branch if, on both materials, dry-¹⁸O2 uptake and H2¹⁸O release stay within blank and the rate is unchanged, and CO treatment evolves H2 within the measurement uncertainty of CO2: H0 then holds and the only operating consequence (no reductive step; use a dry purge or desiccant) is already practiced or patented, so the diagnostic closes. Pivot to a promoter-reducibility study only if O2 uptake scales with rate loss on the promoted sample but not on hydrothermal m-ZrO2 (H2): that is Kondratenko's existing program and would not be an original direction here. Stop entirely if the analytical blanks exceed the site-equivalent oxygen inventory, and report the bound.

## 8. Recommendation

**Do not promote.** Reasons, in order of weight:

1. The discriminating experiments that are cheapest have largely been done for the material and temperature where the LAB model was formulated: He/H2/DME comparison, H2 TPR against titrated site counts, H2 order, Hf control, thermal-history hysteresis (Jaegers 2024 main text §3.6 and SI S3, S6, Figs. S7, S9, S12). The vacancy school has not answered them experimentally; its 2025 Account cites the DME result as titrant removal without reconciliation, and its own best doped materials (GaZrOx, Zn/TiZrOx) are assigned to non-vacancy sites. The scientific contest is therefore already tilted, and what remains (dry-O2 reoxidation, CO stoichiometry, per-site k after reduction) is a bounded confirmation with an informative negative, not a program.
2. The practical stake is second-order. Both models agree that water poisons, that products or CO/H2 reactivate in situ, that H2 suppresses coke, and that small crystallites help. They differ on whether a reductive step is needed after regeneration and on promoter logic. Kondratenko already runs 70 cycles with no reductive step; Iglesia's DME desiccation is patented. The dominant ZrO2 limitations (0.28 h half-life at 873 K without H2, 85–90% selectivity at integral conversion, 1–3.5 kg kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ against 8.7–49 for Pt systems) are not decided by the site question.
3. The territory is densely patented on both sides (LIKAT/Kondratenko publications since 2015; UC/BASF WO2022132843A1, WO2024177986A2, US12330140B2), which limits the originality of any operating recommendation that follows from the site assignment.

If the styrene pilot proceeds on the same m-ZrO2 with pulse MS and site titration already commissioned, add the dry-¹⁸O2 pulse and the CO-treatment H2/CO2 balance as two extra sequences on the same specimens. That bounded addition tests H0 against H1 at marginal cost and records a clean answer for the literature. It should not be presented as a new direction, and it should not be run at all if it requires new apparatus or synthesis.
