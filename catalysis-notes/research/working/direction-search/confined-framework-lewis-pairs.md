# Candidate screen: framework Lewis acid–base pairs in hydrophobic zeolites (Zr-, Hf-, Sn-, Ti-Beta/MFI) as confined analogues of the m-ZrO2 Zr–O pair for heterolytic C–H and H–H activation

Direction search, 2026-09-16 (fourth wave, written against the [promotion threshold](../../standards.md#promotion-threshold)). Screen only; not a selected program. No experiments were performed; nothing under `literature/` was changed. Follows the [shared brief](README.md). Companion screens: [oxide-pair dehydrogenation](oxide-pair-dehydrogenation.md) (ZrO2 site identity), [cross-reaction water rule](zro2-water-rule-cross-reaction.md) (water titration of Zr–O pairs), [zeolite synthesis capability](zeolite-synthesis-capability.md).

## Decision in one paragraph

**Do not promote. Do not retain as a diagnostic either, except as a one-run negative control that could ride on the zirconia pilot if that pilot commissions, with a frozen prediction of no measurable pair activity.** The candidate asks whether an isolated tetravalent framework cation with a siloxane bridge (M–O–Si, M = Sn, Zr, Hf, Ti) in a siliceous zeolite activates C–H or H–H heterolytically as the minority Zr–O pairs on m-ZrO2 do, and whether confinement and hydrophobicity change the per-site rate and water tolerance. The open literature already answers most of it. Sn-Beta has been tested for propane dehydrogenation (Yue et al. 2021): the authors report activity at 903 K but their own DFT gives intrinsic C–H barriers of 242–301 kJ mol⁻¹, against 90 kJ mol⁻¹ (DFT) and 84 kJ mol⁻¹ (measured) on the ZrO2 pair. Isolated Zr on silica is essentially inactive for propane dehydrogenation (0.27% conversion at 823 K) while the same organozirconium on silicon nitride is active, with the computed Zr–O–Si heterolysis barrier 251 kJ mol⁻¹ and the product state +187 kJ mol⁻¹ endergonic (DeMuth et al. 2024). Bell's group, which owns the "isolated Lewis acid centre in siliceous Beta" territory, states in its 2026 Account that Sn, Zr, Ti and Hf grafted into silanol nests form closed sites of low activity, and that the active Zn, Fe and Co centres are open M–OH sites hydrogen-bonded to silanols, that is, the hydroxyl-rich environment the candidate proposes to remove. Where framework or nested cations in siliceous zeolites do dehydrogenate propane (Zn, Fe, Co, Cr, Ga), the field's owners are already counting open sites with CD3CN, measuring H2 orders and cycling regenerations. The illustrative bound below shows that the zeolite has no site-density headroom over ZrO2 per gram (0.01–0.17 mmol g⁻¹ of Sn against 0.12 mmol g⁻¹ of titrated Zr–O pairs), so the per-site rate would have to match the oxide pair, while the basicity penalty implied by the published DFT numbers (about 150 kJ mol⁻¹ in barrier, 10⁹-fold at 823 K) exceeds any confinement magnitude in Gounder's work (tens of kJ mol⁻¹, 10–50-fold) by more than five orders of magnitude. The hypothesis is chemically implausible as posed for closed M–O–Si pairs; for open M–OH/Si–OH sites it is already Bell's program.

## 1. Practical problem, scale, and connection

**Problem.** Propane dehydrogenation (Oleflex Pt–Sn, Catofin CrOx) and alkene hydrogenation rely on Pt or Cr. Bulk m-ZrO2 dehydrogenates C2–C4 alkanes at Lewis acid–base (LAB) Zr–O pairs that are a minority of surface pairs (0.55 nm⁻² against 8–12 nm⁻² total), become active only after chemical desiccation (DME), and are titrated one-to-one by water at ppm levels ([[jaegers2024-heterolytic-ch-activation-routes-in]] p.4-6, p.13-14, [10.1021/jacs.4c07766](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.4c07766); [[jaegers2025-hydrogenation-of-alkenes-cycloalkenes-and]] p.7, [10.1021/acscatal.5c03523](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5c03523)). A single-site framework version in a siliceous zeolite would, if it worked, give a countable, uniform, structurally defined pair, a hydrophobic host that excludes water, and a confining void that could stabilise the ion-pair-like transition state. The [oxide-pair screen](oxide-pair-dehydrogenation.md) §7 records the ZrO2-family productivity (1–3.5 kg propene kg⁻¹ h⁻¹) and the Pt benchmarks (8.7–49 kg kg⁻¹ h⁻¹).

**Scale.** On-purpose propylene is a commodity process; the stake is the same as in the oxide-pair screen (Pt cost, Cr toxicity). A framework-zeolite catalyst would also need to survive cyclic air regeneration at 873 K and coke.

**Connection.** Gounder: Lewis-acid zeolite synthesis in fluoride and hydroxide media, open/closed Sn counting by CD3CN and pyridine ([[harris2016-titration-and-quantification-of-open]] p.8-13, [10.1016/j.jcat.2015.12.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2015.12.024)), hydrophobicity and intrapore silanol control ([[vila2020-quantification-of-intraporous-hydrophilic-binding]] p.7-12, [10.1021/acscatal.0c02918](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.0c02918); [[cordon2019-deactivation-of-sn-beta-zeolites]] p.5-11, [10.1039/c8cy02589d](https://doi.org/10.1039/c8cy02589d)), and, with Iglesia, the thermodynamics of confinement for alkane activation ([[gounder2009-catalytic-consequences-of-spatial-constraints]] p.5-13, [10.1021/ja808292c](https://doi.org/10.1021/ja808292c)). Iglesia: the LAB-pair heterolytic mechanism, DME desiccation, H2O-pulse site counting, isotope and pressure-order methods (Jaegers 2024, 2025). The combination is real; the problem is that the chemistry it would be applied to is already tested or implausible.

## 2. The consequential uncertainty

**Question.** Does a framework M–O–Si pair (M = Sn, Zr, Hf, Ti) in a defect-poor siliceous zeolite activate the terminal C–H bond of propane (or H–H) heterolytically at a rate per site comparable to the titrated Zr–O pair on m-ZrO2 at 723–823 K, and if so, do confinement (12-MR Beta versus 10-MR MFI) and silanol density (fluoride versus hydroxide synthesis) shift the per-site rate and the water-titration behaviour?

**Decisions it would change if the answer were yes.** (i) Material: a metal-free, countable, single-site dehydrogenation and hydrogenation catalyst family. (ii) Operating: whether feed-water specifications can be relaxed in a hydrophobic host relative to the ppm-level constraint on ZrO2. (iii) Measurement/design: a general rule on which oxide-pair chemistries transfer into confined single-site frameworks.

**Why the answer is probably no, and what "no" is worth.** The two established facts that bear directly are the weak basicity of the siloxane bridge oxygen and the absence of a second, labile O–H partner at a closed site (§3, §6). A clean negative (no activity per titrated site within a stated bound, no DME response, no water-titration stoichiometry) would confirm what the Zr/SiO2 result and Bell's closed-site statement already say. That is a bounded confirmation, not a decision-changing result.

**Status labels.** Established: heterolytic C–H and H–H activation on isolated Zn, Co, Fe, Ga, Cr cations bound to silica or siliceous zeolites; open M–OH sites and H-bonded silanol nests as the active motif in Bell's materials; near-zero PDH activity of isolated Zr on silica; computed Zr–O–Si and Sn–O–Si heterolysis barriers of 240–300 kJ mol⁻¹; Sn-Beta PDH activity reported at 903 K without site normalisation; MPV but no H2 hydrogenation on Zr/Hf/Sn-Beta. Inference (this screen): the closed tetravalent M–O–Si pair is too weak a base for the ZrO2-type route, and the reported Sn-Beta activity is either at open/hydroxylated or extraframework sites, at Brønsted sites, or partly thermal. Hypothesis (candidate): confinement rescues the pair. Unvalidated expectation: none favourable.

**What is genuinely absent from the literature.** (a) A per-titrated-site comparison of a group-4/14 framework pair with the m-ZrO2 pair under matched dry conditions with DME pretreatment and in-reaction H2O pulsing. (b) A fluoride-versus-hydroxide (silanol-poor versus silanol-rich) contrast for the same framework metal at 723–823 K in the gas phase. (c) Any test of alkene hydrogenation with H2 on Sn-, Zr- or Hf-Beta. All three are cheap, and all three are expected to be negative for the closed-site materials on the evidence above.

## 4. Hypothesis, alternatives, discriminating observations

**H1 (candidate).** A framework M–O–Si pair in defect-poor Sn/Zr/Hf-Beta activates propane C–H heterolytically; confinement of the ion-pair-like transition state offsets the weaker basicity, so that the per-site first-order constant at 723–823 K is within an order of magnitude of the m-ZrO2 pair (24 h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ at 723 K), rates respond to DME as on ZrO2, water titrates 1:1, and the hydrophobic (F) material tolerates more feed water than the OH material.

**H2 (basicity-limited; expected).** The siloxane oxygen is too weak a base; closed sites are inactive within the detection bound (per-site k < 10⁻² of the ZrO2 pair); any residual activity is thermal, Brønsted (Al traces, M–O(H)–Si), or at extraframework MOx. Predictions: rate indistinguishable from Si-Beta and empty-reactor blanks after correction; KIE ≈ 1 (thermal) or a cracking/dehydrogenation ratio characteristic of protolytic chemistry; no DME response; no water-pulse stoichiometry.

**H3 (open-site route; Bell's program).** Activity exists only at open M–OH sites hydrogen-bonded to silanols, scales with the open-site count and with silanol density, and is promoted by H2 (which opens closed sites) as for Fe-DeAlBEA. Predictions: OH-synthesised material more active than F-synthesised; rate rises with H2 pretreatment; CD3CN open-site count correlates with rate; per-site k at 823 K in the range of Fe/Zn in DeAlBEA (1–15 h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹, §6), i.e., 10–100× below the ZrO2 pair.

**H4 (restructuring).** Under H2/propane at ≥823 K, framework Sn(IV) reconstructs or segregates (Appl. Catal. B 2026; PtSn precedents), so the working site is not the synthesised framework site. Predictions: post-reaction ¹¹⁹Sn NMR/XAS change; activity develops with time on stream; Zr/Hf (non-reducible) do not show it.

**Discriminators.** (i) Per-site k versus m-ZrO2 at 723 and 823 K on the same apparatus (H1 vs H2/H3); (ii) DME pretreatment response (ZrO2 ≥100×; H2 predicts none; H3 predicts a silanol-dependent effect); (iii) in-reaction H2O pulse: uptake–rate slope and intercept, reported as "titratable total and bed-average rate per titratable site" per `standards.md`; (iv) C3H8/C3D8 KIE (2.2 on ZrO2; ≈1 thermal); (v) H2 order (zero on ZrO2; positive for H3; negative if hydride formation is required); (vi) F versus OH synthesis at fixed M content (H1 predicts F ≥ OH; H3 predicts OH > F); (vii) blanks: Si-Beta-F, empty bed, quartz chips at every temperature; (viii) post-run ¹¹⁹Sn NMR or Zr/Hf XAS (H4).

## 5. Smallest informative first campaign (if ever run)

Materials (all existing recipes): Zr-Beta-F and Zr-Beta-OH (Si/Zr ≈ 100, Al-free parent), Sn-Beta-F and Sn-Beta-OH (Si/Sn ≈ 100), Si-Beta-F blank, hydrothermal m-ZrO2 (130 m² g⁻¹) as reference; two preparations per zeolite. Pre-count open/closed sites by CD3CN and pyridine (Harris 2016 coefficients; Abidi caveat), silanol density by CD3CN 2275 cm⁻¹ and methanol uptake (Vega-Vila 2020).

Apparatus: the differential quartz reactor with 5 ppb O2/H2O scrubbers, DME pretreatment line, sample-loop H2O pulsing and online MS/GC already specified for the [zirconia pilot](../../programs/zirconia-styrene-oxygen-fate.md). No new apparatus.

Sequence, with predictions frozen before the runs: (1) blanks (empty bed, quartz, Si-Beta-F) at 723, 773, 823 K, 15 kPa C3H8, 5 kPa H2; (2) each zeolite after He and after DME at 723 K, rates per gram and per CD3CN-counted site, H2 order 0–30 kPa, KIE at one condition; (3) m-ZrO2 interleaved as the same-day reference; (4) in-reaction H2O pulses on any sample showing rate above blank; (5) F versus OH contrast and H2 pretreatment at 823 K; (6) post-run NMR/XAS on Sn samples. Transport: dilution and particle-size checks at the highest rate; Chin criterion. Replication: two preparations, each sequence once. Detection bound: state the minimum resolvable per-site k from the blank variance before running (a choice, not a prediction; e.g., 1% of the ZrO2 per-site constant).

Decision informed: whether group-4/14 framework pairs are dead for heterolytic alkane activation (close the idea) or whether an open-site, silanol-assisted route exists (which is Bell's territory and would not be pursued here). Total effort: weeks on commissioned apparatus, mostly synthesis and site counting.

## 6. Illustrative bound (labelled as such; assumptions stated)

**Oxide reference.** m-ZrO2 at 130 m² g⁻¹ with 0.55 titrated pairs nm⁻² gives 7.2 × 10¹⁹ sites g⁻¹ = **0.12 mmol g⁻¹**. Per-site first-order constant 24 h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ at 723 K (Jaegers 2025 Fig. 4c) gives 2.9 mol kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹, i.e., about 43 mol kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ at 15 kPa C3H8 and 723 K (first-order, far from equilibrium). Arrhenius extrapolation with the measured 84 kJ mol⁻¹ gives per-site constants of about 59 h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ at 773 K and 130 h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ at 823 K; the patent value of 28 mol kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ at 823 K (WO2022132843A1, conditions and deactivation state not matched) is lower than this extrapolation, so the per-site constant is used for the bound and the per-gram figure carries a factor-of-several uncertainty.

**Zeolite site inventory.** At Si/Sn = 100, Sn = 0.17 mmol g⁻¹; at Si/Sn = 200, 0.083 mmol g⁻¹. With open fractions of 0.13–0.46 (Harris 2016 Table 3), open Sn = 0.011–0.077 mmol g⁻¹. The ratio N_ZrO2/N_zeolite is therefore **0.7 (all Sn, Si/Sn 100) to 11 (open Sn only, Si/Sn 200)**. To match a gram of ZrO2, the zeolite pair needs a per-site constant between 0.7× and 11× the ZrO2 pair's. There is no site-density headroom; the per-site chemistry must be at least as good.

**Per-site penalty implied by published DFT.** ZrO2 first C–H proton abstraction ΔE‡ = 90 kJ mol⁻¹ (Jaegers 2024). Sn-Beta intrinsic ΔH‡ = 242–301 kJ mol⁻¹ (Yue 2021). Zr–O–Si on silica ΔG‡ = 251 kJ mol⁻¹, product +187 kJ mol⁻¹ (DeMuth 2024); Zr–N on Si3N4 ΔG‡ = 157 kJ mol⁻¹. Taking the smallest gap, 150 kJ mol⁻¹, the Boltzmann factor at 823 K is exp(150 000/(8.314 × 823)) ≈ 3 × 10⁹; a 100 kJ mol⁻¹ gap gives 2 × 10⁶; 50 kJ mol⁻¹ gives 1.5 × 10³. These are different functionals, models and reference states, so the gap is uncertain by tens of kJ mol⁻¹; it is not uncertain by 100.

**What confinement can offset.** Gounder's alkane-activation measurements give: 8-MR versus 12-MR MOR, more than about 10× at 748 K (12-MR dehydrogenation undetectable); paired versus isolated CHA sites, 12× for propane cracking; occluded EFAl, 24–26 kJ mol⁻¹ lower apparent barriers; framework-to-framework intrinsic barrier spread ±8–11 kJ mol⁻¹ (Jones 2015, topic note). Dispersion stabilisation of a small adsorbate in the Beta 12-MR is −30 to −40 kJ mol⁻¹ (Abidi 2025 periodic DFT). The liquid-phase hydrophobicity factors (8–50× per open Sn at 373 K) are water-network effects with no counterpart at 823 K and ppm water. So confinement plausibly offsets **≤ 30–40 kJ mol⁻¹, i.e., ≤ 10²–10³ at 823 K**, leaving a shortfall of at least 10⁵–10⁶ against the DFT-implied penalty.

**Empirical check (per metal atom, not per titrated site; matched propane pressure).** ZrO2 pair: 59 h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ (773 K), 130 (823 K, extrapolated). Nested Zn/DeAlBEA: 1.7 h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ at 773 K (35× lower). Fe/DeAlBEA: 14 h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ at 823 K without H2 (9× lower; per total Fe). Zr/Si3N4: 2.3 h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ at 823 K (56× lower); Zr/SiO2: negligible. These are the best cases on siliceous hosts, and all use open M–OH or M–N chemistry; the closed M–O–Si case is the Zr/SiO2 row. Per gram, Fe/DeAlBEA at Fe/Al0 = 0.04 is roughly 10 mol kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ at 823 K if Al0 ≈ 1.2 mmol g⁻¹ (inference from the parent Si/Al; not stated in the abstract), within a factor of about three of the ZrO2 patent figure at the same temperature; this is achieved by site count, not per-site rate, and by a redox-capable cation.

## 7. Confidence judgments

- **Hypothesis H1 (confined tetravalent framework pair matches the ZrO2 pair):** very low. Two independent computational results and one direct experimental negative (Zr/SiO2) put the closed M–O–Si barrier 150 kJ mol⁻¹ above the oxide pair; Bell's Account records that group-4/14 cations in silanol nests form closed, low-activity sites; no H2 heterolysis on Sn/Zr/Hf-Beta has ever been reported; the professors' own 2026 PCCP theory says pair heterolysis does not follow a transferable scaling relation. The hydrophobicity leg is inapplicable at 823 K. H3 (open-site, silanol-assisted) is plausible and is Bell's published program for Zn, Fe, Co and, by the Account, a stated weak point for Sn/Zr/Ti/Hf.
- **Feasibility and informativeness:** feasibility high (existing syntheses, existing apparatus, weeks of work); informativeness low. The probable outcome is a clean negative that confirms Zr/SiO2 and the Bell statement. A positive would be surprising and would then need to be separated from thermal, Brønsted and extraframework routes at the same temperature.
- **Practical improvement:** very low. Even the best siliceous-zeolite single-site catalysts (Fe, Zn, Cr) are 10–50× below the ZrO2 pair per metal atom and far below Pt systems per gram; a tetravalent framework pair would be weaker still.

## 8. Fair benchmark and stop/pivot rule

**Benchmark.** Per site: the m-ZrO2 pair at 24 h⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ (723 K) and Fe-DeAlBEA at 213 mol (mol Fe)⁻¹ h⁻¹ (823 K, 15 kPa). Per gram: ZrO2-family 1–3.5 kg propene kg⁻¹ h⁻¹, Zn/TiZrOx 1.25 at 30% conversion (Han 2020), Pt benchmarks 8.7–49 kg kg⁻¹ h⁻¹ ([oxide-pair screen](oxide-pair-dehydrogenation.md) §7). Blanks: empty-bed thermal conversion 0.75/8.6/33.5% at 823/873/923 K in undiluted propane (Sricharoen 2023). Any framework-zeolite claim must report rate per titrated site, the DME response, the KIE, the blank at the same temperature and the Brønsted cracking marker.

**Stop.** Stop the idea now on the literature: the closed-pair hypothesis is contradicted by the computed barriers and the Zr/SiO2 negative, and the open-site route is owned. If the zirconia pilot commissions its DME/H2O-pulse train, one optional day of Zr-Beta-F, Si-Beta-F and empty-bed runs at 723 and 823 K with the frozen prediction "per-Zr rate indistinguishable from Si-Beta and blank; no DME response" would document the negative for the record. **Pivot** only if that run shows a DME-responsive, water-titratable rate above blank with KIE > 1.5; that would reopen H1 and justify the F/OH and H2 contrasts of §5. No other pivot is warranted; an open-site positive belongs to Bell's program and would not be an original direction.

## 9. Assessment against the promotion threshold

1. *Breadth of consequence.* Only if H1 held would the answer change material and operating decisions across two reactions. The probable answer (H2/H3) confirms current practice: no material change, no water-specification change, and the general "transfer rule" reduces to "closed tetravalent pairs do not transfer; open hydroxylated sites of redox-capable cations do", which Bell and DeMuth have already written. Fails as posed.
2. *Decisive first campaign.* Passes; the §5 campaign decides H1 against H2/H3 in weeks on standard apparatus.
3. *Knowledge absent after a documented search.* Fails. Sn-Beta PDH is published (with an unreconciled 240–300 kJ mol⁻¹ barrier), Zr/SiO2 is published negative, the group-4/14 closed-site statement is published, the open-site route with CD3CN counting and H2 orders is being measured by its owners, and a descriptor/volcano study across Lewis-acid zeolites for dehydrogenation is in press. The narrow residue (matched per-titrated-site comparison; F/OH at 823 K; H2 hydrogenation on Sn/Zr-Beta) is absent but expected negative.
4. *Frozen prediction as expansion gate.* Possible (stated in §8), but the prediction is of a null.

**Recommendation: do not promote; do not list as a diagnostic with a priority-table entry.** Record the negative-control option under the zirconia pilot only. The honest summary is that the candidate is a well-formed question that the field has already answered in the negative for closed sites and taken up in the positive, with other cations, for open sites.
