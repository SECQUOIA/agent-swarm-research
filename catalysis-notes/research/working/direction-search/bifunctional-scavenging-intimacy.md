# Candidate screen: metal–acid intimacy as inhibitor scavenging in hydroisomerization and hydrocracking

Third-wave candidate screen, 2026-09-16, under the [shared brief](README.md) and written to the "breadth of consequence" bar proposed in [the portfolio review](../../reviews/direction-search-portfolio-review.md), Section 1. No experiments were performed; nothing under `literature/` was changed. This is a screen, not a selected program.

Labels: **E** established by a cited source as read here; **I** inference; **H** hypothesis; **X** unvalidated expectation. The session's web-search budget was already exhausted when this screen began; prior art was located through Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar and Europe PMC records and through direct page fetches where publishers allowed them. Search-limited negatives are marked as such.

## Decision in one paragraph

**Do not promote.** The territory's premise, that metal–acid proximity in bifunctional catalysts acts through removal of mobile inhibitors rather than through alkene transport, does not survive its own source base. Fischer and Iglesia (2023) describe scavenging of *reaction-derived* partially hydrogenated C7 species on crowded Pt at 393 K, and explicitly tested and rejected removal of *inlet* impurities as the cause of the proximity effect. Hu, Noh and Iglesia (2023) then tested Pt-in-zeolite versus Pt-outside-zeolite for n-heptane isomerization with metal and acid probes, H2-pressure kinetics and DFT, and concluded that proximity acts through intracrystalline alkene gradients and hydrogenation of primary isoalkenes, with no diene-mediated channel and no change in site properties. The de Jong, IFPEN and Guisnet groups reach the same alkene-gradient conclusion from the industrial layout side (Pt on binder versus in zeolite) and add that metal/acid balance often dominates distance. A 2001 study already crossed sulfur with metal location, and nitrogen inhibition of dewaxing and hydrocracking is handled industrially by hydrotreating depth, which no proximity effect of plausible size would displace. A scavenging model does make a sign-distinct prediction (the proximity benefit should *grow* with inhibitor pressure, whereas titration of acid sites under the gradient model makes it *shrink*), and a decisive first campaign on standard apparatus exists, but the most probable outcome is the titration-only result, which confirms current practice and changes no decision. The territory therefore fails the "resolved either way" half of the breadth bar. What is retained is a documented test design and a note for literature maintenance, not a diagnostic entry in the priority table.

## 1. Practical problem, scale, and connection to the professors

Bifunctional Pt/zeolite (or Pt/SAPO-11) catalysts hydroisomerize long n-alkanes to branched isomers for diesel and lubricant dewaxing and hydrocrack heavy feeds. The same layout question recurs in every deployed variant: put the metal on the zeolite crystals ("closest" intimacy) or on the alumina binder ("nanoscale"), or blend metal-on-alumina with zeolite ("microscale"). Zečević, Vanbutsele, de Jong and Martens (Nature 2015, [10.1038/nature16173](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature16173)) frame the industrial stake as hydrocracking of fossil and renewable hydrocarbon feeds into high-quality diesel fuel and name gas-to-liquid wax, vegetable oil and algal oil feeds as the ones that "should benefit especially" from Pt on the binder (E, abstract). Renewable-diesel (HVO) dewaxing is a large deployed process and plastic-oil hydrocracking is emerging; a quantitative production figure could not be retrieved from an openly accessible page in this session, so scale is stated qualitatively (I).

The feed-side stake is inhibitor management. Hydroisomerization and hydrocracking feeds carry organic nitrogen, sulfur, oxygenates and polyaromatics. Basic nitrogen titrates Brønsted sites; H2S poisons Pt; polyaromatics compete for both. Refractory weakly basic pyrrolic nitrogen survives deep hydrotreating and still lowers hydrocracking activity and selectivity on a Ni–MoS2/Y–alumina second-stage catalyst (Energy Fuels 2018, [10.1021/acs.energyfuels.8b02045](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.8b02045), E from abstract). Pretreatment depth (hydrotreating severity, guard beds) is therefore an operating decision that interacts with catalyst layout.

Connection to Iglesia: the entire mechanistic basis is his. The inhibitor-scavenging reinterpretation of "hydrogen spillover" ([fischer2023 note](../../../literature/papers/fischer2023-the-nature-of-hydrogen-spillover/paper.md)), the bifunctional scavenging on Pt/oxide in MCH dehydrogenation ([chen2025 note](../../../literature/papers/chen2025-elementary-steps-and-bifunctional-scavenging/paper.md); [zhang2025 note](../../../literature/papers/zhang2025-elementary-steps-site-requirements-and/paper.md)), and the reaction–transport treatment of alkane isomerization on Pt/zeolite cascades (Noh 2018; Hu 2023, Section 3) are all his group's. Connection to Gounder: methodological (site-count-normalized rates, transport-first diagnosis of diffusion-enhanced secondary reactions, [cao2026 note](../../../literature/papers/cao2026-consequences-of-diffusion-enhanced-secondary/paper.md)); no Gounder material is involved.

## 2. The consequential uncertainty

**Does the ranking of metal–acid layouts (closest / nanoscale / microscale) in Pt/zeolite hydroisomerization depend on the concentration of feed-derived inhibitors (basic N, H2S, polyaromatics), because proximity lets one function remove an inhibitor from the other, or is the ranking set by alkene gradients and metal/acid balance alone, with inhibitors acting only by titrating sites?**

Decisions at stake if the first branch held: (a) material: layout choice would have to be qualified on inhibitor-containing feed, and the "Pt on binder" rule of Zečević 2015 could invert for dirty feeds; (b) operating policy: hydrotreating depth could be traded against layout; (c) the same two decisions would transfer to plastic-oil and pyrolysis-oil hydrocracking. If the second branch held, layouts can be ranked on clean model feeds and inhibitor tolerance is a separate, additive specification, which is what current practice assumes.

Why this is the only uncertainty retained. Two others were considered and dropped. "Does H-atom spillover across the alumina binder couple Pt to zeolite acid sites?" is closed: Karim et al. (Nature 2017, [10.1038/nature20782](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature20782)) measured spillover on Al2O3 to be about ten orders of magnitude slower than on TiO2 and limited to the immediate vicinity of the platinum particle (E, abstract), which excludes it as a micrometre-scale coupling mechanism in a binder-supported layout. "Does Pt proximity protect acid sites from coking in hydroisomerization?" is prior art of long standing (Guisnet's metal/acid-ratio criteria; not re-searched here).

## 3. Prior art and the open boundary

### 3.1 What Fischer 2023 and Chen/Zhang 2025 establish, and what they do not

- **E.** On Pt/SiO2 physically mixed with Al2O3, TiO2 or MgO at 393 K, toluene hydrogenation rate enhancements arise because methylcyclohexadiene intermediates that react slowly on Pt can enter the gas phase, migrate to neighboring oxide surfaces and react there (paraphrase); the enhancement weakens with inter-function distance; propanoic-acid titration of oxide acid–base pairs removes it; the oxide hydrogenates cyclohexadiene and cyclohexene but not toluene (Fischer 2023 abstract, local full text; [note](../../../literature/papers/fischer2023-the-nature-of-hydrogen-spillover/paper.md) p.3–8, p.14–20).
- **E, decisive for this screen.** Fischer and Iglesia placed an Al2O3 pre-bed upstream of Pt/SiO2, separated by quartz, to test whether Al2O3 changes the rate by removing impurities from the incoming feed; the pre-bed does not reproduce the enhancement (local full text, Section 2; note p.3–5). They also showed by Si and Fe impurity accounting that the enhancement is intrinsic to Al2O3 surfaces (local full text, Section 3.1). The "inhibitors" in this paper are therefore reactant-derived surface species on a nearly saturated Pt surface, not feed contaminants. The candidate territory's extension to feed-derived N/S/polyaromatic inhibitors is not in the source (I).
- **E.** In MCH dehydrogenation on Pt at 453–513 K, support acid–base pairs scavenge desorbable Pt-bound species; a Pt/SiO2 + Al2O3 physical mixture recovers only part of the supported-Al2O3 enhancement (χ = 1.6 versus 3.7); the enhancement declines with temperature ([chen2025 note](../../../literature/papers/chen2025-elementary-steps-and-bifunctional-scavenging/paper.md) p.9–13). Pd behaves the same way ([zhang2025 note](../../../literature/papers/zhang2025-elementary-steps-site-requirements-and/paper.md) p.8–14).
- **Open in these papers.** The identity of the scavenged species is inferred, not observed; both systems are non-zeolitic oxides at low temperature with saturated metal surfaces; neither paper treats a Brønsted-acid function, alkane isomerization, or feed contaminants.
- **Discrepancy for literature maintenance (not corrected here).** The topic file `literature/topics/metals-oxides-cox-ft-and-lohc.md` (line 79) states that in Fischer 2023 "polyaromatic cofeeds suppress the enhancement". No naphthalene, anthracene, pyrene, polycyclic or cofeed-suppression statement was found in the local Fischer full text; the only cofeeds are 1-/4-methylcyclohexene and 1,3-cyclohexadiene. The claim should be re-sourced or removed by the literature agent.

## 4. Hypothesis, live alternatives, discriminating observations

Define the proximity factor Φ as the ratio of n-alkane isomerization turnover per accessible H+ (or per Pt site, whichever is limiting) between the closest and the microscale layouts at matched conversion, matched Pt dispersion and matched H+ count.

- **H1 (scavenging, the territory's hypothesis).** Part of the proximity effect arises because the near function removes an inhibitor from the far one: Pt hydrogenates or denitrogenates a base before it reaches the acid site, or acid sites bind sulfur/polyaromatic species that would otherwise sit on Pt. Prediction: Φ *increases* with inhibitor pressure, and does so differently for inhibitors that target Pt (H2S) versus H+ (pyridine).
- **H2 (alkene gradients only, Hu/Noh/Iglesia 2023 and de Jong).** Φ reflects intracrystalline alkene gradients set by the Thiele modulus of the acid domain and by metal/acid balance. Inhibitors titrate sites. Prediction: titrating H+ lowers the Thiele modulus, so Φ *decreases* toward 1 with pyridine, and does so identically when the same H+ count is reached synthetically (lower Al content or partial NH4+ exchange) rather than by pyridine; H2S lowers the Pt count and shifts the balance, again matching a synthetically lower Pt loading.
- **H3 (site-property change).** Inhibitors alter intrinsic site properties differently in the two layouts (e.g., Pt inside crystals is shielded from H2S by size exclusion or sees a different local H2/alkene ratio). Prediction: Φ changes with inhibitor, but the change is reproduced by the layout-specific change in *measured* site counts (CO chemisorption after H2S; pyridine-IR H+ count after pyridine), with no residual.

Discriminators. (i) Sign of dΦ/dP_inhibitor: positive for H1, negative or zero for H2. (ii) The synthetic-titration control: if Φ at pyridine-titrated H+ count equals Φ at the same synthetically set H+ count within replicate precision, H1 is excluded for basic N. (iii) Inhibitor-specific residual after re-counting sites separates H3 from H1. (iv) H2 pressure is *not* a discriminator: both H1 (lower coverage of dehydrogenated Pt inhibitors) and H2 (lower alkene pressure, weaker gradients) predict Φ to fall with H2 pressure (I). (v) Polyaromatic co-feed is likewise ambiguous because naphthalene both competes for Pt and, once partially hydrogenated, is itself a mobile species; it is a secondary probe, not a primary one (I).

The one genuinely separable prediction is (i)+(ii). It is a real test; the question is whether its expected answer changes anything.

## 5. Smallest informative first campaign

Materials: one zeolite where clean-feed intimacy effects are documented (Beta, following Oenema 2020) and MFI as the gradient-dominated reference (Hu 2023), each as a 50/50 zeolite/γ-Al2O3 composite in three layouts: Pt-in-zeolite (closest), Pt-on-binder (nanoscale), Pt/γ-Al2O3 + zeolite powder mixture (microscale). Matched Pt dispersion by H2 chemisorption and CO-IR; matched H+ count by pyridine-IR and CH3OH dehydration turnover (Hu 2023's probe); a fourth sample per zeolite with a synthetically lower H+ count (lower Al or partial Na+/NH4+ exchange) at the same layout as the closest sample.

Reaction: gas-phase n-heptane/H2 at 500–550 K and 1–5 bar in a standard packed-bed unit (the Noh/Hu apparatus class), differential conversion, product-resolved GC. Inhibitor series: pyridine 0–5 Pa, H2S 0–50 Pa, naphthalene as a secondary probe; each at three levels, with return-to-clean-feed checks for reversibility. Measure Φ and isomer/β-scission selectivity at matched conversion for every layout × inhibitor cell.

Controls and balances: carbon balance to within analytical precision; site re-counts (CO chemisorption, pyridine-IR) on sacrificial samples after each inhibitor level; bed dilution and crystal-size (two crystal sizes for one zeolite) checks for external and intracrystalline transport, following the Thiele-parameter estimate of the [cao2026 note](../../../literature/papers/cao2026-consequences-of-diffusion-enhanced-secondary/paper.md) p.3–5; two independent preparations per layout.

Decision informed: whether layout can be ranked on clean feed (H2/H3) or must be qualified against the inhibitor spectrum of the intended feed (H1). Numerical targets above are design choices, not predictions.

Frozen prospective prediction (expansion gate): after the pyridine series is complete and before H2S is run, freeze the predicted sign and size of dΦ/dP_H2S for each layout from the model that fits pyridine. Promote only if H1 survives pyridine *and* the frozen H2S prediction succeeds; otherwise close.

Cost: one standard flow unit, six to eight composite samples, one pyridine-IR and chemisorption capability; a few months. Liquid-phase n-hexadecane in a trickle bed is not needed for the first decision and would add transport ambiguity.

## 6. Confidence judgments (in words)

- **Hypothesis (H1).** Low. Fischer 2023's own inlet-impurity test was negative in the oxide system; Hu 2023 found no diene channel and unchanged site properties with intracrystalline Pt; Karim 2017 removes H spillover across alumina; basic N inhibition of solid acids is stoichiometric titration with large binding constants, which leaves little room for a proximity-dependent term. The scavenging premise (a crowded metal surface bearing many partially hydrogenated isomers) applies at 393–453 K on Pt/oxide, not obviously at 500–550 K under H2 where alkene coverages are low (I).
- **Feasibility and informativeness.** Feasibility high: every element is standard and the materials are the Oenema/Hu class. Informativeness moderate: the sign test plus synthetic-titration control does separate H1 from H2, but the expected result is H2, which reproduces Hu 2023 and the de Jong series with an inhibitor axis added.
- **Practical improvement.** Low. Even a positive H1 would move a layout-dependent tolerance term that is small next to the hydrotreating specification refiners already impose; a negative confirms current practice.

## 7. Fair benchmark and stop/pivot rule

Benchmark: the Pt-on-binder composite of Zečević 2015/Oenema 2020 at matched conversion, dispersion and H+ count, on the same inhibitor-spiked feed. A layout claim must beat it on isomer yield at matched conversion with the inhibitor present, not on clean-feed turnover.

Stop rule: if, for pyridine, Φ at each titrated H+ count equals Φ at the synthetically matched H+ count within the replicate precision of two independent preparations, stop; report that layout ranking is inhibitor-independent for basic N and that Fischer-type scavenging does not transfer to Brønsted-acid cascades under these conditions. Pivot rule: if Φ rises with pyridine only for the closest layout and survives site re-counting, run the frozen H2S prediction; if the prediction fails, close as an inhibitor-specific curiosity without a transferable rule.

## 8. Recommendation and breadth-bar evaluation

**Do not promote.** Record as a documented test design, not a priority-table diagnostic.

Against the reviewer's bar:

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Resolved either way, changes ≥2 decision classes or one class across >1 process | **Fails on the "either way" half.** A positive H1 would change material layout and pretreatment policy across hydroisomerization and hydrocracking (meets the bar); the far more probable negative confirms present practice and changes nothing beyond permitting clean-feed layout qualification, which is already the working assumption |
| Decisive first campaign on standard apparatus within a stated horizon | Met (Section 5) |
| Knowledge absent after documented search | Partly met: no proximity × inhibitor-concentration study found (search-limited), but the 2001 sulfur × metal-location paper and Lee & Ihm 2014 are unverified precedents, and the clean-feed mechanism is settled by Hu 2023 and the de Jong series |
| Expansion gate is a frozen prospective prediction | Met (H2S prediction after pyridine) |

The scientific core, that "spillover" in Pt/oxide hydrogenation is molecular scavenging, is Iglesia's established result; its transfer to Brønsted-acid cascades was tested by Iglesia's own group in 2023 for clean feeds and found unnecessary. Extending that test to inhibitor-laden feeds is a legitimate control experiment, not a program. The Pt/LOHC inhibitor-recycling idea declined in [oxide-screen.md](../oxide-screen.md) and [its review](../../reviews/lohc-independent-review.md) is not re-proposed here.

What would revive the territory: an openly reported observation, from an industrial or academic series, that the Pt-on-binder versus Pt-in-zeolite ranking inverts with feed nitrogen or sulfur at matched site counts. None was found. The 2001 and 2014 sulfur/nitrogen papers should be read in full by the literature agent before this conclusion is treated as final.
