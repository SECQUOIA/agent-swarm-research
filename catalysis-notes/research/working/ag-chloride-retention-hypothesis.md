# A specific chloride-excursion hypothesis for Ni-promoted Ag

2026-09-15. Bounded refinement of the [Ag program](../programs/ag-selective-oxygen-use.md), after the [older Ni prior-art review](../reviews/ag-older-ni-prior-art.md) and [revised value review](../reviews/ag-revised-value-review.md). No experiment has been performed.

**Later scope qualification:** the [operating-aging review](../reviews/ag-operating-aging-prior-art.md) puts ordinary drift and a credible chloride operating alternative ahead of a substantial excursion campaign. The protocol below remains a conditional mechanistic branch. It is not the default first program, and material-versus-operation framing alone is not novel.

## Decision

**Test whether Ni preserves a useful Re-dependent response through a chloride excursion, with a benefit that survives return to the reference feed and predicts cumulative EO output under a new excursion duration.** This is specific enough for a bounded investigation. It is not yet evidence for Ni retaining Ag–Re contact, and a shifted optimum inlet chloride or faster recovery alone would be an incremental result.

The consequential hypothesis is:

> At a Ni loading that preserves useful initial output, Ni reduces chloride-driven loss of the Re-containing contribution to selective output. After the chloride excursion, more of that contribution remains accessible, so a prescribed recovery costs less EO production. This advantage cannot be accounted for solely by the measured fresh Ni response or by a simple change in chlorine uptake/removal.

The proposed microscopic explanation is that Ni changes the relocation or return of Re-containing species near Ag. That explanation has lower confidence than the functional hypothesis. Ni could instead sequester Re in an inactive configuration and slow recovery; observing that outcome would reject the proposed beneficial-retention direction. Do not make either sign inevitable by defining every Ni-dependent result as stabilization.

## 1. What prior evidence already supplies

[Hwang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2026.117021), main text Sections 3.3–3.4 and Figures 5–8, report slow reversible chloride-dependent rate/selectivity changes on their Cs/Re-containing materials. A high-to-low chloride return on B-LiCsRe takes approximately 50 h; an upward step on B-CsRe evolves over approximately 10 h. Their transients closely follow the corresponding steady chloride/selectivity curve. The authors interpret this behavior through slow chlorine scavenging and reversible changes in the population of promoter-associated ensembles. They propose, rather than directly image during reaction, Re detachment, storage in Cs-containing domains and restoration of Ag–Re contact.

At intermediate chloride, their useful response includes stronger ethylene dependence and CO2 inhibition, and a different O2 dependence from the low- and high-chloride states. These are a **bundle of functional observations**, not a calibrated count of Re contacts. Their inlet chloride value at maximum O2 consumption is not the value at maximum selectivity. Neither maximum is automatically the optimum useful EO output.

[US5380885A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5380885A/en) already reports dilute-Ni/Cs/Re/S performance retention through severe aging, with endpoint reoptimization, and proposes ionic Ni association with perrhenate. Thus neither Ni–Re association, reversible chloride history, nor generic improved retention is the new question. The proposed addition is a demonstrated relation between a specific excursion, loss/restoration of useful promoted function, and a better material-versus-recovery decision.

[Jalil et al.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adt1213) give an independent reason for caution: Ni changes oxygen activation and combustion chemistry without Cs/Re. A Ni-induced O2 reaction-order change cannot by itself identify retention of a Re-associated channel.

Iyer2021 Section 3.4 predicts local coverage/output by integration from inlet conditions using separately assessed kinetics. Santos2024 reports measured elevated-pressure temperature/O2/water/CO2 effects. Jalil SI confirms an initial Ni/Cl EO rise followed by decline over approximately 20 h, not a recovery benefit. **Additional transient-model boundary:** the [Brandão–Reece preprint](https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv-2024-1f185), pp. 9–19, compares established models with Ag pulse experiments. The original models lack quantitative agreement; the improved two-site description changes oxide decomposition and pulse size to reproduce qualitative titration trends. This is relevant prior art for transient discrimination, not a validated model of Ni/Cs/Re or proof of a unique oxygen reservoir. A fitted trend is therefore insufficient for the proposed useful prediction. The original 23-page preprint was recovered and submitted for centralized reading; differences from the inaccessible journal version remain unchecked.

## 2. The prediction must distinguish two levels of claim

**Functional prediction.** Relative to each material's own unexposed trajectory, the Ni-containing promoted catalyst loses less useful EO output after the same inlet-chloride excursion and needs less recovery time or lost production to regain the specified useful rate/selectivity region. This residual advantage should depend on Re if it represents retention of the Re-containing response.

**Microscopic prediction.** At states with adequately constrained chlorine populations, the Ni material retains more relevant Re–Ag proximity, or restores it more readily, and that difference tracks the recovered function. This requires a measurement sensitive to the proposed proximity change. A Re oxidation-state shift, unchanged total Re, or an average Ni–Re association alone does not establish retention of productive Ag–Re contact.

An operationally useful chloride tolerance can be established without the microscopic claim. However, if a simple chloride adjustment eliminates it and no new useful policy or limit results, this branch does not meet the requested ambition.

## 3. Smallest sequential test

### A. Qualify one matched promoted pair

Use the functioning Cs/Re-containing anchor and one Ni-treated counterpart, with the matched treatment blank, composition and Ag-size checks already required by the main program. Establish independent-preparation reproducibility for the decisive difference. Do not begin with a promoter-loading library.

Choose a common reference feed and temperature where both produce useful EO and where the anchor displays the relevant promoted response. Keep ethane, CO2, water and residence time controlled. If no useful common window exists, compare separately adjusted operating choices, but do not interpret them as the same working-state exposure.

Measure net EO and CO2, reactant consumption, actual bed temperature and the time-dependent baseline. Report conventional carbon selectivity separately from Hwang's `EO/(3 CO2)` ratio. A no-excursion trajectory supplies the counterfactual for ordinary drift. Returning to the initial feed is not itself proof that the initial state has returned.

### B. Apply one consequential high-chloride excursion and observe return

Choose the excursion amplitude from the reproduced anchor's transition out of its useful response, within a technically relevant operating range. Do not copy Hwang's absolute inlet pressures as universal surface coverages. Hold all other inlet conditions and temperature fixed; record actual conversion and product profiles. Use the same inlet history for the pair to answer the causal operating question, while recognizing that Ni may create different internal chlorine exposures.

Record output during the excursion and a predeclared return/recovery interval. The first return should be the ordinary reference feed; an additional low-chloride recovery is a separate intervention, justified only if ordinary return leaves a consequential deficit. Compare that intervention with continued ordinary return, since the catalyst may recover with time alone. Allow the duration to reflect measured slow evolution; do not label an arbitrary short plateau stationary.

For material `j`, calculate the signed EO deficit over a stated common horizon:

`L_j = integral [r_EO,j,unexposed(t) - r_EO,j,excursion(t)] dt`.

Use matched initial catalyst or Ag inventory and report CO2/ethylene consumption alongside `L_j`. Preserve any transient surplus rather than clipping it to zero. This subtraction separates an excursion penalty from a fresh Ni advantage, but does not identify a molecular cause. Also compare the **absolute integrated outputs**: a catalyst with a smaller relative loss can still make less EO overall.

### C. Add only the contrast needed for the claimed Re dependence

If the promoted pair has a consequential difference, a Cs-containing Re-free pair, with and without Ni and matched processing, tests whether the *history-dependent* Ni benefit requires Re. Compare each material's excursion deficit against its own baseline, rather than comparing only fresh rates across different formulations.

An Ni-by-Re difference in these deficits supports Re dependence within this preparation family. It does not prove a common elementary cycle: removing Re can also change chloride response and product gradients. If the same retention benefit appears without Re, reject the specific Re-retention interpretation and assess whether the independent Ni effect is still practically worth pursuing. Do not add the Re-free pair if the original pair has no useful difference.

At the recovered/reference states, repeat one small CO2 perturbation if that response distinguished the fresh useful state. A second O2 perturbation is justified only if it discriminates a remaining alternative. Require reproducible return after the probe; it can itself change the working state. Neither a CO2 inhibition slope nor an O2 order is a unique Re-contact marker. Joint preservation of EO output, selectivity and the anchor's response is stronger functional evidence than any one derivative.

## 4. Chlorine is the critical identifiability limit

Inlet organochlorine is a controlled input. It is not surface chlorine coverage, especially when Ni changes deposition, removal or oxygen activation. Matching the inlet feed, the nominal chloriding index, total retained Cl, or one steady rate cannot establish equal active chlorine populations.

Where existing analysis permits, measure the inlet/outlet chlorine-containing fluxes during loading and removal, including relevant conversion products and reactor blanks. A closed dynamic chlorine balance can identify a gross inventory difference. Missing chlorine products, trace-level subtraction and stored reactor chlorine can make the estimate uninformative. Do not call an unclosed balance a coverage measurement.

Compare the simple explanation first: can changed chlorine uptake/removal plus each material's fresh operating response account for the observed loss and recovery? If it can, claim the resulting operating relationship, not promoter retention. If it cannot, the residual motivates a promoter-state measurement; failure of that simple account does not uniquely prove relocation, because a total inventory hides site-specific chlorine distributions.

For a **contact-retention claim**, require a demonstrated ability to distinguish the relevant Re environment or location in the compared states, with adequate sensitivity at the actual loading. Re-edge local coordination or spatially resolved promoter mapping might help, but neither is assumed sufficient or available. Quenching, gas removal and vacuum can redistribute the same mobile species under investigation. Validate state preservation before relying on an ex situ difference. If no accessible measurement discriminates the contact alternatives, retain the functional claim or stop the structural branch rather than append a broad characterization campaign.

## 5. One held-out excursion and a decision

Use the first bounded excursion/return to choose **one new duration at the same excursion amplitude**. Before collecting it, state:

- The expected ordering of both absolute integrated EO output and excursion-induced deficit for the pair.
- Whether the prescribed recovery should eliminate, reduce or preserve that ordering over the fixed horizon.
- The selected ordinary-operation or recovery policy for each material, including time, chloride/ethane demand and production lost during intervention.

A useful numerical prediction requires an identifiable transient description from the calibration data. A simple relaxation description can be used only if it represents both observed EO and CO2 evolution and has constrained parameters. It is an empirical prediction tool, not a measurement of promoter populations. Do not assume an arbitrary hidden state, fit multiple unobserved reservoirs, or change the model after the held-out result and call the original prediction successful. If only a directional prediction is supportable, say so and require the magnitude to matter for the operating choice.

The positive-retention hypothesis predicts that an excursion duration causing consequential loss of the Re-dependent response in the Ni-free material causes less loss in the Ni material after accounting for their fresh outputs. It does **not** automatically predict faster recovery once equal functional loss has occurred: stronger retention and faster reattachment are different claims. The held-out test should discriminate the selected one.

The practical conclusion is conditional on demonstrated exposure frequency and relevance. No industrial excursion frequency is established here. Better output under an invented rare upset does not establish routine catalyst-life value. A deliberately damaging pulse can be an informative chemical probe while having negligible operating value. Require either a demonstrated connection to ordinary-operation drift or a documented disturbance frequency and measured output cost before making a substantial practical-benefit claim. Startup recovery is a one-time cost unless evidence establishes repetition.

A convincing result would show, for that documented burden, when ordinary recovery or a defined recovery intervention substitutes for Ni and when Ni retains an integrated-output advantage despite those alternatives. Use measured integrals and the [practical accounting limits](../calculations/epoxidation-practical-metrics.md); do not invent a cycle interval to make one option win. This is more demanding than predicting another selectivity point, and a favorable pulse result cannot substitute for normal-operation retention.

## 6. Stop rules and candid assessment

- Stop this branch if the functioning matched pair has no reproducible, consequential excursion penalty or Ni-dependent difference.
- Reject the proposed Re-retention interpretation if the difference is fully described by the independent Ni response, or if the necessary Re dependence fails. A separate useful Ni result needs its own rationale.
- Do not claim microscopic contact retention from kinetic markers, a total chlorine balance, or bulk promoter retention. Stop that attribution if the remaining alternatives cannot be distinguished with a bounded measurement.
- Reject the specific prediction if the held-out duration/policy reverses the preregistered ordering beyond uncertainty. Do not rescue it by chloride retuning or broadening the composition search.
- Demote the program if existing chlorine-dynamics literature already supplies the same relationship, or if the only result is a small tolerance shift without an improved operating decision.

**Scientific confidence:** plausible but low that Ni specifically preserves productive Re proximity; moderate that a functioning pair can reveal a consequential difference in loss/recovery if one exists. **Experimental confidence:** moderate for the output/history comparison, lower for separating site-specific chlorine dynamics from Re relocation. **Practical confidence:** low before measuring the output benefit and establishing a relevant exposure burden.

This is a credible focused branch, not yet a strong stand-alone mechanistic program. Its strength is the falsifiable connection between a material modification and a recovery choice. Its chief weakness is that Ni-dependent chlorine dynamics can imitate promoter retention, while Hwang already describes reversible contact loss and the older patent already proposes Ni–perrhenate association. Expansion is justified only by a new predictive relationship that changes useful operation, with chemical attribution no stronger than the measurements support.

## 7. Early operational gate: does the transient leave the steady operating curve?

**Use this comparison before chlorine-inventory or Re-location work when the necessary steady data are already resolved.** Hwang's transient/steady-curve agreement makes it a relevant null test, not a new mechanistic diagnostic. It adds information beyond comparing endpoint recovery times: it asks whether the transient visits output combinations unavailable on the material's familiar steady chloride curve.

For each material separately, use its independently measured steady curve

`C_j(c) = (net EO rate, net CO2 rate)`

at fixed temperature, nonchlorine feed, total flow and residence time; `c` labels inlet chloride used to prepare each steady point. The operational null is that each transient rate pair agrees, within joint measurement and steady-curve uncertainty, with **some point on that material's curve**. The corresponding curve coordinate is only a label. It is not instantaneous inlet chloride or measured surface coverage. Use both rates together; allowing a separate curve coordinate for each rate would remove the test. Preserve all resolved branches where the curve turns or steady histories differ.

Use interpolation only over a sufficiently resolved measured interval. Do not invent a many-parameter curve, extrapolate beyond the steady data, or rescale transient axes until they agree. Account for ordinary drift using the unexposed control and repeated reference points; if drift changes the curve by an unknown amount, the comparison is unresolved. Net CO2 subtraction uncertainty, including shared flow/calibration errors between the two rates, can make this gate weak. A single noisy off-curve point is insufficient; require a reproducible excursion beyond the joint uncertainty.

Compare after the independently measured gas-switch/analyzer response, or propagate that known response through the steady-output prediction. Gas holdup, unequal analytical delays and product storage can create an artificial departure. Even after correcting external transport, an axial mixture of differently chlorided regions can fall off a curved steady locus while every local region follows familiar chemistry. Therefore, a sustained departure can establish failure of this **whole-bed steady-curve description**, but cannot uniquely establish a new surface state, promoter relocation or an additional elementary route. Low-gradient operation or an already justified reactor description can narrow that ambiguity; a new distributed-state fitting campaign is not part of this gate.

**If trajectories collapse:** there is no resolved new rate-pair relationship in this projection. Defer structural escalation unless the time spent traversing the curve creates a consequential, independently predictable output difference or another observation demands it. Ni can still preserve useful operation longer, shorten costly recovery, or change the steady curve itself. Several coupled chemical states can move together along one curve; collapse does not prove chlorine-only control or reject promoter retention. It also does not predict the speed of travel, so it cannot by itself predict integrated EO output under the held-out history.

**If trajectories reproducibly depart:** select the cheapest discriminating follow-up among unresolved product/thermal gradients, drift, chlorine dynamics and promoter-state change. Do not jump directly to Re spectroscopy. Proceed only if the departure changes the proposed material or recovery decision. Thus this gate can prevent unnecessary chemical attribution, while neither curve collapse nor departure is a requirement for a practically useful Ni effect.
