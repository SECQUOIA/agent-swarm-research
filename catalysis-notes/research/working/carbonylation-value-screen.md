# Zeolite carbonylation: useful remaining uncertainty, but no stronger program selected

Initial screen: 2026-09-15. Updated after the literature uploads: 2026-09-16. Independent bounded screen; no experiments performed. This screen does not promote carbonylation above the existing Ag/polymer pilots.

## Decision

**Do not launch a new passivation, hydrophobic-coating, coke-control, or generic regeneration campaign.** These strategies have direct, consequential precedents. Retain one conditional experiment: determine whether a specified wet feed can be carbonylated on an existing strong material with enough sustained output to avoid a specified drying/separation operation. Proceed only if the feed and avoided operation are real choices for an available platform. A generic water-tolerance map would not satisfy this condition.

The hypothesis is that suppressing *working-state water occupancy* can preserve carbonylation at a relevant mixed-vapor water activity, with an output advantage that survives equilibration and reuse. This is chemically plausible, but neither hydrophobicity nor the idea of reducing water inhibition is original. The possible contribution is a transferable, experimentally validated relationship between water exposure, retained catalytic function, and a consequential drying/recycle decision. Current evidence does not establish that this opportunity is larger than the existing portfolio opportunities.

The connection to Iglesia is direct: methyl-to-acetyl chemistry and confinement in mordenite (MOR) eight-membered-ring side pockets. The connection to Gounder is site siting, accessible acid populations, and catalyst history. The [existing Bhan–Iglesia review](../../literature/papers/bhan2008-a-link-between-reactivity-and/paper.md) and [Xing 2025 targeted-dealumination study](../../literature/papers/xing2025-targeted-dealumination-via-in-situ/paper.md) establish the background; neither establishes a new direction by itself.

## What the strongest primary evidence already covers

- **Product inhibition and product protection coexist.** Rasmussen et al. measured methyl-acetate (MA) inhibition at high pressure and modeled it alongside carbonylation kinetics. Their explanation of the inhibited state remains conditional. Separately, BP patent experiments already add MA or acetic acid (AA) to suppress methane/hydrocarbon formation. Therefore product-containing recycle cannot simply be categorized as contamination, and deliberately cofeeding product is established. [Rasmussen 2017](https://doi.org/10.1039/C6CY01904H); [US8624054B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8624054B2/en).
- **Removing 12-MR acidity does not eliminate every coke pathway.** Cheng 2021 identifies an 8-MR contribution (main abstract read). Xie 2023 demonstrates cyclic oxygenates on pyridine-modified MOR and proposes acetyl/ketene accumulation followed by larger oxygenate formation. Its experiments already connect higher residual DME concentration with less deactivation. Xie 2024 identifies CO/MA–olefin coupling and methyl cyclopentenones in unmodified MOR (abstract and retrieved original inspected). Thus acetyl consumption, nonaromatic deposits, and operating composition are established research directions. [Cheng](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.1c00159); [Xie 2023](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.3c04344); [Xie 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcata.2024.119701).
- **A strong dry-feed reference exists.** Fan 2024 combines Cu/Zn promotion, 26 h precoking and H2 cofeed. This is a much stronger durability comparison than rapidly deactivating parent MOR. The detailed benchmark and unresolved numerical inconsistencies are below. [Fan](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsami.3c18170).
- **Wet tandem operation and regeneration are established.** Upham 2021 uses phosphate-passivated MOR after a commercial Cu/ZnO/AlMgOx methanol catalyst, without interstage separation. AA and MA formation, coke, phosphorus retention and oxidative recovery are already studied together. The high reported selectivity must be read with low single-pass yields and catalyst amounts; it is not evidence that direct tandem operation is economically superior. [Upham](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2021.04.029).
- **Hydrophobic protection has a direct recent demonstration.** Han 2025 combines surface methylation with a 1:1 physical mixture of MOR and PTFE/CNT promoter for wet CO2-to-AA/MA tandem chemistry. It reports 50 h operation and attributes improvements to water exclusion/escape. Its water-transport simulations and room-temperature MA uptake do not independently establish working-site water occupancy in the hot multicomponent gas. The observed performance improvement is real reported evidence; the unique causal attribution remains less secure. [Han](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66117-9).
- **The now-read 2026 Cu-MOR tandem reference sharpens the comparison.** Shimura et al. report maximum combined AA/MA selectivity **93.7% at 2.8% CO conversion**, using 0.5 g CuMgAlOx (CMA) plus 3.0 g Cu-MOR at 230 °C and 5 MPa. Maximum reported yield **3.22%** belongs to a different catalyst ratio. The separate **10 h** time course uses Y-containing CMYA and includes about **7 h of induction** before AA/MA selectivity settles near 85%. This is a useful wet tandem reference, not evidence of high single-pass yield or long durability at the maximum-selectivity condition. Selectivity and productivity definitions still require the missing SI; details follow below. [Shimura](https://doi.org/10.1039/D6CY00105J).

## Benchmarks that constrain practical claims

### Fan 2024: dry DME/CO with H2-rich cofeed

Main §2.3 uses 0.2 g catalyst, 275 °C, 4 MPa, DME/CO/H2 = 5/35/60 and nominal GHSV 10,000 mL gcat−1 h−1. The CuZn sample first experiences 26 h of DME/CO/N2 to establish coke. Figure 6 then shows about 30% DME conversion, MA selectivity above 98%, and MA space-time yield about 0.4–0.5 g gcat−1 h−1 over roughly 500 h of H2-containing operation. This is a strong continuous-output demonstration, not a repeated regeneration demonstration.

Figure 6 and the methods both specify **10,000** mL gcat−1 h−1; independent inspection corrected the initial screen's erroneous 9000 reading. A duration inconsistency remains: §3.7 says the experiment ends at **514 h**, whereas abstract/conclusions and nearby prose say **over 520 h**. Figure 6 visibly extends slightly beyond 500 h. Preserve the approximate result; do not create an exact integrated production number or exact lifetime from these statements. Initial induction is visible, and the preceding 26 h conditioning belongs in full-cycle accounting. Original Figure 6 and §3.7 were visually checked.

H2/DME feed ratio is 12. This is an **inlet ratio**; the experiment demonstrates neither a recycle loop nor net H2 consumption. It is not automatically an additional dedicated hydrogen burden if syngas is already available. Quantitative process comparison needs measured H2 loss, methane formation, purge, recycle compression and feed-source boundaries. A lower-H2 experiment is potentially useful only if one of those costs is demonstrably material. H2-assisted recovery itself has older patent precedent (US8329606B2).

### Han 2025: reported organic selectivity is not overall feed-carbon yield

Main Figure 2 uses 0.5 g GaZrOx upstream, 0.5 g zeolite downstream, and an additional 0.5 g PTFE/CNT promoter in the full hydrophobic case. Nominal conditions are 623 K upstream, 558 K downstream, 5 MPa, and H2/CO2/Ar = 71.25/23.75/5. Methods specify 21 mL min−1 feed. The promoter adds real bed mass and volume; report output per zeolite, per total solids, and per occupied bed volume where available.

SI Table 2 reports for the full treatment:

- CO2 conversion = 27.5% of entering CO2.
- CO selectivity = 68.0% of converted carbon.
- AA and MA = 45.5% and 32.0% of **organic-product carbon**, excluding CO.

Using those stated denominators, the inferred once-through fraction of feed carbon leaving as AA+MA is

`0.275 × (1 − 0.680) × (0.455 + 0.320) = 0.0682`, or **6.82%**.

The corresponding inferred fraction for unmodified MOR-8 in the same table is `0.278 × (1 − 0.768) × (0.288 + 0.125) = 2.66%`. Thus the table supports an appreciable reported carbonylation improvement, while most feed carbon remains unconverted or exits as CO. These are calculations from reported fractions, conditional on the stated balance and definitions, not new measured balances or recycle-process yields. CO may be recycled; it is not automatically a terminal waste. No recycle advantage or climate benefit follows without the complete process boundary.

SI Table 4 reports AA+MA space-time yield **25.9 mg gcat−1 h−1**, far below Fan's mass-specific dry-feed value but for a different feed, catalyst ensemble and chemistry; direct ranking is inappropriate. The exact mass denominator of this entry needs confirmation before further normalization. The [independent benchmark review](../reviews/carbonylation-benchmark-review.md) finds evidence favoring a GaZrOx-plus-zeolite mass basis, excluding PC, but leaves the Table 4 denominator unresolved. Figure 4 reports mmol g−1 h−1, not grams. Its source-data workbook shows approximately maintained acetate selectivity over roughly 50 h, with late values near 73–74%; it does not support strictly above 75% throughout or zero deactivation. This finite test supplies neither integrated lifetime productivity nor repeat regeneration performance. Keep the 6.82% and 2.66% calculations tied to SI Table 2; Figure 2 source data and Table 4 report slightly different product fractions.

**Temperature inconsistency:** SI Tables 2–3 list upstream **673 K**, while main Figure 2 and SI Table 4 list **623 K** for the principal comparison. This appears in the original SI table images; it is not an extraction error. Do not silently choose one. SI Table 1 gives upstream effluent at 623 K/5 MPa: CO 278.4 kPa, methanol 42.7 kPa, DME 21.1 kPa, and stoichiometrically inferred water formation 3.91 mmol h−1. The water rate is not a measured working-pore inventory.

The main study measures MA uptake at **298 K and 0–10 mbar**. Its approximately twofold change in an inferred diffusion coefficient cannot rule out hot, competitive reactant/product transport changes at 558 K and 5 MPa. Likewise, droplet contact angle is not a direct measure of water occupancy at the catalytic side pocket. This limits mechanistic transfer, without negating the reported catalyst comparison.

### Upham 2021: wet operation and one recovery cycle, with useful limits

The tandem example uses 210 °C, 25 bar, and CO/CO2/H2 = 15/5/5 sccm. Multiple catalyst loadings are tested; interpret the figure-specific amounts. Figure 9 follows 50 h operation with air regeneration at 500 °C for 5 h. Methods specify a 1 °C min−1 ramp; §3.4 says 0.5 °C min−1. This is another protocol detail to resolve before replication. The zeolite is removed separately to avoid contaminating the Cu catalyst.

Table 1 records phosphate retention after an initial P/Al decrease, through 241 turnovers and another 128 turnovers after regeneration, where turnover is moles of acetate products per estimated 8-MR Al. This is meaningful recovery evidence; it is not unlimited cycling or a measured count of identical working sites. Gas-line metal-carbonyl traps and heated product lines are explicit experimental precautions in the paper, relevant to reproducibility.

### Shimura 2026: high selectivity at low conversion; distinguish the operating cases

The [retained main article](../../literature/papers/shimura2026-direct-synthesis-of-methyl-acetate/original.pdf) closes the earlier source-access gap. The 93.7% selectivity case uses 0.5 g CMA plus 3.0 g Cu(3.1)-MOR(18), 230 °C, 5 MPa, 150 mL min−1 syngas and 50 mL min−1 N2. The separate maximum reported yield, 3.22%, uses 0.75 g CMA plus 2.25 g Cu-MOR. Figure 1 instead follows 0.5 g **CMYA** plus 2.5 g Cu-MOR: CO conversion stays near 3.5%, while combined AA/MA selectivity rises from about 70% to 85% during approximately 7 h of induction and stays near that level to 10 h. Do not combine these results into one catalyst's high-yield durability demonstration.

The main article reports maximum space-time yield **0.67 mmol g−1 h−1**, referring to SI Table S9; the mass definition and mathematical selectivity convention were not established from the inspected main text. The [SI package](../../literature/papers/shimura2026-supporting-information-for-direct-synthesis/paper.md) is metadata-only and unread. Do not import Han's CO-free-organic-carbon denominator or compare this molar productivity directly with Fan's mass productivity. Even `2.8% × 93.7% ≈ 2.62%` is conditional on selectivity being defined per converted CO carbon; use the explicitly reported yields until the denominator is verified.

Two protocol details need clarification before replication. The original methods print packed granule sizes **0.35–0.71 nm**; millimetres are physically plausible but unconfirmed. The headline GHSV 3.4 L g−1 h−1 agrees with total gas flow divided by total solids. The later GHSV series numerically agrees with syngas flow alone, without its separate N2 flow; preserve component flows rather than assume a common convention. See the [post-upload audit](../reviews/post-upload-carbonylation-audit.md) for original-PDF locators.

The proposed poisoning of H-MOR by dehydration-derived water is explicitly speculative in this paper. Cu promotion is demonstrated, but its cause is not isolated through a water-on/water-off comparison. The adsorption and Cu-state evidence does not measure working water occupancy.

## One conditional discriminating experiment

**Decision to specify first:** Is drying a particular DME/recycle or methanol-synthesis effluent worth its measured separation and reheating burden? Choose that feed and a credible existing catalyst. A new CO2-conversion train is not justified merely to create a wet-feed test.

1. **Gate entry on an actual decision and adequate measurement.** Record the available wet feed composition, the drying/separation alternative, its measured or explicitly bounded energy and material costs, and the minimum output or cost difference that would change the choice. Select the reference for that feed: Fan for a comparable dry DME/CO stream; Cu-MOR tandem or an existing hydrophobic material for the relevant wet route. Do not compare different feed routes by catalyst-mass productivity alone. If the platform, feed or avoided operation is unspecified, retain the proposal without starting the campaign.
2. **Commission the reference and predefine the observation window.** Quantify MA, AA, methanol, DME, hydrocarbons, CO and water, and bound retained carbon. Check heated-line recovery, analytical residence time, temperature and pellet/contact effects. Establish the reference's induction and ordinary drift before deciding the exposure and recovery durations; Shimura's approximately 7 h induction shows why a short nominal steady state is insufficient. Set balance and rate-repeatability tolerances from calibration and blank runs, tight enough to resolve the preselected useful difference. Failure to meet them stops chemical interpretation.
3. **Run a minimal controlled comparison.** Use at least two independently conditioned beds per arm: a measured target-water step followed by return to the common clean feed, and a sham gas switch with the same elapsed time. Hold CO, DME, methanol, product and H2 partial pressures, total flow and pressure constant by replacing inert with water; record departures if the real feed cannot be represented this way. Hold each stage until water and production settle or the predefined observation limit is reached. Use the common clean-feed return to assess persistence relative to sham drift, not to claim irreversible damage. If carryover prevents a common baseline, use fresh beds or a reversed-order check before attributing a state change. Add a no-zeolite blank for line hydrolysis and a matched-volume inert blank when testing a solid promoter.
4. **Test the simplest adequate prediction.** Fit ordinary inhibition plus measured transport/line response to the first water history; predict a withheld history drawn from the actual feed variation. Add a persistent-state term only if the sham-corrected data require it. A fit-derived occupancy is not an independently measured site inventory. Pass this gate only if reproducible observations distinguish the models at the useful-decision scale and the prediction works without fitting the withheld history. If uncertainty spans the practical choice after the predefined finite study, report that limit and stop expansion.
5. **Compare complete operating choices over a common horizon.** Keep the matched chemistry test separate from the practical comparison: the best dry and wet policies may use different published materials and conditioning. For each policy, integrate net acetate-unit output, `N = integral[(F_MA,out + F_AA,out) − (F_MA,in + F_AA,in)] dt`, over a common calendar-time horizon including conditioning, drying interruptions, recovery and regeneration. Each MA or AA molecule contributes one acetate unit; separately report product mass, MA/AA split, carbon balance and required product purification because the two products have different carbon contents and market/separation value. Include total solids and occupied volume, actual H2 loss/purge, drying/reheating and compression requirements, and replacement/regeneration consumption. Do not charge all incoming H2 as consumed or infer an unmeasured recycle credit. Report the uncertainty in the difference and whether it crosses the predeclared decision threshold; no finite horizon establishes lifetime performance.

A water isotope pulse or operando OH/adsorbate measurement is an optional discriminator if rates cannot distinguish adsorption from persistent change. It is not the initial campaign. Water can exchange oxygen with products and surface species, so label movement alone is not site protection. A full molecular mechanism is unnecessary if the measured relationship already predicts a consequential operating choice.

**End mechanistic expansion** if a published strong material already handles the target feed, ordinary inhibition predicts the relevant behavior, or better wet performance does not change useful throughput/cost. A predicted water penalty smaller than the drying burden can resolve a useful decision in favor of wet operation; it is not a failed practical outcome. Resolve contact/transport confounding before chemical interpretation. A negative result can establish an allowable moisture burden or reject an expensive coating/cleanup step. **Expand** only after the relationship predicts a useful choice at an untested feed condition; a higher contact angle or less coke is insufficient.

## Confidence and ranking

- **Scientific hypothesis:** low-to-moderate confidence that working water occupancy, beyond conventional inhibition, is the consequential missing variable for a specified process. Direct hydrophobic performance evidence supports plausibility; unique attribution and general transfer are unresolved.
- **Feasibility and informativeness:** moderate, conditional on an available high-pressure CO/oxygenate flow platform and credible balances. A finite wet/dry/recovery comparison is conventional; site-specific occupancy attribution is harder.
- **Meaningful practical improvement:** low on current evidence. Dry catalyst productivity is already substantial, wet tandem selectivity alone overstates full-route performance, and real drying/recycle costs are not established here.

This is a conditional validation opportunity, not a selected substantial original program. Improving an existing dry-carbonylation loop may ultimately be more useful than constructing a new CO2 tandem route, but this screen does not establish either choice.
