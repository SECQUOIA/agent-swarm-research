## Decision

**No liquid-phase opportunity examined here has a sufficiently supported intervention and practical advantage to displace the reviewed Ag/polymer investigations.** This is a negative selection result, not a claim that liquid-phase biomass conversion lacks important problems. Concentrated-feed conversion is a real process target, but neither a concentration-dependent rate nor a new working-site descriptor establishes a useful solution.

The screen began with the existing [biomass topic](../../literature/topics/biomass-oxygenates-and-liquid-phase-catalysis.md), [confinement topic](../../literature/topics/confinement-solvation-transport-and-deactivation.md), and underlying Gounder, Botti, and Liu papers. Targeted primary-literature checks then tested two possible advantages: separating the sugar-fragmentation and lactate-forming reactions, and using solid Lewis acids for concentrated lactose conversion. Generic hydration descriptors, Sn leaching, hydrophobic pores, and impurity guards were excluded as proposed novelty.

## Closest candidate: reduce solvent demand without sacrificing useful sugar carbon

### Actual bottleneck and direct connection

Glucose-to-methyl-lactate chemistry can give impressive catalyst-volume productivity while processing dilute sugar. Botti et al.'s continuous comparisons used 1 wt% glucose; their promoted postsynthetic Sn-Beta initially gave about 75% methyl lactate plus more than 20% methyl vinyl glycolate, but combined yield reached roughly 45–50% after 24 h. Initial combined ester yield is therefore neither sustained methyl-lactate yield nor a concentrated-feed result. [Original full text](../../literature/papers/botti2022-high-productivity-continuous-conversion-of/fulltext.md), PDF pp. 9–11, especially Figure 11; [read note](../../literature/papers/botti2022-high-productivity-continuous-conversion-of/paper.md); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.1c06989).

**Calculated illustration, not an energy estimate:** a 1 wt% sugar solution carries 99 kg of other feed components per kg sugar; a 10 wt% solution carries 9 kg. Actual separation duty depends on conversion, solvent recovery, recycle, and heat integration. Higher concentration can also change solubility, viscosity, water activity, and product branching. No achievable 10 wt% operating point or net saving is established here.

The connection to **Gounder** is direct: quantitative Lewis-acid sugar kinetics, substrate access, and solvent inhibition in his 2013 work. Its glucose experiments covered 0.5–1.5 wt%, so extrapolating their dilute first-order behavior to concentrated feeds is unsupported. [Original full text](../../literature/papers/gounder2013-monosaccharide-and-disaccharide-isomerization-over/fulltext.md), PDF pp. 3–5, 11–12; [read note](../../literature/papers/gounder2013-monosaccharide-and-disaccharide-isomerization-over/paper.md); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2013.06.016). The competing papers below include **Jose Iglesias**, who is not Enrique Iglesia.

### Hypothesis considered, and why it fails the promotion gate

**Proposed hypothesis:** increasing sugar concentration raises small-carbonyl condensation relative to conversion into lactate. A separate catalyst that rapidly converts those fragments into lactate could preserve target-product yield at lower solvent demand. That is measurable and has a plausible kinetic basis. It is not yet supported as the dominant loss mechanism in a concentrated glucose feed.

Three primary-source checks weaken its claimed advantage:

1. **Splitting the catalytic tasks is already established.** Orazov and Davis combined distinct retro-aldol and hydride-shift catalysts to form lactates from ketohexoses around 100 °C. A tandem catalyst, lower temperature, or reduced deposition is not new by itself. This screen read the PubMed abstract and indexed primary-text passages; it did not retrieve and read the complete original/SI. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26372958/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1516466112).

2. **Condensation is not necessarily lost carbon.** The 2024 real-hemicellulose study reports aldol reactions of small sugars and attributes convergence of different feed sugars into a limited ester slate partly to aldol/retro-aldol chemistry. Suppressing all fragment recombination could therefore sacrifice useful pentose carbon. That does not prove recombination is beneficial at every concentration. Available evidence here is indexed primary text, including the conclusion; full-PDF reading remains pending. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10880092/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.3c07356).

3. **A stronger continuous comparator exists.** The 2025 [K]Sn-USY study used 40 g/L sugar feeds at 150 °C and reports methyl-lactate yields above 30% for more than 140 h with potassium addition, including real hydrolysate. Separate DHA and glycolaldehyde feeds did not reproduce the sugar-induced activity decline; potassium loss and furanic deposits were implicated. Those experiments weaken a fragment-condensation explanation of deactivation at their conditions, while leaving higher-concentration selectivity open. The original PDF was read, particularly pp. 2–4. [Full text](https://burjcdigital.urjc.es/server/api/core/bitstreams/faf9c304-78d0-4b24-8792-9a0fab58fc43/content); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.5c06986).

The remaining gap is thus **whether a particular concentrated feed has a consequential, avoidable carbon-branching penalty**. It is not evidence that the proposed intervention will solve one. Adding isotope measurements to established tandem catalysis does not by itself make the program stronger.

### What would justify reopening it

This is a gate for reconsideration, not a recommended campaign. First show a reproducible concentration-dependent loss of the desired isolated product on the strongest relevant promoted catalyst, at matched conversion and measured solvent composition. Establish feed solubility, carbon recovery, and mixing/particle-size independence before attributing a loss to chemistry.

Only if that loss exists would a minimal causal comparison be justified: the same retro-aldol catalyst with an active fragment-conversion cocatalyst versus a matched inactive dilution control, both at dilute and concentrated feed. A tracer-level labelled fragment could test whether the intervention diverts its carbon from recombined products into lactate. Match residence time and conversion, quantify unreacted sugars and all significant carbon fractions, and repeat the decisive pair independently. A labelled recombination product alone would establish a route, not its importance or preventability.

The fair catalyst benchmarks are promoted postsynthetic Sn-Beta and potassium-maintained Sn-USY under their appropriate operating windows, followed by the actual mixed feed. Compare sustained isolated methyl-lactate productivity, carbon yield, solvent use, salt demand, and regeneration burden. Include methyl vinyl glycolate separately; count its value only with a credible product/separation outlet. A final process comparison would also require a relevant fermentation/esterification route; no superiority to that route is claimed.

**Stop:** no material concentration penalty; recombination mainly recovers carbon; improvement disappears at matched conversion; or added catalyst/separation/regeneration demands erase the solvent reduction. Do not pivot automatically to a generic hydration or poisoning program.

| Judgment | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis plausibility | Moderate for concentration-dependent reaction branching; low for assigning it as the present limiting loss without measurements. |
| Experimental feasibility and information | Moderate. Feed/product analysis and matched catalyst comparisons are accessible; concentrated-liquid transport and isotope carbon accounting are substantial confounders. |
| Meaningful practical gain | Low and unestablished. No measured concentrated-feed penalty has yet been connected to a selective remedy, and strong tandem/continuous competitors already exist. |

## Short rejection: concentrated lactose-to-lactulose

Gounder and Davis directly demonstrated lactose conversion over Sn-Beta, but the original 1 wt% study fitted a lactulose/lactose equilibrium ratio of 0.19 ± 0.02 at 373 K and reached only a few turnovers. A faster solid catalyst alone cannot remove the dilute-mixture thermodynamic constraint; concentrated-mixture activities and separations would need independent assessment. [Original full text](../../literature/papers/gounder2013-monosaccharide-and-disaccharide-isomerization-over/fulltext.md), PDF pp. 9–10, Tables 4–5 and Figure 6; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2013.06.016).

A strong non-zeolite comparator was already reported in 2015: permeabilised cells containing cellobiose 2-epimerase converted a 600 g/L lactose feed to about 391 g/L lactulose in 2 h under the selected phosphate-buffer conditions. The full HTML methods and results were read. This does not establish industrial adoption or a common equilibrium with the dilute Sn-Beta experiment; it does rule out treating concentration or high lactulose titre as an unmet achievement. [Primary full text, abstract and “Effect of different buffer systems”](https://academic.oup.com/ijfst/article/50/7/1625/7866063); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/ijfs.12776).

No specific selective-separation or reaction intervention emerged that plausibly beats that competitor while retaining a mechanistic question beyond established Lewis-acid/solvation work. Do not initiate a Sn-Beta lactose program on the basis of the 2013 catalyst comparison alone.
