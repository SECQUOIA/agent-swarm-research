# Ammonia cracking: distinguish hydrogen removal from an ideal kinetic limit

Updated 2026-09-16. Root screening analysis; no experiments performed. The [fresh independent review](../reviews/ammonia-hydrogen-inference.md) checked the key original figures, balances, pressure bounds and prior-art limits. This is not a selected substantial program.

## Question and current decision

Can a physically attainable reduction in local hydrogen pressure unlock enough ammonia-cracking rate to change catalyst inventory, operating temperature or recovered hydrogen output? This is consequential for ammonia as a hydrogen carrier and connects to Iglesia's reaction kinetics, coverage effects and reactor analysis. That intellectual connection does not establish available equipment or a faculty priority.

Hydrogen inhibition, membrane-assisted decomposition and mechanistic ambiguity already have extensive precedents. A new isotope transient, another fitted rate law, or an ideal zero-hydrogen simulation is not sufficient novelty. The promising uncertainty is narrower: **does the rate gain inferred from a reactive oxygen probe transfer to oxygen-free hydrogen removal at attainable local pressure?** An informative answer could constrain a reactor design even if no new catalyst emerges. No practical advantage is established here. The independent review and [post-upload audit](../reviews/post-upload-pdh-ammonia-audit.md) establish direct kinetic membrane-design precedent from now-available main texts. The outstanding issue is whether the specific oxygen-free response changes a useful catalyst/device decision, not whether hydrogen removal can help in general.

## What the recovered primary evidence establishes

Qiu et al. [2026, DOI 10.1039/D6RE00086J](https://doi.org/10.1039/D6RE00086J) report a 1 wt% Ru/MgAl2O4 catalyst, 300 mg diluted with 300 mg quartz, 75–106 µm particles and 20,000 normal L kgcat−1 h−1. NH3 feeds span 0.3–2.5% in He, with separate H2 cofeeds up to 25%. The original main and SI were recovered from CNR. Root read the methods, model, diagnostic tests and relevant characterization; original main Figure 3 and SI Table S1/Figure S1 were visually checked.

The study fits an empirical rate expression:

`r = k(T) P_NH3 (1 + K_H2 P_H2)^−1.5 (1 − η)`.

Here `η` is the reaction quotient divided by the equilibrium constant. Figure 1 gives `K_H2 = 5000 atm−1`; the authors explicitly state that the modified equation has no direct mechanistic derivation. Hydrogen cofeed substantially suppresses conversion, a meaningful observation regardless of a unique elementary-step assignment. The ideal reactor simulation sets both `K_H2 = 0` and `η = 0`. SI Table S1 obtains enhancement factors 14, 57 and 633 by fitting the inhibited reactor predictions to pseudo-first-order constants for 1%, 10% and pure NH3 feeds. These are model-derived comparisons, not three measured intrinsic turnover-frequency gains at those feed conditions.

Oxygen cofeeds of 0.05–0.25% at 1% NH3 increase conversion. The plotted diagnostic points are selected where O2 is consumed and no H2 exits. They therefore demonstrate accelerated ammonia conversion with chemical hydrogen consumption. The authors explicitly require another hydrogen-removal method for practical hydrogen production; they do not present the diagnostic as recovered-H2 output.

Important controls exist: an independent bed thermocouple shows approximately 0.7 °C deviation from furnace setpoint in the illustrated oxygen experiment; XRD, electron microscopy and H2-TPR address gross oxidation/restructuring; a reference test remains stable over a 15-day testing period with changing exposures. These controls must not be omitted when evaluating the interpretation. They do not automatically exclude a low-coverage oxygen-assisted pathway or establish identical operating adsorbates under oxygen-free separation.

Post-reaction TPD uses a one-hour He purge at 250 °C, cooling to 50 °C, then a ramp to 450 °C. Its retained/desorbing inventory is not a direct measurement of the reacting coverage. The SI also describes the oxygen-containing TPD pretreatment at approximately 70% conversion and 250 °C, distinct from Figure 3's lower-temperature zero-outlet-H2 diagnostic point. The independent review checked the distinction: with 0.25% O2 and 1% NH3, full consumption of both O2 and generated H2 corresponds to one-third NH3 conversion. At 70% conversion the steady N2/H2O balance instead leaves H2 equal to 0.55 times the incoming NH3 molar flow. Thus the TPD pretreatment is not even nominally a zero-gas-H2 state. This does not negate promotion; it limits the proposed identification of a zero-H* working surface.

The now-available [Qiu2025](../../literature/papers/qiu2025-kinetic-investigation-of-nh3-decomposition/paper.md) main identifies the hydrogen-rich reacting regime as the strongest domain of its NH3-first-order/H2-minus-1.5 law. Low-temperature H2-lean data expose neglected coverage terms; the authors acknowledge correlated parameters in more elaborate fits, possible desorption control at lower temperature, and inability to separate support effects on an elementary barrier from H adsorption using the fitted constant alone. This supports a specific oxygen-free low-H2 validation need, rather than establishing a universal Ru mechanism.

The same source provides meaningful high-NH3 validation: main p.7 reports that the power-law description represents temperature and composition data for a commercial Heraeus Ru catalyst, in 1 mm beads, up to pure NH3 feed (Fig. S7). Pure NH3 generates H2 as conversion proceeds, so that result tests a hydrogen-producing reacting regime rather than the extrapolated near-zero local H2 limit. Separately, the Ru/MgAl2O4 calculation requiring GHSV below about 2500 normal L kgcat−1 h−1 for complete conversion below 500 °C is a **model prediction** in Figure 11, not a measured pure-NH3 operating point. The proposed low-H2 experiment should acknowledge the commercial-catalyst validation while testing the distinct local-pressure regime on the selected material.

[Coelho2025](../../literature/papers/coelho2025-hydrogen-production-via-ammonia-decomposition/paper.md) favors an N-desorption model by reanalysing the experimental dataset of [Lundin2024](../../literature/papers/lundin2024-modeling-of-an-ammonia-decomposition/paper.md): commercial 0.5 wt% Ru/Al2O3, with kinetics measured at 350–400 °C using varied NH3/H2/N2 in Ar. This is not an independent experimental replication of Qiu's 1 wt% Ru/MgAl2O4. Coelho acknowledges that extra fitting flexibility need not establish mechanistic validity. These studies therefore do not demonstrate a contradiction. [Prasad2009](../../literature/papers/prasad2009-assessment-of-overall-rate-expressions/paper.md), also now available, already establishes kinetic-model nonuniqueness and limits of macroscopic conversion data.

## A conditional finite-pressure bound

For the published empirical model, the fraction of the uninhibited kinetic factor at fixed temperature, ammonia pressure and thermodynamic driving force is

`f = (1 + 5000 P_H2[atm])^−1.5`.

Therefore,

`P_H2 ≤ (f^(−2/3) − 1)/5000 atm`.

| Desired fraction of uninhibited kinetic factor | Maximum local H2 partial pressure under this model |
|---|---:|
| 10% | 74 Pa |
| 50% | 12 Pa |
| 90% | 1.5 Pa |

These are calculated thresholds, rounded to avoid implying parameter precision. They are not measured coverage thresholds, general material laws, or uncertainty intervals. The empirical coefficient, extrapolation to low H2 pressure, and possible change in dominant chemistry all require validation. The calculation isolates the inhibition factor; it does not set `η` to zero or guarantee a reactor conversion.

A passive hydrogen-selective membrane needs a hydrogen chemical-potential gradient. In an ideal-gas, approximately isothermal comparison, retentate-side interfacial H2 pressure must exceed permeate-side H2 pressure. Consequently, a device producing pure H2 directly at 1 bar cannot attain the local pressures in this table simply by raising the total ammonia pressure. Deep vacuum, a low-H2 sweep, an active electrochemical pump or chemical consumption would be needed for that particular ideal-rate target. Catalyst-to-membrane concentration gradients impose additional limits. This is a conditional feasibility bound, not a finding that membrane reactors cannot improve ammonia conversion.

For scale only, reversible isothermal compression of pure H2 from 12 Pa to 1 bar would require about 22 kJ mol−1 at 298 K or 39 kJ mol−1 at 523 K, using `RT ln(P_out/P_in)`. These are distinct assumed compression temperatures, not a complete plant energy balance or an unavoidable 523 K duty; gas can be cooled before compression. Sweeps also require separation or another defined use, and real vacuum/compression losses are greater than their corresponding reversible values. No economic conclusion follows from these estimates alone.

A second numerical consistency check was independently reproduced. Figure 1's `k(600 K)=0.0364 mol g−1 s−1 atm−1` and `E=37.5 kcal mol−1` give `k(523.15 K)≈3.59×10−4`, agreeing with SI Table S1. At 20 normal L g−1 h−1 and 1 atm, a constant-flow dilute first-order plug-flow calculation gives `Da≈1.45`, hence approximately 76% conversion at 250 °C, rather than mathematically complete conversion. The original blue curve also appears below complete conversion near 250 °C. This check does not reconstruct the authors' code. At the same first-order constant, pressure and inlet molar flow, a pure-NH3 calculation gives about 87% if all generated H2 is instantly removed, or about 65% if H2 is retained while inhibition and the equilibrium term are artificially deleted. The [review](../reviews/ammonia-hydrogen-inference.md#additional-consistency-check-250-c-conversion-and-gas-expansion) derives these distinct conditional balances. Concentration independence does not follow from first-order chemistry alone when gas flow changes.

## What would justify an experimental program

Full-text evidence sets a concrete comparison:

- [Itoh2014](../../literature/papers/itoh2014-kinetic-enhancement-of-ammonia-decomposition/original.pdf), pp.2,5–6, demonstrates approximately 73% to 87% conversion at 723 K with a roughly 200 µm Pd–Ag membrane and 1000 Pa permeate pressure. The increase is about 14 percentage points, rounded to 15% in the conclusion; the 2 µm membrane gain is modeled. Recovery near 60% is permeated H2 divided by the theoretical `1.5 × incoming NH3`.
- [Napolitano2025](../../literature/papers/napolitano2025-enhanced-ammonia-decomposition-using-a/original.pdf), pp.3,6–8, demonstrates finite-pressure membrane benefits at 400 °C, 3–5 bar feed and atmospheric permeate, with a 4 µm Pd–Ag membrane, 25 cm² area and 7 g of 4 wt% Ru/La2Ce2O7. Its approximately 85% conversion uses a mixed NH3/H2/N2 feed simulating upstream cracking. Its Eq.5 recovery denominator is all outgoing H2, including inlet H2, unlike Itoh's denominator. The headline 3.6 conversion ratio compares mixed-feed membrane operation with pure-NH3 conventional operation; it does not isolate the membrane at matched feed. Pure-NH3 cases have roughly 60–70% permeate purity, while >90% belongs to selected mixed feeds. Figures 6 and 7 give different recovery values for the same nominal mixed-feed, 5-bar condition; do not combine the reported conversion, recovery and purity maxima into one validated operating point.
- [Lundin2024](../../literature/papers/lundin2024-modeling-of-an-ammonia-decomposition/original.pdf) independently measures membrane-reactor performance and couples kinetics, radial transport, fittings, non-isothermal behavior and impurity permeation in a two-dimensional model. The experiment uses 27 g of Ru/Al2O3 and local atmospheric permeate pressure near 82 kPa with no sweep; the model includes a negligible numerical sweep. Its experiments last less than 12 h. Conversion/recovery validation is substantially stronger than trace NH3/N2 purity validation, which is detection-limited. It directly occupies generic coupled-transport/kinetic membrane modeling and shows why faster intrinsic kinetics need not translate to proportionally higher recovered H2.
- [Coelho2026](../../literature/papers/coelho2026-hydrogen-production-through-ammonia-decomposition/paper.md) compares finite-sweep configurations with non-isothermal balances and ideal H2 selectivity. Its optimized values are model predictions, not a new experimental validation.

These results establish useful finite-pressure hydrogen removal without attaining Qiu's ideal zero-H2 limit. Assess a real pressure, product-purity and heat boundary before extending the study. If an existing model already predicts the useful operating choice within the decision tolerance, retain the analysis and close this branch. Do not construct a new membrane solely to test an unattainable zero-pressure limit.

If the attainable-pressure comparison remains consequential, reproduce oxygen-free hydrogen-cofeed kinetics on one existing material over the relevant local H2 range. Check the low-H2 extrapolation without oxygen and with matched NH3 activity, bed temperature and transport. Generated H2 is a constraint even with zero inlet H2. For an ideal constant-pressure feed with initial NH3 mole fraction `y`, no H2 inlet and decomposition only, `y_H2,out = 1.5 y X/(1+yX)`. At 1% NH3 and one atmosphere, keeping the outlet below the model’s half-rate threshold requires conversion below approximately 0.78%; the 90%-rate threshold requires below approximately 0.097%. These are calculated analytical targets, not existing measurements; surface and intrabed gradients can impose stricter limits. Metered H2 addition further reduces the available allowance. An oxygen-cofeed comparison should separately track N/H/O products, not use ammonia disappearance alone as hydrogen production. Only a resolved discrepancy merits a specific surface measurement or alternative mechanism.

The prospective decision must be concrete: whether a measured improvement in oxygen-free rate at attainable hydrogen pressure offsets the required separation and catalyst inventory, or whether a different existing catalyst already performs better within that boundary. A chemically assisted rate that cannot transfer to hydrogen recovery may still be mechanistically informative, but does not establish the proposed reactor benefit.

### Minimum experiment and decision gates

1. **Specify the decision before collecting new kinetics.** Select an existing membrane/device boundary and one credible existing catalyst alternative. State feed and permeate pressure, useful product purity, membrane area, catalyst inventory and heat/sweep/compression limits. Compare net newly produced, recovered H2 per elapsed time and inventory. Credit any upstream conversion and account for inlet H2 once; recovery percentages with different denominators cannot select the system. The finite-pressure point need not be one of the extreme ideal-rate thresholds above.
2. **Commission an oxygen-free kinetic measurement.** Reproduce inhibition on the selected Qiu-type material, with independent preparations and repeat reference points bracketing the sequence. Measure NH3, H2 and nitrogen products, bed temperature and gas recovery. Use particle-size/flow/dilution checks and sufficiently low conversion to keep generated H2 within the intended local-pressure interval. If analytical sensitivity cannot resolve that interval, report the reachable bound and stop extrapolating to zero pressure. Set the smallest useful rate contrast from measured scatter and the specified device decision before testing the competing explanations.
3. **Test the transfer with matched boundaries.** At the attainable local H2 pressure, compare oxygen-free rates with the published empirical prediction and the credible catalyst alternative at matched NH3 activity, temperature and transport. Include oxygen-cofeed measurements only as a separate diagnostic with complete N/H/O accounting and a return-to-reference check; NH3 loss is not recovered H2. Use fresh matched aliquots or demonstrated resets if oxygen exposure changes the subsequent state.
4. **Require a held-out useful prediction.** Fit the minimal rate description on the chosen differential data, then predict a withheld composition or higher-throughput condition with generated H2, transport and energy balances included. Proceed toward hardware only if the validated response changes the preferred material or device setting and the gain survives product-purity and resource constraints. Transfer of the newly measured low-H2 response to the selected material and concentrated-feed device condition requires validation; Qiu2025's existing commercial-catalyst pure-NH3 validation does not establish that different low-H2 boundary.

**Observable outcomes and stops:** if oxygen-free rates recover the predicted reserve and change the device choice, the existing hypothesis gains support in that domain. If an O2-specific excess remains after matched gas composition and thermal controls, simple transfer of the scavenging probe is rejected; a further mechanistic measurement is justified only if the discrepancy is consequential. If ordinary finite-pressure inhibition, a stronger catalyst or transport/heat limitations removes the choice, stop this branch. An unresolved instrumental floor supports a bound, not evidence for or against an ideal bare surface. A fit improvement without a changed held-out prediction does not justify expansion.

**Confidence:** hydrogen inhibition in the tested material is well supported. Quantitative transfer of the oxygen-probe gain to oxygen-free operation remains uncertain. Controlled kinetic testing is moderately feasible on an appropriate existing platform, while very low local H2 pressure with useful recovery is harder. Established finite-pressure membrane benefits are real, but a practical improvement from this particular validation and its substantial originality remain unestablished.
