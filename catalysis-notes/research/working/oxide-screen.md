# Oxide/metal research screen: catalytic recycling or storage of dehydrogenation inhibitors?

**Source and decision update, 2026-09-16:** This is a historical screen of an existing idea, not an active campaign. The [decision brief](../decision-brief.md) controls current priorities. ## Recommendation

Determine whether oxide supports protect Pt during methylcyclohexane (MCH) dehydrogenation by continuously converting trace, slowly reacting intermediates back into recoverable carrier molecules, by storing them as carbonaceous material, or by a mixture that changes with operation. Then test whether increasing the *recycling flux per oxide site*, rather than oxide acidity or carbon capacity, can preserve hydrogen throughput with less Pt.

The proposed intervention is a separately activated zirconia co-catalyst mixed with a common Pt/SiO2 batch. Its purpose is to turn a carbon-accepting support into a catalytic route for returning inhibitors to toluene. Start with conventional alumina as the serious control. Do not synthesize elaborate composites until the flux and benefit ceiling are established.

This is a narrower and stronger question than “acid supports scavenge inhibitors.” That mechanism and metal-free conversion of candidate intermediates to toluene are already published. The original work would have to establish a quantitative relationship among carbon residence time, carbon fate, persistent Pt accessibility, and net hydrogen delivery; demonstrate that altering oxide chemistry changes that relationship; and beat the practical alternatives at matched conversion and carrier loss. Establishing that the relationship cannot be changed usefully would also be consequential.

**Why this fits:** Iglesia's work supplies quantitative inhibitor-scavenging kinetics, oxide acid–base chemistry, isotope methods, and metal-ensemble reasoning. Gounder's work supplies controlled site environments, adsorption and transport measurements, and separation of intrinsic chemistry from accessibility. The direct connection is strongest to Iglesia; no assertion of a particular present laboratory capability is needed.

## What is established, and what is not

### Established in locally available full text

1. On Pt, MCH-to-methylcyclohexene (MCHE) is quasi-equilibrated under the reported dilute-feed conditions, and subsequent C–H activation controls toluene formation. Supports affect the coverage of less competent species, denoted X*, rather than simply the intrinsic activity of an initially clean Pt surface. In the paper, rate corrections hold the inferred X* state at its initial reference condition; these are not measurements of the eventual steady state at every new feed. [[chen2025-elementary-steps-and-bifunctional-scavenging]] p.8-10 p.14
2. Propionic acid suppresses the support promotion, physical Pt/SiO2–Al2O3 mixtures retain part of it, and H2–D2 exchange and CO inhibition support differences in working Pt accessibility. The 453 K enhancement relative to Pt/SiO2 was about 3.7 for alumina and 5.5 for zirconia. These are specific kinetic comparisons, not expected practical gains. [[chen2025-elementary-steps-and-bifunctional-scavenging]] p.9-12
3. Metal-free alumina converts MCHE and a methylcyclohexadiene (MCHD) probe to toluene. The authors already propose MCHD-mediated inhibitor removal and a saturation limit at which additional support competence cannot help because inhibitor desorption from Pt becomes limiting. Their illustrative equations already contain the ratio of support sites to Pt sites. Thus “increase acid-site density,” “convert dienes to toluene,” and “reduce Pt loading to increase support/Pt ratio” are not new mechanistic proposals. [[chen2025-elementary-steps-and-bifunctional-scavenging]] p.13-14
4. The presumed native MCHD shuttle was not measured. The paper estimates an extremely small equilibrium pressure for one MCHD isomer, whereas probe-feed experiments use pressures many orders of magnitude larger. Such probe conversion establishes chemical competence, not the native shuttle's identity or flux. The observed support enhancement also declines as temperature increases from 453 to 513 K. Both points materially constrain the proposal. [[chen2025-elementary-steps-and-bifunctional-scavenging]] p.12-14
5. DME treatment exposes otherwise water/carbonate-covered Zr–O pairs. The same sites catalyze hydrogenation and reverse dehydrogenation; their measured useful population is a minority of surface Zr–O pairs. Strictly dry feeds are required. Their activity for other hydrocarbons does not establish MCHD turnover under MCH reaction conditions. [[jaegers2025-hydrogenation-of-alkenes-cycloalkenes-and]] p.1-3 p.7 p.13-15
6. Trace water can dominate zirconia dehydrogenation rates and apparent deactivation, but does not establish an insurmountable practical limit. The ethylbenzene study reports an asymptotic rate about 70% of the initial DME-activated rate at an **estimated** 4 ppm water; its model predicts about 95% at 1 ppm. Ethylbenzene also activates He-treated zirconia toward the same asymptote. Water levels were inferred from site counts and rate transients, and these results do not establish the same self-cleaning route for MCH-derived inhibitors. Original PDF p.8 was visually verified on 2026-09-15. [[artsiusheuski2025-selective-ethylbenzene-dehydrogenation-to-styrene]] p.6-8

### New contrary evidence found in this screen

Two 2026 primary articles make a generic support-scavenging project insufficiently original. *Understanding the role of the support in the deactivation of Pt catalysts for methylcyclohexane dehydrogenation* compares four oxides and attributes much of Pt protection to removal of soft carbonaceous species from metal sites; it reports hydrogen removal of soft coke at 300 °C. Its mechanistic interpretation must be reconciled with catalytic inhibitor consumption. [DOI 10.1016/j.jcat.2026.116959](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2026.116959).

*Support acidity effect on coke formation in platinum catalysts for low-temperature methylcyclohexane dehydrogenation* assigns most deposited carbon on Pt/alumina to support acid sites, and reports more coke and faster deactivation after NH3 treatment. These observations establish that carbon deposition can coexist with protection; they do not by themselves give the fraction of transferred carbon that turns over instead of accumulating. Full-text methods and site-location assignments need scrutiny before relying on their quantitative partition. [DOI 10.1016/j.apcata.2026.121061](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcata.2026.121061).

The 2022 patent already describes DME treatment, repeated regeneration, and extension of dehydroxylation to other oxide acid–base chemistry. DME activation or cyclic DME recovery cannot be claimed as the invention here. [WO2022132843A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2022132843A1/en).

### Proposed hypotheses

- **H1, carbon-fate hypothesis:** At the same accessible oxide-site inventory, some oxide states increase conversion of Pt-derived inhibitors into recoverable C7 molecules instead of merely increasing the support's carbon inventory. This sustains accessible Pt ensembles without accelerating carrier destruction.
- **H2, intervention hypothesis:** Chemically dehydroxylated zirconia changes the balance of inhibitor conversion and storage enough to outperform ordinary alumina at useful MCH/H2/toluene chemical potentials. This is speculative; zirconia may already be desorption-limited, may rehydroxylate, or may preferentially return carbon to MCH at the cost of H2.
- **H3, useful generalization:** Carbon residence time and recoverable-product yield predict the time over which support protection persists better than fresh acidity or total coke. This may survive even if H2 fails.

Do not assume X* is a single MCHD isomer. Do not assume the “soft coke” of long experiments and the X* of initial kinetics are the same pool.

## Minimal model and its limits

Use a carbon balance before adding a detailed microkinetic network:

`dC_Pt/dt = J_form − J_release − J_Pt,recycle − J_Pt,irreversible`

`dC_oxide/dt = J_capture − J_oxide,recycle − J_oxide,irreversible`

All C terms are carbon-atom inventories; all J terms are carbon-atom fluxes. Add gas-phase residence and exchange explicitly when fitting isotope transients. Observable outputs are gas/liquid carbon isotopologues, retained carbon, hydrogen consumption or release, and a separate accessibility response. A single mass-spectrometric toluene signal cannot identify these fluxes.

Compare three nested descriptions: finite storage with negligible turnover; continuously turning-over carbon with little persistent storage; and a mixed model. Reject parameters that only trade off against unmeasured carbon inventories. Fit the simplest identifiable version to multiple transients, then predict a held-out change in oxide amount or time on stream. A fit alone will not identify an exact inhibitor structure.

Two distinct performance measures are needed:

- Recoverable carbon fraction: labeled retained carbon recovered as intact carrier-pair molecules divided by labeled carbon removed from the measured retained pool, with explicit accounting of unclosed balance.
- Net hydrogen delivery: hydrogen exported over the entire operating/regeneration sequence minus hydrogen imported for cofeed and regeneration, reported alongside Pt inventory, catalyst volume, carrier loss, and thermal duty.

No generic numerical target is justified before establishing analytical uncertainty and a baseline process balance.

## Experimental program and decision gates

### Gate 0: Establish whether there is useful headroom

Use a common Pt/SiO2 batch and independently prepared alumina or monoclinic zirconia co-catalysts. Measure particle sizes and fresh exposed Pt by orthogonal methods. Compare Pt/SiO2 alone, intimate Pt/SiO2–Al2O3, intimate Pt/SiO2–ZrO2, and a directly supported Pt/Al2O3 reference. Keep total Pt, total bed volume, thermal dilution, aggregate size, residence-time distribution and feed purity matched.

First reproduce initial transients near 453–493 K; then repeat near 573–613 K, under more concentrated MCH feeds with product and H2 cofeeds. These are proposed test windows, not an asserted industrial operating specification. Measure actual catalyst temperature and approach to equilibrium. Obtain raw, uncorrected time traces as well as any intrinsic-rate corrections.

The early clean-surface transient, H2–D2 exchange, and reversible CO inhibition provide imperfect independent estimates of how much accessibility is recoverable. They should bound possible gains; H2–D2 exchange is not an absolute count of a universally identical Pt ensemble. Compare differential kinetics at matched chemical potentials, then integral conversion at matched residence time.

**Stop practical development** if ordinary alumina already reaches the plausible accessibility ceiling at relevant conditions, or if support differences disappear at useful conversion. A large ratio to poorly performing Pt/SiO2 is not a meaningful industrial advantage. Continue mechanistic work only if the remaining uncertainty warrants it.

### Gate 1: Carbon residence time and fate without assigning every intermediate

At a selected condition with a reproducible, consequential support effect, switch from uniformly 13C-labeled MCH to unlabeled MCH at unchanged total concentration, H2, toluene, and flow. Collect fast GC–MS or isotope-ratio MS transients. Calibrate reactor and line holdup using an inert tracer and empty-reactor controls; use separate heated lines or measured memory corrections for condensable molecules.

Take sacrificial replicate beds at increasing chase times. Quantify retained 13C by calibrated oxidation and isotope analysis; quantify carbon lost to volatile products and heavy deposits. An initial unlabeled steady-state switch in the opposite direction provides an isotope-effect/control sequence. Interpret long labeled tails jointly with retained-carbon depletion, not as direct proof of catalytically active intermediates.

After gas-phase label has washed out, perturb the oxide function and compare the labeled-product response and Pt accessibility:

1. A modest H2 step tests recoverability, but changes Pt coverage and reaction thermodynamics too. Use the same step on Pt/SiO2 and oxide-only controls, then return to the original feed and require recovery at the original chemical potential.
2. Low-dose propionic-acid titration tests oxide involvement, with identical Pt/SiO2 controls, titrant and decomposition-product balances, and verification that the dose does not independently modify Pt. This is a destructive diagnostic on a replicate, not a selective molecular scalpel.
3. An oxide-only bed exposed to candidate labeled dienes maps possible carbon fates and carbon-storage capacities. It remains a proxy experiment; do not substitute its rates for native inhibitor flux without a justified bound.

Temperature-programmed oxidation peak positions alone cannot locate carbon on Pt, an interface, or an oxide: metal-catalyzed oxidation and oxygen transport can shift peaks. Use them as inventory measurements. Localized IR/NMR or spatial methods become worthwhile only if the initial isotope and kinetic constraints leave a tractable localization question. Failure to detect a putative native diene is not evidence that it does not mediate a low-flux pathway.

**Go** only if the isotope balances distinguish predominantly storage from measurable recycling, or bound the alternatives enough to predict a meaningful intervention. **Stop or redesign** if apparatus memory, too little retained carbon, or an unobservably small exchange flux makes those interpretations inseparable. A generic spectrum-plus-rate correlation is not sufficient.

### Gate 2: Change turnover, not just capacity

Compare zirconia prepared from the same parent batch after He treatment, DME treatment, and deliberate partial rehydroxylation. Activate the oxide separately from Pt whenever possible; use sealed dry transfer and an identical handling control. DME on Pt can create its own deposits and cannot be treated as an oxide-selective perturbation.

Measure accessible oxide pair counts on sacrificial matched specimens using reaction-relevant titration; total NH3 acidity is not the required denominator. Select oxide amounts to match relevant pair inventories as far as measurement allows. Record BET area, hydroxyl coverage, carbonates and DME-derived carbon. Account for uncertainty if different titrants sample different subsets of sites.

Test whether an intervention changes recovered labeled C7 flux and accessibility recovery per oxide site while total storage capacity stays approximately fixed. A complementary oxide-loading series tests capacity dependence. Vary intimate versus more separated contact at matched aggregate size and transport; a contact dependence by itself does not establish hydrogen spillover or a unique molecular shuttle.

A useful result must persist after the initial oxide carbon reservoir is filled or reaches a stationary inventory, and after multiple carbon inventory turnovers inferred from a closed isotope balance. A larger early rate followed by faster saturation fails this gate.

Perform small, measured water additions only after the dry experiment establishes an effect. Determine whether a realistic impurity-control burden erases the benefit. Water may directly inhibit Pt or change carrier transport, so maintain those controls. Do not proceed to hydrophobic encapsulation merely to rescue an intrinsically marginal effect.

### Gate 3: Reduce Pt and test net service

Only after Gates 0–2, reduce Pt inventory while holding co-catalyst inventory and bed volume fixed. Match conversion, carrier selectivity, hydrogen purity and pressure to a conventional Pt/Al2O3 benchmark and one modern high-performance alternative. Report every imported mole of H2 and every discarded or cracked mole of carrier.

Use increasing-duration experiments tied to measured carbon residence times and deactivation times, including startup, shutdown, and regeneration. A 100-hour stable trace is insufficient if carbon capacity predicts later breakthrough. A 2000-hour cycling claim in the literature also must be distinguished from continuous operation; extract the actual protocol before comparing.

**Go to a major program** only if a reproducible Pt reduction or catalyst-volume/temperature advantage remains at useful conversion without moving costs to drying, H2 consumption, regeneration downtime, or carrier replacement. Otherwise preserve the mechanistic findings and stop the materials expansion.

## Fair competition and strongest alternative

Preventing inhibitor formation on Pt may be better than clearing it afterward. Use controlled Pt ensemble modification as the strongest mechanistic alternative: for example a well-characterized Pt alloy or established moderated Pt catalyst, with the same attention to site accessibility and carrier loss. Do not assume every modifier works by geometric blocking.

A 2026 sulfur study uses DFT and microkinetics to predict altered toluene adsorption, activity and demethylation. It is a theoretical comparator, not experimental proof that its predicted optimum will transfer to nanoparticles or long service. [DOI 10.1039/D6CY00183A](https://doi.org/10.1039/D6CY00183A).

A 2025 Pt–Fe@MFI paper reports high-purity hydrogen and long cycling durability and is a serious material benchmark; conditions, Pt inventory and cycle definition require full-text extraction. [DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-55370-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-55370-z). Chiyoda's primary account already reports 10,000-hour continuous pilot operation, so a new support cannot be motivated by implying that practical MCH dehydrogenation lacks durable catalysts. [Chiyoda technology history](https://www.chiyodacorp.com/en/service/lowcarbon/hydrogen/lohc-mch/).

The strongest alternative *within this program* is conventional alumina plus an appropriate Pt ensemble: inexpensive, well studied, and less dependent on maintaining exceptionally bare zirconia pairs. If that wins, do not add a third oxide, coating, or dynamic control scheme without a new specific reason.

## Confidence and value judgment

| Question | Assessment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Do support-mediated inhibitor effects exist in these systems? | High within the published regimes | Multiple kinetic perturbations and physical mixtures support the effect; exact X* identity remains unresolved. |
| Are carbon storage and carbon recycling experimentally separable? | Moderate | Isotope inventory balances and distinct timescales can constrain them, but native flux and spatial attribution may remain inaccessible. |
| Will dehydroxylated zirconia improve useful recycling over alumina? | Low to moderate | Relevant chemistry exists, but the useful intermediate, temperature window, desorption ceiling and water sensitivity are major uncertainties. |
| Will this produce meaningful Pt savings or process improvements? | Low at present | Commercial and academic benchmarks are strong; published support advantages weaken with temperature; drying and regeneration may consume the gains. |
| Can an early experiment produce valuable negative knowledge? | High if Gates 0–1 are followed | It can reject a common inference that more oxide acidity or less metal coke necessarily yields sustainable catalytic improvement. |

These are qualitative judgments, not probabilities. The high-confidence outcome is an informative test of a mechanistic design rule, not a promised catalyst improvement.

## Programs screened out during this assessment

- **DME-renewed bare zirconia for standalone propane or ethylbenzene dehydrogenation:** mechanistically compelling, but activation and repeated renewal are already published/patented. Trace-water vulnerability and the high-conversion selectivity question are serious. A new dry initial-rate study would add little. Local support: [[jaegers2024-heterolytic-ch-activation-routes-in]] p.8-10; [[ammal2026-mechanistic-insights-into-light-alkane]] p.7-12.
- **A general dynamic-interface descriptor for COx hydrogenation:** already a broad repository opportunity. Without a defined intervention and superior benchmark it risks becoming a descriptive operando campaign.
- **An LOHC catalyst ranked by fresh TOF or acid-site count:** the recent literature directly anticipates it. The priority must be carbon fate and net service, not a new support ranking.

## Practical-selection follow-up

The first screen overemphasized water vulnerability as a general reason to deprioritize oxide dehydrogenation. The original ethylbenzene article supports substrate-assisted hydroxyl removal, although the oxygen-containing cleaning products are unidentified and the reported stable interval exceeds 50 ks, not an industrial lifetime. See [practical-selection.md](practical-selection.md) for the corrected assessment and a more consequential isotope test of whether chemical cleaning exports oxygen into reversible products or persistent/recycled titrants. This does not raise confidence in the original MCH proposal automatically.
