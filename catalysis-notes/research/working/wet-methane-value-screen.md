# Wet methane combustion: a bounded test of sulfur tolerance after NO removal

Updated 2026-09-16 after the literature uploads. Existing diagnostic study; no experiments performed. This is a proposed diagnostic study, not a demonstrated catalyst improvement or a selected substantial program. The [experimental review](../reviews/wet-methane-independent-value.md) supports its testability; the later [application-boundary review](../reviews/wet-methane-low-no-relevance.md) lowers its priority because a consequential low-NO sulfur-containing application remains unestablished.

## Decision

**Retain one lower-priority diagnostic: does the reported wet sulfur tolerance of a strongly anchored Pd/SSZ-13 cluster catalyst depend on NO cofeed?** Its immediate value is to establish the boundary of a material claim. Whether that boundary changes a practical catalyst choice remains unresolved. A new material composition is unnecessary. Generic water inhibition, hydrophobicity, alkali exchange, sulfur storage, NO promotion, and rich-pulse regeneration are already established.

This study does not yet displace the Ag or polymer pilots. It needs reproduction of a published catalyst and access to reliable wet SOx/NOx measurements. Unlike a generic contaminant screen, its central contrast is specified by a strong published performance claim whose sulfur experiment includes NO. The main competing explanations are sulfur exclusion, NO-dependent sulfur partitioning, and NO-dependent activity of an already sulfated surface. Existing primary literature supports the latter two possibilities on other Pd catalysts; their applicability to the claimed resistant cluster is unresolved.

The fit to Gounder is zeolite composition, cation environments, and the distinction between confined ions and external clusters. The direct Iglesia connection includes PdOx/ZrO2 redox and vacancy-pair kinetics in [Fujimoto/Ribeiro/Avalos-Borja/Iglesia1998](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcat.1998.2178); its main text is now retained in the [local package](../../literature/papers/fujimoto1998-structure-and-reactivity-of-pdox/paper.md). More broadly, the study connects differential oxidation kinetics, inhibition and working-state identification to useful reactor behavior. These are intellectual connections, not claims about available facilities or an agreed faculty priority.

## Evidence and fair comparison

### A strong result deserves a demanding, fair replication

Ryu and colleagues report approximately 95% methane conversion at 350 °C for their steam/CO/N2/O2-treated Pd/SSZ-13. The main paper includes wet powder and monolith durability and a 150 h monolith NO/SO2 switching experiment. In Figure 6m sulfur accompanies NO; this is not 150 h of uninterrupted sulfur exposure. The corresponding treatment without the N2 hold loses substantial conversion on sulfur introduction. The main PDF and this figure were inspected. The SI, Figure 6 source data and public review file were subsequently recovered and inspected in the independent review. They do not supply the missing NO-free wet sulfur test or a monolith internal-temperature record. [Ryu2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-52698-4)

| Quantity | Ryu2024 strongest monolith | Mortensen2025 protonic CHA |
|---|---|---|
| Nominal Pd | 1 wt% | 3 wt% |
| Initial zeolite Si/Al | 15, extensively steam treated | 6.5, hydrothermally calcined |
| CH4 / O2 / CO2 | 5000 ppm / 15% / 5% | 1000 ppm / 10% / 5% |
| Water | 5% in monolith sulfur test | Dry sulfur experiment, then 10% water added |
| SO2 / NO when supplied | 10 / 200 ppm | 2 ppm SO2; no NO in reported feed |
| Temperature | 350 °C | 420 °C for >200 h result |
| Gas flow per catalyst mass | Approximately 200 L gcat−1 h−1 | 126 L gcat−1 h−1 |
| Outcome | High conversion during reported wet sulfur/NO sequence | Dry sulfur recovery; severe loss after water addition |

Ryu's 20,000 h−1 monolith space velocity and 100 gcat Lmonolith−1 loading give **200 L gcat−1 h−1**. Comparing 20,000 with 126,000 without their denominators would be wrong. Nominal gas throughput per Pd is about 4.76 times higher for Ryu, not lower. Catalyst preparation, reaction concentration, binder, thermal boundary, NO, and sulfur histories still prevent a catalyst ranking from this table.

Mortensen's counter-ion/back-exchange experiment supports a role for protons in dry sulfur recovery, but does not uniquely choose sulfate volatilization versus storage away from Pd. Its wet failure is direct evidence against simply transferring dry sulfur tolerance to wet exhaust. It does not refute Ryu's different catalyst. [Mortensen2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11244-025-02114-y)

**Thermal qualification.** At complete oxidation, an ideal adiabatic estimate using 802 kJ molCH4−1 and an illustrative 32 J molgas−1 K−1 gives temperature rises of about 125 K at 5000 ppm and 25 K at 1000 ppm. These are heat-release bounds, not measured catalyst temperatures. Actual reactors dissipate heat. The retrieved Ryu main methods describe furnace ramps and gas analysis but do not establish an internal axial temperature profile for Figure 6m. The inspected SI and Figure 6 source data do not close that temperature gap; intrinsic chemistry or superior low-temperature performance still requires thermal controls. Mortensen reports an internal thermocouple, which alone also does not prove absence of every gradient.

**Sulfur-inventory bound.** The final continuous SO2/NO interval in Ryu Fig.6m is visually about 80–150 h. At 4.3 L min−1, 10 ppm SO2, approximately 1.29 g catalyst on the specified one-inch-diameter/one-inch-long monolith and 1 wt% Pd, about 0.87 incoming S atoms per total Pd atom per hour are supplied if the gas volume is referenced to 24.45 L mol−1. Roughly 61 S/Pd thus enters over that interval. The exact gas reference and digital switch times need checking. A one-S-per-Pd finite storage picture cannot accommodate all that inlet sulfur. This does **not** prove catalytic sulfur removal or intrinsic sulfur exclusion: gas bypass, low retention, support/binder/cordierite storage, and unmeasured sulfur export remain possible. The reported main monolith analysis measures carbon gases, without establishing an outlet sulfur or NOx balance.

Tan/Hong's **3 wt% Pd/Na-IWV, Si/Al 45**, is a defined wet, sulfur-free comparator. The retained main text (§2.2, Fig.2 and conclusions) reports 0.3 g catalyst, atmospheric pressure, 1500 ppm CH4, 5% O2, 10% water and GHSV 100,000 h−1; neither NO nor sulfur is specified. It gives about 85% conversion at 330 °C over 100 h. This is not a sulfur-tolerance result or a matched ranking against Ryu. Reproduce its own feed before transferring it to the common comparison, and report actual molar flow and measured Pd inventory: GHSV alone does not give precious-metal productivity. The main text identifies IWV; the earlier publisher highlight's IWW wording is a source inconsistency, not a second tested framework. [Tan2025 original](../../literature/papers/tan2025-a-highly-active-and-stable/original.pdf)

### What is already known

- NO can inhibit fresh Pd catalysts yet improve activity after sulfur poisoning. Auvinen2021 measures inhibition on fresh and promotion on sulfur-poisoned Pd/Pt/alumina; changed sulfur partitioning and an HNO2-mediated hydroxyl-removal pathway are proposed explanations. Its calculations and bulk sulfur measurements do not uniquely establish that pathway, particularly on Pd/SSZ-13. Thus observing an NO benefit alone would repeat prior art. [Auvinen2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2020.128050)
- Combining sulfating and nonsulfating supports to improve wet sulfur tolerance is old. A new generic sulfur reservoir or guard material is not the surviving proposal. [DiCarlo2010](https://doi.org/10.1039/C0CC00723D)
- Sodium exchange and three-second oxygen cutoffs every ten minutes already sustain high wet methane conversion in Pd/MOR. [Petrov2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04748-x)
- Slow water-related loss is not an unstudied phenomenon. A commercial Pd/alumina study separates rapid inhibition from reaction-dependent slower loss and proposes Pd dispersion; a separate CO-probe study proposes PdO surface passivation. These are different, material-dependent hypotheses, not a universal settled assignment. Their transient/probe methods also change the catalyst. [Mortensen2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcatb.2023.123646), [Chen2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.4c03690)
- Water-modified PdO redox dynamics and spatially evolving sulfur poisoning have direct operando precedents. A new isotope transient or axial sulfur map alone is insufficient novelty. [Velin2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/cctc.202100829), [Delrieux2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.5c02678)

### Actual-engine and coupled-system checks

The [independent engine-benchmark review](../reviews/wet-methane-engine-benchmark.md) supplies a concrete feed connection: one dual-fuel engine operates near 700 ppm CH4, 10% water and 60 ppm NO, and shows severe history-dependent conversion loss over 32 operating hours. Its authors report negligible SO2, so this supports sulfur-free controls as well as a lower-NO challenge; it is not a wet sulfur benchmark. Conversion increases under engine retuning do not necessarily reduce outlet methane mass flow.

A separate [recovered coupled-catalyst study](../reviews/wet-methane-coupled-catalyst-prior-art.md) already combines upstream sulfur capture/NOx changes with methane oxidation and rich regeneration. Such integration is prior art. Different total catalyst inventories, heat and feed histories limit mechanistic assignment. The surviving proposed contribution is a material comparison across NO cofeed conditions. Practical importance requires an actual feed envelope, and generic tandem placement is established.

**Application limit after fresh review:** combining 60 ppm NO from the engine with 2 ppm SO2 from another laboratory gives NO/SO2 = 30, versus 20 in Ryu’s 200/10 feed. Neither ratio is a validated protection law. Lower absolute NO therefore does not establish a harsher sulfur condition. The documented MOC → SCR arrangement also leaves the methane catalyst upstream of NO removal. No retrieved source jointly establishes the proposed low-NO, wet sulfur and temperature history for a concrete application. Zero NO remains a diagnostic boundary, not an engine specification. [Independent application review](../reviews/wet-methane-low-no-relevance.md)

## Specific hypothesis and alternatives

**H1, proposed:** part of the apparent sulfur tolerance of the best Ryu catalyst requires NO to keep sulfur off the kinetically important Pd population, or to keep sulfated Pd reactive. Removing NO at otherwise matched conditions causes a consequential loss that is absent or smaller without sulfur. Sulfur inventories and washout may constrain these routes; neither those measurements nor methane conversion alone uniquely identify the working Pd species or molecular pathway.

**H0, useful result:** its anchored Pd structure remains highly active with wet SO2 after NO removal, with no consequential extra loss beyond the sulfur-free NO response. This would extend the tested NO-cofeed boundary of a strong material; it would not independently establish an application advantage. It would not prove literal electrostatic rejection of every polar molecule.

**Other explanations:** heat release, gas residence and wet sampling artifacts; NO changing Pd redox or hydroxyl coverage without changing sulfur partitioning; finite support/binder storage; and a different working structure created by the preparation history. Ryu's N2 hold occurs at 300 °C between CO reduction at 700 °C and O2 oxidation at 500 °C. Calling its effect persistent “drying” is not a complete chemical explanation under prolonged wet operation. It changes the treatment trajectory; assigning the effect uniquely to hydration needs more evidence.

## Smallest useful experiment

1. **Commission and reproduce before bridging.** Reproduce the ST-CO-N2-O2 and ST-CO-O2 preparation pair and their contrast under the reported wet NO/SO2 feed and formulation. A powder result can establish a powder contrast but is not a reproduction of Figure 6m's formulated monolith. Match that claim's geometry/formulation if calling it a monolith replication. Measure actual Pd, molar flow and internal temperatures; establish blank switching response, analytical repeatability and ordinary drift. Use independent preparation batches and independent history runs for each decisive comparison; repeated readings from one bed are not independent replication. After commissioning, set a finite horizon, a minimum useful change in cumulative methane slip and sufficient independent repeats to resolve that contrast against batch/run variability. Freeze these choices before the decisive exposure comparison. If modest reproduction effort fails, stop without declaring the publication wrong.
2. **Bridge sequentially to a controlled diagnostic regime.** Establish a dilute, low-conversion point with thermal and dilution/flow checks. Change methane, water, sulfur or temperature in successive controlled steps, retaining source-feed checks; do not jump from Ryu's 5000 ppm CH4/5% water/10 ppm SO2 feed to 1000 ppm/10%/2 ppm and attribute the result to NO. Those latter values are possible laboratory conditions, not a joint measured engine specification. Retain useful-conversion operation for the output decision and a differential point for rate interpretation.
3. **Run the finite wet NO × sulfur comparison.** Cross 0/200 ppm NO with SO2 absent/present at one fixed, stated wet condition on the reproduced pair, then on the practical comparator. Use separate aliquots for sulfur histories. Include sulfur-free NO switches, a never-NO sulfur history, matched ordinary continuation and sham switching. After defined NO/SO2 exposure, remove NO while continuing SO2, then restore NO. Record methane, CO/CO2 and NO/NO2, with N2O where relevant. Calibrate sulfur delivery and wet-line recovery before interpreting exposure. Quantitative sulfur balances and a limited SO2-off arm at fixed NO are conditional follow-ups if the functional contrast matters; missing SO2 is not automatically catalyst storage. Any common assay can alter state, so retain its transient. Do not make exhaustive retained-sulfur/speciation mapping a prerequisite to this first decision.
4. **Measure material advantage against credible alternatives.** Use the omission-treatment pair for the preparation contrast and an available, characterized commercial Pd/Pt catalyst for practical performance. Tan's Pd/Na-IWV is the specified additional wet sulfur-free reference; establish its source-feed behavior before sequential transfer, and measure rather than presume its sulfur response. Compare cumulative slip under the same feed, measured temperature, sulfur input and horizon. Report measured Pd and Pt separately and total precious metal; add an equal-volume comparison if packaging matters. Equal precious-metal mass is a resource comparison, not a turnover-frequency measurement. A common NO penalty that leaves useful material ranking and required inventory unchanged has limited new decision value.
5. **Validate only a consequential result.** If the material advantage changes or survives with useful precision, predict one withheld history. A practical low-NO claim additionally requires one independently identified operating envelope jointly specifying NO/NO2, sulfur, methane, water, temperature and flow. Select one finite-NO test from that envelope; 0/200 ppm does not define an application window. If no such envelope is available, stop after reporting the diagnostic material boundary. Sulfur speciation or binder comparisons follow only if they can resolve a remaining material or operating choice.

Full factorial water levels, many sulfur species, fresh promoters and isotope spectroscopy are unnecessary initially. The initial question is dependence of the reported material advantage on NO at an already wet condition. A practical compatibility claim requires a separately established application envelope. Reaction and sulfur profiles near full conversion can hide large losses of intrinsic activity; use one differential diagnostic point as well as useful-conversion operation. An additive NO×sulfur interaction in conversion can arise from nonlinear reactor response even for independent rate effects. It is descriptive, not by itself proof of coupled surface chemistry. Predeclare an independent-effects baseline on differential rates or a validated reactor model. For a multiplicative rate baseline, `r(NO,S) × r(0,0) / [r(NO,0) × r(0,S)] = 1`; an additive rate contrast tests a different null. Match temperatures and histories and propagate run/batch uncertainty. A departure does not uniquely identify a mechanism. Measure integrated methane slip directly for the useful-output decision.

## Value and limits

For a common feed and horizon, quantify avoided methane slip as

`ΔnCH4 = integral[FCH4,in × (Xcandidate − Xreference) dt]`.

Include startup and recovery periods, and report heat input, pressure drop, precious-metal inventory, sulfur retained/exported, NOx and N2O separately. No generic net-climate benefit is computed without the relevant energy source and emissions factors. No NO addition is proposed as an emissions-control intervention. Testing an existing NO-containing exhaust condition does not justify releasing extra NOx.

At the published 5000 ppm inlet, 95% conversion corresponds to about 250 ppm outlet CH4. That cannot be rescaled to a 1000 ppm feed: reaction state and self-heating change. As a transparent decision example rather than a prediction, increasing sustained conversion from 90% to 95% at 1000 ppm would halve slip from 100 to 50 ppm. The actual attainable gain is unknown. A new bound showing that the apparent benefit vanishes when NO is removed can itself prevent an expensive inappropriate material choice.

Compatibility with an upstream/downstream NOx-treatment location is an eventual application connection, not a result of this simplified experiment. Actual placement also changes temperature, NH3, NO2 fraction and transport. A positive NO effect already established on conventional Pd would justify this study only if it changes the claimed cluster advantage or predicts a materially different low-NOx operating limit.

| Judgment | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Scientific plausibility of consequential NO dependence | Moderate for Pd catalysts generally; low to moderate for this specific cluster. Direct wet NO-free sulfur evidence is missing. |
| Feasibility and informative value | Moderate conditional on catalyst reproduction, thermal control and quantitative wet sulfur sampling. Methane-rate contrast is easier than locating retained sulfur. |
| Practical upside | Methane-slip abatement matters, but the importance of this specific NO/sulfur boundary is unestablished. Present confidence in a practical improvement is low; a bounded validation of the material claim remains possible. |
| Originality | Provisional, specific to the published cluster's transfer and a useful prediction. Generic NO promotion, sulfur storage or response modeling would not qualify. |

**Stop/demote** if thermal or sampling effects explain the contrast, the claim cannot be reproduced within a reasonable preparation effort, conventional-catalyst NO behavior explains all results without changing a useful choice, or the strongest available comparator removes the apparent benefit. Do not promote other portfolio directions merely because this screen closes.
