# First causal test: protection against imposed water after common conditioning

2026-09-16. Proposed experiment; no result is claimed. Revised after the [fresh first-test review](../../reviews/water-management-first-test-review.md). Companion to [physical water management](physical-water-management.md) and the [transport review](../../reviews/ft-hydrophobic-transport-review.md).

## The decision

**Test whether the modest PDVB dose protects an already conditioned cobalt catalyst against a sustained, externally imposed water challenge when reaction-generated water is a small contribution to the gas-phase water balance.** Use a single low–high–low water waveform, with PDVB presence as the intervention. Measure both performance during the plateau and catalytic function after the original water condition is restored.

This is an operating-boundary test with mechanistic discrimination, not a direct measurement of local water activity or surviving cobalt sites. A useful positive result would justify further formulation and mechanistic work for this defined disturbance. It would not establish a humid-feed or water-recycle application without an actual process need and operating envelope. A negative result could bound late additive use in this state; it would not prove that startup is the mechanism.

The strongest clean first contrast is **add PDVB only after the two arms have acquired the same catalyst history**. This deliberately withholds the possibility that one sample owes its apparent protection entirely to a different initial reduction, steam exposure, or wax-forming startup. It asks whether the neighboring material changes the response to a subsequent challenge.

## Why this is not the published water-cofeed test

Fang et al. 2026 report 0.5 g Co/SiO2 with 0.025 g PDVB, separate 40–60 mesh granules, 220 °C, 2 MPa and 2400 mL/(g catalyst h) as the default configuration. Fig. 3a reports additional water at approximately 78 mg/(g catalyst h). Its caption does not repeat the PDVB dose; the methods' default is 0.025 g, so that assignment should be confirmed from the authors or raw experiment record before treating it as an exact comparison. Do not silently substitute the 1.0 g dose used explicitly in SI Figs. 30–31 for changed-flow and humid-pretreatment controls. [Primary article and associated SI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76571-8).

At the headline 44.1% conversion, the source-scale oxygen balance gives roughly 249–272 mg generated water/(g catalyst h), assuming one water per converted CO and standard-volume conventions at 25 or 0 °C. The reported 78 mg cofeed is approximately 29–31% of that estimate. Even at the reported promoted conversion of 34.7% during cofeed, it is only about 37–40% of estimated generated water. This is **not** an imposed-water-dominant experiment. These estimates are not measured water rates; CO2, oxygenates and the gas-volume reference must be resolved by the proposed balance.

The source already tests smaller granules, separate beds, powder mixtures, other hydrophobes and water pretreatment. The contribution sought here is the response to a specified disturbance after common conditioning at a demonstrably different water-source ratio, with a functional recovery endpoint. Changing mesh or reproducing another water cofeed without those distinctions would add little.

Preserve the source's activation distinction. Its synthesis reduces Co/SiO2 in H2 at 400 °C for 6 h, then passivates it in 1% O2/N2 at 50 °C for 30 min. The FT methods describe heating to 220 °C and introducing syngas without an explicit 300 °C H2 re-reduction, but SI Note 10 calls the standard test dry-hydrogen-pre-reduced. Thus the FT restart sequence is unresolved; absence from the main methods is not evidence that re-reduction was absent. Separate TKA/XAS/FTIR procedures use their own H2 conditioning near 300 °C. Document the chosen source-platform activation and qualify its PDVB contrast without silently importing an analytical pretreatment. The common-conditioning intervention below is a subsequent, different-history experiment.

Startup water management itself is established prior art. [Paterson et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cattod.2024.114559) experimentally connects activation-water exposure to subsequent cobalt FT performance, including axial differences in serially activated beds. This first test therefore seeks a specific operating boundary for the physical additive, rather than claiming that activation history matters as a discovery.

The imposed-water and recovery method also has direct precedent. [Wolf et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2019.04.030), original manuscript pp. 6–7 and 14–19, maintain very low CO conversion and fixed H2/CO partial pressures while increasing supplied water on Co/graphite; a final dry return tests recovery. Their strongest challenge produces oxidation, particle growth and persistent activity loss. Thus neither supplied-water dominance nor a recovery endpoint is new alone. The proposed additional comparison is late, low-dose PDVB under a common prior history. Their support, Co particle distribution, total-pressure sequence and water ratios differ; the suggested 4 bar plateau below is an unvalidated planning condition, not a known damage condition derived from that study.

The newly supplied [Hanssen et al. 1997 original](../../../literature/papers/names1997-transient-and-steady-state-studies/fulltext.md), pp. 2–3 and 5–9, goes further: it compares water pretreatment with water cofeed followed by a dry return, including 13 bar FT experiments and low-pressure methane-SSITKA before and after treatment. Already water-pretreated catalysts show little further change on cofeeding. [Rothaemel et al. 1997](../../../literature/papers/rothaemel1997-the-effect-of-water-on/fulltext.md), pp. 2–6, similarly separates lower methane-forming intermediate populations from approximately unchanged residence times after water exposure. Thus prior water history and loss of accessible function versus per-site chemistry are established questions. Their alumina-supported catalysts, methane-focused transient analysis and treatment conditions do not set a damage threshold for this Co/SiO2–PDVB test. The informative novelty remains the late additive contrast and its useful-output consequence, not the treatment/return sequence or a generic history effect.

## One paired intervention

Use the source catalyst, independently prepared parent batches if available, and the source separate-granule geometry. Keep the intervention at 0.05 g PDVB per g Co/SiO2. Replace an equivalent diluent volume, preserving catalyst inventory and packed-bed volume; document the resulting void volume and pressure drop. This is not a powder co-granulate.

1. **Prepare a common conditioned parent state without PDVB.** Establish a low-water FT condition at 220 °C and 2 MPa with reproducible reference function. Do not condition until an extensively damaged catalyst merely appears stable. Recover the full relevant parent inventory under inert conditions, mix gently and split representative randomized portions; do not allocate different axial sections to different arms. Preserve retained liquid and account for catalyst mass separately from wet recovered mass. Both arms undergo the same cooling, transfer and repacking. Add PDVB to one arm and matched quartz to the other. Re-establish the diagnostic condition and record any immediate rate difference; do not force equal initial rates by changing temperature or catalyst quantity.
2. **Apply one water step to a sustained plateau and return.** A starting design at 2 MPa is inlet pH2 = 10 bar and pCO = 5 bar, with water raised from 0.5 to 4 bar by replacing Ar. These are analyst-selected conditions. Hold total pressure, molar flow, H2/CO partial pressures, inventory and temperature fixed. Target CO conversion no greater than about 2% in the more active arm. At that conversion, generated/supplied water is approximately **2.5% at the 4 bar plateau but 20% at the 0.5 bar diagnostic and recovery condition**. Determine the actual balances in every interval, including gas contraction and product oxygen. Common inlet conditions do not establish equal recovery exposure.
3. **Return and compare recovered operating function of the complete formulations.** Measure hydrocarbon production and selectivity after water and product signals settle to the predefined endpoint. Report each arm's post/pre productivity ratio, absolute useful productivity per catalyst mass and complete bed volume, and cumulative useful output over the fixed observation window. A better ratio can coexist with lower useful output. Because PDVB remains present, the endpoint includes any history-dependent promotion or inhibition during the assay; it does not independently measure intrinsic cobalt survival. A loss persisting over this recovery interval is not proof of permanent damage.

Use independent paired preparations for the decisive comparison. A single common-parent split controls material history within a pair; it does not establish reproducibility. Parallel reactors are convenient, but balanced run order on one reproducible reactor is acceptable if inlet-water and catalyst-handling stability are demonstrated.

Choose the plateau and recovery durations in a small parent-only feasibility run, before the paired comparison. The plateau must exceed measured gas/water equilibration and produce measurable, noncatastrophic residual functional loss. Establish background drift over the entire challenge-plus-recovery horizon; if material, add a time-matched low-water sham for each formulation. Predefine the durations and do not extend only the reference until it deactivates. If the relevant range yields no interpretable parent loss, or severe transport/thermal limits, stop or redesign this challenge.

## Minimal measurements and checks

- Quantitative inlet/outlet water and inert-tracer response, with heated lines and a blank apparatus response. Integrate each step **without normalizing away its amplitude**. Constrain the small generated-water contribution with the product oxygen balance and its uncertainty; subtraction of two large water signals may not resolve it. Account for unresolved oxygen storage. A transient integral is not automatically an adsorption capacity.
- CO, H2, hydrocarbon and CO2 rates, together with a useful carbon/oxygen balance. The small conversion requirement reduces bulk H2 and CO gradients, but does not prove their absence within catalyst granules. Keep the same catalyst granules in both arms. Verify that an additional modest increase in flow does not materially change the diagnostic mass-normalized rate or the inferred imposed-water dominance before choosing the final flow.
- Actual bed temperature, total flow, pressure and pressure drop. Record the initial absolute activity penalty, the water-step response, plateau drift and recovered function. Use the same time sampling and recovery protocol for both arms.
- A parent-only handling pilot must qualify cooling, inert transfer, representative splitting, repacking and restart. A reproducible common shift can define a new starting state; uncontrolled shifts, selective liquid/catalyst loss or destructive damage invalidate the comparison. Paterson's serial-bed experiment did not validate unpacking and homogenizing wax-bearing granules. A same-startup paired fallback is weaker and must retain its early-history ambiguity.

Check equipment at the selected flow before committing catalyst. Retaining the source's promoted CO consumption at 2% conversion and inlet CO fraction 0.25 would require roughly 68 L/(g catalyst h), on the source's volume basis. This is a planning scale, not a rate prediction at the new conditions. Water delivery, pressure drop, heated collection and dilute-product quantification must work there, or catalyst inventory and the design must be adjusted.

No isotope switch, synchrotron measurement, distributed transport fit, or full wax mapping is required for this first intervention. A limited post-challenge cobalt-state measurement may help interpret an observed functional loss, but cannot replace the rate and balance evidence.

## Predictions fixed before observing the pair

| Explanation | Expected response in this test | What the result would and would not decide |
|---|---|---|
| The original apparent protection comes only from a lower whole-bed water production rate in the less active PDVB bed | Supplied-water dominance narrows the bulk-exposure difference, but even a small difference could cross a sharp damage threshold. | A benefit constrains this explanation. Reject it as sufficient only if a parent response check bracketing the actual residual plateau difference shows that difference cannot explain the benefit. Intragranular gradients remain unresolved. |
| Lower reversible storage, unchanged stationary transport and catalyst chemistry | If the relevant capacity is actually lower, a step upward reaches its plateau faster, while a downward step clears faster. There is no sustained stationary-exposure change in the simple fixed-source model. The direction of cumulative damage is not universally favorable. | Opposite ingress/tail changes without improved retained function would make a shorter purge an unsuitable durability-selection metric in this regime. A protection difference concentrated in the return transient could justify an operating-history application. |
| Greater conductance removes a significant reaction-generated local water excess | Protection should weaken as the relevant source-generated excess becomes small relative to the imposed environment; faster equilibration may remain. | Disappearance is consistent with a restricted source-removal benefit. Persistence does not falsify all conductance mechanisms, because intragranular gradients have not been independently eliminated. |
| The additive acts only during the original conditioning by preserving a favorable initial catalyst or wax state | Adding it after common conditioning should provide little new benefit if that state has already been set. | A substantial benefit rejects an effect restricted to the original conditioning. It does not exclude protection during the later restart or challenge transient. Absence of benefit leaves startup use viable. |
| The additive changes the working liquid interface, catalyst chemistry, or coupled H2/CO/water access | A benefit can persist under supplied-water dominance, possibly with rate or selectivity differences before the challenge. | Such a result supports an ongoing effect worth developing, but does not by itself assign it to water conductance or establish physical rather than chemical promotion. |

The **predefined primary decision** is whether late low-dose PDVB improves recovered operating function of the complete formulation under this challenge. Set the needed resolution from replicated reference variability. Keep fractional retention and absolute output separate: cumulative useful output over the fixed window charges the initial penalty, while the retention ratio alone does not. No lifetime extrapolation or intrinsic-site-preservation claim follows from this first endpoint.

## Limits and next decision

**Source dominance at the outlet does not prove negligible active-site gradients.** Raising flow or lowering inventory need not reduce production per granule. Fixed inlet H2/CO does not ensure fixed local H2O/H2 or surface oxygen-removal kinetics. This test constrains a whole-bed production explanation; excluding it additionally requires a bound on sensitivity to the residual exposure difference. A benefit rejects an effect confined to original conditioning, but protection during repacking/restart or the challenge transient remains possible.

A persistent benefit justifies a targeted mechanism test, not fitting an unmeasured local-water activity until a preferred explanation succeeds. No benefit, with the source effect and challenge qualified, bounds late addition in this defined state. It leaves startup or internally generated-water use viable, not proven. Without reproduced source behavior, a null challenge is inconclusive about the published phenomenon.

**Conditional next intervention:** if a reproducible contrast and its water-source dependence leave intragranular removal plausible, compare one smaller catalyst-granule fraction with the original fraction, each with and without the same low PDVB dose. Preserve promoter granule size, document packing, match conditioning, and measure generation per catalyst mass. Freeze a resistance-model prediction for how promotion changes; distinguish it from the ordinary size response of the unpromoted arm. This changes internal diffusion length, unlike flow alone. It is not an automatic factorial extension: stop if the first contrast is absent or the predicted size effect cannot be resolved. Fang already varies size, so novelty lies in the prospective prediction under the defined history/source boundary. Contact and liquid-state changes remain alternatives.

An intrinsic cobalt-preservation claim would need a later matched additive-removal assay with sham handling, or an independent catalyst-state constraint. Neither is required for the first formulation-level claim. Commercial value, humid-feed application and universal transferability remain unproved.

The subsequent [size-discrimination analysis](../../calculations/water-size-discriminator.md), [independently checked](../../reviews/water-quantitative-followup-review.md), limits the conditional size comparison above: radius-squared scaling can arise from either internal diffusion or external transfer. A two-size comparison alone cannot identify which changed. Its defensible objective is a prospective substitution or shaping decision with independent parameter bounds, or a directly measured formulation comparison if those bounds are unavailable.
