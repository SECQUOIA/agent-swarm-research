# Disposition of hydrocarbon preloading as a causal intervention

2026-09-16. Completed evaluation of the assigned PDVB-preloading idea. No experiment is proposed for immediate execution, and no new research direction is opened.

**Do not promote PDVB preloading as a clean discriminator between wax redistribution and water transport.** Preloading changes the very liquid–solid interface through which water and reactants would move. A matched hydrocarbon mass elsewhere in the bed does not hold that interface constant. The intervention could establish sensitivity to additive conditioning, but would not uniquely identify the mechanism and presently does not strengthen the lead beyond the already bounded formulation challenge.

## Established evidence and the capacity question

Fang et al. describe the headline PDVB as nonporous, with BET surface area below 3 m²/g. SI Fig. 2 was inspected in the original PDF: it provides a nitrogen isotherm and that surface-area annotation, but no numerical PDVB pore-volume value. The **0.81 cm³/g** pore volume in SI Fig. 1 belongs to **Co/SiO2**, not PDVB. Nitrogen-accessible porosity does not quantify hot hydrocarbon swelling, external liquid films or liquid held at granule contacts. No PDVB wax capacity can be assigned from these data. [Fang et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76571-8), main methods and SI Figs. 1–2.

A simple mass balance limits the strongest sink interpretation. At the reported feed and approximately constant promoted conversion, a CH2-equivalent balance gives roughly 0.097–0.106 g hydrocarbons/h for 0.5 g catalyst, or order 10² g over 1200 h. This is an analyst estimate using the existing 0–25 °C gas-volume convention range, not measured wax accumulation. The 0.025 g PDVB cannot be described as indefinitely absorbing all this product. If its measured retained capacity were C g hydrocarbon/g PDVB, a nonregenerating sink could retain at most 0.025C g. The fraction of total generated hydrocarbon held that way is bounded by `0.025C/M_product`.

That argument does **not** exclude a small redistributed liquid inventory, selective retention of a minor component, an early-history effect with persistent consequences, or continuous liquid drainage through a finite inventory. A material can alter the location and exchange of liquid without accumulating all product. Neither the relevant fraction nor C has been measured here.

The public review already discusses a protective wax layer during startup, uneven wax distribution, and hydrogenolysis contributions to hydrogen uptake. These are disclosed concerns, not new mechanisms discovered by this evaluation. Fang 2022 also reports different product distributions with porous PDVB and attributes them partly to hydrocarbon retention. Its olefin evidence does not establish the long-chain wax behavior of the 2026 nonporous polymer. [Fang et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abo0356).

## What the liquid literature adds

The original [Zheng et al. MRI study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41929-023-00913-8) shows that liquid composition inside Ru/TiO2 pellets can continue changing after outlet behavior appears stable, and differs from collected product wax. It also identifies a water-rich liquid at the pore surface. Thus collected FT wax is a reasonable material to characterize, but not an automatically faithful substitute for the cobalt catalyst's working pore liquid. The primary local text was inspected for these conclusions.

[Unglaub, Thiessen and Jess 2023](https://doi.org/10.21926/cr.2301001) experimentally alternates FT operation and hydrogenolysis to drain wax from Pt-promoted Co/Al2O3 pellets. It reports changes in output and product distribution, with drainage incomplete under some conditions. Hydrogen treatment both reacts with hydrocarbons and changes the catalytic environment, so it is not a liquid-only reset. Their [kerosene-selectivity follow-up](https://doi.org/10.21926/cr.2303022) further constrains any claim to invent wax-management operation. These materials and geometries differ from Fang's granules; their quantitative improvements are not forecasts for the present system.

The newly available [Yang et al. 2020 original](../../../literature/papers/yang2020-investigation-of-the-deactivation-behavior/fulltext.md), pp. 7–9, strengthens the working-liquid alternative without validating preloading as a selective test. In different silica-encapsulated Co catalysts, the authors attribute the 220 °C decline mainly to heavy-hydrocarbon accumulation, supported by 56–59% spent-sample TG mass loss, while assigning the 240 °C behavior more strongly to oxidation. The interpretation is supported by catalyst characterization but is not a direct measurement of local water or the liquid inventory of Fang's mixture. Retained-liquid effects and their temperature dependence must therefore be treated as established competing explanations, not a novel reason to initiate a preload campaign.

## The proposed contrast and why it is not selective

The proposed pair would compare PDVB pre-equilibrated with a characterized FT-like hydrocarbon liquid against initially dry PDVB, while adding the same hydrocarbon mass to catalyst/quartz in the other arm. Both would then be introduced after common parent conditioning. This controls total added carbon, but not its location, coating thickness, polymer swelling, gas access or redistribution time.

Preloading can simultaneously:

- occupy hydrocarbon capacity or change which components exchange;
- coat the hydrophobic surface that is hypothesized to affect water removal;
- change H2/CO dissolution and diffusion, contact wetting and packing;
- introduce liquid that evaporates, redistributes or undergoes hydrogenolysis during restart.

An equal mass on quartz may migrate onto PDVB before a functional difference is measurable. Conversely, a persistent coating may suppress water transfer even if no finite wax-sink mechanism exists. Room-temperature contact angle or nitrogen sorption cannot establish that those pathways stayed unchanged during hot syngas operation.

The relevant preloading endpoint would therefore have to be measured at the working temperature and gas composition, with standardized drainage, repeatable retained amount/composition, and resolved exchange time. Even then, saturation with one hydrocarbon mixture does not prove no capacity remains for a newly produced heavier or oxygenated component. No such validated endpoint is available from the source.

## Falsifiable claim and limited usefulness

A narrow hypothesis can be stated: **the entire benefit requires initially empty PDVB capacity to capture the specified hydrocarbon fraction, and preoccupying that capacity removes the benefit.** If the relevant capacity is independently shown to remain occupied during the decision window and the preloaded formulation retains the same benefit, that restricted empty-sink explanation fails. This would not exclude redistribution, continuous exchange, an earlier irreversible event, or water transport.

Loss of benefit after preloading is weaker evidence: it is compatible with finite-capacity loss, blocked water pathways, altered reactant access, or changed restart chemistry. A total-hydrocarbon-matched control does not distinguish these. No difference is also inconclusive if the initial loading contrast disappears before the catalytic measurement. These interpretation limits are inherent to the intervention, not merely missing replicates.

The immediately useful negative conclusion is therefore that **preloading cannot presently replace the local-exposure constraint missing from outlet-water measurements**. It should remain a recorded rejected mechanism discriminator. If later direct evidence makes additive conditioning itself a consequential formulation variable, its operational effect could be tested on that narrower basis; this note does not authorize or develop that additional campaign.

## Close-out and sources

Scientific plausibility that working liquids matter is substantial; confidence that this preload contrast selectively tests a finite wax sink is low. A conditioning experiment is feasible in principle, but demonstrating the intended state and separating compensating interface changes would require more than the apparent simplicity of “fill the polymer first.” Practical value beyond established wax management is presently unproved. No preloading experiment is included in the water program's bounded first stage.
