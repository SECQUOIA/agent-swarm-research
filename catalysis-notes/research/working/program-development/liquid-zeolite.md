# Liquid-phase zeolite program development: test the transferability of solvent promotion before building a program

2026-09-16. Research screen and proposed experiments; no experimental results. This proposal has now received the [independent closeout review](../../reviews/current-programs-closure-review.md); it remains a conditional study. Literature additions are assigned to the root agent's single reusable `$lit` worker; no knowledge-base files were changed here.

## Decision

**Do not yet promote a new substantial liquid-phase zeolite program from this search.** The most defensible remaining question is whether solvent environments selected for fast dilute-feed epoxidation retain their advantage after products become an appreciable part of the reacting liquid and the catalyst's resting state changes. This can justify a small, conditional experiment on an existing Ti-zeolite platform. It does not currently justify establishing a new platform or advertising a general solution to concentrated-feed catalysis.

This conclusion is based on actual overlap with published knowledge, not on the existence of active competing groups. Product inhibition, solvent-dependent rate enhancement, competition between epoxidation and peroxide decomposition, secondary epoxide consumption, recycle-impurity adsorption, and product-derived pore blockage are already established. The residual opportunity would have to demonstrate **a consequential failure of predictions made from this existing knowledge**, identify its cause, and predict a useful remedy. An additional spectrum of a product-filled pore would not suffice.

The earlier concentrated-sugar/lactate screen remains unselected. This search did not uncover evidence overturning its strongest objections: established tandem catalysis; fragment recombination that can retain useful sugar carbon; continuous potassium-maintained Sn-USY comparators; and no demonstrated high-concentration loss specifically amenable to the proposed fragment-capture remedy. See [the prior practical challenge](../liquid-phase-practical-challenge.md). The earlier wet-VFA ketonization idea is also not revived; it is a different oxide problem already challenged by its own economic and mechanistic evidence.

## Why this remaining question matters

Hydrogen peroxide must produce useful oxygenated product rather than decompose or oxidize unwanted intermediates. A catalyst/solvent combination that gives a large initial epoxidation rate can still be unattractive if it requires excessive dilution, retains epoxide, accelerates ring opening, or forces costly solvent purification. Conversely, failure of a dilute-rate descriptor would be useful knowledge only if it changes a material or operating choice at useful throughput.

The connection to Gounder is direct: site-environment control, quantitative adsorption and Lewis-acid kinetics, and his coauthored work on Ti-zeolite solvent networks. The connection to Iglesia is methodological: elementary-step rates, adsorption thermodynamics, transport, and the distinction between primary and secondary selectivity. No claim about the professors' present equipment or project preferences is made.

The chemically specific question is:

> Can the useful epoxide output and peroxide consumption of a product-rich liquid be predicted from independently measured dilute reaction kinetics and multicomponent adsorption, or does the accumulating product alter the intrapore environment enough to change the relative barriers of productive and unproductive reactions?

The first branch is the default, simpler explanation. A valid positive result for that branch may be useful reaction engineering; it would weaken the case for a new mechanistic program.

## A testable hypothesis, not an established new mechanism

**H1, reaction-generated solvent effect.** In a sufficiently product-rich Ti-BEA liquid, epoxide and/or its first ring-opening product changes pore solvent organization beyond its competitive binding to Ti. This changes the relative free-energy barriers for epoxidation, peroxide loss and epoxide consumption. Rate ratios and catalyst rankings then depart from predictions using dilute-condition rate constants, measured activities, and a fixed competitive-adsorption model.

**Why plausible:** solvent composition and pore polarity affect relative rates strongly, and Torres' thesis already shows epoxide-induced solvent spectral changes in nonreacting Ti-MFI. **What remains weakly established:** an additional, consequential effect on productive and secondary kinetics at product concentrations reached during useful Ti-BEA operation, beyond the existing adsorption and solvent dependencies. Known adsorption and secondary reactions may already explain everything.

Alternatives that must remain live:

1. **Ordinary competition and known reaction network.** A fixed microkinetic model with measured product adsorption predicts the trajectory; apparent changes follow the Ti–OOH/epoxide occupancy balance.
2. **Bulk activity or phase effects.** Adding product changes alkene/peroxide activities or produces liquid-liquid phase separation. The effect need not involve a special pore environment.
3. **Reaction and diffusion.** Product changes diffusivity or external mass transfer, creating gradients and apparent selectivity changes.
4. **Persistent catalyst change.** Ring-opened oligomers, leaching or hydrolysis change the catalyst during exposure.
5. **Adsorbate-induced site geometry.** Product changes Ti coordination and elementary chemistry. That is distinct from extended solvent reorganization and needs separate structural evidence.

A model residual is not a mechanistic assignment. In particular, inverse product order is already known and cannot establish H1. Nor does a solvent-induced change in an adsorption enthalpy establish a change in adsorption free energy: entropy and standard states must be included.

## Decisive first campaign, conditional on an existing platform

Use one characterized Ti-BEA catalyst and one aqueous-organic solvent window for 1-hexene epoxidation as the accessible liquid test. Select the window from the reproduced Potts kinetics, rather than choosing a large catalyst library. Product effects may be easier to resolve on hydrophilic Ti-BEA, but that is an experimental choice, not a prediction of improvement. Start with one catalyst; add its hydrophobic comparator only after an unexplained, reproducible effect exists.

**Gate 1: establish a useful test window.** Reproduce primary epoxidation and peroxide-loss rates, identify all significant products, establish a single liquid phase, and determine whether a product concentration accessible on an integral conversion trajectory causes a resolved effect. Product cofeed is a means of decoupling exposure from conversion, not a claim that a highly concentrated synthetic feed is process-relevant. A realistic product envelope must come from an actual conversion run and its material balance.

**Gate 2: distinguish current knowledge from the proposed excess effect.** Measure a small set of fresh-catalyst differential rates with independently varied epoxide concentration. Run separate peroxide-without-alkene and epoxide-plus-peroxide controls in the same solvent, together with catalyst-free controls. Add the dominant ring-opening product only if measured in the integral run; do not screen a catalog of arbitrary poisons. Measure reversible adsorption or uptake and washout at reaction temperature where practicable. Fit the established network and competitive-adsorption model to these component experiments before examining the held-out trajectory. Require that the baseline reproduce the component experiments within uncertainty before accepting its integral prediction. Kwon's vapor-phase model leaves the measured half-order ring-opening dependence unexplained (p.7); do not import its incomplete elementary scheme as a uniquely established baseline. If this liquid system also requires a bounded empirical secondary-rate relation, determine and freeze it from the component data. A failure already present there is not evidence for a new integral product-induced solvent effect.

The baseline must include known composition changes along conversion. Epoxidation and peroxide decomposition produce water, while epoxide hydrolysis can consume it; feed and sampling also change the liquid inventory. Track the resulting water/organic composition and measured activities, and include independently established solvent-composition dependence of the component rates and adsorption within the selected window. Here a fixed model means fixed equations and pre-estimated parameters, not constant solvent composition or omission of Potts' already established water effects. If those dependencies cannot be constrained well enough, report an underconstrained baseline rather than assigning its residual to a new product-induced pore effect.

**Gate 3: freeze a prediction.** Predict useful epoxide output, peroxide consumption and major secondary products for an integral batch or continuous residence-time trajectory, using the separately estimated parameters. Reserve a new trajectory for validation: the Gate 1 run that selected the product envelope is development data and cannot also serve as the held-out test. Freeze the predicted ordering for a second product level or solvent composition before measuring it. Compare prediction error with propagated analytical uncertainty and uncertainty in identifiable parameters; do not make a flexible product-dependent rate constant the default repair.

**Gate 4: challenge a failure before assigning H1.** Return to the original feed to test reversibility; analyze dissolved Ti and recovered catalyst; measure mixing and particle-size effects at the most product-rich condition; establish a carbon balance including retained/extracted carbon. If the residual remains, use a minimal hydrophilic/hydrophobic matched comparison and competitive solvent uptake/NMR or IR to ask whether solvent displacement tracks the residual. A product band alone cannot distinguish bound product from a reorganized solvent network. Specialized spectroscopy is an expansion after the kinetic result, not the first purchase.

Essential analytical distinction: total peroxide disappearance minus epoxide formation cannot be labelled peroxide decomposition when secondary oxidation consumes peroxide. Measure oxygen evolution and major oxidation products, and explicitly balance peroxide equivalents. Quench samples using a validated procedure so continued oxidation does not create an apparent reaction-network difference after sampling.

Transport controls must be empirical at the working composition. The Potts paper's statement that linear dependence on alkene concentration excludes internal diffusion must **not** be inherited as a general diagnostic: a first-order reaction-diffusion system with concentration-independent diffusivity can remain first order despite substantial internal gradients. Use crystal-size/particle-size and mixing checks appropriate to the limiting length scale, supported by bounded diffusivity estimates.

An initial campaign could be weeks of reaction and analysis work after a validated reactor, catalyst and analytical platform exist. Commissioning, materials preparation and advanced spectroscopy are additional work. No fixed calendar promise is supported by the available information.

## Closeout scope and first decision

Desktop development is complete at **conditional model-transfer study**, with no program promotion or requirement to build a platform. The initial commitment ends after Gates 1–3: one catalyst, one solvent window, the measured dominant products, independently constrained component behavior and one genuinely held-out integral trajectory. Before validation, specify the accepted product and peroxide-balance metrics, the smallest prediction error that could change a catalyst or operating choice, the prediction interval, balance tolerances and independent repeatability. If parameter uncertainty makes that useful error unresolvable, report an underconstrained prediction rather than a failure of the known mechanism.

A prediction that meets the predeclared bounds closes the proposed excess-effect branch in the tested domain. A reproducible consequential failure permits the already specified Gate 4 controls; it does not identify solvent reorganization. If those controls cannot distinguish activity, phase behavior, transport, persistent loss and site geometry from extended solvent organization, retain the operational discrepancy and stop that molecular claim. New solvents, topologies and interventions are later options requiring a positive gate, not unfinished desktop tasks. The uploaded originals strengthen the known-mechanism baseline and the novelty limits; they do not change this conservative disposition. Any unreviewed Supporting Information qualifies the detailed source comparison, not the existence of an experimentally validated anomaly.

## What would justify a substantial program

The advance would be a predictive rule for **when solvent promotion remains useful throughout conversion**, together with a demonstrated intervention that improves a full material-balance outcome. It would not be a new label for known inhibition.

If H1 survives the controls, first determine whether the excess effect changes a real catalyst or solvent ranking. Then use measured adsorption and solvent displacement to choose one intervention: for example, a different solvent composition or defect density predicted to preserve useful product escape while retaining productive peroxide use. Do not assume that water addition, greater hydrophobicity, periodic washing or slower peroxide addition is itself novel; each must be benchmarked against established practice.

The progression would be:

1. Identify a reproducible, consequential failure of the fixed-environment model on one liquid reaction, or validate its transferability and stop the mechanistic expansion.
2. Establish the cause with independently constrained site occupancy, liquid activities, solvent displacement and transport; predict a second material/solvent ordering before testing it.
3. Test the chosen intervention at matched net epoxide output and carbon yield, with peroxide demand, solvent circulation, catalyst inventory and stability all counted.
4. Only then consider propylene/TS-1 or another epoxide as a meaningful external validation. A successful 1-hexene/Ti-BEA result does not establish an HPPO improvement.

**Stop or keep as reaction engineering:** known adsorption and reaction steps predict the data; the discrepancy is purely bulk phase behavior or conventional diffusion; the effect requires implausible product concentrations; the ranking change disappears after activity correction; or the best intervention exchanges a minor peroxide benefit for materially worse product recovery or solvent burden. An informative null result is useful for validating model transfer, but it is not evidence of a new catalytic mechanism.

## Separate confidence judgments

| Question | Assessment and basis |
|---|---|
| Does a product-rich pore change the relevant solvent environment beyond known competition? | **Plausible but unestablished.** Solvent sensitivity and polar products motivate the hypothesis; existing product-inhibition kinetics and extensive prior network studies explain much of the expected behavior already. |
| Can the first experiment be feasible and informative? | **Moderate to strong on an established liquid epoxidation platform.** Rates, product cofeeds, balances and washout are accessible. The attribution to solvent organization is appreciably harder than detecting a failure of a frozen kinetic prediction. |
| Will this deliver meaningful practical improvement? | **Low confidence at present.** No existing evidence demonstrates a practically consequential failure, a superior intervention, or headroom against industrial TS-1 operation. Model validation is more credible than an improved process claim. |
| Is this currently a strong substantial program? | **No.** It is a carefully delimited opening with a useful test, not a demonstrated opportunity that should displace stronger directions. Reassess only if an actual anomaly or favorable full-trajectory intervention is established. |
