# Fluorochemical destruction: closed search of steam scheduling

Date: 2026-09-16. Updated against the subsequently uploaded Chi original; see the [current upload audit](../../reviews/current-uploads-closed-legacy-audit.md). Status: **narrow negative; no new program promoted**. This search was already underway when the user asked to finish developing the current ideas and stop starting new ones. It closes the intervention examined using the evidence already obtained. It does not open a PFAS, refrigerant, adsorbent, or catalyst-screening branch.

## Decision and scope

The examined intervention was temporal separation of CF4 activation and water-assisted removal of surface fluoride on alumina-based catalysts. The proposed chemical benefit was to expose Lewis-acid/oxide pairs during a low-steam interval, then restore them with a short steam-rich interval before fluorination caused persistent structural damage. This is a concrete operating intervention with an appropriate connection to Iglesia's oxide acid–base chemistry and transient kinetics. Gounder's Lewis-acid expertise is relevant; confinement was not needed to formulate the question.

**The intervention itself is directly disclosed in 2026 patent CN121466793A.** Earlier and contemporary papers also establish Ga-assisted regeneration, proton-donor promotion, water inhibition, and sequential fluorination/hydrolysis experiments. These findings remove the proposed novelty of “pulse steam to balance activation and regeneration.” They do not establish that all useful transient science has been done. A collaboration could still resolve a consequential operating boundary. This search did not establish a sufficiently distinct, practically consequential remaining contribution to justify adding a program to the portfolio.

The useful negative is specific: do not promote a new steam-pulsing, hydroxyl-restoration, or generic Lewis-acid-doping program on the premise that the activation–regeneration conflict has not been recognized. The field is not exhausted, and existing groups' participation is not the exclusion criterion. The direct disclosed intervention and the absence of a developed additional contribution are the reasons for closing this search.

## The hypothesis examined, and why it does not survive as a new intervention

**Proposed hypothesis before finding the patent:** a useful time interval exists in which water-derived adsorbates have cleared enough to expose CF4-activating Al–O pairs, while fluoride accumulation remains recoverable. A subsequent steam pulse restores the working surface faster than persistent fluorination develops. Repeated operation might then increase cumulative destroyed CF4 per catalyst mass at equal average water supply.

The chemical alternatives are specific. A transient rate increase could come from reduced competitive adsorption, from CF4 concentration/residence-time changes as water flow changes, or from consumption of a finite oxygen/fluoride-storage capacity. Recovery of an OH spectrum could accompany restructuring without restoring the original reaction rate. Conversely, water removal could accelerate persistent fluorination enough that the apparent activation benefit disappears over complete cycles.

The patent expressly addresses the same water-inhibition/regeneration conflict and implements the proposed timing intervention. Replacing its commercial alumina with Ga/alumina, or adding spectroscopy and a schedule optimization, would not by itself constitute the needed advance. Its inconsistencies leave validation questions; they do not make the disclosed concept new.

## What a bounded validation would have had to establish

This is a record of the discriminating first experiment considered, **not a new recommendation to launch it**. On one existing alumina formulation, hold CF4 molar input, total wet flow, temperature and bed geometry constant while comparing a low/high-water cycle against steady water levels, including the same mean water input. Replace changing water with inert gas so a dilution change is not mistaken for chemistry. Determine the actual inlet/outlet water waveform with a nonreacting blank before interpreting pump commands as catalyst exposure. First obtain differential-rate responses; then assess complete-cycle output at useful conversion.

The crucial observations would be contemporaneous CF4 and carbon-product molar flows, time-resolved recovered fluoride, end-of-cycle solid fluoride, and retained rate after return to a common reference condition. Include an empty-reactor/inert-packing blank, catalyst-free gas-analysis recovery, and independently repeated catalyst charges for the decisive cycling/steady comparison. Check particle-size and flow changes at common residence/contact conditions to exclude transport effects. A quartz reactor or support cannot be assumed chemically invisible under HF exposure; measure silicon-containing effluent or material loss when it could affect the balance.

The first mechanistic claim would be modest: whether an apparent pulsing advantage survives matched inlet histories, complete cycles, and accumulation accounting. It would not establish a unique atomic site or a universal mechanism. A claimed useful time window would require a prospectively specified test at an unfit cycle duration or CF4 loading after determining adsorption and recovery responses. No such prediction was frozen or validated in this search.

A positive short test would justify consideration only if it outperformed the best feasible steady operation beyond measurement uncertainty and kept the relevant carbon/fluorine inventories bounded. A negative would be informative if it showed that time-averaged dilution, stored oxygen, fluoride uptake, or cumulative irreversible loss explained the advantage. Stop if the measurement system cannot distinguish these alternatives, or if an apparent advantage disappears when compared with optimized steady operation. No arbitrary performance percentage is presented as a literature fact or expected gain.

## Process comparison and accounting

The proper catalytic comparators are steady operation of the same catalyst, strong Ga/θ-Al2O3 regeneration formulations, and the newer aluminate under a matched throughput. SO2-assisted operation is a fair option where SO2 is already in the feed; deliberate SO2 addition must include its handling and downstream sulfate burden. A higher-temperature, higher-throughput catalyst can be the better process even when its conversion-temperature headline looks worse.

A noncatalytic comparator already identified is steam plasma. The primary publisher abstract of [Chen et al., 2019, 10.1016/j.jfluchem.2018.10.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfluchem.2018.10.004) reports greater than 99.99% CF4 removal at 16 kW, 1 vol% inlet CF4 and 50 standard L/min. The full original was not retrieved; this supports inclusion of a substantial-flow alternative, not a complete energy or lifecycle comparison. An open plasma/catalysis experimental paper was also located at [IJP E S T 15 (2021), e03004](https://ijpest.com/Contents/15/3/PDF/2021-15-e03004.pdf), and was subsequently retained and read by the central literature worker in [round 12](../../../literature/runs/2026-09-16-queued-supplied/round-12/run.md). It was not reread in this audit; this screen does not add a detailed comparison from it.

For any future comparison, report cumulative carbon converted to identified products and fluorine exported as measured inorganic fluoride per catalyst mass and per elapsed hour, including conditioning, regeneration, bypass and restart. Parent disappearance is insufficient. Fluoride in a scrubber is an accounted product requiring management; fluoride retained in a solid is inventory, not proof of a catalytic cycle. Steam-induced fluoride release is regeneration only to the extent that catalytic function returns; it does not eliminate the fluoride-disposal obligation.

A simple independent inventory bound illustrates why short tests deserve caution. Complete conversion of one gram of Al2O3 to AlF3 could retain 6/101.96 = **0.0588 mol F per gram**. Thus a 2 g alumina charge has a theoretical bulk bound near 0.118 mol F, far greater than a surface-site count. This is an upper bound, not a claim that complete bulk fluorination occurs. Demonstrate throughput well beyond a plausible solid-storage inventory, with stable inventory and measured exported fluoride, before using duration alone to argue catalytic turnover. Long-duration literature experiments can exceed this bound; the bound is not a blanket objection to their activity.

Water savings also require a process-specific account. If steam is inherited from upstream scrubbing, decreasing catalyst inlet water may require dehumidification and reheating. Include those duties, reactor heating, pressure drop, corrosion, catalyst replacement, Ga or other metal consumption, and fluoride/sulfate handling. Neither lower furnace temperature nor lower pump duty alone establishes an energy benefit.

## Separate confidence judgments

- **Underlying chemical conflict:** well supported. CF4 activation, water inhibition and fluoride removal compete on these oxide surfaces. Confidence is lower in the existence of a useful dynamic operating window beyond already disclosed operation.
- **Experimental feasibility and informativeness:** moderate on an established hot-fluorochemical platform; unconfirmed here. Gas switching is straightforward relative to quantitative hot HF/water analysis and complete balances. The latter are essential dependencies, not optional characterization.
- **Practical improvement over strong alternatives:** unresolved and presently too weak to support portfolio promotion. No equal-duty, equal-throughput improvement has been demonstrated by this search.
- **Novel contribution from the proposed intervention:** not supported. Direct pulse-water prior art occupies the intervention. A specific additional contribution would have to be established before reopening; no further candidate is proposed.
