# Does sugar stereochemistry control tungsten extraction and useful turnover?

2026-09-16. A conditional probe for the [tungsten program](oxygenate-program-alternative.md), revised against the [fresh intervention review](../../reviews/tungsten-intervention-review.md). Updated after the [current-upload audit](../../reviews/current-uploads-tungsten-audit.md). Proposed experiments only. This note does not establish a new active-site structure or practical improvement.

**Recommendation:** retain glucose-to-mannose replacement only as a small, conditional feed-composition probe; **do not select it as the program's core causal intervention**. Withdraw the original inactive-mannose-reservoir lead. The hot data establish productive conversion of a mannose feed, potentially through glucose after epimerization. The remaining hypothesis is that mannose enrichment increases net W export without a corresponding gain in useful C2 output. The specified adverse direction is weakly supported. A positive pilot could justify development; a null should retire this lead rather than trigger an expanding measurement campaign.

The [closeout review](../../reviews/current-programs-closure-review.md) retains this disposition. This probe is not a prerequisite for finishing the tungsten proposal. Its only presently specified next decision is whether the published soluble reference gives an analytically resolved window and whether mannose occurs at a relevant feed/recycle level. If either requirement fails, archive the probe. Any later pilot must predeclare the smallest useful absolute W-export difference and C2-output comparison; an imprecise null is inconclusive, not evidence that export is unchanged.

## Evidence and the unresolved chemical thesis

The inspected author manuscript of El Mohammad et al., [Inorganic Chemistry 2024, DOI 10.1021/acs.inorgchem.3c04236](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.inorgchem.3c04236), provides the specific contrast. Its discussion on manuscript pp. 7–8 reports readily observed W–mannose complexes around neutral pH, whereas W–glucose complexes at pH 7 required enriched carbon-13 glucose for detection. The illustrated NMR solutions contain **2 M metal and 1 M sugar**. Mannose mixtures were generally stable over the ambient observation period; that is evidence of persistent complexes under those conditions, not catalytic inactivity at high temperature. Separately, the XANES measurements use **0.1 M metal and 0.05 M sugar** and support octahedral W(VI) environments for mannose/erythrose solutions (pp. 11–13). The two concentration ranges must not be conflated. Glucose was not included in those XANES measurements because its complex population was small.

The chemical inference is specific but unproven: changing C2 configuration changes access to multidentate sugar coordination; stabilization of extracted W could lower its tendency to return to the support **without preventing cleavage**. The matched epimers have the same elemental composition and hydroxyl count. They are therefore a useful contrast to changing sugar concentration, adding a mineral acid, or substituting supports. Their different ring/open-chain populations are part of this molecular perturbation; the experiment does **not** isolate the energy of one particular W–O bond.

**The recovered primary SI supplies a hot feed comparison.** [El Mohammad et al., ACS Catalysis 2024 SI, DOI 10.1021/acscatal.4c02791.s001](https://ndownloader.figshare.com/files/47503932), Figure S7 (PDF p. 6, visually inspected), compares glucose and mannose feeds at 150 °C, 5.0 mM sugar and 1.3 mM W under 40 bar N2. Mannose-fed solutions produce glycolaldehyde with AMT at pH 5, sodium tungstate at pH 7 and AMT/NaOH at pH 11. Their early glycolaldehyde accumulation is comparable to or faster than glucose-fed accumulation in the displayed sampling window. These are net product concentrations, not intrinsic cleavage rates; the plotted 8% concentration-error bars do not establish independent reaction repeatability. Figure S8 (p. 7) shows substantial epimerization, including rapid glucose production from mannose. These figures do not separate direct mannose cleavage from cleavage after epimerization.

Figure S9 (p. 8, visually inspected) shows carbon closure declining **early**: around 10–15 minutes with AMT, unassigned carbon is already comparable to or larger than carbon present as glycolaldehyde in S7. This is an approximate visual comparison, not digitized data; S9 provides glucose, not mannose, closure. Shortening contact does not by itself solve this problem. Recovery and unassigned carbon must be small enough relative to the claimed effect in both matrices before a cleavage-only pilot is interpretable. Figures S26–S31 include hot XANES at 5 mM sugar and approximately 1.3 or 0.4 mM W, 150 or 180 °C, under 40 bar He. The substantial octahedral-like contribution in mannose-fed solutions and two-reference fits in S29 do **not** uniquely count inactive sugar complexes. The main article is now read: Table 1 (PDF p. 5) reports initial net GA formation rates of 0.048 and 0.086 mmol L−1 min−1 for glucose and mannose with AMT at pH 5, respectively. It also states that reported uncertainties use catalytic tests repeated up to three times (p. 3); this improves the evidence beyond the SI-only record, without establishing the repeatability of a future mixed-feed/export test. The productive-mannose result strengthens retirement of the inactive-reservoir premise.

Strong binding as a source of lost activity is already established reasoning. Orazov and Davis's [PNAS 2015 study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1516466112), local full text pp. 3–5, discusses poor W performance at moderate temperature and directly observes product-complexation limits in an aqueous Mo cascade. The potential new contribution here is a prospective relationship between epimer composition, **W export from a solid**, and useful turnover in the hot glucose-cleavage domain. Neither sugar coordination itself nor inhibition alone is the novelty claim.

There is also a consequential contrary result. Zhang et al., [Journal of Energy Chemistry 2015, DOI 10.1016/S2095-4956(15)60278-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2095-4956(15)60278-9), report that W compounds suppress glucose hydrogenation over Ru/C and that stronger suppression accompanies higher EG yields. The primary abstract/introduction were inspected; full-text controls remain unresolved. This does not prove the mannose hypothesis, but it prevents treating stronger W–sugar association as necessarily harmful. Selective protection of glucose, productive mobile W, and an inactive W reservoir are competing outcomes.

## One perturbation, with analytical and soluble references

Reproduce the SI's glucose and mannose comparison first: 150 °C, 5 mM sugar, approximately 1.3 mM W as AMT, stated pH 5 and 40 bar N2. These are an experimentally grounded starting domain, not a concentrated-feed or tandem-process benchmark. The original methods are now available (PDF p. 3): inject 10 mL sugar solution into 50 mL preheated catalyst solution, mix at 1100 rpm, and analyze by HPLC with a Ca-form monosaccharide column. Fructose and threose coelute in that method, so the inferred product assignment needs care when carbon pathways change. Establish direct carbonyl recovery and a short interval with adequate carbon closure. The inspected main article and SI provide single-substrate, concentration, pH, atmosphere and deoxy-sugar contrasts; no deliberate glucose/mannose mixed-feed competition experiment was identified there. This is a statement about these sources, not a literature-wide novelty claim. The two single-substrate curves and hot XANES are prior art, not proposed advances.

Use a small, constant concentration of uniformly carbon-13-labelled glucose as a reporter. Replace part of the remaining unlabelled glucose with unlabelled mannose at constant total hexose concentration, total W, feed counterions and residence time. Include glucose-only and mannose-only references and a label-dilution control. Resolve labelled/unlabelled sugars and products and quantify their recovery. The label distinguishes **feed ancestry**, not epimer identity at cleavage: labelled glucose may first become labelled mannose. Time-dependent labelled epimer populations constrain this network but need not identify every pathway. Mannose is a reactive component, not an inert poison.

Quantify labelled-product formation and total recovered useful C2 carbon, accounting for epimerization and product disappearance. Reporter inhibition need not mean lower total productivity. The 1.3 mM AMT reference establishes reproducibility of the published domain. For a supported-bed interpretation, also match the **measured exported-W concentration, liquid matrix and residence history**, including W-free-support effluent where needed. W speciation and rates can differ at the much lower concentrations exported by a bed. These soluble measurements constrain plausible contributions; subtracting their rate from the supported rate does not identify surface turnover.

The prerequisite is an interpretable analytical window, **not a required soluble inhibition or speciation effect**. Similar homogeneous rates do not select an extraction direction. A ligand might stabilize mobile W, adsorbed W, or neither under hot conditions; a solution without a support cannot resolve that choice. Ambient NMR and quenched samples cannot establish the hot dominant species. If spectroscopy cannot distinguish it at the relevant concentration, withhold the molecular assignment rather than adding apparatus to rescue the hypothesis.

## Prospective extraction test on the same solid

If the analytical window passes, apply the same replacement to **one supported-W formulation** and its W-free support blank. Use independent matched beds or an order-balanced design with measured inventories; glucose followed by mannose on one aging/depleting bed confounds feed and history. Define the observation interval, repeatability and resolvable effect first. A glucose return cannot restore W already exported. The Ti-linked Ni/W/Ti original is now read: it provides useful reuse and postreaction W concentrations, but Ti–O–W signatures covary with carbon, texture, W dispersion and Ni state. It does not isolate a motif-specific modification. Its SI reports a preleachate comparison, but the Ni/M-alone baseline must be subtracted before attributing an effect to that liquid. Neither reuse nor observing leachate activity is new. Removing Ni or H2 does not yield an automatically equivalent catalyst.

Register two separate predictions over the same interval: **absolute cumulative net W export increases, and useful recovered C2 output does not increase proportionally**. Measure both changes and their uncertainty before calculating W/C2. A higher ratio caused solely by lower output is not evidence of increased extraction. Include bed/wall/filter inventories and the soluble references. If a useful difference appears, freeze a simple response rule before testing an intermediate mannose fraction. That checks local feed-response prediction; it does not validate anchor chemistry or select an architecture.

Test fresh exported liquid for labelled-glucose cleavage at matched composition and calibrated hot transfer delay. The main program's immediate-quench bypass, residence-time distribution and recovery controls apply; a negative downstream result cannot exclude a short-lived mobile catalyst. The new claim concerns ligand-dependent export and function, not proof of permanent surface attachment.

**Main falsifier:** absolute W export does not rise within the predefined useful bound. If export rises but C2 output rises proportionally or more, the proposed additional W burden is falsified instead. A higher W/C2 ratio alone establishes neither ligand-assisted extraction nor its mechanism. Report unresolved molecular attribution if composition changes export but the cause remains indistinguishable. Retire this epimer lead if the pilot provides no useful differential effect.

The chief confounds are measurable. Mannose can react and epimerize; measure its time-dependent abundance rather than assign the inlet fraction to the catalyst. Different degradation products can alter proton activity or complex W; begin at low conversion and analyze those products before attributing a response to the parent epimer. Matching room-temperature pH does not guarantee matching hot proton activity, and a coordinating buffer would introduce another ligand. Surface adsorption, transport and support dissolution can also differ; measure support-element export and blank chemistry. A change only after substantial carbon deposition would call for a fouling explanation rather than immediate ligand-assisted extraction.

## What would make this a useful program rather than an inhibition paper?

The attainable pilot result is a reproducible feed-composition effect on net W export and recovered C2 output. Its value depends on a relevant feed/recycle composition and a consequential burden. A supported catalyst can retain its inventory while transiently releasing W; this pilot does not uniquely separate that mechanism from surface turnover. Returning to H2 and the hydrogenation function is necessary before a process interpretation, including both glucose-to-polyol diversion and desired carbonyl hydrogenation.

Only then choose an operating or catalyst response. A reversible mobile reservoir might favor soluble-W management and selective return of an active fraction; an extraction-resistant practical catalyst might favor retention. Neither recovery nor feed cycling is proposed as new by itself. A particular separation or stronger anchor is not justified until the measured response identifies the burden it would remove. Mannose must occur at sufficient concentration in the actual selected hydrolysate or recycle for this epimer-specific intervention to matter; deliberate high mannose loading is a mechanistic test, not evidence of process relevance. Do not propose expensive mannose removal on the present evidence.

**Confidence:** the ambient binding contrast and productive conversion of both feeds are supported. Some ligand-dependent export effect is plausible; its specified adverse direction is weakly supported. Experimental informativeness depends on recovery, relevant W concentrations and independent feed/history comparisons. Practical value is unestablished. This is a conditional supporting probe, not the program's selected core intervention.

## Most efficient route to a defensible core intervention

The newly supplied **Li et al. 2020 main article, DOI 10.1021/acssuschemeng.0c00836**, resolves the reading dependency but does not isolate anchor chemistry. Ti–O–W evidence covaries with W dispersion, Ni state, carbon and texture. Its seven postreaction W concentrations make cumulative export worth measuring, while a 40/100 mL inconsistency prevents a unique inventory-loss calculation. Do not prescribe a calcination/loading series from these correlations alone.

The originals of **10.1016/j.fuel.2017.11.086** and **10.1016/j.fuel.2018.04.115** are also now read. The first changes chelating/reducing agents and metal dispersion/alloy formation. The second compares hydroxyl-rich supports with supports treated at 800 °C; surface area, morphology, actual metal loading and Ni reduction/alloy state change together. Its EG yield declines from 63.1 to 41.2% by the fourth use (PDF p. 10). These are useful preparation studies, but they do not furnish an isolated hydroxyl-to-W-extraction intervention. The related thin-carbon-coating paper, **10.1007/s10562-018-2582-2**, likewise changes metal dispersion and phases and still loses activity on reuse; coating alone is neither new nor a demonstrated retention solution.

The **El Mohammad et al. 2024 main article, DOI 10.1021/acscatal.4c02791**, now supplies the analytical protocol and stronger contrary evidence to the inactive-mannose premise. Deoxy-sugar contrasts and apparent concentration orders constrain the proposed mononuclear O1,O3 cleavage species, but the article does not measure W extraction from a solid. H2/N2 equivalence without a hydrogenation metal does not settle working W states with Ni or Ru. Reading it improves experimental definition without validating the adverse export direction.

Two newly read neighboring studies reinforce existing controls. Carrier, Lambert and Che's **10.1016/S0167-2991(98)80213-9** demonstrates ligand-promoted alumina dissolution during tungstate-catalyst preparation, so support-element export and liquid speciation matter; it does not establish sugar-promoted W extraction or readsorption under turnover. Boudjema et al.'s **10.1021/acscatal.5c05891** identifies glycolaldehyde aldolization products and isotope-resolved Sn-Beta pathways. This supports careful carbonyl recovery and network analysis, without transferring Sn epimerization/cleavage assignments to tungsten or selecting a new program.

The highest-value experimental comparison is then the best reproducible Ti-linked architecture against its soluble-W/hydrogenation reference at a shared useful-output target, with hot W accounting and matched liquid chemistry. Establish whether the remaining burden is excessive W circulation, loss of recovered-W activity, or loss of the hydrogenation function. Only a demonstrated burden linked to a manipulable interaction supports selecting a core intervention. More elaborate mannose measurements cannot supply that missing link. The present evidence does not yet justify naming one particular anchoring modification as the strongest program thesis.
