# Focused alternative search: prevent acid–amine trapping during lactate dehydration

2026-09-16. Updated against the subsequently uploaded originals; see the [current upload audit](../../reviews/current-uploads-closed-legacy-audit.md). Bounded fresh search; no experiments performed and no knowledge-base or index edits. Evaluated under the current [standards](../../standards.md), after reading the [decision brief](../../decision-brief.md), the liquid-oxygenate, confined-framework-pair and zeolite-siting negative screens, and the biomass-oxygenate topic synthesis.

**No stronger challenger found in this pass.** The most credible specific intervention examined was to cofeed methanol during methyl-lactate dehydration over amine-modified Na-FAU, shifting free carboxylic acids toward methyl esters so that the selectivity modifier does not become a retained carboxylate salt that blocks pores. This is chemically motivated and experimentally falsifiable. However, close prior work already addresses the operating intervention and its commercial purpose. A direct experiment could improve mechanistic attribution, but the available evidence does not justify a new substantial program ahead of the current lead.

This is a negative result for one developed candidate, not a finding that acid/base or confined-site catalysis has no worthwhile problems. Activity by competitors is not treated as proof of an unpublished answer. Here the problem is that published catalysts, disclosed operating conditions and an explicit funded project already cover most of the proposed contribution; the remaining distinction is narrower than the important practical problem.

**Problem and professor fit.** Renewable lactate dehydration must maintain acrylate selectivity without sacrificing catalyst productivity or creating an expensive modifier-recovery cycle. Suppressing decarbonylation with a base can introduce another loss mechanism. The opportunity would be consequential if a defined chemical change preserved both selectivity and sustained output, rather than merely improving initial selectivity. Gounder's quantitative zeolite adsorption, acid-site accessibility and confinement expertise fits the distinction between proton capture and pore obstruction. Iglesia's reaction-network, inhibition and transient-kinetics methods fit the causal test. This is a methodological connection; neither professor is claimed to own the lactate process.

**Rejected molecular intervention.** An initial thought was to sterically protect the amine nitrogen against covalent addition to acrylate while retaining proton capture. Murphy's abstract identifies intact acid/base salts, not a demonstrated covalent addition product, as the principal amine-associated deposit. Steric shielding therefore attacks an unestablished failure mode, while also threatening proton access and diffusion. The 2021 spatial-design study already covers much of the steric/basicity axis. Do not develop a substituted-diamine library from this premise.

**The stronger chemical hypothesis considered.** At fixed methyl-lactate and water partial pressures, methanol reduces persistent acid–amine salt inventory by esterifying free acrylic/lactic acid. This preserves accessible catalyst volume and raises cumulative acrylate output without losing the modifier's suppression of decarbonylation. This is a proposed hypothesis, not a result inferred from the NSF project. It is stronger than a solvent screen because it specifies a chemical species to remove and a coupled retention–function prediction.

The main alternatives are substantial. Methanol may lower formation of reactive lactate and transient protons by suppressing methyl-lactate hydrolysis, thereby reducing both the desired flux and deposits. It may reversibly compete for adsorption without changing persistent deposits. It may alter amine desorption or distribution. Finally, diamine-associated deactivation may be mostly ordinary carbon deposition or transport obstruction from the original modifier inventory; suppressing free acid would then have little value. Methanol dehydration and its extra water are additional measured reactions, not assumed absent.

**Smallest informative first experiment, if a collaborator already has this platform.** No apparatus build or catalyst library is justified by this screen. First recover and read Murphy's complete main article and SI to ensure the product-challenge sequence below is not already there. If it is, stop at the documented negative rather than adding characterization.

Use one commercial Na-FAU batch, split into unmodified and one 12BPE-loaded material using the published preparation. Start from the published 300 °C, 78 Pa methyl-lactate and 1.1 kPa water conditions; these are reproduction conditions, not process-optimal targets. Hold substrate/water pressures, total flow, catalyst inventory and temperature constant while replacing inert with a chosen methanol pressure. Select one methanol level in a preliminary phase-equilibrium/blank check, then freeze it before the causal comparison. Do not optimize it against the final durability response.

The key comparison is a four-arm design: amine present/absent × methanol present/absent. At a common conditioned state, introduce an equal, quantified carbonyl-13C acrylic-acid exposure to all four arms at a pressure relevant to their product range. Follow labeled methyl acrylate, labeled free acid and other labeled products during exposure and a defined purge. Quantify retained label and intact modifier from sacrificial matched beds after the same exposure and purge, using extraction with independently checked recovery and appropriate chromatography/MS; use IR as supporting evidence for carboxylate/protonated-amine populations. Total retained carbon alone does not establish a salt or a working-site count.

The claim this test can support is limited: whether methanol prevents *product-derived persistent retention associated with the amine* while preserving subsequent catalytic function. The decisive pattern would be lower retained acid-derived label specifically in the amine-containing arm, more labeled ester recovered, unchanged retained modifier, and better subsequent activity under a common feed. An equal immediate rate response on bare and amine-loaded Na-FAU, without a retention difference, favors adsorption/hydrolysis effects. Lower retention caused by modifier loss is not protection. Lower carbon retention without chemical identification remains a bounded retention result.

Before this comparison, calibrate hot-line/vaporizer recovery with a catalyst bypass and inert packing, including lactic acid/oligomer accounting. Recover condensable, gaseous and retained carbon; freeze a balance acceptance interval from calibration uncertainty rather than accepting apparent conversion from disappearing feed. Establish labeled recovery separately. Check bed dilution and aggregate size at the fastest condition, and quantify substrate/product breakthrough: these checks do not establish absence of intracrystalline diffusion in modified FAU. Use independent replicate beds for the decisive four-arm contrast; uncertainty in retained label and function must resolve the claimed interaction. A finite product challenge tests susceptibility, not the actual rate-limiting cause of ordinary aging.

Only if that causal contrast is real should matched continuous runs test natural aging, fixed in advance at a horizon long enough to reproduce the reference decline. Compare integrated recovered acrylate carbon per complete catalyst charge and bed volume, alongside methanol use, amine export and carbon accumulation. No positive claim should rely on a greater final selectivity obtained by sacrificing throughput. Include a published amine-free K-ZSM-5 or formulated Na-FAU comparator before expanding; bare powder alone is an unfair practical benchmark.

**Decision criteria and value.** For an initial causal test, require a reproducible amine × methanol interaction in persistent acid retention and subsequent function, with both larger than the prespecified measurement uncertainty. A useful process outcome would additionally preserve or raise cumulative recovered acrylate output after charging catalyst/modifier volume, methanol recycle, separation and regeneration. Choose the minimum useful gain from the actual partner's process constraints; no unsupported percentage is imposed here. Stop if methanol only suppresses lactate conversion, changes reversible coverage, or removes the modifier; stop if intact acid–amine accumulation is negligible under the relevant feed. A negative result would reject salt removal as the reason to choose high alcohol feeds, but would not disprove other methanol benefits.

A regeneration claim must include modifier replacement or recovery, lost production time and repeat-cycle behavior. If acrylic acid rather than methyl acrylate is the desired product, downstream ester hydrolysis and alcohol recovery must be charged. A mechanism may favor methyl acrylate production while making acrylic acid production worse. No economics or greenhouse-gas advantage was calculated.

**Separate confidence judgments.** Hypothesis plausibility is moderate for pyridine/NaY because retained acid–base complexes are established; it is lower for the retained diamine material, where the proposed transfer is unproven. Experimental feasibility is good on an existing microreactor/analytical platform; informativeness is moderate because isotope retention and function can test susceptibility, but short challenges do not identify the dominant long-term loss mechanism. Practical gain beyond known alternatives is low-confidence: extra alcohol and modifier handling must compete with already disclosed amine-free formulations. Confidence that this is a stronger original program than the current lead is low, principally because the intervention and purpose overlap the funded project and patents.

**Originality boundary and final disposition.** A chemically resolved relation between acid–amine salt inventory and productivity might still be publishable and valuable to the process owners. No read source proves the exact isotope/product-challenge result already exists. Nevertheless, that narrower attribution does not currently change the material or process choice enough to support a competing standalone program. Retain this document as a useful negative and possible collaboration diagnostic. Do not promote alcohol cofeed, bulky-amine screening, or K–silanol pairing as new proposals from this pass.
