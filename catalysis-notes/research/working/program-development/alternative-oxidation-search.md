# Fresh oxidation/C1 challenge: methanol-derived Mo loss

2026-09-16. Updated against the subsequently uploaded originals; see the [current upload audit](../../reviews/current-uploads-closed-legacy-audit.md). Bounded research pass; no experiments. **No stronger program was found in this pass.** The concrete alternative examined was suppressing methanol-driven molybdenum export during formaldehyde manufacture while preserving productive surface methoxy chemistry. The loss is consequential, but existing chemistry, pellet studies and reactor models already cover the most obvious interventions. This is a negative selection result for the proposal below, not a claim that formaldehyde catalysis is solved.

The current [decision brief](../../decision-brief.md), [standards](../../standards.md), and earlier methane, peroxide and ethane-ODH screens were checked first. Their closed generic water-tolerance, methane-extraction, peroxide-interception and ODH material ideas were not reopened. A preliminary CO2-to-methanol search returned familiar In2O3/interface stabilization work without a stronger specific intervention; that lane was not developed. The present pass concentrated on one oxidation limitation rather than ranking that incidental search output.

## Why this limitation deserved a check

Methanol can export Mo from an operating oxidation catalyst into downstream deposits. Thus activity at the inlet and pressure drop downstream can be coupled through catalyst material transported by the reacting gas. Retaining more metal is useful only if the retained phase still supplies selective oxidation and does not require an unacceptable conversion, selectivity or transport penalty.

The direct Iglesia connection is supported MoOx/VOx chemistry and DME oxidation: Liu, Cheung and Iglesia already changed the underlying oxide to alter redox rates and HCHO selectivity ([2003 original](../../../literature/papers/liu2003-effects-of-al2o3-support-modifications/original.pdf), pp. 1 and 5–6). Gounder's adsorption, coadsorbate and acid/redox-function methods offer a methodological connection; this pass found no direct Gounder FeMo research program or confirmed platform access.

## One chemically specific hypothesis considered, but not promoted

**Intervention:** replace part of the methanol carbon feed by DME over the same conditioned oxide, while independently controlling water, oxygen and temperature. The proposed chemical distinction is whether molecular methanol/hydroxyl-assisted ligand exchange causes more Mo export per accepted HCHO molecule than DME-derived surface methoxy turnover. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated difference. DME is not assumed to avoid methoxy species or methanol: hydrolysis and secondary chemistry may remove the intended distinction.

The live alternatives are:

- Mo loss is set by the same productive methoxy population, regardless of which feed supplies it. Substitution changes the rate but not Mo loss per HCHO.
- DME changes water or methanol activity through hydrolysis, and those measured activities explain the loss without a distinct feed effect.
- Apparent protection comes from lower conversion, a cooler bed, altered residence time or deposition inside the catalyst rather than less export.
- DME opens less selective chemistry or needs more severe conditions, so any Mo-retention gain loses its useful-product advantage.

There is strong prior art for the reaction itself: Iglesia's 2003 paper is directly about DME-to-HCHO on modified supported MoOx/VOx. A January 2026 preprint, Zhang et al., [10.2139/ssrn.6126583](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.6126583), specifically promotes DME oxidation on W-modified FeMo. Its uploaded [preprint](../../../literature/papers/zhang2026-tungsten-modification-promotes-the-selective/original.pdf) was inspected, especially pp. 6–16. It reports 80.0% DME conversion and 80.1% HCHO selectivity at 290 °C and GHSV 4000 h−1, versus 75.0% and 79.1% for FeMo under the same conditions. The manuscript emphasizes adsorption/activation and composition; the inspected text does not establish the proposed matched Mo-export comparison. The preprint remains unreviewed. Neither this comparison nor Liu’s primary-selectivity measurements establishes a retention advantage from substituting DME for methanol. Applying known DME oxidation to a volatility measurement alone is insufficient.

## What a genuinely informative first experiment would require

This is a conditional design for a group already holding the reactor and reference material, **not a recommendation to establish a new platform**.

Use one existing FeMo batch, conditioned under a declared methanol feed. Establish differential HCHO production and Mo export with serial downstream capture and end-of-run recovery of catalyst, lines and deposits. Compare a methanol reference and one partial-DME substitution on independently loaded beds, first at common carbon input and measured temperature. Then make the useful comparison at matched recovered HCHO productivity by changing space velocity, recording all resulting conversion/selectivity differences. Independent decisive bed repeats are needed because the catalyst changes during measurement; an ordinary A–B–A switch alone cannot separate feed effects from irreversible depletion.

Water and oxygen cofeeds, total flow, particle size and bed dilution must be controlled. Measure DME, methanol, HCHO, CO/CO2 and other carbon products, not only methanol disappearance. Verify the collection train with recovery/spike tests and a downstream guard trap; missing Mo cannot be assigned to the gas phase. Use measured internal temperature and smaller-particle/changed-flow checks to test heat and mass transfer. A support blank and catalyst-free heated-feed run bound wall chemistry. Determine useful exposure duration from measurable Mo export, not an arbitrary accelerated temperature.

The first claim could only be: *under the declared conditions, substitution changes externally recovered Mo per recovered HCHO by more than the combined analytical and bed-to-bed uncertainty*. It could not establish identical working-site populations, the molecular volatile species, or industrial service life. A transient IR comparison could help assess surface methoxy populations if the basic contrast survives, but equal average spectra would not prove equal active ensembles.

Stop if the contrast disappears when actual water/methanol exposure, temperature or HCHO output is matched; if carbon/Mo recovery cannot resolve it; or if the required DME conversion/separation penalty dominates the measured retention gain. Expand only if a reproducible effect remains and would change a feed or bed design relative to the existing HAP/front-bed alternative. A practical comparison must charge DME preparation and recycle, unconverted carbon, oxygen, catalyst inventory, accepted HCHO concentration and any regeneration downtime. Stoichiometrically, DME oxidation generates less water per HCHO in that reactor, but producing DME from methanol generates the missing water upstream; no whole-process water or energy saving follows automatically.

## Confidence and decision

- **Importance of Mo transport:** high, supported by direct catalyst and pellet studies with a credible pressure-drop consequence.
- **Chemical hypothesis:** low. A distinction between productive methoxy turnover and molecular-methanol-assisted export is plausible, but this pass obtained no direct evidence predicting its size or even persistence under DME hydrolysis.
- **Experimental informativeness:** moderate on an existing oxidation/ICP platform. A material balance can decide a formulation-level effect; molecular attribution is harder. Material availability and collection sensitivity are unconfirmed.
- **Practical improvement:** low on the current evidence. Strong existing pellet comparators and established DME conversion challenges raise the benchmark.
- **Original contribution:** unresolved and currently insufficient. The direct intervention lacks a supported causal prediction, and obvious support, reservoir, steam, porosity and inlet-bed proposals are already established.

**Close this candidate without a new program recommendation.** The useful output of this pass is the strong negative comparator: better powder retention need not confer pellet durability, and lower Mo export can trade against HCHO selectivity and strength. Do not rescue this lane by adding a support library or characterization campaign. Reopening would require either direct evidence that the proposed feed intervention decouples productive turnover from export, or another chemically grounded intervention with a demonstrable consequence beyond the established pellet work.
