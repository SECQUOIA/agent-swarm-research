# Protect the carbonate during steam exposure, or restore it afterward?

2026-09-16. A specific intervention for the [cyclic-oxide program](cyclic-oxides.md). No experiments have been performed. This timing comparison addresses steam-process compatibility; it does not replace the broader program's investigation of documented decline under Ar purges or claim a new carbonate reaction. It can begin without first reproducing hundreds of hours of Ar aging.

Revised after [independent timing review](../../reviews/cyclic-timing-intervention-review.md). Its substantive corrections are accepted: the comparison identifies the total effect of treatment order, while assigning damage to the steam interval requires an intermediate material measurement. The two treatment arms are unchanged.

**Proposed intervention:** supply CO2 during a steam purge of Li2CO3-coated La0.8Sr0.2FeO3 (LSF), rather than applying the same CO2 dose after an unprotected steam purge. Test whether contemporaneous protection prevents Li loss or persistent redistribution that later recarbonation cannot reverse. The hydrolysis direction has primary experimental support in related melts. Its rate and its connection to irreversible loss from this coating remain hypotheses.

This is more defensible than simply placing a recarbonation pulse before steam: the ensuing low-CO2 steam can drive hydrolysis again. A prior pulse need not protect the coating throughout the exposure.

## Final decision and scope

This is the first bounded commitment for the cyclic-oxide reserve, not a new material search or a simultaneous obligation to run the parent proposal's four-arm Ar-aging campaign. The [prevention-versus-recovery calculation](../../calculations/cyclic-oxide-prevention-value.md) supplies its next decision: persistent protection must improve cumulative desired output or consequential Li handling enough to repay gas, washout and restoration burdens. Full restoration of Brody's documented Ar yield loss would buy only about 2.02 extra minutes per 16-minute cycle; that does not quantify unmeasured steam damage. A useful first result must connect treatment order to retained function or a consequential process-compatibility bound, rather than merely detect Li downstream.

After this diagnostic, only a supported contrast justifies one specified steam-rich comparison in actual reaction cycles, at a declared gas-handling boundary. Do not assume that the 10% steam/5% CO2 diagnostic establishes a useful process dose. Dry Ar is the demonstrated longevity reference; a low-cost recycled-CO2 or promoter-makeup alternative remains unvalidated. Measure any later recovery operation before assigning it a cost or assuming it restores useful coverage. No new coating or salt screening is included.

The scientific hypothesis has moderate plausibility; confidence that the timing experiment will be informative is moderate and conditional on actual steam switching, product/water recovery and Li balance capability; confidence in a practical improvement is low to uncertain. The unknowns are the five-minute coating response, process-relevant loss rate, detection/recovery limits, useful protective dose, final carbonation endpoint and persistent output response. No experimental platform access is established in this repository. A null short exposure gives a bound under its conditions, not proof of lifetime compatibility; a persistent contrast identifies a treatment-order effect before it identifies a molecular loss pathway.

## What the newly available originals change

The [uploaded-literature audit](../../reviews/current-uploads-cyclic-audit.md) narrows the claim. Fereres 2018 already showed that delayed CO2 stops further loss without restoring the initial bulk-carbonate state (§3.6/Fig. 9). The original contribution cannot be prevention versus recovery in general. It must be the existence, cause and operating consequence of a steam-purge compatibility limit for this particular LSF coating. Chacko's dissertation Chapter 4.1 also demonstrates mixed-carbonate/LSF cycling and attributes regeneration H2 to reduced Fe reacting with water released by hydroxide carbonation. Thus an externally dry CO2 reset is not necessarily a water-free or chemically selective restoration step. Retain H2/H2O/CO2 balances throughout the reset and verify the final carrier state; equal gas doses do not supply that verification.

These results strengthen chemical motivation but reduce confidence in broad originality. The timing pair remains a conditional reserve experiment. No reviewed source establishes consequential five-minute steam damage, a useful protective dose, or a practical improvement for the proposed coating.

## Evidence sufficient to motivate the comparison

- **A real process translation is at stake.** Brody's long-duration LSF experiment used Ar purges; its process model substituted steam purges as heat-exchange steps without measuring their effects on the catalyst. The model specifies steam flow relative to ethane flow, not a demonstrated steam-tolerant material. This motivates a purge-chemistry experiment, but steam cannot explain decline already observed under the original Ar protocol. [Brody et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.2c01293), manuscript pp. 4, 6, 11–13; [open manuscript](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/2001472), previously read.
- **Steam and CO2 change carbonate/hydroxide speciation.** Barckholtz et al. directly measured carbonate and hydroxide Raman responses in a 52/48 mol% Li2CO3/Na2CO3 melt at 923 K, using 10 mol% steam and 0–15 mol% CO2. Response trends support carbonate/hydroxide equilibrium. Absolute concentrations were unavailable because Raman response factors were not calibrated, and individual conditions were held for hours. This supports the direction of the perturbation, not an OH fraction or a five-minute response in LSF. [Primary open article](https://doi.org/10.3389/fenrg.2021.669761), experimental section and Figs. 3–4, read in HTML.
- **Pure-salt melting is not the operative switch.** Gao reports that the supported carbonate layer melts between about 600 and 700 °C, below the bulk Li2CO3 reference, and assigns a molten coating under reaction conditions. Thus do not justify this intervention by claiming that hydroxide first makes a solid carbonate coating liquid at 700 °C. Hydroxide could alter an existing melt's chemistry, wetting, or volatility; those effects require measurement. [Gao et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaz9339), Fig. 1 discussion and referenced DSC supplementary figure; main HTML read, original DSC supplement not independently inspected here.
- **The missing volatility source matters.** Mohn and Wendt's 1995 carbonate-transpiration study is directly relevant to hydrolysis and alkali-hydroxide vapor loss, but its original was not retrieved. Crossref verifies the work; a reproduced abstract is insufficient here to endorse numerical vapor-pressure laws or extrapolate a Li-loss rate. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1524/zpch.1995.192.Part_1.101). Until read, treat increased Li escape as the hypothesis being tested, not a proven property of this coating. The uploaded [Kussainova 2025 original](../../../literature/papers/kussainova2025-chemical-reactions-in-molten-lithium/original.pdf), printed pp. 7289–7290 and Conclusions, describes mixture equilibria, not a supported-coating loss rate. It also reports unphysical collapse of a 900 K high-CO2 mixture simulation; its model is not a validated predictor of the proposed 973 K coating response. The now-readable [Berkowitz 1960 original](../../../literature/papers/berkowitz1960-heterogeneous-reactions-studied-by-mass/original.pdf), printed pp. 533–540, directly establishes LiOH vapor and smaller oligomers from Li2O/steam at 1100–1400 K and roughly 0.1 mm Hg water pressure. This supports a possible vapor species, not its identity or rate above carbonate-coated LSF.

The equilibrium argument itself is simple. For the candidate component reaction

`Li2CO3(melt) + H2O(g) ⇌ 2 LiOH(melt) + CO2(g)`,

`a(LiOH)^2 / a(Li2CO3) = K(T) × p(H2O)/p(CO2)`.

At fixed temperature, lower steam/CO2 ratio favors carbonate relative to hydroxide. This is a thermodynamic direction, not a prediction of composition, loss rate, or kinetic equilibration. Nonideal activities, contact with LSF, and the coating's actual phase composition prevent importing a fuel-cell melt constant. If Li leaves the active particles during exposure, restoration of carbonate afterward cannot recreate the missing Li inventory. If only reversible speciation changes, sufficiently complete final recarbonation should erase the functional difference. That distinction justifies the timing experiment.

## One discriminating sequence

Use sister samples from one reproducible, conditioned LSF@Li2CO3 batch at 700 °C. Start **after ordinary oxidative regeneration**, where the oxide is relatively oxidized and residual hydrocarbon/coke effects are minimized. This initially tests the post-oxidation steam-purge translation; it does not represent the more reducing post-ethane purge.

| Sample | First five-minute exposure | Second five-minute exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Protection during steam | Defined steam + CO2, Ar balance | Dry Ar |
| Delayed restoration | Same steam partial pressure, low measured CO2, Ar balance | Same CO2 partial pressure as the first sample, dry Ar balance |

Keep total flow, pressure, temperature history, cumulative steam dose, cumulative CO2 dose, and total treatment time equal. An initial practical diagnostic is 10 mol% steam and 5 mol% added CO2 at atmospheric pressure, motivated by the primary Raman experiment's accessible composition window. These are proposed test settings, not an optimum, a validated carbonate-stability boundary, or a simulation of a nearly pure steam purge. Measure residual CO2 rather than treating the unprotected case as zero chemical potential. Use the actual five-minute purge duration before extending exposure; an effect that requires hours cannot immediately be assigned to one operational purge.

Sacrifice sister specimens **at the end of the first, steam-containing segment**, as well as after the complete sequence. Otherwise a final difference cannot locate damage in steam rather than the subsequent dry Ar/CO2 segments or reset. Segment-resolved traps alone are insufficient because material can deposit and escape later.

Then give **both samples the same final dry carbonation treatment and standardized oxidation/purge**. Define the carbonation endpoint by an uptake plateau plus a phase-sensitive carbonate measurement on retained material; do not force equal absolute carbonate inventories if Li inventories differ. Verify the endpoint after any final conditioning. Extend the reset only if incomplete restoration prevents interpretation, and count the added time. A persistent difference establishes non-recovery under this reset, not absolute irreversibility.

Verify CO2 washout at the probe inlet/outlet before following identical ethane cycles. Gao already reports reversible CO2 inhibition on this material at 700 °C (Fig. 4D); residual CO2 could mimic damaged function. Follow several cycles rather than one recovered peak. Retain an Ar-only reference through equivalent elapsed time and bare LSF to constrain direct carrier responses; bare LSF cannot exclude coating-mediated ferrite changes. These remain controls on one sequence. [Gao primary article](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaz9339), relevant passage rechecked against the review's recovered full text.

Collect Li separately from the two exposure segments, final reset, and probe. Keep catalyst Li, grit/support-bound Li, wall Li, aerosol capture, and downstream condensable deposits separate. Li in a trap does not prove LiOH vapor; Li on alumina may be immobilized by reaction. LiAlO2 formation from precursor-derived alumina and carbonate is primary prior art, but its kinetics cannot be transferred to dense α-alumina tube/grit. Characterize substantial Li-rich deposits and verify contact-material compatibility. [Lee et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0378-7753(01)00626-7), uploaded original checked, especially pp. 93–95; precursor-derived alumina is the demonstrated case. Follow net CO2/H2O exchange, coating signatures, Fe/phase changes, and any CO/H2 produced during treatment. Equal fed doses do not imply equal uptake or oxygen exchange; these are measured outcomes.

**Set the analytical budget before the exposure count.** Illustratively, 1 g containing 10 wt% Li2CO3 has 18.8 mg Li; a 0.1% Li loss is 18.8 µg, smaller than a 1% error in either bulk inventory. At 100 sccm for five minutes, using 22.414 L/mol as the standard-volume convention, the 5% CO2 dose is 1.12 mmol. Conversion of 0.1% of the sample's carbonate gives 1.35 µmol net CO2—about 61 ppm over the gas dose against a 50,000 ppm feed. These are scale calculations, not predicted losses. State uncertainties from sample heterogeneity, blanks, digestion, wall extraction, trap recovery, calibration, and switching holdup; instrument sensitivity alone is insufficient. Trace capture and phase measurements must complement large-inventory subtraction. Independent reactor runs establish reproducibility.

If one exposure is unresolved, accumulate captures over a fixed number chosen from that uncertainty budget. Repeat within ethane/reoxidation cycles before inferring operating lifetime; consecutive steam/CO2 challenges alone represent a different history. Do not increase severity until damage appears. No isotope campaign is initially required.

## Interpretation and falsifiers

**Support for the proposed loss branch:** the end-of-steam specimens resolve a hydroxide-related change and extra Li transfer from active particles; delayed carbonation restores the carbonatable retained fraction but fails to restore useful-product rate, whereas protection preserves inventory and performance. This supports a steam-created loss not recovered by the stated procedure. Quantitative consistency requires an inventory change with uncertainty and a credible interface/function relation; it does not assume that fractional Li loss and fractional rate loss are proportional.

**Falsifiers or redirections:**

- No resolved speciation response weakens the proposed pathway only if the method's sensitivity on this coating bounds a consequential change; bulk melts equilibrating after hours do not establish a five-minute response.
- Equal retained Li and performance after the reset show no resolved persistent timing benefit. Both arms could still lose comparable Li; the Ar reference and absolute balance test that possibility.
- Different performance with no resolved Li loss points toward interfacial redistribution, ferrite changes, or another mechanism; it does not verify volatilization. Ordinary surface microscopy cannot establish identical Li coverage.
- Protection changes only ferrite phase/redox state, or its benefit disappears after matching the measured carrier state: the specific shell-loss attribution fails.

The main confounds are gas-switching carryover, steam/CO2 effects on LSF and Sr/La carbonates, redistribution of an already molten shell, different transient thermal histories, and incomplete final restoration. Preserve differences in net carbon uptake and oxygen uptake rather than forcing them into a single “matched redox state.”

## Value and feasibility

CO2 cover-gas stabilization, failed restoration by delayed CO2 in a bulk melt, carbonate/hydroxide cycling, and combined carbonate/oxide regeneration are prior art. Merely finding that CO2 helps is not the contribution. The useful result would establish whether the proposed steam purge introduces an irreversible material-loss interval that ordinary subsequent regeneration misses, and whether an equal-dose timing change avoids it. A null result could place a meaningful steam-compatibility bound and remove an unnecessary CO2 requirement.

Confidence is **moderate in the speciation perturbation, low in consequential Li loss from this coating, and moderate in the experiment's ability to distinguish prevention from later recovery**. Coating-state sensitivity and quantitative recovery from walls/traps are the difficult parts. A null dilute-steam result does not validate nearly pure steam. A positive result needs the intended steam composition, gas/solid throughput, temperature trajectory, and subsequently the post-ethane state. An operating policy must fit CO2 into an existing purge or count the added segments/reset, gases, separation, and downtime against cumulative useful product. Better Li retention without meaningful product benefit is materials information, not an established catalytic improvement.

The gas-ratio change makes this translation substantial. The diagnostic 10% steam/5% CO2 has `pH2O/pCO2 = 2`; a 90% steam/5% CO2 purge has a ratio of 18. At unchanged temperature and applicable equilibrium, the latter imposes nine times the hydroxide-squared/carbonate activity quotient, not nine times a known hydroxide concentration or loss rate. Preserving the diagnostic ratio in a steam/CO2-only mixture would require one CO2 per two steam molecules. This is an illustrative gas burden, not a required protective dose: the actual useful boundary must be measured. A dilute-steam success therefore cannot support a claim that a small CO2 addition makes the modeled steam purge viable.

## Current source status

The [uploaded-literature audit](../../reviews/current-uploads-cyclic-audit.md) supersedes the earlier source handoff. Young 2020, Kussainova 2025, Berkowitz 1960, Ochoa-Fernández 2009 and Lee 2001 now have readable originals. Young supports nonideal carbonate/hydroxide equilibrium; Ochoa-Fernández demonstrates faster capture/regeneration but poorer cycling stability under steam in different alkali ceramics, without resolving volatilization from sintering/segregation. These support plausibility and competing explanations, not a rate law for the present coating. Mohn/Wendt 1995 remains unavailable and unread. Brody/Gao supplements were not independently inspected in this audit.

Experimentally unresolved are the five-minute response, where and when Li moves, restoration of the retained carbonatable fraction and carrier state, and whether timing changes cumulative useful production under representative conditions. The reviewed originals do not report the specified equal-dose timing test, but this is a bounded literature finding rather than proof of universal novelty.
