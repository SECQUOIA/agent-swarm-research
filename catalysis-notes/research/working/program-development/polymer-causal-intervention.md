# Ethylene activity as an alternative to fresh-Na makeup

2026-09-16. Focused follow-up to the [revised clean-feed program](polymer-clean-feed.md) and its [independent review](../../reviews/polymer-clean-feed-program-review.md), using the newly recovered Chen dissertation. No experiment has been performed. The only intervention developed here is a change in ethylene partial pressure before supplying fresh Na/alumina. Revised after the [independent pressure-intervention review](../../reviews/polymer-pressure-intervention-review.md); its corrections are accepted. The recommendation remains a bounded operating test in an existing platform, with no program promotion.

## Decision

**Retain one bounded operating test within an established clean-feed campaign; do not promote it to a separate major program.** Test whether an aged Na/W inventory can regain useful polymer-carbon output when ethylene partial pressure is reduced, and whether that response reduces fresh-Na demand at the same accepted output and complete cycle time. A reversible response could distinguish an operating mismatch from the practical need to replace catalyst, without first solving every site-assignment problem.

The thesis strengthens the rationale for this specific perturbation, but also closes broad novelty claims. Ethylene inhibition, changing kinetic regimes with chain-end concentration, and synergistic random/end scission already have explicit theoretical and experimental precedents. A useful new contribution would require a measured connection among **catalyst history, ethylene response and catalyst makeup**, followed by successful prediction and intervention in the heterogeneous polymer system. Merely finding an optimum ethylene pressure would be a focused optimization result.

This intervention should compete against the simplest fixed operating condition. Do not begin with an optimized pressure waveform. If one lower fixed ethylene activity works across charges, that is the preferred solution. Change conditions between charges or during a charge only if the measured response demonstrates that one fixed condition sacrifices a material amount of useful output.

The [closeout review](../../reviews/current-programs-closure-review.md) concludes desktop development of this intervention without promotion. Its finite scope is one lower partial pressure against the reproduced reference, followed only after a useful response by the complete-charge makeup comparison. A null with insufficient precision remains unresolved; it does not establish absence of a useful response. No pressure waveform, new catalyst or dedicated reactor is part of this commitment.

## What the recovered thesis and final article add

All page citations below use the extracted PDF page number, not the dissertation's printed pagination; PDF p.58 is printed p.54. The package retains the stable `chen2025` identifier, and its bibliographic year has now been corrected to 2023, matching the original title page. The uploaded final 2024 ACS Catalysis article is now read and supersedes the thesis where its model differs. Thesis observations below are retained with their source-version limits.

1. **The model describes another catalyst system and a different preparation of the reacting chains.** Chapter 6 analyzes Ir-catalyzed dehydrogenation followed by Pd/Ru isomerizing ethenolysis in Conk's 2022 experiments. It assumes initially long chains with random internal double bonds, then rapid first ethenolysis events that create shorter chains bearing double bonds at both ends. This is not the untreated PE/Na–W experiment. [[chen2025-modeling-cross-scales-in-polymer]] p.58–60
2. **Product-rate histories depend on the distribution of reactive chains, not just a single average chain length.** The thesis shows that a first-order chain-end model applied to its geometric distribution predicts declining product rate, unlike the approximately linear product accumulation being analyzed. This is a model–data discrepancy within the stated framework, not proof that all real polymer systems have saturated chain-end kinetics. [[chen2025-modeling-cross-scales-in-polymer]] p.61–63
3. **Ethylene and reactive-olefin concentrations jointly determine the predicted rate.** The elementary-step model produces a maximum in ethylene response and a plateau at high chain-end concentration. At excess ethylene it assigns inhibition to accumulation of a nonproductive metallacyclobutane population. The proposed physical mechanism is model-derived for that reaction network, not directly measured W speciation. Figures 23–24 motivate testing whether the useful ethylene condition changes as the reacting substrate population changes. The thesis marks the Conk condition before its maximum; the final article describes that condition as near the maximum with approximately saturated chain-end and ethylene kinetics (Figures 5–6, pp.6–8). Neither version establishes a gain from lowering ethylene in Na/W. A favorable response in aged Na/W would require a different regime to be established experimentally. The figures do not give the optimum for Na/W. [[chen2025-modeling-cross-scales-in-polymer]] p.68–70
4. **The assumptions prevent direct quantitative transfer.** The model imposes quasi-equilibrated alpha/beta olefin isomerization, uses common forward and reverse constants for classes of metathesis steps, treats propylene-producing steps as irreversible because product is removed, and ignores spatial variation of ethylene in the melt. It estimates metathesis parameters for the Ru system and explicitly notes dependence on catalyst identity. The conclusion calls the microkinetic treatment ongoing and identifies a conflict between its olefin-isomerization approximation and restricted olefin population. The final article resolves finite, nonequilibrium isomerization explicitly and uses the quasi-equilibrium solution only to check its numerical procedure. It limits physical applicability to slow isomerization relative to metathesis, retains only alpha/beta olefins, neglects deactivation and ethylene gradients, and uses catalyst-specific rate parameters (pp.4–8). The unfinished quasi-equilibrium treatment must not be presented as a limitation of the final model. [[chen2025-modeling-cross-scales-in-polymer]] p.64–67, p.70
5. **The simpler intervention of adding random scission to supply more ends is already developed.** Chapter 5 derives when random scission accelerates end scission, conditional on available end-scission capacity, and analyzes catalyst-mixture optimization. Recreating that general idea would not establish a new research program. [[chen2025-modeling-cross-scales-in-polymer]] p.53–57

I visually checked the original thesis PDF pages 68–70 and the final article p.7 (Figures 5–6). Figure 24's caption exchanges the descriptions of panels a and b relative to their plotted axes: panel a varies ethylene, panel b varies olefin concentration. Use the plotted axes when interpreting it. No numerical optimum, inhibition threshold or predicted saving is transferred from this thesis into the proposed experiment.

## The causal proposal and its strongest alternative

**Hypothesis:** part of the lost output from a retained Na/W inventory results from its operating at an ethylene activity that is no longer suitable for its available reactive-chain population. Lowering ethylene activity can increase accepted polymer-carbon conversion enough to reduce makeup demand, even though it does not regenerate missing sites.

The proposed connection to aging is inferential. Reuse might reduce olefin supply, alter the isomerization/metathesis balance, or change access and working-state populations. The thesis and final article demonstrate that substrate and ethylene responses can be coupled in a model; it does not establish which of these changes occurs in Na/W. Measuring less product at a fixed ethylene pressure does not itself show that the system has moved into an inhibited regime. Losing active sites without changing the relevant substrate or working state need not shift the optimum at all.

The strongest competing prediction is that lowering ethylene simply makes the tandem slower. Ethylene is a metathesis reactant and may be the hydrogen acceptor for initiation. It also changes polymer swelling, gas–liquid transport and intermediate removal. More favorable occupancy at one catalytic function can be outweighed by slower initiation or isomerization, poorer contact, or loss of propylene selectivity. The net response of the actual polymer mixture must decide the intervention.

Current Na/W evidence makes that alternative substantial. Conk's 2024 paper reports higher output in a higher-pressure, higher-flow semibatch condition than at ambient pressure and lower flow. Both pressure and flow changed, so those results are not an intrinsic ethylene reaction order; they nevertheless prevent assuming that low pressure is preferable in this platform. The same paper describes mass-transport sensitivity on scale-up. [[conk2024-polyolefin-waste-to-light-olefins]] p.5–6

## One staged experiment with a direct makeup consequence

### First establish whether a useful operating response exists

First bridge the reactor modes. The published three-charge makeup result used sealed batch operation, while this intervention uses gas flow and product removal. Before calling the pressure effect recovery of that reuse deficit, establish a useful fresh/aged contrast and the direction of Na rescue in the semibatch mode actually used. Keep the original sealed-batch replication as the literature benchmark, but compare pressure and makeup within the same operating mode. No additional reactor development is justified by this proposal. [[conk2024-polyolefin-waste-to-light-olefins]] p.4, Figure 4B–C; [[conk2024-supporting-information-for-polyolefin-waste]] p.16–17

Using the same characterized saturated PE lot and independently replicated retained inventories, compare the reproduced reference with one lower ethylene partial pressure within the validated reactor envelope. Initially substitute inert gas for part of the ethylene at fixed temperature, total pressure, calibrated total inlet molar flow and agitation. Measure outlet composition and molar flow: the cylinder blend is not necessarily the reactor composition under substantial conversion. Match gas delivery and purity, and report any material flow or temperature drift. This comparison does not hold dissolved ethylene, swelling or mass transfer constant.

**Prefer parallel complete-charge comparisons unless the apparatus already resolves useful transients.** The published semibatch method samples GC every 12 minutes. The review estimates roughly ten minutes for one gas-volume replacement at representative vessel volume, pressure and flow, before plumbing and dissolved-product response; this is an illustrative estimate, not a measured residence time. Several replacement times may occupy much of the reaction window. Use existing response information or a simple tracer check to decide the method; do not upgrade the platform solely to seek a rapid reversal. [[conk2024-supporting-information-for-polyolefin-waste]] p.16–17; [apparatus estimate and assumptions](../../reviews/polymer-pressure-intervention-review.md)

If a valid transient window exists, a reference–lower–reference sequence with a time-matched control can test reversibility. The interval must exceed gas and dissolved-product equilibration while remaining short relative to substrate evolution and aging. A gas tracer alone does not measure propylene dissolution; a relevant polymer/solids blank or another validated response measurement must bound stored-product release. Otherwise, use independently prepared, time-matched complete charges and limit the conclusion to the operational effect. Equal elapsed time does not mean equal conversion or reactive-chain population, and matching conversion does not match catalyst history; report these differences.

**Positive gate:** the lower-ethylene condition reproducibly improves sustained product formation or complete-charge accepted output beyond analytical and trajectory uncertainty. Transient reversibility, when resolvable, supports an operating response but does not identify a metallacycle or exclude permanent deactivation. Preliminary gross-product improvement is only a screen: validate polymer-carbon attribution before claiming recovery of useful output or lower makeup demand. A different fresh/aged response strengthens relevance to reuse without isolating aging from the evolving feed state.

**Negative gate:** lower ethylene reduces output, gives no useful advantage, or improves only an initial stored-product spike. Close this intervention over the tested domain. A flat response could still motivate lower gas throughput in a separate economic evaluation, but it does not validate the proposed rate-recovery mechanism or catalyst-makeup benefit.

### Then ask whether it actually saves fresh catalyst

Only after the positive screen, validate the beneficial condition over complete polymer charges in the same reactor mode. Compare against the reference at no makeup, the smaller fresh-Na dose and the published full dose already bracketed by the clean-feed campaign. Keep the original second-charge addition timing and third-charge no-addition comparison. Run independent sequences; a specimen used for several conditioning experiments is not an unperturbed lifetime reference.

The target is **less fresh Na/alumina required for the same accepted polymer-carbon output within the same complete cycle time**, while keeping W-material demand and product specification comparable. Predetermine the accepted output, product specification, complete cycle time and smallest material saving worth detecting. The experiment can bracket the saving without claiming a precise optimum. Measure the full product distribution and residual polymer, including heavy olefins that might escape before complete conversion. A larger initial propylene rate does not suffice if later conversion stalls or accepted product falls.

Before a makeup claim, validate polymer-carbon attribution in the decisive reference and beneficial condition, for example through representative isotope experiments and product-resolved balances. Gross propylene carbon, polymer mass loss and total-carbon closure cannot independently assign the carbon because ethylene contributes to propylene. The original labeled-polymer result applies to its tested fresh-batch condition, not automatically to aged semibatch operation. A polymer-free catalyst blank can bound ethylene-only chemistry but does not reproduce a polymer-conditioned catalyst. Account for condensates, residue and retained carbon. [[conk2024-polyolefin-waste-to-light-olefins]] p.4, Figure 4A

A saving over three charges is a bounded inventory saving, not a demonstrated durable makeup rate. If it is reproducible, extend only the best pair of conditions through additional feed sufficient to test whether Na addition was merely deferred or paid for by earlier W replacement, solids loss or downtime. Retain the finite three-charge conclusion until that test supports more.

If a fixed lower condition works, retain it. If a distinct benefit occurs only at one catalyst history, test one predetermined change between charges using the response already measured, and predict its complete-charge output before running it. A more complicated within-charge schedule is justified only by evidence that the fixed and between-charge choices cannot obtain the useful effect. Compare any schedule against the best fixed condition tested, not only the original literature recipe.

Using inert dilution at fixed total pressure is a diagnostic choice. A practical intervention must charge inert supply, gas separation and recycle against the benefit. A later test that actually reduces total pressure would need its own transport and product-removal comparison before claiming compression savings. Lower ethylene partial pressure also does not imply lower stoichiometric ethylene consumption per amount of polymer converted.

## What would constitute mechanistic depth

If the intervention succeeds, make one prospectively testable link to the clean-feed response measurements. For example, predict whether a matched feed with a higher verified fraction of unsaturated chains reduces or changes the rate benefit of lower ethylene, using the measured fresh/aged responses. The direction must be established from the actual Na/W data, not copied numerically from the Ru model. Failure should be retained rather than accommodated with an independently fitted aging parameter for each condition.

A successful prediction would connect polymer state, catalyst history and useful operating conditions. Further component-specific work could then ask whether the shift follows isomerization capacity, initiation supply, W working-state activation or polymer access. It would be premature to demand molecular site assignment in the first pressure experiment. Conversely, attributing every pressure response to nonproductive metathesis would undercut the program's mechanistic value.

If the intervention instead works equally for fresh and aged catalyst with no chain-population dependence, it may still be a useful fixed-condition improvement. Report it at that scope. It would not establish the proposed connection between aging and kinetic regime, and would give less reason to expand this particular mechanistic program.

## Fair comparison and prior-art closure

Wang et al. already discuss ethylene inhibition and use continuous ethylene feed/product removal to operate their tandem system. They also show why chain populations, catalyst lifetime and isomerization rate matter. Thus, neither low-pressure operation nor the general idea that excess ethylene can inhibit the cascade is new. [[wang2022-chemical-recycling-of-polyethylene-by]] p.2–4

Conk's Na/W work already varies pressure and flow, while Chen's thesis and the now-read final 2024 article predict concentration-dependent regimes and explicitly propose pressure, dehydrogenation and molecular-weight perturbations. Guironnet and Peters (2020), p.5, also describe numerical treatment of time-varying ethylene concentration. Thus, a pressure schedule or coupled chain-population model alone would not establish originality. The potentially original question is specifically whether a **measured change in ethylene response during retention can substitute for a material amount of catalyst makeup** in the heterogeneous polymer system, and whether the substitution can be predicted. The inaccessible Conk dissertation remains an overlap uncertainty; this bounded search does not establish worldwide novelty.

Do not promote a small apparent improvement solely because no exact pressure schedule was found in the search. The intervention deserves expansion if it gives a practically useful material or cycle-time benefit and an explanation that predicts a new condition. Without that result, the recovered thesis primarily strengthens prior-art closure rather than providing a new flagship program.

## Confidence and present recommendation

| Judgment | Current assessment |
|---|---|
| Aging creates a recoverable ethylene mismatch | **Low confidence.** Plausible from coupled substrate/ethylene kinetics, unsupported directly in Na/W; initiation, selectivity and transport may favor higher ethylene instead. |
| A bounded operating experiment can give useful knowledge | **Moderate confidence** if a working semibatch platform and quantitative gas analysis are available. Gas holdup and evolving polymer state are the main identification risks; inability to resolve transients still permits a complete-charge operational comparison. |
| Meaningful makeup or process benefit | **Low confidence.** A reversible rate change may be too small, temporary or offset by product losses and gas handling. No magnitude is presently established. |
| Originality and program depth | **Conditional.** Generic ethylene inhibition and pressure optimization are prior art. A predictive link to retained-inventory makeup demand could be meaningful, but first needs the complete-charge test. |

This is the strongest single causal intervention I can justify from the recovered thesis without importing its rate law into a different catalyst system. It warrants a modest perturbation attached to the existing campaign. It does not yet justify displacing other research programs or building a dedicated apparatus.
