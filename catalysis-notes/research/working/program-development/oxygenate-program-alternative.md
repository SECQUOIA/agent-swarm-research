# Predicting tungsten retention and selective sugar cleavage

2026-09-16 proposal, updated after the new literature uploads. No experiments have been performed. This is the existing tungsten candidate, separate from the unselected Ti-zeolite solvent screen. The [current full-text audit](../../reviews/current-uploads-tungsten-audit.md) supersedes earlier source-access limits and corrects the durability and mechanistic comparisons.

## Recommendation and central advance

**Retain at exploratory hold, behind physical water management and cyclic oxides.** Reading the newly supplied originals does not identify a sufficiently supported core intervention. It does remove the need to wait for the key Li, El Mohammad, Qiao, Liu and Zhang papers. The advance sought is a causal rule linking productive sugar coordination, W–support interaction and W export, followed by a prospective catalyst or operating intervention. Useful anchoring is already reported: a Ti–O–W-linked Ni/W/Ti catalyst combines glycol production with reduced postreaction W concentration. Its cumulative export may still be consequential, but the published liquid-volume inconsistency prevents assigning a unique loss fraction. The new contribution must predict when and why changing coordination improves the cleavage–retention tradeoff, beyond that empirical achievement. No process improvement is yet established.

The program is about **catalytic ligand exchange versus metal extraction**, not simply finding another tungsten support. Sugars must bind W through several oxygen groups to react. Those same ligands may remove W from its support, stabilize a mobile active complex, or trap it in an unproductive complex. An initially solid catalyst may therefore be an active surface, a reservoir feeding solution chemistry, or both. Recovering an apparently unchanged solid after cooling does not resolve these possibilities.

**Process retention and permanent atomic attachment are different claims.** Low net W export can be valuable even if individual W atoms detach and return. Detecting a retained W population does not establish that it performs most turnovers. The desired advance is a predictive relationship between W anchoring, substrate coordination, metal mobility and C–C cleavage. It should guide a concrete choice: retain a productive W environment on a solid, or deliberately manage a soluble W catalyst when immobilization destroys the necessary chemistry. Either finding can be consequential, but a negative result for one support cannot establish that all immobilization is impossible.

The potential contribution concerns catalyst architecture for selective bond cleavage and recovery. That broader ambition does not establish a stronger current proposal: a consequential limitation and a core intervention remain unselected. Establish the remaining limitation of the best anchored and soluble-W comparators early; an easier improvement over a strongly leaching oxide is insufficient. An engineering comparison must establish whether reduced W circulation is worth any loss of productivity.

The [independent carbon-comparator audit](../../reviews/tungsten-carbon-comparator-review.md) sharpens that requirement. Two Ni–W/carbon studies retain useful activity over repeated batches, but use 2.5 g/L cellulose, wash/dry between cycles and replace handling losses with fresh catalyst. Their metal assays do not close a hot W balance. This leaves meaningful concentration, throughput and makeup questions, without proving that W extraction is their bottleneck or that they fail at higher concentration. After analytical calibration, establish retained function and W export in a product-rich or fed domain before choosing a new anchoring intervention. Feed-solution concentration, instantaneous reactive-sugar concentration and accumulated product concentration must remain separate variables.

## Closeout scope and first decision

The [independent closeout review](../../reviews/current-programs-closure-review.md) concludes the present desktop development at **exploratory hold**, with a finite analytical and comparator study available on an existing platform. Campaigns 2 onward are contingent paths, not an approved materials or spectroscopy campaign. No replacement intervention is selected to rescue the program.

The first commitment is one soluble-reference recovery calibration and one established retained-W/support-blank comparison, accompanied by the strongest reproducible tandem comparator. Before the comparison, state the observation horizon, useful-output target, allowable carbon/W recovery uncertainty and smallest consequential export or output difference. Use independently prepared repeats to establish whether those differences can be resolved. Where a missing original prevents faithful reproduction or assessment of a claimed linkage, retain that specific comparison as source-dependent instead of substituting an assumed protocol.

Close this first stage with either a measured, consequential reference limitation and a chemically specified perturbation worth developing, a finding that the tested reference already meets the selected output/retention requirement, or an explicit analytical/source limit. In the latter two cases, stop expansion. Hot-export accounting or unresolved mobile-versus-surface attribution alone is insufficient to restart a materials search. The glucose–mannose probe remains optional and must satisfy its own analytical and process-relevance gates.

## Important reaction and connection to the professors

Conversion of carbohydrate streams to ethylene glycol requires selective formation of C2 carbonyl intermediates followed by hydrogenation. Undesired glucose hydrogenation, isomerization, dehydration, fragment condensation and polyol hydrogenolysis divert carbon. The chemistry can process glucose derived from starch or cellulose hydrolysates; direct untreated lignocellulose conversion is a later, substantially harder problem.

Gounder's relevant expertise is quantifying Lewis-acid site environments and sugar reaction pathways rather than ranking materials by nominal acidity. Iglesia's relevant expertise is supported oxide ensembles, adsorption thermodynamics, elementary reaction kinetics and working-site attribution. The question connects these strengths without requiring that either group already operates this particular process.

**Scientific hypothesis:** within a defined support and aqueous-composition range, changing a W–support interaction alters the ligand exchange that controls both productive sugar cleavage and W export in a predictable way. Anchoring can preserve activity, as prior catalysts already show. It can also block productive coordination or redirect W into mobile or inactive complexes. The question is which interaction controls the tradeoff and whether manipulating it changes useful performance. Predictive net retention does not require proving that W never detaches.

**Practical hypothesis:** preserving the productive ensemble while retaining W can lower catalyst makeup, W loading in separation streams, or recovery demands at matched useful glycol output. There is presently no defensible estimate of the size of that saving, and no claim that tungsten purchase cost dominates the process.

Three well-developed pictures of W chemistry now exist. They must be compared under their actual conditions; they are not automatically contradictory.

**Structural evidence has important condition limits.** In the PNAS study, relevant solid spectroscopic samples were prepared by impregnation and vacuum drying at 60 °C, treated at 120 °C, and observed at 25 °C; these are not direct observations of the aqueous active complex under the 205 °C tandem benchmark. The Inorganic Chemistry manuscript illustrates ambient NMR experiments with 2 mol/L metal and 1 mol/L sugar. These are valuable fingerprints, but their dominant complexes need not persist in dilute hot effluent. ACS Catalysis 2024 instead uses 5 mM sugar and 1.3 mM W in its standard 150 °C experiments; kinetic runs use 40 bar N2/H2 and XANES uses He. These qualifications preserve the mechanistic evidence while limiting its transfer. No universal W oxidation-state or nuclearity assignment follows from this combined literature.

The opportunity is not to declare one of these pictures universally correct. It is to learn which active coordination domains persist when substrate, pH, hydrogenation function and W retention are controlled. A result in which solid-associated and mobile W follow different mechanisms would be scientifically coherent.

## What is already achieved and must be beaten fairly

The closest practical anchoring comparator is **Li et al., Enhanced Ni/W/Ti Catalyst Stability from Ti–O–W Linkage for Effective Conversion of Cellulose into Ethylene Glycol**, [10.1021/acssuschemeng.0c00836](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.0c00836). The publisher abstract attributes reduced W loss to Ti–O–W links after calcining a Ni–W/MIL-125(Ti) precursor. The subsequently recovered [SI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.0c00836.s001), Tables S2–S3, strengthens this comparator: it reports seven runs with EG yield changing from 68.7% to 66.9%, and catalyst-derived leachate plus fresh Ni/M gives 29.5% EG yield. Soluble-AMT controls are also present. Testing a preleachate is therefore already prior art. The main article now supplies the essential Ni/M-alone reference: 27.1% EG, versus 29.5% for Ni/M plus preleachate. The 2.4 percentage-point increment has no paired uncertainty established here, and does not demonstrate that the liquid accounts for substantial catalysis.

The leachate was prepared in water/H2 without cellulose; the comparison does not establish hot transfer, working-sugar speciation or significant leachate activity. The ratio 29.5/68.7 is not a homogeneous turnover fraction. The newly read main original specifies filtration, water washing and drying at 363 K in air between cycles (PDF p. 2). Figure 4b reports postreaction W concentrations of **28, 22, 19, 21, 18, 20 and 19 ppm**, and the text reports 4–6 ppm Ni per cycle (p. 6). These concentrations are not a complete W balance. Conditional on the methods' 40 mL liquid charge and a 30 mg nominal W inventory, their sum corresponds to **5.88 mg W, about 20% of the initial nominal W**, before wash or handling losses. The Figure 3 caption instead states 100 mL; that volume would imply about 49%. Neither value is a measured cumulative loss, and the volume conflict must be resolved for reproduction. Calling the W loss negligible solely from ppm values is unwarranted. See the [accounting check](../../calculations/tungsten-process-burden.md).

The Ti–O–W interpretation rests on XPS/Raman, dispersed-W characterization and comparisons among differently prepared supports. Carbon content, texture, W dispersion and Ni state vary together; this is not an isolated anchor-strength intervention. The article's claim that HxWO3 remains permanently attached throughout turnover exceeds what recovered-solid spectra and preleachate controls uniquely establish. Audit and reproduce the measured performance without assuming that molecular attribution. Deleting Ni or H2 can change the W state, so a nominal W/Ti analogue is a new characterized preparation, not automatically a faithful control.

A second direct comparator, **Durable W20O58 Species Catalyzed C–C Bond Cleavage in Glucose**, [10.1021/acs.jpcc.4c02721](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpcc.4c02721), now has its main original available. Correct the earlier abstract-based stable-yield claim: its reported **combined EG+PG yield declines from 70.4 to 61.4 C%** across five cycles (PDF p. 3/Figure 1). Initial EG alone is 55.6 C%. The reported cumulative W loss is about 7%, compared with 27% for WO3, under 518 K/4 MPa H2 with 1.4 mmol glucose, 100 mL water, 0.25 g oxide and 0.25 g 2% Ru/AC per 10-minute run. This is better retention in a dilute, catalyst-intensive comparison, not invariant output or demonstrated high-throughput durability. XRD phase retention coexists with a changed surface XPS population. The cooled fifth-cycle filtrate gives 7.8% EG with fresh Ru/AC; this does not bound hot mobile turnover (pp. 3–5).

**Unraveling the active states of WO3-based catalysts…**, [10.1016/S1872-2067(22)64187-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1872-2067(22)64187-1), now read, supplies more than the earlier bronze/reoxidation warning. It reports immediate wet-transfer XRD, crystallite-size/activity contrasts, 0.05% postreaction W loss, weak recovered-filtrate function, and a water-only conductivity comparison to support heterogeneous bronze activity (PDF pp. 2–6). These are substantive controls. They do not uniquely exclude short-lived or sugar-stabilized mobile W: the conductivity test lacks sugar, while size also affects dissolution kinetics and filtration follows quenching. Preserve the temperature/atmosphere and composition dependence; neither this paper nor Zhang's different 245 °C experiment proves universal bronze insolubility or solubility.

The process also has substantial throughput prior art. Ooms et al. used Ni–W2C/activated carbon and gradual glucose addition to obtain up to 66% EG carbon yield; their separate maximum volumetric productivity was about 293 g EG L−1 h−1. Those are different operating optima. Their concentrated feed and low instantaneous glucose inventory must also be distinguished. Cold-filtrate W measurements were small, but do not bound the hot mobile-W population. [Ooms et al., Green Chem. 2014, 10.1039/C3GC41431K](https://doi.org/10.1039/C3GC41431K), original PDF pp. 1–2, 10–11 read.

The now-read 2025 Ni-MFI study directly supports size-selective hydrogenation that admits glycolaldehyde while suppressing glucose conversion to sugar alcohols. Its 63% EG result uses 12 mM glucose, 3 mg sodium metatungstate and 40 mg Ni@MFI per gram of reaction mixture, 170 °C, 30 barg H2 and one hour; the glucose-to-W molar ratio is about one. It is a strong mechanistic and tandem-selectivity comparator, but its dilute feed and large catalyst inventory must accompany the yield. Molecular sieving of sugar versus fragment is not proposed as new here. [10.1021/acssuschemeng.5c00915](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.5c00915), original PDF pp. 3, 6–8.

Zhao et al.'s [kinetic model, 10.1002/aic.15589](https://doi.org/10.1002/aic.15589), now read, already predicts the value of slow glucose addition with ammonium metatungstate/Ru. It fits a simplified CSTR model to semicontinuous experiments, adopts effective hydrogenation kinetics in a transport-influenced regime, and pools experimental C3/C4 glycols with EG in its main model comparison (PDF pp. 5–8). Its approximately 74% plateau cannot be quoted as an independently demonstrated EG-only yield. Use its reaction-network and feeding logic as prior art, without treating its fitted constants as transferable intrinsic rates.

Industrial disclosures already combine dissolved W with heterogeneous hydrogenation and recover W from product streams. In particular, [US12297166B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12297166B2/en) describes separating lower glycols and using ion-exclusion chromatography to separate W-containing liquid from higher-boiling polyols. [WO2022066160A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2022066160A1/en) addresses continuous operation with low retro-aldol catalyst concentrations. They establish relevant process architectures and comparison requirements. Root's subsequent description audit found no labelled working examples or numerical experimental tables in either patent. The low-W concentration and long-duration conversion ranges, and the recovery fractions, are disclosed operating/claim ranges rather than separately documented run results. They must not be presented as demonstrated practical benchmarks or plant economics. [WO2022064039A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2022064039A1/en), identified in the independent review, additionally describes W recovery/regeneration from ash. Recovery followed by renewed activity is therefore not itself novel. The low-W patent also discloses cycling or ceasing W feed, with optional pH increase, to dissolve deposits while maintaining conversion. A feed-off/reservoir-recovery intervention alone is established disclosure; here it is a diagnostic perturbation whose additional value must be a causal, prospective cleavage/export rule.

Earlier [tungsten solid-Lewis-acid patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130053601A1/en) also preclude claiming supported W biomass conversion itself as the advance. Kourieh et al.'s [WO3/ZrO2 cellobiose study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.catcom.2011.12.030) reports cellobiose hydrolysis, a 50 °C water-leaching check and a 97 °C filtered-liquid/recovered-solid check (original pp. 3, 6–7). These are not tests at 205–245 °C tandem conditions. Hydrolysis is a separate function from selective C–C cleavage; success in one does not establish the other.

**Fair practical objective:** a better yield–productivity–W-circulation relation over sustained operation, not a higher yield than bare WO3 or a low W concentration after cooling. Complete product purification remains necessary even if W is perfectly retained.

## Core experimental strategy: a chemically informative contrast

Begin with a soluble tungstate reference, crystalline WO3, and **one** reproducible supported-W material with characterized W–support contacts. W/ZrO2 is a practical starting contrast because both sugar chemistry and tunable W–support interaction are established; it is not a novelty claim or a commitment to a loading library. Include the W-free support. Select the supported material from established preparations after checking its wet stability and whether both isolated/oligomeric W and the support's own sugar chemistry can be distinguished. Do not assign precise monomer or pair counts from loading alone.

The decisive contrast is the **same supported material in a reactor that measures and perturbs mobile W**, compared with solutions containing the measured mobile species. The goal is to separate solid-associated cleavage, release into solution, return to the solid, and unproductive sequestration. This can be informative on a tractable model support, but W/ZrO2 is not assumed to be the best practical catalyst. Develop the Ti-linked baseline in parallel with this causal comparison so that the remaining performance or recovery limitation is established early.

### Campaign 1: quantify the mass and function of retained and mobile W

Build the campaign in stages; do not commission the entire multizone apparatus at once. First reproduce one fully read soluble-W glucose-cleavage experiment and validate carbonyl recovery through the actual sampling train. Choose temperature and concentrations from that experiment; a 170 °C tandem result does not establish a suitable cleavage-only window. Glucose is the primary test. Erythrose can help validate analysis, but success with that more reactive intermediate is not a substitute for glucose cleavage.

Rapid quench, calibrated HPLC and quantitative NMR should quantify sugars, C2/C3/C4 carbonyls and major degradation products. Validate storage/derivatization recovery and total carbon; glycolaldehyde hydration/dimerization must not become apparent missing carbon. If no direct analytical window provides resolved cleavage and acceptable recovery, explicitly redesign around a validated downstream hydrogenation trap or the tandem domain. A trap introduces fragment residence time and can hydrogenate unreacted glucose; quantify both effects before interpreting its yield as cleavage.

Then compare the selected supported W, its W-free support and soluble W in a pressure-rated solid-retaining flow reactor. Establish blank chemistry, measurable steady behavior and apparatus recovery. Calibrate hydraulic residence-time distribution (RTD), species-specific adsorption/dispersion and carbonyl/W recovery through tubing and filters. Use an immediate-quench bypass and the actual heated-transfer train with soluble references. Detector and sampling delays must be known before a lag is assigned to metal release or chemistry.

Measure W in all effluent fractions and in the bed, reactor walls, filters and downstream deposits. Collect a total-W aliquot before cooling-induced material can be discarded; separately preserve aliquots for particle/speciation analysis. Report dissolved plus mobile-colloidal W operationally until measurements distinguish them. A filter cut-off neither establishes molecular homogeneity nor shows that a retained colloid is a surface active site.

The initial discriminatory sequence is deliberately small:

1. Split fresh effluent between immediate quench and an isothermal solid-free section. Introduce a distinguishable glucose pulse before the heated section, preferably isotope-labelled when transported carbonyls would otherwise obscure new cleavage. Measure matrix blanks and a soluble-W control at matching concentrations. The added substrate can change W speciation and inhibition, so the result tests **exported catalytic capacity**, not its upstream turnover fraction.
2. Repeat over a few calibrated hot transfer delays. A positive result shows that exported material can catalyze new cleavage. Decay with delay reveals loss of that capacity in transit. A null result bounds only activity surviving the tested delay, filter and composition; it does not prove that upstream catalysis is heterogeneous.
3. Perform one feed-to-electrolyte-to-feed change, controlling solution composition and temperature. Measure W and carbon output through return to steady operation; matching room-temperature pH alone does not establish equal hot proton activity. Freeze simple competing inventory/reaction predictions using the measured RTD before interpreting the transient. Early recovery is not structural regeneration.

Only if this sequence discriminates useful alternatives add washout-rate or feed-dosed mobile-W perturbations at matched, modelled reaction contact. Persistent activity after replacing the original liquid shows a retained inventory is needed; it can still reflect continuous release of a short-lived mobile catalyst. Recapture experiments should separately compare hot capture with cooling-induced precipitation, quantify capture yield and subsequent productivity, and avoid calling recovery microscopic return to the original anchor.

**An explicit unresolved model:** let a retained inventory release a reactive mobile W species at molar rate J into liquid volume V. With flow Q and first-order loss constant k_d, its steady concentration is J/[V(k_d + Q/V)]. It can sustain cleavage after all original liquid is washed out, yet lose activity before reaching the downstream test. This is an identifiability counterexample, not a proposed fitted mechanism. Rapid local release/return can also yield little net W export while mobile species perform turnover. RTD-corrected flow/transfer experiments constrain these models but may not uniquely separate them from interfacial catalysis.

Liquid-phase kinetic standards should cover observed W species and concentrations. Comparing reactor rate with the largest measured homogeneous rate gives an **empirical bound over tested solution states**, not an absolute bound for every transient mobile species. Report unresolved and mixed contributions explicitly. A useful prediction of cleavage and net export can still emerge without permanent-attachment attribution.

The cleavage-only comparison isolates one chemical domain. Reintroduce H2 and the intended hydrogenation catalyst as a separately characterized domain; do not infer the published Ni/W/Ti baseline by removing either. Initial investment is justified by calibrated function/export bounds and an identified remaining comparator limitation, even if atomic assignments await later spectroscopy.

### Campaign 2: identify the anchoring–coordination requirement

On the contrasts that Campaign 1 resolves, obtain wet-state W XANES/EXAFS and Raman/UV-visible data with solution references. Operando XAS is an important collaboration requirement for confident coordination assignments; before that access exists, the mass/function experiments remain informative but cannot name a unique W geometry. Ex situ XPS or the color of a solution cannot determine the active oxidation state alone. Establish detection limits at the actual working W concentration: a W–W EXAFS signal does not count active pairs, its absence cannot exclude a small active paired population, and support-O and sugar-O coordination can be hard to distinguish. Do not order monomer and pair activities across uncontrolled differences in pH, sugar/W ratio, H2, temperature or support exposure.

Measure the response to **glucose, mannose and erythrose** separately at matched, measured feed conditions. These substrates test the parent sugar and the C4 intermediate while making use of independently established solution-complex fingerprints. Normalize initially to total retained W and total W throughput; claim a site turnover frequency only when the relevant population is independently constrained. A faster erythrose reaction is already known and is not itself the discovery.

Use one product-ligand perturbation, chosen from the actual effluent, to distinguish productive chelation from W sequestration. Sorbitol is a useful candidate only if it is a significant byproduct and is verified to be effectively nonreactive in the cleavage-only conditions. It may bind W and alter extraction as well as competitively inhibit; both effects must be quantified. Arbitrary multipoison screening is unnecessary.

The first material intervention is chosen **after** this contrast, based on a specific structural prediction. Examples of different justified branches are:

- If an operationally retained W(VI)-rich system sustains cleavage, modify one chemically specified anchoring condition and predict its effect on cleavage and net export before synthesis. Claim a bound-site cause only if short-lived mobile and minority paired alternatives are independently constrained; otherwise state the narrower architecture rule.
- If only exposed W–O–W domains retain productive function, change domain accessibility at approximately fixed W coordination and support identity, rather than maximizing total isolated W. Independently verify that the new material is not simply a faster source of soluble W.
- If productive sugar coordination consistently extracts W from the tested anchors, determine whether exported W is recoverable in an active form. The relevant engineering choice becomes controlled soluble-W circulation and recovery, compared with the architectures already disclosed in patents using measured performance, rather than stronger anchoring at any cost.

These are mutually informative outcomes of one program, not three separate materials searches. A useful prospective claim would be: within a specified support/feed range, changing a defined W–support interaction shifts net W export while preserving the measured cleavage pathway and productivity. Demonstrate this on an unmeasured feed or anchoring condition. Neither a fitted activity–leaching correlation nor a unique atomistic assignment is a substitute for that prediction.

## Chemically specific alternatives and predicted observations

| Alternative | Key observations | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Retained mononuclear coordination is sufficient** | Operational retention and cleavage accompany a W(VI)-rich supported population. A unique assignment additionally requires bounds on minority pairs and transient mobile species beyond washout or negative transfer alone. | Develop the measured anchoring/export rule; describe mononuclear bound-site sufficiency as unresolved unless those alternatives are constrained. |
| **Retained cooperative/reduced domains contribute** | Independently resolved W–W or reduced populations and solid-dependent cleavage respond to a causal perturbation under matched conditions, with competing mobile contributions assessed. | Preserve that ensemble where supported. A correlation with loading/reducibility alone does not establish necessity, and does not invalidate solution W(VI) chemistry elsewhere. |
| **The solid supplies exported catalytic function** | Exported material cleaves newly added distinguishable substrate after calibrated transit; liquid standards and mass balance constrain its capacity. | Quantify mobile capacity and net export. Upstream turnover fractions still require a kinetic model; recapture can change rather than restore the original species. |
| **Retained inventory required; active compartment unresolved** | Cleavage persists with low net export, but local release/return, short-lived mobile complexes and retained/interfacial turnover predict the observations equally well. | Report operationally useful retention without permanent-attachment or unique-site claims. Test prospective cleavage/export predictions if they change architecture choice. |
| **Both retained and mobile pathways operate** | Calibrated perturbations require more than one contribution within experimental resolution. | Report bounded fractions and their dependence on conditions; do not force a single active phase. |
| **Substrate/product chelation creates an inactive W sink** | W remains present but a ligand-dependent species accumulates; cleavage declines and recovers through a controlled ligand/speciation change. | Test whether changing recycle composition restores useful output without more W. Recovery must be separated from simple release of a fresh active species. |
| **Apparent W effects belong to another catalytic function** | Support hydrolysis/isomerization or fragment hydrogenation explains final-product changes while independently measured cleavage does not track the proposed W state. | Revise the network and reject that assignment. Final EG yield is insufficient to establish cleavage-site chemistry. |

W mass closure is necessary in every branch. W release correlated with rate is not proof that released W causes the rate; reactive sugar can cause both. Positive transfer establishes exported capacity, whereas null transfer and low net export do not exclude rapid local mobile turnover. This asymmetry governs all mechanism claims.

## Program progression toward useful catalysis

**First deliverable:** a reaction-temperature map of W inventory and catalytic function for the reference contrasts, with calibrated bounds on retained inventory, exported catalytic capacity and net export, plus an explicit unresolved/mixed category. This provides a reproducible experimental basis for selecting a catalyst architecture; it is not just a post-run leaching number.

**Second deliverable:** one prospective catalyst or operating change predicted from that map, tested against an unchanged reference on held-out sugar concentration or product-ligand content. Success requires retained cleavage productivity and lower W circulation, or a defensible demonstration that a mobile architecture is superior after recovery costs are counted. The [glucose–mannose candidate](tungsten-causal-intervention.md) has been reviewed against the recovered kinetic SI and retained only as a conditional supporting probe; it is not the selected core intervention.

**Third deliverable:** integrate a common, independently characterized hydrogenation function and compare full glucose-to-EG trajectories. Use a common hydrogenation function for a controlled architecture comparison only after both preparations are characterized; retain the published Ti-linked tandem baseline under its original conditions as a separate best-practice comparator; consider the Ni-MFI comparator only when suppression of glucose hydrogenation is the established bottleneck. Intimate W–metal mixing is not assumed beneficial because it can change W reduction, metal leaching and unwanted sugar hydrogenation simultaneously.

**Fourth deliverable:** establish scope on cellobiose or a characterized short-oligosaccharide feed and then a real hydrolysate. Measure hydrolysis separately and report yields per input carbon. Failure on cellobiose could mean glycosidic accessibility rather than failure of the W cleavage mechanism. Do not infer direct cellulose conversion from glucose results, nor use a dilute purified model to claim waste-feed tolerance.

Scale concentration only after the balances are credible. Compare gradual addition or continuous operation with the strongest relevant reference so that lowering instantaneous glucose inventory is not mistaken for superior chemistry. Hydrolysate ions and organic acids enter only when their actual measured concentrations justify testing them; they can change W speciation, not merely poison a fixed site.

## Practical accounting and success criteria

The independently checked [process-burden note](../../calculations/tungsten-process-burden.md) defines the balance boundary, gross export, actual return fraction, inventory and fresh makeup, and records the patent-description audit. It supplies comparison requirements without assuming recovery economics.

Report EG carbon yield, other products separately, volumetric productivity, catalyst-mass productivity, W inventory and **W mass crossing into product/recovery streams per mass of EG**. Distinguish gross W circulation, fresh W makeup, and unrecovered loss: a recirculated kilogram is not a consumed kilogram, but can still impose recovery duty. Also report water throughput, H2 use, regeneration inputs, and the time spent outside productive operation. Check whether W deposition on the hydrogenation catalyst changes its useful life; reducing that burden can matter independently of W purchase cost, but requires measurement.

For a supported catalyst, report cumulative product per initial W and remaining W, with no unsupported assumption that all W is active. For a soluble system, report product per total W inventory and per fresh W addition; a high nominal turnover based only on fresh makeup can hide a large recirculating inventory.

A practical result is convincing when it improves at least one consequential recovery/resource burden without an offsetting loss of product yield or throughput. Establish the required improvement from a measured separation train or a transparent sensitivity calculation. In particular, test whether lower W changes the required ion-exchange/ion-exclusion duty, purge, or finishing step. If the same costly separation remains necessary for other components, reduced W may provide little process benefit.

No numerical economic hurdle is invented here. Compare the full yield–productivity tradeoff, not a single best yield; the 2014 high-yield and high-productivity points must remain separate. The asserted ranges in WO2022066160A1 and US12297166B2 lack a separately documented experimental series in the inspected descriptions. In contrast, WO2022064039A1 contains working model-ash recovery and product-rich catalytic-use examples; its short trial and incomplete recovery balance limit the comparison. Obtain measured comparator performance and recycle accounting before claiming a process advantage; do not treat the disclosed ranges as achieved benchmarks.

## Confidence, risks and stopping decisions

| Dimension | Current judgment |
|---|---|
| **Scientific hypothesis** | **Strong rationale for coordination/retention coupling; moderate confidence in a tractable causal rule.** Productive anchoring already has direct precedent. Its chemical cause, transferability and limits remain the proposed advance, rather than the first demonstration that an anchored catalyst works. |
| **Feasibility and informativeness** | **Moderate, conditional on pressure-flow access and analytical recovery.** Calibrated net-export and mobile-capacity bounds are more attainable than unique active-compartment attribution. RTD, transfer decay and spectral detection limits can leave a real unresolved category. |
| **Meaningful practical improvement** | **Uncertain but credible.** Soluble-W recovery is an explicit process operation, and carbon selectivity matters. The Ti-linked catalyst, durable W20O58 and low-W/recovery processes are serious comparators; a new solid may be slower, less selective or unnecessary. Establish a remaining burden early. |
| **Potential scientific value** | **Substantial if the coordination/retention relation is causal and predictive.** It would explain and predict how changing W–support/substrate interactions alters cleavage and W export, or identify a reversible ligand-induced inactive sink that controls useful recycle. A unique claim of permanent surface attachment is not required for this value. The result need not transfer to several reaction families to matter. |

Redirect or stop if the best established anchored catalyst already meets the relevant output/retention requirement and no consequential coordination question remains experimentally distinguishable. Stop materials expansion if the supposedly distinct initial states rapidly converge into the same mobile W chemistry, unless the release/recovery behavior itself predicts a useful process change. Stop a practical claim if W retention costs more in lost output, dilution or extra catalyst than it saves in recovery. Stop an atomically specific mechanism claim if available measurements cannot distinguish the alternatives; retain the experimentally established mass/function result.

Conversely, do not reject the program merely because the first anchored material loses activity. A decisive observation that productive substrate coordination displaces particular anchor bonds is precisely the knowledge needed to choose the next anchored structure. Limit the next change to that prediction; repeated undirected support substitutions would no longer be this program.
