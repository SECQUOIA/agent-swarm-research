# Polymer ethenolysis: preserve useful conversion with less fresh catalyst

2026-09-16. Program-development candidate, not an experimental finding. Revised after the [fresh independent review](../../reviews/polymer-clean-feed-program-review.md); its identification corrections are accepted. This note supersedes the ketone exposure question as the preferred starting question for developing a substantial polymer program. It does not claim that a substantial original program has already been established.

## Recommendation and central thesis

**Develop a bounded clean-feed campaign before extending the impurity program.** Ask whether a defined feed or restart intervention can preserve useful conversion of successive polymer charges with less fresh catalyst. The intended contribution is a predictive relation among catalyst history, polymer chain populations, and fresh catalyst demand at a specified accepted output and complete cycle time. A chemical explanation for that relation would deepen the program; a binary feed rescue cannot supply it by itself.

One plausible explanation is that reuse reduces the ability to introduce reactive unsaturation into fresh saturated chains faster than it reduces downstream isomerization and metathesis capacity. This **selective initiation-loss hypothesis is weakly supported**, not the default interpretation. Changing feed unsaturation also changes activation, adsorption competition, the reacting chain population and polymer contact. An aged mixture may respond better to unsaturated PE even when downstream capacity has declined. Conversely, failed rescue does not show that initiation is unchanged.

The strongest first outcome would therefore be a reproducible response of retained catalyst to measured feed state or restart conditioning that predicts a reduction in fresh Na demand. Mechanistic work should follow that operational distinction. A negative result can reject the tested intervention at a stated output and cycle time without forcing a unique molecular explanation.

The potential payoff is greater cumulative polymer-carbon throughput from retained solids. Installing unsaturation upstream, regenerating or separating catalysts may cost more than the catalyst saved. Unsaturated probes are justified diagnostically before that economics is resolved; upstream unsaturation is not yet a preferred process intervention. Early practical accounting should favor preserving a useful state or reducing makeup under ordinary saturated feed when the evidence permits it.

The program fits Iglesia's work on supported-oxide metathesis, working-state activation and reaction kinetics. Gounder's interests in active-site environments, kinetics and catalyst stability provide a connection to identifying and retaining the initiating/isomerizing function. This is a scientific fit assessment; platform access and current group commitments have not been confirmed.

## Closeout scope and first decision

The [independent closeout review](../../reviews/current-programs-closure-review.md) retains **bounded clean-feed development**, conditional on an existing platform. The finite first stage is the fresh/retained/once-supplemented three-charge comparison, its handling control and the smaller-dose bracket. Before decisive runs, define accepted polymer-carbon output, product specification, complete cycle time, the smallest worthwhile saving, balance tolerances and independent replication sufficient to resolve that saving. Validate attribution in the decisive conditions; gross propylene output alone is not polymer-carbon output.

After that baseline, choose one response test. The [ethylene-pressure comparison](polymer-causal-intervention.md) can precede preparation of matched unsaturated polymers if a suitable semibatch platform already exists and the reuse response is established in that mode. Otherwise, retain the matched-feed comparison as the conditional branch already specified below. The pressure and matched-feed campaigns are alternatives for the first distinction, not requirements to execute both. Stop the selected intervention after a precise negative result; an unresolved measurement calls for a feasibility decision, not an expanding set of rescue experiments. Molecular characterization and a held-out catalyst-use prediction follow only a reproducible operational distinction.

## Closest competition and the originality bar

1. **The original Na/W developers already study initiation and regeneration.** The distinction here must be a validated relation between catalyst history, evolving polymer chains and cumulative catalyst use, supported by a consequential intervention. Specific function loss is a mechanistic ambition, not an inference from feed rescue. Simply repeating reuse, observing fresh-Na rescue, finding more ethane, or trying DME would not meet the bar. The inaccessible dissertation creates a real scope uncertainty, not proof that all useful work has been done.
2. **Chain-end kinetics already have a theoretical basis.** Chen, Sadow and Peters, [ACS Catalysis 2024, DOI 10.1021/acscatal.4c00465](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.4c00465), model homogeneous isomerizing ethenolysis. The uploaded final article, now checked directly (pp.4–8), resolves finite isomerization kinetics, predicts saturation and possible ethylene inhibition at low chain-end concentration, and explicitly proposes varying pressure, initial dehydrogenation and molecular weight. Guironnet and Peters, [J. Phys. Chem. A 2020, DOI 10.1021/acs.jpca.0c01363](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jpca.0c01363), supply earlier theory. Neither is evidence that the same rate law holds for Na/W in a polymer melt. A deeper contribution could connect independently measured function decay to the polymer-dependent regime and successfully predict a useful intervention; deriving a generic initiation threshold is insufficient.
3. **Other tandem catalysts already exhibit rate–lifetime tradeoffs.** Wang et al., [JACS 2022, DOI 10.1021/jacs.2c07781](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.2c07781), are the relevant prior system; the [existing independent review](../../reviews/polymer-independent-review.md) records the source evidence. A broad claim that tandem components change each other's useful lifetime is not new.
4. **Long operation and light-olefin formation are increasingly demanding process benchmarks.** Xu et al., [Nature Communications 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-70325-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-70325-2), report a liquid-alloy route without ethylene cofeed and extended operation. It is a different high-temperature process and product distribution; its headline production rate is not directly comparable with Na/W propylene yield. It nevertheless makes a few successful repeated batches inadequate evidence of process advantage. The main article is now read in the KB. It reports 120 h of cumulative daytime operation, not uninterrupted lifetime; the detailed SI carbon and energy accounting remains unresolved. [[xu2026-interfacial-configurational-entropy-tuning-strategy]] p.1

This search found no openly inspected source that establishes the proposed unequal-function-decay explanation for clean-feed Na/W reuse. That bounded result is not a global novelty guarantee. An important outcome could still be a precise negative result showing that a specified feed or restart intervention does not reduce catalyst demand under the tested conditions. Failed rescue alone cannot establish which catalytic functions were lost.

## First campaign: establish one useful operational distinction

### 1. Establish what deteriorates and bracket fresh-Na demand

Reproduce the original catalyst preparation and one fresh PE time course, then the unsupplemented and once-supplemented three-charge sequences. Measure product trajectories, ethylene consumption, residual polymer carbon and complete cycle times. Distinguish an induction delay, lower sustained rate and an earlier plateau. Report fresh Na, fresh W and retained solids separately. Start with one well-characterized clean PE lot. The separate-component Figure 2 caption specifies 10 bar ethylene while the associated text and SI procedures specify 15 bar; use the actual SI protocol and retain that discrepancy in the replication record. [[conk2024-supporting-information-for-polyolefin-waste]] p.7–8

Treat the published decline as a reuse observation until interrupted operation has been tested. The reported sequence cools and dismantles under inert atmosphere. Include a fresh catalyst taken through an otherwise empty handling cycle. Compare a closed inert restart with the standard dismantled restart only if existing hardware permits a valid matched temperature, gas and feeding history. No-opening feed addition needs verified delivery and mixing; it is not an assumed capability. If handling explains the measured decline, pursue the useful retention protocol without claiming intrinsic turnover-driven deactivation.

After confirming the decline, compare no makeup, a smaller Na/alumina addition and the published 400 mg addition before charge 2, preserving the three-charge schedule. This brackets fresh-material demand for a defined output and complete cycle time; three doses do not locate a precise minimum. Include an added-support control at a stated matched mass. That control can expose a solids/contact contribution but does not reproduce the surface chemistry or rheology of Na/alumina. Do not infer restoration of a specific function from endpoint rescue at any dose.

Check agitation and catalyst particle-size sensitivity over a limited accessible range and record actual temperature histories. A stirring plateau does not establish absence of pore diffusion or incomplete polymer contact. Use full molecular-weight distributions and retained-polymer fractions as complementary observables. Balzer et al.'s [GPC/DSC chain-population analysis](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscentsci.4c00951) supplies a method precedent, with validation needed for this feed. Its inferred populations are not unique mechanistic assignments.

### 2. Measure how the retained inventory responds to feed unsaturation

Prepare a purified unsaturated PE parent and its hydrogenated counterpart with closely matched initial chain-length and branching distributions, matched workups and verified residual metals, ligands, oxygenates and solvents. Hydrogenation must not materially change chain length or branching. Blend these endpoints to provide saturated PE and low and higher verified fractions of unsaturated chains at fixed total polymer mass. Cross these three feed states with fresh and identically aged catalyst mixtures, using independent specimens rather than attempting to separate intimately mixed Na and W powders. Establish fresh-catalyst behavior for each feed first.

Characterize unsaturation per chain, its position and the fraction of chains bearing C=C bonds as far as the measurements permit. One C=C on every chain differs from several C=C bonds on a small subset. A bulk olefin percentage and number-average molecular weight cannot establish that distribution; state what the parent synthesis and analysis do and do not resolve. If chain occupancy cannot be established, retain the measured bulk descriptor but narrow claims accordingly. Record early evolution and whole product trajectories alongside recovered polymer distributions. Validate quenching and recovery before treating the recovered unsaturation distribution as the reacting distribution. Wang's 2022 dissertation explicitly reports that depressurization followed by 2–3 h cooling permits isomerization and fragment recombination; its recycled-polymer comparison also confounds polymer state and retained catalyst history. Use a quench/recovery control and narrow state assignments if a representative reacting sample cannot be preserved. [[chapter0000-chapter-4-tandem-polyethylene-ethenolysis]] PDF pp.77–79, 136 (printed pp.72–74, 131)

Initial matching cannot keep the reacting chain distributions, viscosity or contact identical: they change as consequences of unsaturation. The comparison measures the **response of retained catalyst to feed state**, not a chemically isolated switch for initiation. Increasing reactive olefin supply can partly compensate for reduced downstream capacity in a substrate-dependent kinetic regime. A high-coverage molecular assay can also conceal losses relevant to sparse reactive chains. Product traces improve the description but do not uniquely separate these effects.

Account for soluble, insoluble, catalyst-bound and volatile carbon, including recovery losses. SEC/NMR of only the soluble or precipitated fraction can miss the least accessible material. State NMR sensitivity on a chain-number basis where possible. Observed unsaturation reflects both formation and consumption, so a low or unchanged signal does not establish a low or unchanged initiation rate. If a small olefin is used in preliminary tests, distinguish its carbon from polymer-derived output.

**Decision:** a reproducible advantage for the aged catalyst inventory for a specified unsaturation level justifies testing its cause and its ability to reduce makeup. Failure to restore useful output rejects that feed intervention under the measured conditions; it does not exclude simultaneous initiation and downstream loss. Reject an initiation-only explanation only when its independent downstream-capacity and access assumptions are supported.

### 3. Test one explanation suggested by the response

If changing olefin supply creates a clear advantage, test one controlled olefin-conditioning intervention on separate aged specimens followed by matched saturated PE, against a sham-conditioned reference. Record dose, duration, purge time, retained carbon and both early and later response. Alkenes can activate supported metathesis catalysts; [Howell, Li and Bell's study of the relevant W/silica lineage](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.6b01842) is direct prior art identified by the independent review. A carryover benefit demonstrates a useful history effect, not W-specific restoration or absence of initiation loss. If the first intervention is reproducibly effective, compare a short and longer delay to determine persistence before expanding conditions.

A negative conditioning result excludes only that protocol. Activation may require continuous olefin supply, decay during the purge or depend on long-chain adsorbates. Neither successful nor failed conditioning resolves all mechanisms behind the unsaturated-feed response. If the response instead tracks substantial polymer shortening, prioritize contact and recovered-chain observations before adding chemical-state measurements.

Use molecular initiation, isomerization or metathesis probes only for a specific remaining inference. Validate operational specificity on individual catalysts and bare supports; keep immediate spent-state responses separate from conditioned responses, and report earliest resolvable behavior. Small probes may reach sites unavailable to polymer or create the state they purport to measure. They do not certify intact polymer-processing capacity.

The campaign should establish an intervention worth explaining or close a defined intervention with a precise negative result. It should not turn into a factorial of every feed, conditioning, mixing and catalyst-state variable. Sequence controls around the first reproducible distinction and use independent repeats where they determine whether that distinction is real.

## How a positive result grows into a substantial program

**Stage A: predict fresh catalyst demand from history and feed state.** Use the measured feed response, range of tested makeup doses, product trajectories and chain-population observations to build the smallest model needed for a useful prediction. Predict the makeup dose or complete cycle time for an accepted output under a withheld catalyst history or chain-length distribution. Do not fit a new deactivation law to the validation trace. An operational predictor is sufficient for this stage; parameters inferred solely from product output must not be called independent initiation and metathesis capacities. Incorporate independently measured function rates only when their specificity and transfer to the polymer conditions are established.

**Stage B: explain one controllable history effect.** Select the leading explanation from the response, such as a persistent activation change, altered contact, or unequal function decay. If evidence supports selective initiation loss, investigate its relation to Na inventory and working state, support acid–base environments, or retained organic species. These are alternatives, not a presumed list of operating mechanisms. Element balances constrain Na loss or redistribution; Na found on W material does not by itself prove poisoning. Spectroscopy should follow a perturbation that changes the relevant operational response, with functional attribution strengthened by independent evidence. Bulk Na speciation–yield correlation is insufficient. No unique site assignment is required to retain a useful, validated catalyst-use result.

**Stage C: demonstrate the net benefit of one intervention.** Choose an intervention justified by the established effect: prevent avoidable handling damage, preserve or restore a useful working state, or supply a measured feed-state change. Do not introduce a formulation library before there is a causal target. Compare with the measured smaller-dose/full-dose Na benchmark and the verified scope of regeneration. Independent upstream initiation is already a known process concept; its value here would require a measured reduction in full catalyst demand or downtime after charging all feed preparation costs.

An unsaturated seed does not automatically initiate saturated PE chains: those chains still need their own entry into the alkene network. Likewise, preserving a residual olefin-rich heel is not a substitute for proving initiation of incoming saturated material. This prevents a superficially attractive but chemically unsupported continuous-feed proposal.

Only after the clean-feed lifetime model predicts useful behavior should one characterized waste-feed perturbation be introduced. Its purpose is to determine whether the validated catalyst-use relation and intervention survive a relevant feed change. The ketone diagnostic remains available if feed chemistry and the identified function make it consequential.

## Practical comparison and stopping rules

Use cumulative **polymer-derived carbon in accepted products per total fresh catalyst solid consumed**, with separate Na- and W-material accounting. Also report accepted polymer-carbon output per reactor volume and complete elapsed cycle time, ethylene net consumption and recycle demand, feed pretreatment, regeneration reagents/energy, and unrecovered polymer carbon. A lower-temperature but much slower run or a higher W TON after repeated Na additions is not automatically an improvement.

For the published three-charge Na supplement schedule, the fresh solid inventory totals 1.2 g versus 2.4 g for three separate fresh 0.8 g charges. At equal mean accepted polymer-carbon output this doubles the simple mass-based catalyst-use metric; more generally the [existing audited calculation](../../calculations/polymer-makeup-burden.md) gives B = 2f. This is a benchmark calculation, not process economics. A proposed selective restoration must improve on that option or resolve a consequential limit that the three-charge test cannot reveal.

Close a tested feed or conditioning intervention if it fails to preserve accepted output at the required cycle time, with precision adequate to exclude the useful effect being sought. Do not infer unchanged initiation from that failure. If independent evidence shows comparable downstream loss or explains the benefit through polymer contact, revise or deprioritize selective initiation loss as the sole explanation. Do not expand chemical-state mapping merely to keep it alive.

If a feed intervention works diagnostically but its full preparation cost exceeds plausible savings, retain the feed-response finding and close that practical intervention; it may still identify a less costly way to preserve the working state. A contact-based improvement can itself be worthwhile if it reduces fresh-material demand and is reproducible. It need not be relabeled as site chemistry.

Proceed toward a larger program when the catalyst-history/feed-state relation predicts a held-out catalyst-use result with a meaningful consequence. A mechanism that explains and improves that prediction strengthens program depth. Reproducing fresh-Na rescue or fitting endpoint yields alone does not.

## Confidence and present merit

| Judgment | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Selective initiation-loss hypothesis | **Plausible but weakly supported.** Fresh-Na rescue motivates the question without localizing function loss. Feed supply, activation and contact remain alternatives, and several functions may decay together. |
| Feasibility and information value | **Moderate, conditional on platform access.** Pressure reactors, gas analysis, inert handling and polymer characterization are established methods. Producing matched polymer probes, preserving spent working states, and closing polymer-carbon balances are the main difficulties. The bounded response and makeup campaign can give useful operational knowledge with greater confidence than unique molecular attribution. |
| Meaningful practical improvement | **Low at present.** Large solids inventory creates an opportunity, but ethylene demand, solids handling, regeneration and feed preparation can dominate. A reproducible lower makeup demand at comparable complete-cycle output is a credible target, with no present evidence for its size or net benefit. |
| Originality | **Conditional.** Initiation mechanism and regeneration are active prior work; the defensible contribution is a validated catalyst-history/feed-response relation and an intervention with a demonstrated consequence for cumulative use. The dissertation body is a material unresolved overlap risk. |

**Present ranking within polymer development:** a stronger basis for the next discriminating experiment than the ketone-specific hypothesis, and a plausible path to a substantial program. It is not yet a sufficiently validated central thesis to justify committing the whole effort to this platform. The critical first result is a reproducible response to measured feed state or restart history that can be tested against a fresh-Na dose bracket. It must not be reported as selective initiation loss from a binary contrast.
