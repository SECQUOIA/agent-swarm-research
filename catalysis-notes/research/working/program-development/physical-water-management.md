# Predicting physical water management in catalytic beds

2026-09-16. Completed candidate development; first priority for bounded feasibility and functional-response tests. The [transport review](../../reviews/ft-hydrophobic-transport-review.md), [first-test review](../../reviews/water-management-first-test-review.md) and [measurement review](../../reviews/water-measurement-feasibility-review.md) are incorporated. No experiments have been performed. A substantial program remains conditional on an independently supported functional prediction or additional source-to-site evidence; an identified stationary mechanism requires the latter. The calculations are limiting-case arguments, not fitted material properties.

## Central question and investment case

Can separately mixed hydrophobic materials protect a catalyst by changing its sustained local water exposure, and can independently measured transport and sorption properties predict when that protection will work? Alternatively, do the additives mainly change water storage, startup history, liquid distribution, or the catalyst itself?

This question is grounded in reported large catalytic effects. Fang and colleagues report sustained Co/SiO2 Fischer–Tropsch performance after adding PDVB, and earlier work reports promotion of CoMn carbide and Cu-based CO2 hydrogenation. The intervention is established. The desired contribution is a predictive rule for useful operating histories or, with additional constraints, geometry. The current proposal does not yet establish an experimentally identifiable stationary-transport explanation. A generic additive screen, a shorter washout, or another demonstration of stable cobalt is insufficient.

The potential practical value is retaining useful catalytic output through a comparatively simple formulation or packing change. It must be assessed per complete catalyst/additive inventory and bed volume, including the observed initial-rate penalty and effects of shaping, wax accumulation and regeneration. This is not evidence that an academic Co/SiO2 reference outperforms a modern industrial formulation. A useful negative result could establish where hydrophobic mixing cannot substitute for actual water removal or where startup treatment is the relevant intervention.

The [independently checked integration of the published source data](../../calculations/water-published-output.md) strengthens this investment case within its proper comparator: over the common 25–585 h window, the C5+ output proxy is about 1.89 times the reference, or 1.80 after charging the 5% PDVB mass. The source explicitly reports matched bed volumes. Broad bounds on unobserved early selectivity and a separate measurement-error sensitivity do not reverse the comparison. This is analysis of an existing benefit, not a new improvement; it leaves optimized-reference, regeneration and scale-up comparisons open.

The scientific connection is direct: Iglesia's reaction–diffusion, Fischer–Tropsch, adsorption and transient-kinetics work; Gounder's work on pore environments, adsorption, transport and kinetic inference. Neither professor's current equipment or interest in executing this particular study is assumed.

Newly available originals sharpen this boundary. [Yang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1039/C9CY02557J), original pp. 7–9, already compare silica-shell geometries at similar initial conversion and calculated bulk water pressure. They attribute better stability at 240 °C to local water removal and the dominant decline at 220 °C to retained heavy hydrocarbons. These are supported mechanistic interpretations, not direct local-water measurements; cobalt loading, pore dimensions and flow also differ. A generic geometry–durability correlation or another wax-versus-water assignment is therefore insufficient novelty. The late low-dose intervention and independently constrained functional prediction remain the intended distinctions.

[Zhao et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nanoen.2023.108350), original pp. 3 and 7, also report physical PDVB promotion of cobalt photothermal olefin synthesis and suppressed water-gas shift. This is a 1:1 powder mixture in a one-hour batch assay, not the 5% separate-granule longevity recipe. The reported 8.82 versus 4.41 cm³/g water uptake is exactly the twofold decrease expected from inert dilution if grams refer to total mixture and PDVB uptake is negligible. It cannot alone establish lower loading on cobalt. The published 6.7-fold WGS-rate contrast also requires its mass denominator before assigning a catalyst-specific ratio. These qualifications preserve the functional observation while limiting the water-removal interpretation.

The 2026 study also varies granule dimensions and mixing arrangements. Its CT Figure 4 caption describes a powder co-granulate; the headline longevity result uses separately prepared granules mixed together. These are both reported configurations, but a quantitative model for one should not silently be assigned to the other.

## Physical distinction that the experiment must resolve

At fixed temperature, two surfaces can have different equilibrium water loadings at the same water chemical potential. A difference in adsorbed concentration alone does not create a sustained chemical-potential gradient in an equilibrated connected gas. Under reaction, a real source, sink or boundary flow can support gradients. Wettability can influence transport, adsorption, surface mobility and liquid configurations; the equilibrium argument does not deny those effects.

In a deliberately simple reversible-storage model,

\[
B\frac{dc}{dt}=q-G(c-c_b),\qquad
\tau=B/G,\qquad c_{ss}-c_b=q/G.
\]

Here c is the water concentration in one coupled compartment, c_b its external boundary value, B its differential storage capacity, G its transport conductance and q a specified water source. In consistent units B has dimensions of volume, G volume/time, and q amount/time. B includes reversible sorption only to the extent that it equilibrates with that compartment. At fixed q and G, changing B changes the transient but not stationary c. Decreasing B or increasing G can give the same normalized washout time while implying different steady water exposure. These are textbook transport consequences, not claimed new theory.

The calculation does **not** establish that storage is irrelevant to catalyst durability. Irreversible damage can depend on startup or shutdown exposure even when later stationary conditions match. Nor does lower storage always protect: for a step into a water-generating state it can make water approach its plateau faster, while after source removal it shortens the tail. Direction and consequence depend on the measured history. If q, G, B or the catalyst state evolve, the simple constant-parameter result must be replaced by a coupled balance.

The useful advance would be establishing which independently measurable quantities control real promotion, and using them to predict a new geometry or history without assigning a free local-water parameter to every experiment.

There is also a resistance-location limit. For unchanged internal resistance R_in and promoter-sensitive external resistance R_out in series, the fixed-source excess is q(R_in + R_out). Eliminating the external resistance can remove at most qR_out. This does not bound a promoter that changes internal liquid connectivity or catalyst chemistry; those would be different mechanisms. A passive removal improvement alone cannot reduce the local steady water activity below the imposed boundary for a compartment with a positive source and no additional sink.

The [reviewed scale calculation](../../calculations/water-transport-limits.md) gives the gas-referenced effective diffusivity required to sustain a specified source-generated gradient under explicit assumptions. It is an experimental-planning constraint, not a numerical estimate of the published material's water exposure. The source's molecular self-diffusivity cannot simply be substituted for the needed transport coefficient.

## Competing explanations and discriminating evidence

| Explanation | Distinct evidence sought | Design consequence if supported |
|---|---|---|
| Lower reversible water storage, little change in stationary transport | Absolute uptake and transient changes explain washout; stationary water sensitivity is unchanged after matched equilibration. | Focus on startup/shutdown and damaging pulse histories. Do not infer continuous local drying from faster washout. |
| Greater effective transport conductance | Independently constrained storage and geometry cannot explain transients; the measured change predicts stationary source-dependent inhibition and its size/flow dependence. | Choose arrangement, size and additive fraction to lower relevant resistance without excessive dilution. |
| Different liquid distribution or evolving pore access | Promotion depends on wax loading/history, and transport/sorption changes follow the working liquid rather than dry contact angle. | Control wetting and pore-liquid evolution; a dry-powder rule will not transfer directly. |
| Chemical or persistent structural modification | The effect survives removal of the neighboring additive, or a material-state change explains response after water/transport matching. | Treat the material as a modified catalyst and investigate the modification; physical mixing alone is not a mechanistic assignment. |
| Hydrodynamic, temperature or source-rate difference | Improved matching removes the apparent effect or a measured field accounts for it. | Retain the engineering consequence where useful, but do not claim an independent water-transport mechanism. |

These effects may coexist. A model must identify the experimentally resolvable contribution rather than force a unique explanation.

## Completed scope and bounded first stage

**The current recommendation is a bounded experimental qualification, not commitment to a substantial identified-transport program.** The [fresh measurement review](../../reviews/water-measurement-feasibility-review.md) distinguishes three deliverables: a qualified measurement, a prospective operating-history result, and an identified stationary mechanism. The first is plausible with suitable existing equipment; the second is untested; a demonstrated route to the third is absent. Ranking this candidate highly relative to alternatives does not remove that distinction.

The [reviewed two-pool counterexample](../../calculations/water-local-exposure-identifiability.md) proves a specific limitation: even inlet-water and source-water tracer responses can agree while exposure at the producing region differs. More outlet curves, multiple flows, or a second isotope input do not guarantee identification. A selective source-region constraint or independently valid local rate law might supply missing information, but neither is established here. No broad isotope campaign is included.

### Exact initial commitment

1. **Qualify one source platform.** Reproduce the low-dose, separate-granule Co/SiO2–PDVB contrast against the quartz comparator, with catalyst inventory, bed volume, preparation history, temperatures and useful-output balances documented. Respect the unresolved FT restart sequence: synthesis includes reduction/passivation; the main FT methods omit a complete re-reduction description while SI Note 10 refers to a dry-hydrogen-pre-reduced standard. Do not silently transfer the analytical 300 °C pretreatment. Existing SiC and other-hydrophobe controls are prior evidence, not a new screen.
2. **Make one apparatus/measurement decision.** In an existing capable FT system, run the hot water-specific blank and packing blanks, then representative parent and low-dose PDVB packings at one qualified state. Use small up/down steps at two flows, one reduced-amplitude repeat, baseline returns and independent packing checks. Resolve or bound source changes and actual instrument delay. If the signal is identifiable, freeze and test one partial-loading pulse selected to distinguish the remaining models. The [measurement note](water-measurement-feasibility.md) defines this stage and its stop criteria. A damaging challenge is not required to qualify the apparatus.
3. **Retain one defined causal formulation test.** The [first-test protocol](water-management-first-test.md), revised after [independent review](../../reviews/water-management-first-test-review.md), adds PDVB after common parent conditioning and applies one low–high–low water challenge. Its endpoint is recovered function of the complete formulation, fractional retention and absolute/cumulative useful output. A handling pilot and, if needed, time-matched low-water shams qualify interpretation. This test can be worthwhile as a formulation boundary even if a local transport coefficient remains unidentifiable; report that narrower result.

These stages have explicit end decisions; they are not permission for an open-ended apparatus-development campaign. Establish the minimum decision-relevant contrast and measurement uncertainty before committing the paired runs. If the platform contrast, handling, source balance or useful measurement resolution fails, stop the affected route and retain the failure record. No experiment has yet been performed or apparatus access confirmed.

### Claims that these tests can support

A held-out outlet response validates that input–output prediction only. To establish a useful operating-history result, the same pulse must also test an independently specified, resolvable prediction of productivity recovery or cumulative useful product. Calibrate nearby reversible water responses on each stable formulation before validation, and account for product-detection delay, thermal/reactant-transfer changes and intrinsic surface relaxation. A steady external-water calibration is not a direct local-water reporter. Agreement can support a bounded operating rule without identifying a unique microscopic pool.

For the separate causal challenge, externally supplied water constrains the whole-bed production difference, but a small residual exposure difference can matter near a sharp damage threshold. At the proposed 2% conversion limit, generated/supplied water is approximately 2.5% during the 4 bar plateau and 20% during the 0.5 bar recovery. Neither common inlet humidity nor higher flow proves equal intragranular exposure. PDVB stays in the recovery assay, so improved retention does not independently demonstrate surviving cobalt sites. Changes in local H2/CO transport and H2O/H2 redox conditions remain possible.

Imposed-water challenges and returns are established methods: [Wolf et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2019.04.030) used low conversion, fixed H2/CO partial pressures and incremental water on Co/graphite. Its measurements support the method, not a damage threshold for this Co/SiO2 formulation. The proposed advance is the late-additive contrast and a prospective functional rule, not introducing steam or measuring recovery.

Preserve uptake normalization. At additive/catalyst ratio r, an unchanged independently sorbing mixture has total-mass-normalized uptake `(q_cat + r q_add)/(1+r)`. Negligible additive uptake alone lowers that value by `1/(1+r)` without lowering catalyst water loading. Source uptake curves require their preparation ratio and mass basis before assigning a catalyst-specific capacity change. Moreover, a reacting pole can contain source feedback: for `B dx/dt = Q(x) + G(cb − x)`, linearization gives `λ = (G − ∂Q/∂x)/B`. Correcting the integrated source is not automatically enough to identify G.

### Closed and conditional follow-ups

The [working-liquid evaluation](water-working-liquid-intervention.md) rejects hydrocarbon-preloaded PDVB as a selective wax-versus-water mechanism discriminator. Preloading changes finite capacity, wetting, reactant access and the proposed water-transfer interface together. The source gives Co/SiO2 pore volume, not a measured hot PDVB wax capacity. Finite polymer inventory excludes indefinite capture of all product, but not a small redistributed liquid inventory or startup effect. No preloading experiment is included.

The [reviewed granule-size calculation](../../calculations/water-size-discriminator.md) is retained as a conditional design constraint. External transfer and internal diffusion can both give radius-squared exposure scaling. Two sizes alone cannot identify them. A smaller-unpromoted versus original-promoted comparison is useful only when a concrete output/pressure-drop decision warrants it; a mechanistic prediction additionally requires independently constrained source weighting and resistance changes. Those constraints are not presently available. No numerical shaping target or automatic size factorial follows from the calculation.

## Confidence and stopping

- **Scientific premise:** physical-promotion effects justify a bounded reproduction and formulation test. Which stationary or history-dependent process causes the effect is unresolved. The reviewed counterexample establishes why outlet data alone cannot guarantee a local-exposure explanation.
- **Experimental feasibility and informativeness:** a modest qualification is plausible conditional on an existing pressurized FT reactor, quantitative water handling, representative packing and reproducible state. An identifiable transport coefficient or a prospective functional rule is not guaranteed. There is no demonstrated source-to-cobalt measurement route for stationary mechanism claims.
- **Practical benefit:** plausible but unproven beyond the published recipe. The observed initial-rate penalty and existing industrial stability strategies are substantial comparators. No cost or lifetime advantage is established here.
- **Originality:** physical mixing, cross-reaction promotion, wetting contrasts, imaging, water challenges, wax management and diffusion hypotheses are established. A consequential held-out functional prediction could support further program development. Apparatus qualification, conceptual corrections and a good outlet fit alone do not constitute that advance.

Close the identified-transport claim if source weighting and resistance remain unidentifiable; failure to measure them is not evidence that water is irrelevant. Retain a useful formulation or operating-history result on its demonstrated scope. Further investment beyond the bounded stage requires a consequential new functional prediction or an explicit independent constraint that resolves the intended mechanism. All substantive independent-review corrections are accepted, with no unresolved scientific-review disagreement. Equipment access, working-state preservation, source balance and the magnitude of any new practical improvement remain experimentally unresolved.
