# Polymer ethenolysis: exposure, useful catalyst function and reuse

2026-09-16. Updated existing screen after the uploaded primary texts. **Retain a bounded experimental decision study; a substantial original program is not yet established.** No experiments were performed. The [post-upload audit](../reviews/post-upload-polymer-audit.md) documents the corrections; the [exposure/recovery design](../reviews/polymer-exposure-falsifiability.md) is the controlling protocol.

## Recommendation and connection to the laboratories

Test whether a measured 2-pentanone exposure leaves persistent loss of a useful Na-containing function while W metathesis remains active or recovers, and whether this contrast predicts actual polymer throughput and a better supported feed-acceptance or catalyst-makeup decision. This is the already refined hypothesis. The early sacrificial cross-protection explanation remains an alternative requiring additional evidence; it is not the default claim or a reason to start an adsorbent screen.

The Iglesia connection is alkene-metathesis kinetics, activation, inhibition and useful catalyst populations. Gounder's connections are accessibility, coadsorbate partitioning, acid/base functions and quantitative relations between catalyst state and performance. Mo metathesis titration methods can inform assay design, but are not a validated W-site calibration at polymer-reaction conditions.

## Established evidence and novelty boundary

Conk et al., [Science 2024, DOI 10.1126/science.adq7316](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq7316), and its SI are now local. Relevant original methods/results and Figures 4B and SI S20 were checked in the upload audit. The standard initial recipe uses 1 g PE, 0.4 g WO3/SiO2 and 0.4 g Na/γ-Al2O3 at 320 °C. In the published three-charge reuse experiment, the supplemented arm receives **0.4 g fresh Na/alumina before charge 2 only; charge 3 receives only PE**. Component supplementation is established. Similar supplemented output over three charges is not a complete lifetime demonstration or unique proof of an unchanged W population.

High-dose PVC, PET and DEHP challenges depress output; each addition is 50 mg per gram HDPE. These establish vulnerability, not a trace specification or delivered poison capacity. Reported waste samples were dissolved, precipitated, dried and ground. Cleaning and changed polymer morphology are confounded in that preparation. The scale-up's substantial inventory and mixing requirement remain practical constraints.

The Science SI scale-up record is distinct from the patent: **19.9658 g warm oil, 29.9717 g remaining solid and 80.1% apparent conversion**, compared with the patent's detailed 11.81 g oil, 35.100 g solid and 69.8%. Science SI S20 reports an estimated 1.00 mol propylene, 28.1% yield and 364 W turnovers; the main article's 438 TON concerns mixed collected olefins. The volume/density/mole-fraction calculation and residual-solid subtraction retain the limitations in the [benchmark accounting](../reviews/polymer-patent-benchmark-accounting.md). These are not interchangeable measures of recovered polymer carbon.

Van Schalkwyk's [2003 WO3/SiO2 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0926-860X(03)00536-2) demonstrates condition-dependent ketone tolerance and recoverable inhibition at 460 °C. Its original figures now confirm the distinct 200 ppm once-through and higher-dose recycle results. Transformed carbonyl species in recycle products reinforce the need to measure actual exposure. Moodley's [2007 study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcata.2006.10.053) shows reduced coke with 100 ppm oxygenate without an assigned additional performance loss over 72 h. Neither establishes a 320 °C threshold, equal intrinsic rates or compatibility with the Na partner. [Maksasithorn 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1872-2067(12)60760-8) demonstrates composition-dependent effects of aqueous NaOH treatment on W leaching and metathesis performance; detecting Na near W alone does not establish destructive migration during polymer conversion.

Adsorbent protection is already known from [Kim 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcatb.2022.121873), and transfer-dehydrogenation/isomerization/ethenolysis and product removal have prior art in [Wang 2022](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.2c07781) and [Conk 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.add1088). The public abstract of [Conk's 2025 dissertation](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/7v07b7c8) and the patent disclose further initiation/catalyst/regeneration work. The dissertation body and original patent regeneration figures remain unavailable; do not assign a DME recovery efficacy or catalyst identity from a method heading.

## Hypothesis and informative alternatives

The proposed contrast is **W tolerance/recovery versus Na-function loss persisting beyond a declared clean operating interval**. Persistence is protocol-specific, not proof of irreversible destruction. Na isomerization and chain initiation are distinct functions; both components can contribute to chain cleavage. Component assay recovery can itself result from assay-induced activation.

Live alternatives are: both components recover; both retain loss; W is impaired while Na survives; transformed ketone products rather than the parent control the response; clean-feed decay dominates; or changed polymer molecular weight, unsaturation, contact and transport explain the apparent memory. Sacrificial Na-to-W protection additionally requires measured reduction in harmful W exposure and preserved W function; Na oxygen uptake or a sharp output threshold alone is insufficient.

A candidate cumulative-dose model is a separate, stronger claim. Equal delivered doses need not be equal effective doses, and equal final function need not give equal integrated product. Define the measured predictor before the withheld challenge; do not rescue a failed model by redefining effective exposure afterward.

## Minimum controlled sequence

1. **Reproduce the reference and handling baseline.** Use one PE and the published recipe. Compare the ordinary time course, three retained-mixture charges without makeup, and the published three-charge control with 0.4 g Na added before charge 2 only. Measure full cycle time, retained solids, polymer-derived accepted product and ethylene supply. Use independent preparations and enough repeated decisive runs to resolve the chosen decision; establish the useful contrast and uncertainty before choosing exposure severity.
2. **Measure one operating-component exposure and recovery.** Use already functioning W in an internal-alkene/ethylene assay and validated Na-associated readouts, with support/handling controls needed to interpret them. Record immediate response and recovery without an unreported activating treatment. Measure ketone delivery, disappearance, transformed products, water and removal from the relevant phases. Select a resolvable, incomplete perturbation; do not increase severity simply to force W inhibition outside a credible feed range.
3. **Predict one withheld mixture history.** Compare short/high and long/low input histories at equal delivered dose and common observation time after characterizing mixing and washout. Predeclare transient ordering and residual output on a subsequent clean PE charge. Hold substrate supply, inventory, thermal history and handling common. Bound retained polymer and products; a batch-rate difference alone is not catalyst memory. Reject transfer if the component model cannot predict the operating outcome without refitting.
4. **Pursue one practical comparison only if prediction survives.** Use one characterized feed/preparation contrast or one predicted makeup timing/dose. The comparator is the published three-charge Na supplementation schedule under matched conditions. Do not assume selective recovery of W from intimately mixed powders. Add a site assay or structural measurement only when it resolves a consequential ambiguity.

The [full design](../reviews/polymer-exposure-falsifiability.md) specifies assay, washout and substrate-trajectory controls. Initial measurements require calibrated product/exposure analysis and polymer-state checks; they do not require an absolute working-site census.

## Accounting and stopping rules

Report accepted **polymer-derived** carbon once across the complete boundary, including final retained inventory, alongside total product, ethylene consumption, fresh solid supplied, retained solid, preparation losses and full cycle time. Do not count ethylene carbon as recovered plastic or sum overlapping conversions of carried-over polymer.

For the published three-charge schedule, fresh solid supplied is 1.2 g versus 2.4 g for three fresh batches. If `f` is mean accepted polymer-derived output relative to the fresh recipe, the conditional material-productivity ratio is **`B = 2f`**. This does not establish equal output, equal capacity, a cost optimum or indefinite reuse. The [accounting note](../calculations/polymer-makeup-burden.md) retains these distinctions.

**Close the selected impurity branch** if it does not cause incremental loss beyond clean decay/transport, its exposure has no measured feed connection, the proposed component contrast fails, or it does not predict tandem behavior. Preserve the resulting exposure limit or negative finding. Do not start another materials or contaminant screen to rescue it.

**Expand only within this question** if the fixed-parameter prediction survives a withheld history and changes a defensible feed or makeup decision relative to the stronger published control. Require a contrast exceeding resolved uncertainty and useful for the declared output/resource objective. A reduced cleanup burden or a justified feed rejection is useful even without higher catalyst activity. Less coke, a higher W TON or repeated demonstration of fresh-Na rescue alone does not qualify.

## Separate confidence judgments

- **Chemical hypothesis: low to moderate.** Unequal susceptibility is plausible; persistent ketone-induced Na loss and its dominance in polymer operation remain unverified.
- **Experimental feasibility and information: moderate, conditional on the platform.** The uploaded source improves reference reproducibility. Assay reactivation, gas–melt transport, transformed exposure and retained polymer remain material difficulties.
- **Practical improvement: low.** The inventory/preparation burden is real, but the existing simple Na-makeup control is stronger than the original incomplete record suggested. A useful feed decision is more credible initially than a large lifetime improvement.
- **Originality: provisional and bounded.** The checked Science methods/results contain no identified ketone exposure/recovery test of the coupled mixture. That bounded reading does not prove novelty across the unresolved dissertation and regeneration record.

## Screened-out alternatives and retained lessons

- **External-acid-site engineering alone:** not sufficiently original. The local Hullfish study already connects surface BAS to polymer activation and subsequent transport. [[hullfish2026-reaction-based-quantification-of-surface]] p.10-11. More directly, 2025 work identifies external acidity as a descriptor, and 2026 selective external exchange establishes accessible-site control. [Nature Communications, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-57158-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57158-1); [Chemistry–Methods, DOI 10.1002/cmtd.202500157](https://doi.org/10.1002/cmtd.202500157).
- **Two-stage PVC removal followed by hydroconversion:** practical, but already demonstrated; a new generic guard-bed proposal is weak. This remains a benchmark if the program later moves from oxygenates to chlorine. [Nature Sustainability, DOI 10.1038/s41893-023-01147-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41893-023-01147-z).
- **Pre-dehydrogenation as a new rescue strategy:** already established and actively developed, including heterogeneous Pt/ZnO transfer dehydrogenation in 2026. It can be a mechanistic control, not a standalone novelty claim. [ChemSusChem, DOI 10.1002/cssc.202502353](https://doi.org/10.1002/cssc.202502353).
