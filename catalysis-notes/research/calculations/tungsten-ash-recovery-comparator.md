# Working recovery evidence beyond the patent disclosures

2026-09-16. Primary-source audit for [tungsten process accounting](tungsten-process-burden.md), revised after [independent review](../reviews/tungsten-ash-recovery-review.md). Source: [WO2022064039A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2022064039A1/en), description and [original PDF](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/dd/8e/4e/7b2978dad9907f/WO2022064039A1.pdf), printed pp. 8–13, Tables 6–7 and Figures 1–6. This patent contains working examples. The earlier finding of no working examples applied only to WO2022066160A1 and US12297166B2; it must not be generalized to soluble-W recovery.

**Recovered W has already been tested as a functioning catalyst in a product-rich continuous reactor.** A new heterogeneous-retention program must compete with that demonstrated capability, while recognizing that this is a short laboratory test of recovered material from model waste, not a validated closed industrial recycle.

## What the experiment actually does

A model waste containing polyols, W and salts is burned at about 1200 °C. Selected collected ash is acid-treated, washed six times and dried, then dissolved in EG/NaOH at 150 °C for two hours. The description reports 84.2% W dissolution relative to boiler ash and a resulting solution containing 1.26 wt% elemental W. This percentage does not establish recovery from the original reactor feed: ash capture, sample selection and all intervening losses must be counted before assigning a whole-process return fraction.

Tables 6–7 compare recovered and fresh W in sucrose hydrogenolysis. The stated mixture contains 20 wt% sucrose, about 19 wt% EG and about 61 wt% water. Table 7 lists H2WO4-equivalent concentrations of 0.34 and 0.30 wt%, and NaOH/H2WO4 molar ratio 0.73 in both. However, Table 6's recovered-solution mass and elemental-W fraction imply approximately **0.296 wt% H2WO4-equivalent**, inconsistent with 0.34 wt%. The actual difference in W loading is therefore unresolved. The apparatus retains Ru/C with an outlet filter. Liquid volume is 148 mL; nominal liquid flow 5–7 mL/min and pressure 65–80 bar. Trials last 300 and 360 minutes. The inspected trial account does not specify an exact reaction temperature; the patent's broad process range is not a substitute.

Figures 1–6 show broadly similar time-dependent product responses for recovered and fresh W. Near 300 minutes, both report EG near 40–42% on their stated mass-based, cosolvent-subtracted convention; these are approximate visual readings, not digitized carbon yields. Their reported mass-balance metric is about the mid-to-high 80% range; it is not an elemental-carbon closure. The compound-selectivity equation is written generically in terms of cosolvent subtraction, so it should not be silently applied to every plotted product. No replicate error or catalyst-normalized intrinsic activity equality is established.

## What follows for the proposed program

- **Credit active recovery and product-rich operation as demonstrated prior art.** Adding EG, dissolving W in glycol, or showing that recycled W can cleave sugars is not new by itself.
- **Do not equate the 84.2% dissolution figure with overall W recycle efficiency.** The comparison starts from selected ash made from a model mixture and covers one recovery/assay sequence.
- **Do not assume identical activity per W.** The inconsistent loading records prevent a precise per-W comparison, and the two short trajectories are not a matched lifetime study.
- **Retain recovery burden as a real question.** Burning, acid treatment, repeated washing, drying and re-dissolution are concrete operations. This laboratory sequence neither proves they are economically prohibitive nor establishes their optimized industrial duties.

This evidence strengthens the need to measure W retention together with sustained product output and recovery requirements. It does not show that improved anchoring is unnecessary; it prevents claiming an advantage merely because W stays in a solid.
