# Standards for selecting and developing a program

## Selection

Prioritize important catalytic or process limitations and the likelihood of learning something consequential. Favor a small number of well-developed programs. An informative negative result may justify a program even when improved performance is uncertain.

Every program must explain:

1. The practical problem, affected process or class of processes, and connection to at least one professor's expertise.
2. What prior work already establishes and the narrow additional knowledge sought. Search competing groups and recent work, including results that weaken the idea.
3. A falsifiable hypothesis with plausible alternatives and the observations that distinguish them.
4. A feasible first campaign with matched controls, quantitative balances, transport checks, and independent repeats where they add confidence.
5. A fair benchmark, a meaningful success criterion, and a stop or pivot criterion. Numerical design targets are choices, not literature facts or predictions.
6. Separate judgments about the hypothesis, the feasibility and informativeness of experiments, and the chance of meaningful practical improvement. Explain each judgment without invented probabilities.
7. A credible path from mechanistic insight to catalytic or process value, including regeneration, resource use, productivity, and downstream constraints when relevant.

### Promotion threshold

Promote a candidate for substantial program development when it addresses an important catalytic limitation or scientific uncertainty, seeks a consequential contribution beyond established work, and offers a credible experimental route to that contribution. Document the closest prior art and competing efforts, the distinct knowledge or capability sought, and why available or obtainable materials, methods, and expertise make pursuit credible. Identify a feasible, informative first stage and the evidence that would justify expansion, redirection, or stopping. Assess hypothesis plausibility, experimental feasibility and informativeness, and practical benefit separately. Breadth across systems strengthens a case when supported but is not mandatory; an important advance in one system can suffice. Neither guaranteed improvement nor useful outcomes in every branch are required. Before each decisive test, specify the claims and decision criteria it can support; freeze prospective predictions before validation when predictive or transfer claims are central. Unresolved dependencies limit the initial investment and must remain visible in the recommendation.

Revised 2026-09-16 after an [independent review of selection criteria](reviews/program-selection-criteria-review.md). This replaces the categorical breadth and prediction threshold introduced after the [direction search](reviews/direction-search-outcome.md). Existing evidence, controls, and prior-art objections remain valid. Activity by other groups is a competition or collaboration consideration, not evidence that an unpublished answer exists. A small absolute performance change can be consequential; its value requires a fair account of output, resource use, durability, and implementation burden. Revising the threshold does not itself promote any candidate.

## Evidence

Label established evidence, inference, proposed hypothesis, and unvalidated expected outcome. Verify consequential numbers against the source, preferably its original artifact. Cite persistent source identifiers and local read notes with exact available page locators. A literature note is an aid; re-read source content when a recommendation depends on the claim.

Keep bibliographic inclusion, retrieval, reading, and synthesis eligibility distinct. Metadata-only papers constrain novelty searches but do not support detailed mechanistic claims. Never represent simulations as experimental results.

Source notes must describe the paper's own findings and conditions. An introductory claim, editorial summary or title from its reference list is not a result of that paper. Keep related years, preprints, main articles and supplements distinct; cite the actual source of each numerical claim. For comparisons, record the catalyst, feed, normalization, observation interval and any regeneration. Preserve discrepancies in original tables or definitions instead of silently selecting the favorable value.

A structural knowledge-base check does not validate scientific content. Review consequential notes against originals and independently sample repaired notes before accepting a large curation batch. If a sample reveals misattribution or an unsupported result, repair it and inspect related notes for the same failure mode.

A during-reaction titration in a packed bed counts titratable sites, not working sites. When the titrant is retained quantitatively as it enters the bed (no breakthrough before the rate falls) or covers sites without regard to their reactivity, the rate-versus-uptake curve is a straight line for any distribution of activity among titratable sites: its uptake intercept is the titratable total and its slope is the bed-average rate per titratable site (assuming 1:1 stoichiometry, differential conversion, an axially uniform bed, and rate proportional to uncovered active sites). A straight line is therefore consistent with, but not evidence of, uniform activity, and a curved line is not evidence of heterogeneous activity until integral conversion, a blurred front, drifting stoichiometry, and titrant-induced restructuring are excluded (Wrasman et al. 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.jcat.2024.115451, note the upstream-first coverage; [model](calculations/titration-front-regime.md)). Report such titrations as "titratable total and bed-average rate per titratable site"; state titrant stoichiometry, reversibility, and breakthrough; do not assign a per-site rate difference between samples to per-site chemistry unless the working fraction is shown unchanged. Support any working-site claim with an affinity contrast, a layered-versus-mixed bed or titrant-pressure invariance check, or a calibration on mixtures of known spectator fraction.

## Review

A fresh reviewer must challenge important recommendations independently. Preserve criticisms, revisions, rejected directions, and unresolved disagreements. Reassess a proposal when prior art or a confounder removes its claimed advantage; do not rescue it merely by adding more measurements.

Freeze a prospective prediction before observing its validation outcome. Separate model development from the held-out decision test, and keep a predefined observation horizon when the hypothesis concerns delayed changes. Equal initial performance does not exclude later divergence; an unresolved difference is not evidence of equivalence without adequate precision.
