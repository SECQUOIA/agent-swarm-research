# Research decision brief

Updated after the [new-upload assessment](reviews/current-uploads-assessment.md) and [fresh independent review](reviews/current-uploads-independent-review.md), 2026-09-17 UTC. These are research recommendations, not experimental results. Laboratory access, costs and unpublished work remain unconfirmed.

**Prioritize bounded feasibility work on physical water management; retain cyclic oxides as the strongest reserve. The current proposals are corrected for the newly available originals; idea generation remains closed at the user’s request.** Tungsten coordination and retention remains on exploratory hold until a consequential limitation and core chemical intervention are selected. None warrants an unrestricted campaign: begin with the measurements that determine whether a substantial contribution is experimentally reachable.

The initial renewed review favored tungsten. Recovery of stronger retained-W and leachate prior art, followed by a [critical test of its proposed ligand intervention](reviews/tungsten-intervention-review.md), changed that ordering. A further review of the concrete first tests and stronger process comparators moved cyclic oxides ahead of tungsten and prompted the bounded alternative searches now closed below. The portfolio review records those changes. The [earlier assessment](decision-brief-before-program-development.md) retained only bounded studies. This change follows new program development and independent criticism, together with a [review of the selection criteria](reviews/program-selection-criteria-review.md). A consequential scientific question and credible staged experiments can justify selecting a program before a successful pilot. Known promotion effects, descriptive characterization and unsupported process benefits remain insufficient.

This ranks the next justified research commitments under broadly comparable access. Water has the strongest existing functional motivation, but its route to a substantial mechanistic program remains conditional. An existing Ag, polymer or FT platform can change which experiment should run first. It does not by itself change which scientific thesis offers the strongest program.

## Why physical water management now leads

Here the reported functional effect is stronger evidence than the proposed transport explanation. A [2026 study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-76571-8) reports long-lived Co/SiO2 performance with a separately mixed hydrophobic additive, alongside an initial activity penalty. The source contains substantial controls, and its public peer review already discusses local water measurements. Repeating promotion or imaging heterogeneity would not constitute the proposed advance. The newly read Yang geometry study and Hanssen/Rothaemel water-history studies further occupy generic geometry, pretreatment and recovery claims. The remaining opportunity is a consequential, prospectively specified response to late additive addition after common conditioning; it is not the first observation that water history matters.

This direction currently has the clearer route from competing explanations to a discriminating geometry or exposure-history prediction. The opportunity is to predict useful protection from independently constrained storage, transport, working liquid and catalyst-state behavior. Faster washout can arise from less stored water without lower stationary exposure; less storage can also accelerate water ingress. The [reviewed limiting calculation](calculations/water-transport-limits.md) makes these distinctions explicit without claiming to estimate actual catalyst gradients. H2/CO transport, wax and irreversible exposure history must remain competing explanations.

An [independently checked integration](calculations/water-published-output.md) finds about 1.89 times the reported C5+ output proxy over the shared 25–585 h interval, or 1.80 after charging additive mass. The source reports matched bed volumes. This supports a meaningful existing benefit despite the initial activity penalty; it is not evidence of a new result or superiority to an optimized industrial reference. A [separate size analysis](calculations/water-size-discriminator.md) shows why two granule sizes alone cannot distinguish internal from external transport. Any stronger predictive claim needs independently constrained coefficients and a calibrated functional consequence.

A successful result would predict a useful packing, shaping or operating change, including additive volume, initial output loss, pressure drop and conditioning. A scientifically valuable negative could identify where physical mixing cannot substitute for water removal. **Confidence:** good support for the reported phenomenon; moderate feasibility of informative tests on an existing FT platform; lower confidence in uniquely resolving the active-site environment; plausible but unproven improvement beyond existing recipes.

The [reviewed first test](working/program-development/water-management-first-test.md) adds the modest PDVB dose after common conditioning and measures the response to one externally imposed water plateau and recovery. It tests an operating boundary and formulation-level function. Low overall conversion does not guarantee low water generation per granule, and a small bulk exposure difference could matter near a sharp damage threshold. These limits constrain the causal claim without eliminating the value of the comparison.

A [measurement-feasibility assessment](working/program-development/water-measurement-feasibility.md) recommends a hot water blank and representative live-packing humidity steps before a broad transport campaign. Its first decision is whether water inventory and exchange contain independent information. A successful outlet-response prediction alone would not identify water exposure at cobalt sites. Elgersma now supplies a concrete independent exchange-NMR precedent in water-filled millimetre pellets, but transfer to hot cobalt/polymer/wax remains unvalidated. Zhao’s separate 1:1 PDVB study has an unresolved mass-normalization issue: half the water uptake per gram can be the dilution-only result. Neither source removes the measurement gate.

## Why cyclic oxides are the reserve

The clearest question concerns a specific process assumption: the published long-duration carbonate/LSF experiment used Ar purges, whereas its process model used steam. Steam can alter carbonate/hydroxide speciation. The proposed [timing comparison](working/program-development/cyclic-oxide-causal-intervention.md) asks whether CO2 present during steam prevents loss or redistribution that the same dose supplied afterward cannot repair. It does not claim steam caused the decline observed under Ar. Fereres already reports that delayed CO2 stops further carbonate degradation without restoring the original state. Chacko’s now-read dissertation addresses carbonate/LSF regeneration and water generated during nominally dry CO2 treatment. Thus the remaining originality is limited to the specific steam duty, timing and persistent functional/material consequence, not the general prevention-versus-recovery concept.

End-of-steam specimens, segment-specific Li collection, a common measured carbonation/oxidation reset, and useful ethylene output make the alternatives testable. A retained functional difference after the reset would establish a timing consequence under that protocol; identifying LiOH evaporation requires further evidence. CO2 itself changes the carrier and inhibits the probe reaction, so washout, reset H2/H2O and carrier-state measurements are essential.

The first 10% steam/5% CO2 setting is diagnostic. It does not validate a steam-rich purge, and the useful protective CO2 dose is unknown. Expansion requires a measurable consequence under the intended gas/solid duty and an advantage over ordinary recovery or makeup after counting gas handling and lost production time. **Confidence:** moderate in the speciation perturbation; low in consequential Li loss from this coating; moderate in an informative timing comparison if the material balance resolves it; uncertain practical improvement.

The [prevention-versus-recovery calculation](calculations/cyclic-oxide-prevention-value.md) makes the burden concrete: completely restoring the documented Ar-aged C2+ yield would allow only about two extra minutes per 16-minute cycle before losing the output gain. This is an illustrative bound for that measured decline, not a steam-damage prediction. CO2 circulation and washout could also be substantial.

## Why tungsten is on hold

The central chemical tension is substantive: the sugar must coordinate to W to cleave, but ligand exchange may also remove W from its support or hold it in an unproductive complex. Understanding how to separate these consequences could change catalyst architecture, allowable feed/product composition or recovery practice. It connects Gounder's Lewis-acid and sugar-kinetics interests with Iglesia's supported-oxide, adsorption and mechanistic work.

The relevant advance is **a causal, predictive coordination–retention relationship**. The now-read [2020 Ti–O–W main article](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssuschemeng.0c00836) strengthens the seven-cycle benchmark but corrects the earlier leachate interpretation: Ni/M alone gives 27.1% EG, compared with 29.5% for Ni/M plus preleachate. That does not establish a substantial leachate-derived contribution. Its low liquid W concentrations also need inventory accounting, and the methods/Table 1 use 40 mL while Figure 3 says 100 mL. The [new tungsten audit](reviews/current-uploads-tungsten-audit.md) preserves these limits and the conditional cumulative-loss calculation. Zhang’s W20O58 main reports combined glycol yield declining from 70.4% to 61.4% across five cycles; it must not be summarized as invariant yield. These are concrete comparator qualifications, not evidence of a new successful intervention.

The first experiments should establish what can be learned from hot W export and transferred catalytic activity. Positive downstream activity establishes exported function. A negative result can reflect rapid decay, and persistent upstream activity after washout can arise from a solid continuously supplying short-lived mobile species. Preserve an unresolved or mixed mechanism when needed. Low external loss can still be practically useful without proving that each active W atom remains permanently attached.

A core causal intervention is still missing. The first attempted glucose/mannose contrast is now only a conditional probe: published epimerization and cleavage complicate interpretation, early carbon recovery is problematic, and the soluble evidence does not establish an extraction direction. The [fresh intervention review](reviews/tungsten-intervention-review.md) is a substantive reservation, not a request for prior experimental success. A subsequent intervention must change a chemically specified ligand or anchoring variable and predict both cleavage and W export under an unmeasured condition. A ligand-dependent loss of function, or a supported architecture that fails for an identified coordination reason, can be valuable negative knowledge. Repeated support screening without that chemical connection would not fulfill the program.

Practical success requires a better sustained yield–productivity–resource balance against strong retained-W and soluble-W alternatives. [Process accounting](calculations/tungsten-process-burden.md) separates gross export, inventory, fresh makeup, recovered function and recovery duty. Existing patents disclose low-W operation, recovery and W-feed cycling. A [newly audited working example](calculations/tungsten-ash-recovery-comparator.md) actually recovers W from model ash and tests it in a product-rich feed. It strengthens the soluble-recovery comparator, despite incomplete whole-process recovery and an internal W-concentration discrepancy. Other inspected patents lack working examples for their asserted ranges. None establishes plant economics.

**Confidence:** strong rationale for consequential coordination chemistry; moderate confidence that calibrated initial experiments will give useful bounds; moderate to uncertain confidence in a transferable chemical rule; uncertain practical gain. Pressure-rated aqueous flow, carbonyl recovery and ICP access are immediate dependencies. Wet-state spectroscopy is a later requirement for atomic structural claims, not a prerequisite for the first mass/function result.

## Existing studies and what changes next

[Ni-dependent retention on Ag](programs/ag-selective-oxygen-use.md) remains a credible bounded study on a functioning platform. Its important application does not remove the close historical Ni-retention and modern chloride-operation precedents. It may be the best immediate experiment without being the strongest basis for a new substantial program. For polymer, the clean-feed limitation now comes before the conditional ketone-exposure branch. The [liquid Ti-zeolite solvent screen](working/program-development/liquid-zeolite.md) remains a conditional test; zirconia, CHA and other earlier diagnostics retain their documented limits. Their [historical status and negative screens](status-before-program-development.md) are preserved.

The final experimental decisions are specific. For water, determine whether accessible independent measurements can support a consequential exposure-history or geometry prediction; more outlet curves alone may not identify it. For cyclic oxides, test whether realistic steam exposure creates a measurable coating change with useful prevention-versus-recovery consequences. The two focused alternative searches are complete: [formaldehyde/Mo retention](working/program-development/alternative-oxidation-search.md) and [lactate dehydration](working/program-development/alternative-acidbase-search.md). Neither produced a stronger candidate. The former has unusually direct pellet-scale negative evidence; the latter overlaps disclosed alcohol/amine work and strong amine-free formulations. The [fluorochemical steam-scheduling screen](working/program-development/fluorochemical-program-search.md), already underway when idea generation closed, also ended without promotion because the examined intervention is directly disclosed. These are narrow negative screens, not evidence that the fields are exhausted. Actual laboratory access remains unverified.

Two reserve interventions now have concrete, independently reviewed tests. [CO2 timing during steam exposure](working/program-development/cyclic-oxide-causal-intervention.md) tests whether prevention succeeds where later carbonate restoration fails; it addresses an unvalidated steam-process translation and cannot explain the original decline under Ar. [Ethylene pressure before Na makeup](working/program-development/polymer-causal-intervention.md) tests a modest operating alternative, with complete-charge comparisons preferred when gas transients cannot be resolved. Neither is promoted solely because a test can be specified; both still need a meaningful cumulative-output or resource-use consequence.

The [closeout review of tungsten, polymer and liquid zeolites](reviews/current-programs-closure-review.md) confirms their finite scope. Polymer pressure and matched-feed tests are alternatives for the first new response, not mandatory parallel campaigns. A model that already explains the liquid-zeolite trajectory can close that branch with a useful negative result. The [new polymer/liquid audit](reviews/current-uploads-polymer-liquid-audit.md) adds material corrections: use Chen’s final finite-isomerization model, validate quench/recovery before interpreting polymer unsaturation, credit Torres’s product-induced solvent spectra, and establish component-model adequacy before the independent integral test.
