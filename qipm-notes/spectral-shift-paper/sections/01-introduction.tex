\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
A positive semidefinite contraction $H$ and its complement $I-H$ have equally simple classical descriptions. Their quantum access costs can be very different. A block encoding supplies $H$ as a designated block of a unitary. A coherent linear combination with the identity immediately supplies $(I-H)/2$, but an algorithm that requires $I-H$ itself cannot discard the factor of two. Near a small eigenvalue of $H$, the desired complement eigenvalue approaches the boundary of the unit disk. Global unitarity then restricts how accurately a finite-query circuit can follow that eigenvalue on a whole interval.

This normalization issue already appears explicitly in Orsucci and Dunjko's positive-definite linear-system algorithms \cite[Section 4.3]{orsucci2021}. They require a normalized encoding of $I-\eta A$, explain the limitation of generic linear-combination constructions, and give specialized constructions under stronger access assumptions. We quantify the conversion cost from a plain block encoding when the spectrum has a shrinking low interval and a separated high band. The question is about a reusable coherent operator, so learning an instance and preparing one output state is a different task.

\subsection{Problem and results}
Fix a low-band ratio $\rho>1$ and a high-band endpoint $0<c\leq1$. Let $\delta>0$ tend to zero with $\rho\delta<c$, and suppose
\[
 \spec(H)\subseteq[\delta,\rho\delta]\cup[c,1].
\]
We seek an output unitary whose designated block approximates $I-H$ in operator norm to error $\eta$, with normalization exactly one. Let $Q(\delta,\eta;\rho,c)$ be the minimum worst-case number of controlled calls to the input block encoding or its inverse. The converter must work for every matrix and every unitary completion consistent with the promise; its gates may depend on the public parameters, but not on the unknown matrix. The count is the cost of applying the output conversion circuit; reusing it requires its oracle calls again. Bounded-query adaptive algorithms are included by deferring measurements. Section~\ref{sec:model} states the complete contract.

For fixed relative accuracy $K>0$, meaning $\eta=K\delta$, the optimal query exponent changes discretely with $K$. The transition values are
\[
 G_r(\rho)=\inf_{\substack{P\in\R[y],\ \deg P\leq2r\\ P\geq0\text{ on }\R}}
                \max_{1\leq y\leq\rho}|P(y)-y|,
 \qquad r=0,1,2,\ldots.
\]
Our first main result computes these thresholds explicitly and proves the full query staircase. If $\ell=\min\{r:G_r\leq K\}\geq1$, then
\[
 Q(\delta,K\delta;\rho,c)=\Theta(\delta^{-1+1/(2\ell)}).
\]
The constants here can depend on the fixed parameters. Every equality $K=G_\ell$ belongs to the cheaper tier. The coarse tier $K\geq G_0=(\rho-1)/2$ costs $\Theta(\log(1/\delta))$ when $c<1$, and $\Theta(1)$ when the high band is the singleton $\{1\}$. Theorems~\ref{thm:staircase} and~\ref{thm:thresholds} give the precise statements. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} illustrates the exponent transitions. In contrast to a continuum, suitable known two-point spectra admit an exact two-query conversion (Proposition~\ref{prop:two-point}). No finite-query converter is exact on a nonempty interval at normalization below two (Proposition~\ref{prop:no-exact}).

The second result measures the cost of a parity restriction. A single real definite-parity quantum singular value transformation (QSVT) has a bounded even or odd polynomial as its transformed block. The even class has its own optimal staircase, governed by the same approximation problem with $P$ also required to be even; denote its thresholds by $F_r$. We prove the exact plateau
\[
 F_1(\rho)=F_2(\rho)=\frac{(\rho-1)^2}{8(\rho+1)}.
\]
Thus the even staircase skips the exponent-$3/4$ tier. At $\rho=2$ and $\eta=\delta/32$, a globally positive degree-six witness gives matching orders $\Theta(\delta^{-5/6})$ for the even class and $\Theta(\delta^{-1/2})$ for unrestricted coherent conversion. The witness bounds $F_3(2)$ from above; it does not compute it. Odd transforms have the matched order $\Theta(\delta^{-1}\log(1/\delta))$ at every fixed $K>0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:even-staircase} and Proposition~\ref{prop:odd-optimal}). These are restrictions on a single definite-parity transform, not on arbitrary compositions of QSVT circuits. Generalized quantum signal processing (GQSP) supplies the unrestricted upper bounds without a normalization loss.

Third, we control joint limits in $\delta$ and accuracy. For every fixed $\beta>0$, uniformly over $0<K\leq\delta^\beta$,
\[
 Q(\delta,K\delta;\rho,c)
 =\Theta_{\rho,\beta}\bigl(\delta^{-1}\log(1/K)\bigr).
\]
For the full spectral interval $[\delta,1]$, the corresponding order is
$\Theta_\beta(\delta^{-1}\log(1/\eta))$ when $0<\eta\leq\delta^{1+\beta}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:joint-matched}). In the intermediate regime $K\to0$ with $\log(1/K)=o(\log(1/\delta))$, let $r=\min\{j:G_j\leq K\}$. Uniform lower and upper bounds have the same power $\delta^{-1+1/(2r)}$ and prefactors $r(G_{r-1}-K)^{1/r}$ and $r^3$, respectively. If $K\leq(1-\gamma)G_{r-1}$ for fixed $\gamma\in(0,1)$, their ratio is at most $O(r^2)=O(\log(1/K)^2)$, including $K=G_r$ for large $r$. Close to the upper tier boundary the displayed margin factor remains necessary in our bound. We do not determine a uniform multiplicative optimum in that regime (Corollary~\ref{cor:joint-near-match}).

Finally, we realize the access distinction in a three-variable, two-equality sparse linear program (LP) at an exactly central primal--dual point. Its normal matrix is $H_t=A_tA_t^T$, with hidden $t\in[\delta,\rho\delta]$ and condition number $\Theta(\delta^{-1})$. Preparing the normalized \emph{dual} Newton direction to a fixed trace error costs $\Theta(\delta^{-1})$ queries from the specified normal-matrix oracle and $\Theta(\delta^{-1/2})$ from the specified rectangular-factor oracle, even after counting access to the right-side preparation unitary. Separately, a matrix-only reusable complement compiler inherits every positive tier and the joint high-accuracy law, whereas factor access gives an exact two-query complement. Public spectral projectors make the LP compiler's coarse tier zero-query (Theorems~\ref{thm:lp-state} and~\ref{thm:lp-compiler}). The example concerns these analog access contracts: its primal predictor is public, and digital entry access reveals the hidden parameter. It is not a lower bound for solving the LP or for an entire quantum interior-point method.

\subsection{Related work and contribution scope}
Quantum signal processing and QSVT provide the bounded-polynomial implementation tools used throughout this paper. Gily\'en, Su, Low, and Wiebe \cite{gilyen2019} establish general singular-value transformations, bounded sign approximation, uniform amplification, and pairwise eigenvalue-transformation lower bounds. Low and Chuang \cite{low2017} develop uniform spectral amplification and its normalization accounting. Motlagh and Wiebe's GQSP \cite{motlagh2024} removes the usual definite-parity restriction through bounded unit-circle polynomial synthesis. We use these implementation results, including a Laurent-polynomial reduction that works for arbitrary input completions. Neither parity removal nor the generic amplification upper bound is a contribution here. Classical phase computation and finite-precision circuit synthesis are separate resource questions; Haah \cite{haah2019} gives a rigorous algorithmic treatment of such decomposition costs. Our complexity measure counts oracle queries and allows exact oracle-independent gates.

The relationship between analog quantum queries and trigonometric polynomials is established prior work. Bessen \cite{bessen2004} uses it to compare real-valued query models, and Laneve \cite{laneve2026} characterizes QSP using state conversion and adversary feasibility. Our scalar-oracle reduction is an instance of this general polynomial method. The additional step is to combine global unitarity with a shrinking correctness interval: rescaled low-degree Taylor limits of the output defect must be nonnegative on the entire real line. This identifies the specific extremal problem $G_r$ and produces lower bounds for general coherent circuits, including circuits outside a single QSP sequence. The pairwise square-root obstruction alone does not give the higher tiers.

Constrained polynomial approximation also has a long history. Taylor \cite{taylor1968} studies restricted ranges, Lewis \cite{lewis1973} treats convex constraints, and Campos-Pinto, Charles, and Despr\'es \cite{campos2019} develop algorithms for positive polynomial approximation. Fawzi, Saunderson, and Parrilo \cite[Sections 3.1--3.2 and Appendix A, Lemma 1]{fawzi2017} study globally nonnegative interpolation of affine targets at finite nodes and use Chebyshev supporting tangents. Our threshold problem instead minimizes uniform error on $[1,\rho]$ under global nonnegativity on $\R$ and determines shrinking-window query costs. Its solution uses the classical exterior Chebyshev inequality, as stated for example by Bos, Ma'u, and Waldron \cite[Proposition 2.1]{bos2020}; our quantitative lower bounds also use classical Fej\'er--Riesz factorization. The odd upper bound uses established sign approximants from QSVT, with approximation-theory antecedents in Eremenko and Yuditskii \cite{eremenko2007,eremenko2011}. The gap-times-log-error behavior of sign approximation is therefore not new. Here it yields a matched comparison for the shrinking-window shift task, with an elementary lower bound that uses correctness only on the positive low interval.

Sarkar and Yoder \cite{sarkar2023} prove density results for QSP-related polynomial families with range constraints both inside and outside the approximation interval, under necessary endpoint matching conditions. They also study step-function approximation. Their qualitative approximation results provide further context for global constraints; our question asks for quantitative degree thresholds and coherent-query costs as a correctness interval shrinks.

A particularly close recent comparison is Dong, Larsen, Lin, and Sarkar \cite{dong2026}. They formulate prescribed-parity QSP design as minimax fitting on a subset subject to a global modulus bound, study Remez methods with active constraints, and introduce nonlinear Fourier retraction to restore feasibility without increasing the degree. Their work makes clear why satisfaction of bounds at sampled points is insufficient, especially near the boundary of the feasible set. We address an analytic asymptotic question for one target and spectral promise: exact limiting thresholds, optimal all-circuit query powers, and constructions that attain the threshold error itself. The global contractivity and contact preservation of our constructions are proved on continuous domains; numerical plots are only illustrations. We do not propose a general minimax solver or a phase-synthesis algorithm.

The closest direct motivation remains Orsucci and Dunjko \cite{orsucci2021}. Their Section 4.3 already formulates normalized $I-\eta A$ construction, invokes QSVT lower bounds against generic amplification, and gives sparse-value constructions for diagonally dominant matrices and local positive semidefinite sums. Their factor-based linear-system algorithms also explain square-root condition-number improvements and the associated support-overlap requirement. Our plain-block analysis does not resolve their separate general sparse-value construction question. Factor and sum-of-squares access are likewise central in King et al.'s spectral amplification \cite{king2026}. Somma and de Wolf \cite{somma2026} prove joint lower bounds for guided ground-state energy estimation and state preparation, including sum-of-squares access, with guide-overlap and dimension assumptions. Those output tasks and hypotheses differ from reusable complement conversion; their bounds are not transferred here. The LP contribution is the explicit exact-central realization, its fully specified oracles, and the matched dual-state and compiler comparisons. Its state upper bounds use standard amplitude estimation \cite{brassard2002}.

Relative to these works, our contributions are the explicit globally nonnegative shift thresholds and their complete fixed-accuracy coherent-query staircase; the even-threshold plateau and sharp concrete parity separation; uniform continuum lower bounds matching high-accuracy amplification and quantifying the remaining intermediate gap; and the exact-central LP realization under matched access contracts. To our knowledge, this combination of exact thresholds, equality-attaining constructions, and optimal shift-query exponents has not appeared in the cited literature. This claim concerns the stated problem and theorems, not the underlying synthesis, approximation, or factor-access techniques.

\subsection{Proof structure and roadmap}
Section~\ref{sec:model} connects block encodings to bounded polynomials and represents scalar-query circuits by trigonometric polynomials. Section~\ref{sec:exact} separates finite known supports from interval promises. In Section~\ref{sec:fixed}, global unitarity forces a nonnegative Taylor limit; exterior Chebyshev growth computes its best approximation error; and a polynomial gate pinned at the extremal contacts turns that limiting solution into a globally contractive converter without exceeding the threshold error. Section~\ref{sec:joint} replaces fixed-order compactness by uniform Taylor estimates and an independent Fej\'er--Riesz argument, then controls the pinned construction for growing order. Section~\ref{sec:lp} gives the LP realization and separates state preparation from coherent compilation. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} states the remaining questions, and Appendix~\ref{app:reproduce} describes the reproducible figures and source build.
