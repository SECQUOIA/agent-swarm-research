\section{An exact-central sparse LP and its oracle separation}\label{sec:lp}
We realize the spectral family in an actual primal--dual Newton system. The state task below outputs the normalized dual Newton direction; it allows measurements and discards auxiliary registers. The compiler task instead outputs a reusable coherent block encoding. Their input contracts are stated separately.

\subsection{The linear program and the predictor equation}
Fix $\rho>1$ and a public $\delta>0$ so small that $\delta<1/8$ and $\rho\delta<1/4$. The parameter $t\in[\delta,\rho\delta]$ is hidden. Define
\begin{align*}
 u_\pm&=(1,\pm1)^T/\sqrt2,& q_+&=(1,1,2)^T/\sqrt6,\\
 v_0&=(1,-1,0)^T/\sqrt2,& k&=(1,1,-1)^T/\sqrt3,\\
 \sin\theta&=2\sqrt{2\delta},\quad 0<\theta<\pi/2,&
 q_-&=\cos\theta\,v_0+\sin\theta\,k,\\
 &&q_0&=-\sin\theta\,v_0+\cos\theta\,k.
\end{align*}
The triples $q_+,q_-,q_0$ and the pair $u_+,u_-$ are orthonormal. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp-family}
 A_t=u_+q_+^T+\sqrt t\,u_-q_-^T,\qquad
 H_t=A_tA_t^T=P_++tP_-,\qquad P_\pm=u_\pm u_\pm^T.
\end{equation}
Thus $\norm{A_t}=\norm{H_t}=1$, $A_t$ has rank two, and $\kappa(H_t)=1/t=\Theta_\rho(\delta^{-1})$. Every row of $A_t$ has at most three nonzero entries and every column at most two; its entries have absolute value at most one. Sparsity here is a constant degree property of this fixed-size example, not an asymptotic dimension claim.

\begin{proposition}[A genuine central Newton system]\label{prop:lp-center}
The three-variable, two-equality linear program
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp-primal}
 \min\ \mathbf1^Tx\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad
 A_tx=b_t:=A_t\mathbf1,\quad x\geq0
\end{equation}
has a nontrivial compact feasible segment and a nonconstant objective on that segment. The point $x=s=\mathbf1$, $y=0$ is exactly central with barrier parameter $\mu=1$. Its affine-scaling predictor has normal equation and solution
\begin{align}
 H_t\Delta y&=b_t=a\bigl(u_++\sqrt{\delta t}\,u_-\bigr),
 &a&=2\sqrt{2/3},\label{eq:lp-normal}\\
 \Delta y&=a\bigl(u_++\sqrt{\delta/t}\,u_-\bigr).
 \label{eq:lp-direction}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The feasible affine line is $\mathbf1+\lambda q_0$. Since $q_0$ is nonzero and orthogonal to the strictly positive vector $q_+$, it has both positive and negative entries. The inequalities $1+\lambda(q_0)_j\geq0$ therefore bound $\lambda$ on both sides and admit an open neighborhood of zero. Their intersection is a nontrivial compact interval. Moreover,
\[
 \mathbf1^Tq_0=\cos\theta/\sqrt3>0,
\]
so the objective varies strictly along this line.
For the dual convention $A_t^Ty+s=\mathbf1$, centrality is the system
$A_tx=b_t$, $A_t^Ty+s=\mathbf1$, $x_js_j=\mu$, $x,s>0$.
The stated point satisfies it at $\mu=1$. Linearizing with target complementarity zero gives exactly
\[
 A_t\Delta x=0,\qquad A_t^T\Delta y+\Delta s=0,
 \qquad\Delta x+\Delta s=-\mathbf1.
\]
Eliminating $\Delta x,\Delta s$ yields $H_t\Delta y=A_t\mathbf1$. Finally,
$q_+^T\mathbf1=a$ and $q_-^T\mathbf1=a\sqrt\delta$, proving both formulas.
\end{proof}
The parameter $t$ changes the representation of the equalities, not the feasible segment: the row space of $A_t$ is fixed. In fact $\Delta x=-(\cos\theta/\sqrt3)q_0$ is independent of $t$, whereas the dual direction \eqref{eq:lp-direction} is not. This distinction matters for interpreting the state lower bound.

\subsection{Complete oracle contracts}
All spaces and basis changes below are public, including their dependence on $\delta$. No numerical value of $t$, $A_t$, $b_t$, or their hidden coefficients is supplied as classical data. In particular, the exact norm $\norm{b_t}=a\sqrt{1+\delta t}$ is not supplied: it would reveal $t$. The objective, central point, formulas, $\delta$, and $\rho$ are public. Each controlled call to a supplied oracle or its inverse costs one query; coherent choices of query type are allowed and counted. Oracle-independent gates and classical computation are free, as in the rest of the paper.

For normal-matrix access supply the following full unitary on $\C^2\otimes\C^2$, with the first factor the block flag:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp-H-oracle}
 U_H(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
 H_t&\sqrt{I-H_t^2}\\
 \sqrt{I-H_t^2}&-H_t
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Its $|0\rangle$ compression is exactly $H_t$. In the $u_+$ and $u_-$ sectors its blocks are respectively $R(1)$ and $R(t)$, where
\[
 R(z)=\begin{pmatrix}z&\sqrt{1-z^2}\\\sqrt{1-z^2}&-z\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For rectangular-factor access let $\mathcal R=\C^2$ be the row-index output space and $\mathcal C=\C^3$ the column-index input space, and supply the full unitary on $\mathcal R\oplus\mathcal C$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp-A-oracle}
 U_A(t)=\begin{pmatrix}
 \sqrt{I_{\mathcal R}-A_tA_t^T}&A_t\\
 A_t^T&-\sqrt{I_{\mathcal C}-A_t^TA_t}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The column-to-row off-diagonal block is $A_t$. More precisely, for the canonical embeddings $J_{\mathcal R},J_{\mathcal C}$,
$J_{\mathcal R}^*U_A(t)J_{\mathcal C}=A_t$.
On each ordered pair $(u_j,q_j)$ this unitary is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\sqrt{1-\sigma_j^2}&\sigma_j\\\sigma_j&-\sqrt{1-\sigma_j^2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
where $\sigma_+=1$ and $\sigma_-=\sqrt t$; it acts as $-1$ on $q_0$. These blocks verify unitarity and specify the completion on the entire space. Fixed identity padding, if needed for a qubit implementation, is public and independent of $t$.

Both state-access models also supply the right-side preparation unitary $B_t$ on $\mathcal R$. In the ordered basis $(u_+,u_-)$ its full matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp-B-oracle}
 B_t=\frac1{\sqrt{1+\delta t}}
 \begin{pmatrix}1&-\sqrt{\delta t}\\\sqrt{\delta t}&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad B_tu_+=b_t/\norm{b_t}.
\end{equation}
Calls to $B_t$ and $B_t^*$, including controlled calls, are counted. Fixing the complete unitaries is essential: unspecified completion blocks could reveal $t$ through an unrelated register.

\subsection{Matching query bounds for the Newton state}
Write $D(\omega,\tau)=\tfrac12\norm{\omega-\tau}_1$ for trace distance and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp-target}
 |\psi_t\rangle=
 \frac{u_++\sqrt{\delta/t}\,u_-}{\sqrt{1+\delta/t}},\qquad
 d_\rho=\frac{\sqrt\rho-1}{\sqrt{2(\rho+1)}}.
\end{equation}
The task is to output a density operator $\omega_t$ with
$D(\omega_t,|\psi_t\rangle\langle\psi_t|)\leq\epsilon_0$ for every $t$ in the interval, using a bounded worst-case number of queries. The density operator includes all measurement randomness; success is not defined by conditioning on a favorable event.

\begin{theorem}[Normal-matrix versus factor state access]\label{thm:lp-state}
For each fixed $\rho>1$ and $0<\epsilon_0<d_\rho/4$, the optimal worst-case total query counts in the two state-access models are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp-state-complexity}
 Q_H^{\rm state}(\delta,\epsilon_0)
 =\Theta_{\rho,\epsilon_0}(\delta^{-1}),\qquad
 Q_A^{\rm state}(\delta,\epsilon_0)
 =\Theta_{\rho,\epsilon_0}(\delta^{-1/2}).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, their orders are $\kappa(H_t)$ and $\sqrt{\kappa(H_t)}$, uniformly up to $\rho$-dependent constants on the family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the lower bounds, the two endpoint target states have distance exactly $d_\rho$: their overlap is $(\sqrt\rho+1)/\sqrt{2(\rho+1)}$. Correct output states thus have distance at least $d_\rho-2\epsilon_0>d_\rho/2$.
For $0\leq x,y\leq1/2$,
\[
 \norm{R(x)-R(y)}
 =2\left|\sin\frac{\arcsin x-\arcsin y}{2}\right|
 \leq2|x-y|.
\]
The factor blocks obey the same bound in their singular-value parameter, by a fixed change of basis. At the endpoints, therefore,
\begin{align*}
 \norm{U_H(\rho\delta)-U_H(\delta)}&\leq2(\rho-1)\delta,\\
 \norm{U_A(\rho\delta)-U_A(\delta)}&\leq2(\sqrt\rho-1)\sqrt\delta.
\end{align*}
The preparation unitary is a rotation through angle $\arctan\sqrt{\delta t}$, so
\[
 \norm{B_{\rho\delta}-B_\delta}
 \leq(\sqrt\rho-1)\delta.
\]
These bounds are unchanged for inverses and controlled calls. Purify the algorithm, defer its measurements, and replace queries one at a time between the two endpoint computations. The resulting vector distance is at most the sum of the per-query operator distances. Trace distance cannot increase on discarding registers and is at most this vector distance. Consequently $T$ total queries give output distance at most $C_\rho T\delta$ in the normal model and $C_\rho T\sqrt\delta$ in the factor model. Comparing with $d_\rho/2$ proves both lower bounds, including algorithms using the right-side oracle.

For the upper bounds we estimate the single hidden parameter. Standard amplitude estimation \cite[Theorem 12]{brassard2002}, applied to a unitary preparation with success probability $a_0$, uses $O(M)$ calls to that preparation and its inverse and returns $\widehat a\in[0,1]$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp-BHMT}
 |\widehat a-a_0|\leq
 \frac{2\pi\sqrt{a_0(1-a_0)}}{M}+\frac{\pi^2}{M^2}
\end{equation}
with probability at least $8/\pi^2>1/2$. Reflections about the public initial states and success subspaces are free; reflecting about a prepared state uses the counted preparation and inverse calls.
In the normal model, apply $U_H(t)$ to $|0\rangle u_-$ and declare flag zero successful; then $a_0=t^2$. Take $M\geq C/\delta$ and define $\widehat t$ by clipping $\sqrt{\widehat a}$ to $[\delta,\rho\delta]$. On the event \eqref{eq:lp-BHMT}, clipping cannot increase its distance to $t$, and
\[
 |\widehat t-t|\leq
 \frac{|\widehat a-t^2|}{t}
 \leq\delta\left(\frac{2\pi}{C}+\frac{\pi^2}{C^2}\right).
\]
In the factor model, apply $U_A(t)$ to $J_{\mathcal C}q_-$ and declare the output subspace $\mathcal R$ successful; then $a_0=t$. Taking $M\geq C/\sqrt\delta$ and clipping $\widehat a$ instead gives
\[
 |\widehat t-t|\leq
 \delta\left(\frac{2\pi\sqrt\rho}{C}+\frac{\pi^2}{C^2}\right)
\]
on the same success event. Choose $C=C_{\rho,\epsilon_0}$ so these bounds are at most $\epsilon_0\delta$. An odd, fixed number of independent repetitions and their median make the failure probability at most $\epsilon_0/2$; the number of repetitions depends only on $\epsilon_0$.

Given the resulting estimate, prepare $|\psi_{\widehat t}\rangle$ using a rotation in the known basis. Its angle is $\varphi(t)=\arctan\sqrt{\delta/t}$, with
\[
 |\varphi'(t)|=\frac{\sqrt\delta}{2\sqrt t(t+\delta)}
 \leq\frac1{4\delta}\qquad(t\geq\delta).
\]
On successful estimation the pure-state trace error is thus at most $\epsilon_0/4$. Convexity of trace distance bounds the error of the average output by $\epsilon_0/4+\epsilon_0/2<\epsilon_0$, including all failure events. These algorithms use no calls to $B_t$, and use $O_{\rho,\epsilon_0}(\delta^{-1})$ or $O_{\rho,\epsilon_0}(\delta^{-1/2})$ matrix or factor queries, respectively. Rounding $M$ upward to a power of two changes only a constant factor. This proves the matching upper bounds.
\end{proof}
The identity $\Delta y=(A_t^T)^+\mathbf1$ also follows directly from the singular vectors, and the squared overlap of $\mathbf1/\sqrt3$ with $\operatorname{span}\{q_+,q_-\}$ is $(8/9)(1+\delta)$. It is bounded away from zero. Factor-based improvements and the role of this support overlap are established in \citet[Sections 1.4 and 5.4--5.5]{orsucci2021}. Here the explicit estimation argument avoids generic solver overhead and gives the exact orders at fixed output error. It is a state-output algorithm, not a reusable coherent converter.

\subsection{The complement compiler on the LP family}
Let $Q_H^{\rm LP}(\delta,\eta)$ be the minimum query count for a single reusable converter that, for every $t\in[\delta,\rho\delta]$, has a designated block within $\eta$ of
\[
 I-H_t=(1-t)P_-.
\]
For this task the sole input oracle is \eqref{eq:lp-H-oracle}; the preparation oracle $B_t$ is not supplied. All other data remain public. Define $Q_A^{\rm LP}$ analogously with \eqref{eq:lp-A-oracle} as its sole oracle and with output on $\mathcal R$.

\begin{theorem}[Compiler transfer and factor bypass]\label{thm:lp-compiler}
For fixed $K>0$ and $\ell=\min\{j\geq0:G_j(\rho)\leq K\}$, the normal-matrix compiler has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp-staircase}
 Q_H^{\rm LP}(\delta,K\delta)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&\ell=0,\\
 \Theta_{\rho,K,\ell}(\delta^{-1+1/(2\ell)}),&\ell\geq1,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
for sufficiently small $\delta$. Threshold equalities belong to the cheaper tier. For every fixed $\beta>0$, uniformly for $0<\eta\leq\delta^{1+\beta}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lp-joint}
 Q_H^{\rm LP}(\delta,\eta)
 =\Theta_{\rho,\beta}\bigl(\delta^{-1}\log(1/\eta)\bigr)
 =\Theta_{\rho,\beta}\bigl(\kappa(H_t)\log(1/\eta)\bigr).
\end{equation}
In contrast, two factor queries implement $I-A_tA_t^T$ exactly with normalization one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the public eigenbasis, the normal oracle is the direct sum of a fixed block $R(1)$ and $R(t)$. Replace the latter by
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\sin\tau&\cos\tau\\\cos\tau&-\sin\tau\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ for every real $\tau$. On $\tau=\arcsin t$ it is the actual oracle. Every matrix element of a $T$-query converter is now a bounded trigonometric polynomial of degree at most $T$, even if its gates mix the two eigenspaces. Its designated $u_-$ diagonal entry approximates $1-\sin\tau$ on the promised interval. Thus every lower bound that uses only the low interval in Sections~\ref{sec:fixed}--\ref{sec:joint} applies without change. In particular, Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy} gives all positive-index lower bounds, and Theorem~\ref{thm:joint-exterior} (or Theorem~\ref{thm:FR-all}) gives the high-accuracy lower bound. No right-side oracle is included in this reduction: its entries would not obey the same degree induction.

The polynomial upper constructions already apply to $H_t$ with the singleton high band $c=1$. They prove the upper half of every positive tier and \eqref{eq:lp-joint}, using $K=\eta/\delta$ and
$\log(\delta/\eta)=\Theta_\beta(\log(1/\eta))$ in the stated regime. For the coarse tier the public operator
$(1-m_\rho\delta)P_-$ is a contraction and differs from $(1-t)P_-$ by at most $G_0\delta$. Its standard unitary dilation uses no oracle queries. The general $c=1$ problem of Theorem~\ref{thm:staircase} has a constant nonzero query cost because it does not supply the spectral projectors; the present fixed-eigenspace family permits this additional zero-query shortcut. There is no positive-width high interval in this example.

For factor access the real even polynomial $p(s)=1-s^2$ has degree two and lies in $[0,1]$ on $[-1,1]$. The even, left singular-vector version of QSVT \cite[Corollary 18, extended version]{gilyen2019}, applied with the public row and column projectors in \eqref{eq:lp-A-oracle}, therefore gives exactly
\[
 \sum_{j=\pm}(1-\sigma_j^2)u_ju_j^T
 =I_{\mathcal R}-A_tA_t^T
\]
as a unit-normalized block using two queries. The output acts on the two-dimensional left singular-vector space $\mathcal R$; the extra column null vector does not create an extra output eigenvalue. Equivalently, if $\Pi_{\mathcal C}=J_{\mathcal C}J_{\mathcal C}^*$, then
\[
 J_{\mathcal R}^*U_A(t)(I-\Pi_{\mathcal C})U_A(t)^*J_{\mathcal R}
 =I_{\mathcal R}-A_tA_t^T.
\]
A free block encoding of the public projector $I-\Pi_{\mathcal C}$ makes this an explicit two-query construction.
\end{proof}
The same scalar reduction transfers the finite-index and growing-index bounds of Section~\ref{sec:joint}, including their stated intermediate-accuracy limitations. In particular, error $o(\delta)$ implies $\Omega_{\rho,\varepsilon}(\delta^{-1+\varepsilon})$ queries for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, while zero error is impossible with finitely many normal-oracle queries on the continuum. If the particular $t$ were public instead, Proposition~\ref{prop:two-point} would give a two-query exact conversion for the known spectrum $\{t,1\}$ even with unknown eigenspaces; the present public eigenspaces would permit direct synthesis with zero queries. The uniform hidden-parameter contract is therefore essential.

\subsection{Access assumptions and scope}
The separation disappears under direct digital entry access. For example,
\[
 (H_t)_{11}=(1+t)/2,\qquad
 (A_t)_{11}=\frac1{\sqrt2}
 \left(\frac1{\sqrt6}+\sqrt t\,(q_-)_1\right),\qquad
 (q_-)_1=\frac{\cos\theta}{\sqrt2}+\frac{\sin\theta}{\sqrt3}>0.
\]
A single exact value query to either displayed entry reveals $t$. A digital query with sufficient precision similarly gives a constant-query approximation at fixed state error, with precision and arithmetic costs accounted for separately. Since the dimension and eigenbases are known, that value permits direct state preparation or synthesis of a complement dilation. This is consistent with the digital sparse-access constructions of \citet[Section 4.3]{orsucci2021}; indeed $H_t$ is strictly diagonally dominant, with diagonal $(1+t)/2$ and off-diagonal $(1-t)/2$.

There is also a one-query coherent linear-combination construction of a normalization-two encoding: compressing a control qubit prepared and unprepared in $|+\rangle$ around $\operatorname{diag}(I,-U_H(t))$ gives $(I-H_t)/2$. Requiring normalization exactly one is what excludes that shortcut. Orsucci and Dunjko already identify the construction of a normalized encoding of $I-\eta A$ as a substantive input requirement \cite[Section 4.3]{orsucci2021}; the present continuum lower bounds quantify that conversion task in the plain matrix-oracle model.

The conclusions are query separations for these specified analog oracles and output tasks. They neither lower-bound all algorithms for the LP nor imply an end-to-end lower bound for quantum interior-point methods. In particular, the primal predictor here is public, a solver need not compile a unit-normalized complement, and classical runtime or digital precision is not measured by the oracle query count. The contribution of the example is the explicit exact-central LP realization, the matched state-access comparison with counted right-side access, and the transfer of the complement hierarchy with its fixed-eigenspace coarse-tier correction.
