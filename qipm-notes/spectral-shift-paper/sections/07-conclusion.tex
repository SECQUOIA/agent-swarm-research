\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Unit normalization makes complement conversion sensitive to spectral support and requested accuracy. Known two-point spectra can admit exact constant-query conversion, while a low continuum forces a sequence of optimal query powers determined by globally nonnegative polynomial approximation. The Chebyshev thresholds and pinned constructions determine every fixed-relative-accuracy tier, including its boundary. The even nonnegative constraint changes this staircase: the exact plateau $F_1=F_2$ produces a concrete exponent-$5/6$ cost where unrestricted conversion has exponent $1/2$. At polynomial and higher relative accuracy, the coherent conversion cost is instead $\Theta(\delta^{-1}\log(1/K))$.

The main quantitative question left open is the multiplicative order in the intermediate joint regime. Away from upper tier boundaries the proved gap is at most quadratic in the threshold index; approaching those boundaries also requires control of the margin factor. Explicit formulas for higher even thresholds $F_r$ would further clarify the parity comparison. Another separate problem is to account for the precision and classical computation needed to synthesize the threshold-attaining circuits, particularly when no error margin is available.

The exact-central LP demonstrates why the input and output contracts must be kept explicit. A dual Newton state, a reusable complement block, and a digital matrix description are different resources even for a fixed sparse instance family. The separation here quantifies conversion from specified analog matrix access; deciding when a larger algorithm can avoid that conversion, or obtain stronger access efficiently, requires its own analysis.
