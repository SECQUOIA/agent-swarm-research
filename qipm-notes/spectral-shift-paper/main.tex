\begin{abstract}
We determine the oracle cost of converting a block encoding of a positive semidefinite contraction $H$ into a reusable encoding of $I-H$ with normalization exactly one. For spectra in $[\delta,\rho\delta]\cup[c,1]$, with fixed $\rho>1$ and $0<c\leq1$, globally nonnegative polynomial approximation gives explicit thresholds $G_r$. At error $K\delta$ with fixed $K>0$, the optimal query count is $\Theta(\delta^{-1+1/(2\ell)})$ for $\ell=\min\{r:G_r\leq K\}\geq1$, including threshold equality; we also determine the coarse tier. An even-polynomial threshold plateau yields a sharp $\Theta(\delta^{-5/6})$ versus $\Theta(\delta^{-1/2})$ separation from unrestricted conversion at a concrete accuracy, while odd transforms cost $\Theta(\delta^{-1}\log(1/\delta))$. Uniformly for $0<K\leq\delta^\beta$ with fixed $\beta>0$, the unrestricted cost is $\Theta(\delta^{-1}\log(1/K))$. In the intermediate joint regime we quantify a remaining multiplicative gap. The lower bounds cover general coherent oracle circuits, and the upper bounds preserve global contractivity. An exactly central sparse linear program realizes matched normal-matrix versus factor-access bounds for the dual Newton state and transfers the complement hierarchy, with a zero-query coarse exception. These are analog oracle separations, not lower bounds for solving the linear program.
\end{abstract}
