## Mathematical checks

- **Section 2, arbitrary completions and circuit model.** Hermitianizing an arbitrary input completion gives a Hermitian involution with compression exactly $H$. Its reflection walk has the displayed two-dimensional matrix, so the Laurent substitution implements (p$H$) after compression. Multiplication by $V^{-d}$ cancels the Laurent offset without rescaling. GQSP's bounded-unit-circle characterization supplies the claimed implementation; the real definite-parity QSVT statement also preserves normalization. The scalar lower reduction uses only matrix elements of a unitary circuit, not an unjustified assumption that every converter is a polynomial in $H$. Its global trigonometric bound survives arbitrary ancillas, inverse calls, and coherent controls. The restriction to a reusable unitary and bounded worst-case queries is explicit.

- **Section 3, exactness and normalization.** The quadratic's endpoint range and factored error are correct. The interval impossibility follows by trigonometric analytic continuation to the unpromised negative input; it does not require correctness there. I recomputed the pairwise complementary-amplitude bounds and the differentiated normalization-slack expression. The text correctly prevents interpretation of the latter as an attainable exact tradeoff below normalization two.

- **Section 4, thresholds and lower bounds.** Exterior Chebyshev growth gives the necessary threshold formula. The proposed extremizer is globally nonnegative: the exterior negative half-line follows from the maximizing ratio, while the potentially negative interior lobes are controlled by $G_r/h_\rho<1/(2r)^2$. The stationary point is unique because $z-T_n/T_n'$ increases strictly. The first-threshold formula and large-index constant agree with direct algebra and the substitution $t=a+1/n+O(n^{-2})$. The compactness proof correctly transfers nonnegativity to the limiting Taylor polynomial on every fixed real compact set, forcing its odd leading term to vanish. It proves an all-circuit lower bound with the claimed exponent.

- **Section 4, exact threshold attainment.** Pairing the shifted kernels produces an even algebraic polynomial. Its range, pinning, low leakage, and tail bounds hold continuously. The blend's contractivity is established independently of correctness. The signed-error argument uses the positive-error contact slack in the right direction; pinning removes overshoot at the contacts. The separate coarse constructions and high-interval interpolation obstruction correctly distinguish $c<1$ from $c=1$.

- **Section 4, parity.** The even minimizer exists and has a nonempty finite positive-contact set, so the same pinned construction attains equality. Nonnegativity on $v\geq0$ forces an admissible quadratic in $v=y^2$ to be convex; the chord argument therefore proves $F_2=F_1=E_1$. The degree-six witness is globally positive and its tail estimate is below $1/32$. The odd lower proof supplies the logarithm using only the short positive interval and $q(0)=0$; the normalized sign-based upper polynomial is bounded globally. Thus the three stated query orders at $\rho=2$, $K=1/32$ are matched for their expressly restricted classes.

- **Section 5, joint limits.** Retaining the sine Taylor polynomial avoids an accuracy floor when $K\ll\delta^2$. The remainder-to-$K$ comparison is uniform in arbitrarily large index. The independent Fejér–Riesz argument creates a globally nonnegative square polynomial. Its adaptive-radius and fixed-radius branches establish the finite-margin and unrestricted-index statements respectively. In the growing-index upper bound I checked the kernel degree, the $2^{n+4}$ tail factor, contact-slack ratio, all three contractivity regions, and both high-band terms. Their powers of $\delta$ and exponential constants are consistent with $r=o(D)$. The intermediate comparison retains its upper-boundary margin and makes no unsupported uniform optimality claim.

- **Section 6, LP integration.** The orthonormal bases, central equations, normal matrix, dual direction, public primal predictor, and compact feasible segment check algebraically. The full oracle completions are unitary. Endpoint distances give the state lower bounds even with counted right-side queries; amplitude estimation gives matching upper bounds for the unconditional output density operator. The matrix-only compiler reduction remains trigonometric even when gates mix public eigenspaces. The coarse zero-query exception and two-query factor projector construction are correct and do not conflict with the general problem.
