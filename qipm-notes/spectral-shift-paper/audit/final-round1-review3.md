## Minor finding

**M1. A close Chebyshev/nonnegative-linear-polynomial antecedent should be acknowledged.** Location: `sections/01-introduction.tex:52` (approximation-theory paragraph), with the mathematical connection at `sections/04-fixed-accuracy.tex:47–54`, Theorem 4.2.

Fawzi, Saunderson, and Parrilo, *Equivariant semidefinite lifts of regular polygons*, Mathematics of Operations Research 42(2), 472–494 (2017), DOI `10.1287/moor.2016.0813`, study globally nonnegative polynomials that interpolate linear functions. Their Section 3.1 and Appendix A, Lemma 1, use the fact that an even Chebyshev polynomial lies globally above a suitable tangent. I checked the [primary full text](https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/ccc24100-4e06-460d-b2fb-ccf567a429c3/content); the [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.4379) identifies the authors and paper.

This is more specific context than general positivity-constrained approximation. In the manuscript's notation, put `n=2r` and `x=(y-mρ)/hρ`. The stationary equation gives

`P*r(y)/hρ = [Tn(x) − Tn(−z*) − Tn′(−z*)(x+z*)]/Tn′(z*)`.

Thus admissibility of the extremizer has exactly the Chebyshev-minus-supporting-tangent form covered by their lemma, after reflection. Their result does not establish the manuscript's minimax threshold formula, shrinking-interval query exponents, or pinned coherent constructions. **Fix:** add one concise citation/comparison sentence to the approximation-theory paragraph, distinguishing their discrete interpolation application from the present continuum minimax/query problem. The current self-contained admissibility proof can remain unchanged. This is an attribution/completeness improvement, not a challenge to the stated scoped novelty claim.
