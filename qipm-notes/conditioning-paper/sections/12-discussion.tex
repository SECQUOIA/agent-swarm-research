\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

At a point with Newton decrement below one, the Dikin ellipsoid of the
barrier is determined, up to a factor depending only on the barrier
parameter and the Newton decrement, by the difference body of the
current objective sublevel set, which does not depend on the barrier.
This gives the diameter law and the equal-gap comparison of all
eigenvalues of different barriers. Within a fixed representation and
metric, changing the finite-parameter barrier therefore cannot
remove a conditioning exponent imposed by the sublevel sets; if
the barrier varies with the requested gap, this needs a uniform bound on
the barrier parameters.

The sublevel geometry determines the conditioning more precisely than
uniqueness of the optimum, primal degeneracy, or singularity degree
alone. Outside the polyhedral
setting, a unique optimum does not by itself imply bounded
conditioning. Conversely, in an LP, a degenerate primal optimal vertex
does not prevent a positive-definite limit of the scaled log-barrier
Hessian. In SDP, an attained fractional error bound can determine the
conditioning exponent, but singularity degree alone cannot: the two
problems of \cref{sec:fractional-sdp}, whose optimality systems both
have singularity degree two, have diameters $D(g)$ that shrink at
different rates. The width bounds show
why the ratio of the extreme eigenvalues need not describe the
intermediate eigenvalues.

Solver cost also depends on the right-hand side and the
required accuracy. Near the end of an LP central path, the exact Newton
right-hand side has a small component in the eigenspace of the small
eigenvalues, yet the oscillatory barrier of
\cref{prop:oscillatory-barrier} shows that discarding this component
can prevent accurate recovery of the Newton direction. In exact
arithmetic, conjugate gradients can exploit a spectrum in separated
clusters; the guarantee bounds the energy-norm error, which the
numerical examples show must be distinguished from the recursively
computed residual. Quantum applications need additional assumptions on
data access and output before either fact gives a runtime bound.

These results concern one operator, the reduced Hessian in a fixed
metric. Changes of representation,
gap-dependent preconditioners, and primal--dual formulations can have
different spectra and can be useful. Our comparison shows which
conditioning the objective sublevel sets force before any linear
algebra is accounted for.
