\begin{abstract}
The barrier Hessians in interior-point Newton systems can become
ill-conditioned as the objective gap $g$ tends to zero. We ask how much
of this is forced by the feasible set and the objective alone. We
consider a compact convex feasible set, a nonconstant linear objective,
and the Hessian of a self-concordant barrier on the tangent space of the
affine constraints, in a fixed Euclidean metric. Let $L(g)$ be the set
of feasible points with gap at most $g$. For every barrier with finite
parameter $\nu$, the unit Dikin ellipsoid at the central-path point
with gap $g$, translated to the origin, lies in the difference body
$L(g)-L(g)$, which lies in the same ellipsoid scaled by
$2(\nu+2\sqrt\nu)$. Hence Hessians of different barriers at equal gap
agree in Loewner order up to parameter-dependent factors, and the
condition number satisfies
$\kappa(g)=\Theta((\operatorname{diam}L(g)/g)^2)$ as $g\to0$. These
bounds extend to points with Newton decrement below one, with explicit
constants that are uniform over bounded barrier parameters and
decrements at most a fixed number below one. The $j$th eigenvalue of
each Hessian lies between the inverse squares of two minimax
(Courant--Fischer) radial widths of $L(g)-L(g)$, up to a parameter-dependent
factor. For a trace-normalized version of a classical $3\times3$ semidefinite
program of Sturm, whose optimality system has singularity
degree two, every fixed barrier has condition number
$\Theta(g^{-3/2})$, and we compute all four eigenvalues of the
log-barrier Hessian with exact leading constants. Adding two linear
constraints preserves the singularity degree but gives
$\Theta(g^{-1})$. For linear programs, every fixed barrier Hessian has
eigenvalues of orders $\Theta(1)$ and $\Theta(g^{-2})$ only; the
eigenspace of the $\Theta(1)$ eigenvalues approaches the tangent space
of the optimal face at rate $O(g)$, and $O(g^2)$ for the logarithmic
barrier. An explicit barrier attains the rate $O(g)$; for it,
discarding a right-hand-side component of vanishing relative size gives
a relative Newton-direction error tending to one. We also bound the number of
conjugate-gradient iterations, in exact arithmetic, for spectra in two
clusters. The bounds concern this Hessian, not Schur-complement or
augmented Newton matrices, and we give no running-time bounds for
quantum linear-system solvers. Computations with exact rational certificates
illustrate the results.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} self-concordant barriers; central path;
Hessian conditioning; objective sublevels; semidefinite programming;
spectral clustering.
