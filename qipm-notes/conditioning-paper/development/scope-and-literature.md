## Central finding and proposed contribution

The intended paper concerns the equality-reduced **primal barrier Hessian in a fixed Euclidean metric** (product Frobenius metric for matrix variables), at a specified **primal objective gap**. It should explain which conditioning rates come from objective-sublevel geometry, rather than from a particular barrier. It is not a lower bound for arbitrary preconditioned Newton systems, all interior-point formulations, or end-to-end quantum computation.

The original Sections 3–4 form a coherent base, but copying them would preserve both an obsolete open problem and unnecessarily weak quantifiers. During this stage the coordinating author proposed a stronger geometric development: the tangent Dikin ellipsoid at any center of gap g should be comparable with the difference body L(g)−L(g). If verified, this compares any two barrier Hessians at equal gaps in Loewner order, and a homothetic interior ball gives a matching upper bound for every fixed barrier. The paper should then state the general law κ_F(g)=Θ_{P,c,F}((D(g)/g)^2), rather than presenting canonical barriers as the only rate-optimal choice. This is a candidate pending Stage 2 proof review, not an established result of Stage 1.

The principal missing antecedents are Xiong–Freund (2024), for the sublevel/Hessian geometric method, and Peña (2002), for objective-gap parameterization and matrix-conditioning bounds. Both require prominent discussion. Novelty must be narrowed to precisely stated further consequences and their assembly, not the first connection between sublevel geometry and conditioning, use of the objective gap, or inverse-square upper bounds.

All finite-gap comparisons concern centers that exist at the prescribed gap. For two fixed barriers this means their common attained interval; asymptotic statements concern a sufficiently small common tail. If constants depend on the barrier only through its parameter, the same bounds cover gap-dependent barrier families with uniformly bounded parameters and existing centers. They do not justify uniformity when those parameters grow without bound.
