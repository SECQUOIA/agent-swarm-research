## Mathematical and structural assessment

1. **Sections 2–3: assumptions, path parameterization, and the main theorem.** The external metric, relative tangent space, unscaled Hessian, finite barrier parameter, and nonconstant objective are specified before they are used. The one-dimensional Dikin and semiboundedness arguments support the stated constants. The support inequality and central-path identities establish the objective-gap parameterization without silently assuming that gap equals a fixed multiple of the path parameter. The proof of asymmetric containment correctly handles residuals below one, including the deterioration as the residual approaches one. In the difference-body inclusion, the downhill half of the symmetric Dikin ellipsoid is enough for the inner inclusion; the outer factor of two follows from taking differences. The equal-gap Loewner inequalities have the correct directions and constants. The fixed interior ball gives thickness proportional to gap, and the diameter comparisons give both sides of the conditioning law. The claims for uniformly bounded barrier families and for compact sublevels are appropriately delimited; the latter does not assume a globally defined central path on every unbounded domain.

2. **Section 4: the whole spectrum.** I checked the increasing eigenvalue convention against both Courant–Fischer formulas. The lower bound uses the maximum-over-subspace minimum radial width and the upper bound uses the minimum-over-subspace maximum radial width, as required after taking reciprocal squares. The intersection argument for the ordering of the two profiles is valid. The first and last widths reduce to the diameter and centered inradius. Directed exits use the longer side on objective-orthogonal lines, avoiding an unjustified continuity assumption at a change of orientation.

3. **Sections 5–6: classifications and LP limits.** The error-bound exponent yields an upper conditioning rate; a matching exponent is claimed only when the corresponding diameter scale is attained. Positive-dimensional optima and unique polytope optima are treated separately. The near-degenerate simplex estimates retain uniform constants, and the iterated limiting formula is not presented as a uniform joint limit. At a unique, possibly degenerate LP optimum, the injectivity argument on the inactive coordinates establishes that the scaled reduced Hessian has a positive-definite limit. The classical centered dual endpoint result is identified as an input. The nonorthogonal coordinates in the explicit degenerate LP are accompanied by their Gram matrix, so the reported eigenvalues concern the stated Euclidean operator.

4. **Section 7: fractional SDP example.** I checked the principal-minor upper bounds, explicit positive-definite witnesses, uniqueness of the optimum, and both diameter exponents. Singularity degree is defined for the optimality systems in the displayed cone representations; the proof correctly distinguishes those systems from the strictly feasible original feasible sets. The first exposing matrix must be a multiple of the second diagonal projector, and the second reduction reaches the optimal ray in both examples. The exact log-det center, its objective-gap/path-parameter relation, the off-block eigenvalues, and the generalized eigenvalue calculation for the remaining block are consistent. In particular, the Gram determinant and the Hessian-block determinant give the claimed smaller generalized eigenvalue; taking the four powers in increasing order gives the two conditioning constants. The paired section retains exactly the stated block. Only orders, rather than leading constants, are transferred to arbitrary barriers.

5. **Sections 8–10: projectors, forcing, and solver scope.** The canonical LP decomposition has the fixed optimal-face tangent as its kernel in the divergent part. Loewner transfer yields the all-barrier quadratic-form estimate and hence the general projector rate; the stronger logarithmic-barrier rate uses its additional bounded remainder. The approximation statement does not transfer that stronger rate to arbitrary approximate points. The oscillatory perturbation has a globally verified finite parameter, rather than only a local differential check. On the explicit subsequence, the weak forcing has the claimed order while its inverse-Hessian amplification dominates the solution, establishing the relative solution-error limit. The two-interval polynomial controls the amplification of its first factor before suppressing it with the second; its conclusion is explicitly an exact-arithmetic CG energy bound. Ambient versus reduced matrices, fixed metric transport, gap-dependent transformations, block-encoding normalization, and quantum access/output requirements remain distinct. The inverse-power example correctly violates the finite-parameter assumption needed by the main theorem.

6. **Sections 1, 11–12 and overall organization.** The abstract now says “at most two spectral scales,” which includes a unique LP optimum. The introduction promises results proved in the later sections and qualifies the principal priority claim. The ordering from geometry through exact examples to forcing and solver implications is coherent. The numerical section separates exact input certificates from finite-precision spectral diagnostics, retains rejected benchmark points, and does not fit an asymptotic theorem to unresolved tails. Its CG discussion distinguishes recursive residual, true residual, and energy error. The conclusion does not broaden the operator or complexity claims. I found no circular proof dependency or missing development essential to an advertised result.

## Literature and novelty audit

The final comparisons are materially precise. Nesterov–Nemirovskii's Proposition 2.3.2(iii), equation (2.3.9), already gives a comparison through the symmetric chord at the **same interior point**; the manuscript acknowledges this and identifies its use of generally different equal-gap centers. Xiong–Freund's Section 5.1 is credited as a direct level-geometry/Hessian antecedent. Peña's objective-gap parameterization and Schur conditioning bound are explicitly excluded from the novelty claim. The reduced primal operator and the matching diameter dependence identify the narrower additional statement. Prior access limitations for Peña 2001 and Renegar 1996 do not create a false theorem-level claim in the final prose.

The SDP construction is correctly credited to Sturm and its survey presentation. I independently checked the **published** Sremac–Woerdeman–Wolkowicz primary PDF available from Wolkowicz's institutional site:

<https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~hwolkowi/henry/reports/errorbndsMP2021.pdf>

Its Section 4 uses an externally perturbed affine right-hand side. Under Assumption 4.1, Corollary 4.3 separates primal/dual eigenvalue behaviors, Theorem 4.4 gives the stated degree-one characterization, and Theorem 4.7 separates dual diagonal-block rates. The manuscript describes these distinctions accurately; it does not replace the last result with an unconditional theorem about all dual eigenvalue rates. Its specific four-constant claim is qualified and tied to the fixed-affine, equality-reduced log-det Hessian. The text does not claim priority for fractional SDP geometry or for a general connection between singularity degree and spectra.

The treatment of oscillation credits Duistermaat's Remark 2.3 and distinguishes Bolte–Pauwels' finite continuous Legendre example from a boundary-divergent finite-parameter self-concordant barrier. The new claim is the certified rate and Newton-solution calculation, rather than oscillation itself. Classical LP endpoint and log-Hessian splitting results are credited separately from their all-barrier consequences. The quantum discussion neither converts worst-case oracle results into an instance-specific lower bound nor disregards positive results based on other access and representation assumptions.
