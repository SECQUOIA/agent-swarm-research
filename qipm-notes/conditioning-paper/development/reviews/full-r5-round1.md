## Minor issues and proposed corrections

### 1. Explicitly distinguish Euclidean coordinate-matrix conditioning from transport of the original external metric

**Location:** `sections/10-formulation-scope.tex:6–22`, especially the introduction to `eq:coordinate-condition-comparison` and the whitening statement.

The congruence inequality is correct, but “change of tangent coordinates” can also mean expressing the same physical operator and the same fixed external metric in another basis. In that interpretation the original operator's eigenvalues are exactly unchanged, and the coordinate calculation is the generalized eigenproblem for `(R^T H R, R^T R)`. Ordinary Euclidean eigenvalues of `R^T H R` instead correspond to assigning the standard Euclidean metric to the new coordinates. The paper correctly makes the analogous distinction for the nonorthonormal SDP tangent coordinates in Section 7; Section 10 should state it explicitly to avoid an apparent inconsistency.

**Correction:** Before the inequality, say that the condition numbers in this paragraph are Euclidean matrix condition numbers in the respective coordinate systems. Add a sentence such as: “If the original external metric is transported instead, its Gram matrix is `R^T R`; the generalized spectrum of `(R^T H R, R^T R)` is exactly the spectrum of `H`.” The later whitening example then plainly concerns the Euclidean matrix used for linear algebra, rather than changing the spectrum of the original fixed-metric operator by notation alone. The existing inequality and cost qualifications can remain.

**Severity:** Minor clarification. The displayed inequality, its proof, and the intended preconditioning conclusion are mathematically correct.

### 2. Explain or remove the otherwise undefined contact terminology

**Location:** `sections/10-formulation-scope.tex:102–106`.

“Derivatives of selected contact representations” appears only here. The manuscript supplies no definition, example, or citation explaining what a contact representation is. An optimization reader can follow the preceding objective-sensitivity example, but cannot identify the extra mathematical object in this final sentence. This reads as a residual reference to a broader repository discussion rather than standalone prose.

**Correction:** Preserve the intended scope conclusion using ordinary defined language, for example: “More generally, differentiating an auxiliary representation of a central point introduces derivatives of that representation's data maps. Comparing its sensitivity with Newton conditioning requires bounds on those maps and on the Jacobian of its defining equations.” Alternatively, define the specific contact object and supply a reference, but that would add material unnecessary to the paper's main argument.

**Severity:** Minor reader-comprehension issue; no disputed quantitative claim.

The diameter law correctly uses the circumradius of the difference body, the sublevel chord length, and the homothetic relative ball. Uniform attained gaps and the compact-sublevel localization have adequate hypotheses; localization does not silently assert global path existence.
- **Section 4:** Checked both Courant–Fischer applications, reciprocal extrema, the distinction between the two width profiles, and the stronger placement of their superscripts. The subspace-intersection proof has the right dimensions. The discontinuous directed-exit sign convention does not invalidate the argument because only infima/suprema and uniform positive bounds are used. The endpoint identification with the longest sublevel chord is justified.
- **Sections 5–6:** The paper separates upper error bounds from attained exponents. The polytope vertex proof includes degenerate vertices. The simplex diameter and projected-objective constants are uniform in the perturbation, while the exact plateau constant is correctly an iterated limit. The LP matrix-limit proof correctly establishes injectivity on the tangent space and does not confuse strict complementarity with primal nondegeneracy. The active-face maximizer and quantitative eigenvalue bounds are justified. The unbounded example is explicitly localized.
- **Section 7:** Recomputed the PSD minor and determinant estimates for the quarter-power chords, the two-step exposing chain and exclusion of a one-step certificate, and the unique rank-one optimal dual slack. The singularity degree refers to the optimality system, not the strictly feasible original system. The exact center equations and `g ~ 3 mu/2` conversion are correct. The off-block metric gives `A_2^{-1}/b`, while the diagonal block uses the Gram matrix with determinant seven. Its trace/rank-one limit and determinant produce the four stated leading constants and both condition-number constants. The all-barrier transfer concerns orders, not invariance of those constants.
- **Section 8:** The fixed optimal-face tangent is the kernel of the hard logarithmic block. Equal-gap Loewner comparison gives the full all-barrier spectrum and the quadratic-form bound needed for the `O(g)` projector angle; the canonical eigenvector equation gives `O(g^2)`. The off-path extension is limited to spectra and the general projector rate, while forcing bounds require exact centrality. The strong-subspace residual estimate states both the subspace restriction and its solve criterion. Rechecked all three perturbation derivatives and the global self-concordance/gradient parameter certificate for the oscillatory barrier. Its subsequence centers, weak-projector asymptotic, restored factor four, solution-error limit, and nonconvergent endpoint calculation are mutually consistent.
- **Section 9:** The Chebyshev construction controls both intervals, including singleton intervals; its degree accounts for suppression of the lower-interval polynomial's growth on the upper interval. The CG claim is an energy-error statement for any initial vector in exact arithmetic, with the dimension termination alternative. The manuscript correctly distinguishes it from residual stopping. Quantum block-encoding normalization, actual right-hand side, solution-state output, whole-interval polynomial boundedness, and the dimension restriction in the cited worst-case lower bound are clearly separated. A Gram factorization is not presented as a generic quantum acceleration.
- **Section 10:** Verified the Schur invariance using the ambient Hessian, the augmented congruence, and the Lorentz boost's `lambda^{-4}` condition number. The cone example is expressly outside the compact-feasible-set theorem. The objective derivative is correctly `-mu^{-1}H^{-1}`; its varying-data example does not claim unbounded sensitivity per unit objective perturbation. The inverse-power example satisfies differential self-concordance but violates every finite gradient parameter, with the claimed cubic conditioning rate. The only issues here are the two presentation clarifications above.

## Scientific positioning and organization

The abstract, introduction, proofs, examples, numerical interpretation, and discussion agree on the operator, metric, gap, barrier class, and asymptotic quantifiers. The paper explains its central question before technical detail and then progresses from general geometry to examples and solver consequences. The former standalone-stage issues about an empty LP weak cluster and uniform residuals are correctly resolved in the assembled text.

The main originality statement precisely identifies comparison at different centers with equal gap, explicit residual dependence, and the matching diameter characterization. Standard containment and same-point Hessian comparison are credited; Peña's gap parameterization and Schur bound are not claimed as new. The LP endpoint corollary and Sturm construction are expressly classical. The SDP claim is limited to its explicit reduced-Hessian calculation and transfer. Oscillatory derivatives and earlier qualitative path nonconvergence receive separate credit.

I rechecked the current [Xiong–Freund primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01942) and [July 2024 manuscript, Section 5.1](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/arXiv_0715.pdf). Fact 5.2 and Remark 5.1 support the acknowledged sublevel/ellipsoid antecedent; the manuscript's comparison accurately identifies its primal-dual setting. Targeted fresh searches did not identify an additional direct equal-gap/difference-body comparator, which supports retaining qualified rather than absolute priority language.

I also read Orsucci–Dunjko's Proposition 6 and neighboring discussion directly in the local primary full text: the cited bound is `Omega(min(kappa,N))`, distinct from their subsequent weaker Grover-based proposition. The [current Apers–Gribling primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03215) confirms the tall-LP row-query setting, explicit feasible output, and Hessian spectral approximation/gradient method that avoids direct Hessian-condition-number dependence. The paper's positive-algorithm discussion is accurate and prevents an unsupported quantum impossibility inference.

The repository inclusion map is reflected in the finished manuscript: the old degeneracy question is resolved, numerical fractional scales are now analytically proved, and relevant representation/sensitivity boundaries are retained without importing separate oracle-hardness or preconditioner projects. The two minor clarifications above improve the remaining transition to that scope material.
