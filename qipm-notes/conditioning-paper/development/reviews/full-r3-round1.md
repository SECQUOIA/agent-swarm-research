## Mathematical assessment

1. **Setup and general geometry (Sections 2–4).** The nonconstant relative objective, compactness, positive relative Hessian, and finite barrier parameter are explicit. The proofs of Dikin containment and semiboundedness support the subsequent constants without importing an unchecked theorem. The central-path range, strict gap monotonicity, and fixed-barrier relation between gap and path parameter are consistent. Gap-parametrized Hessians use hats, avoiding the earlier ambiguity. The one-sided approximate-centrality argument has the correct sign, handles endpoints before its chosen intermediate point, and establishes the stated constant 
   \(C=(\nu+2\sqrt\nu+\rho)/(1-\rho)\).
   The two difference-body inclusions imply the claimed Loewner directions and constants. The interior-ball homothety provides the required upper spectral edge; the objective direction provides the lower edge. Diameter and inradius therefore yield a matching law, rather than only an obstruction. Uniformity is restricted to bounded parameters and residuals bounded away from one, and the common attained gap range is established. The compact-sublevel extension is correctly pointwise and does not silently assert global path existence on arbitrary unbounded sets.

   I independently rechecked both Courant–Fischer arrangements in the radial-width and directed-exit theorems. In particular, the lower bound uses the dimension-\(j\) profile and the upper bound the codimension-\(j-1\) profile. The stronger displayed bounds with \(w_j^+\) and \(w_j^-\) have the correct ordering. The intersection argument relating the profiles is valid. The directed-exit sign convention does not require continuity that its proof fails to establish.

2. **Geometric classifications (Section 5).** The error-bound proposition states an upper conclusion unless a matching diameter witness is supplied. The positive-face and unique-polytope cases then have matching lower bounds. The elementary vertex decomposition proves sharpness for degenerate vertices as well as nondegenerate ones. The simplex objective norm is bounded above and below uniformly in the perturbation, the objective range is fixed, and its lower-sublevel diameter has the claimed uniform crossover. The exact plateau is the generalized eigenvalue ratio in the Euclidean tangent metric; its iterated-limit interpretation is distinguished from a joint limit. The curved trace-one SDP uses the Frobenius Gram factors consistently.

3. **Compact and degenerate LP endpoints (Section 6).** The centered dual limit is explicitly credited to classical central-path theory. Uniqueness of the primal optimum makes the restriction to inactive coordinates injective on the equality tangent, proving positive definiteness of the limiting scaled Hessian even at a degenerate vertex. The active-dual analytic-center characterization has the needed boundedness and strict-concavity argument. The singular-value bounds keep the geometry of the inactive-coordinate restriction separate from slack dispersion. I rechecked feasibility, the dual-face parametrization, the maximizing root, the tangent Gram matrix, and the limiting eigenvalues for the compact four-variable example. The second example is explicitly treated through compact objective sublevels; it is not used as a compact feasible-set example.

4. **Fractional SDP (Section 7).** The feasibility bounds and matching witnesses prove both attained diameter orders. The facial-reduction argument concerns the optimality system and proves that one exposing step cannot suffice. The dual slack calculation establishes failure of strict complementarity without contradicting strict feasibility of the original primal and dual problems. Symmetry removes the two off-block variables at the exact center. I checked the asymptotic orders of the scalar path formulas, the off-block inverse-matrix calculation, and the generalized eigenproblem on the remaining tangent coordinates. The determinant and leading trace give the stated four constants and condition-number constants. The paired section has the same singularity degree and a different diameter exponent, exactly as required for the claimed limitation. Equal-gap transfer extends orders, not the canonical leading constants, to other barriers.

5. **All-barrier LP spectra and forcing (Section 8).** The canonical leading quadratic form has kernel equal to the optimal-face tangent. Its coercivity on the orthogonal complement, the bounded canonical remainder, and the equal-gap Loewner comparison give the two spectral scales for arbitrary fixed barriers. Applying the coercivity to a low-eigenvalue basis gives the general projector rate \(O(g)\); the canonical eigenvector equation improves it to \(O(g^2)\). No convergence of an arbitrary barrier's center is assumed. The objective annihilates the fixed face tangent, so the forcing conclusion follows for the exact central Newton right-hand side. The approximate-centrality extension promises the general rate and spectral scales, not an unjustified canonical rate. Actual approximate-iterate forcing is appropriately distinguished from exact forcing.

   For the oscillatory barrier, I checked the derivative envelopes against the base Hessian, the global self-concordance scaling, the finite parameter bound, boundary divergence, centrality on the chosen subsequence, and the weak eigenvector asymptotics. The inverse amplifies its \(O(g)\) weak forcing enough that discarding it loses asymptotically all relative Euclidean solution mass. This is a valid sharpness and solution-contract counterexample, not merely a residual example.

6. **Linear solves and formulation boundaries (Sections 9–10).** The two-interval polynomial damps the strong interval enough to compensate for the first factor's growth there; the displayed degree follows from the choices of degrees and repetition count. Its CG conclusion is exact-arithmetic energy error, with finite-dimensional termination kept separate. The strong-subspace solve is explicitly conditional on obtaining the projector, and its residual guarantee is not promoted to a direction-error or inexact-IPM theorem. The quantum discussion distinguishes spectral polynomials from globally bounded QSVT polynomials, encoded normalization from spectral conditioning, and matrix/RHS/output access assumptions. It does not infer an end-to-end lower bound. Coordinate congruence preserves exponents only for fixed invertible maps. The Schur-invariance and Lorentz example consistently distinguish ambient and reduced matrices. The infinite-parameter scalar example satisfies self-concordance but violates the finite barrier-gradient bound, so it does not contradict the principal theorem.
