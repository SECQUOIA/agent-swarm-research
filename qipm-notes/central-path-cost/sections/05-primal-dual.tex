\section{Primal movement and primal--dual completion}
\label{sec:primal-dual}

A primal shortcut need not admit a short dual completion. We first state
classical primal--dual geometry with its metric and endpoint contract
explicit, and then compare both geometries on the same dyadic LP.

\subsection{Orthogonal speed allocation and the feasible gap set}
Let $K$ be a proper cone (closed, pointed, full dimensional), and let $F$
be a standard self-concordant barrier satisfying
$F(tx)=F(x)-\nu\log t$ for $t>0$. Use the negative-pairing conjugate
\[
 F_*(s)=\sup_{x\in\interior K}\{-\ip{s}{x}-F(x)\},
 \qquad \widehat F(x,s)=F(x)+F_*(s).
\]
Consider the strictly feasible primal--dual pair
\[
 \min\{\ip{c}{x}:Ax=b,\ x\in K\},\qquad
 \max\{\ip{b}{y}:A^*y+s=c,\ s\in K^*\},
\]
and its exact centers $z(\eta)=(x(\eta),s(\eta))$, where
$s=-\eta^{-1}F'(x)$ and $\ip{x}{s}=\nu/\eta$.
All full primal--dual distances in this section use the Hessian of
$\widehat F$ on the ambient product cone; restricted feasible distances
can only be larger. A dot denotes differentiation in $\lambda=\log\eta$.

\begin{proposition}[Classical speed allocation]
\label{prop:pd-split}
Put $H=F''(x)$, $g=H^{-1/2}F'(x)$, and let $\Pi_x$ be the Euclidean
orthogonal projector onto $H^{1/2}\ker A$. Then
\begin{align}
 \nu_{\rm P}(\eta)&:=\norm{\dot x}_{F,x}^2=\norm{\Pi_xg}^2,
 &\nu_{\rm D}(\eta)&:=\norm{\dot s}_{F_*,s}^2
       =\norm{(I-\Pi_x)g}^2,\notag\\
 \nu_{\rm P}(\eta)+\nu_{\rm D}(\eta)&=\nu.
 \label{eq:pd-split}
\end{align}
If $A$ has full row rank, then
\begin{equation}
 \nu_{\rm P}(\eta)=\eta^2\ip{c}{Mc},\qquad
 M=H^{-1}-H^{-1}A^*(AH^{-1}A^*)^{-1}AH^{-1}.
 \label{eq:activity-schur}
\end{equation}
Writing $p(\eta)=\ip{c}{x(\eta)}$ and
$d(\eta)=\ip{b}{y(\eta)}$, one has
\begin{equation}
 -\eta^2p'(\eta)=\nu_{\rm P}(\eta),\qquad
 \eta^2d'(\eta)=\nu_{\rm D}(\eta).
 \label{eq:objective-progress}
\end{equation}
If both objectives converge to the finite optimum $p^*$, then
\begin{equation}
 p(\eta)-p^*=\int_\eta^\infty\frac{\nu_{\rm P}(u)}{u^2}\dd u,
 \qquad
 p^*-d(\eta)=\int_\eta^\infty\frac{\nu_{\rm D}(u)}{u^2}\dd u.
 \label{eq:integrated-gap-allocation}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Logarithmic homogeneity and conjugacy give
$Hx=-F'(x)$, $\norm{g}^2=\nu$, and
$F_*''(s)=\eta^2H^{-1}$. Differentiating the central relation yields
$\eta\dot s=F'(x)-H\dot x$.
Thus $v=H^{1/2}\dot x$ and
$d_0=\eta H^{-1/2}\dot s$ satisfy $g=v+d_0$.
Feasibility gives $v\in H^{1/2}\ker A$ and
$d_0\in(H^{1/2}\ker A)^\perp$, proving \eqref{eq:pd-split}.
Removing redundant equalities leaves the feasible tangent spaces unchanged.
With full row rank, differentiated primal stationarity gives $\dot x=-\eta Mc$;
$MHM=M$ proves \eqref{eq:activity-schur} and the first progress identity.
Differentiate $p-d=\nu/\eta$ to obtain the second, and integrate for
\eqref{eq:integrated-gap-allocation}.
\end{proof}
The constant product speed is the calculation underlying
Nesterov--Todd's primal--dual theorem~\cite[Theorems~4.1 and~5.2]{NesterovTodd2002}.
Metric projections and conjugate Hessian isometries also appear explicitly
in Ohara, Ishi, and Tsuchiya~\cite[Sections~4--5]{OharaIshiTsuchiya2024}.
The projection calculus itself is not a contribution of this paper.
Its role here is to identify exactly which part of the constant total
speed is visible in a primal projection.

\begin{theorem}[Classical distance to feasible small-gap points]
\label{thm:pd-gap-set}
Let $z_0=z(\eta_0)$ be exact central with gap $\Delta_0=\nu/\eta_0$.
For $0<\eps<\Delta_0$, let $\mathcal Z_\eps$ consist of all strictly
primal--dual feasible pairs of gap at most $\eps$. Then
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt{\nu/2}\log\frac{\Delta_0}{\eps}
 \leq d_{\widehat F}(z_0,\mathcal Z_\eps)
 \leq \sqrt\nu\log\frac{\Delta_0}{\eps}.
 \label{eq:pd-gap-set}
\end{equation}
The upper bound is the length of the central route to $z(\nu/\eps)$.
Every sequence of forward $R$-chords, $0<R<1$, from $z_0$ to
$\mathcal Z_\eps$ therefore has at least
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\sqrt{\nu/2}\log(\Delta_0/\eps)}{-\log(1-R)}
 \label{eq:pd-gap-count}
\end{equation}
chords, even if intermediate points violate the affine equations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At a center $z_c=z(\eta)$, conjugacy gives
$F_*'(s_c)=-\eta x_c$ and
$\widehat F(z_c)=\nu\log\eta-\nu$.
For any feasible $z=(x,s)$, orthogonality of primal and dual feasible
differences gives $\ip{x-x_c}{s-s_c}=0$. Hence
\[
 D\widehat F(z_c)[z-z_c]
 =-\eta\{\ip{s_c}{x-x_c}+\ip{x_c}{s-s_c}\}
 =-\eta\{\gap(z)-\nu/\eta\}.
\]
At $\eta=\nu/\eps$, convexity proves that $z_c$ minimizes
$\widehat F$ on $\mathcal Z_\eps$.
The ambient dual norm of $D\widehat F$ is exactly $\sqrt{2\nu}$,
since each logarithmically homogeneous barrier contributes $\nu$.
Integrating this gradient bound along any ambient curve proves the lower
bound. Equation~\eqref{eq:pd-split} gives the upper bound, and
\cref{lem:chord-distance} proves \eqref{eq:pd-gap-count}.
\end{proof}
This is a quantitative restatement of
\cite[Theorem~5.1(c), Theorem~5.2, and Corollary~5.1]{NesterovTodd2002},
which already treats the entire feasible gap set and permits intermediate
affine infeasibility. It is not a new endpoint-set extension.

\subsection{An optimal cone barrier with little primal movement}
For positive block dimensions $s_a$, define the one-cone homogenization
\[
 \mathcal H=\{(t,y_1,\ldots,y_k):t\geq\norm{y_a}_2\ (1\leq a\leq k)\}.
\]
Its classical barrier
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F(t,y)=-\sum_{a=1}^k\log(t^2-\norm{y_a}^2)+(k-1)\log t
 \label{eq:product-ball-homogeneous}
\end{equation}
has exact logarithmic parameter $\nu=k+1$.
For clarity, the positive term in this formula is not justified by a
subtraction rule for barriers. Place each $y_a$ in a private column block
of row $a$ of a $k$-row matrix $Y$. Then $YY^*=\diag(\norm{y_a}^2)$,
and \eqref{eq:product-ball-homogeneous} is the restriction of the
spectral-norm-cone barrier
$(k-1)\log t-\log\det(t^2I-YY^*)$ from
\cite[Proposition~5.4.6(i), p.~199]{NesterovNemirovskii1994}.
This supplies standard self-concordance and the parameter $k+1$.
Restricting further to one scalar direction in each block gives the
$(k+1)$-dimensional infinity-norm cone; its barrier lower bound is $k+1$
\cite[Corollary~6.2]{Hildebrand2013} for $k\geq2$.
For $k=1$, its two-dimensional section is the orthant $\R_+^2$,
whose sharp parameter is two. Thus the parameter is also optimal
over logarithmically homogeneous barriers on $\mathcal H$.

On $t=1$, the restriction is $\sum_a b(\norm{y_a})$, whose exact
parameter is $k$ by \cref{prop:standard-parameters}.
For unit $u_a$ and weights $w_a\geq0$, minimize
$-\sum_aw_a\ip{u_a}{y_a}$ on this slice. Its centers are
$y_a(\eta)=q(\eta w_a)u_a$, using the scalar $q$ of
\cref{sec:sharp-centrality}. Direct radial differentiation, followed by
\eqref{eq:pd-split}, gives
\begin{equation}
 \nu_{\rm P}(\eta)=q_{\rm eff}(\eta)
 =\sum_a\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt{1+(\eta w_a)^2}}\right),\qquad
 \nu_{\rm D}(\eta)=k+1-q_{\rm eff}(\eta).
 \label{eq:product-ball-allocation}
\end{equation}
For $w_1=1$, all other weights zero, and $0<\eta_0<\eta_1$,
\[
 L_{\rm P}\leq\log(\eta_1/\eta_0),\qquad
 L_{\rm D}\geq\sqrt{k}\log(\eta_1/\eta_0).
\]
The full endpoint distance is at least
$\sqrt{(k+1)/2}\log(\eta_1/\eta_0)$, by \cref{thm:pd-gap-set}.
Starting the primal curve at its analytic center $\eta=0$ instead costs
at most $1/\sqrt2+\log\eta_1$ for $\eta_1\geq1$, since
$q_{\rm eff}(\eta)\leq\eta^2/2$ on $(0,1]$.
Thus the ambient parameter alone gives no comparable primal lower bound.

This conclusion does not depend on a nonunique projected optimizer.
Fix $0<\eps\leq1$, write $\nu=k+1$, and take
\begin{equation}
 w_1=1,\quad w_a=\eps^2/\nu^2\ (a\geq2),\quad
 \eta_0=1,\quad\eta_1=\nu/\eps.
 \label{eq:positive-weights-projection}
\end{equation}
All weights are positive, so the product-ball optimizer is uniquely
$(u_a)_a$. The scalar inequality
$1-(1+z^2)^{-1/2}\leq z^2/2$ gives, throughout this interval,
\[
 q_{\rm eff}(\eta)\leq1+\frac{\eps^2}{2\nu},\qquad
 L_{\rm P}\leq\sqrt{1+\frac{\eps^2}{2\nu}}\log\frac{\nu}{\eps},
 \qquad
 L_{\rm D}\geq\sqrt{k-\frac{\eps^2}{2\nu}}\log\frac{\nu}{\eps}.
\]
The terminal full gap is exactly $\eps$; the primal error is at most
$\eps$. These objectives depend on the requested accuracy. For a single
fixed objective with every $w_a>0$, $q_{\rm eff}(\eta)\to k$ eventually.
The example rules out a bound uniform over objectives; it does not erase
the small-weight scale from an instance-dependent asymptotic statement.
The distribution and decay estimates of \cref{sec:distribution} describe
this scale more precisely.

\subsection{Three movement scales on one sparse LP}
\begin{theorem}[The cost of a feasible dual completion]
\label{thm:completion}
Use exactly the dyadic $w_i$, $a_i$, $T=a_r=\Theta(r)$ and
$\eps_r=re^{-T}$ of \eqref{eq:dyadic-family}. Write the box LP in the form
\begin{equation}
 u_i=1+x_i,\quad v_i=1-x_i,\quad u_i+v_i=2,\quad
 \min\sum_i\left(-\frac{w_i}2u_i+\frac{w_i}2v_i\right).
 \label{eq:box-standard-form}
\end{equation}
The orthant barrier $\mathcal F=-\sum_i(\log u_i+\log v_i)$ has
parameter $2r$ and restricts exactly to $U(x)=\sum_i b(x_i)$.
Start at the finite full center $z^c(0)$, where $s=\log\eta=0$.
For every fixed $0<R<1$, the optimal forward $R$-chord counts have the
following orders as $r\to\infty$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{.68\textwidth}l@{}}
\toprule
Metric and endpoint contract & Number of chords\\
\midrule
Restricted primal, arbitrary route from $x^c(0)$ to $x^c(T)$
 & $\Theta_R(r\sqrt{\log r})$\\
Restricted primal, exact central iterates between those endpoints
 & $\Theta_R(r\log r)$\\
Full primal--dual, any strictly feasible output of gap $\leq2\eps_r$
 & $\Theta_R(r^{3/2})$\\
Full primal--dual, exact central route from $z^c(0)$ to $z^c(T)$
 & $\Theta_R(r^{3/2})$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The second order also holds if iterates may lie in any fixed metric tube
around labeled primal centers, with arbitrary label order and actual
terminal primal accuracy. The lower full bound allows intermediate
affine infeasibility. The terminal point $x^c(T)$ is $\eps_r$-accurate,
and $z^c(T)$ has full gap exactly $2\eps_r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\alpha_i,\beta_i$ be the dual slacks associated with $u_i,v_i$.
The signs in \eqref{eq:box-standard-form} give
\begin{equation}
 \beta_i-\alpha_i=w_i,\qquad
 u_i\alpha_i=v_i\beta_i=e^{-s}.
 \label{eq:completion-signs}
\end{equation}
Consequently $x_i^c(s)=q(e^{s-a_i})$ and
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\dot z_{\rm P}^c}^2=q_{\rm eff}(e^s),\qquad
 \norm{\dot z_{\rm D}^c}^2=2r-q_{\rm eff}(e^s)\geq r,
 \qquad \norm{\dot z^c}^2=2r.
 \label{eq:completion-speeds}
\end{equation}
The full central length on $[0,T]$ is exactly $\sqrt{2r}\,T$.
The scalar error bound $w[1-q(\eta w)]\leq1/\eta$ proves terminal
primal accuracy; summing \eqref{eq:completion-signs} gives full gap
$2re^{-T}=2\eps_r$.

Here is a direct lower bound to every feasible full target. Initially
$1/\alpha_i^0+1/(\alpha_i^0+w_i)=2$; as $w_i\leq1$, the decreasing
left side implies $\alpha_i^0\geq1/\sqrt2$. At every feasible target,
\[
 \gap=\sum_i(u_i\alpha_i+v_i\beta_i)
      =\sum_i(2\alpha_i+v_iw_i).
\]
Thus gap $\leq2\eps_r$ forces every $\alpha_i\leq\eps_r$.
The dual orthant metric is Euclidean in coordinatewise logarithms.
Projecting an arbitrary full curve onto these $r$ log-slacks proves
\begin{equation}
 d_{\widehat{\mathcal F}}(z^c(0),\mathcal Z_{2\eps_r})
 \geq\sqrt r\,\pos{T-\log r-\tfrac12\log2}.
 \label{eq:completion-gap-lower}
\end{equation}
Together with the central upper route and \cref{lem:chord-distance},
this proves both full rows, with no prescribed final dual certificate.
\Cref{thm:pd-gap-set} supplies another lower proof of the same order.

For the primal rows, \cref{thm:dyadic} starts at the analytic center and
ends at the first accurate parameter $s_\eps\in(T-1,T]$.
Its negative-parameter tail has length $O(1)$: the scalar bound
$v(s-a_i)\leq e^{s-a_i}/\sqrt2$ bounds its length by
$(\sum_iw_i^2/2)^{1/2}=O(1)$.
The last segment from $s_\eps$ to $T$ has length at most $\sqrt r$.
Triangle inequalities therefore preserve the primal endpoint distance
$\Theta(r\sqrt{\log r})$ and central length $\Theta(r\log r)$.
The endpoint distance and chord sampling prove the arbitrary-route row
and the central upper bound. For the central lower bound, prepend a
bounded number of sampled chords on the negative-parameter tail and
apply the discrete potential theorem, \cref{thm:tube}. This argument
also proves the fixed-tube statement and allows backward labels.
It uses the discrete potential, not central arclength alone.
\end{proof}
The ordinary rational encoding length of these same data is
$B=\Theta(r^2)$ by \cref{thm:dyadic}. Thus the three scales are
\begin{equation}
 \Theta(\sqrt{B\log B}),\qquad
 \Theta(\sqrt B\log B),\qquad \Theta(B^{3/4}).
 \label{eq:completion-bit-scales}
\end{equation}
A feasible dual certificate in this metric increases the best movement
by a factor $\Theta(\sqrt{r/\log r})$ relative to the best primal route. Each equality row has two nonzeros and each column one;
sparsity does not remove this geometric cost. This comparison concerns
bounded local movement. Neither this theorem nor the speed identity is
an unconditional running-time or oracle-query lower bound.
