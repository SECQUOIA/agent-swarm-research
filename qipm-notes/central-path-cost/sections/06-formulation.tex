\section{What a change of formulation can preserve}
\label{sec:formulation}

The completion theorem fixes a formulation as well as a barrier.
This section gives two ways to obtain movement bounds for other
formulations. Exposed support rank controls primal movement for standard
symmetric-cone metrics; a dimension cap controls full primal--dual
movement for arbitrary logarithmically homogeneous barriers.
Neither statement infers a primal lower bound from a parameter upper bound.

\subsection{Exposed support rank and arbitrary auxiliary motion}
Let $K=\prod_iK_i$ be a product of symmetric cones in Euclidean Jordan
algebras with trace inner products, and let
$\Omega=\{x\in\interior K:Ax=b\}\ne\varnothing$.
Suppose a support objective has finite optimum $\ell_*$ and an attained
dual certificate $s=(s_i)\in K$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \ell_*-\ell(x)=\sum_i\ip{s_i}{x_i}\qquad(x\in\Omega).
 \label{eq:exposing-certificate}
\end{equation}
Let $c_i$ be the support idempotent of $s_i$, let
$q_i=\rank(s_i)$, and omit zero-rank factors from expressions below.
Here $P(c_i)$ is the orthogonal Peirce projection onto the algebra with
unit $c_i$, and $\det_{c_i}$ is its determinant.

\begin{theorem}[Distance forced by the exposed minor]
\label{thm:exposed-rank}
Use the restricted barrier
\[ F(x)=-\sum_i\alpha_i\log\det_i x_i,\qquad \alpha_i\geq1. \]
Put $Q_\alpha=\sum_i\alpha_iq_i>0$ and, for
any reference $x^c\in\Omega$, define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{c,\alpha}=Q_\alpha
 \left[\prod_i
 \frac{\{\det_{c_i}(s_i)\det_{c_i}(P(c_i)x_i^c)\}^{\alpha_i}}
      {\alpha_i^{\alpha_iq_i}}\right]^{1/Q_\alpha}.
 \label{eq:weighted-exposed-scale}
\end{equation}
For $\eps>0$, every feasible $y$ of objective gap at most $\eps$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 d_F(x^c,y)\geq\sqrt{Q_\alpha}
            \pos{\log(\Delta_{c,\alpha}/\eps)}.
 \label{eq:exposed-distance}
\end{equation}
There is no restriction on unexposed eigenvalues or auxiliary fibers,
and strict complementarity is not required. If
$d_F(x_0,x^c)\leq D_0$ and each of $N$ counted rounds has metric
displacement at most $B_0>0$, then
\begin{equation}
 N\geq\frac{\pos{\sqrt{Q_\alpha}\log(\Delta_{c,\alpha}/\eps)-D_0}}{B_0}.
 \label{eq:exposed-rounds}
\end{equation}
In particular, an initial local error $\norm{x_0-x^c}_{x^c}\leq\rho$, $0\leq\rho<1$
and at most $m\in\N$ forward $R$-chords per round, $0<R<1$, allow
$D_0=-\log(1-\rho)$ and $B_0=-m\log(1-R)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We give the contraction argument to specify the metric normalization,
including the exceptional Jordan algebra. For one idempotent $c$ of
rank $q$, put $a=P(c)x$ and $\phi_c(x)=-\log\det_c a$.
The Hessian of $-\log\det x$ is $P(x^{-1})$ with inverse $P(x)$,
where $P(u)=2L_u^2-L_{u^2}$ is the quadratic representation and
$L_uv=u\circ v$. Since $a^{-1}$ belongs to the Peirce algebra,
\[
 d\phi_c(x)[h]=-\ip{a^{-1}}{h},\qquad
 \norm{d\phi_c(x)}_{x,*}^2=\ip{a^{-1}}{P(x)a^{-1}}=q.
\]
Indeed, the fundamental identity $P(P(u)v)=P(u)P(v)P(u)$ with $u=c$
gives $P(c)P(x)P(c)=P(a)$; then $P(a)a^{-1}=a$ and
$\ip{a^{-1}}{a}=q$. The compression $a$ is positive definite in its
Peirce algebra: if $x\succeq\delta e$, positivity of $P(c)$ gives
$a\succeq\delta c$.
For $\Phi=\sum_i\alpha_i\phi_{c_i}$, the product metric therefore gives
$\norm{d\Phi}_{F,*}^2=Q_\alpha$ before affine restriction and at most
$Q_\alpha$ afterwards. These quadratic-representation identities are
standard symmetric-cone algebra~\cite{FarautKoranyi1994}; the
principal-minor metric identity is not asserted as new.

Set $g_i=\ip{s_i}{y_i}>0$ on the active factors. Inside the Peirce
algebra, $z_i=P(s_i^{1/2})P(c_i)y_i$ has trace $g_i$ and determinant
$\det_{c_i}(s_i)\det_{c_i}(P(c_i)y_i)$.
Spectral AM--GM gives
\[
 \det_{c_i}(P(c_i)y_i)
 \leq\det_{c_i}(s_i)^{-1}(g_i/q_i)^{q_i}.
\]
Under $\sum_i g_i\leq\eps$, the weighted product
$\prod_i(g_i/q_i)^{\alpha_iq_i}$ is maximized at
$g_i=\eps\alpha_iq_i/Q_\alpha$. Thus
\[
 \prod_i\det_{c_i}(P(c_i)y_i)^{\alpha_i}
 \leq(\eps/Q_\alpha)^{Q_\alpha}
 \prod_i\alpha_i^{\alpha_iq_i}\det_{c_i}(s_i)^{-\alpha_i}.
\]
Comparing with $x^c$ proves
$\Phi(y)-\Phi(x^c)\geq Q_\alpha\log(\Delta_{c,\alpha}/\eps)$.
Integrate the covector norm bound along every feasible curve.
The triangle inequality and \cref{lem:chord-distance} prove the round
statements.
\end{proof}
For unit scales this gives $Q=\sum_iq_i$ and
$\Delta_c=Q[\prod_i\det_{c_i}(s_i)\det_{c_i}(P(c_i)x_i^c)]^{1/Q}$.
The coefficient $\sqrt{Q_\alpha}$ is asymptotically sharp on the full
cone. A quadratic-representation isometry sends the reference to the
unit; diagonalize the transformed certificate and multiply its support
coordinates by $t$, keeping all other coordinates one. The gap is
$t\ip{s}{e}$ and the curve has length
$\sqrt{Q_\alpha}\log(1/t)$. Together with
\eqref{eq:exposed-distance}, this proves the exact leading coefficient
as $\eps\downarrow0$. An affine slice may exclude this upper path;
no sharp upper bound on an arbitrary slice is claimed.

Hauser and G\"uler's classification
\cite[Theorem~5.5]{HauserGuler2002} states that every self-scaled barrier
on a symmetric cone is, up to an additive constant, a sum of these
log-determinants on its irreducible factors with coefficients
$\alpha_i\geq1$. Thus \cref{thm:exposed-rank} covers every such barrier,
with its actual weighted scale; simply replacing $Q$ by $Q_\alpha$
while retaining the unweighted $\Delta_c$ would be incorrect.
The theorem is conditional on the certificate rank. A general lower
bound on the ranks attainable by different lift dictionaries is a
separate question, not an assumption hidden in this distance estimate.

\subsection{A dimension-only consequence for arbitrary cone barriers}
\begin{theorem}[Bounded operational factor dimension]
\label{thm:dimension-dictionary}
Let a compact full-dimensional convex body $C\subset\R^D$, $D\geq1$,
have an exact affine lift $C=\pi(K\cap\mathcal L)$, where $K$ is a
finite product of proper cones, $\mathcal L$ is an affine subspace,
and $\pi$ is an affine map. The minimal operational face is the
smallest face of $K$ containing the feasible lifted slice
$K\cap\mathcal L$; it is a product of faces of the original factors.
Regard this product in its linear span and remove its zero factors to
obtain the operational product cone. Suppose each factor of this
reduced cone has dimension at most an integer $d\geq2$.
For $h\geq1$ define
\[
 \Psi_d(h)=2\lfloor h/d\rfloor+\min\{h\bmod d,2\}.
\]
Every logarithmically homogeneous standard self-concordant barrier on
that operational product cone, including a coupled barrier, satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \nu\geq\Psi_d(D+1)\geq\frac{2(D+1)}d.
 \label{eq:dimension-parameter}
\end{equation}
For a strictly feasible conic pair on that cone, an exact central start
of gap $\Delta_0$, a strictly feasible terminal gap at most
$0<\eps<\Delta_0$, and at most $m$ forward product-metric $R$-chords
per round, where $m\in\N$ and $0<R<1$, the number of rounds is at least
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\sqrt{\Psi_d(D+1)/2}\log(\Delta_0/\eps)}{-m\log(1-R)}.
 \label{eq:dimension-movement}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $M$ be the dimension of the operational product space.
The affine projection of its feasible slice spans $\R^D$, so $M\geq D$.
If $M=D$, its linear projection is invertible and the slice has full
affine hull in the operational space. There can then be no effective
affine equality restriction: the feasible slice is the whole cone.
Its invertible affine image is unbounded, contrary to compactness.
Therefore $M\geq D+1$. This elementary dimension argument is compatible
with the classical slack-factorization framework~\cite{GouveiaParriloThomas2013},
but needs no selection of boundary certificates.

Let $r_0$ count ray factors and $q_0$ count the factors of dimension at
least two. In each nonray factor choose a two-dimensional linear plane
containing an interior point. Its cone section is proper and
full-dimensional in the plane, hence linearly isomorphic to $\R_+^2$;
its relative interior lies in the factor's interior and its boundary in
the factor's boundary. Together with all rays, these sections give an
interior orthant section of dimension $r_0+2q_0$.
Restriction preserves standard self-concordance, logarithmic degree,
and barrier blow-up. The classical orthant parameter lower bound
\cite{NesterovNemirovskii1994,Hildebrand2013} therefore gives
$\nu\geq r_0+2q_0$, even when the original barrier couples factors.
The dimension cap gives $r_0+dq_0\geq M\geq D+1$.

To solve the integer minimization, write $h=ad+b$, $0\leq b<d$.
For $q_0\leq a$ the smallest possible charge is
$2q_0+h-dq_0=h-(d-2)q_0$, minimized at $q_0=a$ and equal to $2a+b$.
For $q_0\geq a+1$ the charge is at least $2a+2$, attained with no
rays. The minimum is therefore $2a+\min(b,2)$; the case $b=0$ is
attained with $q_0=a$. Since $\min(b,2)\geq2b/d$, this proves
\eqref{eq:dimension-parameter}. Apply \cref{thm:pd-gap-set} for
\eqref{eq:dimension-movement}.
\end{proof}
The parameter conclusion concerns a barrier on the operational cone,
not an arbitrary barrier on the projected body or affine slice.
This is a short synthesis of classical restriction, dimension, and
primal--dual geometry. It does not assert an optimal lift-dimension
frontier. For a product of balls, $D=\sum_as_a$; the bound therefore
sees the total source dimension without curvature or regularity
hypotheses.

\subsection{Grouped balls, norm trees, and PSD completion}
\label{subsec:concrete-formulations}
The following concrete formulations illustrate why reducing the number
of conic blocks need not reduce movement in the displayed restricted
barrier. Full proofs, including the treatment of all auxiliary variables,
are in \cref{app:formulations}.

Take $k$ unit balls, a unit support direction $c_a$ for each, and
objective $\ell=\sum_a\ip{c_a}{w_a}$ of optimum $k$.
For a grouped paraboloid lift, partition each ball into $h$ coordinate
groups, introduce $q_{aG}=s_{aG}-\norm{w_{aG}}^2>0$ with
$\sum_Gs_{aG}=1$, and use $F=-\sum_{a,G}\log q_{aG}$.
For a PSD packing, place these $H=kh$ group columns into arbitrary real
matrix blocks $W_\ell$, allow every off-diagonal completion variable in
$S_\ell$, and use
\[
 D_\ell=S_\ell-W_\ell^TW_\ell\succ0,\qquad
 F=-\sum_\ell\log\det D_\ell,
 \qquad \sum_{\gamma\text{ from ball }a}(S_{\ell(\gamma)})_{\gamma\gamma}=1.
\]
Padding a group column with zeros is allowed; its free entries are its
own group coordinates. Each group occurs exactly once.
Both barriers have standard self-concordance and exact restricted
parameter $H$. Their central path $z(\tau)=\argmin(F-\tau\ell)$ has
$w_a=r(\tau)c_a$, with
\begin{equation}
 q(\tau)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{h^2+\tau^2}+h},\quad
 r(\tau)=\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{h^2+\tau^2}+h},\quad
 \left\|\frac{dz}{d\log\tau}\right\|^2
   =H\left(1-\frac h{\sqrt{h^2+\tau^2}}\right).
 \label{eq:grouped-packed-center}
\end{equation}
The residuals are $q_{aG}=q(\tau)$ or $D_\ell=q(\tau)I$.
For every $\tau_0\geq0$ and every accurate feasible $y$,
\begin{equation}
 d_F(z(\tau_0),y)\geq\sqrt H\,
          \pos{\log(q(\tau_0)H/(2\eps))}.
 \label{eq:grouped-packed-distance}
\end{equation}
In particular the analytic-center logarithmic scale is $k/(2\eps)$,
independent of the packing and its off-diagonal variables.

For a rooted norm tree with $b$ internal nodes, each with at least two
children, use one positive Lorentz block per node:
\begin{equation}
 q_{av}=t_{av}^2-\sum_{u\in I(v)}t_{au}^2
                    -\sum_{j\in J(v)}w_{aj}^2>0,
 \qquad t_{av}>0,
 \qquad t_{a,\mathrm{root}}=1.
 \label{eq:norm-tree-domain}
\end{equation}
Here $I(v)$ and $J(v)$ denote internal and leaf children. The positive
sheet is essential for convexity. Use $F=-\sum_{a,v}\log q_{av}$ and
put $L=kb$. Its exact parameter is $\nu=k\nu_T$, where
\begin{equation}
 \nu_T=\begin{cases}2b-2,&\text{the root has only internal children},\\
                         2b-1,&\text{the root has a leaf child}.
          \end{cases}
 \label{eq:tree-parameter}
\end{equation}
Its central path is \eqref{eq:grouped-packed-center} with $h=b,H=L$,
regardless of tree shape. If $n_v$ counts internal nodes below $v$
including $v$, and $C_{av}=\sum_{j\text{ below }v}c_{aj}^2$, the
internal coordinates are $t_{av}^2=n_vq+r^2C_{av}$.
At the analytic center $\bar z$, $q_{av}=1/b$, and every accurate $y$
satisfies the sharper barrier-height bound
\begin{equation}
 d_F(\bar z,y)\geq\frac{L}{\sqrt\nu}
                        \pos{\log(k/(2\eps))}
 \geq\sqrt{L/2}\,\pos{\log(k/(2\eps))}.
 \label{eq:tree-distance}
\end{equation}
The exact parameter proof is included because subtracting the fixed
root's contribution without a metric calculation would be unjustified.

For all three formulations the central route to error $\eps$, from the
analytic center, has length
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt{M_0}\,\rho(1-\eps/k)
 =\sqrt{M_0}\log(k/\eps)+O(\sqrt{M_0}),
 \qquad M_0=H\text{ or }L,
 \label{eq:formulation-route}
\end{equation}
for $0<\eps<k$, with the asymptotic as $\eps/k\downarrow0$.
Thus sampling this route and applying the distance lower bounds gives
matching $\Theta_R(\sqrt{M_0}\log(k/\eps))$ chord counts,
uniformly for $0<\eps\leq k/4$.
Approximate initialization and a fixed number of chords per round are
handled exactly as in \eqref{eq:exposed-rounds}.
These are statements about the specified metrics; block count alone
would prove none of these primal lower bounds.
