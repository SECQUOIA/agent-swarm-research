\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Path-following interior-point methods approach an optimum along the
central path of a self-concordant barrier $F$. Each step is short in the
local norm defined by the Hessian of $F$: a step from $x$ to $y$ is
allowed if $\norm{y-x}_{F,x}\leq R$, for a fixed $0<R<1$. Up to integer
rounding and constants that depend only on $R$, the least number of such
steps between two points equals their distance in the Riemannian metric
defined by this Hessian (\cref{cor:optimal-movement}). We ask how much
longer the central path is, in this metric, than the shortest route that
achieves the same goal.

The answer depends on the goal. A competing route may have to reach the
same central point, reach any point with the same objective accuracy, or
end at a feasible primal--dual pair with a small duality gap. Which
routes are allowed also matters. For the sparse linear program on the
cube $(-1,1)^r$ of \cref{sec:discrete}, the least number of
steps takes three different orders:
\[
 \underbrace{r\sqrt{\log r}}_{\text{any primal route}},
 \qquad
 \underbrace{r\log r}_{\text{near the central path}},
 \qquad
 \underbrace{r^{3/2}}_{\text{any feasible primal--dual route}}.
\]
Items~2 and~4 below give the precise conditions.

These are geometric comparisons, not operation counts: a linear
objective on a box is optimized by reading the signs of its
coefficients. The examples measure the cost of requiring short steps in
the barrier metric. They give no lower bound for algorithms without
this requirement and no arithmetic or oracle advantage for any method.

\subsection{Why the central path can be long}

The standard logarithmic barrier of the cube is $U(x)=\sum_i b(x_i)$
on $(-1,1)^r$, where $b(t)=-\log(1-t^2)$. In the coordinates
$y_i=\rho(x_i)$, where $\rho(t)=\int_0^t\sqrt{b''(u)}\dd u$, its
Hessian metric is Euclidean and shortest paths are straight lines. For a linear objective with positive weights, the
central path in these coordinates is $y_i(s)=H(s-a_i)$ for one fixed
increasing function $H$. Here $s$ is the logarithm of the central-path
parameter, the shift $a_i$ is determined by the $i$th objective weight,
and the common speed $H'$ increases from zero to one. When the shifts
are spread out, groups of coordinates start to move one after another,
so the central path turns through successive coordinate
directions, whereas the shortest route moves all coordinates together
(\cref{fig:flattened}).

\subsection{Main results}

We write $d_F$ for distance in the Hessian metric of $F$. The objective
gap of a point is the optimal value minus its objective value. We write
$L_\opt(\eps)$ for the distance from the analytic center to the set of
points with gap at most $\eps$ (the $\eps$-accurate set), and
$L_\CP(\eps)$ for the length of the central path from the analytic
center to its first point with gap $\eps$.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=\arabic*.]
\item \emph{Exact distances and the sharp rank factor.}
Consider spectral-norm balls of real or complex rectangular matrices,
spectral intervals in any Euclidean Jordan algebra, and products of
these domains, with their standard log-determinant barriers. The
distance from the analytic center is the Euclidean norm of the singular
values or eigenvalues after applying $\rho$ to each of them; paths that
rotate the spectral frame are not shorter. The squared distance
$L_\opt(\eps)^2$ is the value of a strictly convex problem in the
spectral coordinates, whose solution is determined by a single Lagrange
multiplier (\cref{thm:exact-distance,thm:water-filling}).
Suppose that all factors have the same scale (the coefficient of their
barrier) and that the objective has $r$ nonzero singular values or
eigenvalues. Then the length $L_\CP$ of every arc of the central path
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 L_{\CP}\leq\Gamma_r d_F(\text{its endpoints}),\qquad
 \Gamma_r=\left[\sum_{i=1}^r(\sqrt i-\sqrt{i-1})^2\right]^{1/2},
 \qquad \Gamma_r^2=\tfrac14\log r+O(1),
 \label{eq:intro-Gamma}
\end{equation}
and no smaller constant holds for all such objectives and arcs.
For the same accuracy $\eps$,
$L_{\CP}(\eps)\leq c_\star\Gamma_r L_\opt(\eps)$, where $c_\star$ is
the best constant in a one-dimensional inequality; we prove
$1.37486<c_\star<1.38$ with exact rational arithmetic
(\cref{thm:sharp-prefix,thm:scalar-dilation}). We do not claim that the
product $c_\star\Gamma_r$ is sharp. With unequal scales, the best
constant depends on the order in which coordinates start to move. It
can be of order $\sqrt r$, but only when the ratio of the largest to the
smallest scale is exponential in $r$ (\cref{prop:scale-order}).

\item \emph{Step sequences near the central path.}
For an explicit objective on the cube whose weights are powers of two
(the dyadic objective), an explicit tolerance $\eps_r$, and rational input of
length $\Theta(r^2)$, we have $L_\opt=\Theta(r\sqrt{\log r})$ and
$L_\CP=\Theta(r\log r)$ (\cref{thm:dyadic}). Since a step may cut
across a long part of a curve, arclength alone does not bound the number
of steps, so we prove directly that every sequence of steps of
local size at most $R$ that stays within a fixed distance of the central
path needs $\Omega(r\log r)$ steps to reach accuracy $\eps_r$, even if the
nearby central points occur in any order; following the path achieves
$O(r\log r)$. If the distance $\delta_r$ from the path grows with $r$, the ratio of
this step count to the optimal one still tends to infinity whenever
$\delta_r=o((\log r)^{1/3})$ (\cref{thm:tube}). This covers the usual
Newton-decrement neighborhoods of fixed radius $0\leq\beta<1/2$.

\item \emph{Other barriers.}
Let $f$ be a fixed self-concordant barrier on an interval with
$(f')^2\leq f''$ on the side of its analytic center toward the optimum.
For objectives with positive weights, the separable barrier
$\sum_i f(x_i)$ has the same sharp factor $\Gamma_r$, satisfies
$L_\CP(\eps)\leq C_{\SC}\Gamma_r L_\opt(\eps)$ with a constant
$C_{\SC}<2$ that does not depend on $r$, and shows the same step-count
gap on the dyadic example. This class contains the universal and entropic barriers of the
cube (\cref{thm:normalized-scalar,prop:canonical-cube}). Without the
condition $(f')^2\leq f''$, the ratio can approach $\sqrt r$ for scalar
barriers whose parameter grows.
If a convex coupling term added to $U$ has first and second derivatives
bounded on the whole facet collar $\{x_1\geq1-\delta\}$, $0<\delta<1$,
then the worst-case ratios $L_\CP/d_F$ and
$L_\CP(\eps)/L_\opt(\eps)$ are still at least $\Gamma_r$
(\cref{thm:facet-coupling}). We give a coupled barrier with a dense
Hessian and a coupled barrier invariant under all signed coordinate
permutations, both with barrier parameter $r$, the least possible for
the cube. For the second, the explicit radial family
\[
 F_{\lambda,c}(x)=U(x)-\lambda\log(c-\norm x^2),
 \qquad \lambda\geq1,\quad c-r\geq4\lambda,
\]
we also prove a matching upper bound $O(\Gamma_r)$, and the separation
for step sequences holds uniformly, even when $\lambda$ and $c$ depend
on $r$ (\cref{thm:radial-upper,cor:radial-discrete}). Hence neither an
optimal barrier parameter nor a large symmetry group guarantees a
bounded ratio. For general couplings we prove only the lower bound.

\item \emph{The cost of a dual solution.}
It is classical that, along the primal--dual central path, the speed in
the combined metric of a logarithmically homogeneous barrier and its
conjugate is $\sqrt\nu$ in the logarithmic parameter. We compute how
the objective splits this speed between the primal and dual parts. For
the classical barrier of the cone over a product of $k$ balls, whose
parameter $\nu=k+1$ is optimal among logarithmically homogeneous
barriers on this cone, the primal part can be much smaller than
$\sqrt\nu$, even when all objective weights are positive and the
optimizer is unique.
Write the dyadic linear program in standard form with the orthant
barrier. From its central point with $s=0$ to the central point with
$s=T=\Theta(r)$, which is $\eps_r$-accurate, the least number of
primal steps is again
$\Theta(r\sqrt{\log r})$, or $\Theta(r\log r)$ through exact central
points. In the primal--dual barrier metric, the least
number of steps to a strictly feasible primal--dual pair with gap at
most $2\eps_r$ is $\Theta(r^{3/2})$; the lower bound holds even if
intermediate points violate the equality constraints (\cref{thm:completion}).
\end{enumerate}

For any distribution of the objective's singular values or eigenvalues,
we also give explicit two-sided bounds on the
central speed and on the objective gap along the path, with geometric
and polynomial decay as examples; a simpler lower bound on
$L_\opt(\eps)$, based on determinants, can be zero while
$L_\opt(\eps)$ tends to infinity (\cref{sec:distribution}). For
reformulations with auxiliary variables over products of symmetric
cones, with log-determinant barriers of coefficients $\alpha_i\geq1$,
the rank of a dual solution that exposes the optimum bounds primal
distance from below, even when the auxiliary variables move freely. A
bound on the dimensions of the cone factors bounds primal--dual movement
from below for every logarithmically homogeneous barrier. Grouped balls,
norm trees, and packings of positive semidefinite columns are worked out
completely, including exact parameters of the restricted barriers and
balls of different sizes with unequal objective weights
(\cref{sec:formulation,app:formulations}).

\subsection{Relation to prior work}
\label{subsec:geometric-prior}

Nesterov and Todd~\cite{NesterovTodd2002} developed the Riemannian
geometry of self-concordant barriers, its relation to short steps, and
the rule that squared distances add over products. Their Section~6.2
flattens the metric of a separable barrier on the hypercube by
integrating its scalar local norm. Their primal--dual theorem bounds the
central-path length by $\sqrt2$ times the endpoint distance in the
metric of a logarithmically homogeneous cone barrier plus its conjugate,
and their Theorem~5.1(c) and Corollary~5.1 extend this bound to
the whole set of feasible pairs with small gap, allowing intermediate
points to violate the affine constraints. We restate this known result
as \cref{thm:pd-gap-set} for contrast with shorter primal routes.

Nesterov and Nemirovski~\cite{NesterovNemirovski2008} compared primal
central paths with geodesics. If $D$ is the distance between two central
points, their Theorem~4.1 gives
\begin{equation}
 L\leq\log2+\nu^{1/4}\sqrt{D(D+\log12)}.
 \label{eq:NN-comparison}
\end{equation}
Their Example~1.1 distinguishes a central endpoint from a set of
accurate points, and their Theorem~5.3 compares with such a set under an
explicit separation assumption. On specific spectral domains, we
compute the distance to the set of accurate points exactly and replace
the dependence on the barrier parameter $\nu$ by an exact constant that
depends only on the objective rank.

Worst-case central paths and lower bounds for path-following methods
have a separate history. Deza, Nematollahi and
Terlaky~\cite{DezaNematollahiTerlaky2008} prove iteration lower bounds
with Klee--Minty constructions. Allamigeon, Benchimol, Gaubert and
Joswig~\cite{AllamigeonEtAl2018} construct long, winding logarithmic
central paths with exponential iteration lower bounds for methods that
stay near the path, and Allamigeon, Gaubert and
Vandame~\cite{AllamigeonGaubertVandame2022} prove exponential lower
bounds for self-concordant path following in their neighborhood model. Allamigeon, Dadush, Loho, Natura and
V\'egh~\cite{AllamigeonEtAl2025} compare interior-point methods, across
self-concordant barriers, with \emph{straight-line complexity}, the
number of affine segments needed in a wide neighborhood of the central
path; Dadush, Ma, Natura and V\'egh~\cite{DadushMaNaturaVegh2026}
improve these wide-neighborhood bounds and prove that trust-region path following in the narrow $\ell_2$
neighborhood is approximately instance optimal. These works count
affine segments or iterations, not the fixed-radius steps counted here.
Our dyadic example separates movement near the central path from
unrestricted movement in the same metric on a simple domain; it is
neither the first example of an inefficient central path nor an
exponential lower bound for every barrier.

Several ingredients are classical. The prefix inequality behind
$\Gamma_r$ is a finite weighted Lorentz-norm inequality
\cite{Lorentz1951}; what is new is that central-path velocities come
arbitrarily close to it. The Hessian calculations for matrices and Jordan algebras use
spectral differentiation~\cite{LewisSendov2001,Baes2007}. Ohara, Ishi
and Tsuchiya~\cite{OharaIshiTsuchiya2024} give metric projections and
Hessian isometries between conjugate barriers. The universal and
entropic barriers and their exact parameters are known
\cite{NesterovNemirovskii1994,LeeYue2021,BubeckEldan2019,Chewi2023};
we derive their central-path consequences. Parameter-preserving
diagonal quadratic regularization of box barriers is also
known~\cite{CastroCuesta2011}; for our coupled examples, which also have
the exact parameter, we add central-path comparisons.

Geodesic steps in symmetric-cone algorithms~\cite{Permenter2023} and
interior-point methods on Riemannian manifolds
\cite{HiraiNieuwboerWalter2026} address different settings. The real
Hessian metric on a bounded spectral ball used here differs from the
affine-invariant metric on a symmetric cone and from complex Bergman
metrics.

To our knowledge, the following are new: the exact supremum
\eqref{eq:intro-Gamma} and, combined with the exact distance to the
accurate set, the bound $L_\CP(\eps)\leq c_\star\Gamma_r L_\opt(\eps)$;
the gap between $\Theta(r\sqrt{\log r})$ and $\Theta(r\log r)$ steps for
sequences whose nearby central points may occur in any order; the
comparisons for the radial family, uniform in $\lambda$ and $c$; and
the three step-count orders on one linear program.

\subsection{Organization}
\Cref{sec:foundations} defines the metric and the step counts.
\Cref{sec:exact-distance,sec:sharp-centrality} compute exact distances
and prove the sharp comparison. \Cref{sec:distribution,sec:discrete}
treat the distribution of objective weights and step sequences,
\cref{sec:barrier-dependence,sec:coupled-barriers} other separable and
coupled barriers, \cref{sec:primal-dual} primal versus primal--dual
step counts, and \cref{sec:formulation} other formulations. The
appendices contain the rational certificate for $c_\star$, the proof
that $C_{\SC}<2$, a smooth barrier with nonmonotone central speed, and
the proofs for the concrete formulations. The figures illustrate
proved results numerically and can be reproduced with the included
scripts.
