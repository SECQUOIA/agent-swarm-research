This agrees with the Hermitian-dilation formula and confirms the factor $2g''=b''$. Repeated and zero singular values do not defeat the almost-everywhere argument: at a repeated dilation eigenvalue one diagonalizes the compressed derivative, and all divided-difference coefficients stay positive. Fixed-frame interpolation attains the lower bound; no assertion of equality for arbitrary equal-spectrum endpoints is made.
- Verified the Jordan inversion coefficient directly by differentiating $z\circ z^{-1}=e$. On $V_{ij}$, $L_z$ acts by $(z_i+z_j)/2$ and $L_{z^{-1}}$ by $(z_i^{-1}+z_j^{-1})/2$, giving $-(z_i z_j)^{-1}$. This validates the Peirce Hessian normalization, including repeated eigenvalues. The argument uses Jordan identities rather than associative matrix multiplication and therefore covers the Albert algebra. Direct-product factors create zero Peirce subspaces, not an exception.
- Checked signed Jordan alignment: the transition matrix of two Jordan frames is doubly stochastic under the trace inner product. Taking absolute values and rearranging bounds the objective; arbitrary assigned signed magnitudes are valid Jordan eigenvalues in the objective frame.
