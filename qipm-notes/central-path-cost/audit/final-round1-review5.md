## Requested minor corrections

1. **MINOR — qualify the exact-distance and convex-allocation sentences in the abstract.** Location: `main.tex:12–14`. The abstract first introduces unspecified barrier Hessian metrics, then says that distance is exactly Euclidean in transformed spectral coordinates and target distance is a convex allocation. Later it also includes general normalized scalar and coupled radial barriers. Exact spectral flattening and the strictly convex allocation are the results for the displayed standard barriers; the general scalar allocation is expressly not proved convex, and the radial coupled metric is only uniformly comparable to the standard one. The body makes these distinctions correctly, so this is a scope ambiguity in the standalone abstract, not a failure of the theorems. Fix: start the exact-distance sentence with “For the standard logarithmic and log-determinant barriers, …” or equivalent. Retain the later, separately scoped barrier-dependence claims.

The review currently discusses the 2025 straight-line-complexity paper but omits its published 2026 successor, Daniel Dadush, Haoyuan Ma, Bento Natura and László A. Végh, *Trust Region Interior Point Methods: Optimal ℓ₂- and Faster Wide-Neighborhood Path Following*, STOC 2026, pp. 755–766, DOI `10.1145/3798129.3800790`. This work improves wide-neighborhood running time measured by straight-line complexity and obtains an approximately instance-optimal narrow ℓ₂-neighborhood trust-region method. Its ability to traverse near-linear portions independently of their length makes the manuscript's distinction between affine segments and bounded Hessian movement especially relevant. Add a short precise sentence and citation adjacent to the 2025 discussion, preserving the distinction between these resources. The classical full gap-set theorem is explicitly credited to Nesterov–Todd, including intermediate affine infeasibility. The optimal product-ball cone barrier is justified by restriction of a classical spectral-cone barrier, not by an invalid subtraction rule.
- The exposed Jordan minor has the correct weighted scale, and its covector contraction holds before restriction, hence permits all auxiliary fibers.
