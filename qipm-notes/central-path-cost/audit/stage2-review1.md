## Findings

The gap identity alone does not imply the scalar coordinate formula or the unit-scale primal metric used to prove that bound. The intended statement is valid if the conic barrier restricts, up to an additive constant, to the unit-scale spectral barrier under discussion, so that its primal projection is exactly the central path analyzed here. **Repair:** state that hypothesis and that the length uses the restricted Hessian metric, as is done carefully in the later residual-transfer paragraph in `03b-discrete.tex`.

   Concrete reason the restriction matters: represent the scalar interval by $k$ duplicate copies of each of its two slacks and equip the resulting orthant with its usual logarithmic barrier. This has $\nu=2k$, induced primal barrier $kb(x)$, central coordinate $q(\eta/k)$, and primal length to $\eta_f=\nu/\varepsilon$ equal to $\sqrt{k}\,\rho(q(2/\varepsilon))$. At $\varepsilon=1$, the unit-scale expression with $m=w_1=1$ and zero tail is $1/\sqrt2+\log(2k)$, which fails for large $k$ (for $k=10^4$, approximately $109.03$ versus $10.61$). This is a scope omission in the explanatory transfer sentence, not an error in the unit-scale theorem or in the later explicitly qualified conic residual transfer.

- **Distribution and rank certificates.** Checked all scalar constants, the antiderivative, normalized-head and nuclear/L2-tail schedules, the stronger factor-one allocation certificate, and its counterexample.
