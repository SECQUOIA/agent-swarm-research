## Weighted exposed minors and arbitrary cone factors

- Re-derived the principal-minor gradient norm from $P(c)P(x)P(c)=P(P(c)x)$. The Peirce compression is positive definite in its support algebra. Its inverse is taken only there, so a singular ambient certificate causes no inverse-domain problem. The norm identity is exactly $q$, including all inactive coordinates and cross-fiber directions; it does not assume that those fibers are fixed.
- Weighting by $\alpha_i$ gives a squared product dual norm $\sum_i\alpha_iq_i$, with possible reduction after affine restriction. The weighted AM–GM optimization puts $g_i=\varepsilon\alpha_iq_i/Q_\alpha$, producing both the $\alpha_i^{\alpha_iq_i}$ denominator and the powers in $\Delta_{c,\alpha}$. Keeping the old unweighted scale would be wrong, and the manuscript does not do so.
- The endpoint bound, positive part, initialization error, and per-round conversion follow with the stated metric displacement hypotheses. Zero-rank factors are omitted but otherwise unconstrained. The exact leading coefficient on the full cone follows from a cone isometry and radial contraction of precisely the exposed support coordinates; the manuscript does not extend this upper path to arbitrary affine slices.
- Checked the operational-dimension argument. If the product-space dimension equaled the projected body's full dimension, the projection would be invertible and a full-dimensional feasible affine slice could impose no effective affine equalities. Its image would be an unbounded affine image of the cone, contradicting compactness. Thus $M\geq D+1$.
- In every nonray proper factor, a two-dimensional linear section through an interior point is a proper planar cone, hence an orthant. Its relative interior lies in the original interior and its boundary in the original boundary, so restriction preserves the barrier property and logarithmic degree. Together with ray factors, this supplies the lower charge $r_0+2q_0$ even for a coupled barrier. The integer minimization under $r_0+dq_0\geq D+1$ is exactly $\Psi_d(D+1)$, and the further bound by $2(D+1)/d$ is valid.
- The conclusion concerns a barrier on the operational cone after minimal-face reduction. It does not incorrectly transfer an ambient cone parameter to a projected-body barrier or claim an optimal lift frontier.

The central coordinates, speed, late-center distance scale, and exact restricted parameter $H$ all follow.
