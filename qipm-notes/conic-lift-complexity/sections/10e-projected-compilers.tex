\section{Projection, sufficient statistics, and limits of local compilation}
\label{sec:projected-compilers}

In a problem with first-stage variables $x$ and recourse variables for
$N$ scenarios, an exact reformulation may eliminate the recourse
variables and optimize over the projection onto $x$.  This changes what
an algorithm can access: a small-parameter barrier on $x$ does not by
itself produce the recourse variables, and a small-parameter barrier
known only to exist need not come with a cheap reusable oracle.  Farkas
elimination, quadratic epigraph barriers, and quantum spectral
approximation are established tools; we combine them under explicit
access assumptions.  We also prove parameter lower bounds for two
compilation rules: a scalar-rescaled convolution construction and a
weighted sum of local logarithmic barriers.  We do not claim that a
small-parameter projected barrier preserves the geometry of the full
product of slacks.

\subsection{Polyhedral and ordered recourse}

Let $W\in\R^{q\times p}$ and $T\in\R^{q\times k}$ be public and consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-recourse}
 x\in X_0,\qquad Wy_i=h_i-Tx,\qquad y_i\geq0\quad(1\leq i\leq N).
\end{equation}
Write $H=\operatorname{range}W$ and
$C=\{z\in H:W^Tz\geq0\}$.  This polyhedral cone is pointed in $H$:
if $z,-z\in C$, then $W^Tz=0$ and $z\in H$, whence $z=0$.
Let $z_1,\ldots,z_L$ generate its extreme rays; $L=0$ is allowed.

\begin{proposition}[Shared recourse directions]\label{prop:projected-fan}
The projection of \eqref{eq:projected-recourse} is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-fan}
 x\in X_0,\qquad h_i-Tx\in H\ (i\leq N),\qquad
 a_\ell^Tx\geq\beta_\ell\ (\ell\leq L),
\end{equation}
where $a_\ell=-T^Tz_\ell$ and
$\beta_\ell=\max_i(-z_\ell^Th_i)$.
On the affine space defined by the displayed equalities, suppose $X_0$
has a $\nu_0$-barrier $G$, and remove inequalities that are identically
satisfied.  If the remaining inequalities have a common strict feasible
point in $\ri X_0$, then
\[
 G(x)-\sum_{\ell=1}^L\log(a_\ell^Tx-\beta_\ell)
\]
is a barrier of parameter at most $\nu_0+L$ on that relative domain.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Farkas' lemma gives $Wy=h_i-Tx$, $y\geq0$, precisely when
$z^T(h_i-Tx)\geq0$ for every $z$ satisfying $W^Tz\geq0$.
The lineality space of this dual cone is $H^\perp$; it imposes exactly
$h_i-Tx\in H$.  In $H$, the extreme rays generate $C$, so only their
inequalities remain.  Taking the maximum over $i$ gives
\eqref{eq:projected-fan}.  The barrier statement follows by affine
restriction and addition of logarithmic half-space barriers.
\end{proof}

If $W$ has full row rank, the equalities $h_i-Tx\in H$ hold
automatically.  Assume a coherent oracle for the blocks $h_i$, exact
comparisons at the given data precision, and public rays.  Then
quantum maximum finding computes all thresholds $\beta_\ell$, jointly
correct with constant probability, using
$\widetilde O(L\sqrt N)$ block queries
\citep{DurrHoyer1996}.  Here and below, the tilde hides logarithmic
factors from success amplification.  Derivatives of the compiled barrier then need no scenario
queries.  An iteration bound for path following also needs an initial
point and depends as usual on the target accuracy and the initial gap;
it does not follow from the barrier parameter alone.  Without full row
rank, the compatibility equalities must also be supplied or checked.

The following block-by-block reconstruction rule requires strict
feasibility.  Suppose $h_i-Tx\in\ri(W\R_+^p)$, equivalently there exists
$y>0$ with $Wy=h_i-Tx$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-canonical-recourse}
 y_i^c(x)=\underset{Wy=h_i-Tx,\ y>0}{\operatorname{argmin}}
 \left\{\tfrac12\norm y^2-\sum_{j=1}^p\log y_j\right\}
\end{equation}
exists uniquely.  Indeed, the objective is coercive at infinity, diverges
at the coordinate boundary, and has positive-definite Hessian.
Let $x$ vary in the affine compatibility space
$\mathcal A_i=\{x:h_i-Tx\in\operatorname{range}W\}$.
For a direction $h$ tangent to $\mathcal A_i$, after reducing $W$ to
independent rows and applying the same row operations to $T$, the
derivative of the KKT conditions is
\[
 \begin{bmatrix}I+\operatorname{diag}(y_j^{-2})&W^T\\W&0\end{bmatrix}
 \begin{bmatrix}Dy[h]\\D\lambda[h]\end{bmatrix}
 =\begin{bmatrix}0\\-Th\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The matrix is invertible by positive definiteness and full row rank.
Thus $y_i^c$ is smooth on the relatively open subset of $\mathcal A_i$
where strictly positive feasible recourse exists, and depends only on
block $i$ and $x$.  Computing it requires solving a convex problem for
that block, not one arithmetic operation.
Nonnegative feasibility alone does not suffice for
\eqref{eq:projected-canonical-recourse}; at a boundary point one must
instead solve a local feasibility problem or first determine its minimal
face.

For varying $W_i\in\R^{q\times p}$, suppose first that each has full row
rank.  Its pointed dual cone has at most $\binom p{q-1}$ extreme rays.
An extreme ray has $q-1$ independent active inequalities: otherwise its
minimal face has dimension at least two.  Each such subset specifies a line,
and pointedness permits at most one orientation of that line.  This proves
the count, including $q=1$.  For rank $r_i<q$, apply the argument in
$\operatorname{range}W_i$, obtaining $\binom p{r_i-1}$ rays when $r_i>0$,
and retain the orthogonal compatibility equalities separately.  A rank-zero
block gives only equalities.  Consequently, for fixed $q$, one block query
and polynomial-time local arithmetic enumerate the inequalities defining
the projection of that block.

This has a conditional algorithmic consequence.  Suppose these
inequalities and any constraints for $X_0$ define a full-dimensional LP
in $k$ effective variables with finite optimum.  Assume coherent row
access, together with the data-precision assumptions and an initial point
close to the central path that are required by \citet[Theorems~1.3 and~6.1]{ApersGribling2026}.
Their algorithm returns an explicit feasible $\epsilon$-optimal first-stage
vector.  With fixed $q$ and a polynomial number of public constraints for
$X_0$, simulating each row query locally gives
$\widetilde O(\sqrt N\,\operatorname{poly}(p,k))$ block queries.
This is only a reduction to that algorithm; initialization, facial
reduction, and output of the full recourse are separate tasks.

Finally, if the recourse constraints have the ordered form
$h_0+\alpha_i e-Tx\in K$, where $K$ is a proper cone and $e\in K$, they
are equivalent to the single constraint with
$\alpha_* =\min_i\alpha_i$, because
$(\alpha_i-\alpha_*)e\in K$ and the minimum is attained.
For a symmetric cone of rank $r$, its standard barrier adds at most $r$
to the first-stage parameter.  The threshold again takes
$\widetilde O(\sqrt N)$ queries.  The scalar family $x\geq z_i$,
$z_i\in\{0,1\}$, gives the standard OR lower bounds
$\Omega(\sqrt N)$ quantum and $\Omega(N)$ randomized
\citep{BBBV1997} for computing the threshold with error below $1/2$.
These statements concern ordered offsets only; arbitrary offsets have no
such single-threshold reduction.

\subsection{Additive quadratic and matrix sufficient statistics}

Consider the closed formulation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-quadratic-lift}
 r_i=A_ix-b_i,\qquad t_i\geq\norm{r_i}^2,\qquad
 \tau\geq\sum_i t_i,\qquad x\in X_0.
\end{equation}
It uses $N$ rotated Lorentz factors and one ray.  With the canonical
barriers, its ambient parameter is $2N+1$, excluding a barrier for $X_0$.
Its exact projection is
$\tau\geq q(x)=\sum_i\norm{A_ix-b_i}^2$.

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic compilation]\label{prop:projected-quadratic}
Set $C_i=[A_i,-b_i]$, $\xi=(x,1)$, and
\[
 M=\sum_i C_i^TC_i,\qquad Q=\sum_i A_i^TA_i,\qquad
 g=\sum_i A_i^Tb_i.
\]
Then $q(x)=\xi^TM\xi$.  On the quotient by $\ker Q$, the function
$f(x,\tau)=-\log(\tau-q(x))$ is a barrier of exact parameter one.
On unquotiented variables, if $G$ is a $\nu_0$-barrier for $X_0$, then
$G+f$ is a barrier of parameter at most $\nu_0+1$.
With $s=\tau-q(x)$ and $d=2(Qx-g)$, the derivatives of $f$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-quadratic-hessian}
 \nabla f=(d,-1)/s,\qquad
 \nabla^2 f=\begin{bmatrix}2Q/s&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}
              +(d,-1)(d,-1)^T/s^2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expansion gives the sufficient statistic and the derivatives.  Since
$\ker Q=\bigcap_i\ker A_i$, $q$ is constant along the deleted directions.
For a line direction $h$, put $a=Ds[h]$ and
$b=-D^2s[h,h]\geq0$.  Direct differentiation gives
\[
 Df[h]=-a/s,\quad D^2f[h,h]=a^2/s^2+b/s,\quad
 D^3f[h,h,h]=-2a^3/s^3-3ab/s^2.
\]
For $u,v\geq0$, $2u^3+3uv\leq2(u^2+v)^{3/2}$, as is seen by
squaring and subtracting, leaving $3u^2v^2+4v^3\geq0$.
Apply this with $u=|a|/s$, $v=b/s$ to obtain self-concordance.
Also $(Df[h])^2\leq D^2f[h,h]$.  The Hessian is positive definite
on the quotient, and the function diverges at the epigraph boundary.
The direction that varies only $\tau$ attains equality in the gradient
inequality, so the parameter is exactly one.  Addition of $G$ removes any
remaining flat directions and gives the asserted parameter bound.
\end{proof}

This is the standard quadratic epigraph barrier, not a new formula.  It
replaces the $N$ product factors exactly by one statistic while keeping
local access to the recourse variables.
Querying block $i$ recovers $r_i=A_ix-b_i$ and $t_i=\norm{r_i}^2$.  These
values extend every feasible point $(x,\tau)$ of the projection to a
feasible point of \eqref{eq:projected-quadratic-lift}; strict
feasibility follows by distributing part of the positive coupling slack
$\tau-q(x)$ among the $t_i$.  Materializing all $N$
recourse scalars requires $\Omega(N)$ output operations, whereas one
requested block uses one block query plus local arithmetic.

The statistic can also be approximated without losing feasibility.  Let $C$ be the stack of
all $C_i$, with $m$ rows and $k+1$ columns.  The quantum spectral
approximation theorem of \citet[Theorem~3.1]{ApersGribling2026} gives, with
high probability, $\widetilde M$ satisfying
\[
 (1-\delta)\widetilde M\preceq M\preceq(1+\delta)\widetilde M
\]
in $\widetilde O(\sqrt{m(k+1)}/\delta)$ row queries, for $0<\delta<1$.
Row sparsity affects arithmetic cost separately; rank deficiency is allowed.
Taking $\widehat M=(1+\delta)\widetilde M$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-gram-inflation}
 M\preceq\widehat M\preceq\frac{1+\delta}{1-\delta}M.
\end{equation}
Thus $\tau\geq\xi^T\widehat M\xi$ implies original feasibility.
For pure least-squares minimization over a fixed $X_0$, an exact minimizer
of the inflated objective has original objective at most
$(1+\delta)/(1-\delta)$ times the original infimum, whenever a minimizer
exists; an additive solve error adds the same error to this bound.
Subsequent optimization uses only the explicit statistic.  When $m=Np$,
the cited bound is $\widetilde O(\sqrt{Np(k+1)}/\delta)$ row queries;
it bounds block queries only if the given block oracle simulates one row
query at constant cost.  The case $A_i=0$, $b_i\in\{0,1\}$, with zero or
one nonzero $b_i$, shows that approximating the objective with error
below $1/2$ computes OR, which gives the corresponding quantum and
randomized lower bounds on scenario queries.

The same exact compilation holds for matrix residuals.  Let
$R_i(x)=B_{i0}+\sum_{j=1}^k x_jB_{ij}\in\R^{d_i\times s}$ and let
$R(x)$ stack the $R_i(x)$.  We quotient $x$ by the directions $h$ with
$DR[h]=0$; the remaining coordinates and $Y$ are the effective variables.

\begin{proposition}[Matrix quadratic compilation]\label{prop:projected-matrix}
On $Y-R(x)^TR(x)\succ0$ in the effective variables,
\[
 f(x,Y)=-\log\det\bigl(Y-R(x)^TR(x)\bigr)
\]
is a barrier of exact parameter $s$.  No barrier for this domain has
parameter below $s$.  Without quotienting, addition of a $\nu_0$-barrier
for $X_0$ gives a barrier of parameter at most $\nu_0+s$.
All data enter through the positive-semidefinite block Gram matrix
\[
 \mathcal M_{j\ell}=\sum_i B_{ij}^TB_{i\ell},\qquad0\leq j,\ell\leq k,
\]
of order $(k+1)s$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $S=Y-R^TR$.  Along a direction, set
\[
 A=S^{-1/2}DS\,S^{-1/2},\qquad
 B=2S^{-1/2}(DR)^TDR\,S^{-1/2}\succeq0.
\]
Differentiation gives
\[
 Df=-\tr A,\quad D^2f=\tr A^2+\tr B,\quad
 D^3f=-2\tr A^3-3\tr(AB).
\]
The bounds $|\tr A^3|\leq\norm A_F^3$ and
$|\tr(AB)|\leq\norm A_F\tr B$, together with the scalar inequality
in Proposition~\ref{prop:projected-quadratic}, prove self-concordance.
Moreover $(\tr A)^2\leq s\tr A^2\leq sD^2f$.
The Hessian vanishes only if $DR=0$ and $DY=0$, so is positive definite
in the effective variables.  The boundary divergence is immediate.
Varying only $Y$ in direction $S$ gives decrement squared $s$.
For the lower bound against any barrier, fix $x$ and restrict
$Y=R(x)^TR(x)+\operatorname{diag}(u_1,\ldots,u_s)$.
This is the positive orthant, with its coordinate boundary in the boundary
of the full domain, so its parameter lower bound is $s$.
Finally, expanding $R^TR$ gives
$\sum_{j,\ell}\xi_j\xi_\ell\mathcal M_{j\ell}$, with $\xi_0=1$.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, by a Schur complement, the domain is a slice of a PSD cone
with one diagonal block fixed to the identity; the parameter $s$ does
not count that block.  Applying spectral
approximation to the stack $[B_{i0},\ldots,B_{ik}]$ and using
\eqref{eq:projected-gram-inflation} gives a uniform Loewner upper bound
on $R(x)^TR(x)$, by congruence with $\xi\otimes I_s$.
Thus the scalar feasibility argument also compiles a matrix epigraph.  It does not by itself give a relative
objective guarantee for an arbitrary SDP objective.

\subsection{A scalar normalization cannot repair every convolution barrier}

The preceding reduction uses an additive quadratic statistic.  For
general blocks, a natural alternative convolves measures associated with
the blocks and takes the Legendre transform of the log-Laplace function
of the result.  The next proposition concerns exactly this construction,
multiplied by a scalar that depends only on the number of blocks.

\begin{proposition}[Convolution normalization obstruction]
\label{prop:projected-cramer}
Let $\mu$ be the uniform probability measure on $[0,1]$, with log-Laplace
function $f$ and conjugate $F=f^*$.  For $N$ independent variables, consider
the conjugate of the log-Laplace function of their average, multiplied by a
positive scalar $a_N$.  Suppose this rule must be a standard
self-concordant barrier for both of the following collections:
$N$ copies of $\mu$, and one copy of $\mu$ with $N-1$ point masses at zero.
Then $a_N\geq1$.  Its barrier parameter on the replicated collection is
therefore at least $N$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Independence gives $f_{\mathrm{av}}(\theta)=Nf(\theta/N)$ for identical
copies, hence $f_{\mathrm{av}}^*(x)=NF(x)$.
For the second collection it gives $f_{\mathrm{av}}(\theta)=f(\theta/N)$,
hence $f_{\mathrm{av}}^*(x)=F(Nx)$.
For $\theta<0$,
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 f(\theta)&=\log(1-e^\theta)-\log(-\theta),&
 f'(\theta)&=-1/\theta+O(e^\theta),\\
 f''(\theta)&=\theta^{-2}+O(e^\theta),&
 f'''(\theta)&=-2\theta^{-3}+O(e^\theta).
 \end{aligned}
\]
As $x=f'(\theta)\downarrow0$, Legendre differentiation gives
$F'=\theta$, $F''=1/f''(\theta)$ and
$F'''=-f'''(\theta)/(f''(\theta))^3$.  Therefore
\[
 \frac{(F')^2}{F''}\longrightarrow1,\qquad
 \frac{|F'''|}{2(F'')^{3/2}}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Affine rescaling $x\mapsto Nx$ does not change either ratio.
Multiplying a function by $a_N$ divides the second ratio by
$\sqrt{a_N}$, so self-concordance for the second collection forces
$a_N\geq1$.  On the first collection, the first ratio tends to $a_NN$,
which is a lower bound on its barrier parameter.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis requires a barrier also for the collection with $N-1$
point masses at zero.  The proposition does not rule out a rule that uses
the individual distributions, and gives no lower bound for all barriers
of the projected interval; in particular, it does not exclude a
nonfactorized entropic or universal barrier.  We claim only the stated
impossibility for scalar normalizations; the conjugacy identities used
are classical.

\subsection{Two obstructions to reusable exact summaries}

Quadratic sufficient statistics have bounded dimension for fixed
first-stage dimension.  For sums of exponentials this fails,
even with one first-stage variable.

\begin{proposition}[Continuous exact summaries]\label{prop:projected-exp-summary}
Fix distinct real $a_1,\ldots,a_N$ and let
$q_w(x)=\sum_i w_i e^{a_ix}$, with $w\in\R_{++}^N$.
Suppose a continuous statistic $S:\R_{++}^N\to\R^d$ determines the entire
strict epigraph of $q_w$.  Then $d\geq N$.  The decoder need not be
continuous.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equal statistics give equal epigraphs and hence equal functions.  Their
first $N$ derivatives at zero give the Vandermonde system
$\sum_i(w_i-v_i)a_i^j=0$, $0\leq j<N$, so $w=v$.
Thus $S$ is injective.  If $d<N$, composing with the coordinate inclusion
$\R^d\hookrightarrow\R^N$ contradicts invariance of domain, since its
image lies in a subspace with empty interior.
\end{proof}

The next statement counts preprocessing queries instead of bounding the
dimension of a continuous statistic.  An offline summary, classical or
quantum, is \emph{reusable} if, after preprocessing, it answers all the
stated requests with joint success probability at least $2/3$ and makes
no further input queries.  This excludes treating a single quantum state,
which measurement consumes, as an oracle for arbitrarily many later
answers.

\begin{proposition}[Reusable boundary oracles]\label{prop:projected-reusable}
Let $z\in\{0,1\}^N$, $\gamma=1/4$, and define the planar epigraph
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-exposed-arcs}
 E_z=\{(x,t):t\geq\varphi_z(x)\},\qquad
 \varphi_z(x)=x^2+\max_{1\leq i\leq N}
                       (2ix-i^2+\gamma z_i).
\end{equation}
Each of its $N$ defining inequalities has a constant-size
representation with a rotated Lorentz cone.
A reusable summary giving all values $\varphi_z(i)$ to absolute error
less than $\gamma/3$ requires $\Omega(N)$ quantum preprocessing queries
to the bit oracle for $z$.
The same lower bound holds for a reusable summary that answers membership
queries at the public points $(i,2i^2+\gamma/2)$, each at vertical
distance $\gamma/2$ from the boundary of $E_z$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $x=i$, the $i$th affine term exceeds term $j\ne i$ by
$(i-j)^2+\gamma(z_i-z_j)\geq3/4$.  Thus
$\varphi_z(i)=2i^2+\gamma z_i$.  Either oracle recovers every bit.
For completeness, a $T$-query quantum algorithm on an $N$-bit input has
final state in the span of at most $D_T=\sum_{j=0}^T\binom Nj$ fixed
vectors: expand its amplitudes in the characters of the input signs,
whose degree increases by at most one per query.  Purification handles
intermediate measurements and randomness.  An ensemble of $2^N$ states
in a space of dimension at most $D_T$ can be identified, on a uniform
input, with probability at most $D_T/2^N$, because each density matrix is
bounded above by the identity on that space and the decoding POVM sums
to that identity.  For $T\leq N/4$, the binomial entropy bound makes
this probability tend to zero.  Constant successful recovery therefore
requires $T=\Omega(N)$, as in oracle interrogation
\citep{vanDam1998}.
\end{proof}

Rescaling the exposed locations to $x=i/N$ and the affine terms to
$2Ni x-i^2$ preserves the gap.  Replacing the common quadratic by
$x^r$ on $x>0$, for fixed rational $r>1$, or by $e^x$, gives the same
recovery proof with constant-size power or exponential constraints.
The locations then stay in a bounded interval, so the common exponential
term needs no high precision.  The lower bound applies only to reusable
preprocessing: a single requested boundary value can still be computed
with $\widetilde O(\sqrt N)$ fresh maximum-finding queries.  The bound
also does not rule out approximate summaries with error in a weaker
norm, or summaries that need to succeed only under an input distribution
for which the recovery argument fails.

\subsection{The parameter cost of additive local logarithms}

The same epigraph $E_z$ forces a large parameter for any barrier that is
a weighted sum of the local logarithms of the slacks, even though $E_z$
is two-dimensional.

\begin{proposition}[Additive local barrier cost]\label{prop:projected-local-barrier}
For the slacks
$s_i(x,t)=t-x^2-2ix+i^2-\gamma z_i$ in
\eqref{eq:projected-exposed-arcs}, suppose
\[
 F(x,t)=-\sum_{i=1}^N w_i\log s_i(x,t),\qquad w_i>0,
\]
is a standard self-concordant barrier with parameter $\nu$ on
$\intr E_z$.  Then $w_i\geq1$ for every $i$, and $\nu\geq\sum_iw_i\geq N$.
Nevertheless, this line-free planar domain admits a barrier of parameter
two.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $(i,\varphi_z(i)+\epsilon)$, the $i$th slack is $\epsilon$ and all
other slacks stay at least $3/4$.  In direction $h=(0,1)$,
\[
 D^2F[h,h]=w_i\epsilon^{-2}+O(1),\qquad
 |D^3F[h,h,h]|=2w_i\epsilon^{-3}+O(1).
\]
Hence the self-concordance ratio tends to
$1/\sqrt{w_i}$ as $\epsilon\to0$, forcing $w_i\geq1$.
For fixed $x$ and $t\to\infty$,
\[
 \frac{(DF[h])^2}{D^2F[h,h]}
 =\frac{(\sum_iw_i/s_i)^2}{\sum_iw_i/s_i^2}
 \longrightarrow\sum_iw_i.
\]
The gradient inequality implies the parameter bound.
The epigraph is full-dimensional and contains no affine line, because its
boundary grows quadratically in either horizontal direction and is bounded
below on each vertical line.  The universal-barrier theorem in dimension
two therefore supplies a two-parameter barrier \citep{LeeYue2021}.
\end{proof}

The lower and upper bounds concern different classes of barriers: a
barrier that combines the scenarios through a global construction can
have parameter independent of $N$; a weighted sum of these local
logarithms cannot.  Together with the lower bound for reusable oracles,
this shows that three requirements are distinct: existence of a
small-parameter projected barrier, of an explicit sufficient statistic,
and of a cheap oracle.
