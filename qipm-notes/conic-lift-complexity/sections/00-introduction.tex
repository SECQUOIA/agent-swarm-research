\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Many convex bodies in optimization are the linear image of an affine
slice of a product of standard cones, such as Lorentz cones or cones of
positive semidefinite (PSD) matrices. Such an exact conic lift turns
linear optimization over the body into a conic program that
interior-point methods can solve. The lifts of one body differ in the
number and dimensions of the cone factors, the ranks of the dual
certificates that prove a bound on a linear objective, the barrier
parameter on the affine slice, the data that a Newton step needs, and
the outputs that are inexpensive to produce; none of these resources
determines the others. We ask how small each resource can be over a
specified class of lifts, and how the resources bound distances in
barrier metrics and computational work.

Euclidean balls and their products are the main examples; their lifts
already show certificates shared between factors, boundary choices that
cannot be made globally smooth, and different barrier metrics. We also
treat $\ell_p$ norm balls, matrix balls, balance slices, power and
entropy cones, and exact compilers, which map given input data to an
exact representation in a prescribed format. Every exact formula is stated with the
class of lifts over which it is optimized; a lower bound for one
representation need not hold for every representation of its projection.

\subsection{Main results}
Let $C\subset\R^s$ be a compact convex body with the origin in its
interior. An \emph{exact lift} of $C$ is a triple $\mathcal L=(K,A,\pi)$
with $C=\pi(K\cap A)$, where $K=\prod_iK_i$ is a finite product of
proper cones, $A$ is an affine subspace, and $\pi$ is affine. Ranks and
barriers are computed after the reduction of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction},
which eliminates free variables and replaces $K$ by the smallest face
containing the feasible points; the result is a relative-Slater lift.
For $v\in\Sph^{s-1}$, a \emph{genuine certificate} of the support slack
$1-\langle x,v\rangle$ of the ball $\B_2^s$ is a dual element
$Y=(Y_i)\in K^*$ with $\langle X,Y\rangle=1-\langle\pi(X),v\rangle$ for
every $X\in A$, that is, on the whole affine slice and not only at
selected boundary points. These certificates form the nonempty
\emph{certificate fiber} $\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}(v)$. For a simple
symmetric cone, $r$ is its Jordan rank and $a$ its Peirce constant;
$\jrank Y$ is the Jordan rank of an element $Y$.

\paragraph{Curvature and cone dimension.}
Suppose that $C$ has a $C^2$ boundary patch with strictly positive
curvature at some point, and that $\mathcal L$ is an exact lift by
proper cones of real dimensions $m_i$ that are definable in a fixed
o-minimal structure (for example, semialgebraic). In the mixed second
derivative of the slack identity $1-\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_i\langle
X_i,Y_i\rangle$ at a boundary point, each factor of dimension $m$
contributes rank at most $\max\{m-2,0\}$, so
$s-1\leq\sum_i\max\{m_i-2,0\}$. For simple symmetric cones, at some
boundary point the primal ranks $p_i$ and the complementary dual ranks
$q_i$ satisfy $s-1\leq\sum_ia_ip_iq_i$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:curvature}).
Definable local selections suffice; no globally smooth selection is
needed. For a ball of dimension $s\geq2$ and an integer cap $d\geq3$ on
the cone dimensions, the least number of factors and the least total
cone dimension over exact definable lifts are therefore
\[
 k=\left\lceil\frac{s-1}{d-2}\right\rceil,
 \qquad M=s-1+2k,
\]
and classical norm trees attain both
(Corollary~\ref{cor:dimension-resources}).

\paragraph{Certificate rank.}
Fix a finite dictionary $\mathscr K$ of simple symmetric cones; a lift
through $\mathscr K$ may use any number of copies of each member, and
nonnegative rays. Put $B=\max_{K\in\mathscr K}a(K)(r(K)-1)>0$; for
example, $B=r-1$ for real PSD matrices of order $r$, $B=m-2$ for the
Lorentz cone $Q_m$ of dimension $m$, and $B=16$ for the Albert cone. For
every $s\geq2$, Theorem~\ref{thm:rank-frontier} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-rank}
 \inf_{\mathcal L}\ \max_{v\in\Sph^{s-1}}
 \min_{Y\in\mathcal D_{\mathcal L}(v)}\sum_i\jrank Y_i
 =\left\lceil\frac{s-1}{B}\right\rceil,
\end{equation}
where the infimum is over all exact finite relative-Slater affine lifts
of $\B_2^s$ through $\mathscr K$. The inner minimum runs over the whole
certificate fiber, not over a certificate selected by an algorithm, and
no smoothness of the lift is assumed. For each fixed lift, the lower
bound holds for every certificate at every direction in a dense open set
whose complement has surface measure zero. A construction from Peirce
perspectives (Lemma~\ref{lem:perspective}) attains the bound in every
simple family, including the Albert cone. The lift is fixed before the
direction; if it may depend on $v$, the value is one
\eqref{eq:quantifier-reversal}. Section~\ref{sec:products} treats
products of balls.

\paragraph{Global selection.}
If instead the boundary points of the lift and their certificates must
be $C^1$ functions on the whole sphere of directions, equality in a
local bound forces a map into a product of support orbits or of
boundaries of cone bases, and covering and cohomology arguments make
some resources larger. For example, the least worst-case certificate
rank becomes $\kappa_{\mathscr K}(s)$, which is one if $s=B+1$ and
$\mathscr K$ contains $Q_{B+2}$, and $\lceil s/B\rceil$ otherwise
(Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth-rank}). We also determine the least Lorentz
resources under global selection (Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth-lorentz}) and
prove rigidity theorems for products
(Sections~\ref{sec:regularity}--\ref{sec:full-fiber-rigidity}); those of
Section~\ref{sec:full-fiber-rigidity} assume a stronger hypothesis on
entire boundary fibers, not smooth selection.

\paragraph{Barrier parameters.}
We distinguish the standard product barrier on the ambient cone, its
parameter $\nustd$ after restriction to the affine slice
\eqref{eq:exact-gradient-parameter}, and the least parameter
$\vartheta_{\mathrm{opt}}$ of any self-concordant barrier on a fixed
affine domain. The restricted parameter is at least the total nullity
$\sum_i(r_i-\jrank X_i)$ at every feasible $X$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary-order}); recession directions give a similar
bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:recession}). With the rank theorem, this gives
$\nustd\geq\lceil(s-1)/B\rceil$ for every lift of $\B_2^s$ and the exact
minimum under global selection
(Proposition~\ref{prop:barrier-frontiers}). Without selection
assumptions, we determine the minimum over lifts in several cases
(Proposition~\ref{prop:barrier-frontiers},
Theorems~\ref{thm:one-channel} and~\ref{thm:wide-cap}). For dictionaries
of Hermitian PSD cones over one field up to a fixed order, or of Lorentz
cones up to a fixed dimension, the remaining single-ball cases are
$s=qB+1$ with $q\geq B+2$, where we prove only $q\leq\inf_{\mathcal
L}\nustd\leq q+1$ \eqref{eq:unsettled-range}.
Section~\ref{sec:nonsymmetric-barriers} likewise proves only bounds for
nonsymmetric cones. We compute $\vartheta_{\mathrm{opt}}$ for grouped,
column-packed, and balance slices (Theorems~\ref{thm:grouped-intrinsic},
\ref{thm:packing-intrinsic}, and~\ref{thm:balance-slices}), and the
restricted standard parameter of every norm tree
(Theorem~\ref{thm:tree-parameter}).

\paragraph{Movement in barrier metrics.}
For a barrier $F$, $d_F$ is the distance in the Riemannian metric of its
Hessian. A step of local norm at most $R<1$ moves at most
$\log(1/(1-R))$, so lower bounds on $d_F$ bound the number of steps
(Corollary~\ref{cor:movement-rounds}). If a certificate $(s_i)$
represents the objective gap on the whole slice of a symmetric-cone
lift, then in the barrier $-\sum_i\alpha_i\log\det x_i$ with
$\alpha_i\geq1$ the distance from a fixed reference point $x^0$ to the
points with gap at most $\epsilon$ is at least
$\sqrt{Q_\alpha}\,[\log(\Delta_\alpha/\epsilon)]_+$, where
$Q_\alpha=\sum_i\alpha_i\jrank s_i$ and $\Delta_\alpha>0$ is explicit
and depends on $x^0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:movement-minor}); with \eqref{eq:intro-rank}, this
gives bounds in terms of $B$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:movement-dictionary}).
A classical bound in the primal--dual metric of an arbitrary
logarithmically homogeneous barrier measures the distance from a point
on the central path to the strictly feasible primal--dual pairs with
gap at most $\epsilon$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:pd-gap-movement}).

A norm tree represents a ball by a rooted tree: each internal node $v$
has a variable $t_v$ that bounds the Euclidean norm of its children's
values, the leaves carry the ball coordinates $w$, and the root variable
equals one. The barrier sums the Lorentz log-determinant barriers with
weights $\omega_v\geq1$. Fix a unit leaf objective $c$, and call an
internal node $v$ active if its subtree contains a leaf $j$ with
$c_j\neq0$. For the target $1-c^Tw\leq\epsilon$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:tree-exact-distance} gives, from any fixed interior
reference point,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-tree}
 d(\epsilon)=\sqrt{K_c}\log(1/\epsilon)
              +O(\log\log(1/\epsilon)),\qquad
 K_c=\sum_{v\text{ active}}\omega_v
                  +\frac12\sum_{v\text{ inactive}}\omega_v,
\end{equation}
with the tree, weights, and objective fixed as $\epsilon\to0$. The
central path from the analytic center has coefficient
$\sqrt{\sum_v\omega_v}$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:tree-central-excess}). Thus
inactive subtrees add to the least distance although their certificate
blocks vanish, and they make the central path asymptotically longer than
the shortest path by the factor $(\sum_v\omega_v/K_c)^{1/2}$.

\paragraph{Newton systems of PSD packings.}
In the column-packed formulation of a product of balls, the ball
coordinates are placed in the columns of matrices $W_\ell$, each
constrained by
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}S_\ell&W_\ell^T\\W_\ell&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)\succeq0$,
with one linear allocation equation per ball on the diagonal entries of
the $S_\ell$ (Section~\ref{newt:section}). Fix the projected point. At
the fiber center, the minimizer of the barrier over the auxiliary
variables, the residuals $D_\ell^0=S_\ell-W_\ell^TW_\ell$ are diagonal
and the projected Newton system equals that of the grouped formulation
(Proposition~\ref{newt:center}). Let $H_0$ and $H_D$ be the projected
Hessians, after elimination of the auxiliary directions, at the fiber
center and at another feasible auxiliary point with residuals $D$. If
$\mu D^0\preceq D\preceq L D^0$, where $0<\mu\leq1\leq L$,
Theorem~\ref{newt:linear} proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-transfer}
 L^{-1}H_0\preceq H_D\preceq\mu^{-1}H_0,
 \qquad\kappa(H_0^{-1/2}H_DH_0^{-1/2})\leq L/\mu.
\end{equation}
A feasible example with two balls attains the condition factor
(Example~\ref{newt:obstruction}). Exact allocations are essential: for a
relative residual error $\delta<1$, the condition bound
$(1+\delta)/(1-\delta)$ becomes its square without them
(Corollary~\ref{newt:inexact}). The comparison neither determines the
right-hand side of the reduced Newton system nor constructs a cheap
preconditioner.

\paragraph{Computational costs.}
Section~\ref{res:section} gives exact integer tradeoffs among ambient
coordinates, free coordinates, and restricted barrier parameters. For
the grouped, norm-tree, and centered packed ball formulations without
dense additional affine coupling, sparse elimination solves each Newton
equation exactly with linearly many field operations. Hence, for an
outer method that accepts any direction satisfying a stated Newton
residual condition and outputs the full direction in every round, a
quantum Newton subroutine gives no polynomial saving in the dimension
when classical elimination has the same data access and arithmetic is
exact (Section~\ref{newt:section}). In the query model, we separate the
cost of a normalized quantum state of a projected solution from the
costs of readable values, full vectors, reusable exact compilers, and
scale updates after fresh data. On one explicit family with $n$ hidden
signs, the amplitude state of the projected optimizer needs one query,
whereas for every fixed $0<\epsilon<1/8$ any explicit $\epsilon$-optimal
output needs $\Theta_\epsilon(n)$ queries (Theorem~\ref{query:eja}). Our
work lower bound (Theorem~\ref{work:total}) assumes that every round
costs at least a stated amount of new work; this does not follow from
the access model, and a query lower bound for one solve cannot simply be
multiplied by the number of steps that a distance bound forces.

\subsection{Relation to the literature}
Conic lifts and slack factorizations are the framework of Gouveia,
Parrilo, and Thomas~\cite[Theorem~2.4]{GPT2013}; see also the survey
\citet{FGPPST2022Survey}. Fixed-size PSD factor counts
\citep{FP2013blocks}, PSD rank and bounds from algebraic boundaries
\citep{FGPRT2015,FSED2018}, and semidefinite extension degree, which
minimizes the largest PSD block while allowing finite products
\citep{Averkov2019}, measure related but different resources. Fawzi's
obstruction for second-order cone (SOC) lifts and Saunderson's theorem
on short chains of faces use neighborliness to exclude finite product
lifts in their settings~\citep{Fawzi2019SOC,Saunderson2020}. Kummer's
bounds for balls concern direct spectrahedral descriptions
\citep{Kummer2016}, not projections of products of cones. Positive
curvature does not obstruct SOC representability:
Scheiderer~\cite[Theorem~1.2]{Scheiderer2025SOC} proves that compact
convex semialgebraic sets with Nash-smooth boundary and strictly
positive curvature everywhere have SOC lifts, with an arbitrary finite
number of PSD blocks of order two. That existence theorem does not
determine the least number of factors, the minimax certificate rank, or
the minima under global selection.

The Jordan classification, Peirce spaces, quadratic representations, and
support manifolds are classical~\citep{FK1994,Nomura1994,GS2010}, and
factorization algorithms for symmetric cones are studied
in~\citep{SV2024}. Recursive norm representations by small Lorentz cones
are also classical~\cite[Section~2]{BTN2001}. We use standard covering,
bundle, and cohomology tools~\citep{Hatcher2002,Steenrod1951}; our
contributions are the quantitative inequalities, the quantifiers over
certificate fibers, the rigidity consequences, and the attaining
constructions.

Self-concordant barrier theory is the analytic framework \citep{NN1994}.
Optimality of the standard barriers on symmetric cones in the ambient
space is due to G\"uler--Tun\c{c}el and Cardoso--Vieira
\citep{GT1998,CardosoVieira2006}; self-scaled barriers have been
classified~\citep{HauserGuler2002}. Conic extension, affine restriction,
and projective transformation of barriers belong to an existing theory
\citep{Hildebrand2022Projective}, so the distinction between ambient and
restricted parameters is not new; we compute particular restricted and
intrinsic parameters. For nonsymmetric cones we use established
constructions~\citep{Hildebrand2013,Chares2009,FS2023}. The parameter
bound for the universal barrier~\citep{LeeYue2021} and practical
methods for natural cones~\citep{CKV2022natural} show why a small parameter should be
separated from the cost of evaluating derivatives.

Nesterov--Todd and Nesterov--Nemirovski compared central paths with
Riemannian distances in barrier metrics
\citep{NT2002,NesterovNemirovski2008}. The unpublished companion
\emph{The Cost of Following the Central
Path}~\citep{CentralPathCompanion} already contains the weighted
exposed-minor estimate, several movement bounds that depend on the
distribution of the objective weights, and the standard-barrier
calculations for grouped, packed, and norm-tree formulations; we
reproduce the proofs needed here. New here are the combination with the
certificate-rank theorem, the general exposure profile for affine PSD
slices, and the weighted-tree coefficient \eqref{eq:intro-tree}. The
general distinction between central-path length and shortest distance is
not new.

The computational arguments use search, oracle interrogation,
approximate counting, and information
bounds~\citep{BBBV1997,vanDam1998,NayakWu1999,Nayak1999,BHMT2002};
sparse cone augmentation~\citep{DomahidiChuBoyd2013}; and elimination
from a supplied tree decomposition~\citep{FurerHoppenTrevisan2025}. The
relevant algorithms for comparison are quantum interior-point methods
and their end-to-end resource
analyses~\citep{AugustinoEtAl2023,DalzellEtAl2023}. Recent iterative
refinement improves their high-precision behavior
\citep{MohammadisiahroudiEtAl2026}; we do not claim that all such
methods retain the earlier dependence on inverse accuracy. The
first-power improvement in \eqref{eq:intro-transfer} uses elementary
trace inequalities and the allocation equations; it is not a new general
principle about matrix order.

To our knowledge, the following results are new, in the models stated
above: the selection-free minimax rank \eqref{eq:intro-rank}, the
weighted-tree coefficient \eqref{eq:intro-tree} with its active and
inactive nodes, and the sharp Hessian comparison
\eqref{eq:intro-transfer} for feasible allocations. The claim concerns
these formulas and their quantifiers, not the general methods used to
prove them.
