\section{Closing synthesis}\label{sec:synthesis}
Conic reformulation has several distinct costs. Local curvature constrains
cone resources; the full certificate fiber determines the least exposing
rank; global regularity can impose additional topological costs. Affine
restriction can lower a standard barrier parameter, while changing the
barrier on the same domain is another optimization problem. The exact
formulas proved here identify these costs in their stated models. The
bounded interval \eqref{eq:unsettled-range} and the nonsymmetric parameter
intervals delimit their reach. The general integer ledgers give necessary
resource bounds; their integer minima need not be attainable lift optima.

The weighted tree coefficient makes the metric distinction concrete.
Active support ranks explain part of the distance, but objective-free
linked subtrees contribute half their barrier weight to its squared
leading coefficient. A central route pays their full weight. The PSD
quotient theorem gives a different concrete lesson: retaining exact
source allocations improves a spectral transfer from squared to first
powers, while changing the auxiliary point can still change the Newton
right-hand side and the required data.

These geometric conclusions become computational conclusions only through
a specified interface. A short description, an inexpensive normalized
state, a readable solution, and a reusable exact compiler are different
outputs. Fresh scale updates have a temporal query cost under the stated
commitment contract; fixed data can instead be cached. Thus the paper's
work bounds use explicit access and charging assumptions, and its metric
bounds retain their objective, endpoint, and barrier. Keeping these
conditions visible is what makes the formulation comparisons reusable.
