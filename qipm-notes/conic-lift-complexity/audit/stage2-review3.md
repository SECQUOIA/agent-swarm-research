## Verdict

**No major issues found.** The Lorentz phase calculation, pointwise no-sharing, the compact-piece/top-class cover proof, the heterogeneous smooth resource minima, the joint-contact comparison, and the everywhere-differentiable obstruction are sound. Two small statement clarifications should be made before accepting the stage.

## Valid minor corrections

1. **State that the capacity cap is an integer.** At `04-global-regularity.tex:87`, replace “Fix $c\geq1$” with “Fix an integer $c\geq1$.” The formulas and the proof use an integral cap (including divisibility and grouping into sets of size at most $c$). As literally written, allowing a real cap gives false formulas: for $p=2$, $c=3/2$, only $Q_3$ is available but the stated factor count is $\lceil3/(3/2)\rceil=2$, whereas the proved integer-cap result requires three factors. This is a missing explicit convention, with an immediate local correction; it does not affect the intended theorem or its proof. Use the same integer convention in Proposition `prop:differentiable`, if it is intended to stand independently.

2. **Identify the class minimized over in the nonsmooth comparison.** At `04-global-regularity.tex:158–168`, explicitly say that the exact minima are over exact relative-Slater affine lifts, or over full-row factorizations admitting definable selectors. The preceding theorem concerns abstract full-row factorizations, while the comparison invokes generic definable selectors without stating their source. For affine lifts, existence of the required selectors follows from the earlier lemma, and the argument is correct. A reader should not have to infer this change of scope. Suggested start: “For exact affine lifts without a global $C^1$ selection requirement, definable selection and the pointwise argument give ...”. State that a common regular contact is chosen from the primal regular locus intersected with the inverse images of the dual regular loci, if useful.

## Substantive checks

- At a zero Lorentz factor every two-sided derivative is zero, since it lies in both the cone and its negative. At complementary nonzero factors the local expressions $X=\alpha(1,q)$, $Y=\beta(1,-q)$ give $-\langle DX,DY\rangle=\alpha\beta\langle Dq,Dq\rangle$; amplitude terms cancel. The cylinder identity makes the phase independent of the other source coordinates. This proves pointwise no-sharing without imposing a globally fixed source assignment on each factor label.
- In Lemma `phase-domain`, every active dual phase is defined on a neighborhood of the projected compact set. Saturation gives an invertible joint phase derivative. A global domain would give a finite covering of a product of spheres. A circle factor gives infinite fundamental group; with no circle factors the target is simply connected and has nonzero intermediate cohomology. Both cases exclude a sphere domain under the lemma’s strict individual-capacity inequality.
- The saturated sets $E_a$ are closed because active loci are open and the capacity sum is bounded below by $p_a$. Their exact-label pieces are clopen in $E_a$: nearby existing active labels persist, and a new positive-capacity label would violate saturation. Finiteness and compactness justify pairwise disjoint neighborhoods of the pieces for each fixed source. Consequently the pulled-back source top class vanishes on the union for that source. The relative cup product for the resulting open cover contradicts the nonzero product of source top classes. This proves the additive capacity premium despite smooth switching through open vertex sets.
- The factor-count argument isolates precisely $p_a>c$ with $c\mid p_a$. At a deficient source there are $p_a/c$ active labels, all necessarily of full capacity $c$, so the same top-class argument applies. Combining the separately sharp bounds for $T$ and $L$ yields the sharp $D=T+2L$ bound, because the grouped construction attains both simultaneously.
- Low-dimensional source spheres $p_a=1$ require no topological obstruction: the direct $Q_3$ construction realizes the asserted minimum. Optional rays have zero mixed channel. The theorem explicitly counts the non-ray ambient dimension, so optional rays cannot improve those minima.
- The grouped rotated-cone formula gives $t_G\geq\|x_G\|^2$, and the displayed certificate pairing is exactly $(t_G+\|v_G\|^2)/2-\langle x_G,v_G\rangle$. Summation on the affine slice yields the entire support identity. At the origin, positive $t_G$ summing to one give Slater feasibility. The direct-cone and grouped constructions are polynomial boundary selections.
- The nonsmooth norm-tree construction realizes the stated resource counts, with a rooted chain of the required excess arities. In the explicit binary example, the pairings telescope on the entire affine slice, not only on the boundary. The squared cone slacks telescope to $1-\|x\|^2$, so every slack vanishes at a boundary point; this proves the asserted unique boundary fiber. The bad strata have codimension at least two because each nonroot internal node contains at least two descendant leaves.
- For the two-source weighted contact kernel, the rank-saturation obstruction lifts all $Q_3$ phases to real-valued angles and would produce a local diffeomorphism from a compact manifold to Euclidean space. The stereographic construction has no pole because the first-block weight is strictly less than one, and its coefficient and factor counts are correct. It does not supply the separate full labelled rows, which the text states explicitly. The subsequent product-of-spheres hypersurface construction is valid by triviality of the normal bundle of an oriented Euclidean hypersurface.
- Proposition `differentiable` legitimately uses an inverse theorem stronger than the usual $C^1$ inverse function theorem. I checked the primary publisher description of Jean Saint Raymond, *Local inversion for differentiable functions and the Darboux property*, Mathematika 49 (2002), 141–158, DOI `10.1112/S0025579300016132`: its stated purpose is precisely finite-dimensional local inversion for everywhere differentiable maps with nonvanishing Jacobian. The application here has nonsingular derivative everywhere. The Poincare-polynomial comparison recovers the exponent multiset by the least positive exponent and its coefficient, so it gives the claimed contradiction under the cap. The weaker several-source differentiability conclusion is appropriately distinguished from the full additive $C^1$ theorem.
