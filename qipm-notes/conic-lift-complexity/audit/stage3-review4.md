## Verification details

- **Grouped quadratic barrier.** For `q=u-||w||^2`, differentiation gives `f''=A^2+C` and `f'''=-2A^3-3AC`. The stated polynomial difference is correct. The identity `H^{-1} grad f=(-q,0)` is correct even for nonzero `w`, because the mixed Hessian terms recover the `w` component of the gradient. Thus the unrestricted paraboloid has parameter one. Restriction and summation establish the upper bound. The box section has exactly one independent active facet per group at a vertex and its entire relative boundary is on the ambient domain boundary. It establishes the lower bound for every barrier on the grouped domain.

- **Primary facet bound.** The author-hosted primary text of Nesterov and Nemirovskii, Proposition 2.3.6, explicitly gives the lower bound by the number of independent active facets, including the cube. The relevant primary source is `https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~nemirovs/InteriorPointalgorithmsforConvexProgrammingNestrov%26Nemirovski2001.pdf` (printed page 42). The indexed primary text exposes the proposition; a subsequent full-file web open exceeded the service's size limit. No secondary statement is needed for the manuscript's use of this result.

- **Bounded-fiber projection.** The first paragraph is valid: an unbounded sequence over bounded projected coordinates produces a vertical recession direction through a fixed point of a closed convex set, contradicting its bounded fiber. This supplies the uniform boundedness needed later, rather than merely pointwise fiber bounds. It also proves closedness of the image. At each interior projected point, the relative-interior image identity supplies an interior point in the fiber, barrier divergence supplies a minimizer, and the positive definite Hessian gives uniqueness and the implicit-function argument. The Hessian Schur complement and cancellation of the vertical acceleration term give the stated derivatives. Boundedness over convergent projected sequences proves barrier divergence of the minimum. There is no missing coercivity or smoothness premise in the finite-dimensional setting used here.

- **Primary partial-minimization reference.** I read Chares's thesis Assumption 4 (printed page 156), the setup on pages 157--158, and Theorem 5.2.1 (page 158), at `https://perso.uclouvain.be/francois.glineur/files/theses/Chares-PhD-thesis-2007.pdf`. It states the preservation of a nondegenerate self-concordant barrier and its parameter under exact partial minimization under the stated bounded-fiber and no-line hypotheses. The manuscript correctly calls its lemma a form of this classical theorem and proves its particular hypotheses directly. The filename is not evidence for changing the thesis year from 2009.

- **Column packing over all three fields.** The Schur complement, exact projection, bounded closed fibers, and relative Slater point work over real, complex, and quaternionic Hermitian matrices. The trace must be real in the quaternionic case, as explicitly stipulated. At a fixed base point `(S0,W0)`, the shear can be written `W'=W-W0`, `S'=S-W0*W-W*W0+W0*W0`; it is affine and leaves `S-W*W` unchanged. At `W'=0`, the displayed derivative expressions yield dual gradient norm squared `b`. Neither the upper nor lower proof uses orthogonality of the columns or `b<=p`. Restricting to the column subspaces preserves the upper bound, and their assumed positive real dimension makes the projected product-of-balls lower bound exactly `b`. Hadamard's inequality is used only for positive definite residuals with fixed diagonal, where its equality condition is the diagonal matrix.

- **Repeated blocks.** The factors sum to the unit-ball slack; the restricted product logarithm is a multiple of the ball barrier. Its exact parameter is `m`, while the same affine domain admits a parameter-one barrier. This correctly refutes only an arbitrary-barrier inference from simultaneous multiplicity, not the standard-barrier boundary-order bound.

- **Tree calculations.** I checked the root-leaf limiting direction, the Schur-complement comparison in the all-internal-root case, the cap combinatorics, the polytope section and its independent active facets, and the homogeneity qualification on the conic bound. The values in the proposed corrected-barrier counterexample are consistent: the second derivative is `53/16`, the third is `-441/32`, and the difference of the squared inequalities is positive as stated. The text correctly stops short of claiming the standard tree barrier is intrinsically optimal.

- **Section 6 scan.** The determinant-order proof uses a legitimate positive quadratic congruence. The recession proof explicitly controls the scaled first and second derivatives; its iterated limit and test direction yield the sum of recession rank and compressed nullity. The one-channel argument uses convexity of the whole normalized certificate fiber and its bounded normalization, rather than an arbitrary selection. The wide-cap proof's strict codimension inequality is correct under `B>=q-1`. The exceptional-fiber discussion does not infer an extension or a degree of the kernel-line map from Vietoris--Begle. The unresolved narrow-cap interval is clearly labeled and is not used to prove a claimed exact frontier.
