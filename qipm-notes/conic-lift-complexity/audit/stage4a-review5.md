## Mathematical checks

- **General cone bases:** The argument first obtains full-rank image patches,
  then uses invariance of domain and compactness to cover the initially
  nonsmooth base boundary. Only after that step does it assert a smooth
  boundary. The common-kernel argument uses independent primal and dual
  derivatives and does not differentiate the contact correspondence. The
  strict-cap resource corollary correctly separates an ambient barrier from a
  barrier restricted to an affine feasible slice.
- **Product support orbits:** Complementary rank saturation, universal-cover
  topology, the degree-two signed assignment, and the PSD4 ruling argument
  are consistent. For larger sphere dimensions, an invertible matrix on
  cohomological indecomposables provides a nonzero restriction degree even
  when higher-dimensional cohomology contains decomposable classes: those
  decomposables restrict to zero on the relevant literal sphere. The proof
  correctly retains finite coverings in the presence of scalar rays. The
  isolated PSD3 row meets the hypotheses of the earlier fully proved
  real-order-three exclusion, including the possibility of exchanging the
  two arguments.
- **Entire fibers:** Compactness, connected fibers, and upper semicontinuity
  give constant individual nullities on the full preimage. Convexity then
  makes supports constant within each fiber. The successive capacity
  equalities force full-sized rank-two factors and exclude nullity in rays.
  Injectivity uses genuine row identities on the whole affine slice. The
  accessibility corollary uses the correct upper-semicontinuity direction
  and does not assert that a standard-barrier bound alone gives accessibility.
- **Topological refinements:** The clutching argument establishes existence
  of a frame, not triviality of the initially supplied smaller plane bundle.
  The middle-rank endpoint is separately excluded by Euler class. I checked
  the parity algebra in the rational formal-dimension calculation. The
  target-S2 case explicitly uses a fiber model instead of applying the
  simply connected formal-dimension theorem to a possibly non-simply-connected
  fiber. The Grassmannian proof separately treats positive-dimensional and
  zero-dimensional fibers and supplies the quadric square relation needed
  for the exceptional numerical candidate.
- **Restricted kernels:** The projective angle map is both immersive and
  injective; finite fundamental group, rather than mod-two H1, is the relevant
  lifting condition. The inverse Stiefel--Whitney bound, Veronese scale, and
  scalar chord identity agree. The inertia count only proves paired
  optimality in the stated positive block-additive subclass. The Rado proof
  handles infinite index sets, correctly identifies the deficient span as a
  direct sum of block-supported subspaces, and loses at most j-1 dimensions
  when combining cylindrical column spaces. Its uniform inequality and the
  squared-kernel rank s(s+3)/2 are correct.
