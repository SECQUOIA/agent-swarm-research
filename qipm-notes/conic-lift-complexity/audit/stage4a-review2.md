## Product support-orbit classification

I independently checked the following points in the principal assigned focus.

- Scalar rays contribute zero mixed derivative at contact, even though they can prevent injectivity of the joint support map. The proof correctly retains a finite covering until the full row identities have been used.
- Equality in the Peirce rank chain forces balanced complementary ranks in every positive-capacity factor. Lower semicontinuity of both complementary ranks gives local constancy. The support-differential calculation in Section 5 therefore supplies the asserted covering for a product source, not only a sphere source.
- The universal-cover exclusions are valid. The complex and quaternionic Schubert squares survive reduction modulo two; the Albert square does as well. For balanced real orders at least six, the homotopy sequence gives a simply connected oriented cover with second homology `Z/2`, which cannot occur in the source product. At order five, the rational square-zero-span obstruction is sufficient and avoids an unnecessary integral-ring assumption.
- The signed permutation assignment in degree two is made after passing to universal covers. Thus degree-two products of circle classes do not invalidate the argument. Primitive square-zero integral degree-two classes are precisely the signed literal sphere generators.
- For an order-four block, the locus of oriented primal planes in a fixed three-space maps to the two Hodge spheres with degrees equal up to sign. It cannot realize the assigned cylinder degrees `(±1,0)`. Applying the argument on both assigned cylinders annihilates every row in that block.
- For an order-three block, a nonzero unrelated dual row confines the primal line or plane sufficiently to make the assigned cylinder degree zero. The determinant assignment on cohomological indecomposables assigns all remaining spin blocks; the finite-index fundamental-group map supplies the circle assignments. This does not require the covering itself to split as a product map.
- After row isolation, the rank-two/rank-one maps on the remaining sphere have the hypotheses of the Section 5 affine-kernel exclusion, including the local-diffeomorphism hypothesis supplied by the mixed metric. Exchanging primal and dual arguments when needed is legitimate because the isolated ball slack is symmetric.
- The resulting exact spin profile and disappearance of every unrelated dual row follow. The theorem does not incorrectly extend that profile to generic selections or arbitrary affine lifts.

## Other new material

The normalized-base covering proof in 08a establishes smoothness of the initially nonsmooth base boundaries before applying local-diffeomorphism arguments. The common-kernel argument uses independent primal and dual derivatives and does not differentiate the contact map. The smooth proper-cone frontier distinguishes the full ambient barrier from a barrier on an affine slice; restricting an arbitrary ambient barrier to an interior-crossing product of planar sections gives the claimed orthant lower bound.

The compact full-fiber theorem in 08d uses the entire preimage, as its conclusion requires. Compactness and connected fibers give connectedness of the preimage; constant total nullity then forces constant individual nullities. Convexity of each fiber forces equal supports, and the quotient-map argument descends those supports continuously. Equality in the displayed dimension/nullity chain forces full-size rank-two factors. The injectivity argument legitimately uses genuine full affine-slice certificates. The accessibility corollary supplies the missing lower nullity inequality at every fiber point and does not infer accessibility from a barrier bound alone.

In 08e, the clutching argument proves existence of a frame from the smaller-rank summand without claiming that the given summand is itself trivial. The middle-rank case is separately excluded by Euler class. I checked the parity algebra in the rational formal-dimension argument and the explicit acyclic extension for the target two-sphere. The latter excludes the higher-dimensional equal-sphere sources without assuming simply connected fibers. The real Grassmannian argument handles both positive-dimensional fibers and zero-dimensional covers; its order-five quadric calculation is consistent with the displayed affine paving and hyperplane degree.

In 08c, the phase lift gives both immersion and injectivity, the inverse Stiefel–Whitney calculation yields the stated bound, and the Veronese/stereographic construction has the claimed factor normalization. The local and partition statements maintain their different quantifiers. The functional-inertia estimate correctly distinguishes arbitrary common-scale sums from positive block-additive summands. The Rado argument applies to arbitrary index sets because the ambient vector spaces are finite dimensional; its block-supported span and cylindrical column-rank estimates are correct. The squared-slack rank and the resulting prescribed-splitting penalty also check out.
