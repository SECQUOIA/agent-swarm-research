## Minor finding

1. **Restrict the balanced-rank and support-differential argument explicitly to positive-capacity factors.** In `sections/05-support-orbits.tex`, lines 53–57 state that saturation forces `p_i+q_i=r_i` and balanced ranks, and lines 59–74 then discuss their support maps. These conclusions hold for `c_i>0`, as the theorem statement correctly specifies. They need not hold for a ray: one may append an identically zero primal and dual ray to any saturated factorization, obtaining `p_i=q_i=0` although `r_i=1`. Add “for every positive-capacity factor” before the rank conclusion and retain that index restriction through the support-map argument. This is a local exposition/quantifier issue, not a failure of the theorem or proof strategy.

## Substantive checks

- **Independent boundary charts.** At a polar contact, the pairing of `z^perp` and `x^perp` is nondegenerate because `<x,z>=1`. Differentiating the full slack separately in the two charts requires only the stated C1 maps. The Peirce rank bound uses the possible overlap of the two derivative images; it does not require differentiating the contact correspondence or claiming that the pulled-back form is a Gram form in these independent charts.
- **Rank constancy and the support differential.** For positive-capacity factors, equality in the summed dimension bound forces balanced complementary ranks. Lower semicontinuity of both ranks then makes each locally constant. Differentiating the support relation yields the stated half-Peirce identity; `L_X` has eigenvalues `lambda_j/2` there. The resulting joint support differential is injective and square, so the compact-domain local diffeomorphism is indeed a finite covering.
- **Topology of every simple family.** A product of at least two positive-dimensional compact simply connected universal covers has intermediate mod-two cohomology and cannot be a sphere. Circle factors are separately excluded by the finite-cover fundamental-group obstruction. For balanced real Grassmannians with both ranks at least two, the isotropy fundamental-group map has a nonzero kernel, including the rank-two endpoints involving `SO(2)`; hence the oriented Grassmannian has nonzero pi2 in dimension at least four. The analogous complex and quaternionic arguments give nonzero pi2 and pi4 below the top dimension. The Cayley-plane middle class excludes the Albert case. The remaining real order-three orbit is correctly retained until the ball-specific argument.
- **Finite affine gluing.** Two distinct affine maps with matching tangent first jet at a sphere point differ by `M(z dot x - 1)` and cannot match first jets at a second point. The closures of the finitely many affine pieces therefore intersect only in a finite set. Removing that set preserves connectedness for the stated `n>=2`, proving the gluing lemma even when pattern sets are disconnected.
- **Real order-three exclusion.** The finite zero/positivity pattern sets really eliminate each ray term on every pair of points in the same pattern set. An open projective set of rank-one dual directions spans all real symmetric matrices, forcing an affine primal pencil on each set. The gluing lemma makes it globally affine. A scalar affine function nonnegative on the sphere cannot have two distinct zeros unless it vanishes identically, so the common-kernel argument proves injectivity. This contradicts the universal double cover onto RP2. No unproved determinantal classification is needed.
- **Uniform dictionary selected-rank frontier.** In a saturated rank chain, every nonzero dual factor has rank one and achieves capacity B. Constant total rank plus lower semicontinuity makes the factor ranks constant. The product-orbit argument excludes multiple such factors. In the one-factor real case of any order at least three, the other dual factors vanish identically, so the full slack directly makes the primal pencil affine and the same injective-kernel obstruction excludes the projective double cover. The matching spin exception is therefore exactly the stated exception.
- **Attainment and product compression.** The fixed-tail construction represents the ball, has a strictly feasible point, and its displayed rank-one polynomial certificate sums to the whole-slice slack. Proper faces have strictly smaller per-rank capacity than their parent non-ray simple cone, including the rank-two Albert face. Thus sequential compression cannot create a new spin exception at capacity B; the product selected-rank bound follows with the stated contact/weight quantifiers.
- **All-symmetric resource corollary.** The inequality `a floor(r^2/4)<=m-2` is strict for every rank at least three, including the exceptional cone. If the total reaches `s-1`, global saturation therefore forces the single matching spin factor. Under a strict dimension cap the total is consequently at least s. The separate lower bounds for L, D, and ambient rank are simultaneously attained by the grouped Lorentz construction. They do not assert a restricted-barrier parameter equality.
