## M1 — Minor: state that the Lorentz dimension cap is an integer

**Location:** `sections/07-concrete-barriers.tex:113`, Corollary `cor:tree-cap-parameter` (equation `eq:tree-cap-parameter`).

The opening hypothesis says only “Fix a Lorentz dimension cap $d\geq3$.” The formula uses $A=d-2$ as an integer arity capacity. It is false for a noninteger numerical cap.

For example, take $d=7/2$ and $N=4$. The displayed definitions give $A=3/2$, $E=3$, $L_0=2$, and $\chi=0$, predicting minimum parameter $3$. But a norm tree with cone dimensions at most $7/2$ can use only binary internal nodes, since its integer cone dimensions are at most $3$. Four leaves require three internal nodes. A balanced tree has an all-internal root and parameter $2(3)-2=4$, while a chain has parameter $5$. The minimum is therefore $4$.

**Fix:** Change the opening to “Fix an integer Lorentz dimension cap $d\geq3$.” The integer convention is explicit in several other cap results, but is not stated as a global convention making this local hypothesis redundant. No proof change is needed for the intended integer setting. This omission does not undermine the root-incidence theorem or the principal results.

## Mathematical and scientific checks

### Curvature, certificates, and products

I checked the distinction between a genuine certificate on the whole affine slice and an arbitrary boundary slack factorization, including facial reduction and changes of pairing. The minimum-rank argument, open-set rank persistence, Peirce perspective construction, and the resulting whole-fiber quantifier support the selection-free rank bounds. The sequential product compression correctly reuses original certificates; it does not silently extend a certificate defined only on a restricted slice. Claims about an aggregate high-rank certificate are not confused with a lower bound for every certificate in its aggregate fiber.

The support-orbit dimension calculations and the local capacity bounds are consistent with the real, complex, quaternionic, spin, and exceptional Jordan cases. I checked the rank-one perspective argument in the exceptional case and the use of associativity on subalgebras generated by two octonions in the Albert balance-slice construction.

### Global regularity, topology, and rigidity

I checked the separation of local smooth selection hypotheses, global support maps, genuine-fiber hypotheses, and compactness assumptions. The arguments using constant active patterns, relative cup products, sphere submersions, Grassmannian topology, and exceptional Hopf cases do not implicitly promote a local section to a global one. The treatment of open vertex switching and higher-rank kernels is appropriately qualified. Full-fiber compactness is assumed or established where needed rather than inferred from compactness of the projected body.

The appendix results distinguish the extra restrictions under which stronger conclusions hold. They do not present those refinements as unconditional improvements of the general curvature bounds.

### Barriers and formulation families

I checked the restriction identity for standard barriers, the distinction between ambient homogeneity and the exact restricted parameter, and the root Schur-complement calculation for norm trees. Apart from M1, the combinatorial optimization of the root type is consistent with the tree identity. Intrinsic lower bounds are not substituted for exact standard-barrier parameters.

The bounded-fiber partial minimization argument handles coercivity through the recession structure. The power-cone dual scaling, characteristic-barrier asymptotics, entropy perspective closures, and treatment of zero columns retain the necessary boundary conditions. Statements about optimal nonsymmetric barriers identify the remaining interval where an exact optimum is not proved; they do not overstate the scope of the proved formulas. The spectral and chordal constructions keep source-sharing, factor-count, and face-cap restrictions distinct.

### Approximation and compiler claims

I checked that robust curvature conclusions use derivative control and not just a uniform error in function values. The rank truncation arguments, contact-chart conditioning, and topology stability statements retain their quantitative hypotheses. The examples showing that contact regularity does not control Newton conditioning have the claimed scope.

For exact and approximate compilers, I checked the difference between a direct representation and an arbitrary extended lift. The essential exponential terms argument is applied to the stated direct model. The source-free compiler lower bounds decode finite data using finitely many witnesses and do not require charging the unbounded offline arithmetic to a query lower bound. The growing-decoder statement correctly distinguishes a sum of query costs from a maximum error over witnesses. Input loading is charged separately under its stated fan-in model.

### Query, access, and output models

I paid particular attention to `12b`–`12e`:

- The centered PSD/Lorentz comparison has the same reduced Hessian and right-hand side under its declared fiber-center contract. The off-center allocation minimization produces the exact reduced quadratic form. - The compiler for implicit centered PSD blocks charges supplied scale data, block-encoding normalization, coordinate access, and explicit Gram output separately. A normalized input state does not silently reveal radial information.
