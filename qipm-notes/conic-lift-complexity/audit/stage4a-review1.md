# Stage 4A independent review 1

Verdict: **no major issue found**. Three small scope clarifications should be made before closing this stage. I read all five new sections and the author report, and checked relevant dependencies in Sections 1 and 5. I did not read other reviewers' reports or edit the manuscript.

## Findings

1. **Minor: specify that the dimension cap is an integer.** In `sections/08a-general-topology.tex`, Corollary `proper-smooth-frontier` begins “Let N≥3, d≥3.” Change this to “Let N≥3 and let d≥3 be an integer.” The formulas depend on the integer cap, rather than an arbitrary real upper bound on cone dimension. For example, N=3 and d=7/2 would give h=2 in the stated expression, although all allowed factors still have dimension at most three and the correct smooth count is three. This is a missing elementary hypothesis, not a defect in the proof for its intended integer cap. The earlier dimension-resource corollary already makes integrality explicit.

2. **Minor: qualify the comparison with the joint-cover theorem by capacity equality.** In `sections/08e-topology-refinements.tex`, lines 22–25, replace “For exact globally smooth cone factors, Theorem ... is stronger” by “For exact globally smooth cone factors at capacity equality, Theorem ... is stronger.” The immediately preceding assumption is only that the sum of the actual mixed-channel ranks equals n. That does not imply that the sum of the cone capacities equals n: an individual channel can have rank strictly smaller than its cone capacity. The later results use the correct hypotheses, but this connecting sentence should retain the distinction.

3. **Minor: require positive capacity in the individual-channel normalization statement.** In `sections/08e-topology-refinements.tex`, lines 88–91, say that the chosen channel has positive capacity c_i and rank c_i everywhere. Rank zero saturation is automatic for rays and two-dimensional cones and does not imply the nonzero complementary boundary factors used by the normalization argument. All applications in the following propositions have positive target dimension, so the fix is local.

## Mathematical checks

- The general joint-cover proof is sound for initially nonsmooth cone bases. The quotient derivative has exactly the claimed dimension; a constant-rank transversal and invariance of domain first produce relatively open embedded patches. Compactness then makes the entire boundary regular. This avoids assuming smoothness of the cone boundary before it has been proved. The argument never differentiates the contact correspondence.
- The common-kernel argument and equal-dimensional inverse-function theorem give a finite covering. Circle targets are incompatible with simply connected compact sources; the remaining product targets are simply connected. Cohomological indecomposables give the stated multiset without assuming the covering splits into coordinate maps.
- The general-cone ambient barrier lower bound is valid for coupled barriers: an interior-crossing two-dimensional linear section of each proper cone is a pointed planar wedge, and the product restriction is an orthant barrier. The result is explicitly ambient and is not silently applied to an affine feasible slice.
- The clutching argument supplies the existence of an r-frame from an r-plane field when r<n/2. It does not mistakenly claim triviality of the original rank-r bundle. The middle-rank even-sphere endpoint is separately excluded by Euler class. The subset-sum and near-full-summand deductions then follow.
- I independently checked the rational homotopy dimension algebra in the equal-sphere-product submersion proposition. The odd/even target cases force the displayed rank to equal one. The Euler-characteristic exclusion for even source sphere dimension and odd target dimension is valid. For target S², the explicit acyclic extension has D²=0, and its pullback leaves an unrestricted even polynomial generator, so compact-fiber cohomology gives the intended contradiction.
- Browder's sphere-fiber theorem is applied only after smoothing and obtaining a proper submersion bundle with connected fiber. In the Grassmannian argument, positive-dimensional fibers and zero-dimensional covers are treated separately. The Euler-characteristic comparisons eliminate all larger balanced real orders except the order-five quadric, whose explicit cell filtration and relation x²=2y give the necessary additional mod-two contradiction.
- The product-orbit proof preserves finite covers in the presence of rays and uses universal covers correctly. The real order-six-and-above torsion obstruction, complex/quaternionic and Albert cup-square obstructions, and real order-five rational degree-two obstruction are valid. The degree assignments in the real order-three/four cases are enough for the cylinder argument; no unjustified global product decomposition is needed.
- In the full-fiber theorem, compactness and connected fibers prove connectedness of the total preimage. Upper semicontinuity plus constant total nullity makes each nullity constant. Convexity of each projection fiber then makes its support constant, and the compact quotient descends this support continuously. Equality in the capacity chain forces full-size spin factors and eliminates ray nullity. Genuine certificates give injectivity of the descended map. The accessibility corollary does not extend its hypothesis to inaccessible fiber points.
- The projective phase-embedding proof uses integral H¹, as required for real angle lifts. Its characteristic-class lower bound and stereographic upper construction have consistent dimensions and normalization. The functional-inertia bound is correctly restricted to the stated feature class. The Rado argument works with arbitrary index sets and actual column ranks: the block-supported range observation is needed and is present. The squared-kernel column dimension and resulting factor-count penalty check out.

## Primary-source checks

- I inspected Browder's author-uploaded article, Theorem 1, at <https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251964661_Fiberings_of_spheres_and_H-spaces_which_are_rational_homology_spheres>. It states the connected-fiber homotopy types used here, and the surrounding text gives the intended nontrivial sphere-bundle setting.
- I inspected Kathryn Hess's author lecture notes at <https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~bshipley/Hess.Chicago.pdf>, Theorems 2.2.1–2.2.2 and 2.4.3. The fiber-square theorem requires a simply connected base and source of the pullback, which hold in the S² argument; it does not require the resulting fiber to be simply connected. The formal-dimension identity and its locator in Félix–Halperin–Thomas agree with the manuscript.
- The manuscript attributes the topology to classical literature and makes its contribution claims about the cone-factorization implications. I found no unsupported universal novelty claim in these new sections. Its distinction from the universal positive-map factorization quantifiers is explicit.

After the three minor clarifications above, I have no unresolved substantive objection to Stage 4A.
