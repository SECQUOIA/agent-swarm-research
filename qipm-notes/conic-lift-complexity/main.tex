\title{Curvature, support certificates, and barrier complexity of conic lifts}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Conic lifts change factor counts, support-certificate ranks, affine barrier
parameters, and computational interfaces in different ways. We establish
quantitative relations among these resources for balls and related convex
bodies. Mixed contact curvature gives a dimension-minus-two bound for
proper definable cones and a complementary-rank bound for symmetric cones.
For a finite repeatable dictionary of simple symmetric cones, put
$B=\max a(r-1)>0$, where $a$ is the Peirce constant and $r$ the Jordan rank.
The exact least worst-support certificate rank of an
$s$-dimensional ball, $s\geq2$, is
$\lceil(s-1)/B\rceil$, with minimization over each entire genuine
certificate fiber and no global selection assumption. Global differentiable
selections can impose additional topological costs. Boundary and recession
tests yield delimited exact restricted-barrier frontiers and concrete
intrinsic parameter formulas. For a fixed unit objective in a fixed weighted
norm-tree metric, call a node active if its subtree carries nonzero objective
support. The squared leading distance coefficient is the sum of active node
weights plus half the inactive node weights; this determines the central route's
asymptotic excess. For feasible PSD column packing, exact source allocations
give a sharp first-power comparison between off-center and centered
projected Hessian quotients. We also give resource ledgers, conditioned
approximation and exact compilation results, and access-dependent Newton
and query comparisons. The remaining bounded narrow-cap and nonsymmetric
parameter intervals are stated explicitly. Movement bounds imply work
bounds only under the specified charging and output contracts.
\end{abstract}
