\title{Curvature, support certificates, and barrier complexity of conic lifts}

\date{}

\begin{abstract}
A conic lift writes a convex body as the linear image of an affine slice
of a product of cones, turning linear optimization over the body into a
conic program. Mainly for Euclidean balls and their products, we ask how
small the cone factors, dual certificate ranks, and barrier parameters
of lifts can be, and how they bound distances in barrier metrics. If an
$s$-dimensional compact convex body has a $C^2$ boundary patch with
strictly positive curvature at some point, every exact lift by proper
cones of dimensions $m_i$ that are definable in an o-minimal structure
(for example, semialgebraic) satisfies
$s-1\leq\sum_i\max\{m_i-2,0\}$. Fix a finite list of simple symmetric
cones, each usable repeatedly, and put $B=\max a(r-1)>0$, where $a$ is
the Peirce constant and $r$ the Jordan rank; for example, $B=m-2$ for
the $m$-dimensional Lorentz cone and $B=r-1$ for real $r\times r$ PSD
matrices. Ranks and barriers are computed after replacing the product of cones by its smallest face containing the affine slice. A certificate for a direction $v$ is a dual element that represents the slack $1-\langle
x,v\rangle$ on the whole affine slice. For the $s$-dimensional ball,
$s\geq2$, the least total Jordan rank of a certificate, maximized over
directions and then minimized over lifts, is $\lceil(s-1)/B\rceil$,
without smoothness assumptions on the lift; requiring boundary points
and certificates to be $C^1$ in the direction can increase it. For every
such lift, the log-determinant barrier restricted to the affine slice
has parameter at least $\lceil(s-1)/B\rceil$. We determine the least
such parameter in several cases, and the least parameter of any barrier
for several concrete formulations; for some remaining cases, and for
nonsymmetric cones, we prove only bounds. A weighted norm tree
represents a ball by nested Euclidean-norm constraints along a rooted
tree, with a barrier weight at each node. For such a tree and a fixed
unit objective, the distance in the barrier metric from a fixed interior point to the $\epsilon$-accurate
points is $\sqrt{K}\log(1/\epsilon)+O(\log\log(1/\epsilon))$, where $K$
is the total weight of the nodes whose subtree meets the support of the
objective plus half the remaining weight; for the central path from the
analytic center, $K$ becomes the total weight. In the column-packed PSD
formulation of a product of balls, the ball coordinates fill the columns
of matrices $W_\ell$, and auxiliary matrices $S_\ell$ satisfy one linear
equation per ball on their diagonals. Let $D$ be the residuals
$S_\ell-W_\ell^TW_\ell$ at a feasible point and $D^0$ their value at the
barrier-minimizing auxiliary point over the same projected point. If
$\mu D^0\preceq D\preceq LD^0$ ($0<\mu\leq1\leq L$), the Hessians $H_D$
and $H_0$ at these two points, after eliminating the auxiliary
directions, satisfy $L^{-1}H_0\preceq H_D\preceq\mu^{-1}H_0$, and this
is sharp. We also give exact resource tradeoffs, approximation results
with controlled cone factors, and exact compilation of input data, and
compare Newton-step and query costs; distance bounds imply work bounds
only under explicit assumptions on output and per-round work.
\end{abstract}
