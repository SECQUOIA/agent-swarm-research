# A strict barrier premium for products of non-Lorentz \(p\)-cones

Status: Proved; literature-screened; independently audited  
Started: 2026-09-04  
Paper status: Not incorporated  
Confidence: High for the stated \(p\)-cone-product and minimum-space theorems

## Result

For \(1<p<\infty\), put

\[
 c_p=\min\left\{\frac1p,1-\frac1p\right\}.
\]

When \(p\ne2\), let \(\gamma_p\in(0,1)\) be the unique root of

\[
 (1-2c_p)\gamma^{1-c_p}
 +(1-c_p)\gamma^{1-2c_p}-c_p=0                     \tag{1}
\]

and define

\[
 h(p)=1+\frac{1+\gamma_p^{c_p}}
                 {1+\gamma_p^{1-c_p}}.             \tag{2}
\]

Set \(h(2)=2\). For the \(m\)-dimensional \(p\)-order cone

\[
 K_{p,m}=\{(t,u)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^{m-1}:
                 t\geq\|u\|_p\},                   \tag{3}
\]

the following product theorem holds.

> **Theorem (coupled product premium).** Let \(m_i\geq3\). Every
> logarithmically homogeneous self-concordant barrier on
> \(\prod_{i=1}^kK_{p,m_i}\), including a barrier that couples all factors,
> has parameter
> \[
>                    \boxed{\nu\geq k h(p).}        \tag{4}
> \]
> Consequently \(\nu>2k\) whenever \(p\ne2\).

This strengthens the universal \(2k\) product lower bound for this fixed
block dictionary. It is not a separable-barrier argument: (4) applies to
an arbitrary barrier on the whole product.

Two consequences delimit the result.

1. In the exact \(p\)-order-tree lift of \(B_p^N\) with block cap \(d\geq3\),
   the exact factor minimum is
   \[
     k_* = \left\lceil\frac{N-1}{d-2}\right\rceil.
   \]
   Thus every ambient barrier on the tree product has
   \[
                       \nu\geq h(p)k_*>2k_*          \tag{5}
   \]
   for \(p\ne2\). More generally, the same conclusion holds for every
   lift whose cone is a product of \(p\)-order blocks of dimensions between
   three and \(d\), because the curvature-capacity theorem already forces
   at least \(k_*\) such blocks.

2. Without a block-dictionary restriction, a strict statement survives in
   the minimum-space regime. Every exact cone lift of \(B_p^N\), \(N\geq2\),
   with the minimum possible \(N+1\) cone coordinates has a lifting cone
   linearly isomorphic to \(K_{p,N+1}\). Hence every barrier on that cone
   satisfies
   \[
                       \nu\geq h(p)>2                \tag{6}
   \]
   for \(p\ne2\), independently of the chosen cone dictionary.

Equation (5) is a barrier lower bound, not an iteration lower bound for all
interior-point algorithms. It says that the usual short-step guarantee for
any chosen ambient LHSC barrier cannot insert a smaller barrier parameter;
it does not rule out a better algorithmic analysis or a different method.

## The positive-branch product lemma

The reusable engine is more general than \(p\)-cones. For each proper cone
\(C_i\subset\mathbb R^{n_i}\), suppose there is a Hildebrand
Theorem-5.4 configuration \((e_{i1},\ldots,e_{in_i},x_i^*)\) such that
\(x_i^*\) is in the simplicial cone generated by the boundary basis,
all associated cross-ratios are nonnegative, and

\[
                   S_i:=\sum_{a=1}^{n_i}q_{ia}\geq2. \tag{P1}
\]

Then every possibly coupled LHSC barrier on \(\prod_iC_i\) satisfies

\[
                           \nu\geq\sum_iS_i.         \tag{P2}
\]

Indeed, put each local boundary basis in its own direct summand and take a
positive weighted sum of the local interior points. The projective argument
in (8)--(9) preserves each \(q_{ia}\). The global cross-ratio sum is
\(\sum_iS_i\geq2\), so Hildebrand's positive branch gives (P2). This is a
general certificate for additive lower bounds on arbitrary coupled product
barriers; it does not assume that the barrier separates across factors.

## The scalar premium is strict

For \(p\ne2\), one has \(0<c_p<1/2\). The left side of (1) is strictly
increasing on \((0,1)\), tends to \(-c_p\) at zero, and equals
\(2(1-2c_p)>0\) at one. This proves existence and uniqueness of
\(\gamma_p\in(0,1)\). Since \(c_p<1-c_p\),

\[
             \gamma_p^{c_p}>\gamma_p^{1-c_p},
\]

so the fraction in (2) is greater than one and \(h(p)>2\). The definition
is invariant under Hölder duality: if \(q=p/(p-1)\), then
\(c_q=c_p\) and \(h(q)=h(p)\).

Using (1), the excess has the useful exact form

\[
 h(p)-2=\frac{1-2c_p}{1-c_p}\,\gamma_p^{c_p}.       \tag{2a}
\]

Thus \(2<h(p)<3\) throughout the open non-Euclidean range. The premium
vanishes as \(p\to2\) and approaches one as \(p\to1\) or
\(p\to\infty\).

## Proof of the coupled product theorem

### Three-dimensional factors with \(1<p<2\)

[Hildebrand](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-012-0576-1), Section 7.2 and
Corollary 7.2, constructs for \(K_{p,3}\) a family indexed by
\(\delta\downarrow0\) consisting of three linearly independent boundary
points \(\bar e_1,\bar e_2,\bar e_3\) on a compact affine base and an
interior point \(\bar x^*\) in their simplex. Let
\(\bar q_a(\delta)\) be the three projective cross-ratios in that
construction. For \(1<p<2\),

\[
 S_\delta:=\sum_{a=1}^3\bar q_a(\delta)>2
 \quad\hbox{for all sufficiently small \(\delta\)},
 \qquad S_\delta\longrightarrow h(p).                \tag{7}
\]

Normalize the local base by \(\ell_i=1\). In
\(C=(K_{p,3})^k\), use the compact base
\(\ell=\sum_i\ell_i=1\), and define

\[
 E_{ia}=(0,\ldots,\bar e_a,\ldots,0),
 \qquad
 X^*=(\alpha_1\bar x^*,\ldots,\alpha_k\bar x^*),     \tag{8}
\]

where \(\alpha_i>0\) and \(\sum_i\alpha_i=1\). The \(3k\) points
\(E_{ia}\) are a basis of the product space, each is on the boundary of
the global base, and \(X^*\) is in the relative interior of their simplex.

Fix \((i,a)\). On the line through \(E_{ia}\) and \(X^*\), every component
other than \(i\) remains a positive multiple of \(\bar x^*\) while moving
from \(X^*\) toward the boundary point opposite \(E_{ia}\). Therefore the
first opposite boundary contact is controlled exactly by component \(i\).
The intersection with the span of all \(E_{jb}\) except \(E_{ia}\) is
also controlled by deleting the coefficient of \(\bar e_a\) in component
\(i\). Equivalently, projection to component \(i\), followed by the scalar
normalization \(1/\alpha_i\), is a projective isomorphism of all four
points defining this cross-ratio. Hence

\[
                         q_{ia}=\bar q_a(\delta).     \tag{9}
\]

The total cross-ratio sum is \(kS_\delta>2\). The \(S\geq2\) branch of
Hildebrand's Theorem 5.4, equation (22), now gives

\[
                         \nu\geq kS_\delta.
\]

Letting \(\delta\downarrow0\) proves (4) for \(1<p<2\).

### Hölder dual exponents

For \(p>2\), let \(q=p/(p-1)\in(1,2)\). The dual of \(K_{p,3}\) is
\(K_{q,3}\), and the dual of a product is the product of the dual cones.
If \(F\) is a \(\nu\)-LHSC barrier on \(C\), its Legendre dual

\[
 F_*(s)=\sup_{x\in\operatorname{int}C}
              \{-\langle s,x\rangle-F(x)\}          \tag{10}
\]

is a \(\nu\)-LHSC barrier on \(C^*\). Applying the preceding case to
\(F_*\) yields \(\nu\geq kh(q)=kh(p)\).

This dual step is material. Hildebrand's direct \(p>2\) limiting
configuration lies on the other branch of equation (22), so simply
concatenating those witnesses does not establish additivity.

### Larger factors

For each \(m_i\geq3\), retain the height coordinate and any two norm
coordinates. The resulting linear section of \(K_{p,m_i}\) is
\(K_{p,3}\), and its interior meets the ambient interior. Restriction of
an LHSC barrier to the product of these sections preserves both
self-concordance and the logarithmic-homogeneity parameter. The
three-dimensional theorem therefore proves (4) for all \(m_i\geq3\).

The hypothesis \(m_i\geq3\) is necessary: every \(K_{p,2}\) is linearly
isomorphic to \(\mathbb R_+^2\) and has an optimal parameter-two barrier.

## Application to exact \(\ell_p\)-ball lifts

The companion [exact cone-granularity
theorem](2026-09-04-lp-ball-exact-cone-granularity.md) proves that every
exact lift of \(B_p^N\) over definable proper blocks of dimension at most
\(d\) uses at least \(k_*\) non-ray blocks, and that the \(p\)-order tree
attains the count with blocks of dimensions at least three. This gives
(5) under the stated \(p\)-order-block restriction.

The companion [minimum-dimensional lift-rigidity
theorem](2026-09-04-minimal-cone-lift-rigidity.md) proves that an exact lift
of a full-dimensional compact \(N\)-body in only \(N+1\) cone coordinates
must use a cone linearly isomorphic to its homogenization. For \(B_p^N\)
that homogenization is \(K_{p,N+1}\). Restriction to two norm coordinates
and (4) with \(k=1\) prove (6).

There is also an existential upper bound, but it is not computational.
The canonical barrier of an \(m\)-dimensional regular cone has parameter at
most \(m\). Therefore the tree product, whose exact total dimension is

\[
 M_*=N-1+2k_*,
\]

admits some barrier with

\[
                  h(p)k_*\leq\nu_{\rm opt}\leq M_* . \tag{11}
\]

Efficient evaluation of this canonical barrier, its derivatives, or a
quantum Newton oracle is not supplied by the existence theorem.

## Why nonhomogeneity does not settle arbitrary dictionaries

[Ito and Lourenço](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.05801) prove that
\(K_{p,m}\), \(m\geq3\), is nonhomogeneous for \(p\ne2\). This explains why
Lorentz-cone intuition is unreliable, but nonhomogeneity alone supplies no
numerical barrier lower bound and is not inherited by the factors of an
extended formulation.

For a concrete warning, let \(p=a/b>1\) be rational in lowest terms and
set \(\alpha=b/a=1/p\). Then

\[
 B_p^N=\left\{u:\ \exists y,r\in\mathbb R_+^N,
 \ \sum_jy_j=1,\quad y_j^\alpha\geq r_j,
 \quad r_j\geq|u_j|\ \forall j\right\}.             \tag{12}
\]

Indeed, the last two inequalities imply \(y_j\geq|u_j|^p\), and equality
in the sum may absorb unused mass in the converse direction. Rational
weighted-geometric-mean, hence rational power-cone, inequalities have finite
rotated-second-order-cone representations; see
[Wang](https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1531257). Thus every
rational \(B_p^N\), including every rational \(p\ne2\), has an exact lift
over a finite product of three-dimensional Lorentz cones. More explicitly,
if \(p=a/b\) in lowest terms, Wang's bivariate construction plus one cone
for the absolute-value epigraph gives at most
\(N(\lceil\log_2a\rceil+1)\) Lorentz factors. Every one of those factors
has optimal barrier parameter two even though the target homogenization is
a nonhomogeneous \(p\)-cone. The sum barrier has parameter exactly twice
the factor count; the universal product lower bound shows that no coupled
barrier has a smaller parameter on this Lorentz product.

This proves a precise no-transfer statement: the premium \(h(p)>2\) cannot
be assigned factor by factor after replacing the \(p\)-cone dictionary by
an arbitrary one. It does **not** disprove a stronger target-level lower
bound above the universal \(2k_*\), because the SOC lift can use more than
\(k_*\) factors. Outside the minimum-space regime, the optimal barrier
parameter over all bounded-dimension definable dictionaries remains open.
For irrational \(p\), semialgebraic SOC shadows cannot give an exact lift
of the generally nonsemialgebraic \(B_p^N\), but this observation likewise
does not yield a quantitative barrier gap against arbitrary definable
cones.

## Literature boundary

Hildebrand's Corollary 7.2 is exactly the one-factor lower bound \(h(p)\).
The product amplification for an arbitrary coupled barrier, its use with
the exact \(p\)-order-block count, and the minimum-space consequence appear
to be new. Targeted searches found no paper stating these extensions.
Novelty remains subject to specialist review.

Hildebrand's [canonical-barrier
paper](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.2013.0640) supplies the
dimension upper bound used in (11). Existing work on rational
weighted-geometric-mean representations establishes the SOC warning, but
does not conflict with the fixed-\(p\)-cone product lower bound.

## Audit record

An independent hostile audit checked the root uniqueness and strictness,
the full product cross-ratio construction, arbitrary barrier coupling,
restriction from \(m_i\) to three dimensions, Hölder duality, and the
two-dimensional-factor exception. It found the theorem correct. The audit
also caught an invalid direct concatenation for \(p>2\); the proof above
uses Legendre duality instead. A second independent audit verified the
Hildebrand formula, the product geometry, and the literature boundary; it
found no explicit prior product theorem. A third audit verified the
rational SOC construction and the no-transfer wording, including the
important caveat that this does not settle a target-level lower bound in
terms of the minimum possible factor count.
