# Exceptional Albert blocks obey the sourcewise exposed-rank law

Status: Proved; independently hostile-audited  
Started: 2026-09-04  
Paper status: Not incorporated  
Confidence: High on the incidence lemma and its sourcewise corollary

## Result

The private-row exposed-rank bound for products of Euclidean balls extends
to the exceptional symmetric cone.  Consequently it holds for arbitrary
products of finite-dimensional symmetric cones, not only classical matrix
and Lorentz cones.

Let

\[
                 C=\prod_{a=1}^b B_2^{s_a},\qquad p_a=s_a-1,
\]

and suppose its full extreme-row slack has globally labelled \(C^1\)
factors over a finite product of irreducible symmetric cones.  At a
simultaneous contact, choose positive row weights and let \(q_i\) be the
Jordan rank of the resulting aggregate dual slack in factor \(i\).  Put
\(Q=\sum_iq_i\).  If every nonray factor has real dimension at most
\(d\), then

\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 Q\geq\sum_{a=1}^b
       \left\lceil {s_a-1\over d-2}\right\rceil .}       \tag{1}
\]

Combined with the exposed-minor distance theorem, (1) gives a standard
Jordan-barrier distance coefficient at least the square root of its
right-hand side.  It gives the same coefficient lower bound for every
classified self-scaled barrier because its weighted exposed rank is no
smaller than \(Q\).

The Hermitian private-quotient and Lorentz private-ray arguments already
prove (1) when no exceptional factor occurs.  The only missing case is the
Albert algebra \(H_3(\mathbb O)\), of real dimension 27, rank three, and
Peirce constant eight.  The lemma below closes it.

## Albert incidence lemma

Fix one Albert factor at a simultaneous contact.  Write

\[
 X=X_i(x),\qquad
 Y^a=Y_i^a(x_a),\qquad
 s=\sum_a\lambda_aY^a,\qquad \lambda_a>0,
\]

and let \(q=\operatorname{rank}s\).  Call row \(a\) **productive** in
this factor if its mixed-curvature summand is nonzero.  Then

\[
 \boxed{\#\{a:\text{row }a\text{ is productive}\}\leq q.}           \tag{2}
\]

Every productive row summand has real rank at most

\[
                  8\left\lfloor{3^2\over4}\right\rfloor=16.        \tag{3}
\]

### Proof

Positivity and zero pairing give Jordan complementarity between \(X\)
and \(s\).  A productive row must satisfy \(Y^a\ne0\): a two-sided
\(C^1\) map into a pointed cone that vanishes at an interior parameter
point has zero derivative there, and hence zero mixed channel.

If \(q=0\), positivity of the weighted sum makes every \(Y^a\) zero.  If
\(q=3\), complementarity makes \(X=0\), so its two-sided derivative and
every mixed channel vanish.  It remains to consider \(q=1,2\).

If \(q=1\), the face generated by \(s\) is a ray.  Every nonzero
\(Y^a\) lies on that same ray.  Suppose rows \(a\ne d\) were both
productive.  Holding \(x_d\) fixed and varying the unrelated coordinate
\(x_a\), the cylinder contact identity gives

\[
                         X(x)\circ Y^d=0.
\]

Differentiation in an \(x_a\)-tangent direction gives
\(dX[u_a]\circ Y^d=0\).  Multiplication by the primitive idempotent on
this ray is one half of the identity on the primal--dual cross-Peirce
space.  Thus the cross component of \(dX[u_a]\) vanishes, killing the
row-\(a\) mixed channel.  This contradiction proves that at most one row
is productive when \(q=1\).

Now let \(q=2\).  A productive factor has primal support rank one.  The
rank-two dual face is a spin factor isomorphic to the Lorentz cone
\(Q_{10}\).  For a fixed row \(a\), put

\[
                         S_{-a}=\sum_{d\ne a}\lambda_dY^d.
\]

Cylindrical complementarity gives
\(dX[u_a]\circ S_{-a}=0\).  If \(S_{-a}\) has rank two, diagonalize it
inside the dual face as \(\mu_1c_1+\mu_2c_2\), with
\(\mu_1,\mu_2>0\).  If \(e\) is the primitive primal support, the full
cross-Peirce space is

\[
                       V(e,c_1)\oplus V(e,c_2).
\]

On \(V(e,c_j)\), multiplication by \(S_{-a}\) is the scalar
\(\mu_j/2\).  It is therefore invertible on the cross space, so the
row-\(a\) cross derivative and mixed channel vanish.  Productivity forces

\[
                         \operatorname{rank}S_{-a}\leq1.             \tag{4}
\]

Three productive rows are impossible.  Their dual values are nonzero,
and applying (4) to each row says that every pair of the other two lies
on one boundary ray of the rank-two face.  The three values must therefore
lie on the same primitive ray, contradicting \(\operatorname{rank}s=2\).
This proves (2).  The Peirce cross-space bound gives (3).  The argument
uses only Jordan products and Peirce multiplication, so exceptional
nonassociativity causes no additional term.  \(\square\)

## Sourcewise token count

Give one token to every productive source row in an Albert factor.  By
(2), factor \(i\) receives at most \(q_i\) tokens, and by (3) one token
carries at most 16 tangent dimensions.  Hermitian factors use their
private quotient tokens, and a productive Lorentz rank-one factor uses
its private-ray token.  If

\[
 \kappa=\max\left\{
   \max_{H_{r_i}(\mathbb F_i)}
       \dim_{\mathbb R}(\mathbb F_i)(r_i-1),
   \max_{Q_{m_j}}(m_j-2),
   16\,\mathbf 1_{\{\text{an Albert factor is present}\}}
 \right\},                                                        \tag{5}
\]

then source \(a\)'s nondegenerate tangent metric requires at least
\(D_a\geq\lceil p_a/\kappa\rceil\) tokens.  Tokens are private within
each factor and

\[
                         \sum_aD_a\leq\sum_iq_i=Q.                  \tag{6}
\]

This proves

\[
                 Q\geq\sum_a\left\lceil{p_a\over\kappa}\right\rceil.
                                                                         \tag{7}
\]

For every classical factor of real dimension at most \(d\), its entry in
(5) is at most \(d-2\).  An Albert factor can occur only when \(d\geq27\),
and then \(16\leq d-2\).  Hence \(\kappa\leq d-2\), and (7) proves (1).

## Scope and audit

The theorem requires the full cylindrical row identities and globally
labelled \(C^1\) contact factors.  It is an exposed-rank and fixed
standard/self-scaled-barrier consequence, not a lower bound for arbitrary
coupled barriers or unrestricted quantum algorithms.  Scalar rays have
zero contact mixed curvature and do not affect the token count.

An independent hostile audit checked the two exceptional ranks.  For
\(q=1\), it verified that the complementary primitive idempotent acts by
one half on the relevant cross space.  For \(q=2\), it diagonalized the
unrelated-row sum in the rank-two Albert face and verified that its two
positive eigenvalues act invertibly on
\(V(e,c_1)\oplus V(e,c_2)\).  It also checked that three productive rows
force a common primitive ray.  No associativity assumption is used.

The classical ingredients and the exposed-minor distance theorem are in
[Exposed Jordan rank forces bounded-Dikin iterations on symmetric-cone
lifts](2026-09-04-symmetric-cone-exposed-rank-dikin-lower-bound.md).
