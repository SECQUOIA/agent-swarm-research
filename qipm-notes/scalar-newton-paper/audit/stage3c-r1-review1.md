## Findings

1. **Minor — explicitly require both directions of access to each one-entry map.** Location: lines 28–31, the representation `X_j=T_jv_j` and “Its locations and values are queryable”; also the local evaluations and support materialization at lines 89–100 and 146–149. The proof uses both `T_j` and `T_j^T`, and must enumerate the possible nonzero from either a requested row or a requested column at constant cost. Merely having a forward location oracle for a partial permutation does not provide efficient inverse location access. This is an access-contract ambiguity, not a flaw in the intended theorem: the diagonal maps and standard embeddings used in the cone applications have both directions. Replace the sentence by an explicit requirement of row- and column-sparse location/value access, using Definition 2.1's convention, and state that it has constant cost.

2. **Minor — restate full row rank at the start of the profile subsection.** Location: lines 424–438, preceding Proposition `prop:profile`. The proposition asserts positive definiteness of `P_theta`, and its implementation factors `S`, but this subsection does not state that `A=[A_1 ... A_L]` has full row rank. That hypothesis occurs in the earlier cone corollary, rather than as a standing assumption for all subsequent results. Without it, `A=0` is an immediate counterexample to the standalone proposition. Add “Assume that A has full row rank” when defining `N,S`. This is a missing restatement of the clearly intended assumption; all subsequent profile arguments are correct under it.

3. **Minor — define the sparsity condition in the concluding full-output claim.** Location: lines 687–690, “scalar-sparse data ... admit D^{1+o(1)} arithmetic per solve.” The phrase “scalar-sparse” has not been defined in the manuscript. The profile cost contains `nnz A`, so the near-linear conclusion requires an explicit bound such as `nnz A=D^{1+o(1)}` (the stronger `O(D)` would also suffice). Replace the informal phrase by the numerical hypothesis. For the supplied latent-width arm the incidence graph itself already bounds the structural nonzero count by `O(D(tau_L+1))`; noting that avoids suggesting an unnecessary additional restriction there.

4. **Minor — identify the Lorentz barrier whose Hessian is being used.** Location: lines 202–207, first Lorentz inverse identity. The subsection introduces a Lorentz point and then gives a formula for `H^{-1}` without explicitly saying that `H` is the Hessian of `F_L(t,z)=-log(t^2-||z||^2)`. The formula and later one-hub/two-hub identities have exactly this normalization; scaling the standard barrier changes them. Add that definition and, for complete clarity, say that `t>||z||`. The existing phrase “Lorentz point” conveys the intended cone, but the normalization should be explicit in a standalone paper.

## Independent mathematical checks

- **Indefinite core.** With `K=X^T C^T S^{-1} C X`, the projection identity gives `0<=K<=I`. Direct multiplication verifies `(I+LK)^{-1}=I-LX^T C^T N^{-1} C X`, without assuming `L` invertible. Its norm is at most `1+ell/a`. The Woodbury solution representation and `||h||<=sqrt(kappa)` follow. This avoids the usual unjustified symmetric-core assumption.
- **Polynomial biases and statistics.** Writing `C=S^{1/2}V`, `VV^T=I`, gives all three displayed polynomial norm bounds and biases. Source-vector estimators have second moments at most `4` and `4 kappa`. Entry tolerances, the Frobenius-to-operator conversion, symmetrization, and the displayed sample counts are consistent.
- **Perturbation constants.** The small-inverse perturbation gives `||zhat-z||<=2U_*(nu+rho U_*sqrt(kappa))`. Combining the two polynomial biases and `PCX` error gives the displayed vector bound. The stated tolerances make each of its three contributions at most `epsilon/(12 beta)`. The lower bound on `||N^{-1}b||` and the good-event bound on `||zhat||` have ample slack.
- **Raw mixture sampler.** Re-deriving the rectangular rejection trial gives raw mass `|(Tv)_i|^2/(W B_T^2 ||v||^2)`, including zero columns. Mixing with weights `c_j^2 D_j/Z` and the final cancellation-aware acceptance produces exactly `|ytilde_i|^2/((r+1)Z)`. The proposed denominator is positive on every reachable final step. The lower success bound, inverse and coefficient guards, and finite cap are sufficient, including when a bad setup produces the zero vector. Capping identical independent trials preserves the accepted conditional distribution. No transformed-column norm is implicitly acquired.
- **Scalar readout.** With normalized `b`, `q>=1/beta` and `||N^{-1}b||<=kappa/a`. The tighter vector tolerance bounds the scalar bias. Independent sampling from `p_b` has conditional second moment at most `4 kappa^2/a^2`, giving the stated sample cost and total failure budget.
- **Cone algebra.** Independently differentiating the power barrier reproduces the diagonal base, both off-diagonal signs, and the normalized two-dimensional matrix. Its physical clipping interval ensures positive determinant, comparison bounds, coefficient norm bound, and the derivative `L'=L diag(1,0)L`. The coefficient perturbation through `Nhat` to the final vector/scalar has sufficient tolerance slack. Lorentz inverse eigenvalues and both latent expansions are correct. The aggregated-logarithm Hessian is indeed a positive diagonal plus a rank-at-most-three correction.
- **Scalar acquisition lower bound.** Equal pair norms make the original vector SQ calls simulable by one hidden-bit query. The geometric-mean expression determines Hamming weight exactly. The proposed growing-log-range pairs give adjacent value ratio greater than three, so relative error below one half also recovers the weight. The limitation concerning alpha-weight versus alpha-squared sampling is correctly stated.
- **Full-output comparisons.** The profile Loewner inequalities, residual certificate, primal energy identity, small signed-core invertibility, and setup/application costs check out after stating full row rank. The inertia proof of the rank charge is valid; the two kernel intersections have positive dimension. Both quasidefinite constructions have the required positive and negative principal groups. Regularization bias is a direct valid Neumann bound. The generic one-star impossibility and the coarse-forest tree decomposition are correct. The CGLS estimate uses the correct unsquared singular condition number after passing to the normal equations.

Replacing each graph vertex in a bag by row and column copies yields the manuscript's claimed width and compactness bounds.
