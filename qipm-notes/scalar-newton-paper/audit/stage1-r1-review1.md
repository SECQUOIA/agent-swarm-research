1. **Minor: make the finite-error transfer a coupling statement and define off-support behavior.** Location: `sections/03-classical.tex`, Proposition `prop:precision`, lines 294–308. The proof is correct as a coupling argument, but the assertion that the final median differs from “the exact-oracle output” should explicitly say that the two outputs admit such a coupling. Independent exact and approximate runs need not be close even when both oracle laws are identical. Also a distribution at TV distance delta from `p_b` can output an index with `b_i=0`; the displayed exact estimator is undefined there. This is harmless for the proof because it is necessarily a coupling-failure event, but the algorithm must still be defined. Concrete fix: say “there exists a coupling with an exact-oracle run under which ...”; prescribe an arbitrary finite fallback value, such as zero, whenever a sampled index has `b_i=0`, and impose the arithmetic-error condition only on indices in the support of `b`. Keep the `L delta` union bound. No change in theorem complexity or error constants is needed.

2. **Minor: explicitly attribute the established Kantorovich inequality.** Location: `sections/03-classical.tex`, Lemma `lem:kantorovich` and the opening paragraph. The proof of the sharp constant is correct, but the lemma's visible title is only “Positive-form second moment,” and neither its statement nor its proof identifies the final inequality as the classical Kantorovich inequality. This makes the boundary between a new estimator specialization and its established analytic ingredient less clear than the source note. Concrete fix: name the inequality in the lemma title or its introductory sentence and add an appropriate verified reference, while retaining the elementary proof and the careful statement that sharpness here concerns this estimator. ## Checks and attribution observations

- The mean is correct for complex input: `|b_i|^2/b_i = conjugate(b_i)` on the sampling support. The second moment must be an inequality if `P b` has mass outside that support; the manuscript correctly preserves that inequality.
- The claimed sharpness is attained by the stated diagonal positive matrix and endpoint spectral weights. It does not imply that every positive matrix has large estimator variance, and the manuscript does not make that stronger claim.
- With `eta=epsilon/4`, the exact scalar error is at most `17 epsilon q/32` for `epsilon<=1/2`; another `epsilon q/4` fits. A group size `64 C kappa/epsilon^2` yields group failure at most `1/4` at tolerance `epsilon Q/4`.
- The general inverse residual identity is valid without normality: `P A = q(A* A) A* A`. The singular-value bounds and rejection acceptance probabilities are correct. Both row and column access are needed in that theorem and are supplied.
- Unknown inverse-energy norms in the bilinear corollary do not require an extra oracle: only the bound on variance divided by squared tolerance determines the predetermined group size.
- The rational-input paragraph no longer asserts that bounded polynomial values alone control bit precision after division by a small sampled coordinate. This is a sound narrowing of the source note.
- The bibliography's Gharibian–Le Gall title corrects erroneous local catalogue metadata. Primary records checked during this review support the stated high-level comparisons: [Gharibian–Le Gall](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09079), [Montanaro–Shao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06999), [Cifuentes et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13937), [Andoni–Krauthgamer–Pogrow](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02995), and [Li–Sra–Jegelka](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/lig16.html).
