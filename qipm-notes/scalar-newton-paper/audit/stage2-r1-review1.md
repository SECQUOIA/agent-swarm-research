# Stage 2, round 1: independent review 1

Verdict: **no major mathematical issue found**. Three minor statement/clarity issues should be repaired. I reviewed both Stage 2 sections in full, the author record and source-map entries, and their interface to the current Stage 1 model. I did not read any other review reports.

## Findings

1. **Minor — state the success convention and accuracy explicitly in all scalar lower theorems.** Locations: `04-lower-bounds.tex`, Theorem `thm:statistical-lower` and Corollary `cor:coherent-counting`; `05-composition.tex`, Proposition `prop:contrast` and Theorems `thm:distributional-product`, `thm:path-product`. The first clock theorem specifies probability 2/3, but these later scalar statements omit the success probability. The path theorem also says only “estimate its inverse form relatively,” without explicitly saying relative error epsilon. Their proofs consistently use bounded error, so this is a contract clarity issue, not a failed proof. The models section defines zeta but does not set a default success probability for lower bounds. Concrete fix: add a general convention that lower bounds refer to success at least 2/3 on every promised input unless specified otherwise, and say “to relative error epsilon” in the path theorem. Distinguish the expected-cost distributional composition theorem from the worst-case query-bounded scalar claims as the existing discussion already does.

2. **Minor — the abstract compiler should explicitly quantify over every granted oracle operation.** Locations: `05-composition.tex`, Proposition `prop:contrast`, lines 38–40, and Theorem `thm:distributional-product`, line 100. The conditional proposition explicitly constrains only “every matrix value request,” while “public full-SQ metadata” is not formally defined; the next theorem uses the undefined shorthand “oracle-preserving.” All concrete clock families have the required stronger property, but a standalone abstract compiler should not rely on readers interpreting metadata to include sparse locations and all sampling distributions. Concrete fix: require that every granted sparse-location, coordinate-value, norm, and sampling request (including any constraint-factor requests) can be simulated with O(1) queries to the hidden input, with all remaining data public. This can be stated once as the definition of an oracle-preserving realization and cited by both statements. Without that interpretation, a hidden support or input-dependent locator is not covered by the value-query hypothesis.

3. **Minor — avoid the vacuous endpoint of the rational sparsity bound.** Location: `04-lower-bounds.tex`, Proposition `prop:rational-clock`, base replacement `(s-1)/2 -> (s-1)/8`. At the permitted endpoint s=9, the replacement base is 1, so the displayed specialization no longer states any exponential lower bound, despite the text calling the replacement harmless. The statement is not false, but it unnecessarily loses the promised dependence at that endpoint. The proof actually chooses a power-of-four gate size at least 4 and at least `(s-1)/8`. Concrete fix: use `max{4,(s-1)/8}` as the weaker displayed base, or state the rational lower as `exp(Omega(sqrt(kappa) log(s) log(1/epsilon)))` with the same denominator after explaining the bounded factor in logarithms. The construction already proves the stronger wording.

## Mathematical verification

- **Reciprocal approximation.** The minimax scaling in equation `eq:reciprocal-degree` is correct, including n=0. I checked the primary [Kraus–Vassilevski–Zikatanov PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.1859), Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2. Multiplication by `a` gives the stated prefactor, and `(sqrt(kappa)-1)^2 rho^(n-1)=(kappa-1)rho^n`. The uniform Theta degree statement is sound with a sufficiently small absolute tau0.
- **Montanaro–Shao transfer.** I checked Theorem 1.8, Corollary 5.1 and Section 5.1 directly in the primary extraction `/tmp/qipm-ms.txt`. The degree parameters, factor 1/4 in target entry accuracy, denominator and Forrelation gap match. The affine shift and two polarization calls have the stated error propagation. Public endpoint pads make the condition exactly kappa without affecting the forms. The exposed query interface is simulated independently rather than inherited merely from entry hardness.
- **Full-SQ and LP factor.** Disjoint clock supports imply that every row/column squared norm and every conditional sampling mass is independent of the hidden signs. The block Cholesky multiplication is correct: `B B^T=H`, and `C=B^T` gives `C^T C=H`. Both orientations of sparsity stay O(s). The paired log barrier has gradient zero, Hessian `2H`, and a unique analytic center because the factor is injective. The linear objective produces the stated decrement and the eta choice gives at most 1/32.
- **Dyadic robustness and rational factors.** Nonnegative bipartite Jacobi matrices have norm equal to their Perron root. Contracting then rounding downward preserves the contraction bound. The row-sum perturbation and resolvent estimate give exactly `3 tau/256`, leaving the advertised Forrelation slack. The 3/5,4/5 polarization factor is `25a/48`, and tau=5 epsilon suffices. Rational LDL pivots are positive; replacing each square-root row by four rational multiples gives the exact same Gram matrix because `p q=sum z_r^2`. Column sparsity grows by at most four, and the support/magnitude tables remain public. The generator is only claimed to terminate, which is justified by finite enumeration and the rounded witness; no efficient preprocessing or bit-complexity claim has been smuggled in.
- **Statistical and confidence proofs.** The stopped Hamming-sphere argument avoids support singularities. The stated t, Delta choices obey the lemma for epsilon<=1/128 and u>=1/(8 epsilon), and their scalar relative intervals are disjoint. The small-population zero-versus-one case saturates at M. The confidence proof correctly needs the population assumption; Bernoulli concentration and transcript KL give the logarithm. The coherent bound uses a canonical completion and the specified commuting eigenbasis, so it is not an unqualified general sparse-matrix claim.
- **Composition quantifiers.** I checked the actual proof of Ben-David–Blais Theorem 35 in `/tmp/qipm-bdb.txt`, together with Definitions 33–34. The hard inner pair is fixed before the outer problem/distribution, as needed. The manuscript is not incorrectly inferring that quantifier ordering from a worst-case composition conclusion alone. The Hoeffding exponent, 0.004 bound, separation `S>14 epsilon d`, and central outer weights at n>=200 are correct.
- **Explicit witness.** The half-angle formula, alternating signs, barycentric cancellation, positive spectral measure and Gram–Schmidt identification of the penultimate coordinate establish the cross coefficient and two diagonals. Differentiating `N_r/L_r` with A and omega held as parameters gives the displayed diagonal formula. The restricted support does provide a full-domain dual witness; the manuscript does not confuse it with a full-domain minimax polynomial.
- **High-accuracy path.** Cofactor/Chebyshev expressions, the kappa^(-3/2) boundary factor, coefficient ratios for successive admissible lengths, and the genuine `3 ell+2` source-circuit depth are consistent. The kappa>=1024 choice gives enough contrast for the distributional product. It yields the kappa-squared outer factor only in the stated high-accuracy regime.
- **Construction limitations.** Cyclic dilution, two-cluster inverse algebra, the Schur Rayleigh quotient, and convex endpoint maximization all follow under the written hypotheses. These are correctly presented as limitations of specific constructions, not a general no-go theorem.
