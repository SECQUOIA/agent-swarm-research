## Findings

1. **Minor — require bidirectional access to the one-entry maps.** At `sections/10-structured.tex:29–31`, “locations and values are queryable” should explicitly mean row and column nonzero-count/location/value access at constant cost. Local evaluation of T_i^T and materialization of both rows and columns require the inverse location map. A general injection supplied only in the forward direction does not provide this for free. The diagonal maps and block embeddings actually used for cones have the required access, so the applications need no change.

2. **Minor — specify the zero-radial-vector convention.** The cone corollary at `sections/10-structured.tex:314–316` grants SQ(z), while the model defines SQ only for nonzero vectors. The algebra explicitly includes z=0. State that the radial-vector norm oracle is available also at zero, and that conditional sampling is requested only if its returned norm is positive (otherwise omit that block's correction). This makes the zero case implementable without a dimension-dependent search for a nonzero coordinate.

3. **Minor — repeat the full-row-rank hypothesis for the profile result.** At `sections/10-structured.tex:425–438`, explicitly retain full row rank of A, or equivalently assume S positive definite. The nearby earlier cone corollary has this hypothesis, but the new profile subsection and proposition currently assert P_theta positive definite without stating that it carries over. Without full row rank, all these reduced matrices can be singular; the proposition's inverse, Cholesky, and CG claims require the hypothesis. This is a statement-scope correction, not a failure of the intended argument.

4. **Minor — specify the quantum oracle in the scalar-acquisition lower bound.** At `sections/10-structured.tex:381–389`, the classical SQ simulation is explicit, but “quantum ... under this original vector access” could refer either to classical SQ calls or to a coherent version. State the canonical coherent version for the stronger interpretation: coherently select a uniform pair, query its bit, perform the corresponding known two-coordinate rotation, and uncompute the bit; value queries and controlled inverses also take O(1) bit queries. This proves the quantum lower even with that specified preparation oracle, without assuming that every unitary completion of a classical SQ data structure has bounded simulation cost. The constant-relative construction uses the same interface.

## Probability and cost audit

- Entry estimators start from the supplied unit vectors, so their second moments are bounded by 4 and 4 kappa without needing any lower bound on a transformed column norm. The entrywise errors imply the stated operator/vector errors after a joint median-of-means bound and symmetrization.

- For each fixed setup, the two-stage raw proposal has final coordinate probability exactly |tilde y_i|^2/((r+1)Z). The coefficient-weighted proposal is valid even for zero or nearly zero transformed columns. A selected positive-probability raw output has a positive second-stage denominator.
- The good-event norm and Z bounds give the displayed uniform acceptance lower bound pi_*. Capping independent identical trials at ceil(pi_*^{-1} log(1/delta)) gives flagged failure at most delta on every good setup and preserves the exact conditional output law. This is a reusable fixed-vector contract, not a fresh unflagged failure guarantee.
- Singular-core, inverse-Frobenius, and coefficient-norm guards cannot reject a statistically good setup. They and the finite trial cap terminate on every bad setup, including tilde y=0. Large exact sampled estimator values do not violate the ideal-arithmetic cost model; no finite-bit bound is implied.
- W bounds both local support and materialization cost. Setup, r+1 local coordinate evaluations per proposal, and capped repetitions remain within the stated exponential-times-polynomial envelope; extra powers of W are absorbed by the absolute exponent constant. All tolerances are polynomial in R_* and epsilon^{-1}, so their logarithms have the asserted order.
- The scalar estimator samples independently after setup. On the good setup event its mean has the stated bias and its second moment is bounded by 4 kappa^2/a^2. The two failure budgets combine by a union bound; no positivity or exact norm of the approximate output is presumed.
- The power-cone t estimates are clipped to the physically valid interval. Even bad estimates leave L_hat bounded. On the good t event, the true Gram structure and the coefficient perturbation give a valid spectral promise for N_hat; conditional application of the subsequent randomized setup and the final union bound are legitimate. On the complementary event, the unconditional guards and caps still bound runtime.
