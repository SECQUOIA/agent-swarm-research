\section{Scalar outputs of sparse conic and linear programs}\label{sec:realizations}

The preceding matrix bounds need not disappear when the scalar is required
to be a conventional optimization output.  This section constructs sparse
programs whose designated optimizer coordinate, optimal value, or Newton
decrement contains the same hidden amplitude.  The construction also
identifies exactly what changes when the output is a sample instead of a
number.  Its clock mechanism builds on the Forrelation matrix-function
reductions of Montanaro and Shao \citep{MontanaroShao2023} and the inverse
sampling separation of Gr\o nlund and Larsen
\citep{GronlundLarsen2025}; those mechanisms are not claimed as new.

\subsection{A cyclic inverse with public norms}

Let $q=2^r$, $r\geq1$, and let $n=r\ell$ be the number of work qubits.
A two-function Forrelation circuit $V$ has three global Walsh--Hadamard
transforms and two hidden diagonal sign gates.  Its real amplitude
$\Phi=\langle0^n|V|0^n\rangle$ obeys the promise
\[
 |\Phi|\leq1/100\quad\text{or}\quad\Phi\geq3/5.
\]
The randomized query lower bound is
$\Omega(2^{n/2}/n)$ \citep{AaronsonAmbainis2018}.
Split each global transform into $\ell$ transforms on disjoint $r$-qubit
blocks.  Padding by at most one identity makes the forward length $T$ even,
where $T=3\ell+2$ or $3\ell+3$.  Each layer is $q$-sparse.

Use a clock of length $L=3T$.  The transitions run $V$ forward, hold its
final state for $T$ steps, then run its gates in reverse transposed order.
Let $U$ be this real orthogonal clock transition.  With $W_t$ the forward
product of transitions through clock $t$, $W_0=I$, the orthogonal gauge
$W=\sum_{t=0}^{L-1}|t\rangle\langle t|\otimes W_t$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:history-gauge}
 W^TUW=S\otimes I,\qquad U^L=I,
\end{equation}
where $S|t\rangle=|t+1\bmod L\rangle$.  In particular $W_t=V$ for
$T\leq t<2T$.  For $K\geq3$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-M}
 \gamma=\frac{K-1}{K+1},\qquad
 \alpha_K=\log\frac{K+1}{K-1},\qquad
 M=\frac{I-\gamma U}{1+\gamma},\qquad e=|0,0^n\rangle.
\end{equation}
Here $M$ need not be symmetric; the associated Newton Hessian will be SPD.
Since $L$ is even, the cycle spectrum contains $1$ and $-1$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-spectrum}
 \norm M=1,\qquad \sigma_{\min}(M)=K^{-1},\qquad
 \kappa_2(M)=K,\qquad \nnz(M_{i,:}),\nnz(M_{:,j})\leq q+1.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Inverse history and full sampling access]\label{lem:cyclic-history}
Write $z_0=\gamma^{3T}$, $S_0=\sum_{t=0}^{3T-1}\gamma^{2t}$,
$S_I=\sum_{t=T}^{2T-1}\gamma^{2t}$, and $z=\gamma^{2T}$.  Then
\begin{align}
 x_*:=M^{-1}e
 &=\frac{1+\gamma}{1-z_0}\sum_{t=0}^{3T-1}\gamma^tU^te,
 & R^2:=\norm{x_*}^2&=K\frac{1+z_0}{1-z_0},\label{eq:history}\\
 p:=S_I/S_0&=\frac{z}{1+z+z^2},
 & K_{\rm eff}:=K/R&=\sqrt{K\frac{1-z_0}{1+z_0}}.
 \label{eq:plateau}
\end{align}
Full SQ access to $M$, to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cyclic-hessian}
 H_0=2M^TM=\frac{2}{(1+\gamma)^2}
       [(1+\gamma^2)I-\gamma(U+U^T)],
\end{equation}
and to $M^Te$ requires at most a constant number of hidden sign queries
per oracle call.  The Hessian has condition number $K^2$ and sparsity
at most $2q+1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying the finite geometric series by $I-\gamma U$ proves the
inverse formula.  Different clock positions are orthogonal and each
$U^te$ is a unit vector.  Hence
$R^2=(1+\gamma)^2 S_0/(1-z_0)^2$; summing the geometric progression and
using $(1+\gamma)/(1-\gamma)=K$ gives \eqref{eq:history}.
Partitioning $S_0$ into three equal clock segments gives \eqref{eq:plateau}.
Normality of $U$ gives \eqref{eq:cyclic-hessian} and the spectrum assertion.

For the access claim, the diagonal, forward-clock, and reverse-clock
supports are disjoint.  A transition is either a public Hadamard block,
a hidden diagonal sign, or an identity.  Its row and column squared norm
is one and its magnitudes and locations are public.  Therefore every row
and column of $M$ has squared norm $(1+\gamma^2)/(1+\gamma)^2$; every
row and column of $H_0$ has the same public squared norm
$4[(1+\gamma^2)^2+2\gamma^2]/(1+\gamma)^4$.
Their global row distributions are uniform.  Conditional samples choose
between the disjoint clock blocks with public probabilities and then
sample a Hadamard row uniformly, or take a deterministic sign location.
The corresponding column rules are identical.  Locations and norms need
no hidden query; a value uses at most one.  The vector $M^Te$ is a
public-norm mixture of the clock-zero basis vector and a row of the
last transition.  The same simulation supplies its SQ interface.
\end{proof}

Fix a sufficiently small constant $\delta_0>0$.  For
$0<\delta\leq\delta_0$, choose an allowed $T$ nearest to
$\log(1/\delta)/\alpha_K$.  Allowed lengths have bounded gaps and
$\gamma\geq1/2$, so, with universal constants,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-clock-parameters}
 p=\Theta(\delta^2),\quad R=\Theta(\sqrt K),\quad
 \ell=\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{3\alpha_K}+O(1),\quad
 N=3Tq^\ell.
\end{equation}
Taking $\delta_0$ small ensures $\ell\geq1$.  All lower bounds below
are for these parameter-selected dimensions; they are not asserted for
every prescribed $N$.

\subsection{An optimizer coordinate and a feasible-output guarantee}

Let $Q_{N+1}=\{(t,u):t\geq\norm u\}$ be the Lorentz cone.  Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-cone-program}
 \max_{x,u}\ 2e^Tu
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad u=Mx,\quad(1,u)\in Q_{N+1}.
\end{equation}
Its unique optimizer is $(x_*,e)$ and its value is the public constant
two.  Thus this unperturbed problem has no value-estimation hardness.
Its pulled-back barrier is $\phi(x)=-\log(1-\norm{Mx}^2)$.
This restriction has barrier parameter one, even though the canonical
logarithmically homogeneous barrier on the entire Lorentz cone has
parameter two.  Self-concordance is inherited by the affine restriction;
in $u=Mx$ coordinates its squared gradient norm in the inverse Hessian
is $2\norm u^2/(1+\norm u^2)\leq1$, proving the sharper parameter.
At the public analytic center zero, for
$F_\eta(x)=\phi(x)-2\eta e^TMx$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-newton}
 \nabla F_\eta(0)=-2\eta M^Te,\qquad
 \nabla^2 F_\eta(0)=H_0,\qquad
 d_\eta=\eta M^{-1}e,\qquad \Lambda_\eta^2=2\eta^2.
\end{equation}
For $\eta<1$, this full Newton step is strictly feasible; for
$\eta\leq1/(4\sqrt2)$ its decrement is at most $1/4$.
The central path is
$x(\eta)=\rho(\eta)x_*$, where
$\rho/(1-\rho^2)=\eta$ and $0<\rho<1$.
These identities concern the reduced Hessian.  They make no assertion
about an augmented KKT matrix containing redundant free variables.

For $t\in I:=\{T,\ldots,2T-1\}$ set $i_t=(t,0^n)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:public-readout}
 a=\sum_{t\in I}\frac{\gamma^t}{R\sqrt{S_I}}e_{i_t},
 \qquad h=\sqrt p.
\end{equation}
Then $\norm a=1/R$ and $a^Tx_*=h\Phi$.
This linear observable can be made a literal optimizer coordinate.
Append free variables satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:accumulator}
 y_T=a_{i_T}x_{i_T},\qquad
 y_{t+1}=y_t+a_{i_{t+1}}x_{i_{t+1}},\qquad
 w=y_{2T-1}.
\end{equation}
The chain has $O(T)$ rows and each row has at most three entries.  Every
selected $x$ column gains one incidence.  These equalities and their
norms are public, so full SQ access to the augmented formulation is a
public mixture with the interface of Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclic-history}.
The maximum row and column sparsity remains $s=\Theta(q)$.
Free accumulator variables can be eliminated when differentiating the
barrier; they do not add a cone.

\begin{theorem}[Sparse scalar coordinate separation]\label{thm:scalar-coordinate}
For the parameters in \eqref{eq:scalar-clock-parameters}, estimating the
optimizer coordinate $w_*=h\Phi$ to additive error $c\delta$, for a
sufficiently small universal $c$, requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-coordinate-lower}
 Q_{\rm classical}=\Omega\left(\frac{q^{\ell/2}}{r\ell}\right)
\end{equation}
full-SQ input queries at bounded error.  The same bound applies to
returning this coordinate of any exactly feasible point of objective gap
$\tau\leq c_0\delta^2/K$, for a sufficiently small constant $c_0$.
For the source-equivalent coherent input oracle, estimating the exact
optimizer coordinate at the stated accuracy takes $O(1)$ hidden queries
and $\operatorname{polylog}N$ public gates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coordinate identity follows by taking the overlap of
\eqref{eq:history} with \eqref{eq:public-readout}.  Its low and high
intervals are separated by $(3/5-1/100)h=\Theta(\delta)$.
Every formulation query has constant source-query overhead, so the
Forrelation lower bound proves \eqref{eq:scalar-coordinate-lower}.
If $(\widehat x,\widehat u)$ is exactly feasible and has gap $\tau$, then
\[
 \norm{\widehat u-e}^2\leq2-2e^T\widehat u\leq\tau,
 \qquad
 |a^T\widehat x-h\Phi|
 \leq\frac{K}{R}\sqrt\tau\leq\sqrt{K\tau}.
\]
Exact accumulator feasibility equates the returned coordinate with
$a^T\widehat x$.  The specified $\tau$ therefore preserves the promise
gap.  An approximate equality or cone residual would require an
additional error budget; it is not implicit in this statement.

A coherent query to a designated transition entry recovers any desired
hidden sign, and conversely each coherent matrix-oracle call uses a
constant number of sign queries.  Run the source circuit and estimate
its real amplitude to a sufficiently small constant error, then multiply
by public $h$.  No explicit $N$-coordinate output is included.
\end{proof}

The last distinction is substantive: a numerical estimate of an optimizer
coordinate is a scalar output, whereas producing a complete feasible
vector is a different task.  The classical lower bound for coordinates
of approximately optimal feasible points uses the latter contract only
as a sufficient source of an accurate scalar.  The matched quantum
comparison in this paper is for the scalar estimate, not dense output.

Since $\alpha_K=\Theta(1/K)$, the logarithm of
\eqref{eq:scalar-coordinate-lower} is
$\Omega(K\log s\log(1/\delta))$, after retaining the displayed
rounding and polynomial denominator.  This is an exponent in sparsity,
not in ambient dimension.  The family has a stronger dimension exponent
when a fixed number of hidden sign tables is allowed.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-order Forrelation]\label{cor:fixed-k}
Fix $k\geq2$.  Replace $V$ by the $k$-Forrelation circuit and use the
primary promise
\[
 |\Phi|\leq\alpha_k:=2^{-5k-1}
 \quad\text{or}\quad \Phi\geq\beta_k:=2^{-5k}.
\]
Then $T=(k+1)\ell+O(k)$ and
\[
 \ell=\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{(k+1)\alpha_K}+O_k(1).
\]
At coordinate accuracy $c(\beta_k-\alpha_k)\delta$, the lower bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-k-lower}
 \Omega_k\left(\frac{q^\ell}{r\ell}\right)^{1-1/k}
 =\widetilde\Omega_k(N^{1-1/k}).
\end{equation}
The feasible-point gap sufficient for the same decision is
$c_k\delta^2/K$, with $c_k=2^{-O(k)}$.
The coherent scalar algorithm uses $2^{O(k)}$ hidden queries.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4 of Bansal and Sinha
\citep{BansalSinha2021} give, for fixed $k$, the stated randomized
lower bound and a $2^{O(k)}$-query bounded-error algorithm.  The source
promise has a positive high case; allowing either high sign in a
magnitude-output problem only enlarges the promise domain, so the lower
bound still applies to that problem.  The $k+1$ global Hadamards give
the claimed clock length.  All remaining identities and oracle
simulations are unchanged.  Estimating the amplitude to error
$O(\beta_k-\alpha_k)$ proves the numerical upper bound, and the
feasible-point error $\sqrt{K\tau}$ proves the last accuracy claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Public norm normalization gives a hard optimal value}

The unperturbed value is public, but a public objective tilt makes the
value itself informative.  Define
\[
 g=M^{-T}a,\qquad G=\norm g.
\]
At first $G$ might appear to contain hidden data.  The hold plateau makes
it exactly public, which avoids treating the quadratic tilt term as an
unknown error.

\begin{lemma}[Public tilt norm and plateau optimality]\label{lem:tilt-norm}
Let $m=(I-\gamma S)/(1+\gamma)$ and
$\bar a=\sum_{t\in I}\gamma^t|t\rangle/(R\sqrt{S_I})$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:public-G}
 G=\norm{m^{-T}\bar a}=\Theta(\sqrt K),\qquad
 h\leq G\leq K/R\leq\sqrt K.
\end{equation}
For any clock vector $\bar c$ supported on $I$, with
$b_c=m^{-T}\bar c\ne0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plateau-optimality}
 \frac{|(b_c)_0|}{\norm{b_c}}
 \leq\left(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^{-2t}\right)^{-1/2}
 =\Theta(\delta/\sqrt K).
\end{equation}
Thus the readout \eqref{eq:public-readout} attains, to constants, the
largest normalized inverse-transpose overlap among these
plateau-supported clock readouts.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the plateau $W_t=V$.  Applying \eqref{eq:history-gauge} gives
\[
 W^Ta=\bar a\otimes V^T|0^n\rangle,\qquad
 W^Tg=(m^{-T}\bar a)\otimes V^T|0^n\rangle.
\]
This proves that $G$ is a clock-only quantity.  Its value follows from
the finite geometric inverse of $m^T$, requiring no hidden queries.
If $b=m^{-T}\bar a$, then
$b_0=\bar a^Tm^{-1}|0\rangle=\sqrt{S_I/S_0}=h$.
Since $\bar a_t=0$ for $0\leq t<T$, the equation
$b_t-\gamma b_{t+1}=0$ gives $b_t=h\gamma^{-t}$ there.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G-lower}
 G^2\geq h^2\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^{-2t}
 =\frac{1-z}{1+z+z^2}\frac{\gamma^2}{1-\gamma^2}
 =\frac{1-z}{1+z+z^2}\frac{(K-1)^2}{4K}.
\end{equation}
For the chosen $z=\Theta(\delta^2)$ this is $\Omega(K)$.
The upper bound is $\norm{m^{-1}}\norm{\bar a}=K/R$.
The same homogeneous recurrence for $b_c$ proves
\eqref{eq:plateau-optimality}; evaluating its geometric sum gives the
last order estimate.  For any public readout on $(t,0^n)$ over this
plateau, its hidden overlap is $(b_c)_0\Phi$ and its direction norm is
$\norm{b_c}$.  Our choice has ratio $h/G=\Theta(\delta/\sqrt K)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Optimal value and direct Newton decrement]\label{thm:tilted-value}
Replace the objective in \eqref{eq:one-cone-program}, retaining
\eqref{eq:accumulator}, by $e^Tu+\lambda w$, where $\lambda=1/(2G)$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-value}
 \OPT^2=\frac54+\frac hG\Phi,\qquad \frac12\leq\OPT\leq\frac32.
\end{equation}
Optimal-value estimation to additive error
$c(\beta-\alpha)\delta/\sqrt K$ has lower bound
\eqref{eq:scalar-coordinate-lower}, or \eqref{eq:fixed-k-lower}, with
the corresponding source thresholds $\alpha,\beta$.
The source-equivalent coherent algorithm attains that accuracy with
$O(1)$ or $2^{O(k)}$ hidden queries, respectively.

At the public analytic center, with objective multiplier $\eta>0$, the
squared Newton decrement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-decrement}
 \Lambda_\eta^2
 =\frac{\eta^2}{2}\left(\frac54+\frac hG\Phi\right).
\end{equation}
The same lower bounds hold for its additive estimation at scale
$c(\beta-\alpha)\eta^2\delta/\sqrt K$, even when full SQ access to
the reduced Hessian and its right-hand side is supplied directly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Eliminating the equalities gives $w=g^Tu$.  Maximizing
$(e+\lambda g)^Tu$ on the unit ball gives $\OPT=\norm{e+\lambda g}$.
Since $e^Tg=h\Phi$ and $\lambda^2G^2=1/4$, this proves
\eqref{eq:tilted-value}.  The bound $h/G\leq1$ proves its value range.
The low and high cases have respectively absolute squared deviations
from $5/4$ at most $\alpha h/G$ and at least $\beta h/G$.
On $[1/2,3/2]$ squaring and taking square roots have constant Lipschitz
bounds, proving the accuracy transfer.  The objective has only the
public $e$ and $w$ coefficients, so its access adds no hidden query.
The coherent algorithm outputs
$\sqrt{5/4+(h/G)\widehat\Phi}$ after estimating $\Phi$ to a small
multiple of $\beta-\alpha$; clipping $\widehat\Phi$ to $[-1,1]$ is harmless.

The reduced gradient is $-\eta c$, where
$c=M^Te+\lambda a=M^T(e+\lambda g)$.
Multiplying by $(2M^TM)^{-1}$ gives
\eqref{eq:tilted-decrement}.  The supports of $M^Te$ and $a$ occupy
disjoint public clock regions, and both component norms are public.
Thus $\SQ(c)$ is a known mixture, with at most one hidden query for a
value.  Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclic-history} supplies full SQ access to the
Hessian.  This proves the direct-Newton-system assertion.
\end{proof}

The norm-normalized tilt has bounded eliminated coefficients:
$G\geq\norm{\bar a}=1/R$, because every singular value of $m^{-T}$ is
at least one, so $\norm{\lambda a}\leq1/2$.
The optimality statement in Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt-norm} is restricted to
this plateau and a constant-scale normalized objective direction.
It does not rule out other scalar reductions.
For comparison, using only $G^2\leq K$ would permit the smaller public
tilt $\lambda=\theta h/K$.  Then
\[
 \OPT_\lambda^2=1+2\theta h^2\Phi/K+\theta^2h^2G^2/K^2.
\]
Taking $\theta\leq c(\beta-\alpha)$ separates the cases at accuracy
$\Theta(\theta(\beta-\alpha)\delta^2/K)$, even without computing $G$.
This fallback explains why accounting for the exactly public norm
improves the signal from order $\delta^2/K$ to $\delta/\sqrt K$.

Parameterizing the two-function result by requested value accuracy
$\varepsilon$, choose $\delta=\Theta(\varepsilon\sqrt K)$ within the
small-$\delta$ regime.  The exponential lower dependence becomes
\[
 \exp\left(\Omega\left(K\log s\,
       \log\frac1{\varepsilon\sqrt K}\right)\right),
\]
with \eqref{eq:scalar-coordinate-lower} retaining the exact denominator.
For the reduced Hessian condition $\kappa_H=K^2$, this substitutes
$K=\sqrt{\kappa_H}$; it is a high-accuracy structural realization,
not a replacement for the wider-range lower bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:clock-lower}.

\subsection{Two LP realizations with different access implications}

The direct decrement also occurs in a box LP.  Set
$c=M^Te+\lambda a$ as above and consider
\[
 \max_x c^Tx\quad\text{subject to}\quad-\mathbf1\leq Mx\leq\mathbf1.
\]
The paired logarithmic barrier
$\phi_{\rm box}(x)=-\sum_i\log(1-(Mx)_i^2)$ has analytic center zero
and Hessian $H_0$.  Its decrement is exactly
\eqref{eq:tilted-decrement}.  Both the stacked constraint matrix
$[M;-M]$ and $c$ have the previously proved full-SQ simulation.
Thus the direct scalar diagnostic lower bound already applies to scalar
inequalities.  This statement does not identify the box LP's optimal
value with the SOCP value.

For the unperturbed objective $2e^TMx$, the box barrier subproblem in
$u=Mx$ is
\[
 F_\eta(u)=-\sum_i\log(1-u_i^2)-2\eta e^Tu.
\]
Separability gives $u(\eta)=\rho(\eta)e$, with
$\rho/(1-\rho^2)=\eta$.  Therefore the nonzero path has the same
inverse-state ray as \eqref{eq:first-newton}, and its accumulator
coordinate is $\rho h\Phi$.  Its Hessian is at least $2I$ in
$u$ coordinates.  Consequently a barrier-subproblem gap at most $\tau$
implies $\norm{\widehat u-\rho e}\leq\sqrt\tau$ and accumulator error
at most $\sqrt{K\tau}$.  For any fixed public $\eta>0$, the scalar
coordinate lower bound survives a centering-subproblem gap
$c\rho(\eta)^2(\beta-\alpha)^2\delta^2/K$, with an absolute
sufficiently small $c>0$. Accumulator equalities must again be
exact, or the reported scalar must be computed as $a^T\widehat x$.
The box barrier has parameter $N$, since each paired interval barrier
has parameter one; no small iteration bound follows from this realization.

A second LP trades the full-dimensional box for a one-dimensional
affine slice.  Let $\widetilde a=Ra$, so $\norm{\widetilde a}=1$, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-lp}
 \max_{x,t}\ \widetilde a^Tx
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad Mx=te,\quad-1\leq t\leq1.
\end{equation}
Replace $a$ by $\widetilde a=Ra$ in the accumulator equalities
\eqref{eq:accumulator} and use $w$ as the objective. This scales only
the readout coefficients multiplying $x$, and makes the objective
literally one-sparse without changing the feasible projection.

\begin{theorem}[Affine-slice value and matched upper exponent]\label{thm:affine-lp}
The LP \eqref{eq:affine-lp} has a unit objective, full-SQ input access
with constant source-query overhead, and exact value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-value}
 \OPT=Rh|\Phi|=\Theta(\sqrt K\,\delta)|\Phi|.
\end{equation}
Its core equality operator $A_{\rm eq}=[M\ -e]$ has
$K\leq\kappa_2(A_{\rm eq})\leq\sqrt2K$.
Additive value accuracy $c(\beta-\alpha)\sqrt K\,\delta$ has the
classical lower bounds \eqref{eq:scalar-coordinate-lower} and
\eqref{eq:fixed-k-lower}, and the corresponding constant or $2^{O(k)}$
coherent hidden-query upper bounds.  In particular, for fixed $k$ and
constant additive value accuracy, one can take
$\delta=\Theta_k(K^{-1/2})$, yielding lower exponent
$\Omega_k(K\log s\log K)$.

For the two-function family, at additive accuracy $\varepsilon$ with
$\varepsilon/\sqrt K$ sufficiently small, the classical lower and
upper exponential dependences agree up to constant factors:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-matched}
 \log Q=\Theta\left(K\log s\log\frac{\sqrt K}{\varepsilon}\right)
\end{equation}
when the displayed exponent dominates the polynomial and rounding
factors.  The exact lower bound remains
\eqref{eq:scalar-coordinate-lower}, with
$\ell=[\log(\sqrt K/\varepsilon)+O(1)]/(3\alpha_K)+O(1)$.
The constant accuracy calibration inside the numerator changes the length
by $O(K)$; only the final $O(1)$ accounts for allowed-length rounding.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Invertibility forces $x=tM^{-1}e$, so maximizing over $|t|\leq1$
gives \eqref{eq:affine-value}.  The added column $-e$ changes only one
public row norm of $M$; its location, norm, and all conditional mixture
weights are public.  The objective $\widetilde a$ is also public.
Moreover,
\[
 K^{-2}I\preceq A_{\rm eq}A_{\rm eq}^T=MM^T+ee^T\preceq2I.
\]
The $K^{-2}$ eigenspace of $MM^T$ has multiplicity $q^\ell\geq2$.
A vector in it orthogonal to $e$ preserves the smallest eigenvalue.
The largest singular value is at least $\norm M=1$, proving the
condition bounds.  No such bound is asserted for the accumulator-augmented
KKT matrix.  The value identity and the source gap prove the lower
and coherent upper bounds exactly as before.

For the classical upper bound, apply Theorem~\ref{thm:general-overlap}
to $(M,\widetilde a,e)$, using the exact public
$R_e=R=\Theta(\sqrt K)$.  The theorem permits
$0<\varepsilon\leq R/2$, which contains the chosen small-$\delta$
value-accuracy regime even when $\varepsilon>1/2$.
The absolute-value function is 1-Lipschitz,
so the theorem estimates \eqref{eq:affine-value} at cost
\[
 O\left(R^2\varepsilon^{-2}\log(1/\zeta)\,
       (q+2)^{O(K\log(2R/\varepsilon))}\right).
\]
This yields the upper exponent in \eqref{eq:affine-matched} without
constructing a nullspace basis, an optimizer, or an SQ interface to
$M^{-1}e$.  Substituting $\delta=\Theta(\varepsilon/\sqrt K)$ in
the lower family yields the matching exponent.
\end{proof}

The affine slice has a parameter-one barrier in its intrinsic coordinate:
$\varphi(t)=-\log(1-t^2)$.  Indeed,
\[
 \varphi'=\frac{2t}{1-t^2},\quad
 \varphi''=\frac{2(1+t^2)}{(1-t^2)^2},\quad
 \varphi'''=\frac{4t(3+t^2)}{(1-t^2)^3}.
\]
The gradient bound is $(\varphi')^2/\varphi''=2t^2/(1+t^2)\leq1$.
Squaring the self-concordance inequality
$|\varphi'''|\leq2(\varphi'')^{3/2}$ reduces to
$(1-t^2)^2(t^2+2)\geq0$.
At its analytic center, the tangent is
$\operatorname{span}\{(M^{-1}e,1)\}$, its scalar barrier Hessian is two,
and its projected objective is $Rh\Phi$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-decrement}
 \Lambda_\eta^2=\frac{\eta^2}{2}R^2h^2\Phi^2
              =\frac{\eta^2}{2}\OPT^2.
\end{equation}
Estimating this squared decrement at scale
$c(\beta^2-\alpha^2)\eta^2 K\delta^2$ has the same source lower bound.
Here the scalar projected right-hand side is \emph{not} supplied: it
already contains the hard quantity.  Supplying a nullspace basis and its
objective projection reduces the program to an explicit interval problem.
Thus the constant-barrier result isolates equality preprocessing and
scalar readout, not repeated high-dimensional barrier work.

\subsection{Three-dimensional cones preserve the scalar construction}

For completeness, the single large Lorentz cone can be replaced by an
elementary binary norm tree.  Let $D$ be the smallest power of two at
least $N$, pad $u$ by zeros to $D$ leaves, and for each internal node
$v$ impose $(t_v,z_{v,0},z_{v,1})\in Q_3$, where its children are leaf
coordinates or lower node variables.  Fix the root $t$ to one.
Recursive use of $t_v^2\geq z_{v,0}^2+z_{v,1}^2$ proves that the feasible
projection is precisely $\norm u\leq1$; choosing each $t_v$ as the
subtree norm proves the converse.  Public two-sparse consensus equalities
can copy shared variables into standard product-cone form.  This uses
$D-1$ cones, constant incidence per new variable, and only public new
SQ data.  All optimizer-coordinate and optimal-value results above are
therefore unchanged.

The first Newton system can also be computed exactly.  In the identified
coordinates, the product barrier is
\[
 \Psi(t,u)=-\sum_{v\ \mathrm{internal}}
       \log(t_v^2-z_{v,0}^2-z_{v,1}^2).
\]
First leave all $D$ leaves free, with only the root fixed to one.
Put $J=\log_2D$. The analytic center of this full tree at $u=0$ has common squared
variable at depth $d$ given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tree-center}
 (t_d^\circ)^2=\frac{D-2^d}{2^d(D-1)},\qquad0\leq d<J.
\end{equation}
To verify this, set $y_d=2^dt_d^2$, $y_0=1$, and $y_J=0$.
The barrier on depth-symmetric points, up to a constant, is
$-\sum_{d=0}^{J-1}2^d\log(y_d-y_{d+1})$.
Its minimizing gaps are $2^d/(D-1)$, giving
\eqref{eq:tree-center}.  This point is stationary in all tree variables:
automorphisms are transitive at each depth, so vanishing of each depth
derivative makes every coordinate derivative vanish.
Strict convexity of the sum of Lorentz barriers on the affine slice
then proves it is the unique center; each nonzero variable direction
affects at least one strictly convex cone term.  All leaf derivatives
vanish at $u=0$. This full-tree center lies on the affine slice fixing
the padded leaves to zero, so restriction preserves its minimizing
property and uniqueness. This argument does not require the padded
problem itself to retain the full tree symmetry.

Each leaf enters quadratically only in its bottom cone.  Hence at this
center
\[
 \nabla^2_{ut}\Psi=0,\qquad
 \nabla^2_{uu}\Psi=c_DI,\qquad c_D=2(D-1).
\]
After $u=Mx$, the $x$ Hessian block is $c_DM^TM$ with zero cross block.
For the objective $2e^Tu$, its first $x$ direction is
$(2\eta/c_D)M^{-1}e$.  For the normalized tilt the squared decrement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tree-decrement}
 \Lambda_\eta^2=\frac{\eta^2}{c_D}
        \left(\frac54+\frac hG\Phi\right).
\end{equation}
The tree-variable block is public, has constant degree, and can be
preprocessed without hidden queries.  Thus the direct full-SQ Newton
simulation survives, but its additive decrement gap is
$\Theta((\beta-\alpha)\eta^2\delta/(c_D\sqrt K))$.
The standard product barrier has parameter $2(D-1)$.
Neither a small condition number of the full lifted Hessian nor a
polylogarithmic iteration bound follows from the reduced clock block.
The norm tree is a standard repeated Lorentz norm representation
\citep{AlizadehGoldfarb2003}, not a new cone lift.

\subsection{Sampling outputs and the rare-event cost}\label{subsec:cyclic-sampling}

The distinction between scalar and sampling output can be quantified
without appealing to an unspecified failure convention.
Let $\Pi$ project onto $(t,0^n)$ over the hold segment.  The normalized
inverse history $v=x_*/R$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:history-projector}
 \norm{\Pi v}^2=p\Phi^2.
\end{equation}
If $0\leq\varepsilon_v<1$ and
$\norm{\widehat x-x_*}\leq\varepsilon_v R$, then
\[
 \frac{(\sqrt p|\Phi|-\varepsilon_v)_+^2}{(1+\varepsilon_v)^2}
 \leq\frac{\norm{\Pi\widehat x}^2}{\norm{\widehat x}^2}
 \leq\frac{(\sqrt p|\Phi|+\varepsilon_v)^2}{(1-\varepsilon_v)^2}.
\]
For a fixed source gap $\beta>\alpha$, choosing
$\varepsilon_v\leq c(\beta-\alpha)\sqrt p$ with a sufficiently small
constant makes the low and high Bernoulli event probabilities separated
by $c' p$, and both are $O(p)$.  Averaging $O(1/p)$ independent samples
therefore decides the source promise with constant error, by the variance
bound for a Bernoulli mean.  It follows that a per-sample algorithm needs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rare-sampling-lower}
 \Omega\left(p\frac{q^{\ell/2}}{r\ell}\right)
\end{equation}
queries in the two-function case.  On the scalar family
$p=\Theta(\delta^2)$, this carries the explicit $\delta^2$ factor.
The argument also permits mixtures of good approximate-vector laws, as
long as every component satisfies the same perturbation bound.

Equation~\eqref{eq:rare-sampling-lower} assumes the overall sample law
has that guarantee.  Alternatively, failure may be flagged with bounded
expected retry cost, or unflagged contamination must have mass at most
$c'p$ for a sufficiently small constant.  Constant unflagged failure
on each fresh sample call can overwhelm a vanishing plateau and is
insufficient.  A different contract permits one randomized setup that
produces a fixed good sampler with constant probability: conditional on
this single good-setup event, repeated draws all have a good sample law.
Taking a constant number of independent setups and a majority of their
source decisions gives the usual bounded-error reduction, provided setup
success is at least $1/2+c_{\rm setup}$ for an absolute constant
$c_{\rm setup}>0$, and each conditional decision has error at most
$c_{\rm setup}/2$. Each setup then gives a correct decision with a
fixed positive margin above one half, even if bad setups always err.
This does not require vanishing setup failure. The
corresponding cost statement is
$Q_{\rm setup}+O(1/p)Q_{\rm draw}=\Omega(Q_{\rm source})$;
it does not lower-bound a draw while leaving its preprocessing uncharged.
The
deterministic polynomial approximation in
Theorem~\ref{thm:solution-sampling} has no setup-approximation failure;
its rejection randomness samples a fixed approximate vector.
If the sampler
is promised relative vector error $\varepsilon_v$, choosing instead
$p=\Theta(\varepsilon_v)$ gives the earlier, weaker but useful form
\[
 \Omega\left(\frac{\varepsilon_v q^{\ell/2}}{r\ell}\right),\qquad
 \ell=\frac{\log(1/\varepsilon_v)}{6\alpha_K}+O(1),
\]
under the same contamination condition, for vector error sufficiently
small relative to the fixed source gap $\beta-\alpha$. The sharper perturbation
inequality above also allows $p=\Theta(\varepsilon_v^2)$ with a sufficiently
large fixed prefactor, at the cost of its $\varepsilon_v^2$ repetition factor.
Ordinary bounded-error scalar estimation in
Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar-coordinate} needs neither repetition penalty nor
a vanishing-failure contract.

At fixed sufficiently small vector error and fixed $q$, choosing
$p=\Theta(1)$ gives $T=\Theta(K)$ and $\log N=\Theta(K)$.
The fixed-$k$ construction then gives
$\widetilde\Omega_k(N^{1-1/k})$ classical sample hardness at
$\kappa(M)=\Theta(\log N)$ and
$\kappa(H_0)=\Theta(\log^2N)$.  These attain the necessary
conditioning scales following Theorem~\ref{thm:solution-sampling}.
For full first-Newton access use $(H_0,M^Te)$, whose inverse solution
is $M^{-1}e/2$ and whose full-SQ simulation was proved above.
An exactly feasible point of SOCP objective gap $\tau$ has relative
vector error at most $K_{\rm eff}\sqrt\tau\leq\sqrt{K\tau}$;
thus $\tau\leq\varepsilon_v^2/K$ transfers its sample output to this guarantee.

There are explicit upper bounds for this constructed family.  Classically,
reading all hidden sign tables costs $O_k(q^\ell)$ queries; computing
their circuit prefixes and sampling the public geometric clock gives
the exact inverse-state distribution in
$q^\ell\poly_k(r,\ell)$ arithmetic.  A designated matrix value query
recovers each sign, so this upper bound uses the same input interface.
Consequently the classical logarithmic complexity is
$\Theta(K\log s\log(1/\varepsilon_v))$ whenever its leading term dominates the
displayed repetition and denominator factors.
Quantumly, prepare the public geometric clock and apply a gate whenever
the clock has passed that gate's position.  Forward and reverse segments
use each hidden sign gate twice in total. This prepares the exact
normalized history in the ideal circuit model. For any requested Euclidean
state error $0<\eta<1$, synthesize the public circuit to operator error at
most $\eta$: with $G_{\rm public}$ public gates, allocating error at most
$\eta/G_{\rm public}$ to each gate suffices by telescoping. The
implementation retains $O(k)$ hidden queries and public gate cost
polynomial in $n,T,\log(1/\eta)$. Taking $\eta\leq\varepsilon_v$
identifies the synthesized normalized state with a vector $\widehat x/R$
satisfying the relative-vector sampling tolerance above.
It is an algorithm for this
source-equivalent coherent realization; a classical SQ data structure
alone does not provide its controlled sign queries.

There is also a generic feasible-\emph{sample} upper envelope.  The
normal-equation polynomial used in Theorem~\ref{thm:solution-sampling}
can produce $z=P e$ with $\norm{Mz-e}\leq\xi$, at degree
$O(K\log(1/\xi))$.  This follows directly from the singular-value
residual bound for $M P$, not just the relative error in $z$.
For $\xi<1/2$, the normalized point
\[
 x=z/\norm{Mz},\qquad u=Mz/\norm{Mz}
\]
is exactly feasible for \eqref{eq:one-cone-program} and has objective
gap $O(\xi^2)$: its squared angular distance from $e$ is bounded by
a constant times $\norm{Mz-e}^2$.  Scaling $z$ does not change its
squared-coordinate sample law.  Setting $\xi=\Theta(\varepsilon_v/\sqrt K)$
therefore gives a sample from a point of gap $O(\varepsilon_v^2/K)$ with
exponential cost
$\exp(O(K\log(s+1)\log(\sqrt K/\varepsilon_v)))$, up to polynomial factors.
This assertion supplies the $x$-block sample law; it does not supply the
normalization or explicit coordinates of the feasible point for free.

\subsection{Prior work and the meaning of the optimization separation}

Forrelation decision and its near-linear classical lower bounds are due
to Aaronson--Ambainis and Bansal--Sinha
\citep{AaronsonAmbainis2018,BansalSinha2021}.  The history-state sampling
obstruction is already present in Gr\o nlund--Larsen
\citep{GronlundLarsen2025}.  Ellipsoids, norm trees, and their SOCP
formulations are standard.  Apers and Gribling
\citep[Lemma 8.5]{ApersGribling2026} already prove a full-SQ classical
lower bound for constant-error LP optimal-value estimation.  Therefore
neither an SQ optimization lower bound nor scalar optimization hardness
alone is new here.  To the best of our knowledge, the additional content
is the combined conditioning--sparsity--accuracy accounting for these
specific wrappers, the public inverse-transpose norm that permits the
larger value signal, its plateau-restricted optimality, and the exact
direct-Newton and parameter-one affine-slice realizations under their
stated interfaces.

The coherent upper bounds use explicit structured algorithms.  They do
not establish a general quantum interior-point method for arbitrary
SOCPs or LPs.  Quantum SOCP methods that reconstruct classical Newton
directions have a different output ledger
\citep{KerenidisPrakashSzilagyi2021}.  Building an explicit matrix table
or outputting an entire optimizer has dimension-dependent cost; those
tasks are absent from the scalar query contracts studied here.
