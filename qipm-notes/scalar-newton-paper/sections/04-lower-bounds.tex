\section{Classical lower bounds with full sampling access}\label{sec:lower}
The lower bounds in this section charge queries to hidden Boolean inputs.
All other input data are public. This convention is particularly strong:
we grant every operation in Definition~\ref{def:access}, and verify its
simulation, rather than infer sampling hardness from entry-query hardness.
The dimensions of the clock families are selected by the parameters; no
bound exceeding the cost of reading a fixed finite input is asserted.

\subsection{The exponential dependence}
Write $a=1/\kappa$, $m=(1+a)/2$, $h=(1-a)/2$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalized-reciprocal}
 f_\kappa(x)=\frac{a}{m+hx},\qquad
 \rho=\frac{\sqrt\kappa-1}{\sqrt\kappa+1}.
\end{equation}
For a continuous real function $f$ on $[-1,1]$, write
$\deg_\tau(f)$ for its least uniform approximation degree at error $\tau$.
The classical minimax formula for the reciprocal gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reciprocal-degree}
 E_n(f_\kappa)=\frac{\kappa-1}{2\kappa}\rho^n,
 \qquad
 \deg_\tau(f_\kappa)=\Theta(\sqrt\kappa\log(1/\tau))
 \quad(\kappa\geq4,\ 0<\tau\leq\tau_0).
\end{equation}
Here $E_n$ is the best degree-$n$ error, including $n=0$; see
\citet[Corollary 2.2]{KrausVassilevskiZikatanov2012}.
Indeed their reciprocal error, after multiplying by $a$, is
$(\sqrt\kappa-1)^2\rho^{n-1}/(2\kappa)$ for $n\geq1$,
which equals the displayed expression. For $n=0$ half the range of
$f_\kappa$ gives the same answer. The degree assertion follows because
$\log(1/\rho)=\Theta(\kappa^{-1/2})$ and the prefactor lies in $[3/8,1/2]$.
This is an approximation-theoretic input, not a new degree lower bound.

\begin{theorem}[Relative inverse-form lower bound]\label{thm:clock-lower}
There is an absolute $\epsilon_0>0$ such that, for every $\kappa\geq4$,
integer $s\geq5$, and $0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_0$, a family of real SPD
matrices satisfying $aI\preceq H\preceq I$, $\kappa(H)=\kappa$, and
row sparsity at most $s$ has the following property. Returning relative
$\epsilon$ estimates of both $q(H,b_+)$ and $q(H,b_-)$ for two public
unit vectors of support two, with success probability $2/3$, requires
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clock-lower}
 \Omega\left(
 \frac{((s-1)/2)^{\Omega(\sqrt\kappa\log(1/\epsilon))}}
 {\log(s)\sqrt\kappa\log(1/\epsilon)}\right)
\end{equation}
classical queries, even with full matrix sampling and query access.
Consequently a routine that estimates one form for every supplied $b$
has the same worst-case lower bound, up to an absolute factor.
The family has a sparse box-LP realization with public analytic center
and a full-SQ constraint oracle using $O(1)$ hidden queries per request.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We use the matrix-function lower bound of
\citet[Theorem 1.8, Corollary 5.1 and Section 5.1]{MontanaroShao2023}
(the theorem numbering refers to arXiv:2311.06999).
For continuous $f:[-1,1]\to[-1,1]$, their construction gives a
$d$-sparse real symmetric contraction $A_y$ and distinct public indices
$i,j$ for which additive error $\tau/4$ in $(f(A_y))_{ij}$ costs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ms-lower}
 \Omega\left(
 \frac{(d/2)^{(\deg_{2\tau}(f)-1)/6}}
 {\log(d)\deg_\tau(f)}\right).
\end{equation}
For arbitrary integer sparsity budgets, take the largest admissible
power-of-two gate size. Its loss is an absolute constant in the
exponential rate for $s\geq5$; we use this interpretation of the
$\Omega$ in \eqref{eq:clock-lower}.

For clarity, we describe the input structure needed to strengthen their
oracle conclusion. Their matrix is a weighted Feynman path
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-clock}
 A_y=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\beta_t
 (|t\rangle\langle t-1|\otimes U_t+
 |t-1\rangle\langle t|\otimes U_t^T).
\end{equation}
The positive weights $\beta_t$ are public. Each real orthogonal $U_t$ is
a public block Walsh--Hadamard gate, a public identity, or a diagonal
hidden-sign gate. The latter implements one query to either of two
Boolean functions on $n$ bits. The source problem distinguishes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forrelation-promise}
 \Phi(y)=\langle0|W D_1 W D_2 W|0\rangle\geq3/5
 \quad\text{from}\quad |\Phi(y)|\leq1/100,
 \qquad W=H_2^{\otimes n},
\end{equation}
where $H_2$ is the normalized two-dimensional Hadamard matrix.
Its randomized query lower bound is $\Omega(2^{n/2}/n)$
\citep[Theorem 1]{AaronsonAmbainis2018}. Factoring each $W$ into local
Hadamard layers produces the sparsity dependence in \eqref{eq:ms-lower}.
The diagonal orthogonal gauge with blocks $V_0=I$ and
$V_t=U_t\cdots U_1$ conjugates $A_y$ to $J\otimes I$, where $J$ is the
public scalar Jacobi matrix with weights $\beta_t$. Thus its spectrum
is public, and the relevant entry has the form $c\Phi(y)$ with a
public $c\geq\tau$ (a public endpoint sign can be absorbed).
For the even-function branch the queried clock positions are the second
and penultimate vertices; identity gates at both ends give the same
formula. This is why the diagonal entries at both queried positions are
also independent of $y$.

Set $H_y=mI+hA_y$. Then $aI\preceq H_y\preceq I$ and
$f_\kappa(A_y)=aH_y^{-1}$. Adjoin the public scalar blocks $a,1$ to make
the condition number exactly $\kappa$, with right-hand sides zero on
these blocks. The shift adds one nonzero per row. For
$b_\pm=(e_i\pm e_j)/\sqrt2$, polarization gives
\[
 (f_\kappa(A_y))_{ij}=\frac a2(q(H_y,b_+)-q(H_y,b_-)).
\]
Each form lies in $[1,\kappa]$, so relative $\epsilon$ errors propagate
to additive error at most $\epsilon$. Set $\tau=4\epsilon$ in
\eqref{eq:ms-lower}, use \eqref{eq:reciprocal-degree}, and amplify the
two scalar calls by a constant number of repetitions. Choosing
$\epsilon_0$ sufficiently small makes the degree numerator positive
uniformly in $\kappa$. This proves the query bound.

It remains to justify the stronger oracle. In every clock row, the
supports of the two adjacent transitions and the diagonal are disjoint.
A unitary transition has squared row and column mass one; a Hadamard
row is uniform on a public support and a sign-gate row has one public
location. Hence the squared row norm of $H_y$ is
$m^2+h^2(\beta_t^2+\beta_{t+1}^2)$, omitting nonexistent endpoint edges.
Conditional samples first choose one of these three public masses, then
a uniform Hadamard location or the single sign location. Nonzero counts,
locations and their ordering are public. Requested values need at most
one sign query. Row-norm sampling first chooses a public clock position
and then a uniform work index; the Frobenius norm is a public sum.
The same argument applies to columns and the scalar pads. The public
weight table can be preprocessed freely in this hidden-input query
model. Both right-hand sides have public exact SQ. Therefore no granted
norm or sampling request reveals any extra hidden information.
The LP realization follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:box-factor} below.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sparse analytic-center realization]\label{prop:box-factor}
Every clock $H=mI+hA_y$ above has a factor $C_y^TC_y=H$ with
$O(s)$ nonzeros per row and column and public full-SQ metadata.
The box LP $\max_x\eta b^Tx$ subject to
$-\mathbf1\leq C_yx\leq\mathbf1$ has at $x=0$ the barrier Hessian
$2H$ and squared Newton decrement $\eta^2q(H,b)/2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $d_0=\sqrt m$, $e_t=h\beta_t/d_{t-1}$, and
$d_t=\sqrt{m-e_t^2}$. These are positive: their squares are the pivots
of the scalar positive definite tridiagonal matrix $mI+hJ$.
The lower bidiagonal block matrix $B$ with
$B_{tt}=d_tI$ and $B_{t,t-1}=e_tU_t$ obeys $BB^T=H$;
its diagonal blocks are $(d_t^2+e_t^2)I=mI$ and its off-diagonal
blocks are $e_td_{t-1}U_t=h\beta_tU_t$.
Take $C_y=B^T$, and factor the public scalar pads separately.
Only a diagonal block and one transition meet any row or column.
Their disjoint supports and public magnitudes prove both sparsity and
the full-SQ simulation, exactly as above; no product $U_t\cdots U_1$
appears in this factor. The paired logarithmic barrier
$\phi(x)=-\sum_j\log(1-(C_yx)_j^2)$ has gradient zero and Hessian
$2C_y^TC_y$ at zero. The factor has full column rank, so zero is the
unique analytic center. For $\phi(x)-\eta b^Tx$, the Newton gradient
there is $-\eta b$, giving the asserted decrement. Choosing
$\eta=1/(4\sqrt\kappa)$ makes its square at most $1/32$ for unit $b$.
The barrier parameter grows with the number of interval constraints;
this is a local diagnostic lower bound, not an iteration lower bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite rational instances and public preprocessing}
The existential public clock does not require infinite information about
the hidden input. The following statement deliberately distinguishes
finite description from efficient uniform preparation.

\begin{proposition}[Rational realization]\label{prop:rational-clock}
For rational $\kappa,\epsilon$ and $s\geq9$, the clock weights may be
chosen dyadic with $O(\log(\kappa/\epsilon))$ fractional bits each,
while retaining \eqref{eq:clock-lower} with the harmless base replacement
$(s-1)/2\mapsto\max\{4,(s-1)/8\}$. The matrix and two unit right-hand sides can
be rational. An exactly rational $O(s)$-sparse constraint factor also
exists. The public tables admit a terminating uniform generator; this
statement makes no polynomial-time preprocessing or factor-bit-length
claim.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let the ideal public coefficient be $c\geq\tau$, where
$\tau=\Theta(\epsilon)$ will have a sufficiently large absolute
constant. Set $\theta=\tau/(128\kappa)$ and choose $L$ with
$2^{-L}\leq\tau/(256\kappa)$. Round each edge by
\[
 \widehat\beta_t=2^{-L}\lfloor2^L(1-\theta)\beta_t\rfloor.
\]
The Jacobi matrix is nonnegative and bipartite, so its norm is its
Perron root. Entrywise monotonicity gives
$\norm{\widehat J}\leq1-\theta$, and the tridiagonal row-sum bound gives
$\norm{J-\widehat J}\leq2(\theta+2^{-L})$.
For symmetric contractions $X,Y$, the resolvent identity yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resolvent-stability}
 \norm{f_\kappa(X)-f_\kappa(Y)}
 \leq ah\norm{(mI+hX)^{-1}}\norm{(mI+hY)^{-1}}\norm{X-Y}
 \leq\frac\kappa2\norm{X-Y}.
\end{equation}
Thus the coefficient changes by at most $3\tau/256$, and
$|\widehat c|\geq253\tau/256$. An additive $\tau/4$ estimator of
$\widehat c\Phi$ estimates $\Phi$ with error at most $64/253$,
strictly below half the promise gap $0.59$. In particular, rounding
cannot disconnect the two queried clock positions. The gauge identity
makes this perturbation bound uniform over every hidden input.

Choose a power-of-four Hadamard block size, so every normalized gate
entry is rational; the largest allowed size is at least both $4$ and
$(s-1)/8$ for $s\geq9$.
Use the rational unit vectors $(3e_i\pm4e_j)/5$.
Their difference gives the entry through
\[
 a(H^{-1})_{ij}=\frac{25a}{48}(q_+-q_-),
\]
with propagated error at most $25\epsilon/24$. Taking, for example,
$\tau=5\epsilon$ suffices. The scalar pads are rational. Squared norms
and sampling probabilities are rational and public; their square roots
are public algebraic numbers, without hidden information.

For completeness an exact rational factor avoids taking irrational
Cholesky square roots. Write the rounded off-diagonal coefficient as
$w_t=h\widehat\beta_t$. The block $LDL^T$ factorization uses rational
pivots
\[
 D_0=m,\quad \ell_t=w_t/D_{t-1},\quad
 D_t=m-w_t^2/D_{t-1}>0,
 \qquad L_{t,t-1}=\ell_tU_t,
\]
with identity diagonal blocks. For $D_t=p_t/q_t$ in lowest positive
terms, the four-square theorem gives integers $z_{t,r}$ such that
$p_tq_t=\sum_{r=1}^4z_{t,r}^2$. Replace the block row
$\sqrt{D_t}(L^T)_{t,*}$ by the four rational block rows
$(z_{t,r}/q_t)(L^T)_{t,*}$. Their Gram matrix is exactly $LDL^T$.
Each row has one identity block and one gate block; column sparsity
increases by at most four. Zero rows can be deleted publicly. All
metadata remain public. The scalar pads are treated in the same way.

The finite table has $O(T\log(\kappa/\epsilon))$ fractional bits,
where $T=\Theta(\sqrt\kappa\log(1/\epsilon))$.
A terminating generator can enumerate dyadic tables of this clock
length and test, using exact rational arithmetic, the strict spectral
margin and the normalized coefficient condition
\[
        a\left|[(mI+h\widehat J)^{-1}]_{i,j}\right|>9\tau/10.
\]
Here $i,j$ are the queried clock vertices: the endpoints in the endpoint
branch, and the second and penultimate vertices in the even branch.
Equivalently, after absorbing the known public endpoint sign, the test is
$a[(mI+h\widehat J)^{-1}]_{i,j}>9\tau/10$.
Positivity of rational matrices is decidable by exact principal-minor or
elimination tests; the tested quantity is the corresponding normalized
entry of $f_\kappa(\widehat J)$. The rounded witness proves the
search nonempty. A four-square search subsequently produces the
constraint factor. This proves computability, but supplies neither
a polynomial bound for this search nor a stable efficient
inverse-spectral construction. Public preparation is therefore
unpriced only because the theorem counts hidden-input queries.
\end{proof}

The structured clock also illustrates a coherent-output qualification.
Its forms have the public representation $q_\pm=D\pm C\Phi$,
with $|C|\leq D\leq\kappa$ by the scalar clock's positive-semidefinite
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality. Put
$q_*:=\min_{\Phi\text{ in the source promise},\,\pm}(D\pm C\Phi)$;
positivity gives $q_*\geq1$ for the realizable inputs. Coherent
amplitude estimation of the Forrelation circuit to additive error
$O(\epsilon q_*/|C|)$ costs
$O(1+|C|/(\epsilon q_*))$ hidden-sign queries, and suffices for
relative estimates of the two forms. If $|C|\leq\epsilon q_*$,
outputting the public baseline $D$ already suffices with no hidden
query. On clocks calibrated to $c=\Theta(\epsilon)$ we have
$|C|=\Theta(\kappa\epsilon)$, so the nontrivial cost is
$O(\kappa/q_*)$. It is $O(1/\epsilon)$ when
$\kappa\epsilon=O(1)$, or under an additional noncancellation
bound $q_*=\Omega(|C|)$. A constant-query quantum algorithm for
distinguishing the Forrelation promise alone does not imply the
same numerical-estimation cost. No uniform noncancellation estimate
for all minimax clocks is assumed here.

\subsection{The statistical factor, confidence, and finite population}
\begin{lemma}[Two Hamming spheres]\label{lem:weight}
For integers $1\leq t\leq M/4$ and $1\leq\Delta\leq t/4$,
distinguishing weights $t$ and $t+\Delta$ with success probability
$2/3$ requires $\Omega(\min\{M,tM/\Delta^2\})$ randomized bit queries.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the uniform measures on the two spheres. Conditional on any
transcript with $j$ distinct queries and $z$ observed ones, the next
fresh bit has probabilities $(t-z)/(M-j)$ and
$(t+\Delta-z)/(M-j)$. Adaptively choosing its address does not change
these probabilities. Stop a $T$-query transcript if $z\geq t/2$,
where $T\leq cM$ for a small absolute $c$. The chance of this stopping
event is at most $2T(t+\Delta)/(Mt)\leq(5/2)c$ under either measure,
by the expected number of ones in sampling without replacement.
Before stopping, both probabilities are at least $t/(2M)$, and
both are bounded away from one. Their difference is
$\Delta/(M-j)$. The elementary bound
$D_{\rm KL}(\operatorname{Ber}(p)\Vert\operatorname{Ber}(q))
\leq(p-q)^2/(q(1-q))$ therefore bounds the stopped transcript's
divergence by $CT\Delta^2/(tM)$, by the chain rule.
Pinsker's inequality bounds its total variation by the square root
of half this quantity. Restoring the stopped suffix changes total
variation by at most the sum of the two stopping probabilities.
For $T$ a sufficiently small constant times
$\min\{M,tM/\Delta^2\}$ the resulting total variation is below $1/3$.
No such transcript distinguishes the hypotheses with average success
$2/3$. Internal randomness can be included in the transcript; equivalently
apply the distributional minimax principle.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Statistical lower bound]\label{thm:statistical-lower}
For $\kappa\geq4$, $0<\epsilon\leq1/128$, and every integer $M\geq1$,
there are two-sparse real SPD matrices of dimension $2M$ and one public
unit vector for which relative inverse-form estimation costs
\begin{equation}\label{eq:statistical-lower}
 \Omega(\min\{M,\kappa/\epsilon^2\})
\end{equation}
classical full-SQ queries. If $0<\zeta\leq1/10$ and
$M\geq C\kappa\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\zeta)$, failure at most $\zeta$
requires $\Omega(\kappa\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\zeta))$ queries.
Both conclusions hold for a two-sparse box-LP constraint oracle at its
public analytic center.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $z\in\{0,1\}$ set
\[
 B_z=\begin{pmatrix}m&(-1)^zh\\(-1)^zh&m\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad H_x=\bigoplus_{j=1}^M B_{x_j},\qquad
 b=M^{-1/2}\bigoplus_{j=1}^M(1,1)^T/\sqrt2.
\]
Each block has eigenvalues $a,1$. Its supports, entry magnitudes, row
and column norms and Frobenius norm are independent of the sign.
Thus every matrix or vector SQ operation is public, except a queried
entry sign, which uses one input bit. The plus vector has eigenvalue
one for $z=0$ and $a$ for $z=1$, giving the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sign-block-form}
 q(H_x,b)=1+(\kappa-1)|x|/M.
\end{equation}
For $M\geq\kappa/(8\epsilon)$ put $u=M/\kappa$,
$t=\lfloor u\rfloor$ and $\Delta=\lceil8\epsilon u\rceil$.
Then $u\geq1/(8\epsilon)$, $t\leq M/4$, and
$\Delta\leq8\epsilon u+1\leq(u-1)/4\leq t/4$.
The last inequality follows by taking the worst endpoint
$\epsilon=1/128$, $u=16$, or directly by rearranging.
The value at weight $t$ is below two, and the increment is at least
$8\epsilon(\kappa-1)/\kappa\geq6\epsilon$.
Therefore the two relative-error intervals are disjoint.
Moreover $t=\Theta(u)$ and $\Delta=\Theta(\epsilon u)$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:weight} proves \eqref{eq:statistical-lower}.
For smaller $M$, weights zero and one give an increment exceeding
$6\epsilon$ and again disjoint intervals; locating a uniformly hidden
one costs $\Omega(M)$ queries, as required.

For confidence, use independent Bernoulli bits with parameters
$p_0=1/\kappa$ and $p_1=(1+16\epsilon)/\kappa$.
Under the population assumption in the theorem, Chernoff bounds give
$||x|-Mp_j|\leq2\epsilon M/\kappa$ with probability at least
$1-\zeta/10$ for either $j$, by increasing $C$.
On these events the scalar bands are separated by at least
$12\epsilon(\kappa-1)/\kappa\geq9\epsilon$ and are below three;
their relative-$\epsilon$ bands are disjoint. Thresholding the
estimator thus tests the two product distributions with each error
at most $1.1\zeta$. Each fresh addressed bit has the same Bernoulli
law regardless of prior queries, and its KL divergence is at most
$C'\epsilon^2/\kappa$. The transcript divergence for $T$ queries is
at most $C'T\epsilon^2/\kappa$. Data processing to the binary test
bounds it below by $\Omega(\log(1/\zeta))$ uniformly for
$\zeta\leq1/10$. This proves the high-confidence assertion, including
randomized algorithms by placing their random seed in the transcript.
Public SQ requests contribute no divergence.

Finally take $u_0=(1+\sqrt a)/2$, $v_0=(1-\sqrt a)/2$ and
$C_z=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}u_0&(-1)^zv_0\\(-1)^zv_0&u_0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Then $C_z^2=B_z$. Its full-SQ metadata are sign-independent and it
is two-sparse. The box LP with factor $\bigoplus_jC_{x_j}$ has
Hessian $2H_x$ and decrement $\eta^2q(H_x,b)/2$ at zero, as before.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Coherent counting comparison]\label{cor:coherent-counting}
For $\kappa\geq4$, $0<\epsilon\leq1/128$, and
$M\geq C\kappa/\epsilon$, the canonical coherent versions of the
sign-block oracles require $\Omega(\sqrt\kappa/\epsilon)$ quantum
queries, and $O(\sqrt\kappa/\epsilon)$ suffice on this family.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The sparse-value oracle is reversibly simulable with a constant number
of bit queries; all other unitaries are fixed public circuits. This
specifies their completion and excludes unspecified side information.
The weight-decision consequence of
\citet[Theorem 1.1]{NayakWu1999} is
$\Omega(\sqrt{M/\Delta}+\sqrt{t(M-t)}/\Delta)$ for the two weights
used above. In the stated population range, its second term is
$\Omega(\sqrt\kappa/\epsilon)$.
For the upper bound prepare a uniform block index, query its bit and
rotate an ancilla with success probability $1/\kappa$ or one according
to that bit. The average success probability is $p=q(H_x,b)/\kappa
\geq1/\kappa$. Amplitude estimation
\citep[Theorem 12]{BrassardEtAl2002} estimates $p$ relatively with
$O(1/(\epsilon\sqrt p))$ calls and constant success probability.
This proves the bound. Its public common eigenbasis is essential to
this simple upper bound; the corollary does not give that complexity
for general sparse inverse forms.
\end{proof}

The exponential and statistical theorems certify different aspects of
the upper bound. Their separate existence does not multiply their costs.
The next section proves products that hold on a single scalar instance,
and states exactly which stronger multiplication they do not establish.
