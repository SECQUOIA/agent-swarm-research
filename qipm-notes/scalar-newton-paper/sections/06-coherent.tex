\section{Coherent access: a sharp bound and its oracle boundary}\label{sec:coherent}

The distinction between a matrix entry and a normalized block of a unitary
is decisive for quantum lower bounds.  We make that distinction explicit
before transferring scalar hardness to optimization.

An $(\alpha,a,\delta_{\rm in})$ block encoding is a unitary $U_H$ such that
\[
 \norm{H-\alpha(\langle0^a|\otimes I)U_H(|0^a\rangle\otimes I)}
 \leq\delta_{\rm in}.
\]
The interface includes controlled uses of $U_H$ and $U_H^\dagger$.
A second controlled unitary prepares $|b\rangle=b/\norm b$, and $\norm b$
is known.  In the \emph{plain block-encoding model}, no additional entry
oracle is supplied.  The normalization $\alpha\geq1$ is part of the input
contract; it is not the condition number.

\begin{theorem}[Relative inverse forms under block access]\label{thm:coherent}
Let $\kappa^{-1}I\preceq H\preceq I$, $\kappa\geq4$, and
$0<\epsilon\leq1/16$.  At constant failure probability, relative
$\epsilon$ estimation of $Q=b^*H^{-1}b$ has worst-case block-query
complexity
\[
 \Omega(\alpha\kappa/\epsilon)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \widetilde O(\alpha\kappa/\epsilon).
\]
The upper bound is a specialization of the variable-time negative-power
norm estimator of Chakraborty, Gily\'en, and Jeffery
\citep[Theorem 33 in the full version]{ChakrabortyGilyenJeffery2019}.
Writing $T_H,T_b$ for implementation costs of the two controlled
unitaries, its cost at failure probability $\zeta$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cgj-cost}
 O\!\left(\frac{\log^3\kappa}{\epsilon}
 \left[\alpha\kappa(T_H+a)\log^2\frac\kappa\epsilon
       +\sqrt\kappa\,T_b\right]
 \log\frac{\log\kappa}{\zeta}\right).
\end{equation}
Their sufficient encoding accuracy specializes to
$\delta_{\rm in}=o(\epsilon/[\kappa\log^3(\kappa/\epsilon)])$.
The lower bound already holds for exact encodings in dimension three,
with public $b$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $R_b=\norm{H^{-1/2}|b\rangle}$.  The cited theorem with negative-power
parameter $c=1/2$ estimates $R_b$ multiplicatively.  If
$|\widehat R_b/R_b-1|\leq\epsilon/3$, then
$|\widehat R_b^2/R_b^2-1|\leq\epsilon$ for $\epsilon\leq1$.
Thus $\norm b^2\widehat R_b^2$ estimates $Q$, with the displayed cost.
This invocation estimates a norm directly; preparing the normalized
inverse state alone would not provide $Q$.

For the lower bound, let
\[
 H_z=\diag\left(\kappa^{-1},
           2(1+4\epsilon z)/\kappa,1\right),\qquad
 b=e_2,\qquad z\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Both matrices have condition number exactly $\kappa$, and
$Q_0=\kappa/2$, $Q_1=\kappa/[2(1+4\epsilon)]$.  Their relative-error
intervals are disjoint, since
$(1-\epsilon)(1+4\epsilon)>1+\epsilon$.
For a Hermitian contraction $A$, define the exact unitary completion
\[
 W(A)=\begin{pmatrix}A&\sqrt{I-A^2}\\
                    \sqrt{I-A^2}&-A\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Take $U_z=W(H_z/\alpha)$.  The sole changing scalar signal is at most
$5/8$, and $x\mapsto\sqrt{1-x^2}$ has bounded derivative there.
Consequently
$\norm{U_0-U_1}=O(\epsilon/(\alpha\kappa))$.
Replacing the oracle calls one at a time bounds the Euclidean distance
between final purified states of any $T$-query algorithm by
$T\norm{U_0-U_1}$; inverse and controlled calls satisfy the same bound.
Measurements and adaptive operations can be deferred or purified.
Constant-bias distinction of the disjoint output intervals requires a
constant trace distance and hence $T=\Omega(\alpha\kappa/\epsilon)$.
\end{proof}

This lower bound does not hold for the same family with exact sparse-value
access: one query to the second diagonal entry reveals $z$.  A standard
coherent $d$-sparse encoding has normalization at most
$d\norm H_{\max}\leq d$ \citep{GilyenEtAl2019}.  Together with
Corollary~\ref{cor:coherent-counting}, the justified generic comparison is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-quantum-gap}
 \Omega(\sqrt\kappa/\epsilon)
 \ \leq\ Q_{\rm sparse}\ \leq\
 \widetilde O(d\kappa/\epsilon),
\end{equation}
for $d\geq2$, $\kappa\geq4$, $0<\epsilon\leq1/128$, and the
sufficiently large dimensions required by the counting family,
with input-state preparation charged separately.  The lower and upper
bounds in \eqref{eq:sparse-quantum-gap} do not match in general.
The lower bound already holds for the two-sparse sign-block family.
The commuting sign-block family has public eigenvalues; hidden input
only changes their weights in $b$.  Its tight
$\Theta(\sqrt\kappa/\epsilon)$ law therefore does not contradict the
unknown-eigenvalue obstruction in Theorem~\ref{thm:coherent}.

\subsection{Why a single polynomial transform loses a square-root factor}

A useful but weaker construction block-encodes
$B=H^{-1/2}/(2\sqrt\kappa)$, whose success probability on $|b\rangle$ is
\[
 p_b=\frac{\langle b|H^{-1}|b\rangle}{4\kappa}
 \in[1/(4\kappa),1/4].
\]
Amplitude estimation \citep{BrassardEtAl2002} gives additive error
$O(\sqrt{p_b}/L+L^{-2})$ after $L$ uses of this circuit.  Relative error
$\epsilon$ therefore needs $O(\sqrt\kappa/\epsilon)$ uses in the worst
case.  A top-block error $\xi=O(\epsilon/\sqrt\kappa)$ is sufficient,
because its induced probability error is at most
$2\xi\sqrt{p_b}+\xi^2$.  Globally bounded negative-power approximation
\citep{GilyenEtAl2019} supplies this transform in
$\widetilde O(\alpha\kappa)$ block queries.  This procedure costs
$\widetilde O(\alpha\kappa^{3/2}/\epsilon)$ overall.  Variable stopping
times in \eqref{eq:cgj-cost} avoid multiplying the two worst-case costs.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded-transform obstruction]\label{prop:bounded-transform}
Suppose $\kappa\geq8$, $\alpha\geq1$, and a degree-$n$ complex polynomial
$P$ satisfies $|P(x)|\leq1$ for every $x\in[-1,1]$.  If $P$ approximates
$1/(2\sqrt{\alpha\kappa x})$ to absolute error $1/32$ at both
$x_1=1/(\alpha\kappa)$ and $x_2=4/(\alpha\kappa)$, then
$n=\Omega(\alpha\kappa)$.
The same conclusion holds for a bounded Laurent transform of the signal
phase with bandwidth $n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The target values are $1/2$ and $1/4$, so the real part of $P$ changes
by at least $3/16$ on an interval of length $3/(\alpha\kappa)$.
At some $\xi\in[x_1,x_2]\subset[0,1/2]$, its derivative has magnitude
at least $\alpha\kappa/16$.  Bernstein's polynomial inequality gives
$|\operatorname{Re}P'(\xi)|\leq n/\sqrt{1-\xi^2}\leq2n/\sqrt3$.
For a Laurent transform $F(e^{i\theta})$ bounded by one on the unit
circle, the two phases $\arccos x_1,\arccos x_2$ are separated by
$\Theta(1/(\alpha\kappa))$.  The trigonometric Bernstein inequality
$\norm{dF/d\theta}_\infty\leq n\norm F_\infty$ gives the same bound.
\end{proof}

The elementary Bernstein inequalities are used here only for bounded
polynomials and trigonometric polynomials.  This is an interior
signal obstruction: the physical interval is a small part of the full
unitary signal domain.  Removing QSVT's parity restriction, including by
generalized signal processing \citep{MotlaghWiebe2024}, preserves global
boundedness and does not remove the obstruction.  This proposition is
not a lower bound for variable-time, rational, or postselected algorithms.

The access dependence is also central to Orsucci--Dunjko
\citep[Proposition 6 and Sections 4.2--4.3]{OrsucciDunjko2021}.
They prove a worst-case $\Omega(\kappa)$ query lower bound for
positive-definite solution-state preparation under their matrix-oracle
contracts. With a normalized encoding of $I-\eta A$, their inverse
approximation instead has degree $\widetilde O(\sqrt\kappa)$ for fixed
$\eta>0$: the steep part of the approximation is near a signal endpoint.
Such a normalized shifted encoding is an additional access assumption;
obtaining it from an arbitrary encoding of $A$ is not free. Their
solution-state output and the shifted-access construction differ from our
relative scalar output and the unshifted bounded transform above. The
square-root polynomial degree alone also does not include their cost of
preparing the solution state.

\subsection{Relation to earlier scalar quantum algorithms}

Scalar readout of a linear-system solution is established prior work.
Alase et al.\ \citep{AlaseEtAl2022} study additive estimation of $x^*Cx$
for $Ax=b$.  Their expectation-value subproblem for a supplied normalized
state has tight complexity $\Theta(\alpha_C/\epsilon)$ in queries to
an encoding of the \emph{observable} $C$, and supplies a lower bound
for the linear-system scalar task. They also explicitly distinguish block
and entry access for a fixed computational-basis state: their block-query
lower bound persists, whereas one exact-entry query suffices
\citep[discussion after Theorem IV.13 and Appendix A]{AlaseEtAl2022}.
Our lower bound
queries the inverse's matrix $H$, uses relative error, and exposes an
additional $\kappa$ through a changing eigenvalue.  Thus neither a first
scalar-output quantum algorithm nor the negative-power upper primitive
is claimed here.  The contribution of Theorem~\ref{thm:coherent} is the
matching condition-number dependence for the relative inverse form and
an explicit realization of this access distinction for that task.
Likewise, a constant-query
quantum decision of an underlying Forrelation promise is not automatically
a constant-query numerical estimator of every associated inverse form;
the $q_*$ qualification following Proposition~\ref{prop:rational-clock}
still applies.
