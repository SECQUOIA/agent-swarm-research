\title{Advances in Quantum Interior-Point Methods\\
for Sparse Problems: Conditioning Geometry, Query Lower Bounds, and
Limits of Access Models}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A quantum interior-point method (QIPM) solves the Newton systems of an
interior-point method with a quantum linear-system (QLS) solver.  We ask when
QIPMs beat classical ones on sparse linear and semidefinite programs (LPs and
SDPs), counting queries to the input under explicit input-access and output
models.
(i) For every self-concordant barrier on a compact feasible region with a
nonconstant linear objective, the barrier Hessian on the feasible affine
subspace at the central point with objective gap \(g\) has condition number
at least \(c\,(D(g)/g)^2\), where \(D(g)\) is the diameter of the feasible
points with gap at most \(g\) and \(c\) depends only on the barrier
parameter and the projection of the objective onto that subspace.  Log and
log-det barriers attain this bound up to instance-dependent factors.
(ii) Near the optimum of a degenerate LP, the log-barrier Hessian \(H\) has
two eigenvalue clusters, and conjugate gradients needs iterations
polylogarithmic in their separation.  Yet a known bound gives worst-case
cost \(\Thetatilde(\kappa)\) on such spectra for a QLS solver given only a
block encoding of \(H\) with normalization of order \(\lVert H\rVert\),
where \(\kappa\) is the condition number and the dimension is at least of
order \(\kappa\).  Given instead such an encoding of a factor of \(H\) (a
matrix whose Gram matrix is \(H\)), there is an even polynomial in the
factor's singular values that approximates the inverse of \(H\) scaled to
unit norm, with degree times maximum modulus \(\Otilde(\sqrt\kappa)\); a full solve at this cost also assumes that
its two pseudoinverse steps have constant relative output amplitude.
(iii) Explicit sparse LP and SDP families have Newton matrices of condition
number one on the null space of the equality constraints, yet preparing the
Newton direction or solution as a quantum state in the original coordinates
needs a number of input queries linear in the problem size.
Convex-mixture, cut--resistance, Walsh-row, Fourier, congruence,
preconditioning, and state-interface theorems mark the limits of the classes
of gadgets and access models that these lower bounds cover.
(iv) Let \(G\) PSD blocks share one trace constraint, with \(G-1\) of them
identical, one whose cost matrix has a strictly smaller least eigenvalue,
and block dimension and spectral gaps fixed.  As \(G\to\infty\), the log-det
central point at path parameter \(\tau=\alpha/G\) keeps a constant fraction of
its trace on that block exactly when \(\alpha\) is below an explicit
threshold set by the cost spectra.  This trace controls the reduced-Hessian
condition number, the trace distance of the central state from a reference
state independent of the hidden input, and the query cost of preparing that state.
For a sparse holonomy SDP we prove matching query bounds, and we give query
bounds for state preparation as functions of the accuracy.
(v) Conditional positive results include reuse of Newton directions checked
by residuals, stable equilibration and face repair, dual output in
compressed form, and a block-angular border solver whose \(\sqrt N\)
dependence on the number \(N\) of blocks is optimal in the block-oracle
model.
No unconditional end-to-end advantage or disadvantage follows; the costs
left open are data loading, recovery of the solution or Newton direction,
oracle updates between iterations, and output.  We also correct the
simulator-norm estimate and the clock analysis of the quantum central-path
method (QCPM) preprint.
\end{abstract}
