\section{Gadget-Class Frontiers}
\label{sec:gadget-frontiers}

Section~\ref{sec:lp-hardness} exhibited three ways to make a local parity
signal robust: replicate constraints, scale rows, or increase the height of
the propagated signal.  This section asks whether a different sparse LP
gadget can avoid all three costs.  The answer is negative for several broad,
precisely stated classes.  The strongest default result is a graph-free
convex-mixture theorem.  Electrical resistance gives a geometric refinement
for signed-copy graphs and bounded-arity Walsh rows, while multiplier
conservation explains why complete central and Newton states must pay through
row scale, dual scale, multiplier size, or input incidence.

Throughout this section the hidden input is
\(\varsigma\in\{\pm1\}^N\), with \(N\geq2\), and
\(p(\varsigma)=\prod_{i=1}^N\varsigma_i\).  Input locality always refers to
the raw sparse coefficient access of Definition~\ref{def:raw-sparse-access}.
Residuals are absolute when displayed as \(\norm{Ax-b}_2\); when a relative
threshold is named, it is the global RHS-relative residual of
Definition~\ref{def:global-residual}.  These are structural obstructions to
output contracts.  They become query lower bounds only after a separate
reduction verifies local oracle simulation and decodes parity.

\subsection{The convex-mixture frontier}
\label{sec:frontier-mixture}

Consider a fixed-support family
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F_{\varsigma}
 =\{x\in\R_{\geq0}^n:A_{\varsigma}x=b\},
 \label{eq:frontier-local-family}
\end{equation}
where \(b\) is public.  Every row has at most \(s\) nonzeros, every
coefficient has magnitude at most \(B\), and each input-dependent position
\((r,j)\) is a function of one designated bit
\(\varsigma_{\ell(r,j)}\).  Let \(M_{\rm dep}\) be the number of those
positions.  A bit may label many positions, including several in one row.
For a fixed base input, let \(\varsigma^{(i)}\) flip bit \(i\), and suppose
there is an exact witness
\begin{equation}
 x^{(i)}\in\mathcal F_{\varsigma^{(i)}},
 \qquad \norm{x^{(i)}}_\infty\leq H
 \quad(1\leq i\leq N).
 \label{eq:frontier-neighbor-witnesses}
\end{equation}
The magnitude bounds \(B,H\) are nonnegative.  The residual estimates
allow \(s=0\); the divided resource inequalities below assume \(s>0\).

\begin{theorem}[Single-flip convex mixture]
\label{thm:frontier-single-flip}
The average \(\bar x=N^{-1}\sum_i x^{(i)}\) is nonnegative and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\norm{A_{\varsigma}\bar x-b}_2
 \leq \frac{2BH\sqrt{sM_{\rm dep}}}{N}.}
 \label{eq:frontier-single-flip}
\end{equation}
If a public objective \(c\) has the same optimum \(v_*\) on every instance
and the witnesses in \eqref{eq:frontier-neighbor-witnesses} are optimal, then
\(c^\top\bar x=v_*\): the mixture is objective-exact, although generally
infeasible for the base instance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Feasibility for the neighboring instances gives
\[
 N(A_{\varsigma}\bar x-b)
 =\sum_{i=1}^N
   (A_{\varsigma}-A_{\varsigma^{(i)}})x^{(i)}.
\]
Let \(D_r\) be the dependent positions in row \(r\), and set
\(m_r=|D_r|\).  Expanding the \(r\)-th coordinate by positions, only the
summand \(i=\ell(r,j)\) can change position \((r,j)\).  The numerator is
therefore a sum of at most \(m_r\) terms, each of magnitude at most \(2BH\).
Since \(m_r\leq s\),
\[
 \left|N(A_{\varsigma}\bar x-b)_r\right|^2
 \leq4B^2H^2m_r^2
 \leq4sB^2H^2m_r.
\]
Summing over rows and using \(\sum_rm_r=M_{\rm dep}\) proves
\eqref{eq:frontier-single-flip}.  Convexity proves nonnegativity, and
linearity proves the objective statement.
\end{proof}

The theorem also handles a one-bit-local right-hand side by applying it to
\([A_{\varsigma},-b_{\varsigma}](x,1)=0\).  One then uses row sparsity
\(s+1\), height \(\max\{H,1\}\), replaces \(B\) by a bound on every entry
of the augmented matrix \([A_{\varsigma},-b_{\varsigma}]\), and counts the
dependent RHS positions.
Inequalities are covered after standard slack conversion only when the
selected slack coordinates satisfy the same height bound.

The output hypothesis cannot be omitted.  For an affine public score
\(\phi(x)=a^\top x-a_0\), suppose every selected witness for input \(\xi\)
obeys \(p(\xi)\phi(x^\xi)\geq\gamma>0\).  All single-flip witnesses have
parity \(-p(\varsigma)\), so
\(p(\varsigma)\phi(\bar x)\leq-\gamma\).  Thus a soundness contract excluding
every nonnegative, objective-exact, wrong-output point with relative residual
at most \(\eta\geq0\), with \(b\neq0\) as in
Definition~\ref{def:global-residual}, requires
\begin{equation}
 M_{\rm dep}B^2H^2
 >\frac{\eta^2\norm b_2^2}{4s}N^2.
 \label{eq:frontier-mixture-product}
\end{equation}
In particular, with fixed \(s,B,\eta,\norm b_2\), linear incidence and
bounded height are incompatible with a fixed residual tube.
Here and in the fixed-tube asymptotic consequences below, \(\eta>0\).

The same conclusion applies to the normalized primal-state contract under a
concrete mixture-stability condition.  Let \(\mathcal S\) be \(L\geq1\) pairwise disjoint,
public output pairs \((u_\ell,v_\ell)\), and suppose
\begin{equation}
 p(\xi)(u_\ell^\xi-v_\ell^\xi)\geq a>0
 \quad(\ell\in\mathcal S)
 \label{eq:frontier-pair-margin}
\end{equation}
for every selected witness.  Averaging gives
\(-p(\varsigma)(\bar u_\ell-\bar v_\ell)\geq a\).  Nonnegativity gives
\(\bar u_\ell+\bar v_\ell\geq a\), hence
\[
 -p(\varsigma)(\bar u_\ell^2-\bar v_\ell^2)\geq a^2.
\]
For
\(Q_{\mathcal S}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal S}
 (\ket{u_\ell}\!\bra{u_\ell}-\ket{v_\ell}\!\bra{v_\ell})\),
\begin{equation}
 -p(\varsigma)
 \left\langle\frac{\bar x}{\norm{\bar x}_2}\middle|
 Q_{\mathcal S}\middle|\frac{\bar x}{\norm{\bar x}_2}\right\rangle
 \geq\frac{La^2}{nH^2}.
 \label{eq:frontier-pair-decoder}
\end{equation}
Measure a coordinate, output its pair sign on the selected pairs, and
use an independent fair sign on all other coordinates.  This binary
measurement has half the displayed signed expectation as its wrong-parity bias.
Thus \(La^2=\Omega(nH^2)\) gives a constant wrong-parity bias.
Without an affine score, a common output template, the pair margin
\eqref{eq:frontier-pair-margin}, or another explicit mixture-stability
property, Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-single-flip} makes no claim about a
normalized quadratic decoder.

Two useful refinements expose the role of sensitivity and uneven input
incidence.  For a Boolean function \(f\), fix a base input with sensitive set
\(S_f\) of size \(k>0\), and average only its sensitive neighbors.  If \(M_S\)
counts positions labelled by bits in \(S_f\), the same proof gives
\begin{equation}
 \norm{A_{\varsigma}\bar x_S-b}_2
 \leq\frac{2BH\sqrt{sM_S}}{k}.
 \label{eq:frontier-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
Under the common-objective and mixture-stable output hypotheses, relative
soundness at radius \(\eta\) therefore requires
\[
 M_SB^2H^2>
 \frac{\eta^2\norm b_2^2k^2}{4s}.
\]
More sharply, let \(M_i\) be the number of positions labelled by sensitive
bit \(i\).  For weights \(w_i\geq0\), \(\sum_iw_i=1\), rowwise
Cauchy--Schwarz, with \(x_w=\sum_iw_ix^{(i)}\), gives
\begin{equation}
 \norm{A_{\varsigma}x_w-b}_2^2
 \leq4sB^2H^2\sum_iM_iw_i^2.
 \label{eq:frontier-weighted-mixture}
\end{equation}
When every \(M_i>0\), its right side is minimized by
\(w_i=M_i^{-1}/\sum_hM_h^{-1}\), yielding
\[
 \norm{A_{\varsigma}x_w-b}_2
 \leq2BH\sqrt{\frac{s}{\sum_iM_i^{-1}}}.
\]
The corresponding relative soundness condition is
\[
 B^2H^2>
 \frac{\eta^2\norm b_2^2}{4s}\sum_iM_i^{-1}.
\]
If some \(M_i=0\), that neighbor is exactly feasible for the base matrix and
already defeats the contract.  These mixtures preserve any uniform affine or
pair margin and the common objective.  Their governing resource is therefore
the harmonic incidence profile, not merely its arithmetic sum.

For a fixed relative tube and fixed \(s,\eta,\norm b_2\), the three pure
amplification currencies are as follows.
\begin{table}[t]
 \centering
 \small
 \caption{Sharp endpoints of the one-bit-local mixture frontier.}
 \label{tab:frontier-currencies}
 \begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
  \toprule
  construction & \(M_{\rm dep}\) & \(B\) & \(H\) \\
  \midrule
  bounded-height parallel replication
    & \(\Theta(N^2)\) & \(\Theta(1)\) & \(\Theta(1)\) \\
  direct row scaling
    & \(\Theta(N)\) & \(\Theta(\sqrt N)\) & \(\Theta(1)\) \\
  gain--plateau scaling
    & \(\Theta(N)\) & \(\Theta(1)\) & \(\Theta(\sqrt N)\) \\
  \bottomrule
 \end{tabular}
\end{table}
Every row has \(M_{\rm dep}B^2H^2=\Theta(N^2)\).  The parallel and
gain--plateau families of
Theorems~\ref{thm:lp-parallel-path} and~\ref{thm:lp-gain-plateau} attain the
first and third endpoints.  Scaling the propagation rows by \(\sqrt N\)
attains the second without changing the feasible set, while exposing the cost
in matrix normalization.  This sharpness is about the frontier's scales, not
an assertion that the formulations have identical conditioning or QIPM cost.

This is not an extension-complexity lower bound.  Carr--Konjevod give an
\(O(N)\)-size dynamic-programming extended formulation for the parity
polytope, and Ermel--Walter develop further flow formulations
\cite{carr2005-polyhedral-combinatorics,ermel2018-parity-polytopes-and-binarization}.
Those formulations do not promise a robust parity output from every point in
a fixed equality-residual tube.  Sparse LP decoding likewise has classical
fractional pseudocodewords and graph-cover descriptions
\cite{feldman2005-using-linear-programming-to-decode,
koetter2005-characterizations-of-pseudo-codewords-of,
vontobel2005-graph-cover-decoding}; this is a conceptual collision, not the
quantitative bound \eqref{eq:frontier-single-flip}.  In particular, expander
LP decoding may use an input-dependent decoding objective, whereas exact
objective preservation above requires one common objective and optimum.

\subsection{Supplements: broader rows, KKT stacks, cones, and centrality}
\label{sec:frontier-mixture-supplements}

If coefficient position \((r,j)\) may depend on a set
\(I_{rj}\subseteq[N]\), define
\[
 d=\max_r\left|\bigcup_jI_{rj}\right|,
 \qquad d_{\rm coeff}=\max_{r,j}|I_{rj}|\leq d,
 \qquad M=\sum_{(r,j)}|I_{rj}|,
\]
while retaining row sparsity \(s\), coefficient bound \(B\), and witness
height \(H\).

\begin{proposition}[Multi-bit coefficient supplement]
\label{prop:frontier-multibit}
The single-flip average obeys
\begin{equation}
 \norm{A_{\varsigma}\bar x-b}_2
 \leq\frac{2BH\sqrt{d_{\rm coeff}sM}}{N}
 \leq\frac{2BH\sqrt{dsM}}{N}.
 \label{eq:frontier-multibit}
\end{equation}
For fixed \(d_{\rm coeff},s\), the product frontier remains
\(MB^2H^2=\Omega(N^2)\) under the objective and output assumptions above.
In particular, the stronger bound recovers the one-bit constant when
every coefficient depends on at most one bit, regardless of how many
distinct bits appear in a row.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(f_{rji}=(A_{\varsigma,rj}-A_{\varsigma^{(i)},rj})x_j^{(i)}\).
It vanishes outside \(i\in I_{rj}\), and \(|f_{rji}|\leq2BH\).
Cauchy--Schwarz first over the at most \(s\) dependent positions in a
row, and then over each coefficient's input set, gives
\[
 \left(\sum_j\sum_{i\in I_{rj}}f_{rji}\right)^2
 \leq s\sum_j |I_{rj}|\sum_{i\in I_{rj}}f_{rji}^2
 \leq4s d_{\rm coeff} B^2H^2\sum_j|I_{rj}|.
\]
Sum over rows and divide by \(N^2\).  The row-union estimate follows
from \(d_{\rm coeff}\leq d\).
\end{proof}

There is also a simultaneous primal--dual version.  Suppose \(A_\varsigma\)
has maximum row and column sparsities \(s_r,s_c\), its one-bit-dependent
positions number \(M\), and selected exact optimal triples
\((x^\xi,y^\xi,s^\xi)\) have all coordinates bounded in magnitude by \(H\)
and common optimum \(v\).  Split \(y=y^+-y^-\), stack primal feasibility
and stationarity, and put
\[
 s_{\rm KKT}=\max\{s_r,2s_c+1\},
 \qquad B_{\rm KKT}=\max\{B,1\}.
\]
Every dependent position occurs once in the primal block and twice in the
split-dual block.  Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-single-flip} therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \left\|
 \begin{bmatrix}
 A_\varsigma\bar x-b\\
 A_\varsigma^\top\bar y+\bar s-c
 \end{bmatrix}\right\|_2
 \leq\frac{2B_{\rm KKT}H\sqrt{3s_{\rm KKT}M}}{N}.
 \label{eq:frontier-kkt-stack}
\end{equation}
Linearity preserves \(c^\top\bar x=b^\top\bar y=v\), so the algebraic gap
is exactly zero.  This covers combined feasibility, stationarity, and gap
contracts; it does not preserve coordinatewise complementarity.

The mixture proof uses only convexity of the domain.  Let \(\mathcal K\) be
a fixed convex set or cone, and suppose that for every selected input
\(\xi\in\{\varsigma,\varsigma^{(1)},\ldots,\varsigma^{(N)}\}\) there is a
base witness \(x^\xi\in\mathcal K\) with
\(A_\xi x^\xi=b\), \(\norm{x^\xi}_\infty\leq H\) in one fixed basis, and
\(c^\top x^\xi=v\) for one public objective and common value.  If, in
addition, a fixed parity-neutral linear map satisfies
\(Lx^\xi=p(\xi)y\) for a fixed nonzero \(y\), then the half-base mixture
\[
 \widehat x=\frac12x^\varsigma+
              \frac1{2N}\sum_i x^{\varsigma^{(i)}}
\]
belongs to \(\mathcal K\), has the common objective, satisfies
\(L\widehat x=0\), and has half the residual in
\eqref{eq:frontier-multibit}.  For a sparse SDP this statement applies after
one fixed \(\operatorname{svec}\) identification; all row sparsity,
locality, coefficient, and coordinate bounds must be measured in that basis.
It gives no normalized-state, rank, determinant-neighborhood, or
complementarity conclusion by itself.

Centrality can be retained only with an explicit stability premise.  Retain
the preceding row, column, one-bit-locality, and height bounds, and suppose
the selected neighboring triples are exact central points at a common
\(\mu>0\), so \(x_j^{(i)}s_j^{(i)}=\mu\).  Define their arithmetic
averages.  For \(t_i=x_j^{(i)}\), direct symmetrization gives the exact
multiplicative-variance identity
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \frac{\bar x_j\bar s_j}{\mu}-1
 =\frac1{2N^2}\sum_{i,k=1}^N
   \frac{(t_i-t_k)^2}{t_it_k}.}
 \label{eq:frontier-multiplicative-variance}
\end{equation}
In particular, the excess is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when the
neighboring primal coordinates agree.  If, for every coordinate \(j\),
\(\max_i x_j^{(i)}/\min_i x_j^{(i)}\leq R\), where \(R\geq1\), then
\begin{equation}
 \mu\leq\bar x_j\bar s_j\leq K(R)\mu,
 \qquad K(R)=\frac{(R+1)^2}{4R}
 =1+\frac{(R-1)^2}{4R}.
 \label{eq:frontier-kantorovich}
\end{equation}
Indeed, for \(\alpha\leq t_i\leq\beta\),
\(t_i+\alpha\beta/t_i\leq\alpha+\beta\).  Averaging and maximizing the
product under this linear constraint gives
\((N^{-1}\sum_it_i)(N^{-1}\sum_it_i^{-1})
\leq(\alpha+\beta)^2/(4\alpha\beta)\), the scalar Kantorovich inequality
\cite{kantorovich1948-functional-analysis-applied-mathematics}.

\begin{proposition}[Central-mixture soundness dichotomy]
\label{prop:frontier-central-dichotomy}
Assume independently a uniform mixture-stable wrong-output premise for the
base input.  Concretely, write
\(Z^{(i)}=(x^{(i)},y^{(i)},s^{(i)})\), and suppose either that a fixed affine
decoder \(\Phi\) satisfies
\(p(\varsigma)\Phi(Z^{(i)})\leq-\gamma<0\) for every \(i\), or that the
primal components have the public pair decoder of
\eqref{eq:frontier-pair-decoder}, with
\(-p(\varsigma)(u_\ell^{(i)}-v_\ell^{(i)})\geq a\) for every \(i,\ell\)
and \(La^2=\Omega(nH^2)\).  The decoded object is respectively the stacked
triple \(Z_w\) or the normalized primal state
\(\ket{x_w}=x_w/\norm{x_w}_2\); under either premise every probability
mixture has a uniform wrong output for the base input.

Let \(w\) be any probability vector and set
\(Z_w=(x_w,y_w,s_w)=\sum_iw_i(x^{(i)},y^{(i)},s^{(i)})\).  With \(M_i\) the
one-bit incidence of bit \(i\), and with \(t_{ij}=x_j^{(i)}\), the stacked
residual and centrality defect obey
\begin{align}
 \left\|
 \begin{bmatrix}A_\varsigma x_w-b\\
 A_\varsigma^\top y_w+s_w-c\end{bmatrix}\right\|_2^2
 &\leq12s_{\rm KKT}B_{\rm KKT}^2H^2\sum_iM_iw_i^2,
 \label{eq:frontier-weighted-kkt}\\
 \delta_j(w):=\frac{(x_w)_j(s_w)_j}{\mu}-1
 &=\frac12\sum_{i,k}w_iw_k
   \frac{(t_{ij}-t_{kj})^2}{t_{ij}t_{kj}}.
 \label{eq:frontier-weighted-centrality}
\end{align}
The weighted residual estimate admits the sharper constant
\begin{equation}
 \left\|
 \begin{bmatrix}A_\varsigma x_w-b\\
 A_\varsigma^\top y_w+s_w-c\end{bmatrix}\right\|_2^2
 \leq4(s_r+s_c)B^2H^2\sum_iM_iw_i^2.
 \label{eq:frontier-direct-kkt}
\end{equation}
Apply the weighted matrix-change estimate separately to the primal
block and to the transposed coefficient changes in stationarity.
The latter permits signed multipliers and has the same incidence profile,
with column sparsity \(s_c\).  Adding the squared bounds proves
\eqref{eq:frontier-direct-kkt}; the earlier bound follows by increasing
the constant.  Thus the displayed soundness inequalities also hold with
\(12s_{\rm KKT}B_{\rm KKT}^2\) replaced by \(4(s_r+s_c)B^2\).
The algebraic objective difference remains exactly \(n\mu\).  This follows
from feasibility, stationarity, and complementarity of each neighboring
triple; common individual primal or dual objective values are unnecessary.
A sound
contract imposing absolute combined residual at most \(\varepsilon\) and an
\(\ell_\infty\) neighborhood of width \(\theta\) around the common \(\mu\)
therefore requires, for every \(w\),
\begin{equation}
 12s_{\rm KKT}B_{\rm KKT}^2H^2\sum_iM_iw_i^2>\varepsilon^2
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \max_j\delta_j(w)>\theta.
 \label{eq:frontier-central-dichotomy}
\end{equation}
Here \(\varepsilon,\theta\geq0\).  For the point-centered convention, with
\(n>0\),
\(\mu_w=x_w^\top s_w/n\), replace the second term by the exact width
\[
 \frac{\max_j|\delta_j-\bar\delta|}{1+\bar\delta},
 \qquad \bar\delta=\frac1n\sum_j\delta_j.
\]
For uniform weights, suppose for every coordinate \(j\) that
\[
 \frac{\max_i t_{ij}}{\min_i t_{ij}}\leq R.
\]
Either convention then has width at most \(K(R)-1\), so the
simpler necessary dichotomy is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{12s_{\rm KKT}B_{\rm KKT}^2H^2M}{N^2}>\varepsilon^2
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \frac{(R-1)^2}{4R}>\theta.
 \label{eq:frontier-central-uniform}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The stacked mixture estimate is the single-flip argument applied to primal
feasibility and stationarity.  Pairwise symmetrization gives the displayed
formula for \(\delta_j(w)\), and linearity preserves the algebraic objective
difference.  The stated affine or pair-margin premise makes the named decoded
object wrong for the base input.  If neither strict inequality in
\eqref{eq:frontier-central-dichotomy} held, that wrong-output mixture would
meet every certificate in the contract.  The coordinatewise Kantorovich
bound gives \eqref{eq:frontier-central-uniform} for uniform weights.
\end{proof}

The stability premise is necessary.  Fix \(0<\mu<1\), let
\(b=\mu e,c=0\), and let bit \(j\) choose
\(a_j\in\{\mu,1\}\) in the diagonal LP
\(\operatorname{Diag}(a)x=b\).  Its exact \(\mu\)-central point is
\[
 x_j=\mu/a_j,\qquad s_j=a_j,\qquad y_j=-1.
\]
At the all-\(\mu\) base input, each single-flip neighbor changes one
coordinate from \((x,s)=(1,\mu)\) to \((\mu,1)\).  Thus every coordinate of
the average has product
\[
 \bar x_j\bar s_j
 =\frac{N-1+\mu}{N}\frac{1+(N-1)\mu}{N},
\]
which is \(\Theta(1/N)\), rather than \(\Theta(\mu)\), when
\(\mu\ll1/N\), although primal and stationarity residuals vanish as
\(O(N^{-1/2})\).  Bounded coefficients and bounded central coordinates alone
therefore do not imply central-mixture stability around the common
\(\mu\).  This particular uniform mixture has the same complementarity
product in every coordinate, so its point-centered width is exactly zero.
Its algebraic objective difference is still \(N\mu\), whereas its
complementarity sum is \(N\bar x_j\bar s_j\); these need not agree at
an infeasible mixture.

There is also a bounded-coordinate counterexample for the point-centered
convention.  Append \(N\) public diagonal coordinates with coefficient
\(\mu\) and central coordinates \((x,s,y)=(1,\mu,-1)\).
The moving coordinates have the common product
\(q=(N-1+\mu)(1+(N-1)\mu)/N^2\), while the public coordinates have
product \(\mu\).  The point-centered mean is \((q+\mu)/2\), and
every coordinate has relative deviation \((q-\mu)/(q+\mu)\).
For \(N\geq8\) and \(\mu=N^{-2}\), the exact estimates are
\[
 \left\|\begin{bmatrix}A\bar x-b\\A^\top\bar y+\bar s-c\end{bmatrix}\right\|_2^2
 =\frac{(1+\mu^2)(1-\mu)^2}{N}\leq\frac2N,
 \qquad \frac{q-\mu}{q+\mu}\geq\frac13.
\]
The original unpadded example has the same residual and common-parameter
defect at least \(N/8\) for \(\mu=N^{-2}\), \(N\geq2\).
Both constructions have row and column sparsity one, coefficient and
coordinate magnitudes at most one, and exactly \(N\) dependent positions.
The padded construction therefore rules out an inference from these bounds
and vanishing residual to point-centered width below any fixed
\(\theta<1/3\).

\subsection{Electrical frontiers}
\label{sec:frontier-electrical}

The graph route constructs an obstruction within one fixed instance and
exposes the geometry hidden by the general mixture argument.  Let
\(G=(V,E)\) be a connected oriented multigraph with root \(r\), nonempty
output set \(S\), public heights \(h_v>0\) with \(h_r=1\), and propagation
rows
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_e(d_v-g_ea_e(\varsigma)d_u)=0,
 \qquad g_e=h_v/h_u,
 \label{eq:frontier-signed-edge}
\end{equation}
where \(e=(u,v)\), \(\lambda_e\neq0\) is a public scale, \(a_e\) is a fixed
sign or a fixed sign times one input bit, and the rows are cycle-consistent.
Thus signs \(\tau_v\) exist with
\(\tau_r=1\) and \(\tau_v=a_e\tau_u\).  Assume independently that the
designated outputs carry parity:
\begin{equation}
 \tau_s(\varsigma)=\epsilon_s p(\varsigma)
 \quad(s\in S),\qquad \epsilon_s\in\{\pm1\}\text{ public}.
 \label{eq:frontier-cut-output-parity}
\end{equation}
Embed each \(d_v\) as the capped pair of
Section~\ref{sec:lp-foundation}: write \(d_v=u_v-v_v\),
\(q_v=u_v+v_v\), pin a reference variable \(h_v^{\rm var}=h_v\), impose
\(q_v+t_v-2h_v^{\rm var}=0\), and use local objective
\(2u_v+2v_v+h_v^{\rm var}+t_v\).  Put
\[
 L=\max_{e=(u,v)}|\lambda_e|h_v\leq BH,
 \qquad M=|E|,
\]
when \(0<|\lambda_e|\leq B\) and \(h_v\leq H\).

\begin{theorem}[Cut--resistance frontier]
\label{thm:frontier-cut-resistance}
There is a nonnegative, objective-exact point satisfying every root,
reference, and cap row exactly, erasing all output differences, and obeying
\begin{equation}
 \norm{Ax-b}_2^2\leq\frac{ML^2}{N^2}
 \leq\frac{MB^2H^2}{N^2}.
 \label{eq:frontier-cut-residual}
\end{equation}
There is also a fully sign-flipped point with four times this squared-residual
bound.  If the two unit anchors are the only nonzero RHS entries, so
\(\norm b_2=\sqrt2\), any relative-residual contract at threshold \(\eta\)
that rules out the erased point requires
\begin{equation}
 MB^2H^2>2\eta^2N^2.
 \label{eq:frontier-cut-product}
\end{equation}
If each edge label contains at most \(k\) raw signs, the erased residual in
\eqref{eq:frontier-cut-residual} acquires a factor \(k^2\), and soundness
requires \(Mk^2B^2H^2=\Omega(N^2)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For bit \(i\), let \(E_i\) be the edges whose label changes under its flip.
Cycle consistency makes every cycle meet \(E_i\) evenly, so signed-graph
switching identifies \(E_i\) with a cut separating the root from every
output \cite{harary1953-balance-signed-graph,
zaslavsky2013-matrices-signed-simple-graphs}.  One-bit locality makes the
\(N\) cuts edge-disjoint.

Give edge \(e=(u,v)\) conductance
\(c_e=(\lambda_eh_v)^2\leq L^2\), and wire the outputs together.  For any
unit root-to-output flow \(f\), one unit crosses every cut.  Weighted
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \sum_{e\in E_i}\frac{f_e^2}{c_e}\geq\frac1{C_i},
 \qquad C_i=\sum_{e\in E_i}c_e.
\]
Because the cuts are disjoint, Thomson's principle and a second
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality give the weighted Nash--Williams bound
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal R_{r,S}\geq\sum_i\frac1{C_i}
 \geq\frac{N^2}{\sum_iC_i}
 \geq\frac{N^2}{ML^2}.
 \label{eq:frontier-nash-williams}
\end{equation}
This proof is included to expose the oracle-specific role of disjoint input
cuts; the resistance inequality itself is classical
\cite{nashwilliams1959-random-walk-electric-currents}.

Let \(w\in[0,1]^V\) be the harmonic voltage with \(w_r=1\) and \(w_s=0\)
on every output.  Dirichlet's principle and
\eqref{eq:frontier-nash-williams} give energy at most \(ML^2/N^2\).  Set
\[
 d_v=\tau_vh_vw_v,\quad q_v=h_v^{\rm var}=t_v=h_v,
 \quad u_v=(h_v+d_v)/2,\quad v_v=(h_v-d_v)/2.
\]
The maximum principle proves nonnegativity.  Since every $\lambda_e$ is
nonzero and the graph is connected, every feasible point has $|d_v|=h_v$,
hence $q_v=u_v+v_v\ge|d_v|=h_v$; the local objective reduces to $q_v+3h_v$,
so this point, with $q_v=h_v$, is objective-exact.  All nonpropagation rows
are exact, while edge \(e\) has residual
\(\lambda_e\tau_vh_v(w_v-w_u)\), whose squared sum is the Dirichlet energy.
Every output has \(d_s=0\).
Replacing the output boundary value zero by \(-1\) doubles every voltage
difference and proves the sign-flipped statement.

For \(k\)-local labels, an edge lies in at most \(k\) bit cuts.  Hence
\(\mathcal R_{r,S}\geq k^{-1}\sum_iC_i^{-1}\), while
\(\sum_iC_i\leq kML^2\).  This gives
\(\mathcal R_{r,S}\geq N^2/(k^2ML^2)\) and the stated loss.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-cut-resistance} makes the gain--plateau family
optimal throughout its cycle-consistent signed-graph class: linear size and
bounded rows force \(H=\Omega(\sqrt N)\).  The two other rows of
Table~\ref{tab:frontier-currencies} attain the remaining endpoints.  An
adaptive trajectory does not repair the pointwise output contract, because
the harmonic witness exists at every requested stage.  Conversely, it gives
no unconditional fresh per-iteration lower bound: after all \(N\) fixed
signs are cached, later raw-query cost may be zero under
Definition~\ref{def:amortized-accounting}.

The same resistance mechanism extends to genuine multi-variable rows.  Give
wire \(v\) public height \(h_v\in(0,H]\) and intended value
\(h_v\chi_{J_v}(\varsigma)\), where
\(\chi_J=\prod_{i\in J}\varsigma_i\).  Let each of \(m\) homogeneous rows
have arity at most \(q\):
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{v\in V_a}\alpha_{av}\chi_{I_{av}}(\varsigma)d_v=0,
 \qquad |\alpha_{av}|\leq A,\qquad |I_{av}|\leq k.
 \label{eq:frontier-walsh-row}
\end{equation}
Roots have \(J_v=\varnothing\), outputs have \(J_v=[N]\), and every row is
valid on the intended wires for every input.  The comparison graph in the
proof may be assumed to connect the anchored roots to the outputs: otherwise
the output character is unconstrained and can be erased with zero propagation
residual.
For the objective-exact conclusion, assume also that the intended
full-height assignment is an optimum of the capped LP.  A sufficient
magnitude-floor hypothesis is that every exact feasible assignment obeys
\(|d_v|\geq h_v\) for every wire, since nonnegativity then forces
\(q_v\geq h_v\).

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-arity Walsh-row frontier]
\label{thm:frontier-walsh}
The capped-pair embedding has a nonnegative, objective-exact point that
satisfies anchors, references, and caps, erases every output, and has
\begin{equation}
 \norm r_2^2\leq
 \frac{4k^2q^3mA^2H^2}{N^2}.
 \label{eq:frontier-walsh-residual}
\end{equation}
Consequently fixed \(k,q\) and constant residual soundness require
\(mA^2H^2=\Omega(N^2)\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitution of the intended wires into row \(a\) gives
\(\sum_v\beta_{av}\chi_{K_{av}}=0\), where
\(\beta_{av}=\alpha_{av}h_v\) and
\(K_{av}=I_{av}\mathbin\triangle J_v\).  Independence of Walsh characters
forces \(\sum_{v\in G_{a,K}}\beta_{av}=0\) within each equal-character
group.  Choose one pivot per group and join it to the other group members by
comparison edges of conductance \(q^2\beta_{av}^2\).  There are at most
\(qm\) edges, each with conductance at most \(q^2A^2H^2\).  For arbitrary
interpolation scalars \(w_v\), a character group with pivot \(p\) has
residual
\[
 \sum_{v\in G}\beta_{av}w_v
 =\sum_{v\in G\setminus\{p\}}\beta_{av}(w_v-w_p).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz first over a group's at most \(q\) terms and then over a
row's at most \(q\) groups gives
\[
 \norm r_2^2\leq
 \sum_ec_e(w_{h(e)}-w_{t(e)})^2.
\]

Endpoints of a comparison edge satisfy
\(J_u\triangle J_v=I_{au}\triangle I_{av}\), so that edge crosses at most
\(2k\) of the vertex cuts \(\{v:i\in J_v\}\).  The bounded-overlap form of
the Nash--Williams calculation already proved above gives
\[
 \mathcal R_{\rm eff}
 \geq\frac{N^2}{4k^2q^3mA^2H^2}.
\]
Take the harmonic interpolation equal to one at the roots and zero at the
outputs.  Its energy gives \eqref{eq:frontier-walsh-residual}; the maximum
principle and the capped-pair construction used in the preceding proof give
nonnegativity and exact auxiliary rows.  The obstructing point has
\(q_v=h_v\) for every wire and therefore has the same local objective as the
intended full-height assignment; the explicit capped-optimum hypothesis gives
the exact optimal objective.
\end{proof}

Exact local XOR hulls have complementary, concrete failures.  If a set
\(U\) of unfixed variables meets every parity check in zero or at least two
positions, then setting \(x_j=1/2\) on \(U\) and retaining a satisfying
Boolean codeword elsewhere lies in every local parity polytope.  For a check
meeting \(U\) in \(r\geq2\) variables, it is the uniform average of the
\(2^{r-1}\) assignments with the required residual parity, proving the
claim.  This stopping-set pseudocodeword can make an output exactly unbiased.
If checks instead peel along a dependency chain of length \(D\), linearly
interpolating the gauged wire error from zero to one violates one opposing
facet at each gate by \(1/D\), for total Euclidean violation
\(1/\sqrt D\).  Finally, every satisfying truth-table assignment is a vertex
of its exact gate hull, so some standard-form inequality slacks vanish; a
Boolean circuit forced by its inputs is not strictly primal feasible.
Thickness restores positive slacks only by admitting fractional drift.  These
facts explain the LP-decoding analogy but are not a universal lower bound for
all inequality extended formulations.

\subsection{Multiplier conservation}
\label{sec:frontier-multiplier}

Residual robustness constrains primal witnesses.  Complete central and Newton
outputs face a second conservation law.  In a signed-copy graph write
\(G=(V\cup T,E)\), vertices in \(V\) carry variables with
\(d_v=x_v^+-x_v^-\), \(q_v=x_v^++x_v^-\), and vertices in \(T\) are fixed
terminals used for anchor rows.  A variable--variable edge \(e=(u,v)\)
represents \(d_v-a_ed_u=0\).  A terminal edge is oriented from its terminal
to its variable endpoint, with the fixed terminal term moved to the RHS.
Let \(B_a\) and \(B\) be respectively the signed and ordinary oriented
incidence matrices with all terminal columns omitted.  For
\(S\subseteq V\), its boundary includes edges to \(V\setminus S\) and all
terminal edges incident to \(S\).  Each normalized edge equality has
multiplier \(y_e\); an actual LP row may be multiplied by a public nonzero
scale \(r_e\ne0\), in which case its invariant row force is
\(f_e=r_ey_e\).
Assume exact primal--dual centrality, \(d_v\ne0\), a pair-symmetric objective,
and that all nongraph equality columns are pair-symmetric.

\begin{theorem}[Gauge flow and isoperimetric law]
\label{thm:frontier-gauge-flow}
Let \(\tau_v=\operatorname{sgn}(d_v)\).  After the edge gauge
\(z_e=\tau_{h(e)}y_e\), signed stationarity is the positive-divergence law
\begin{equation}
 B^\top z=g,
 \qquad g_v=\frac{2\mu|d_v|}{q_v^2-d_v^2}>0.
 \label{eq:frontier-gauge-flow}
\end{equation}
For every vertex set \(S\) with nonempty boundary,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{e\in\partial S}|y_e|\geq G_S,
 \quad
 \norm{y_{\partial S}}_\infty\geq\frac{G_S}{|\partial S|},
 \quad
 \norm{y_{\partial S}}_2\geq\frac{G_S}{\sqrt{|\partial S|}},
 \quad G_S=\sum_{v\in S}g_v.
 \label{eq:frontier-cut-flow}
\end{equation}
If \(S\ne\varnothing\) but \(\partial S=\varnothing\), no such positive
central point exists.
If \(|d_v|\geq a>0\) and \(q_v\leq Q\) on \(S\), then
\begin{equation}
 \norm{y_{\partial S}}_\infty
 \geq\frac{2\mu a}{Q^2-a^2}\frac{|S|}{|\partial S|},
 \qquad
 \norm{y_{\partial S}}_2
 \geq\frac{2\mu a}{Q^2-a^2}\frac{|S|}{\sqrt{|\partial S|}}.
 \label{eq:frontier-isoperimetric}
\end{equation}
For a row written as \(r_e(d_v-a_ed_u)=0\), all statements hold with the
row force \(f_e=r_ey_e\); hence \(|r_e|\leq B_{\rm row}\) charges
\(B_{\rm row}\norm y_\infty\), not the basis-dependent multiplier alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Subtract pair stationarity equations:
\[
 2(B_a^\top y)_v+s_v^+-s_v^-=0.
\]
Since \(x_v^\pm=(q_v\pm d_v)/2\) and
\(s_v^\pm=\mu/x_v^\pm\), this becomes
\((B_a^\top y)_v=2\mu d_v/(q_v^2-d_v^2)\).  Feasibility implies
\(a_e=\tau_u\tau_v\) on variable--variable edges; terminal rows have the
same single variable coefficient in \(B_a\) and \(B\).  Switching the signed
incidence matrix therefore gives
\eqref{eq:frontier-gauge-flow}.  Summing over \(S\) cancels internal edges;
the triangle inequality and Cauchy--Schwarz give
\eqref{eq:frontier-cut-flow}.  Positivity gives \(q_v>|d_v|\), and the
margin hypotheses lower-bound every \(g_v\) by
\(2\mu a/(Q^2-a^2)\).  A diagonal row scaling enters stationarity only as
\(r_ey_e\), proving the final statement.
\end{proof}

Pair symmetry is not needed for a two-input comparison if the
pair-antisymmetric source is the same numerical vector in the original pair
coordinates.  Let two exact central points, denoted \(+\) and \(-\), have
row forces \(f_e^\pm=r_ey_e^\pm\), and suppose their subtracted stationarity
equations are
\[
 (B_{a^\pm}^\top f^\pm)_v
 =h_v+\frac{2\mu_\pm d_v^\pm}
              {(q_v^\pm)^2-(d_v^\pm)^2}.
\]
If \(d_v^+>0>d_v^-\) on \(S\), switching the two inputs, adding their
equations, and summing over \(S\) cancels both the common source and every
internal edge.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{e\in\partial S}(|f_e^+|+|f_e^-|)
 \geq\sum_{v\in S}(g_v^++g_v^-),
 \qquad
 g_v^\pm=\frac{2\mu_\pm|d_v^\pm|}
 {(q_v^\pm)^2-(d_v^\pm)^2}.
 \label{eq:frontier-two-input}
\end{equation}
If \(\mu_\pm\geq\mu_0\), \(|d_v^\pm|\geq a\), \(q_v^\pm\leq Q\), and
\(|r_e|\leq B_{\rm row}\), this gives
\begin{equation}
 \max_{p\in\{+,-\}}\norm{y_{\partial S}^p}_\infty
 \geq\frac{2\mu_0a}{Q^2-a^2}
       \frac{|S|}{B_{\rm row}|\partial S|}.
 \label{eq:frontier-two-input-norm}
\end{equation}
Thus a public asymmetric objective can cancel one parity orientation, but not
both behind a small interface.  Coefficients of another public asymmetric
constraint do not suffice unless their multiplier-mediated contribution is
also the same vector \(h\) on both inputs.

The graph is not essential to the global work.  Let \(Cd=b_a\) be an
arbitrary pair-antisymmetric equality block, with columns \(C,-C\), and let
all other local data be pair-symmetric.

\begin{theorem}[Central and Newton work identities]
\label{thm:frontier-work}
At an exact central point,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{b_a^\top y_a
 =\sum_v\frac{2\mu d_v^2}{q_v^2-d_v^2}\geq0.}
 \label{eq:frontier-central-work}
\end{equation}
The work is strictly positive exactly when \(d\ne0\).  At a pair-symmetric
Newton start with pair-symmetric dual and complementarity right-hand sides,
pair-symmetric other equality columns,
\(x_v^{0,+}=x_v^{0,-}=\xi_v\), and
\(s_v^{0,+}=s_v^{0,-}=\zeta_v\), the antisymmetric block satisfies
\begin{equation}
 C_\varsigma\Delta d=r_a,
 \qquad C_\varsigma^\top\alpha=D\Delta d,
 \qquad D=\operatorname{Diag}\!\left(\frac{\zeta_v}{2\xi_v}\right),
 \quad
 \boxed{r_a^\top\alpha=\Delta d^\top D\Delta d.}
 \label{eq:frontier-newton-work}
\end{equation}
Both pairings are invariant under
\((C,b_a,y_a)\mapsto(UC,Ub_a,U^{-\top}y_a)\), and likewise for
\((C_\varsigma,r_a,\alpha)\).  If \(D\succeq\rho I\),
\(|\Delta d_v|\geq a\) on \(L\) coordinates, and \(\norm{r_a}_1\leq R\),
then \(\norm\alpha_\infty\geq\rho a^2L/R\).
Likewise, if \(|d_v|\geq a\) and \(q_v\leq Q\) on \(L\) coordinates,
then
\begin{equation}
 \norm{y_a}_\infty\geq
 \frac{2\mu a^2L}{(Q^2-a^2)\norm{b_a}_1},
 \qquad
 \norm{y_a}_2\geq
 \frac{2\mu a^2L}{(Q^2-a^2)\norm{b_a}_2}.
 \label{eq:frontier-central-work-norms}
\end{equation}
A zero denominator with nonzero work is an impossibility statement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pair subtraction gives
\(C^\top y_a=2\mu d/(q^2-d^2)\) coordinatewise.  Its inner product with
\(d\), followed by \(Cd=b_a\), proves
\eqref{eq:frontier-central-work}.  At the Newton start, subtracting the dual
and complementarity equations and eliminating the slack difference gives
\(C_\varsigma^\top\alpha=D\Delta d\); primal antisymmetric feasibility gives
\(C_\varsigma\Delta d=r_a\).  Taking inner products proves
\eqref{eq:frontier-newton-work}, and H\"older gives the norm bounds.  The
contragredient transformations preserve both scalar pairings.
\end{proof}

For the bounded secant start of Section~\ref{sec:lp-dual-homogeneity},
\(D=(2/9)I\), \(\Delta d_v=\tau_v\), and the root antisymmetric RHS is a
unit vector.  Equation~\eqref{eq:frontier-newton-work} therefore gives the
root multiplier \(2P/9\).  Scaling all dual data by \(\lambda\) changes this
to \(2\lambda P/9\); bounded complete directions require
\(\lambda=O(1/P)\), exactly the inverse dual-scale price paid by
Theorem~\ref{thm:lp-dual-homogeneous-full}.  Section~\ref{sec:lp-beyond-pairs}
states the corresponding unrestricted Newton virtual-work identity.  We use
the paired forms here because they expose gadget resources;
\cref{sec:structural-tools} will place them in the general
involution/symmetry framework.

The cut laws also hold directly for Newton directions.  Under a
pair-symmetric start, pair-symmetric Newton right-hand sides, and homogeneous
signed-copy primal equations, switching by
\(\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta d_v)\) gives
\begin{equation}
 B^\top z=g^{\rm N},
 \qquad g_v^{\rm N}=\frac{\zeta_v}{2\xi_v}|\Delta d_v|.
 \label{eq:frontier-newton-flow}
\end{equation}
The proof is the Newton half of Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-work} followed by
the cut summation in Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-gauge-flow}.  In particular,
if \(\zeta_v/\xi_v\geq r\) and \(|\Delta d_v|\geq a\) on \(S\), then
\[
 \norm{\Delta y_{\partial S}}_\infty
 \geq\frac{ra}{2}\frac{|S|}{|\partial S|}.
\]

Now assume terminal values, labels, and terminal Newton residuals are public
and fixed, and fix a base input for which it and all single-flip neighbors have exact
central points with nonzero differences.  Let \(O\) be a fixed region of
\(K\) output vertices whose orientation flips under every one-bit change,
and define
\[
 S_j=\{v:\operatorname{sgn}d_v(\varsigma^{(j)})
             =-\operatorname{sgn}d_v(\varsigma)\}.
\]
Let \(M_j\) count the signed rows changing under flip \(j\), and put
\(M_{\rm dep}=\sum_jM_j\).  Feasibility on an internal edge gives
\(a_e=\operatorname{sgn}(d_u)\operatorname{sgn}(d_v)\), so its label changes
exactly when one endpoint is in \(S_j\).  Public terminal signs act as fixed
outside vertices, so the same boundary accounting includes terminal edges.
Hence
\(|\partial S_j|\leq M_j\), with equality when rows rather than expanded
coefficient positions are counted, and \(O\subseteq S_j\).  The base
multiplier must satisfy all \(N\) cut inequalities simultaneously.  Assume
explicitly that the base central point has
\(|d_v|\geq a=\Omega(1)\) and \(q_v\leq Q=O(1)\) on \(O\).  Applying this
central margin only on \(O\) and summing over \(j\) proves
\begin{equation}
 M_{\rm dep}B_{\rm row}\norm{y}_\infty
 \geq\frac{2\mu a}{Q^2-a^2}KN.
 \label{eq:frontier-central-incidence}
\end{equation}
For Newton directions, suppose instead that the base and every single-flip
system satisfy the hypotheses of \eqref{eq:frontier-newton-flow}, define
\(S_j\) from the signs of \(\Delta d\), and assume that every output
direction flips with \(|\Delta d_v|\geq a=\Omega(1)\) on \(O\).  The same
argument gives
\begin{equation}
 M_{\rm dep}B_{\rm row}\norm{\Delta y}_\infty
 \geq\rho aKN,
 \qquad \rho=\min_{v\in O}\frac{s_v^0}{2x_v^0}.
 \label{eq:frontier-newton-incidence}
\end{equation}
Thus \(M_{\rm dep}=\Theta(N)\), \(K=\Theta(N)\), bounded row scale, and
bounded multipliers, together with the explicit constant margin
\(a=\Omega(1)\) and central range \(Q=O(1)\), force
\(\mu=O(1/N)\) in the central system and \(\rho=O(1/N)\) in the Newton
system.  Equation
\eqref{eq:frontier-newton-incidence} is the general conservation framework
behind the beyond-pair design obstruction in Section~\ref{sec:lp-beyond-pairs}.
It says that every signed-copy gadget pays somewhere: in incidences, row
scale, multiplier height, or inverse dual scale.  Branching and redundant
copy rows merely move the payment into the relevant boundary count.

Pair-asymmetric local data do not provide a cheap universal cancellation.
Suppose a prescribed direct source \(h_v(\varsigma)\) changes central
stationarity to
\[
 (B_a^\top f)_v=
 \frac{2\mu d_v}{q_v^2-d_v^2}+h_v(\varsigma),
 \qquad f_e=r_ey_e.
\]
On a fixed output region \(O\), assume
\(\operatorname{sgn}(d_v)=\kappa_vp(\varsigma)\),
\(|d_v|\geq a\), and \(q_v\leq Q\) for every input.  With the uniform
Fourier convention
\(\widehat h_v([N])=2^{-N}\sum_\varsigma p(\varsigma)h_v(\varsigma)\), put
\(\varepsilon_O=K^{-1}\sum_{v\in O}|\widehat h_v([N])|\).

\begin{theorem}[Fourier obstruction to direct cancellation]
\label{thm:frontier-fourier-source}
For some input \(\varsigma^*\),
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{e\in\partial O}|r_ey_e(\varsigma^*)|
 \geq K\left(\frac{2\mu a}{Q^2-a^2}-\varepsilon_O\right).
 \label{eq:frontier-fourier-source}
\end{equation}
In particular, bounded-bit juntas that depend on strictly fewer than \(N\)
input bits, and every source of Fourier degree less than \(N\), have zero
full-parity coefficient and cannot cancel the demand on all inputs.  The
bound is tight: a direct source
\(h_v=-\kappa_vp(\varsigma)g_0\) cancels a constant demand \(g_0\) exactly.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After switching, sum the stationarity equation over \(O\).  The direct
contribution is
\(H(\varsigma)=\sum_{v\in O}\kappa_vp(\varsigma)h_v(\varsigma)\), whose
input average is at least \(-K\varepsilon_O\).  Some \(\varsigma^*\) has
\(H(\varsigma^*)\) at least this average.  The positive central demand is at
least \(2\mu aK/(Q^2-a^2)\); internal edge forces cancel, and the triangle
inequality proves \eqref{eq:frontier-fourier-source}.
\end{proof}

There is an exact Newton analogue.  At a pair-symmetric start put
\(x_v^{0,\pm}=\xi_v\), \(s_v^{0,\pm}=\zeta_v\), and allow asymmetric dual
and complementarity right-hand sides.  Pair subtraction gives
\[
 (B_a^\top\Delta f)_v
 =\frac{\zeta_v}{2\xi_v}\Delta d_v+h_v^{\rm N}(\varsigma),
 \qquad
 h_v^{\rm N}=\frac{r_{d,v}^+-r_{d,v}^-}{2}
 -\frac{r_{c,v}^+-r_{c,v}^-}{2\xi_v}.
\]
If \(\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta d_v)=\kappa_vp(\varsigma)\),
\(|\Delta d_v|\geq a\), and \(\zeta_v/(2\xi_v)\geq\rho\) on \(O\), then
the same proof gives some input \(\varsigma^*\) with
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{e\in\partial O}|r_e\Delta y_e(\varsigma^*)|
 \geq K(\rho a-\varepsilon_O^{\rm N}),
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_O^{\rm N}=K^{-1}\sum_{v\in O}
 |\widehat h_v^{\rm N}([N])|.
 \label{eq:frontier-newton-fourier}
\end{equation}
This Fourier coefficient must be checked after division by the start
coordinates: input dependence in \(1/\xi_v\) can manufacture full parity
from otherwise local raw data.  The word \emph{direct} also matters.
An unknown multiplier of another antisymmetric constraint may depend on all
input bits even when that row is local, so Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-fourier-source}
does not silently assign it low Fourier degree.

Switching and incidence-flow conservation are classical
\cite{harary1953-balance-signed-graph,zaslavsky2013-matrices-signed-simple-graphs};
the work identities are standard KKT virtual-work and Schur-energy
specializations.  Electrical flows on interior-point central paths are also
established algorithmic tools \cite{madry2013-navigating-central-path}.
The contribution claimed here is only the design-constraint conjunction:
the local input cuts, parity-sized output region, multiplier conservation,
row scaling, and incidence accounting must all be met at once.  Effective
resistance has a separate established role in quantum graph algorithms
\cite{belovs2012-span-programs-st-connectivity,
jarret2018-quantum-connectivity,apers2022-quantum-speedup-for-graph-sparsification};
those works do not imply these LP residual or multiplier frontiers.

\subsection{Relative value and shortcut remnants}
\label{sec:frontier-remnants}

The path family also gives a clean value-output statement.  Put
\(p_0=1\) and \(p_i=\prod_{k=1}^i\varsigma_k\).  On one unit signed path
minimize
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{N+1}\sum_{i=0}^N(u_i+v_i)
 +\frac14(u_N-v_N)
 \label{eq:frontier-relative-value-lp}
\end{equation}
subject to \(d_0=1\) and
\(d_i-\varsigma_id_{i-1}=0\).

\begin{theorem}[Relative-value gap]
\label{thm:frontier-relative-value}
In raw sparse coefficient access, estimating the optimum of
\eqref{eq:frontier-relative-value-lp} to target-relative error
\(\epsilon<1/4\) requires \(\Omega(N)\) queries.  The LP has a finite
attained optimum, is strictly primal--dual feasible, and has a unique
nondegenerate strictly complementary optimum.  Its objective norm is bounded
by an absolute constant, and its reduced primal log-barrier Hessian has
condition one along the entire central path.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exact feasibility fixes \(d_i=p_i\) and \(q_i=u_i+v_i\geq1\).  On this
affine space the objective is
\((N+1)^{-1}\sum_iq_i+p_N/4\), so the unique optimum has every \(q_i=1\)
and
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{OPT}=1+\frac14p(\varsigma)
 \in\left\{\frac34,\frac54\right\}.
 \label{eq:frontier-relative-value-gap}
\end{equation}
The two target-relative error intervals are disjoint for every
\(\epsilon<1/4\); hence such value estimation requires \(\Omega(N)\) raw
queries by the parity lower bound.  Even an exactly feasible point with objective at most
\(3\operatorname{OPT}/2\) distinguishes the cases by a threshold between
\(9/8\) and \(5/4\).  Every sparse coefficient query is simulated by one
raw sign query.  Also
\(\norm c_2^2=2/(N+1)+1/8=O(1)\).

The equality matrix is full row rank, and its public orthonormal null basis
consists of \((e_{u_i}+e_{v_i})/\sqrt2\).  Taking every \(q_i=2\) proves
strict primal feasibility.  Taking \(s_{u_i}=s_{v_i}=1/(N+1)\) gives a
positive slack for which \(c-s\) is the endpoint difference term; it is
orthogonal to the nullspace and hence lies in \(\operatorname{range}(A^\top)\),
proving strict dual feasibility.  At the optimum, one positive coordinate
from each pair gives a triangular basis, and every opposite coordinate has
positive reduced cost \(2/(N+1)\).  This proves nondegeneracy and strict
complementarity.

Finally, on the feasible slice the barrier objective is
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^N\left[\frac{q_i}{N+1}
 -\mu\log\frac{q_i^2-1}{4}\right]+\frac14p_N.
\]
Every central coordinate solves the same scalar equation
\((N+1)^{-1}=2\mu q/(q^2-1)\), so the Hessian in the displayed orthonormal
null basis is a positive scalar multiple of the identity.
\end{proof}

This is a normalization-strengthened value reduction, not a generic LP query
lower bound.

One tempting repair is a long two-coordinate shortcut.  If
\(0\leq i<j\leq N\) and a homogeneous row
\(a(\varsigma)d_i+b(\varsigma)d_j=0\) is valid on every exact signed path,
with both \(a(\varsigma)\) and \(b(\varsigma)\) nonzero for every input
covered by the conclusion,
then substitution gives
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{a(\varsigma)}{b(\varsigma)}
 =\frac{p_j}{p_i}=\prod_{k=i+1}^j\varsigma_k.
 \label{eq:frontier-shortcut-parity}
\end{equation}
Thus the row itself carries interval parity, invariant under common row
rescaling, and generally costs \(\Omega(j-i)\) raw queries to simulate.
The older replicated-path and expander calculations are otherwise superseded
by the mixture, resistance, and Walsh-row theorems above.

\subsection{Boundary of the conclusions}
\label{sec:frontier-synthesis}

The parallel-path and gain--plateau constructions of \cref{sec:lp-hardness} are optimal in
their stated bounded-height and linear-size gadget classes.  The all-unit
family's \(\Theta(N^{-1/2})\) residual threshold in
Theorem~\ref{thm:lp-unit-threshold} is the matching unamplified boundary, and
dual homogeneity attains the \(\rho=\Theta(1/N)\) escape demanded by
\eqref{eq:frontier-newton-incidence}.  None of these conclusions says that
parity needs a quadratic-size LP, that every bounded-range LP fails, or that
an adaptive QIPM pays a fresh linear query cost at every iteration.

A bounded-range, linear-incidence construction must leave at least one
premise used here.  The recorded escape routes are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item use a nonlinear or genuinely non-mixture-stable output promise and
       prove its robustness directly;
 \item require only central or Newton outputs and permit the neighboring
       central coordinates to leave every fixed Kantorovich neighborhood;
 \item use nonlocal input coefficients and charge their raw-query
       implementation honestly;
 \item abandon the common objective or common optimum, so neighbor mixtures
       need not be objective-exact; or
 \item impose integrality or another nonconvex promise, leaving linear
       programming.
\end{enumerate}
The second route alone is not automatic: equations
\eqref{eq:frontier-central-dichotomy}--\eqref{eq:frontier-central-uniform}
show exactly what central instability it must supply.

Two problems remain open within the present scope.  First, can an arbitrary
sparse bounded-range equality LP with a nonlinear normalized-state decoder
achieve a fixed global residual tube without nonlocal coefficient access?
Second, can the bounded-scale complete-direction problem in
Open Problem~\ref{open:lp-bounded-full-kkt} be solved by a nonorthogonal
mixed-mode gadget while charging all input-dependent sources and recovery
maps?  Together with the orthogonal-copy cut law in
\cref{sec:lp-beyond-pairs}, the theorems here rule out the natural
convex-mixture-stable, signed-copy, constant-arity Walsh, permutation,
rotation, and flat-copy routes; they do not settle either question.
