\section{Discussion and Open Problems}
\label{sec:discussion}

The motivating question was whether QIPMs beat classical IPMs on sparse
problems.  The results support a more useful answer than a single yes or no.
They separate three layers that are often combined in informal complexity
claims: the geometry of the central path, the access model for the resulting
linear-algebra objects, and the interface by which one iteration produces the
next.  Each layer has its own obstruction and its own escape routes.

\subsection{What the program establishes}
\label{sec:discussion-verdict}

\paragraph{Geometry.}
For barrier-Newton methods, part of the condition growth is intrinsic to the
optimization problem.  \Cref{thm:sublevel-chord-law} lower-bounds the reduced
barrier Hessian at a fixed objective gap for every self-concordant barrier,
and \cref{thm:canonical-achievability} shows that log and log-det barriers
attain the resulting order up to instance constants.
\Cref{thm:width-spectrum-rigidity} refines the two
extreme eigenvalues into a geometric description of the full spectrum.  This
settles the barrier-redesign question in the stated minimax sense: changing
the barrier cannot generally eliminate a sublevel-chord obstruction.

That conclusion is not the folklore claim that every central path has
\(\kappa=\Theta(\mu^{-2})\).  The rate depends on sublevel geometry.  A
positive-dimensional optimal face gives inverse-square gap growth, a curved
unique optimum can give inverse-linear growth, a unique LP vertex can remain
bounded, and attained singularity-degree behavior can give fractional laws
\cref{cor:conditioning-regimes,cor:fractional-conditioning-laws}.  Nor does a
large condition number alone determine solver cost.
\Cref{prop:two-cluster-hessian,prop:two-cluster-cg} show that classical Krylov
convergence can be
polylogarithmic in an arbitrarily large inter-cluster ratio.  The path's own
right-hand side may avoid the weak modes as well
\cref{prop:newton-rhs-alignment,cor:top-window-solve}.

\paragraph{Access models.}
Quantum cost depends on how the matrix is supplied, not only on its spectrum.
The clustered comparison in \cref{thm:quantum-cluster-obstruction} separates
plain normalized block-encoding access from access to a factor
\(H=G^\dagger G\).  Plain access retains the known worst-case
\(\Thetatilde(\kappa)\) query cost even on two-point spectra in sufficiently
large dimension.  Norm-tight factor access has a square-root polynomial route, but only
an overlap-qualified end-to-end solve; a generic square-root factor-access
QLS would contradict the same plain-access hard instance because its square
root factor is easy to query.  The exact coupling and spectral-dimension laws
in \cref{thm:dls-coupling,thm:spectral-dimension-laws} further show that
right-hand-side mass determines whether truncation or filtering can improve
on \(\kappa\).

Preconditioning obeys the same discipline.  A whitening factor may exchange
condition for subnormalization; a congruence may exchange transformed
condition for physical recovery sensitivity; and an input-dependent perfect
preconditioner may move the hard information into setup or RHS preparation
\cref{thm:prec-whitening-frontier,thm:prec-congruence-frontier,thm:prec-factor-rhs-hardness,thm:prec-parity-ledger}.
These are product and
ledger statements.  They do not say that preconditioning is useless.  They
say that a favorable transformed condition is not, by itself, a complexity
bound.

\paragraph{Interfaces.}
The strongest recurring obstruction is often loading or recovery rather than
inversion.  The prefix LP has a condition-one reduced Hessian along its path,
yet preparing a robust original-primal direction requires linear raw queries
\cref{thm:lp-prefix-capstone}.  The SDP families distinguish an intrinsic
value gap \cref{thm:sdp-intrinsic}, parity-hard primal and complete-KKT
solution states \cref{thm:sdp-newton-state}, and a public Schur solve with an
original-coordinate loading frontier
\cref{thm:sdp-schur,thm:sdp-accuracy-frontier}.
The canonical KKT block encoding itself is exact at one raw query per call;
the linear first-synthesis bound instead applies to the tangent projector
\cref{sec:sdp-intrinsic-access}.  A normalized state is also not a reusable
coordinate oracle: producing the changing diagonal for the next Newton system
obeys the state-to-diagonal and state-to-next-solve products in
\cref{thm:prec-state-diagonal,thm:prec-state-next-solve}.

The condensation results make the interface visible rather than implicit.
Winner mass controls both the reduced-Hessian condition and the trace distance
from a public loser state
\cref{thm:cond-mass-conditioning,thm:cond-visibility}.  The bounded-incidence
holonomy realization then has matching query bounds for value and compact
winner output \cref{thm:cond-unique-or,thm:wta-search-upper}, while
central-state preparation changes across the condensation phases
\cref{sec:wta-central-state,eq:wta-preparation-phases}.  The verified-sample
decoder
turns trace-close mixed output into a public, one-sided decision procedure,
and parity collapse gives the intervening accuracy curve
\cref{thm:sc-m1,thm:sc-small-success,cor:sc-s2}.  The lower bound is tied to a
specified output accuracy; a scalar objective gap alone would not supply it.

Together these results give a three-layer verdict.  Conditioning is governed
by sublevel geometry but may be algorithmically benign when its spectrum or
right-hand side is structured.  Quantum performance is governed by the
operator and state access actually supplied.  End-to-end performance is
governed by the loading, recovery, update, certification, and output
interfaces that connect iterations.  Sparsity can help at each layer, but no
single notion of sparsity controls all three.

\subsection{What the program does not establish}
\label{sec:discussion-nonclaims}

The paper proves neither an unconditional sparse-QIPM advantage nor an
unconditional sparse-QIPM lower bound.  The tall-row result of Apers--Gribling
remains the clearest positive quantum--classical row-query separation in the
literature considered here
\cite{apers2026-quantum-speedups-linear-programming-interior}; our results do
not weaken it.  Conversely, none of the parity or holonomy families rules out
a method with a different oracle, a weaker but still useful output, a
condition-number-free optimization primitive, or a structure-aware
preconditioner outside the stated class.

The barrier theorem is about the reduced Hessian in an orthonormal tangent
basis.  It is not automatically a theorem about an augmented KKT system,
primal--dual scaling, or a Hamiltonian central-path method.  The clustered QLS
theorem is access specific and includes dimension and overlap qualifications.
The LP and SDP loading theorems charge raw coefficient queries; a supplied
parity, prefix, tangent-projector, or preconditioned-product oracle is a
stronger input model.  The online direct sum requires fresh independent input,
whereas a fixed instance can be cached
\cref{thm:prec-online-direct-sum,sec:preconditioning-lazy}.  The physical
light-cone bound concerns locally placed data and output with bounded site
occupancy, not all-to-all query access \cref{thm:structural-light-cone}.

Likewise, the positive modules are components rather than a hidden complete
algorithm.  Projective reuse, as analyzed here, needs finite realized
variation and a fixed residual contract; active-range equilibration and face
repair need a certified tail and spectral gaps; compressed dual output needs a
genuinely smaller retained dimension and a charged coordinate compiler; and the block-angular
module needs a low-parameter projected barrier and persistent local
representation \cref{sec:positive-synthesis}.  These qualifications are not
technical decorations: the counterexamples in
\cref{sec:positive-lazy,sec:positive-face-repair,sec:positive-checkpoint-span}
fail precisely when one of them is removed.

The five referee rules introduced in \cref{sec:positive-modules} are therefore
the standard we propose for future positive claims:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
 \item Expand loading, normalization, RHS preparation, recovery, and every
 output compiler into the declared access model; a small spectral condition
 alone makes none of them free.
 \item State the data-dependent tail threshold, separation margin, and active
 singular gaps used by any strict-complementarity argument.
 \item Claim a dimensional benefit from compressed dual output only when the
 retained dimension is asymptotically smaller and the classical comparator is
 charged under the same oracle and output contract.
 \item Treat a coherent coordinate oracle as substantive output, especially
 when its values permit constant-overhead conversion to a solution state.
 \item Before claiming that a dual-only certificate contradicts a primal
 lower bound, give an explicit low-query reduction from that certificate to
 the hard primal output contract.
\end{enumerate}
To these five rules one should add the comparison discipline used throughout
the paper: match residual and precision contracts, charge setup and reuse on
both sides, and compare with the best classical method for the same structure.
A query advantage is not automatically a gate or time advantage, and a
comparison of two upper bounds is not a separation.

\subsection{Collected open problems}
\label{sec:discussion-open}

The following list collects every explicit open-problem environment in
\cref{sec:conditioning,sec:spectra,sec:state-conversion,sec:lp-hardness,sec:sdp-hardness}
and the additional frontiers left in the technical discussion.  They are
grouped by the layer at which new
information is needed.

\subsubsection{Geometry and conditioning}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=G\arabic*.]
 \item \textbf{Degenerate-vertex constants.}  Determine the limiting spectrum,
 not only boundedness, of the
 canonical reduced log Hessian at a unique degenerate LP vertex directly from
 active-set geometry \cref{open:degenerate-vertex-constants}.
 \item \textbf{Facial characterization of chord growth.}  Characterize the
 exact objective-sublevel chord growth \(D(g)\) of an
 SDP from facial data and decide when the singularity-degree exponent is
 attained by objective sublevel diameters
 \cref{open:facial-chord-growth}.
 \item Relate the blockwise relative stationarity and trace-residual
 neighborhood used by robust condensation to the algorithm-specific inexact
 Newton residual of \cref{def:inexact-newton-residual}, the unresolved
 condensation \(\eta\)-channel \cref{sec:condensation-discussion}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Quantum access and state conversion}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=A\arabic*.]
 \item \textbf{Exact subnormalization shift.}  Decide whether plain access to
 \(H\) permits a block encoding of \(I-H\) with unit subnormalization without
 paying the apparent uniform-amplification cost at the shifted spectral edge
 \cref{open:exact-subnormalization-shift}.  This is the promise-sensitive
 form of the normalization question of Orsucci and Dunjko
 \cite{orsucci2021-on-solving-classes-of-positive}.
 \item \textbf{General-inner composition.}  Prove or refute the
 \(\sqrt\delta\,Q(f)\sqrt{G/k}\) small-success composition lower bound for a
 general inner predicate \(f\), beyond the parity-collapse proof
 \cref{open:sc-general-composition}.
 \item Determine the minimax output radius and query curve when the public
 loser internal states differ by \(\eta_{\rm int}\), including the below-condensation
 regime \cref{sec:state-conversion-open}.
 \item Resolve the state-conversion sliver \(\delta=o(k/G)\), where a public
 uniform-label output may already satisfy part of the contract and winner
 mass alone does not determine the boundary
 \cref{sec:state-conversion-open}.
 \item \textbf{Intrinsically nonabelian word hardness.}  Prove a local-symbol
 query lower bound for a genuinely noncommuting word promise and transfer it
 to conjugacy-class output in the group-holonomy SDP, rather than restricting
 to a cyclic subgroup
 \cref{open:sdp-nonabelian}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Hard families and gadget frontiers}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=F\arabic*.]
 \item \textbf{Bounded-scale full-KKT parity amplifier.}  Construct or rule
 out a linear-size standard-form LP with bounded row and column degree,
 bounded coefficients with one-bit-local input dependence, public simply
 preparable Newton right-hand sides, primal and slack starts bounded above and
 below by positive constants, a coordinatewise bounded complete Newton
 direction, and a parity-hard primal component with constant mass in that
 complete direction state
 \cref{open:lp-bounded-full-kkt}.
 \item Determine whether an arbitrary sparse bounded-range equality LP can
 support a nonlinear normalized-state decoder with a fixed global residual
 tube without introducing nonlocal coefficient access
 \cref{sec:frontier-synthesis}.
 \item Determine whether a nonorthogonal mixed-mode gadget can solve the same
 bounded-scale complete-direction problem while charging every
 input-dependent source and recovery map
 \cref{sec:lp-beyond-pairs,sec:frontier-synthesis}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Positive algorithmic frontier}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=P\arabic*.]
 \item Exhibit one explicit family and access model satisfying all six
 requirements simultaneously---stable direct normalization and inverse,
 stable forcing and RHS preparation, finite projective variation or a
 low-parameter barrier, cheap static construction, useful compressed output,
 and a classical lower bound surviving randomized preprocessing---as required
 in \cref{sec:positive-synthesis}.
 \item Find a structured access model that supplies a constant-normalized
 quantum operator without at the same time supplying a bounded-leverage
 classical sampling distribution, and realize it on an explicit sparse LP
 family \cref{sec:positive-checkpoint-span}.
 \item Construct a projected block-angular barrier with parameter
 \(\Otilde(k+\poly(p))\) whose local center and derivatives are recomputable
 from one scenario block and the explicit border
 \cref{sec:positive-block-angular}.
\end{enumerate}

The list deliberately does not include directions already closed by a
frontier theorem.  For example, expanderizing a bounded-degree local-linear
parity gadget does not evade the mixture and resistance bounds, and ordinary
short-step residual control does not imply finite projective variation
\cref{sec:frontier-synthesis,sec:positive-lazy}.  A new proposal must leave a
proved premise, not merely relabel the same construction.

\subsection{Outlook}
\label{sec:discussion-outlook}

The most promising next result would be small and complete rather than broad
and schematic: one structured family, one explicit access model, one useful
output contract, a full path ledger, and a matched classical lower bound or
algorithmic comparison.  The six-desiderata problem above is deliberately
hard because each individual module already has a classical or quantum
counterweight.  Stable spectral geometry is not enough if RHS preparation is
hard; cheap coherent access is not enough if classical sampling sees the same
average; compressed output is not enough if the next iterate requires full
materialization.

There are also concrete nearer-term targets.  The exact-subnormalization
question would clarify whether the plain/factor separation in clustered QLS
is an artifact of the current construction or an access-model invariant.  A
general-inner small-success theorem would turn the state-conversion curve from
a parity-specific result into a reusable composition principle.  A genuinely
nonabelian word bound would separate matrix-valued holonomy from its abelian
restriction.  A projected block-angular barrier would connect the border
module---whose \(\sqrt N\) block-count exponent is optimal in the block-oracle
model---to an end-to-end QIPM candidate.

The central methodological lesson is that a future sparse-QIPM advantage
should not be advertised by one favorable parameter.  It should survive the
whole diagram: sublevel geometry constrains the orthonormal-tangent reduced
barrier Hessian; the access model determines what transformations are
available; and the output interface determines whether the computation can
continue.  The present paper supplies laws and counterexamples at every arrow
in that diagram, together with
conditional modules where the arrows can be made cheap.  Closing even one of
the open problems above would sharpen the boundary; closing the six-desiderata
problem would finally give the end-to-end verdict that this paper does not
claim.
