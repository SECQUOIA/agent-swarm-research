## Independent mathematical checks

1. **Compact geometric assumptions.** The multiple-zero set includes every spatially multiple zero, so an identically vanishing realization would contradict its finiteness. Continuity on the compact parameter interval therefore gives a strictly positive minimum of the integrated rate. At a simple zero a fixed-sign spatial derivative gives at most one root in a product neighborhood; at a fold a fixed-sign second derivative gives at most two. A finite cover bounds the total root count. The zero set outside open fold products is compact and contains no multiple zero, giving the uniform ordinary-root slope bound. If distinct ordinary roots could approach each other, their limit would be a multiple zero outside those products; this is excluded. Thus the uniformly bounded partition used for upper bracketing is justified.

2. **Direct normal form.** The implicit critical point exists because `g_ss` is nonzero. The Taylor coefficient `b_j` is C² under the C⁴ family assumption; its sign stays fixed and its absolute value stays bounded away from zero after restricting the neighborhood. Hence the displayed spatial coordinate has a bounded positive Jacobian and produces `g=σ_j(x²−t)` exactly. The signed parameter has nonzero derivative at the fold, and the critical-point shift is `O(|t|)`. This proves that root distance, separation, and slope have order `sqrt(t)` on the two-root side. Differentiating `g(u,C_j(u))=0` twice at the fold yields exactly `C_j''=−g_ss/g_c`, so root-position intervals have parameter probability of order their squared distance. Pulling the almost-everywhere density bound back to any shell is legitimate because the branch map is a smooth diffeomorphism away from the fold point.

3. **Arbitrary-design lower certificates.** Averaging the squared derivative of a translated bump gives the stated `m/(r ell)` estimate by Fubini. It balances the quadratic reaction bound at `ell∝(m/r³)^(1/4)`. The parameter density contributes a factor of order `r²`, yielding `r^(2−5q/4)m^(−q/4)`. Negative-power Jensen is valid for every positive q; the proof does not require q≥1 or any regularity of the competing field. With zero local mass the same averaged quotient diverges as its width decreases. The geometric shells can have both disjoint spatial enlargements and disjoint parameter images because the chosen branch is monotone and has quadratic parameter scaling. The critical lower bound uses only their shared total mass. The independent fold-centered bump gives response at least `R^(−3)` on a parameter set of probability at least a constant times `R²`, proving the supercritical lower order.

4. **Protection of ordinary and stationary roots.** The nonnegative grading exponent is a substantive choice: the global lower bound `D_M≥c a_R` remains valid even when an ordinary root sits at another fold's site. For q≤2/3 the choice alpha=0 is sufficient; no sharp cosine profile is improperly imposed. Exact normalization gives `a_R∼` the three stated orders, and in every case `a_R` is comparable to `R^(6+alpha)`.

5. **Uniform Neumann upper bounds.** Free endpoints enlarge the variational class, so the response estimates have the correct direction. At separated fold roots, mobility and quadratic coefficient are comparable to `a_R r^(−alpha)` and `r²`. The harmonic interval length relative to its oscillator scale is bounded away from zero once r/R exceeds the fixed threshold. The local reciprocal-rate complement has order `r^(−3)` and is absorbed by the root estimate. In the central window, the scaled Taylor potential converges uniformly on its fixed interval to a nonzero quadratic polynomial squared. Together with the uniformly positive derivative coefficient, this excludes zero Neumann coercivity: a normalized sequence with vanishing derivative energy would tend to a nonzero constant, inconsistent with the limiting reaction energy. The boundary of the central interval can be chosen beyond all local roots. The rootless integral has order `|t|^(−3/2)`. The ordinary-root cover and positive-potential remainder complete the partition, including parameter values at which ordinary roots are stationary.

6. **Integration across all regimes.** The root-side exponent is `e=2−3q/2+alpha q/4`; it is positive below 8/5, zero at 8/5, and negative above it. For alpha=0 and q≤2/3 it remains strictly positive. The central-to-regular ratio is `R^e`; I independently simplified this identity symbolically. The rootless logarithm at q=2/3 is lower order than `M^(−q/4)`. At q=8/5 the central and rootless terms have logarithm power 2/5, compared with 7/5 for the root contribution. Above 8/5, `a_R^(−q/4)R^e=R^(2−3q)` and the ordinary-root contribution is smaller. Thus no parameter region has been omitted from an upper bound.

7. **Full-bulk consequence.** The compact-family bounds verify the accepted uniform rate assumptions. Every optimized scalar order dominates the required logarithm power.
