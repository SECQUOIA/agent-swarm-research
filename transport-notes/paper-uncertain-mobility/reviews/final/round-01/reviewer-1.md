## 1. Physical model, weighted forms, and dispersion

### Scalar and physical admissibility are correctly separated

The smooth-test supremum is defined for every nonnegative integrable coefficient, while a physical process is assigned only when the derivative preform is closable. That distinction remains in force through the design theorems and the measure-limit arguments. There is no step that silently turns an arbitrary measure or nonclosable coefficient into a diffusion process.

The positive-background closability argument is valid even without an upper bound on the coefficient. If `sqrt(D) v_n'` is Cauchy and `v_n -> 0` in L2, the lower bound on D makes the unweighted derivatives Cauchy. Their distributional limit is zero. Almost-everywhere subsequences then identify the weighted limit as zero because D is finite almost everywhere. The same reasoning embeds the minimal form closure in H1. The weighted energy need not be assigned to all of H1, and the paper does not make that stronger claim.

The anchored Poincare estimate controls the mean by the positive integral of the reaction and controls the remaining component by the derivative. Together with the floor, it yields the stated coercivity. Normal-contraction stability, density of the smooth core, positivity of the source solution, and testing with the constant yield the required identities `J = integral h = a[h]` and `integral kh = P`.

### The coupled form and stochastic identification are consistent

On the disjoint union of the bulk closure and the separate wall, the stated measure has full topological support. The trace term is continuous in the bulk H1 and surface form norms, so it does not obstruct closedness. Applying the same contraction to both components controls the exchange term. Smooth pairs provide a regular form core, and the common constant has zero energy. The regular-form construction therefore supplies the stationary conservative reversible process used subsequently. For positive-floor fields, the gauge argument proves a gap and rules out nonconstant zero modes; no such gap is assumed for arbitrary degenerate fields.

Integration by parts gives the displayed backward bulk boundary condition and wall generator. Detailed balance with the wall weight K recovers the forward exchange signs in the concentration equations. In particular, adsorption is proportional to desorption, so the stationary affinity and mean velocity stay fixed as the kinetic rate changes. The divergence-form mobility is essential for that claim and is explicitly specified.

The stationary covariance identity gives the long-time variance coefficient with the correct factor `1/(2t)`. The spectral time kernel increases to `1/lambda`, and its value at zero is `t/2`. Thus a nonzero zero-eigenspace component produces an infinite coefficient, exactly as represented by the variational trials along that component. The positive-spectrum cutoff trials justify the variational formula even when the spectral inverse integral diverges. The paper neither needs nor asserts a central limit theorem to conclude the variance equivalent in the finite case.

The separately driven axial stochastic integral has finite second moment under the stated axial diffusivity assumptions. Conditional mean zero given the transverse path eliminates its covariance with the advective displacement. The stationary molecular contribution and the isotropic-wall budget term have the correct equilibrium factors.

### Schur elimination does not require an unjustified L2 inverse

For a positive floor, ordinary inverse completion gives the constant term `K V^2 J`, the cross load `-K V integral kh b`, and the nonnegative Schur penalty. The normalization by Z produces the stated chi. The bulk source integral is `KPV`, which cancels the wall source on common constants; the bulk-mean gauge is therefore valid.

The finite-J extension is also sound. The source is bounded precisely in the scalar energy norm, so its Riesz representative belongs to the energy completion even if it does not belong to L2. The continuous map into L2 given by multiplication by `sqrt(k)` makes `w = sqrt(k)(sqrt(k)h)` meaningful. It also makes the boundary load continuous. Completing squares in this Hilbert space avoids subtracting infinite energies. Density identifies the Schur infimum taken in the form domain with its energy-completion version. Finally, `w in L2`, the bulk trace estimate, and bulk Poincare make the remainder finite for each such fixed design. None of these steps requires a uniform spectral gap for the degenerate scalar problem.

### Uniform physical transfer has the needed quantifiers

The crude floor estimates give `J = O(1/m)`, `||h'||_2 = O(1/m)`, and `||kh||_infinity = O(1/m)`. Positivity and fixed total flux bound each Fourier coefficient of kh. Splitting its H^(-1/2) norm at a frequency of order its supremum yields a logarithm, rather than a power of `1/m`. Pairing with the bulk H^(1/2) trace and dropping the Schur penalty gives the stated uniform logarithmic remainder.

The countable smooth-test families make both scalar and extended full variational responses Borel. The argument does not need the set of closable coefficients itself to be Borel. Adding the uniform fraction preserves the per-observation mass, uses only the original observation, and converts any scalar policy into a physical policy. Full-form ordering gives the factor `(1-theta)^(-1)` for J. Minkowski for q at least one and subadditivity below one give the stated moment bounds without interchanging expectation and pointwise infimum. The compact regular-offset bump proves the all-information `M^(-q/5)` lower bound, so the logarithmic correction is negligible uniformly over observation laws in the cosine ensemble.

The units are consistent: physical J has units length times time; chi has units length divided by time squared; their product is diffusivity. The reference scales are fixed before taking the budget limit.

## 2. Whole-line source models and the uniform baseline

The oscillator and quartic-pair responses are correctly defined as energy-dual source quantities. The constant source is not falsely treated as an L2 vector on the line. The core anchor and confining potential control the weighted L2 norm. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives source-tail decay of order `L^(-1/2)` for quadratic confinement and `L^(-3/2)` for quartic confinement. These bounds are sufficient to pass source integrals through local weak compactness, including for free endpoints. Local convergence and uniform comparability of the metric weights are exactly the properties needed in subsequent coordinate changes.

I checked the oscillator heat-kernel integration and beta substitution. They give `C0 = pi Gamma(1/4)/(2 Gamma(3/4))` with the stated numerical value. The simple-root length is `(epsilon/a)^(1/4)` and its integrated response scales as `epsilon^(-1/4) a^(-3/4)`. The fold length is `(epsilon/b^2)^(1/6)`, the offset scale is `epsilon^(1/3)b^(1/3)`, and its response scales as `epsilon^(-1/2)b^(-1)`. These factors feed the later constants consistently.

For large positive pair parameter, the two harmonic neighborhoods contribute `2 C0 (4 mu)^(-3/4)`. The chosen shrinking relative neighborhoods have expanding harmonic radii, while their complementary reciprocal integral is smaller. For negative parameter, scaling the reciprocal quartic potential gives `pi/2` and the derivative correction tends to zero. These are whole-line tails, and the paper correctly avoids claiming an unrestricted joint finite-interval equivalent on the rootless side.

The cosine estimates cover compact fold parameters, separated roots, and rootless offsets separately. The exact sine coordinate supplies bounded metric weights and removes Taylor remainder difficulties at the fold scale. Neumann bracketing gives the response and gap estimates without hidden matching conditions. The separated-root matching condition `epsilon/t^3 -> 0` is the correct one.

The three uniform moment calculations use valid domination or an explicit critical cutoff. In the supercritical coefficient I checked the product of two folds, density 1/4, response amplitude `2^q`, and offset Jacobian `2^(-1/3)`, giving `2^(q-4/3)`. At q = 4/3 the retained logarithmic annuli and the controlled omitted fraction yield the stated coefficient. The squared mean is lower order than the second moment, so the variance claim follows. Direct inversion gives the reported limiting tail and its atom at zero.

The flux identity for the uniform trial follows by multiplication by kh and periodic integration by parts. With `k'' = 2(1-c^2) + 6c(c+cos s) - 4(c+cos s)^2`, the response/gap estimates give squared flux error `O(epsilon^(1/6))`. The resulting L2 flux bound and the vanishing Schur penalty at the regular Neumann bulk solution justify the realization-uniform remainder limit. The required H2 regularity is used only with the stated C2 domain, L2 bulk load, and constant Neumann datum.

The Gaussian amplitude-collapse example and the marked-root formulas are internally consistent. The constant test gives `2P/R^2` independently of the design; the Rayleigh density makes its expectation infinite. By contrast, the local inverse-slope sum has finite first moment. This demonstrates the failure of exchanging limits and expectations rather than contradicting the uniformly anchored cosine results. The first marked Kac–Rice identity is attributed as an established tool, and no independence of nearby zeros is inferred from it.

## 4. Generic folds

The C4 assumption suffices for the stated C2 Taylor-based coordinate. At a fold, the critical-point curve and the nonzero parameter derivative give a transverse quadratic normal form with bounded Jacobians. On the zero set, finite compact covers by monotone or strictly convex spatial slices give a uniform root count. Outside fold neighborhoods, compactness supplies nonzero slopes and separated ordinary-root neighborhoods. An identically zero realization would violate the finite-fold assumption, so the global rate anchor follows.

Only one positively sampled fold is needed for the lower bounds. Its root-to-parameter Jacobian is of order distance to the fold, which produces the same shell probability and powers as in the cosine case. The upper design protects every fold site and all ordinary roots. Replacing a negative grading exponent by zero for small q is justified because an ordinary root can coincide spatially with another parameter's fold site. The additional ordinary-root terms have the stated lower or matching orders. The paper claims only orders for this generality, with constants depending on the fixed family and density. The assumptions exclude simultaneous folds, unknown fold sites, nontransverse unfolding, and vanishing sampling density at the chosen fold; none of these is silently included in the conclusion.

## 6. Finite precision and uniform assembly

The uncertain-center scaling gives the local prefactor `a^(-4/5)m^(-1/5)` and uncertainty argument `w/(m/a)^(1/5)`. For attainment of the local value, vague limits may lose mass. Singular mass can be removed, and any missing density mass can be added monotonically. This suffices for existence and lower semicontinuity; unlike the supercritical fold argument, it does not need to prove tightness of every minimizing sequence. Upper semicontinuity comes from regularizing a nearly optimal density with a positive integrable tail, then using continuity for that fixed field. The quadratic source-tail estimate and boundedness argument justify its energy completion with free endpoints and rough coefficients.

In a regular bin, reflection preserves every individual kinetic realization, so the half-wall mass is exactly M/2 without requiring offset symmetry of the bin. The lower bound rescales arbitrary mobility measures and uses the actual bin parameterization. Singular-mass removal and completion to unit mass give the local variational lower bound. The upper bound uses an exact root coordinate whose metric is canceled in the derivative term; the exterior is uniformly rootless. Continuity and compact covers make the local recovery uniform and permit deterministic choices for finitely many bins. The global envelope handles bins that cross the folds and is independent of their alignment. On regular bins, the root-span and placement-length scales are `Delta/sqrt(t)` and `(M/t)^(1/5)`. The Riemann-sum assembly first retains a compact regular offset interval and only afterwards removes its artificial ends using these integrable envelopes. This ordering justifies the continuous global crossover and the endpoint at exact observation. The simultaneous coarse limit is proved separately with harmonic tests, padded root arcs, and a uniform lower certificate; it is not obtained by an impermissible substitution of an unbounded ratio into the fixed-ratio theorem. I checked that its coefficient equals the large-tau limit of the explicit crossover integral. The result concerns equal noiseless bins and an exact per-bin budget; sensor noise, observation cost, and arbitrary nonnested partition ordering are not asserted.
