# Prior-art audit: measurement resolution before mobility placement

Literature audit, 2026-09-07. This assesses [finite-precision-mobility.md](finite-precision-mobility.md). The uniform order law and both sharp endpoint constants have passed [independent mathematical review](review-finite-precision-mobility.md). The intermediate exact crossover remains unproved.

## Assessment

No direct match was found for the two-parameter law

\[
\Phi(M,\Delta)\asymp M^{-1/5}+M^{-1/4}\Delta^{1/4}
\]

for the compact cosine-offset killing model. The potentially original result is a uniform quantitative relation between measurement resolution and the mobility budget, including control of bins that meet a coalescing-zero configuration. The general framework of choosing actions from incomplete observations, the benefit of refining observations, and convergence of quantized decisions toward full-information decisions are established.

The strongest general precedents are observation-channel and quantized-control theory, alongside stochastic conductivity design. They do not directly provide the singular budget-dependent resolution threshold. Engineering value-of-information studies are useful context but are less close mathematically.

## Precise scope

The unknown offset is `c∼Uniform[-2,2]`, the killing rate is `(c+cos s)²`, and the designer observes the equal-width bin containing `c`. A distinct deterministic mobility field can be chosen for each bin, with budget `M` available for each manufactured wall. The decision minimizes conditional expected scalar compliance, and those conditional optima are averaged over bins.

The observation has noiseless quantization width `Δ`. It is not additive noise, a random confidence interval, a bound on estimation mean-square error, or a finite number of noisy measurements. Likewise, the budgets are not pooled over observation outcomes. These distinctions matter for both correctness and comparison with prior work.

The verified threshold is `Δ∼M^(1/5)`: coarser observations retain a resolution penalty, while finer ones approach the exact-information order. The source note also gives independently verified sharp coarse and fine equivalents. Its suggested formula for a finite nonzero resolution-to-budget ratio remains unproved and is separate from those endpoint results.

## Observation channels and quantized decisions

Yüksel and Linder, *Optimization and Convergence of Observation Channels in Stochastic Control* (2012), study optimal expected cost when decisions are based on an observation channel. The work focuses substantially on a single-stage problem and analyzes continuity and existence under weak, setwise, and total-variation convergence, with explicit applications to quantizers. This already supplies the general mathematical language for the current finite-bin decision problem. Its channel-convergence results do not give the present simultaneous `M→0`, `Δ→0` law. The mobility action space and potentially infinite compliance also need assumptions beyond simply substituting this example into their displayed Euclidean action setup. [Open paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.3824), [published article](https://doi.org/10.1137/100808976).

Saldi, Yüksel and Linder, *Finite Model Approximations and Asymptotic Optimality of Quantized Policies in Decentralized Stochastic Control* (2015 preprint), construct finite models by uniformly quantizing observation and action spaces. Under technical assumptions, strategies from those models approach the optimal cost. This is a closer precedent than a generic appeal to Bayesian inference for the claim that increasing resolution can recover optimal decision performance. It does not supply a uniform rate as an independent PDE coefficient budget degenerates. [Open paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.04657).

Consequently, neither “finite observations can suffice for almost optimal decisions” nor “better resolution improves the decision” should be claimed as novel. For nested partitions the latter follows directly because any coarse-bin policy is also admissible under the refined observation. Arbitrarily shifted partitions need not be ordered by information merely because their numerical widths differ.

## Partial information in PDE and conductivity design

Conti, Held, Pach, Rumpf and Schultz, *Shape Optimization Under Uncertainty—A Stochastic Programming Perspective* (2009), put the information available when a shape is selected at the center of stochastic shape design. Their framework distinguishes selecting a shape in advance from allowing it to depend on a realized load. Restricting a policy to a sigma-algebra generated by a bin observation is a standard intermediate information structure. [Published primary article](https://doi.org/10.1137/070702059), [open author presentation](https://www.math.uni-bonn.de/people/dmv2006/minisymposien/20/vortraege/Held.pdf).

Buttazzo and Maestre, *Optimal Shape for Elliptic Problems with Random Perturbations* (2010 preprint; 2011 publication), optimize deterministic conductivity under random forcing and a material budget. Conditional optimization inside each observed bin uses the same expected-compliance decision principle with a conditional probability law. Their model has bounded positive conductivities and no random vanishing killing field, so it does not directly yield the present singular resolution law. [Open paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.2770).

Alphonse, Kunštek and Vrdoljak, *Optimal design with uncertainties: a risk-averse approach* (2026), extend stochastic elliptic design to general risk criteria and homogenized mixtures of two positive conductors. Their existence and stationarity theory is a relevant general framework, but it neither studies quantized measurements nor identifies a vanishing-budget measurement threshold. [Open full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19869v1).

Further details on these overlaps and the exact-information comparison are in [robust-design-prior-art.md](robust-design-prior-art.md). Conditioning the ensemble on bins does not by itself create a new optimization framework.

## Engineering value of measurement

Capser and Nikolaidis, *Assessing the Value of Information for Multiple, Correlated Design Alternatives* (2017), use Bayesian updating to evaluate the expected utility obtained with increasing observation size when choosing among correlated alternatives. This is a direct engineering precedent for relating information quality to subsequent design performance, although the paper concerns a finite collection of alternatives rather than an elliptic coefficient field. The accessible primary abstract supplies no counterpart of the present budget powers. [Primary article record](https://doi.org/10.4271/2017-01-0208).

Task-oriented quantization also explicitly allocates higher precision to observations with greater downstream decision value. For example, Mostaani, Vu, Habibi, Chatzinotas and Ottersten, *Task-Oriented Communication Design at Scale* (2023), quantify observations for cooperative control using the value of information and bit budgets. This is conceptual precedent for judging resolution by the decision it enables rather than by reconstruction error alone; its multi-agent control setting does not contain the current transport law. [Open primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08481).

The current note does not optimize a sensor, likelihood, measurement count, or observation cost. Calling its result an optimal experimental design or an optimal measurement protocol would therefore exceed the stated problem. It quantifies performance under a specified quantization model.

## Elementary scaling versus the theorem-level contribution

For a quadratic defect with known curvature `a`, an optimally placed mobility budget `m` has spatial scale `ℓ=(m/a)^(1/5)`. If the defect center is uncertain over width `w`, dimensional rescaling makes `w/ℓ` the natural resolution parameter. In the cosine model an offset uncertainty `Δ` moves a regular root by about `Δ/√a`, so the local comparison is

\[
\Delta\sim m^{1/5}a^{3/10}.
\]

This dimensional matching is a short consequence of the local placement law. It predicts the threshold but is not, alone, a robust global result. The potential advance is proving the two-parameter bounds uniformly over all mobility placements and all relative rates of `M` and `Δ`, then showing that singular bins near `c=±1` do not change the global threshold. The unrestricted lower certificate prevents improved scaling from narrow spikes or rapidly varying mobility fields that a fixed-shape calculation would miss.

## Recommended claim

A narrow claim is a uniform resolution-budget law for an explicit singular transport-design problem: quantized knowledge of the kinetic pattern recovers the full-information budget exponent once the offset resolution reaches the physical placement scale. The accompanying bound quantifies the coarse-resolution penalty and accounts for coalescing zeros.

No direct collision was found in searches on partial-information elliptic design, stochastic conductivity and reinforcement, observation-channel convergence, quantized policies, decision-oriented experimental design, and engineering value of information. This search result is not proof of global originality. The physical crossover and its operator-level estimates are the relevant candidate contribution; the information and conditional-decision framework should be attributed as established.


The final [scalar-to-bulk transfer](scalar-to-bulk-design-transfer.md) also verifies these optimized-value laws for fixed finite bulk mixing under its stated assumptions. The transfer uses a same-budget uniform-background mixture, quadratic-form ordering, and a logarithmic bulk bound; those general tools are not an additional novelty claim. It does not change the unresolved status of the proposed exact intermediate scalar crossover.
