# Prior art: periodic removal, Floquet decay, and transport spectroscopy

Literature audit begun 2026-09-06. This note separates established results from a possible new measurement protocol. It does not establish novelty by absence of search results.

## Main assessment

The claim that constant removal maximizes the asymptotic decay rate at fixed pointwise time-average is established mathematics. Reversibility is unnecessary for that inequality. Global monotonic improvement of the decay rate as the driving frequency increases is also established for important reversible diffusion classes. Neither should be presented as a new theorem.

The more promising candidate is an experimental inference method: weak periodic modulation of spatially selective removal, with the change in the long-time decay exponent used to measure the fluctuation spectrum of the process conditioned on long survival. The components of this interpretation have substantial prior art. **A subsequent search located the exact general second-order Floquet frequency-response formula in Calvez, Gabriel, and Gaubert (2014), discussed below.** I have not located a primary paper that explicitly combines this removal protocol, the Floquet readout, the nonzero removal bias, and inference of the conditioned transport spectrum. This is a limited search outcome, not a priority claim.

## Exact and close mathematical overlap

1. **Janusz Mierczyński, “Estimates for principal Lyapunov exponents: A survey” (2014).** [Open preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.0992), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.0992). Theorem II.9 of the preprint proves that a strongly cooperative periodic matrix system with constant off-diagonal entries has principal growth exponent at least the dominant eigenvalue of its time-average. Setting the matrix to a fixed exchange generator minus diagonal removal gives exactly the proposed no-improvement result, after reversing the sign from growth to decay. The proof uses Jensen's inequality on positive Floquet-vector ratios. The survey attributes the broader result to Mierczyński and Shen's 2008 monograph. Its section IV.3 also states the analogous stationary random-environment bound. Consequently, merely extending periodic removal to a stationary random schedule is not a novel route. The strong-cooperativity assumption is stronger than irreducibility with some missing edges, but routine extensions across zeros are unlikely to support a substantial novelty claim.

2. **V. Hutson, W. Shen, and G. T. Vickers, “Estimates for the principal spectrum point for certain time-dependent parabolic operators,” Proceedings of the AMS 129 (2001), 1669–1679.** [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-00-05808-1), [author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251815763_Estimates_for_the_principal_spectrum_point_for_certain_time-dependent_parabolic_operators). Their periodic parabolic comparison gives the same time-average inequality for a fixed elliptic transport operator and variable zeroth-order coefficient. Equality occurs precisely when the time-dependent part is spatially constant. Their treatment also includes almost-periodic coefficients. The full-text search extraction was available; the AMS PDF endpoint did not open in this session. This is direct prior art for spatial diffusion with removal and for its equality condition.

3. **Shuang Liu, Yuan Lou, Rui Peng, and Maolin Zhou, “Monotonicity of the principal eigenvalue for a linear time-periodic parabolic operator” (2019).** [Open primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11757), [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/proc/14653). Theorem 1.1 proves that the decay-sign eigenvalue is nondecreasing with temporal frequency for symmetric diffusion without drift; with time-independent diffusion, strictness fails exactly for additive separation of spatial and temporal potential. Theorem 1.2 includes constant scalar diffusivity and a fixed gradient drift. Theorem 1.3 supplies slow-frequency and fast-frequency limits; the fast limit is the averaged operator. Thus a positive frequency derivative, the constant-removal upper limit, and the additive-separation equality case are known in these classes. Their examples show why arbitrary time-dependent drift changes the problem. A finite-state reversible analogue would be useful exposition but needs further checking before being called novel.

4. **Peter Hänggi, “Error Bounds of Continued Fractions for Complex Transport Coefficients and Spectral Functions,” Z. Naturforsch. 33a (1978), 1380–1382.** [Open author PDF](https://www.physik.uni-augsburg.de/theo1/hanggi/Papers/17.pdf). The scanned first page was inspected directly. The paper concerns autocorrelation spectra, complex transport coefficients, Stieltjes representations, and continued-fraction error bounds. Searchable extraction of the later pages explicitly connects positive spectral measures with self-adjoint generators and detailed balance. Therefore positive Lorentzian mixtures, Stieltjes moment constraints, and their use in spectral bounds are classical structures, even if a particular removal-based observable is new.

5. **Yong Chen, Min-Ping Qian, and Jian-Sheng Xie, “On characterization of reversible Markov processes by monotonicity of the fluctuation spectral density,” Journal of Mathematical Physics 47 (2006), 103301.** [Primary publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2338763). **Chen, Qian, and Xie, “Irreversibility implies the occurrence of nonmonotonic power spectra,” Journal of Mathematical Physics 48 (2007), 073301.** [Primary publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2747615). These directly address power-spectrum monotonicity as a reversibility test. The accessible abstracts distinguish conditions on observables and continuous versus discrete time. A scalar nonmonotonic spectrum as evidence against a reversible Markov model is consequently established. Do not infer a converse for one observable, or for restricted nonnegative removal masks, from a theorem quantified over a richer observable class. Full proofs and exact hypotheses remain to be checked before borrowing their converses.

6. **Raphaël Chetrite and Hugo Touchette, “Nonequilibrium Markov processes conditioned on large deviations,” Annales Henri Poincaré 16 (2015), 2005–2057.** [Open primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5157). This develops driven processes through generalized Doob transforms and relates them to exponentially tilted path measures. It is the appropriate prior-art framework for interpreting a killed generator's ground-state transform. Calling the conditioned process itself new would be incorrect. A measurement that accesses its fluctuations through a controllable physical removal process might still be distinct.

## Close experimental and transport precedents

**Additional direct formula collision: Vincent Calvez, Pierre Gabriel, and Stéphane Gaubert, “Non-linear eigenvalue problems arising from growth maximization of positive linear dynamical systems” (2014).** [Open primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.1868), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.1868). Section III.B, Proposition 10, equation (15), gives the second Floquet derivative for a general periodic scalar modulation of a positive matrix system. The unnumbered cosine formula immediately after equation (16), on PDF page 5, is exactly the gap-weighted rational frequency response used below, with left/right eigenvector products replacing squared matrix elements. Equation (17) and Proposition 12 identify its high-frequency coefficient with a generator quadratic form. The section is framed around a locally optimal constant control; removing that hypothesis for zero-mean cosine forcing changes no substantive perturbation mechanism. The reversible formula is therefore a specialization of explicit prior work, not merely an independently familiar-looking calculation. Static-curvature and high-frequency sum-rule claims also overlap closely. Related earlier work is [Coron, Gabriel, and Shang, arXiv:1309.2477](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.2477).

7. **A. Delon, Y. Usson, J. Derouard, T. Biben, and C. Souchier, “Continuous Photobleaching in Vesicles and Living Cells: A Measure of Diffusion and Compartmentation,” Biophysical Journal 90 (2006), 2548–2562.** [Open primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1403194/), [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16428281/). This is especially close physically: spatially localized photobleaching is removal, while particles diffuse in a compartment. The paper analyzes long-time fluorescence decay and extracts diffusion/compartment information. It distinguishes weak bleaching, where the decay rate scarcely depends on diffusion, from strong bleaching, where diffusion controls the rate. It does not, in the material inspected, propose frequency scanning of a weak modulation about a fixed bleaching intensity or identify the resulting spectrum with a Doob-conditioned process. It rules out novelty of the broad claim that long-time localized-removal decay reveals transport.

8. **“Exact analytical results for ADC with oscillating diffusion sensitizing gradients” (2013).** [Open primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3771359/). Oscillating-gradient NMR obtains frequency-dependent diffusion information from signal attenuation. Equations (27)–(28) express attenuation through a modulation filter and a velocity autocorrelation spectrum. This is a direct conceptual comparator for frequency-selective interrogation through a multiplicative field. The NMR field contributes an imaginary potential; physical removal contributes a real potential, with a nonzero baseline that changes the relevant path ensemble. That difference may matter experimentally, but merely presenting attenuation as a spectral probe would not be new.

9. **“Photobleaching reduction in modulated super-resolution microscopy” (2021 issue).** [Primary article](https://academic.oup.com/jmicro/article/70/3/278/5925787). The reported experiments vary excitation modulation frequency while holding average excitation power fixed and examine photobleaching. The inspected passage says frequency had little effect under their conditions. This is a real use of modulated bleaching, but the mechanism involves fluorophore photophysics and lock-in imaging rather than an identified compartment-transport spectrum. It is a relevant experimental confound and an adjacent protocol, not an exact mathematical collision on present evidence.

## What remains potentially distinct

For a reversible baseline killed operator with ground state \(u_0\), gaps \(\Delta_j\), and a removal mask \(K\), the candidate weak-cosine result is

\[
\lambda(\epsilon,\omega)
=\lambda_0-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2}
\sum_{j>0}\frac{\Delta_j|\langle u_j,Ku_0\rangle|^2}
{\Delta_j^2+\omega^2}+o(\epsilon^2).
\]

This formula is recorded here to specify what was searched; the independent derivation and verification belong in the mathematical note. A plausible contribution would combine a physically realizable modulation/readout protocol with a proof of exactly which conditioned transport quantities are identifiable and which are not. It should explicitly distinguish the invariant distribution of the process conditioned never to die from the distribution of particles surviving only to the observation endpoint. A single scalar spectral measure cannot generally reconstruct an entire exchange network.

Another possible extension is to drive two spatial masks with a relative phase and compare opposite phase orderings. Their mixed response could isolate antisymmetric cross correlations that vanish under reversibility. This needs independent mathematics and a dedicated comparison with stochastic-pump, geometric-phase, and multichannel noise-spectroscopy literature. No novelty assertion is made here.

A first comparator for this extension is [“Fitness in time-dependent environments includes a geometric phase contribution”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3350737/) (2012). It identifies a growth/fitness contribution that is quadratic in weak environmental variations and changes sign when their order is reversed. Its mechanism is a slowly driven population model with varying birth/death parameters; it is not the proposed finite-frequency diagonal-removal test of a fixed exchange generator. Nevertheless, an opposite-phase growth-rate difference or its adiabatic geometric interpretation is not novel by itself.

## Search limitations and useful negative outcomes

Searches included combinations of periodic killing, Floquet survival, removal modulation, time-dependent counting fields, Doob response, modulated photobleaching, Stieltjes detailed balance, and oscillating-gradient diffusion spectroscopy. Searches using “killing” alone were frequently contaminated by relativity papers about Killing vectors. Full texts were directly available for the Mierczyński and Liu papers; the Hänggi PDF is scanned; some publisher routes were inaccessible. No systematic forward-citation audit has yet been completed.

The most useful negative result is that the original fixed-average optimality theorem, its equality characterization, and a major class of frequency-monotonicity extensions are already known. The spectroscopy direction should be assessed as a potential method and inference result built on established spectral theory, rather than as a discovery of the underlying positive Lorentzian representation.
