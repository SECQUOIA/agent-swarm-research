## Upper reduction: independently reconstructed

The supplied global bound \(\nabla^2f\succeq\mu I\) makes \(f\) coercive
and gives a unique stationary minimizer \(p\). Strong monotonicity gives
\(\|p\|\le\|\nabla f(0)\|/\mu\). With the stated expanded binary
input length, the displayed search radius, function bounds, and derivative
bounds have polynomial bit length. I checked the small-input constants:
the four derivative estimates really are below \(2^{20L}\) for
\(L\ge2\).

The initial objective error \(\mu^3/(32B^2)\) yields distance
\(\mu/(4B)\) by strong convexity. This is only polynomial-bit accuracy.
[Hesse's Redemption, Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03440v1)
does provide a point with the needed objective guarantee in polynomial
time. I inspected the primary statement directly. The source's more general
solution-radius theorem is unnecessary here because the gradient and
supplied curvature already bound the minimizer.

The Newton identity has the correct Hessian sign and gives
\(e_{t+1}\le B e_t^2/(2\mu)\). The selected initial radius preserves the
neighborhood at every step. Positive definite Hessians permit a fixed
no-pivot rational \(LDL^{\mathsf T}\) computation with no sign query.
Each step adds polynomially many circuit gates when preceding expressions
are shared. It need not preserve polynomial size after expanding the
fractions, and the proof never requires that expansion.

For a polynomial observable, the critical equations and the equation
\(z=h(X)\) define exactly one real value. I checked the coefficient-bit
clause of [Basu's Theorem 2.16, printed page 12](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011.pdf)
directly. The one-block elimination bound supplies a univariate integer
polynomial of singly exponential degree and coefficient bit size vanishing
at that value. A sign-description of a singleton must contain a nonzero
polynomial vanishing there; otherwise every sign condition is locally
constant. Removing a power of \(z\) and comparing its nonzero integer
constant coefficient with the remaining terms proves the stated nonzero
gap. A finite complex critical locus is not needed.

The proof uses quantifier elimination only for this bound. It does not run
the elimination algorithm. The gap is constructed by polynomially many
squarings, and polynomially many Newton steps reach error at most one eighth
of the gap. All five shifted comparisons correctly handle the case in which
the exact observable is zero. Division elimination with squared
denominators preserves signs even if an intermediate divisor is negative.
There is one final PosSLP instance, rather than an unrecorded sequence of
oracle calls.

For a supplied positive definite full Hessian Gram, coefficient matching
and rational positive-definiteness checks are polynomial-time operations.
The determinant-over-trace formula is a valid polynomial-bit curvature
bound. It converts the semantic curvature promise into an ordinary checked
input language. The enlarged input length must include that derived bound;
the September note does so.

## Lower reductions and the combined classification

The [fresh cubic-root lower review](lower-review.md) reconstructs the
analytic simulation, signed-root realization, full positive definite Gram,
rational coefficient recovery, and cubic perturbation. In particular:

- The simulation tolerates cancellation and unequal signal orders without
  replacing relative errors by insufficient absolute errors.
- Extremely small signals are generated by short circuits. They are not
  printed as enormous binary fractions in the output instance.
- The full Hessian Gram is positive definite as a matrix. Positivity only on
  the rank-one tensor vectors of the Hessian biform would not suffice.
- The distinct extra signal makes the movement of the minimum too small to
  reverse the encoded sign. The constructed minimum is nonzero on both
  branches.

The proof therefore supplies hardness for strict and weak value comparisons.
Replacing an integer output by \(1-V\) reverses the appropriate yes/no
branch, so the opposite inequalities are also covered. The zero-minimum
realization supplies the coordinate lower bounds. Nothing here gives a
matching lower bound for equality.

The [fresh rational-optimizer review](rational-review.md) independently
checks the quaternion compiler and realization. It verifies repeated
parents, shared gates, noncommutative product order, positive scalar parts,
rounding tolerances, and the exact preservation of the common zero. The
optimizer is the original list of rational unit-quaternion gate values.
It is rational and bounded, but its expanded fractions may be very long.
The quartic and its \(N+1\) rational square factors remain short.

The rational-optimizer statement is a promise restriction of the checked
quartic class. It does not recognize rationality of an arbitrary optimizer.
It concerns coordinate comparisons; it does not transfer rationality to the
new minimizer created by the separate cubic value perturbation.

## SOS and representation distinctions

The Taylor argument correctly gives a real SOS for \(f-f(p)\) in the
supplied SOS-convex class. Consequently global nonnegativity and real SOS
membership agree there. When the minimum is strictly positive, a rational
center sufficiently close to the minimizer makes the completed constant
positive. The full Hessian Gram contributes every homogeneous quadratic
monomial, while the completed linear factors and constant complete the
span. This proves existence of a rational positive definite polynomial
Gram. A full strict Hessian Gram is stronger than an arbitrary supplied
SOS-convexity certificate and must remain in this statement.

The converse follows because the full ordinary quadratic monomial vector
contains its constant coordinate. Thus a positive definite polynomial Gram
forces a strictly positive minimum. These two implications support the
strict-Gram decision classification, but neither gives a polynomial-size
expanded Gram.

At minimum zero, real and rational SOS must remain separate. I independently
reconstructed the [ternary boundary example](../../../research-20260927/ternary-rational-sos-convex-counterexample.md).
The new [exact diagnostic](check_boundary_sos.py) verifies its full Hessian
identity, twelve positive principal minors, its algebraic zero, the
rank-five evaluation map, and all twenty-five identities for the separating
functional. The proof that each rational square must vanish at the unique
zero then excludes rational SOS. The argument uses the zero and the rational
coefficient field; the separating functional is not nonnegative on arbitrary
squares. This audit uses the example to separate real and rational SOS and
does not rely on its separate literature-dependent minimal-dimension claim.

The [fresh witness and Gram review](witness-review.md) checks the remaining
output-size statements. Their distinctions are essential:

| Claim | Correct scope |
| --- | --- |
| Strictly feasible instances have polynomial-size rational-circuit witnesses | The circuit is efficiently constructed; ordinary polynomial-time exact validation is not claimed. |
| Some rational feasible points require exponentially many bits | This concerns expanded coordinates, with a dimension lower bound \(\Omega(n2^{n/2})\), not \(2^{\Omega(L)}\) in total input length. |
| Some positive definite ordinary Grams require long denominators | The constructed family still has a short singular PSD Gram and short rational SOS. |
| A \(\operatorname{poly}(L)2^{O(n)}\) interior-Gram upper bound holds | It uses the supplied full strict Hessian Gram and a rational point in a strict open semialgebraic set. |

These are not NP lower bounds and do not show that PosSLP lies outside P.

The [fresh singleton review](singleton-review.md) also checks the compact
two-variable example with minimum zero and unique minimizer
\((\sqrt[3]{2},\sqrt[3]{4})\). It has both a short rational polynomial SOS
and a supplied full positive definite rational Hessian Gram. Thus an exact
zero sublevel set can lack every rational point even under those strong
certificates. This addresses the rational feasible-point witness question
in Hesse's Redemption, Table 1 and Appendix C. It does not exclude short
algebraic descriptions or other witness models. The same review checks the
more general algebraic-singleton construction and records its irreducible,
one-real-root input promise.

## Developed extension and remaining boundaries

The new [variable-degree upper theorem](general-degree/strong-convex-polynomial-posslp-upper.md)
removes fixed degree from the upper reduction for sparse explicit polynomials
with unary degree bounds and supplied strong curvature. Its proof and
[independent review](general-degree/general-degree-review.md) check that
derivative bounds, polynomial-precision initialization, uniform elimination
bounds, sparse evaluation, and shared Newton circuits all remain polynomial
in the stated encoding. The original quartic lower instances give the
matching order-comparison classification in its checked certificate class.

The [rational-circuit interior-Gram corollary](general-degree/circuit-interior-gram.md)
and its [independent review](general-degree/circuit-interior-gram-review.md)
complete one further representation comparison. For the original full
strict-Hessian quartic class, polynomial time constructs a shared
rational-circuit matrix \(Q\) representing \(f\). It is positive definite
exactly when \(\min f>0\). The degree-six observable
\(f-\|\nabla f\|^2/(2\mu)\) selects a circuit-encoded rational Taylor
center before any PosSLP query. This supplies a short circuit Gram even for
the families requiring exponentially long expanded positive definite
Grams. It does not promise short unweighted rational SOS factors or
polynomial-time ordinary exact validation of the circuit certificate.

This extension does not cover unrestricted sparse binary-degree input,
missing curvature information, exact constrained optimization, or a general
matching upper bound for rational SOS at a zero minimum. These remain
different questions. Completing this audit and extension does not justify
calling those questions solved.

Two presentation clarifications from the dependency audits are useful but
do not change any theorem: state the full Frobenius/operator norm bound
before the signed-root Jacobian singular-value estimate, and use “vector
approximation error” for the quaternion rounding errors. Existing constants
already suffice.

The September exact upper note already cites Hesse's Redemption and
distinguishes it from exact sign comparison. A claim that this entire core
had not considered that paper would be inaccurate. The separate globally
convex point-approximation theorem needs its own literature comparison.
