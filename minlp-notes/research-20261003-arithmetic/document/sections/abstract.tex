\begin{abstract}
Polynomial optimization has several distinct output problems: an objective
gap, distance to the optimizer set, approximation of one fixed optimizer,
exact comparison, and an exact representation. We develop these distinctions
in one bit-complexity framework. For sparse globally convex polynomials over
arbitrary rational polyhedra, we give an effective global error bound and a
minimum-norm point oracle polynomial in input length, numerical degree, and
requested precision. For residual-convex cubics on product boxes, finite
noise on the nonconvex core supports full selected-point approximation at
every precision without a joint core convexifier. For strongly convex
quartics under arbitrary rational linear constraints, exact comparison is
ordinary fixed-parameter tractable in the nonlinear dimension of the
objective. Structured box and network-flow problems admit deterministic
polynomial-time comparison with a PosSLP oracle. An unconstrained exact upper
reduction extends to unary degree, and a circuit representation gives short
interior Grams despite exponential expanded-output lower bounds. We combine
these extensions with audited point-hardness and exact-quartic results,
identify their dependencies on prior work, and state the remaining
unrestricted constrained comparison problem precisely.
\end{abstract}
