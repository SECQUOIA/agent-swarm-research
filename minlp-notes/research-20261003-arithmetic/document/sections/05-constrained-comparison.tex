\section{Exact comparison with linear constraints}
\label{sec:constrained}

Arbitrary linear constraints leave a gap in the exact classification.
For a globally strongly convex rational quartic over a rational
polyhedron, the current general bound in this research program is
$\mathrm{UP}^{\PosSLP}\cap\mathrm{coUP}^{\PosSLP}$: the full active
set is an unambiguous certificate, but finding it deterministically is
unresolved. The
\href{../../research-20260927/polyhedral-strong-quartic-unambiguous-upper.md}{active-set proof}
checks the gradient inequality without guessing a multiplier support.
This section gives three positive extensions with different structural
assumptions. They do not establish deterministic
$\mathrm P^{\PosSLP}$ for arbitrary constrained quartics.

\subsection{Few nonlinear directions, arbitrary constraints}

Write $f=q+r$, where $q$ has degree at most two and $r$ consists of the
cubic and quartic monomials. Define
\begin{equation}
 K_f=\{v:D_vr\equiv0\},\qquad k=n-\dim K_f.
 \label{eq:nonlinear-dimension}
\end{equation}
We call $k$ the nonlinear dimension. It is the rank of the coefficient
matrix of $\nabla r$: each row collects the coefficients of one monomial
across the gradient components. Rational linear algebra therefore computes
$k$ and a full-row-rank matrix $U\in\Q^{k\times n}$ such that
$r(x)=g(Ux)$. A rational right inverse $V$ gives $g(t)=r(Vt)$.
These operations have polynomial bit cost for quartics. This is the
essential-variable construction, applied to the cubic and quartic pieces;
its homogeneous antecedent is \citet[Proposition~1]{Carlini2006}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact comparison in nonlinear dimension]
\label{thm:nonlinear-dimension}
Let $P=\{x:Ax\le b\}$ be an explicit rational polyhedron, and let
$f,h\in\Q[x]$ have degree at most four. Supply a rational $\mu>0$
with the promise $\nabla^2f(x)\succeq\mu I$ for every $x\in\R^n$.
If $L$ is the total input length and $k$ is
\eqref{eq:nonlinear-dimension}, a deterministic ordinary bit algorithm
runs in $F(k)L^C$, for a computable $F$ and an absolute $C$, and either
reports $P$ empty or decides the sign of $h(p)$ at its unique minimizer
$p$. No exact-comparison oracle is used. The algorithm also recovers the
full active set and describes $p$ as a rational affine image of the
unique stationary point of a strongly convex rational quartic in at
most $k$ variables.
\end{theorem}

The constraints may have full rank, redundant rows, empty interior, or
unbounded feasible sets. The curvature assumption is a promise; a rational
positive semidefinite Gram for
$v^{\mathsf T}(\nabla^2f(x)-\mu I)v$ supplies a checkable subclass.
This does not claim that every strongly convex quartic has such a Gram.
The exact optimizer description is implicit and need not have rational
coordinates. The proof and coefficient bookkeeping appear in the
\href{../constrained-exact/theorem.md}{nonlinear-dimension companion}.

\paragraph{Elimination on the unknown optimal face.}
Let $S$ be all tight rows at $p$. Polyhedral first-order optimality
implies that $p$ uniquely minimizes $f$ on the affine space
$E=\{x:A_Sx=b_S\}$. Choose a rational chart $x=\bar x+Zy$.
Splitting $y$ along the kernel of $UZ$ gives coordinates $(t,w)$ with
$s=\dim t=\operatorname{rank}(UZ)\le k$, in which
\begin{equation}
 f(\bar x+ZTt+ZWw)
 =\tfrac12 w^{\mathsf T}Hw+w^{\mathsf T}(Jt+a)+\psi(t).
 \label{eq:quadratic-directions}
\end{equation}
The nonlinear part is independent of $w$, and
$H\succeq\mu(ZW)^{\mathsf T}(ZW)\succ0$ when this block is nonempty.
Thus $w=-H^{-1}(Jt+a)$ and
\[
 p=u+Dt_*,\qquad
 \phi(t)=f(u+Dt),\qquad \eta(t)=h(u+Dt),
\]
where $D$ has full column rank and $t_*$ uniquely solves
$\nabla\phi(t)=0$. The reduced objective remains globally strongly
convex. All coefficients have uniformly polynomial bit length in $L$:
only a fixed number of rational linear-algebra and degree-four substitution
steps are used, on an independent subset of the original rows.
The unknown face establishes this bound; it is not guessed or enumerated.

\paragraph{Parameter-dependent separation, then ordinary approximation.}
The formula
\[
 \exists t\in\R^s:\quad \nabla\phi(t)=0,\qquad z=\eta(t)
\]
defines the singleton $\{h(p)\}$. The coefficient clause of
\citet[Theorem~2.27]{Basu2014}
bounds output coefficient bits linearly in the input coefficient-bit
bound, multiplied by a function of $s$ for fixed degree. Consequently,
for effective $G$ and an absolute polynomial $T$,
\begin{equation}
 h(p)\ne0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 |h(p)|\ge\gamma:=2^{-G(k)T(L)}.
 \label{eq:nonlinear-dimension-gap}
\end{equation}
Indeed, a nonzero integer polynomial must vanish at a singleton defined
by sign conditions. Removing a power of its variable and comparing its
nonzero constant term with the other coefficients gives the root bound.
This reasoning neither executes elimination nor assumes a finite complex
critical locus. No factor $L^{O(k)}$ enters the coefficient bound.

A polynomial-bit rational feasible point and strong convexity give a
polynomial-bit radius containing $p$. Let $B\ge1$ bound
$\norm{\nabla h}$ on its unit enlargement. Set
\[
 \delta=\min\{1,\gamma/(8B)\},\qquad
 \varepsilon=\min\{1,\mu\delta^2/2\}.
\]
Corollary~1.2 of \citet{SlotSteurerWiedmer2025} produces a rational
$\widehat x\in P$ with objective error at most $\varepsilon$ in time
polynomial in $L+\log(1/\varepsilon)$. Constrained strong convexity gives
$\norm{\widehat x-p}\le\delta$, hence
$|h(\widehat x)-h(p)|\le\gamma/8$. Comparing the rational value with
$\pm\gamma/2$ decides its exact sign, including zero, in ordinary
$F(k)L^C$ bit time. Applying this procedure to every slack recovers $S$;
then \eqref{eq:quadratic-directions} constructs the implicit optimizer.

For example, $f(x)=\tfrac12x^{\mathsf T}Qx+c^{\mathsf T}x+(a^{\mathsf T}x)^4$
with $Q\succeq I$ has $k\le1$, while its quadratic term and constraint
matrix may have full rank. This differs from bounding constraint rank
or the rank of the whole objective.

The
\href{../../research-20260927/mixed-linear-strong-quartic-candidate-list.md}{existing mixed-linear candidate-list theorem}
also gives a consequence under the same global joint strong-convexity
promise: with $t$ integer variables, exact selection is ordinary FPT in
$k+t$. The list contains every optimal integer block in
parameter-polynomial size. Fixing such a block leaves nonlinear dimension
at most $k$. Two fiber minima are compared on the product polyhedron using
the sum objective and difference observable, whose nonlinear dimension is
at most $2k$. This uses the candidate-list theorem as an imported integer
search result; it does not supply a new lattice algorithm.

\subsection{A transfer principle from exact quadratic subproblems}

A second route retains unrestricted nonlinear dimension but requires a
suitable arithmetic algorithm for every Taylor quadratic. Let $P$ be a
nonempty bounded rational polyhedron with a supplied rational bounding box.
For $x\in P$, define
\begin{equation}
 N(x)=\argmin_{y\in P}
 \left\{\nabla f(x)^{\mathsf T}(y-x)
 +\tfrac12(y-x)^{\mathsf T}\nabla^2f(x)(y-x)\right\}.
 \label{eq:constrained-newton}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact-QP arithmetic transfer]
\label{thm:constrained-newton-transfer}
Suppose $f$ is an explicit globally $\mu$-strongly convex polynomial of
fixed degree. Assume \eqref{eq:constrained-newton} can be solved exactly
using polynomially many rational arithmetic operations and comparisons,
with a bound polynomial in the original instance length and independent
of the expanded encoding lengths of $x$ and its Taylor coefficients.
Then exact fixed-degree polynomial-observable comparisons at the minimizer
of $f$ over $P$ belong to deterministic $\mathrm P^{\PosSLP}$.
\end{theorem}

Only addition, subtraction, multiplication, division by nonzero quantities,
and comparisons are permitted in this interface. A polynomial
bit-complexity QP algorithm alone does not meet it.

Let $M$ be a polynomial-bit bound on Hessian Lipschitz variation on the
bounding box and set $K=\max\{1,M/(2\mu)\}$. The first-order inequalities
for $N(x)$ and $p$ give
\[
 \norm{N(x)-p}\le\frac{M}{2\mu}\norm{x-p}^2.
\]
This is the established exact proximal Newton estimate
\citep[Theorem~3.4]{LeeSunSaunders2014}, specialized to the indicator of
$P$. It requires neither strict complementarity nor a previously identified
face. A feasible warm start of objective error at most
$\min\{1,\mu/(8K^2)\}$ therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \norm{x_j-p}\le K^{-1}2^{-2^j},\qquad x_{j+1}=N(x_j).
 \label{eq:constrained-newton-rate}
\end{equation}

Retain every rational operation as a shared numerator-denominator circuit.
The assumed QP algorithm then adds polynomially many gates per step;
$\PosSLP$ answers its comparisons without expanding the fractions.
The original rational KKT system, augmented by $z=h(x)$, has singleton
real projection $\{h(p)\}$. One-block elimination gives a uniform
alternative $h(p)=0$ or $|h(p)|\ge2^{-2^{a(L)}}$ for an effective
polynomial $a$. Polynomially many steps in
\eqref{eq:constrained-newton-rate} make the observable error less than one
quarter of that gap, after absorbing a polynomial-bit bound on
$\norm{\nabla h}$ on the box.
Repeated squaring represents the gap, and final circuit comparisons
identify the sign. QP decisions depend on oracle answers, so this is a
Turing reduction, not the single-instance reduction of
Section~\ref{sec:exact-arithmetic}. The
\href{../constrained-exact/structural-newton.md}{transfer companion}
provides the complete constants and circuit construction.

\subsection{Boxes with structured Hessians}

For a symmetric matrix $H$, let its comparison matrix be
$B_{ii}=H_{ii}$ and $B_{ij}=-|H_{ij}|$ for $i\ne j$.

\begin{corollary}[Structured boxes]
\label{cor:structured-boxes}
Let $f$ satisfy the preceding global curvature promise and have fixed
degree. Empty boxes are detected first. On a nonempty rational box $P$, suppose the comparison matrix of
$\nabla^2f(x)$ is positive definite for every $x\in P$. Exact fixed-degree
observable comparisons and recovery of all active bounds are in
$\mathrm P^{\PosSLP}$. Sufficient cases are a supplied forest containing
all Hessian interactions, or nonpositive off-diagonal Hessian entries
throughout $P$. The dimension and number of active bounds are unrestricted.
\end{corollary}

\citet[Algorithm~I and Proposition~2.1]{PangHan2023} solve the box QP in
at most $2n$ pivots given $v>0$ satisfying
$H_{JJ}^{-1}v_J\ge0$ for every principal subset $J$. Degeneracy is
allowed. If $B\succ0$, their comparison-matrix construction gives
\[
 d=B^{-1}\mathbf1>0,\qquad v=(H+B)d/2\ge\mathbf1.
\]
It uses rational linear systems and sign tests only. For a forest, changing
coordinate signs along each tree makes $B$ congruent to $H$, so $B\succ0$.
For a positive definite matrix with nonpositive off-diagonal entries,
all principal inverses are nonnegative and $v=\mathbf1$ suffices.
The transfer theorem now applies to circuit-valued Hessians and costs.

The structural promises are separate from their recognition. Missing
forest interactions are checked by coefficient identities; no general
polynomial-time recognition of the other promises is asserted. Dense
graph objectives
$\frac\mu2\sum_i x_i^2+\sum_{ij}w_{ij}(x_i-x_j)^4+c^{\mathsf T}x$,
with $w_{ij}\ge0$, supply an explicit family satisfying the second condition.
Its box constraint rank may be $n$.

\subsection{Separable polynomial flows}

\begin{corollary}[Network-flow polyhedra]
\label{cor:polynomial-flow}
Let $A$ be a directed node-arc incidence matrix and
$P=\{x:Ax=b,\ \ell\le x\le u\}$ have rational data. Let
$f(x)=\sum_e f_e(x_e)$, with each $f_e$ an explicit polynomial of degree at most four and a supplied
rational $\mu>0$ satisfying $f_e''\ge\mu$ on $\R$. After testing rational
feasibility, exact value,
coordinate, and fixed-degree observable comparisons belong to deterministic
$\mathrm P^{\PosSLP}$. Constraint rank is unrestricted; redundant balance
rows, fixed arcs, and infinite capacity endpoints are allowed.
\end{corollary}

For finite capacities, the Taylor subproblem is a separable quadratic flow
with arc cost
\[
 \tfrac12 f_e''(x_e)y_e^2+
       \bigl(f_e'(x_e)-f_e''(x_e)x_e\bigr)y_e.
\]
\citet[Theorem~20]{Vegh2016} gives a strongly polynomial exact quadratic-flow
algorithm using elementary rational arithmetic and comparisons, including
capacitated instances. Its operation count is independent of coefficient
encoding length, which supplies the transfer interface. Capacity gadgets
and zero-cost auxiliary arcs are included in that source. The
\href{../constrained-exact/network-flow-review.md}{source-interface review}
also bounds artificial-arc costs by rational circuits, avoiding any use of
expanded coefficient lengths. For quartics, the univariate curvature
promise itself is checkable by rational quadratic nonnegativity tests.

Infinite endpoints in both corollaries reduce to finite ones. Find a
polynomial-bit rational feasible point $\bar x$. Strong convexity and
$f(p)\le f(\bar x)$ give
\[
 \norm{p-\bar x}\le2\norm{\nabla f(\bar x)}_1/\mu.
\]
Intersect the bounds with the coordinate box of this radius plus one
around $\bar x$. It has polynomial encoding length, preserves the optimizer,
and introduces no active artificial bound. The
\href{../constrained-exact/network-flow.md}{flow companion}
also proves the extension to every fixed polynomial degree. None of these
results promises rational coordinates for the exact optimizer.

\subsection{The remaining general boundary}

The unresolved step in the Newton route is exact QP with an arbitrary
circuit Hessian over an arbitrary rational polyhedron. Known QP arithmetic
bounds that retain dependence on expanded Hessian encoding do not supply
the transfer hypothesis. The hypothesis is sufficient; no equivalence with
strongly polynomial QP is claimed.

A direct penalty argument does not bypass this issue. For
$\min_{x\ge0}(x+1)^2/2$, the squared-hinge penalty with coefficient
$\lambda/2$ has minimizer $-1/(1+\lambda)$. Reaching error $g$ requires
$\lambda\ge g^{-1}-1$. A short circuit for that coefficient does not make
it an explicitly encoded polynomial-bit coefficient for a warm-start
algorithm. Likewise, an iteration bound polynomial in
$\log(1/\varepsilon)$ becomes exponential when
$\varepsilon=2^{-2^{a(L)}}$. These are failed interfaces, not hardness
results. The
\href{../constrained-exact/general-boundary.md}{boundary note}
records their precise scope.

The essential-variable construction, Newton recurrence, box QP, and flow QP
are established ingredients. The additional statements here are their
specified parameterized exact-sign and arithmetic-circuit consequences.
The \href{../literature/constrained-prior.md}{primary-source comparison}
and \href{../constrained-exact/verification.md}{targeted verification record}
separate those claims from their dependencies and document independent
internal review. They do not establish publication priority.
