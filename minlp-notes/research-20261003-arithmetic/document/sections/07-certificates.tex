\section{Certificates and exact representations}
\label{sec:certificates}

An exact witness is useful only with a specified representation and checking
contract. Section~\ref{sec:exact-arithmetic} separates expanded rational
coordinates from shared arithmetic circuits. The same distinction applies to
positive definite Gram matrices: an exponential lower bound on expanded
denominator lengths can coexist with a polynomial-size shared circuit.

\subsection{A compact interior Gram without a sign oracle}

\begin{theorem}[Circuit representation of an interior Gram]
\label{thm:circuit-gram}
Let $f\in\Q[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$, $n\ge1$, have degree at most four.
Suppose its input includes a full rational positive definite matrix $A$ with
\[
 v^{\mathsf T}\nabla^2f(X)v
  =(v,X\otimes v)^{\mathsf T}A(v,X\otimes v).
\]
Let $z_2(X)$ list every monomial of degree at most two. A deterministic
polynomial-time algorithm constructs a symmetric shared rational-circuit
matrix $Q$ such that
\[
 f(X)=z_2(X)^{\mathsf T}Qz_2(X),\qquad
 Q\succ0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \min_{\R^n} f>0.
\]
The identity and all divisions in the construction are valid on every valid
input, independently of the sign of the minimum. Construction requires
neither a sign oracle nor a supplied positive objective margin.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $m=n+n^2$ and $\mu=\det(A)/(\operatorname{tr}A)^{m-1}$, so
$A\succeq\mu I$. The explicit observable
\[
 h(X)=f(X)-\frac{\norm{\nabla f(X)}^2}{2\mu}
\]
has degree at most six and polynomial encoding length. If $p$ is the unique
minimizer, then $h(p)=f(p)$. The constructive upper reduction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-upper}, stopped before its final sign query, gives a
rational-circuit vector $a$ for which
\[
 |h(a)-h(p)|\le g/8,
 \qquad h(p)\ne0\ \Longrightarrow\ |h(p)|\ge g.
\]
Thus $c=h(a)>0$ whenever $f(p)>0$. No sign of $c$ is tested.

Set $d=X-a$, $b=\nabla f(a)$, and
$\bar A=A-\mu\operatorname{diag}(I_n,0)$. Define coefficient matrices by
\[
 (d,a\otimes d)=C_Uz_2,\qquad
 (0,d\otimes d)=C_Vz_2,\qquad
 d+b/\mu=C_{\rm aff}z_2.
\]
Their dimensions and their shared circuit sizes are polynomial in the
original input length. If $e_0$ selects the constant monomial, put
\begin{align*}
 Q={}&\tfrac12(C_U+C_V/3)^{\mathsf T}\bar A(C_U+C_V/3)
       +\tfrac1{36}C_V^{\mathsf T}\bar A C_V\\
    &+\tfrac\mu2 C_{\rm aff}^{\mathsf T}C_{\rm aff}
       +c e_0e_0^{\mathsf T}.
\end{align*}
Taylor's integral identity and completion of the gradient square give
$f=z_2^{\mathsf T}Qz_2$ exactly. In particular, $a$ denotes an exact
rational vector even though it approximates an optimizer.

Since $\bar A\succeq\mu\operatorname{diag}(0,I_{n^2})$, the first
three summands are positive semidefinite. When $c>0$, the second summand
controls all translated homogeneous quadratics $d_id_j$, the third controls
the affine factors $d_i+b_i/\mu$, and the last controls the constant.
Those polynomials span the full space of degree at most two. Their Gram
sum is therefore positive definite. Conversely, $Q\succ0$ would imply
$f(X)\ge\lambda_{\min}(Q)\norm{z_2(X)}^2\ge\lambda_{\min}(Q)>0$.
This proves the equivalence. Every matrix operation appends polynomially
many gates while retaining the shared circuit for $a$.
\end{proof}

The \href{../reviews/exact-core/general-degree/circuit-interior-gram.md}{complete
companion argument} and its
\href{../reviews/exact-core/general-degree/circuit-interior-gram-review.md}{independent
review} give the exact Taylor constants and representation contract.
The expanded positive definite Gram lower bound in
Theorem~\ref{thm:expanded-witnesses} still applies. The entries of the
circuit matrix can still require exponentially many bits when expanded.

It also does not give ordinary polynomial-time verification of
circuit-encoded identities or positive definiteness. In fact the equivalence
links positive definiteness of this constructed circuit matrix to exact
optimization. Nor does it produce a polynomial-size unweighted rational SOS
factorization: decomposing circuit-encoded positive weights into rational
squares is a separate task.

\subsection{A reference checker for the three approximation contracts}

The \href{../implementation/README.md}{reference implementation} makes the
output distinctions executable with exact rational arithmetic. It accepts a
sparse rational polynomial, a rational box, optional additional rational
linear inequalities, and an explicit identity
\[
 f(x)=a^{\mathsf T}x+b+
           \sum_j w_j(u_j^{\mathsf T}x+v_j)^{2d_j},
 \qquad w_j\ge0,\quad d_j\ge1.
\]
Coefficient comparison verifies the identity and hence global convexity.
This is a sufficient certificate language; general convexity recognition is
not included. Expanding a succinct factored identity can be expensive, so no
polynomial complexity claim in the factored encoding is made.

A witness provides a feasible candidate and a tangent anchor. The checker
forms a supporting affine function and minimizes it over the box to obtain
a justified lower bound on the constrained optimum. It accepts no asserted
optimal value. Its three entry points then check, respectively:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an objective-gap certificate;
\item distance to the optimizer set using an error-bound constant derived
      from the checked polynomial and domain;
\item distance to the fixed minimum-norm optimizer, using a checked
      regularized objective and the effective selector schedule.
\end{enumerate}
The gradient coefficient matrix is extracted exactly; its constant row is
retained so that an affine slope is not mistaken for an invariant direction.
Every comparison uses rational arithmetic. Floating-point inputs are
rejected.

These checkers are sound but incomplete even in their supported class. A box
tangent may be too weak for an optimum determined by an extra linear
inequality. Rejection says that the supplied certificate is insufficient;
it does not prove that the candidate is inaccurate. A regression test
records this case. The implementation searches for no optimizer or witness,
does not implement the unbounded-domain reduction, and does not solve
$\PosSLP$. Its role is to make the contract distinctions testable without
presenting a small example as a general optimization solver.

The ten focused tests cover nonunique minimizers, a tiny value gap with
large coordinate error, fixed minimum-norm selection, lower-dimensional
domains, affine objectives, incorrect convexity identities, and infeasible
points. The deterministic demonstration also shows thirteen shared
recurrence nodes whose expanded rational denominator has 4,097 bits.
These finite checks support the implementation and the displayed identities;
the proofs establish the asymptotic theorems.
