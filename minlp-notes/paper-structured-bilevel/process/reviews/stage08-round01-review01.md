## Mathematical assessment and resolved concerns

### Foundations, exact responses and Appendix A

The model separates the fixed structural dimensions from the growing follower and local-row counts, and it explicitly distinguishes numerical polynomial degree from sparse binary exponent encoding. Compactness is supplied by the leader set and explicit coordinate bounds. The output convention is a simultaneous univariate representation; it does not silently combine independently sampled algebraic extensions.

I checked the local normal-cone argument in `lem:local-branches` without imposing a constraint qualification. Modulo the equality row space, a dependence in the positive multiplier support can indeed be removed while retaining nonnegative coefficients. The surviving normals form one of the enumerated independent subsets. Positive definiteness then makes the bordered KKT matrix nonsingular. Squaring its determinant and multiplying Cramer numerators by the determinant is a sound positive-denominator representation. Feasibility of every original local row is retained, including dependent equality rows.

For `lem:moving-compression`, an equality subset need not span all equality rows for soundness, because the branch is separately tested against all original rows. Completeness does require a basis at the particular leader, which the enumeration supplies. The determinant guard is indispensable and is present. The example `xz=0` correctly exercises the rank-zero stratum and the failure of optimistic attainment when normals move.

The central complexity argument is sound: the algorithm enumerates realizable signs in the fixed coordinates `(x,w,lambda,mu)`, not the Cartesian product of block choices. A sign conjunction may be disconnected, but branch validity is constant on it. The product denominator grows in degree with the number of blocks; expansion still has polynomial size because the polynomial variable dimension remains fixed. Explicit upper monomial substitution respects this distinction. The degree bounds are consistent with the polynomial compositions in the effective linear cost.

The global comparison predicate compares actual values of feasible candidates, and a true global minimizer always has a candidate representation. Consequently it removes every nonglobal KKT point while retaining every global response and tie. It does not need sufficiency of the nonconvex follower KKT conditions. Replacing its quantified subformula by a quantifier-free formula before copying it into upper optimization prevents hidden growth in the number of variables.

The attainment and pessimistic constructions are also correct. Fixed-normal Hoffman repair proves the closed global-response graph along feasible leader sequences; it does not require all leaders to be follower feasible. Pessimistic feasibility is tested universally over the original optimal-response set before maximizing the upper objective. The maximum at each leader exists, but the leader infimum can be unattained. The endpoint formula explicitly distinguishes these cases. Both nonattainment examples have the stated solution sets and values.

I checked the constant-Hessian refinement against the actual Basu–Pollack–Roy source: the degree of the output polynomials and of simultaneous sample representations can be bounded independently of the number of input polynomials. The manuscript also controls the fixed number of successive eliminations. Thus the refined field degree does not inadvertently acquire a follower-count factor. Coefficient and total output lengths may still grow, as the text correctly states.

Appendix A supplies a complete related theorem rather than assuming an algebraic LP oracle. Local vertex enumeration includes lower-dimensional polytopes because a vertex of a bounded polyhedron still has a full-rank active row subset. The universal support condition excludes empty blocks and characterizes membership in the compact convex Minkowski sum. The recovery filter is deliberately broader than realization of the old sign condition at the sampled core: retaining any feasible tuple is safe, and for every support direction the actually realized maximizing tuple survives. Therefore the retained aggregate hull is exact. Enumerating affinely independent subsets of at most `h+1` points gives weights in the already sampled field; applying the same weights in every original block preserves all measurements and the objective. This operation is not used to convexify a nonconvex response set.

The supplied low-rank SPD LP corollary correctly uses weak cell closures, includes all threshold faces, and needs neither positive semidefiniteness nor invertibility of the small coupling matrix.

### Robustness and dense screening

The measurement-fiber threshold lemma is valid even for nonconvex nominal followers. A global fiber minimizer gives the required candidate, while any feasible fiber candidate under the *true nominal* cost budget already witnesses attainability of its measurement. Globality is essential for the nominal value but is not needed again to certify that witness. Each upper criterion is eliminated separately before conjunction, so fixing measurement dimension per criterion is enough even when the union of measurement rows has growing rank. The common-field guarantee is correctly limited to one jointly selected adverse witness or separate requested witnesses.

The convex fixed-normal robustness attainment proof handles a zero budget by nominal-response uniqueness, and a positive budget by interpolation to the nominal minimizer followed by Hoffman repair. The polynomial positive-budget counterexample establishes that uniqueness and a positive budget do not replace convexity. The Max-Cut boundary has the correct NP/coNP directions and uses a budget large enough to make the entire unit cube near-optimal.

For screening, I rederived the summed variational inequalities and the completed-square center `-Q^{-1}Ey/2`. Both coordinate and gradient enclosures have the stated signed centers and radii. The strict gradient tests are not confused with zero-gradient bound points. The relative-interior refinements extend a valid stationarity or bound label to the cell closure by continuity; their strict-somewhere conditions are necessary and present. Exact recovery includes every ambiguous status assignment and uses the true dense Hessian. The `M 3^t` count is consistently a recovery-LP count, not the total cost of preprocessing.

The transition-neighborhood proof uses injectivity of the surrogate graph projection to bound cell affine dimension by the leader dimension. The cover by all small vertex simplices need not be a triangulation, and overlap is harmless. The perturbation radius makes both response and gradient errors at most half the explicit margin. Inner and outer objective/feasibility enclosures have the correct directions. Empty inner sets are never called original-infeasibility certificates.

### Accuracy and Appendices B–C

I followed the complete proof chain: effective inverse modulus; critical-value construction and analytic panels; rational Taylor reversion; fixed-resource projection; bounded multipliers and repair; polynomial growth; frozen-gradient residual bounds; branch coverage; rational recovery; upper objective optimization; upper-row qualifications.

Appendix B protects against nonreal critical values, constructs centers from rational *response* points, and accounts for intermediate coefficient encodings. The inverse branch on the complex disk follows from properness and the covering argument, not just a local inverse on a real interval. Its constant bad-region branches and analytic branches both remain valid at shared endpoints. The positive-coefficient argument is explicitly separated from the signed-marginal theorem.

Appendix C's support-ray projection includes coordinate hyperplanes and rank-deficient cases. The multiplier and repair estimates use independent active normals and integer Gram cofactors and do not rely on Slater feasibility. The polynomial Bregman bound remains valid in both response directions. The stronger `1/P` response modulus uses a correction tangent to a common active pattern; the gradient cancellation produces the improved exponent. A bound on the number of patterns alone would not suffice along a segment, but the proof also bounds connected components of each pattern using a real algebraic lift and then partitions at their endpoints. Its potentially exponential numerical constant has polynomial encoding length. The one-power example proves sharpness of the exponent.

The main approximation algorithm does not round into a nonlinear branch region without justification: it rounds into the true rational base polytope and compares the continuous clipped inverses across possible branch changes. Its complementarity transfer uses an absolute residual bound, including large inactive slacks. Degree multiplication for upper monomial substitution includes the aggregate inverse-argument degree. The optimum-value and leader-error ledgers are consistent. Outer, inner, tightening, convex-anchor and reserve statements preserve their distinct guarantees. The isolated-feasible-optimum and irrational-only-leader examples show why these qualifications are substantive rather than optional.

### Boundaries and Appendix D

I checked both dense reductions separately. The ternary Boolean-response construction has a margin large enough to dominate the auxiliary feedback, and its conditional shortfall variables give the zero-versus-two upper gap without upper rows. The near-identity construction instead controls *relative scaled coordinates*; a small absolute error would not suffice. Its stated gap, coefficient bounds, norm bounds, identity proximity and accuracy-bit consequence follow. The text correctly does not infer strong hardness from bounded coefficients and an exponentially small gap.

The conditioned grid saturates costs first, uses a maximal minor to control all remaining row combinations, and minimizes the direct leader cost inside each grid region. The exact rational follower oracle's denominator bound concerns determinants, and its projected-gradient precision is sufficient for continued-fraction reconstruction. Its inverse-error dependence is compatible with the preceding hardness.

The growing-leader classification is attributed, while the mixed-radix alternative contains its own continuous rounding gap. The bounded-core argument retains candidate feasibility at cell boundaries and does not confuse this with optimality of that particular candidate at every boundary. The two path constructions are explicitly different graphs. Subset Sum messages are exponential representations, not automatic hardness of every identical-factor instance.

For the follower path, the telescoping polynomial is nonnegative precisely with vertex zeros, the parabola exposes each vertex, and the small quadratic perturbation preserves exact vertex response intervals. Padding forces lower bits at the inserted positions and keeps the local alphabet fixed while allowing binary data elsewhere. The nonconvex upper row is explicitly retained in the hardness statement; deleting it does not preserve the reduction. The sparse-shadow factor argument applies only to unextended two-variable descriptions. Equal-gain strips, zero edges, retained states, and fixed *total* cycle rank have complete projection and same-field recovery proofs. The arithmetic examples distinguish common-field size, explicit rational output, and the unresolved complexity of exact Square Root Sum comparison.

They did not reveal a result establishing the exact combination of hypotheses and output guarantees claimed here. This is bounded evidence, not proof of universal absence. A newly surfaced 2026 publisher page about pessimistic coupling constraints could not be opened; I do not rely on it for a claim about its full results.

The final introduction now states precisely what is claimed: complete exact quadratic-block and global accuracy-bit guarantees for unbounded follower dimension under specified fixed shared dimensions, with constructive output and selection qualifications. It explicitly credits global polynomial comparisons, multiplier expressions, support reduction, prior inverse and allocation approximation, near-optimal robustness, the unconstrained `1/P` exponent predecessor, and earlier envelope algorithms. The table warns that the models of cited work are not all subsumed. I find the qualified novelty statement justified by the examined evidence and substantially more informative than a generic assertion of a new method.
