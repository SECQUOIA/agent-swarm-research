## Robustness and algebraic output

**The thresholded measurement-fiber argument is exact** (`sections/03-robustness-screening.tex:63–175`). A near-optimal point implies that the minimum of its compact measurement fiber meets the same budget. A fiber minimizer has a compressed polyhedral KKT representation, including for nonconvex nominal costs. Conversely, any feasible fiber KKT candidate below the true nominal threshold is itself near-optimal. This converse requires feasibility and the cost inequality, not global optimality on that fiber. The proof correctly retains global comparison for the nominal value graph. It therefore neither replaces the nominal minimum by a stationary value nor discards nonstationary near-optimal responses.

The bounded measurement domain is constructible with polynomial coefficient-bit length from the compact leader domain and explicit coordinate bounds. Adding its fixed number of equality rows preserves the stage 2 compression hypotheses, including moving normals and potentially empty fibers. The rational outward measurement bounds do not introduce new algebraic fields into the input formulas.

**The quantified dimension and witness accounting are correct** (`sections/03-robustness-screening.tex:127–174`). Each criterion's attainable-value and violation predicates are eliminated separately before their conjunction. The growing number of criteria therefore increases formula length and the number of independent computations, not the number of simultaneously quantified variables. The robust domain shares one nominal-value variable. The worst-value graph, bounded attainable-value set, infimum, and attainment test then use only fixed-size copies of eliminated predicates. Recovery adds one uneliminated thresholded fiber candidate for the objective. Its decoded original follower has the correct measurement, meets the true cost budget, and attains the actual worst criterion. Separate witness encodings for many requested criteria avoid an unjustified common-field compositum claim.

**The new constant-local-matrix refinement is valid** (`sections/03-robustness-screening.tex:184–209`). The local saddle matrix depends on `Q_b,E_b,G_b`, which are constant under the corollary's hypotheses. Moving shared normals and measurement matrices appear in the effective linear costs and consistency rows, not in those saddle matrices. Local decoding is therefore polynomial in the fixed compressed coordinates with rational constant denominators. The degrees of all candidate, cost-threshold, and criterion equations are bounded in terms of input degree and the fixed dimensions, independent of follower/criterion counts. Criterion-by-criterion elimination does not accumulate algebraic extensions: its outputs remain rational polynomial formulas. Each dependency chain contains a fixed number of eliminations and one joint sampling operation. The degree bounds used in the accepted stage 2 corollary consequently apply, even though the total number of independent criterion computations grows. The refinement does not claim attainment or one joint field for unrelated witnesses.

**The attainment and boundary statements are correct** (`sections/03-robustness-screening.tex:213–320`). The follower-feasible leader set is compact. Fixed normals supply the required Hoffman repair along feasible leaders. Convex aggregate cost plus positive local curvature gives a unique continuous nominal minimizer and continuous value. At positive budget, mixing toward the nominal minimizer creates strict budget slack before repair; at zero budget, nearby nominal minimizers approximate the singleton response. These arguments establish the continuity of criterion maxima and robust attainment without assuming the budget is bounded away from zero.

The positive-budget example has the stated factorization and derivative. Its isolated response at `z=1` disappears immediately above the limiting leader, yielding both the nonclosed robust feasible set and the unconstrained unattained objective infimum. The inequalities on `0<z<=a<1/3` are sufficient for the strict objective lower bound. The Max-Cut transfer uses a budget containing the entire cube, a convex quadratic whose maximum occurs at a binary vertex, and threshold `K-1` for the complement. This proves the stated NP-hard value task and restricted coNP-complete universal-feasibility task. It does not claim hardness for growing collections of individually low-dimensional affine criteria.

## Screening, exact recovery, and the simplex refinement

**The enclosure and whole-cell tests are sound** (`sections/03-robustness-screening.tex:358–464`). Combining the two variational inequalities has the stated sign. Completing the square in the true Hessian gives the directional ellipsoid. Coordinate and gradient choices produce the stated centers and radii. The cell-wide quadratic maximum occurs at a vertex because it is convex, and rational vertex enumeration handles affine-hull degeneracy. The strict gradient tests imply the correct bound statuses. The `F` label is consistently a zero-gradient equation and permits bound points.

The closure refinements correctly require nonnegative affine margins and strict positivity somewhere. Such a margin is positive throughout relative interior, so continuity of the true response and gradient extends the status certificate to the closed cell. Identically zero margins and singleton cases are not incorrectly certified. The text does not assume every ellipsoid point is a realizable response.

**The recovery theorem has the stated scope and count** (`sections/03-robustness-screening.tex:466–524`). Once labels are fixed, every dense principal free-coordinate matrix is invertible by SPD, regardless of its dimension. Rational elimination and the true KKT rows yield a bounded rational LP over the supplied cover cell. Soundness follows from convex follower optimality; completeness assigns each ambiguous coordinate according to the actual gradient sign, with zero gradient assigned free. Certified zero-gradient bound coordinates remain covered. Thus at most `sum_P 3^(t_P) <= M 3^t` recovery LPs suffice. The theorem explicitly excludes cover construction and screening from this count while accounting for their bit complexity. A failed whole-cell guessed assignment is correctly retained for possible recovery on a smaller feasible region.

**The new overlapping-simplex cover is valid** (`sections/03-robustness-screening.tex:526–566`). Uniqueness of the surrogate response makes projection onto the leader coordinates injective on the cover polytope. A kernel direction in its affine hull would contradict that injectivity near a relative-interior point, so cell dimension is at most `r`. Carathéodory reduction then covers it by convex hulls of affinely independent original vertex subsets of size at most `r+1`. These may overlap; recovery requires a cover, not disjoint interiors. Enumeration is polynomial for fixed dimensions, and no new vertices are introduced. Each simplex's transition union therefore has size at most `(r+1)q`. Outside that union, the containing clipping cell's nominal label has the claimed strictly positive vertex margins, and affine interpolation preserves them over the simplex.

**The perturbation radius and remaining bounds are correct** (`sections/03-robustness-screening.tex:568–693`). Symmetry makes the induced infinity norms valid spectral bounds. The radius ensures positive definiteness and limits both response displacement and gradient displacement to at most half the corresponding positive margin. This certifies every nontransition nominal status throughout the simplex. The proof expressly permits an increased polynomial exponent from the simplex cover and does not claim a uniform large radius or bounded transition multiplicity. The directional inner/outer LP bounds have the correct signs for signed objectives and rows. Empty inner systems are not mistaken for true infeasibility, and equalities can be lost by the inner approximation. The complete-ambiguity example and shifted-switch example correctly distinguish conservative certificates from actual follower difficulty and show why unchanged surrogate labels are unsafe.
