## Mathematical assessment of the complete paper

### Section 1: scope, model, and prior work

The motivation identifies the right structural issue: a growing follower dimension with a fixed dimension for shared interaction. The paper distinguishes a joint optimizing follower from independent-agent equilibrium and states the optimistic and universally feasible pessimistic conventions explicitly. These conventions are consistent with later revenue maximization after sign reversal. The input model separates numerical degree from binary exponent length, and the common-field representation includes the full coordinate encoding. The XP qualification is necessary and is present.

The contribution paragraphs and comparison table are appropriately scoped. In particular, global comparisons, multiplier arrangements, sparse conic supports, approximation of allocation vectors, and scalar convex envelopes are credited as established tools. The claim concerns the combined theorem classes, their bit bounds and output guarantees. The statement about the sharp `1/P` response exponent now explicitly credits its unconstrained uniformly convex predecessor; it does not claim the exponent itself was previously unknown.

### Section 2 and Appendix A: exact compression and recovery

I checked the local KKT necessity on an arbitrary polyhedron, the support reduction modulo equalities, and the positive-denominator Cramer construction. The determinant guards correctly cover moving ranks. Enumerating signs in the shared variables avoids a Cartesian product of local active sets. Product denominators can grow in degree with the block count but have polynomial expansion size in fixed dimension; upper monomial substitution preserves the claimed numerical-degree dependence.

The global comparison tests actual feasible candidate values, so the nonconvex aggregate introduces no false global optima. Every true optimum supplies a candidate, which is the critical completeness fact. Quantifier copies remain fixed in number, including pessimistic upper-row checking and attainment decisions. Fixed normals permit Hoffman repair along feasible leader sequences; moving normals do not support the same graph-closedness claim, as the example correctly shows. The constant-local-matrix common-field degree conclusion uses degree bounds independent of the polynomial count, rather than multiplying coordinate extensions.

In Appendix A, local vertex enumeration includes lower-dimensional polytopes. Support predicates characterize the Minkowski sum exactly, and the post-sampling filter retains enough support tuples without requiring each former sign region to remain realizable. The common barycentric weights preserve every block's constraints and all appended measurements. The fixed-size recovery systems stay in the sampled field. This argument is not incorrectly applied to convex combinations of nonconvex follower optima.

### Section 3: near-optimal robustness and screening

The measurement-fiber threshold is sound even with a nonconvex nominal follower: a candidate must be feasible and below the true global value plus the budget, while completeness is supplied by a fiber minimum. Nominal stationarity alone would be insufficient, but the paper does not use it that way. Eliminating each criterion's adversary separately is essential to the per-criterion measurement bound and is done before conjunction. Witness-field claims are appropriately separate.

The fixed-normal convex robust-attainment proof treats zero budgets by uniqueness and positive budgets by mixing toward the nominal minimizer. The positive-budget counterexample correctly demonstrates the failure without convexity. The Max-Cut transfer matches the NP/coNP directions and does not confuse many separate affine rows with one arbitrary quadratic criterion.

For dense recovery, I recomputed the variational-inequality sign and the centered ellipsoid. The gradient-center and coordinate-center formulas are correct for signed vectors. Strict and relative-interior certificate conditions are used with the required strict-somewhere qualifications. Every completed status assignment is subsequently tested against the true dense KKT system, so screening cannot silently replace the follower. The `M3^t` count is explicitly a recovery-LP count; preprocessing is included separately. The simplex-cover transition argument, its polynomial-bit radius, and the negative screening example do not assert a uniform useful perturbation radius.

### Section 4 and Appendices B/C: accuracy bits

The approximation theorem's exact base-domain feasibility is distinct from upper-row feasibility. The inverse construction includes nonreal critical values, covers small bad intervals using an effective increment modulus, and uses rational response centers for rational Taylor reversion. The denominator recurrence and the analytic magnitude bound control bit length as well as degree. The positive-coefficient refinement has its own assumptions.

The resource projection uses nonnegative support rays of a fixed-dimensional arrangement. The integer Gram/cofactor estimates provide valid, albeit coarse, repair and multiplier bounds without Slater. The residual certificate includes inactive-resource slack in transferring complementarity. Across nonlinear branch boundaries the rounded point is kept in the true base polytope, and error is compared through true continuous clipped inverses; the proof does not evaluate a branch outside its validity region.

I checked the `1/P` modulus separately. Same-pattern gradients lie in the active-row span, and the row-space correction removes the moving right-hand-side displacement. Adding both Bregman inequalities and dividing only after the appropriate displacement test yields the claimed exponent. The semialgebraic component argument controls changes along the leader segment rather than simply counting formal patterns. The potentially exponential component count has polynomial logarithm, which is all the algorithm needs for its precision bound. The power example proves sharpness for this class.

The global upper-polynomial substitution accounts for both inverse-branch and aggregate-argument degrees. The error ledgers leave sufficient slack. Outer and inner feasible sets have different empty-set meanings, and the a posteriori value sandwich uses an actual inner feasible point. The tightening, convex-anchor and reserve corollaries are valid additional hypotheses. The isolated-optimum and irrational-only-feasibility examples correctly rule out stronger unconditional rational-feasibility conclusions.

### Section 5 and Appendix D: boundary proofs

The dense weighted-square reduction retains polynomial encoding despite its small weights. Its Boolean KKT margin dominates both auxiliary feedback terms, and the continuous rounding argument gives the constant gap at every exact response. NP membership follows from rational active-face systems. The multiplicative corollary explicitly gives up bounded upper coefficients when scaling the gap.

The near-identity reduction uses relative, not absolute, coordinate error after diagonal network scaling. I checked the clipped fixed-point identity, the nilpotent inverse bound, the backward-feedback matrix, and the inequality leading to `8MC theta^2`. The upper coefficients are bounded because the final scale is no larger than each coordinate scale. The yes/no gap has polynomially many encoding bits but need not be inverse polynomial numerically. Thus the accuracy-bit hardness and inverse-accuracy cost grid are consistent. Strict diagonal dominance follows from the row norm bound as stated.

For the grid, saturation remains valid at equality. Discarded coordinates contribute no response difference. A maximum-volume minor among independent columns bounds the full-row dependence coefficients. The grid pays for the widths of normalized active cost slabs, while direct leader cost is minimized by LP in each rectangle. The exact follower oracle's rational denominator bound is valid even though its numerators can contain sums of fixed-coordinate costs. Its iteration count makes continued-fraction recovery unambiguous with polynomial dependence on the numerical condition parameter.

The growing-leader transfer preserves parameter dimension, constant gap and polynomial construction size after duplication. The alternative mixed-radix argument gives an independent continuous rounding gap. The bounded-core algorithm enumerates local candidate vertices uniformly over a fixed core and handles candidates newly feasible at boundaries through completeness of the relative-cell enumeration.

The two path notions are kept separate. The leader-path objective equals the normalized distance to subset sums. The identical-factor message example proves exponential representation size, not hardness of that family's trivial global optimum. On the follower path, the telescoping polynomial forces original endpoint vertices, and the perturbation argument makes every required vertex a unique strongly convex response on an interval. Padding retains those vertices under the unary bounds and keeps the local coefficient alphabet fixed. The reduction genuinely needs its nonconvex upper quadratic row; the paper proves that deleting it makes the slab-only feasibility task trivial. The fixed alphabet does not establish strong hardness because global weights/costs still carry binary data.

The sparse-shadow proof and backward pointwise oracle are compatible. The epigraph lower bound concerns explicit unextended polynomial formulas and follows from distinct linear boundary factors; it does not rule out short circuits or descriptions with auxiliary states. Equal-gain strip projection handles sign changes, zero gains, multiple node bounds and fixed total cycle rank with same-field recovery. Opposite-gain path inequalities lie outside that positive result.

The common-field radical argument correctly establishes exponential degree and does not label Square Root Sum NP-hard. The one-power rational-output examples use their stated polynomial objective or upper row and leave the narrower affine/no-row subclass unresolved as an explicit scope boundary, not as an unproved claim.

### Section 6 and conclusions

The convex certificate mechanism validates both KKT and complete interval coverage; it does not claim numerical proposals always succeed. The aligned sweep's monotonicity follows from positive definiteness even for a negative rank-one coefficient. The nonconvex fiber construction preserves original contacts, including flat intervals and ties. Equal winning value polynomials on an open price interval have equal aggregate derivatives and therefore the same full fiber response. The upper solver treats singleton feasible intersections, endpoint limits, constant revenue and universal tie feasibility correctly.

The original-coordinate baseline's nonzero-principal-minor restriction justifies its finite complete response set. The second-order argument uses the minimal face, so it does not discard a genuine global response on a larger singular face without justification. The numerical section distinguishes finite correctness checks from theorem proofs, repeated types from heterogeneous populations, baseline limits from timeouts, and archived timings from the controlled fresh measurements. Its negative screening result and numerical tolerance discrepancy are stated candidly. Table regeneration and the diagnostic reruns support those reported contracts. The conclusions match the actual theorem hypotheses and do not assert industrial superiority or universal algorithmic completeness.

## Overall recommendation and limits

The manuscript is coherent as a long theoretical optimization paper with supporting computational specializations. The main importance is the distinction between growing primal dimension and fixed response-description dimension, together with quantitative limits that prevent overgeneralization. The exact and accuracy-bit theorems are substantial and clearly separated from their classical ingredients. The appendices provide the details needed for a reader to check the main arguments without consulting internal notes.
