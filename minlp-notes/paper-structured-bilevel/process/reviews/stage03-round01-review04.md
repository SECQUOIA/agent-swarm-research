## Robust-response findings

All locations in this section refer to `sections/03-robustness-screening.tex`.

- **Measurement threshold, lines 63–90:** minimizing on a nonempty compact measurement fiber preserves the desired measurement and can only reduce follower cost. Thus a fiber global minimizer supplies a KKT representative whenever a near-optimal point with that measurement exists. Conversely, every feasible fiber candidate under the true budget is near-optimal, whether or not it is globally optimal on its fiber. The proof correctly avoids imposing unrestricted-follower stationarity on near-optimal responses. It still computes the nominal value using the exact global response predicate.
- **Fixed-dimensional construction, lines 92–175:** measurement coordinates and their equality multipliers add only fixed dimension. The rational measurement box can be computed with polynomial bit length by fixed-dimensional optimization of the supplied polynomial data and outward rounding. Each criterion is eliminated separately before the upper rows are conjoined. A single shared nominal-value variable therefore suffices, even when the combined measurement rank grows. Empty followers are excluded by the nominal value graph. The worst-value relation and inherited infimum construction distinguish feasibility, finite value, attainment, and witnesses correctly.
- **Witnesses and budget control, lines 162–182:** a sampled thresholded fiber candidate realizes the worst criterion and has cost within the nominal global budget. Rational block decoding preserves the sampled common field. The theorem promises this field jointly for one leader/witness pair and separately for independently requested witnesses, so it does not form a compositum of an unbounded number of unrelated fields. The leader-controlled budget adds only one structural dimension. The scalar irrational adverse response is correct.
- **New degree refinement, lines 184–209:** polynomial shared and measurement normals occur in the effective linear cost and shared consistency equations, rather than in the local KKT matrix. With constant local `Q_b,E_b,G_b`, local responses therefore remain polynomials of degree bounded in terms of input degree. Sums across blocks and conjunctions across already eliminated criteria do not increase degree. The number of successive eliminations on each dependency chain is fixed, so the BPR degree bound used in the accepted constant-Hessian corollary remains applicable. The result does not promise rational output, simultaneous fields for all witnesses, or attainment.
- **Convex attainment, lines 213–255:** the feasible leader domain is compact. Fixed normals give feasible-point repair, and strict convexity gives a continuous unique nominal response and value. The near-optimal correspondence has a closed graph and the needed approximation property: mix toward the nominal minimizer when the budget is positive, and use nearby nominal minimizers at zero budget. This proves continuity of each criterion maximum and attainment on the robust feasible domain. No uniform positive lower bound on the budget is needed.
- **Positive-budget nonattainment, lines 257–292:** the factorization, derivative, and isolated endpoint at `c=rho` are correct. For `c>rho`, there is only the initial budget-sublevel interval. The derivative lower bound and `h(z)<z` give `4x+a_(rho+x)>a`, justifying the claimed unattained objective infimum as well as the nonclosed robust feasible domain. Unique nominal optima do not invalidate this example.
- **Growing measurement boundary, lines 294–319:** the chosen budget makes the whole cube near-optimal. A convex quadratic cut objective has maximum equal to a binary cut value. The threshold `K-1` correctly complements the integer decision `MaxCut >= K`, and a violating binary point certifies the complement. This proves the stated restricted coNP-completeness and adverse-value hardness without confusing many separate affine rows with one unrestricted quadratic criterion.

## Screening findings

- **Model and enclosure, lines 323–383:** the surrogate cover consists entirely of consistent surrogate responses and covers every leader. The two variational inequalities give `e^T Q e + p^T e <= 0`; completing the square yields the stated ellipsoid. The directional inequality is valid for signed vectors. Its gradient specialization uses `b=Q e_i`, which gives the stated center and half-width. The text correctly requires the exact surrogate response and does not identify the entire ellipsoid with realizable true responses.
- **Whole-cell and closure tests, lines 385–464:** the convex quadratic defining `eta_P` reaches a maximum at some vertex, so enumeration in fixed cover dimension is sufficient. All affine extrema and square-root comparisons can be checked exactly with rational arithmetic. Positive/negative true gradient forces the appropriate bound; strict interiority forces zero gradient. The closure refinements retain both nonnegativity everywhere and strict positivity somewhere. Relative-interior positivity of the affine slack and continuity of the true response extend the label to every point of the closed cell. This argument also applies in a lower-dimensional affine hull. The `F` label intentionally includes zero-gradient bound points.
- **Exact recovery, lines 466–524:** every completion is solved with the true dense Hessian. Its principal free submatrix is invertible, and exact rational elimination has polynomial bit complexity even with many free coordinates. The recovery LP includes the original upper rows and complete box KKT inequalities. Conversely every true feasible leader has a cover point and a compatible completion of the uncertified labels. Thus no true solution is lost. There are at most `M 3^t` recovery LPs, with cover construction and screening explicitly excluded from that count. A failed whole-cell guessed assignment is correctly retained for possible validity on a smaller part of the cell.
- **New overlapping simplex cover, lines 528–566:** injectivity of the leader projection on a polytope implies injectivity on its affine hull, using a relative-interior neighborhood. Cell dimension is therefore at most `r`. Convex hulls of all affinely independent vertex subsets of size at most `r+1` cover the cell by the finite convex-combination reduction. They need not form a triangulation, and overlap causes no problem for optimization over their union. Their count is polynomial for fixed dimensions, and their vertices are original cell vertices. Consequently each transition union has at most `(r+1)q` coordinates. Outside that union, the retained nominal label has the claimed strictly positive vertex margins.
- **Radius and complexity, lines 568–626:** the induced infinity norms provide rational lower/upper spectral bounds because the relevant matrices are symmetric. The proposed radius guarantees positive definiteness, response error at most `sigma/2`, and gradient error at most `sigma/2`. These imply the stated fixed labels outside the transition union. The proof uses its own sound norm certificates and does not assume the ellipsoid tests must recognize them. Vertex enumeration, simplex descriptions, and dense rational inversion retain polynomial bit complexity. The additional simplex enumeration is allowed to increase the dimension-dependent exponent.
- **Approximate bounds and limitations, lines 628–693:** inner rows are sufficient for true feasibility; outer rows are necessary. Signed directional objective bounds therefore yield valid global lower and upper bounds. Empty inner LPs do not prove infeasibility, and upper equalities may defeat the inner construction. The small-residual ambiguity example and shifted switching point both have the stated responses. The text separates conservative certificates from actual response complexity and gives no unsupported small-loss or solver-superiority guarantee.
