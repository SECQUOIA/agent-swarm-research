## Scientific assessment

The central contribution is substantial and precise: eliminating the primal local variables, then restricting joint regimes in fixed shared dimension, supports complete global bilevel optimization with growing follower dimension. The manuscript correctly credits the individual tools and does not claim that multiplier elimination or global value comparison alone is new. The accuracy theorem is a distinct global rational-bit result, with genuinely different hypotheses from the exact nonconvex-aggregate theorem. The structural boundary results explain why the assumptions matter. The scalar computations are appropriately narrower than those general theorems.

I checked the following interfaces in particular:

- **Exact compression:** polyhedral KKT necessity does not require a constraint qualification; support reduction modulo the equality span preserves nonnegative multipliers; positive determinant-square guards handle moving ranks; fixed-dimensional sign enumeration avoids the exponential Cartesian product. Global comparison is sound because every candidate is feasible and at least one global minimizer is represented. Growing polynomial upper support changes construction size, not the number of quantified coordinates. Fixed normals are used exactly where the compact response graph and optimistic attainment require them.
- **Output and semantics:** the fixed-depth real-algebraic operations control degree independently of polynomial count in the constant-local-matrix refinements. Reconstruction evaluates all coordinates in the selected field. Independently chosen adverse witnesses are not asserted to share one small field. Universal pessimistic upper rows, nonempty follower tests and finite-infimum/attainment predicates remain consistent across Sections 1–3 and the scalar maximization interface.
- **Robustness:** fixing one criterion's measurements makes its fiber minimum a valid budget witness; soundness does not require a budgeted candidate to be globally minimal on that fiber. The nominal reference value must be global and is. Separate elimination before combining many criteria controls dimension. The convex attainment proof handles zero budgets and positive budgets separately. Both nonattainment examples and the Max-Cut boundary support their stated restrictions.
- **Screening:** the signed ellipsoid center and both directional applications give the displayed coordinate and gradient bounds. Strict signs and the relative-interior extension distinguish zero-gradient bounds correctly. The transition-union bound uses a simplex cover of cell vertices and the injective leader projection; overlap does not invalidate it. The recovery count excludes preprocessing, and a failed full-cell assignment is not discarded from later recovery on a subcell.
- **Accuracy construction:** the inverse proof includes nonreal critical values, proper covering over a simply connected critical-value-free disk, rational response centers, and explicit denominator growth in Taylor reversion. Closed branch validity sets are used instead of unjustified closures of nonlinear sign cells. Rational recovery stays in the true base polytope while transferring true inverse responses across branch boundaries. The complementary-slackness transfer explicitly controls large negative inactive slacks. The polynomial upper substitution and the objective/violation error ledgers remain polynomial in numerical degree and accuracy bits.
- **Quantitative modulus:** Appendix C's same-pattern tangent cancellation yields the claimed 1/P exponent; the bound on the number of parameter components, rather than just the number of patterns, closes the segment argument. Its exponential numerical constant still has polynomial encoding. The sharp example is valid. The exact-feasibility corollaries preserve their additional tightening, reduced-convexity or reserve assumptions; the irrational-only and isolated-optimum counterexamples explain why they cannot be dropped.
- **Hardness and approximation:** I followed both dense reductions, including the coordinate-relative error in the near-identity construction. Their different gaps and encoding restrictions are preserved. The conditioned grid bounds the unsaturated cost ranges and retains direct leader cost by LP; its exact projected-gradient/continued-fraction recovery has the claimed dependence on numerical K. There is no conflict with accuracy-bit hardness. Parameterized hardness is attributed, the mixed-radix construction has a valid continuous rounding gap, and the bounded-core closure step remains complete.
- **Paths and arithmetic:** leader-cost paths and follower-constraint paths are distinguished. The message examples are not promoted to pointwise hardness. The Klee–Minty telescoping, strict vertex exposure, perturbation margin, fixed-alphabet padding and slab equivalence are internally consistent; the nonconvex upper row is indispensable and explicitly retained. Equal-gain forest projection handles negative/zero gains and fixed total cycle rank. Radical and sparse-power output lower bounds are stated for their actual representation models, without declaring Square Root Sum NP-hard.
- **Executable specialization:** the scalar fiber bound, original contact tests, flat components, quadratic-field outputs and attained/unattained upper endpoints are correct under the given hypotheses. The baseline's all-principal-minors restriction supplies its completeness and excludes unsupported continua. Independent tests and raw measurements support the limited computational claims. Negative screening timings and numerical feasibility discrepancies are reported candidly.

The organization is long but coherent for the explicitly requested comprehensive paper. The initial scope and comparison table, separate model statements, local limitations and full supporting appendices make it standalone for an optimization/complexity readership. I found no missing proof dependency requiring an internal note and no relevant inventory item silently omitted; the pooling-only and binary cut-flow investigations are reasonably excluded from this continuous bilevel scope.

## Required minor clarification

**R05-1 — specify the order of subtraction in the sparse-power example.**

Location: `sections/04-accuracy.tex:180–182`, Example `ex:sparse-power-output` (printed Example 4.3).

After listing responses `x^{1/p}` and `x^{2/p}`, the text says “Their difference has minimum −1/4.” The following completed square uses the second response minus the first. A reader can naturally read “their difference” in the order just listed, which would be nonnegative and have minimum zero. The subsequent proof is correct for the intended signed upper objective, so this is a local clarification, not a defective theorem.

Fix: write the objective explicitly, for example “The upper objective `x^{2/p}-x^{1/p}` has minimum `-1/4` at `x=2^{-p}`,” and retain the completed-square and output-length argument. No other proof or experiment needs changing.
