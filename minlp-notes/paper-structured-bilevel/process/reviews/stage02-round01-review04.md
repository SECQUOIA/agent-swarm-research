## New developments checked

**Support-tuple hull, appendix lines 180–212.** This construction does not need to enumerate the Cartesian product of all local vertices. For each direction at the sampled core, its actual sign condition is in the globally enumerated list. The selected tuple is feasible at that core and attains the full Minkowski-sum support. It survives the proposed feasibility filter. Conversely, every tuple admitted by the filter is blockwise feasible, even if its original sign condition is not realizable at that core. The retained hull is therefore contained in the aggregate image and has the same support function in every direction. The appendix proves the separation step that yields equality. I found no missing requirement to retain or reconstruct a support direction for each filtered tuple.

**Common-field block recovery, appendix lines 214–258.** The dependence-reduction argument preserves a convex representation and ends with at most `h+1 = k+2` affinely independent lifted columns. Enumerating subsets of that bounded size is polynomial. A real solution on an independent subset is unique and belongs to the sampled field because its coefficient matrix and target do. The remaining equations and signs are tested, so solving only independent rows does not admit spurious weights. Applying the same weights to every block preserves the sum of measurements and the objective coordinate. This is valid for the stated single-level polyhedral problem. The manuscript explicitly prevents its use as a license to mix nonconvex follower responses.

**Numerical-degree-uniform fixed-core theorem, appendix lines 26–38, 84–101, 158–178, and 260–266.** Local determinants, feasibility tests, and comparisons have degree linear in numerical input degree for fixed block dimension. Product denominators grow with the number of blocks, but the polynomial ambient dimension remains fixed. Their expansions and coefficient lengths consequently remain polynomial in `(L, delta)`. Both the joint optimum formula and support enumeration use fixed total dimension. Recovery adds only fixed-size algebraic systems. No fixed-degree hypothesis is silently used to justify the stronger theorem.

**Constant-Hessian algebraic degree, exact-response section lines 474–510.** Constant local Hessians and fixed normals make each local KKT inverse rational and independent of the leader. Its response formula is therefore polynomial, with degree independent of follower and row counts. Sums increase coefficient length and expression size but do not increase degree. Substituted upper polynomials still have degree bounded by a function of the fixed input degree. Basu–Pollack–Roy's quantifier-elimination output-degree bound and sample-representation degree bound are independent of the number of input polynomials. I checked both bounds in the extracted primary source and against original PDF pages 4 and 27 (printed pages 1005 and 1028). A fixed number of elimination/sampling steps thus gives the claimed constant common-field degree. This is an existence and algorithmic output bound for an optimizer, not a claim that every real optimizer has bounded algebraic degree. The stated fixed-normal scope is essential and is present.
