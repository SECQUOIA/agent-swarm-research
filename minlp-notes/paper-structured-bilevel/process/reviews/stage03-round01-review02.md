## Robust-response proof assessment

Locators in this report refer to the frozen `sections/03-robustness-screening.tex` unless another file is named.

The measurement-fiber threshold at lines 63–90 is correct in both directions. A near-optimal point supplies a nonempty compact fiber whose global minimum has a KKT representation and no greater cost. Conversely, any feasible fiber KKT candidate below the true budget is itself near-optimal, even if it is not globally optimal on that fiber. Thus the simplified threshold predicate correctly avoids repeating fiber-global comparisons. It does not discard the indispensable global comparison used to obtain the nominal value.

The construction at lines 92–175 controls the total quantified dimension. Measurement coordinates and their multipliers add only fixed dimensions. Polynomial data on compact `C` admit the stated polynomial-bit rational measurement bounds through fixed-dimensional algebraic optimization. Each criterion is eliminated separately before its violation predicate is conjoined with the others. The shared nominal-value variable is unique; empty followers contribute no nominal value and cannot become robustly feasible by vacuity. The worst-value relation describes exactly the attainable criterion values over the near-optimal set. Compactness gives a worst response at each feasible leader, while endpoint membership distinguishes a leader infimum from an attained optimum.

Joint sampling adds only one adverse witness. Rational block recovery stays in its sampled field; separate criterion witnesses need not be combined into one field. This distinction is correctly stated in both theorem and proof. The example requiring the adverse response `1/sqrt(2)` also correctly excludes a general rational-witness promise.

The new constant-local-matrix corollary at lines 184–209 is valid with moving **shared** normals. Such normals, including the measurement rows, enter the effective linear term and shared consistency equations, not the local bordered KKT matrix. Constant local `Q_b,E_b,G_b` therefore give polynomial local responses with rational constant denominators. For fixed numerical degree, both candidate and upper-criterion degrees remain bounded independently of block and row counts. Separate criterion elimination does not create a growing composition depth. The BPR elimination and simultaneous sampling degree bounds verified in stage 2 then give the claimed bound for one joint leader–witness output or each separately requested witness. No attainment claim is smuggled into this arithmetic corollary.

The fixed-normal convex attainment argument at lines 213–255 is complete. Hoffman repair supplies the lower approximation property of feasible fibers on the compact feasible-leader domain. Strict convexity gives a unique continuous nominal response. Positive budgets permit mixing with that response to obtain strict cost slack before repair; a zero budget is handled by nearby nominal minimizers. These arguments prove continuity of each near-optimal criterion maximum and hence compact robust feasibility and attainment.

The positive-budget counterexamples at lines 257–292 have the claimed sublevel sets and unattained infima. I independently checked the factorization, derivative identities, and algebraic endpoint bound. The restriction `c<=1/8` ensures the asserted single interior derivative sign change; the inequalities used to prove `4x+a_c>a` hold on the displayed interval. The Max-Cut transfer at lines 294–320 is also correct: the budget makes the entire cube near-optimal, convexity places a criterion maximum at a binary vertex, and threshold `K-1` gives the complement of integral cut threshold `K`. The restricted universal test is coNP-complete, while the general conclusion is adverse-value NP-hardness. ## Screening, recovery, and quantitative refinement

The paired variational inequalities at lines 358–383 give `e^T Q e + (Ey)^T e <= 0` with the correct sign. Completing the square and taking support in the `Q` norm gives the directional bound. Substituting a coordinate vector and its image under `Q` produces the stated response and gradient centers and radii. The ellipsoid is only an enclosure, and the surrogate response is required to be exact.

At lines 387–458, the convex quadratic defining `eta_P` attains its maximum at some vertex of the compact rational cell. Fixed-dimensional vertex enumeration covers lower-dimensional cells and singletons. The strict gradient signs for bound certification, weak coordinate tests, and interiority test for a zero gradient are sound. In the closure refinement, each affine nonnegative slack that is not identically zero is positive throughout the relative interior. Continuity then extends the certified equation or bound to the closed cell. The strict-somewhere conditions are necessary and are retained. The `F` label explicitly permits zero-gradient bound points.

The dense recovery theorem at lines 466–515 uses the **true** principal submatrix `Q_FF`, which is invertible even for a large free set. The substituted affine equations, box bounds, and gradient signs are the full KKT system of the true strictly convex follower. Sound certificates plus a compatible status for every ambiguous coordinate ensure coverage of every true feasible leader. Each recovery LP is compact, and rational dense elimination and reconstruction have polynomial bit cost. The count `sum_P 3^{t_P} <= M 3^t` correctly counts only recovery LPs. Cover construction and screening are explicitly excluded from that count and included in the total complexity statement. The theorem is conditional on a valid supplied cover; it does not purport to discover a useful surrogate automatically.

The new overlapping-simplex construction at lines 539–552 is sound. Uniqueness of the surrogate response makes leader projection injective on a cover cell and therefore on its affine hull, so the cell dimension is at most `r`. Carathéodory reduction ensures that convex hulls of affinely independent vertex subsets of size at most `r+1` cover the entire cell. A disjoint triangulation is unnecessary for a recovery algorithm that already tolerates overlaps.

The transition and radius calculation at lines 554–620 has the correct constants. Outside a simplex’s transition union, every nominal margin is strictly positive at each vertex and hence at least the rational minimum margin throughout the simplex. Symmetry justifies using induced infinity norms as spectral-norm upper bounds. The radius yields positive definiteness, response error at most `2 epsilon R/m_0`, and gradient error at most `2 epsilon R(1+L_0/m_0)`. Both are at most `sigma/2`, which preserves the certified statuses. The transition union contains at most `(r+1)q` coordinates. The text properly notes that enumerating the simplex cover may increase the polynomial exponent; it does not identify its size with the original arrangement size.

The signed inner/outer bounds at lines 628–665 have the correct inequality directions and feasibility implications. A feasible inner leader is truly feasible; empty inner problems do not prove infeasibility. All outer problems empty do prove infeasibility. The computed objective gap, rather than perturbation size alone, supplies the guarantee. The conservative-screening and shifted-switch examples correctly demonstrate the stated limits without asserting intrinsic hardness from failed certificates.

## Primary sources and attribution

The new citations support the claims assigned to them:

- [Besançon–Anjos–Brotcorne (2021), inspected preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.00824), PDF p.3, expressly gives an objective-robust epigraph variant; Lemma 1 and Theorems 1–2, pp.5–7, concern conditional complexity-class membership. The manuscript correctly distinguishes these statements from its structural polynomial-time global optimization theorem. The [2024 published paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10898-024-01422-z) supports near-optimal feasibility robustness and criterion-specific adversaries. The paper does not claim these modeling choices as new.
- [Liu–Zhao–Wang–Ye (2014)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/liuc14.pdf), Section 2.2, PDF p.3, explicitly combines variational inequalities at two parameters to enclose a target response. This is an appropriate methodological predecessor, without being attributed the present dense-Hessian formula or whole-cell theorem.
- [Dantas–Gribonval, v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.06635v3), Section III, PDF pp.4–5, derives screening from an approximate dictionary; Remark 2 requires safety for the original problem. The draft accurately credits structured-surrogate screening rather than claiming that principle as new.

- The [Garey–Johnson–Stockmeyer publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304397576900591) explicitly identifies NP-completeness of Simple Max Cut with unit edge weights. That suffices for the exact classical premise used by the manuscript’s self-contained transfer.
