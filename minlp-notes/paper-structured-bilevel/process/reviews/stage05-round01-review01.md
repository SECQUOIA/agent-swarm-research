## Mathematical assessment

### Dense scalar hardness and near-identity hardness

For `lem:box-np`, each active-status partition gives a rational affine response formula with polynomial-size coefficients. Weak endpoint inequalities correctly cover degenerate statuses. Nonemptiness of the resulting rational polyhedron supplies a polynomial-bit leader certificate even when the leader dimension grows. This certificate concerns affine upper data; the separate path hardness theorem gives its own certificate for its nonconvex upper row.

In `thm:dense-hardness`, I checked the ternary witness, residual signs, strict gradient margin and both sources of auxiliary feedback. The clause transformation preserves polynomial size and ensures that a false rounded clause uses distinct coordinates. The conditional shortfall and minority-distance identities imply the claimed nonnegative objective and the zero-versus-two gap over every continuous response, rather than just Boolean leaders. The triangular weighted residual map proves positive definiteness. The jointly convex representation statement is appropriately distinguished from the expanded follower objective after removal of its leader-only term. Weight and witness encodings have polynomial length.

For `thm:conditioned-hardness`, the feedforward state and residual bounds are valid on the whole leader interval. Positive diagonal scaling makes the network point a clipped fixed point with no active upper clipping. The exact optimizer's KKT identity yields the relative-coordinate error inequality without assuming that this error is small in advance. Multiplication by the nonnegative triangular inverse, and the stated bounds on that inverse and on the backward matrix, justify the final error bound. This is the necessary estimate for the scaled readout; an absolute error estimate for the very small response coordinates would not suffice.

The spectral and induced-norm bounds, bounded linear-cost and upper coefficients, and strict diagonal dominance follow from the selected scale. Decreasing that scale for a supplied rational proximity tolerance improves the error estimates and preserves polynomial encoding length. The new readout scale still separates yes and no instances at the stated threshold. The exponentially small absolute gap is correctly kept separate from strong hardness and from a constant-gap approximation lower bound. The conclusion excluding an algorithm polynomial in accuracy bits is compatible with the next theorem.

### Conditioned normalized approximation and exact rational oracle

I checked all steps of `thm:conditioned-grid`, including the cases with no followers, no leaders or zero follower coefficient norm. The saturation inequalities force the endpoint responses even at equality, so closed arrangement cells can be used. The variational-inequality argument may omit saturated coordinates because their response differences vanish. The maximum-minor row selection bounds all row expansion coefficients by one and can be enumerated in polynomial time for fixed leader dimension. The resulting cost-coordinate ranges have width at most `K`; their potentially large offsets affect encoding length, not the grid count.

Minimizing the direct leader term within each closed inverse-image rectangle is essential and is correctly included. The response estimate then gives the promised additive error multiplied by the upper follower coefficient one-norm. Neither arbitrary direct leader coefficients nor arbitrary affine follower-cost magnitudes require a numerical dependence beyond what is stated. The estimate `K <= N kappa_2(Q)` is valid for symmetric positive definite matrices.

The exact inner oracle is also sound. With step size `1/||Q||_infinity`, the affine gradient map has Euclidean operator norm at most `1-1/K`, and clipping cannot increase distance. Clearing denominators in both the Hessian and the rational cost produces an integer active principal system. Its determinant bounds every reduced response denominator by `N! H^N`; the potentially larger right-hand side affects numerators, not this denominator bound. The prescribed iteration count reaches strictly less than `1/(4B^2)` coordinate error. Distinct rationals with denominator at most `B` cannot occupy that error interval. The continued-fraction criterion applies to the true coordinate, with endpoints treated separately, and exact KKT verification supplies a final certificate. Rational iterate bit lengths grow polynomially in the iteration count and input length. Thus the oracle is polynomial in the numerical `K`, as required, rather than relying on an unproved exact-output property of a floating-point solver.

### Growing leaders, bounded core and leader paths

The transfer in `thm:growing-leader` preserves the source parameter and polynomial input size. The source's spike formulas have bounded polynomial-magnitude locations and at most two input coordinates per neuron. Input rescaling, a common cap scale, and polynomial duplication therefore give the claimed bounded follower and upper coefficients. The manuscript uses the proved continuous gap; it does not assume that arbitrary maximizing inputs are discrete. I also checked the independent mixed-radix interpolation, its Lipschitz rounding gap, and its capped-difference identity. The duplication counts remain polynomial and do not increase the number of leaders per follower.

The bounded-core algorithm enumerates every relevant component arrangement vertex for a fixed core point. Its candidates depend affinely on core variables because their component normals are constant. Feasibility, clipping status and candidate-value comparisons admit the asserted fixed-dimensional arrangements. The closure argument is correct: a selected candidate stays feasible and preserves its continuous value, while completeness is obtained from the relative cell containing an optimal core point. A candidate becoming newly feasible on a boundary does not invalidate either direction. The dependence is polynomial for fixed core and component sizes, with no FPT claim.

The leader-path reduction has the exact normalized Subset Sum distance asserted in `eq:subset-path-distance`. The telescoping lower bound and a cumulative subset path prove both directions. The distance-message identities, including the identical-factor example, hold because changing the last state costs at most its displacement. Their stated piece counts are correct. Exponential message size is explicitly separated from hardness of the identical-factor family's optimum and from strong hardness.

### Follower path geometry, padding and strips

I checked the vertex description, edge calculation, exposing prices and exact backward evaluation in `app:klee-shadow`. The price interval contains an open exposing neighborhood for each vertex. Distinct exposed projected vertices give the terminal message's stated piece count. The bounded-epigraph argument correctly forces one distinct linear factor per boundary segment in the product of the formula's nonzero polynomials. This proves the unextended total-degree lower bound, without implying a lower bound for pointwise LP evaluation, circuits or formulations retaining states.

The telescope in `lem:klee-telescope` has the stated coefficients and vanishes precisely at original cube vertices. The terminal values are distinct, so the parabolic score exposes a unique vertex. The Subset Sum slab error is at most one eighth in the forward direction and less than one half in the reverse integer comparison.

For the new padded form of `thm:follower-path-hardness`, unary padding bounds force padding bits to zero at an original cube vertex. Every free-bit pattern extends to such a vertex, and the accumulated gain between free coordinates gives the required small error. The quadratic perturbation argument was proved over the full original cube, so the same strict optimum remains valid on its padded subset; it does not need the padded polytope's new vertices to be original cube vertices. Conversely, the upper vertex polynomial excludes any such new nonvertex response from the feasible certificate. Scaling the whole follower objective gives identity Hessian without changing its response. The fixed alphabet claim is expressly limited to local inequalities, not the slab weights or rescaled costs.

The nonconvex upper row, polynomial-bit exposing leaders, slab-only nonemptiness, attainment and failure of the reduction after deleting the vertex row are all accurately stated. The result is not presented as all-linear-upper feasibility hardness.

In `prop:strip-projection`, normalization gives difference intervals on each nonzero-gain component, including arbitrarily oriented trees. The unique simple path is shortest after removing nonnegative backtracks. The all-pairs inequalities and minimum formula prove necessity, sufficiency and recovery. Zero-gain edges add bounds to their original heads, which is the correct direction-sensitive operation. Retaining both endpoints of each removed nonforest edge is sufficient for fixed total cycle rank. Products and path sums have polynomial numerical degree and coefficient encoding in fixed parameter dimension; sign conditions retain zeros. Witness comparisons and rational operations stay in the retained solution's common field. The objective and attainment restrictions prevent an unsupported extension to dense objectives on eliminated states or noncompact parameter domains.

### Curvature and arithmetic output

Both curvature reductions encode the Boolean assignments exactly, with the stated optimistic semantics and different upper-degree requirements. The one-power examples force a positive rational leader at most `(5/8)^P`; the reduced rational denominator is therefore at least `(8/5)^P`. This establishes the claimed output-length obstruction at fixed error, including the case with a strict anchor and convex reduced row. The sparse upper equation has exponential minimal-polynomial degree by Eisenstein's criterion. The discussion correctly leaves open the different one-power affine-objective/no-upper-row subclass.

The multiquadratic field induction and the distinct signed conjugates prove the sum's degree. The singleton leaf construction establishes an exact Square Root Sum comparison encoding, not NP-hardness. These claims respect the distinction between explicit minimal-polynomial/common-field output and a short radical expression, and do not conflict with accuracy-bit approximation.
