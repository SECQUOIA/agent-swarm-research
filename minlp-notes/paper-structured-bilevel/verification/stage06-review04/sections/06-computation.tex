\section{Executable scalar algorithms and reproducible computations}
\label{sec:computation}

The preceding fixed-dimensional algorithms establish bit complexity without
promising an efficient general implementation of real quantifier elimination.
This section gives executable specializations. We distinguish three tasks:
certifying a proposed convex response path, constructing a complete nonconvex
response graph, and optimizing an upper problem over that graph. In particular,
an exact follower oracle at finitely many prices does not solve a continuous
leader problem.

\subsection{Convex quadratic paths and an aligned sweep}
\label{subsec:convex-implementation}
Consider the unique response on the unit box to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:implemented-convex}
 \min_{0\le z\le1}\ \tfrac12z\trans Qz+(c+Cx)\trans z,
 \qquad Q=D+UHU\trans\succ0,\quad x\in[L,R],
\end{equation}
where all data are rational, $D$ is positive diagonal, $H$ is symmetric,
and the displayed decomposition is supplied. Here $x$ is scalar. The
implemented upper objective is the minimization of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:implemented-upper}
 o_xx+o_z\trans z+o_{xx}x^2+x\,o_{xz}\trans z
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad a_jx+b_j\trans z\le h_j.
\end{equation}
Thus tariff maximization is represented by a negative $x$--response coefficient.
The nonconvex interface below instead directly maximizes revenue.

For a status assignment with free coordinates $F$ and upper coordinates $B$,
set $z_B=1$, set the remaining bound coordinates to zero, and solve
\[
 Q_{FF}z_F=-c_F-C_Fx-Q_{FB}\mathbf1.
\]
This yields a rational affine function. Free-coordinate bounds and bound-gradient
signs give its exact closed validity interval. Degenerate zero gradients are
allowed. The affine stationarity identities and the signs at the interval's
two endpoints certify KKT throughout the interval. Positive definiteness then
certifies the unique global response. This is the familiar active-set mechanism
behind explicit parametric quadratic programming
\citep[Theorems 2 and 4]{BemporadEtAl2002}; here the certificate also includes
coverage of the entire requested price interval.

No inverse of $H$ is required. For a right-hand side $v$, put
$S=U_F\trans D_F^{-1}U_F$ and solve
\[
 (I+SH)w=U_F\trans D_F^{-1}v,
 \qquad z_F=D_F^{-1}(v-U_FHw).
\]
The determinant identity
$\det Q_{FF}=\det D_F\det(I+SH)$ proves invertibility, even when $H$ is singular
or indefinite. For rank $k$, a pattern solve uses
$O(N(1+k^2)+k^3)$ rational arithmetic operations; this count excludes validation
of the supplied positive-definiteness assumption. The implementation checks
that assumption, using direct exact matrix tests when the small-rank sufficient
tests do not apply.

\begin{proposition}[Certified and exhaustive convex paths]
\label{prop:convex-certified-path}
A finite collection of rational affine pieces for \eqref{eq:implemented-convex}
is a complete exact response path if every piece passes the preceding KKT test
and its closed intervals cover $[L,R]$. Enumerating all $3^N$ status assignments
always produces such a path. On any certified complete path,
\eqref{eq:implemented-upper} is solved exactly by rational arithmetic.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
KKT is necessary and sufficient on a box for a convex differentiable objective.
Every optimizer supplies a status assignment, including at a degenerate
boundary, so exhaustive enumeration covers every price. Conversely each
certified piece contains only optimizers. Substitute its affine response into
each upper row and intersect intervals, retaining singleton intersections.
The upper objective becomes a rational quadratic. Compare its endpoints and,
when its quadratic coefficient is positive, its interior stationary point.
These rational candidates cover its minimum. Taking the least value over the
finite path solves the upper problem, or certifies infeasibility.
\end{proof}

The generic implementation uses a numerical box-QP routine only to propose a
status assignment at a price in an uncovered gap. It tries several numerical
status tolerances, recomputes the proposed segment exactly, and verifies that
the queried price belongs to it. For small $N$ it can fall back to exhaustive
search. It returns a path only after exact KKT and coverage verification;
otherwise it raises a recovery or coverage error. A cap on the number of
segments and unsuccessful numerical proposals are explicit failure modes.
This procedure is not an unconditional polynomial-time path-construction
algorithm. No numerical tolerance is used to declare global coverage.

There is a complete simpler sweep for the aligned rank-one case
$Q=D+h uu\trans\succ0$ and $C=\gamma u$, with $\gamma>0$. Define
\[
 z_i(t)=\clip_{[0,1]}(-(c_i+u_it)/d_i),\qquad A(t)=u\trans z(t).
\]
Zero entries of $u$ give constant coordinates. Signed nonzero entries give
at most two rational breakpoints each. On a multiplier interval,
$A(t)=A_0+A_1t$, with $A_1=-\sum_{i\in F}u_i^2/d_i$. The relation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex-sweep-inversion}
 x=(t-hA(t))/\gamma,\qquad
 t=(\gamma x+hA_0)/(1-hA_1)
\end{equation}
is strictly increasing: every free principal matrix is positive definite,
so $1+h\sum_{i\in F}u_i^2/d_i>0$. This includes negative $h$ whenever the
whole Hessian is positive definite. The map tends to both infinities on the
tails and therefore covers every price. Updating the affine coefficients at
each grouped breakpoint gives the complete response path. Maintaining the
weighted response sums in the objective and $m$ upper rows takes
$O(N\log N+N(m+1))$ rational arithmetic operations; only the final selected
response needs reconstruction in all coordinates. This is an arithmetic count,
not a constant-cost claim for arbitrarily long rational numbers.
Sorting and clipped multiplier allocation are classical resource-allocation
ingredients \citep{Kiwiel2008}; the implementation combines them with exact
upper-interval optimization. Its sweep and upper solve are fused, which matters
when interpreting their timings.

\subsection{Complete nonconvex scalar compression}
\label{subsec:nonconvex-compression}
The second specialization allows an indefinite follower Hessian:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonconvex-implemented}
 f_x(z)=\sum_{i=1}^N\left(\tfrac12d_i z_i^2+c_i z_i\right)
       -\tfrac h2(u\trans z)^2+\gamma x u\trans z,
 \quad \ell\le z\le r,\quad x\in[L,R].
\end{equation}
Here $N\ge1$, all data are rational, $d_i>0$, $h\ge0$, $\gamma\ne0$, $\ell_i\le r_i$,
and $L\le R$. Signed and zero $u_i$, fixed coordinates, negative prices,
and either sign of $\gamma$ are allowed. The upper problem maximizes $xw$,
where $w=u\trans z$, subject to rational affine upper rows. Optimistic and
pessimistic meanings follow Section~\ref{sec:foundations}, with maxima and
suprema in place of minima and infima.

Let $[w_-,w_+]$ be the image of the follower box under $u\trans z$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-fiber-value}
 C(w)=\min\left\{\sum_i(\tfrac12d_i z_i^2+c_i z_i):
                  \ell\le z\le r,\ u\trans z=w\right\},
 \qquad \psi(w)=C(w)-\tfrac h2w^2.
\end{equation}
The fiber problem is strictly convex even when \eqref{eq:nonconvex-implemented}
is nonconvex. Its minimizer $z(w)$ is unique. Every global follower optimum
must be this minimizer on its fiber; otherwise its objective could be decreased
without changing the aggregate terms. Thus the original global problem is
exactly $\min_{w\in[w_-,w_+]}\{\psi(w)+\gamma xw\}$, with actual responses
recovered by $z(w)$.

\begin{lemma}[Rational fiber pieces]\label{lem:scalar-fiber-pieces}
If $w_-<w_+$, the interval has at most $2N-1$ nondegenerate pieces on which
$z(w)$ is rational affine and $\psi(w)$ is rational quadratic. They and their
endpoints can be constructed in polynomial bit complexity. If $w_-=w_+$,
there is one constant aggregate and one unique fiber response.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a multiplier $\lambda$, strict convexity gives
\[
 z_i(\lambda)=\clip_{[\ell_i,r_i]}((\lambda u_i-c_i)/d_i).
\]
For $u_i=0$ this is interpreted as the constant clipped unconstrained
minimizer. Ignore fixed coordinates when forming events. Sort the at most
$2N$ numbers $(d_i\ell_i+c_i)/u_i$ and $(d_ir_i+c_i)/u_i$ for $u_i\ne0$.
On each intervening interval, $z=a+b\lambda$ and
$w=w_0+S\lambda$, with $S=\sum_{i\in F}u_i^2/d_i\ge0$.
If $S>0$, invert to obtain $z(w)$. If $S=0$, the interval maps to a single
aggregate already supplied by its adjacent pieces. The two infinite tails
likewise supply only endpoints. Continuity of clipping shows that the union
covers $[w_-,w_+]$; the fiber KKT equations and strict convexity prove the
recovered response is the unique fiber optimum, also at shared endpoints.
Substituting it into \eqref{eq:scalar-fiber-value} gives each quadratic.
If all aggregates are equal, the box constraints with nonzero $u_i$ fix those
coordinates and the others minimize independently. Rational sums, products,
sorting and inversions give polynomial bit bounds. Explicitly storing every
length-$N$ affine response uses at most quadratic, rather than linear, storage.
\end{proof}

Write a piece as $\psi(w)=Aw^2+Bw+D$ on $[a,b]$. Its complete minimizer
candidates under the price tilt are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item its endpoints, available at every price;
\item if $A>0$, the stationary point $w=-(B+\gamma x)/(2A)$ on the rational
price interval where it lies in $[a,b]$;
\item if $A=0$, the whole interval $[a,b]$ at $x=-B/\gamma$.
\end{enumerate}
A piece with $A<0$ contributes only endpoints. The stationary candidates have
quadratic value functions in $x$; endpoint candidates have affine values.
These are feasible candidates, so comparison of their \emph{original} values,
not their stationarity alone, decides globality.

\begin{theorem}[Complete scalar atlas and upper optimization]
\label{thm:nonconvex-atlas}
For \eqref{eq:nonconvex-implemented}, a finite exact response description and
both upper tariff problems can be computed in polynomial bit complexity.
The description consists of rational affine responses on open price intervals
with algebraic endpoints of degree at most two, together with all isolated
responses and all flat fiber intervals at individual prices. The algorithm
decides upper feasibility, computes the supremum and its attainment, and
returns an attaining response when one exists. Each reported optimizer or limiting pair, together with its value,
belongs to one field of degree at most two; different atlas cuts may lie in
different quadratic fields. Optimistic
feasibility implies attainment; pessimistic feasibility need not.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Form all candidate domain endpoints, all flat-piece prices, and every real
crossing of two candidate value polynomials in their common domain. Include
$L,R$ and discard prices outside them. There are $O(N^2)$ cuts, each rational
or quadratic algebraic. On each open interval, a rational sample determines
the valid candidates and every comparison sign. Keep all global winners.
At every cut compare all valid candidates anew, adding a flat interval exactly
when its value equals the global minimum. This proves completeness, including
isolated ties and lower-dimensional price strata.

On an open interval, two winning value polynomials must agree identically.
Their derivatives equal $\gamma w(x)$, both for endpoint and stationary
candidates. Since $\gamma\ne0$, they describe the same aggregate, and uniqueness
of the fiber minimizer gives the same original response. Different optimal
responses can therefore coexist only at the recorded individual prices.

For an open interval, substitute the affine response into the upper rows.
Their intersection is an interval, possibly empty or a singleton inside the
original open interval. Maximize its quadratic revenue by comparing endpoint
limits and any interior stationary point with negative quadratic coefficient.
Also retain an interior rational sample to recognize attainment of a constant
value. Flag an endpoint as attained only if it belongs to the original open
price interval; upper inequalities themselves are closed.

At a recorded price, an optimal response component is $z(w)$ on a singleton
or a closed fiber interval. Optimistically, intersect each component with all
upper rows and maximize the linear function $xw$ on what remains.
Pessimistically, require every upper row at both endpoints of every component,
then take the least $xw$ over all those endpoints. These endpoint tests are
exact because $z(w)$ is affine on a fiber piece. Comparing the finite list of
values and one-sided limits gives the supremum. Prefer an attained candidate
when its value ties a limit; if none attains the best value, return the limiting
price and aggregate with an explicit nonattainment flag. The optimistic graph
is closed in a compact box, which separately proves its attainment assertion.
The pessimistic assertion cannot be inferred from that closedness.

The all-pair construction has polynomially many branches, cuts and comparisons.
All rational coefficient lengths are polynomial in the input. Quadratic root
isolation and comparison, including comparisons between different quadratic
fields, have polynomial bit complexity. A rational sample between distinct
quadratic cuts can be found by dyadic refinement with polynomial bit length.
Upper stationary points and nontrivial row boundaries on open pieces are
rational; flat-piece prices are rational; all remaining endpoint outputs lie
in the field of their one quadratic price. Thus no compositum of all switch
fields is needed for any reported optimizer or value.
\end{proof}

The executable compressed implementation inserts candidates into the current
lower envelope. On each existing envelope interval it splits at the new
candidate's validity boundaries and its crossings with the current winner,
then compares at rational interior samples. It keeps the resulting envelope
and also a separate set of contact prices, including tangencies. Finally it
compares \emph{all} original candidates at retained contacts and flat prices.
To justify this shorter list, consider a final global tie when the later of
two tied branches was inserted. No then-existing branch could have had smaller
value there. The tie was therefore at a current boundary, a new domain
boundary, or a crossing with a current winner, unless the value polynomials
coincided. In that last case their derivatives give the same response and
there is no missing distinct contact. An isolated-domain candidate also
contributes its domain endpoint. Consequently the incremental construction
preserves every final global contact. Conservative bounds are $O(N^2)$ retained
cuts and $O(N^3)$ interval processing steps. The implementation retains full
response vectors and uses symbolic arithmetic; these counts do not predict
its practical running time.

\subsection{Convex-envelope values and original contacts}
\label{subsec:contacts}
Extend $\psi$ by $+\infty$ outside $[w_-,w_+]$. Its conjugate gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contact-conjugacy}
 v(x)=-\psi^*(-\gamma x)
     =\min_w\{\psi^{**}(w)+\gamma xw\}.
\end{equation}
For completeness, put $m=\min_w(\psi(w)+\gamma xw)$. The affine function
$m-\gamma xw$ minorizes $\psi$ and hence lies below its closed convex envelope
$\psi^{**}$. The rightmost minimum is at least $m$ and, because
$\psi^{**}\le\psi$, at most $m$. Moreover the original minimizers are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-contact}
 \{w:\ \psi^{**}(w)+\gamma xw=v(x),\quad \psi(w)=\psi^{**}(w)\}.
\end{equation}
The second equality is essential for upper feasibility and tie-breaking.
Convex envelopes of piecewise quadratic functions and their conjugates have
established algorithms \citep{GardinerLucet2010,MoehleEtAl2023}. Our direct
candidate proof supplies the original contact sets and rational-bit guarantees
needed here; it does not require a tangent formula from an external
implementation.

\begin{example}[False choices from convexification]\label{ex:false-contacts}
Take $N=1$, $d=u=1$, $c=-1$, $h=3$, $\gamma=1$, $z\in[0,1]$,
and $x\in[1,3]$. Then $\psi(z)=-z^2-z$ has convex envelope $-2z$.
The actual response is $1$ for $x<2$, $0$ for $x>2$, and $\{0,1\}$ at
$x=2$. The convexified response at $2$ is the entire interval $[0,1]$.
Consequently upper rows requiring $z=1/2$ are infeasible for the original
bilevel problem, although convexification admits revenue $1$. Mixing the
two original optima cannot repair this discrepancy. Without those rows,
the optimistic maximum is $2$, attained at $(2,1)$, whereas the pessimistic
supremum is $2$ and is not attained.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Capacity at an interior jump]\label{ex:capacity-jump}
Let $d=(1,1)$, $c=(-1,-1/2)$, $u=(1,1)$, $h=3/5$, $\gamma=1$,
$z\in[0,1]^2$, $x\in[0,1]$, and impose $w\le1$ above. The fiber formula is
\[
\psi(w)=\begin{cases}
 w^2/5-w,&0\le w\le1/2,\\
 -w^2/20-3w/4-1/16,&1/2\le w\le3/2,\\
 w^2/5-3w/2+1/2,&3/2\le w\le2.
\end{cases}
\]
At $x_*=17/20$, its only tilted minima are $w=3/8$ and $13/8$, both of
value $-9/320$, giving responses $(3/8,0)$ and $(1,5/8)$. The middle piece
is strictly concave; its endpoints have value $-1/40$, so supply no additional
minimizer. Comparing with the larger contact shows that for $x<x_*$ every
optimal $w$ is at least $13/8>1$: a putative smaller $w$ has nonnegative
excess value at $x_*$ and gains strictly less from the negative price change.
For $x>x_*$, comparison with the smaller contact forces $w\le3/8$;
minimization on the low piece gives $w=(5/2)(1-x)$. Its revenue decreases
on $[x_*,1]$. Only the smaller contact satisfies capacity at $x_*$.
Thus the optimistic maximum is $51/160$, attained there, while the
pessimistic supremum is the same number and is unattained. This is a failure
of universal upper feasibility at a tie, not a failure to compute the nominal
global follower value.
\end{example}

Local stationarity alone is also insufficient: on $[0,1]$,
$f(z)=z/4-z^2/2$ has a strict one-sided local minimum at $0$, of value $0$,
but its global minimum is at $1$, of value $-1/4$. Both satisfy box KKT.
The examples and the atlas construction address global selection explicitly.

\subsection{An independent full-task stationary-face baseline}
\label{subsec:full-task-baseline}
To compare complete bilevel tasks, we implement a second solver directly in
original coordinates. It shares rational instance data with the compressed
method, but no fiber allocation, envelope construction or upper-optimization
code. Its declared restriction is that \emph{every principal minor} of
$Q=\diag(d)-huu\trans$ is nonzero. It checks all $2^N-1$ nonempty minors
and rejects an unsupported instance before solving. This restriction excludes
flat follower directions; the compressed method has no such restriction.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness of the original-coordinate baseline]
\label{prop:original-face-baseline}
Under the stated nonzero-principal-minor restriction, enumerating original
stationary box faces, comparing their original objective values over the
whole price interval, and retaining all ties yields the complete response
graph of \eqref{eq:nonconvex-implemented}. Both upper tariff problems, including
attainment, are then solved exactly. Enumeration is exponential in $N$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take any global minimizer and its minimal box face. On its free coordinates
$F$, first-order stationarity holds and the second-order necessary condition
makes $Q_{FF}$ positive semidefinite. Its nonzero determinant therefore makes
it positive definite. Hence this optimizer appears in an enumerated face with
\[
 z_F(x)=-Q_{FF}^{-1}(c_F+\gamma u_Fx+Q_{FB}z_B).
\]
Here $B$ comprises both lower and upper fixed coordinates. The baseline
solves this dense original-coordinate system, rejects non-positive-definite
free faces, and intersects the free bounds and bound-gradient signs to get an
exact rational validity interval. A coordinate whose lower and upper bounds
coincide needs no gradient sign. Each retained candidate is feasible; every
global optimum is retained. Comparing their \emph{original} quadratic values
therefore gives exactly all global optima, regardless of nonglobal KKT points.

Substitution into $\tfrac12z\trans Qz+(c+\gamma xu)\trans z$ gives a rational
quadratic in $x$. Form all validity endpoints and all pairwise value crossings
on overlaps, including crossings above the global envelope. Exact comparison
on open cells and separately at their endpoints retains all ties. The
nonzero-minor condition precludes a missing continuum: any minimizer in a
positive-dimensional minimal face has the unique stationary point just solved.
Equal winning value polynomials on an open cell have equal derivatives
$\gamma u\trans z$ and hence equal aggregate responses. Strict fiber convexity
then makes their entire responses equal.

The baseline independently clips each open response cell with the upper rows
and compares quadratic revenue endpoints, stationary points, and interior
samples, tracking whether each endpoint belongs to the cell. At a cut,
optimism compares all upper-feasible global responses; pessimism requires
all global responses to satisfy the rows and takes their least revenue.
This finite decomposition proves the upper result, including nonattainment.
There are at most $3^N$ faces and quadratically many pair comparisons in that
number; each solve and comparison uses exact rational or quadratic algebraic
arithmetic. This is an exponential reference algorithm, not a scalability claim.
\end{proof}

An older independent oracle enumerates original stationary faces only at a
requested price. It can skip singular free faces while preserving the global
\emph{value}: a stationary positive-semidefinite singular face admits a null
direction along which the quadratic stays constant; moving to the boundary
reduces the face until it is nonsingular or a vertex. That argument does not
recover a whole flat minimizer set and does not optimize over all prices.
The new baseline's stronger checked restriction makes it a complete-response,
complete-upper-task comparison. Separate tests continue to exercise the main
method on singular and flat cases.

\subsection{Computational protocol and findings}\label{subsec:computations}
All fresh runs use the supplied rational data and deterministic generators in
the accompanying code and data archive. The platform was Linux on an Intel
Xeon w5-2565X, with Python 3.13.11, SymPy 1.14.0, NumPy 2.5.1 and SciPy 1.18.0.
Each reported fresh timing is the median of three serial, fresh-process runs;
all individual times are retained. The thread environment for OpenBLAS, OpenMP,
MKL and NumExpr was set to one, and the SciPy MILP calls additionally forwarded
\texttt{threads=1} to HiGHS. The wrapper's warnings and solver output are
retained. Processes start with the SymPy cache cleared, and the order of paired
methods alternates between repetitions. This controls shared-cache ordering;
the machine was shared and no hardware isolation or statistical speed guarantee
is claimed. Every worker had an external 90-second limit. All 60 recorded
workers completed; no timed-out algorithm run is omitted.

The new correctness tests compare both global upper outcomes on eight
adversarial and ten seeded small random instances. They compare the entire
response sets at every independently constructed face-atlas cut and an interior
sample of every face-atlas cell. For each attained output from \emph{both}
methods, they directly check bounds, $w=u\trans z$, revenue, every upper row,
and original follower cost against all valid original-coordinate faces at
that returned price. Pessimistic witnesses also undergo universal feasibility
and least-revenue checks. These prices include upper row boundaries and
stationary revenue points, not just follower switches. A singular principal
minor is rejected by the baseline, while separate compressed tests retain
flat intervals, fixed boxes and singleton aggregates.

Additional, distinct diagnostics exercise exact convex path certification,
forced numerical-proposal failures, corrupted certificates and missing coverage;
nonconvex original-face value comparisons on signed and fixed-coordinate
instances; and incremental-envelope contacts against a full pairwise partition.
The convex checks include negative rank-one coefficients with positive-definite
total Hessian, nearly singular rational data, and data outside floating-point
range. These finite checks support the implementation contracts and expose
specified failure modes. Their counts are recorded by test family and are not
combined into a claim of formal verification of the general theorems.

\paragraph{The same continuous leader task.}
The heterogeneous family draws $d_i$ uniformly from
$\{8,\ldots,16\}/8$, $u_i$ from $\{4,\ldots,8\}/4$, and $-c_i$ from
$\{4,\ldots,24\}/8$, using seed $17+N$ in that order. It has the unit box,
$x\in[0,5]$, $h=(7/5)/(\sum_i u_i^2/d_i)$, and upper capacity
$u\trans z\le\tfrac12\sum_i u_i$. Its Hessian is indefinite: diagonal
congruence gives $I-hvv\trans$ with $v\trans v=\sum_i u_i^2/d_i$ and one
negative eigenvalue. Both solvers construct the whole reaction graph and solve
\emph{both} tariff semantics with these same upper rows.
Table~\ref{tab:full-task} also includes Example~\ref{ex:capacity-jump}.
Every exact value and attainment flag agrees between the methods and across
repetitions. The face algorithm is faster on the jump and $N=4$ instances;
the compressed method is faster on the tested heterogeneous $N=2,6$ instances.
Thus even these small complete-task comparisons do not show uniform dominance.
The exponential baseline was deliberately limited to $N\le6$, where it checks
at most 63 principal minors and 729 statuses. Larger instances were not
attempted with it; they are not reported as timeouts.

\paragraph{Symbolic cost and population structure.}
A profile of the unchanged scalar implementation on the $N=12$ heterogeneous
family identified repeated symbolic cancellation in sign tests as the principal
cost: cancellation accumulated 5.74 of 9.88 profiled seconds across the build
and one unconstrained upper solve. Profiled times include instrumentation
and are not benchmark times. The paper copy first decides the sign of an
already rational expression and, for two rational interval endpoints, uses
their rational midpoint. The full exact algebraic fallback and the candidate,
envelope and contact logic are unchanged. The fallback still uses a rational
sample between unrelated quadratic cuts, avoiding unnecessary output-field
extensions.

The controlled ordinary measurements compare the original and paper copies on
the same constrained $N=12$ task. Original build times were
$3.150,2.929,3.107$ seconds; paper-copy times were $2.748,2.679,2.708$ seconds.
Median totals including both upper solves were $4.055$ and $3.605$ seconds,
respectively. This modest measured improvement supports the two local changes;
it does not remove the symbolic cost of heterogeneous events.
Table~\ref{tab:scalar-scaling} reports the remaining fresh scalar runs.

The two-type family has $N=2m$: $m$ copies of $(d,c,u)=(1,-1,1)$ and $m$
copies of $(1,-1/2,1)$, with $h=3/(5m)$, $x\in[0,1]$ and capacity $w\le m$.
Its Hessian is indefinite since $h\sum_i u_i^2/d_i=6/5$.

$N=48$ has 66 fiber pieces; their timings measure different structural regimes.

\paragraph{Dense screening: a negative timing result.}\label{para:screening-results}
The exact screening prototype implements the scalar-cell specialization of
Section~\ref{sec:robust-screening}. The identity surrogate has a diagonal
response cover; the true dense Hessian is a rational symmetric perturbation
with verified strict diagonal dominance. Screening fixes certified statuses,
and exact original KKT recovery enumerates the ambiguous ones. Dense inversion,
cover construction and certification are real computational costs: the bound
$M3^t$ counts recovery LPs only.

The independent numerical comparator uses two binary variables $l_i,u_i$ per
coordinate and $g=Qz+c+Cx$, imposing
\[
 z_i+l_i\le1,\quad z_i-u_i\ge0,\quad l_i+u_i\le1,
 \qquad -M_i u_i\le g_i\le M_i l_i,
\]
with $0\le z_i\le1$ and
$M_i=1+|c_i|+|C_i|\max(|L|,|R|)+\sum_j|Q_{ij}|$.
If both binaries vanish, $g_i=0$; if $l_i=1$ or $u_i=1$, the corresponding
bound and gradient sign hold. Conversely every box KKT point admits such
binaries, including zero-gradient bounds. Positive definiteness makes this an
exact real-arithmetic reformulation. Its SciPy--HiGHS solve is numerical,
with a 30-second limit and requested relative MIP gap zero; numerical primal
and dual reports are not rational optimality certificates.

The accompanying scripts preserve prepared rational instances, generator seeds,

and they provide no evidence of superiority on industrial instances or on
