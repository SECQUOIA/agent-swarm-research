\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

The structural quantity controlling the positive results is the dimension
needed to describe follower responses after local elimination. For quadratic
blocks, positive local curvature gives rational response formulas, while a
fixed number of shared multipliers and aggregates bounds the joint regime
complexity. Comparing actual follower values in this compressed space makes
the description global even when the aggregate cost is nonconvex. The same
description supports polynomial upper data, both selection conventions and
explicit attainment decisions. For near-optimal responses, fixing the
measurements of one criterion gives the corresponding reduction without
assuming that every admissible follower is stationary.

For higher-degree local costs, arithmetic output becomes part of the problem.
A large algebraic field can be unavoidable even for independent, uniformly
convex scalar followers. Uniform rational inverse approximations allow global
upper optimization without forming that field. The resulting bound is
polynomial in accuracy bits for the specified fixed dimensions and numerical
degrees. Its rational output is exactly feasible for the base leader polytope.
Response-dependent upper feasibility needs a separate argument: relaxed upper
rows, an inner certificate and an original-feasibility decision cannot be
interchanged. The effective sharp $1/P$ response modulus and the stated
margin conditions make these distinctions quantitative.

The boundary results show why numerical simplicity and graph sparsity are
insufficient substitutes for these hypotheses. A dense follower Hessian can
be arbitrarily close to the identity and still encode an exact global bilevel
hardness reduction. This is compatible with a condition-dependent scheme
polynomial in inverse normalized error. A path can have exponentially many
response pieces or elimination segments, yet admit inexpensive pointwise
evaluation. Hardness of an upper problem, size of an explicit response
representation and cost of one follower solve are separate conclusions.
The positive bounded-core and equal-gain projection results likewise require
their full stated assumptions; neither implies unrestricted path tractability.

The scalar implementations make the response-set requirement visible.
Convexification preserves every tilted follower value, but an original contact
test is necessary to prevent false upper feasibility. The independent
original-coordinate solver agrees with both complete upper tasks on the tested
small instances. Larger computations separate heterogeneous events from
repeated coordinate types. The dense screening experiments are also
informative: even small certified ambiguity did not compensate for exact
preprocessing and recovery costs against the tested numerical MILP comparator.
Thus the computational results support the specified algorithms and
certificates, while the general complexity results rest on their proofs.
