# Accuracy-bit bilevel optimization with a fixed number of resource rows

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: two independent full proof audits passed, including the signed-marginal extension in Section 8. The focused primary-source comparison found no matching combined theorem in the checked literature; novelty remains provisional.
[First audit](../notes/review-bilevel-fixed-resource-accuracy-bit-algorithm.md);
[second audit](../notes/review-bilevel-fixed-resource-accuracy-bit-algorithm-second.md);
[source assessment](../notes/bilevel-resource-accuracy-bit-novelty.md).
This closes the previously started extension of the
[one-resource theorem](../notes/bilevel-one-resource-accuracy-bit-algorithm.md).
No novelty is claimed for KKT compression, Hoffman error bounds, or fixed-dimensional real algebraic optimization separately.

## 1. Statement

Fix the leader dimension `r` and resource-row count `k`. The rational input consists of a polytope `X subset [0,1]^r`, a matrix `C in Q^(k by N)` independent of the leader, affine functions `b(x) in R^k` and `ell_i(x)`, and marginals

```
g_i(z)=sum_(j=1)^(P_i) a_ij z^j,  a_ij>=0, G_i=sum_j a_ij>0.
```

Put `P=max_i P_i`. At a feasible leader, the follower uniquely minimizes

```
F_x(z)=sum_i [integral_0^(z_i) g_i(t) dt - ell_i(x) z_i]
over 0<=z<=1, Cz<=b(x).
```

The leader minimizes `H(x)=d_0+d^T x+c^T z*(x)`, with arbitrary signed rational coefficients. There are no additional upper constraints on the response.

**Theorem.** For fixed `r,k`, feasible-leader existence is decidable in polynomial time. If a feasible leader exists, a rational feasible leader with objective at most `OPT+2^(-B)` and a rational estimate of `OPT` with absolute error at most `2^(-B)` are computable in time polynomial in input bit length, numerical `P`, and `B`. Output rational bit length is polynomial. Equalities are allowed by representing each as two inequalities; the resulting row count must be fixed. No curvature lower bound or constraint qualification is assumed. Numerical coefficient magnitudes enter only through their encodings.

Section 8 extends the theorem to arbitrary dense strictly convex univariate polynomial local costs, including signed coefficients and interior zeros of the marginal derivative.

The claim is an accuracy-bit result, not exact optimization of algebraic sums, a sparse binary-degree theorem, or a practical complexity estimate. The resource matrix is fixed with respect to `x`; it need not be fixed across problem instances.

## 2. Exact feasible-leader polytope in fixed resource dimension

Let `C_i` denote the columns of `C`. The system `Cz<=b, 0<=z<=1` is feasible exactly when

```
lambda^T b >= sum_i min(0,lambda^T C_i) for every lambda>=0.       (1)
```

Indeed `b` must belong to the closed polyhedron `C[0,1]^N+R_+^k`. Separation from this polyhedron gives (1), since a finite lower support functional must have nonnegative coefficients. Conversely, (1) is valid at every member of that polyhedron.

The right side is linear on each cone of the central hyperplane arrangement `lambda^T C_i=0` restricted to the nonnegative orthant. Each such cone is pointed. It is generated by its extreme rays, so it is enough to impose (1) on all these rays. An extreme ray lies in `k-1` independent hyperplanes selected from the column hyperplanes and coordinate hyperplanes. Enumerate these subsets, retain both possible nullspace directions when nonnegative, and impose the associated inequality (1). For `k=1`, the empty subset supplies its two directions and only the nonnegative one is retained. Zero columns and deficient rank cause no difficulty because the coordinate hyperplanes are included. The case `k=0` has no inequalities.

There are `O((N+k)^(k-1))` candidates, with rational directions of polynomial bit length by Cramer's rule. Thus

```
X'={x in X: all the finitely many inequalities (1) hold}
```

is an explicit rational polytope of polynomial size for fixed `k`. Rational LP decides its emptiness. This is the exact polytope preserved during the final rounding step.

## 3. Explicit uniform multiplier and feasibility-repair bounds

The following coarse bounds are included to make the bit argument independent of a numerical conditioning assumption. If `N=0`, follower feasibility is simply `0<=b(x)` and the leader problem is an LP. Suppose henceforth `N>=1`. Choose a positive integer `D` clearing all denominators of `C`, and let

```
A = D [ C ; I ; -I ],
M = max(1,max absolute entry of A),
V = N! (1+N M^2)^N,
K = D N^3 M V.
```

These positive integers have bit length polynomial in the input. Define a rational number `Gbar>=1` bounding every `|g_i(z_i)-ell_i(x)|` on the unit leader and follower cubes; for example, use `Gbar=1+max_i(G_i+|ell_i(0)|+sum_j |coefficient_j(ell_i)|)`. Put

```
Lambda=max(1,K Gbar),  L=N Gbar.
```

Here `L` bounds the Lipschitz constant of `F_x` in infinity norm on the follower cube.

A useful elementary representation bound is as follows. If a vector `v` is a nonnegative combination of some rows of the integer matrix `A`, conic Caratheodory reduction gives such a representation using linearly independent rows `A_J`, with `t=|J|<=N`. The coefficients satisfy

```
alpha=(A_J A_J^T)^(-1) A_J v, alpha>=0.
```

The Gram determinant is a positive integer. Its entries have magnitude at most `N M^2`, so the cofactor formula and the determinant lower bound give every inverse entry magnitude at most `V`. Consequently

```
||alpha||_1 <= N^3 M V ||v||_2.                                (2)
```

The constants deliberately dominate the elementary factors `t`, `sqrt(N)`, and all smaller minors.

**Bounded KKT multipliers.** At a follower optimum, the polyhedral normal-cone formula represents `-gradient F_x(z*)` as a nonnegative combination of active resource and box normals, even on a degenerate feasible face. Apply (2) to the scaled normals `A`. Since the gradient infinity norm is at most `Gbar`, the individual representation coefficients are at most `N^3 M V Gbar`: this follows directly from the inverse formula, which bounds each coefficient by `N^2 M V ||v||_2<=N^3 M V Gbar`. Undoing the scaling gives a resource multiplier

```
0<=lambda<=Lambda 1,   lambda^T(Cz*-b)=0,                       (3)
```

for which `z*` minimizes the box Lagrangian. This is an existence bound; the proof does not compute an exact algebraic multiplier. There is no Slater assumption.

**Uniform feasibility repair.** Fix any `x in X'` and `q in [0,1]^N` satisfying `(Cq-b(x))_j<=delta`. Let `y` be the Euclidean projection of `q` on the nonempty feasible follower polytope, and write `v=q-y`. Its projection normal is a nonnegative combination of active scaled rows of `A`. Choose the independent representation from (2). For every active row, its scalar product with `q-y` equals that row's violation at `q`; box violations are nonpositive and resource violations are at most `D delta`. Therefore

```
||v||_2^2 = alpha^T A_J(q-y)
          <= D delta ||alpha||_1
          <= K delta ||v||_2.
```

Thus a feasible `y` exists with

```
||q-y||_infinity <= K delta.                                   (4)
```

This is a classical Hoffman bound with an explicit polynomial-bit overestimate. Neither projection nor the active set is needed by the final algorithm.

## 4. Approximate dual certificates control the entire response

For `lambda in [0,Lambda]^k`, let `q=q(x,lambda)` be the unique box-Lagrangian minimizer. Its coordinates are clipped inverses:

```
q_i=h_i(ell_i(x)-C_i^T lambda),
h_i(t)=0 (t<=0), g_i^(-1)(t) (0<t<G_i), 1 (t>=G_i).              (5)
```

Assume

```
(Cq-b)_j<=delta,   |lambda^T(Cq-b)|<=zeta.                       (6)
```

By Lagrangian weak duality, `F_x(q)+lambda^T(Cq-b)<=F_x(z*)`. Repair `q` to the feasible `y` in (4). Lipschitz continuity yields

```
0<=F_x(y)-F_x(z*)<=L K delta+zeta.                              (7)
```

Put `mu=min_i G_i/(P+1)>0`. For `u,v in [0,1]` and an integer `j>=1`, the Bregman divergence of `z^(j+1)/(j+1)` is at least `|u-v|^(j+1)/(j+1)`. For `u>=v`, integrate `(v+t)^j-v^j>=t^j`; for `u<v`, integrate `v^j-(v-t)^j>=t^j`. Summing nonnegative coefficients and using `|u-v|<=1` gives

```
F_x(y)-F_x(z*)-gradient F_x(z*)^T(y-z*)
 >= mu ||y-z*||_infinity^(P+1).
```

The first-order term is nonnegative since `y` is feasible and `z*` minimizes a differentiable convex function on that polytope. Combining with (4) and (7),

```
||q-z*||_infinity
 <= K delta + ((L K delta+zeta)/mu)^(1/(P+1)).                   (8)
```

This replaces the sharper exact identity available for a single equality. Constants may be exponentially large or small numerically, but their logarithms are polynomial. Taking `delta` to a power proportional to `P+1` therefore costs only polynomially many accuracy bits.

The true response is continuous on `X'`. Along any sequence of feasible leaders, select optimal multipliers from the common compact box (3). Any convergent subsequence of leaders and multipliers has a continuous clipped-inverse response limit satisfying feasibility and complementarity, hence equal to the unique follower optimum at the limit. Every response subsequential limit is therefore the same. In particular the upper objective attains its minimum on nonempty `X'`.

## 5. Polynomial surrogate in `r+k` variables

If `c=0`, solve the leader LP over `X'`. Otherwise put

```
epsilon=2^(-B), A_c=sum_i |c_i|,
S=max(1,max_j sum_i |C_ji|), tau=epsilon/[16(1+A_c)],
eta=min{1/4, tau, tau/(6 S K),
        mu (tau/2)^(P+1)/[3 S (1+L K+k Lambda)]}.                (9)
```

For `k=0`, use the reviewed diagonal algorithm with the positive inverse lemma. Suppose henceforth `k>=1`. Every number in (9) is rational, with encoding polynomial in the input, numerical `P`, and `B`.

Apply the [reviewed inverse approximation lemma](../notes/certified-positive-polynomial-inverse-approximation.md) to each `h_i` with uniform error `eta`. It supplies polynomially many rational branches and breakpoints, polynomial degree, and polynomial coefficient bit length. Substitute `lambda=Lambda theta`, `theta in [0,1]^k`. Pull the rational breakpoints back through the affine arguments in (5), and form their arrangement inside `X' times [0,1]^k`. Since `r+k` is fixed, there are polynomially many rational cell closures, including lower-dimensional cells. On each closure `Q`, select valid polynomial branches `p_i(x,theta)` satisfying `|p_i-q_i|<=eta` throughout `Q`.

On this cell set

```
H_Q=d_0+d^T x+sum_i c_i p_i,
E=Cp-b(x), J=lambda^T E, lambda=Lambda theta.
```

Optimize the polynomial `H_Q` on the compact semialgebraic set

```
(x,theta) in Q, E_j<=S eta for every j,
|J|<=k Lambda S eta.                                            (10)
```

At a true optimal leader and an optimal bounded multiplier, feasibility and complementarity imply (10): coordinate approximation changes each residual by at most `S eta` and complementarity by at most `k Lambda S eta`. Thus at least one candidate set is nonempty, and a winning minimizer `v*` satisfies

```
H_Q(v*)<=OPT+A_c eta.                                          (11)
```

Fixed-dimensional real quantifier elimination and algebraic sampling optimize (10) exactly with polynomial degree and bit length. The minimizing-point formula needs `2(r+k)` variables, regardless of `N`. Pairwise comparison of winning cell values avoids constructing a common field containing all follower inverse values. This is the same reviewed algebraic interface as the [bounded-power theorem](../results/bilevel-bounded-power-accuracy-bit-algorithm.md).

## 6. Rational recovery and error ledger

Preserve membership only in the rational cell `Q`, not in the nonlinear set (10). A rational simplex of at most `r+k+1` cell vertices containing `v*` can be found by enumeration in fixed dimension. Rounding its algebraic barycentric weights down on a sufficiently fine rational grid and assigning the remainder to one vertex gives a rational `vhat in Q`, including when `Q` has exact equalities or is lower dimensional.

For every polynomial here, the sum `max(1,sum_nu |a_nu||nu|)` bounds its infinity-norm Lipschitz constant on the unit cube. Choose the rounding distance small enough that

```
|H_Q(vhat)-H_Q(v*)|<=epsilon/8,
|E_j(vhat)-E_j(v*)|<=S eta,
|J(vhat)-J(v*)|<=k Lambda S eta.                                (12)
```

The necessary logarithmic grid precision is polynomial in the existing coefficients and `log(1/eta)`. Since `vhat in Q`, its leader `xhat` belongs to `X'` exactly. The polynomial constraints now obey twice the allowances in (10).

At `vhat`, the true box-Lagrangian response `q` therefore satisfies

```
(Cq-b)_j<=3S eta=delta,
|lambda^T(Cq-b)|<=3k Lambda S eta=k Lambda delta.
```

By (8) and (9), `K delta<=tau/2` and `((L K+k Lambda)delta/mu)^(1/(P+1))<=tau/2`. Consequently `||q-z*(xhat)||_infinity<=tau`. The response approximation gives

```
|H(xhat)-H_Q(vhat)|<=A_c(eta+tau)<=epsilon/8.                    (13)
```

Combining (11)-(13),

```
H(xhat)<=OPT+A_c eta+epsilon/8+epsilon/8
        <=OPT+5epsilon/16<OPT+epsilon.
```

The rational value `H_Q(vhat)` is itself an optimum estimate: (11)-(12) bound it above by `OPT+3epsilon/16`, while exact leader feasibility and (13) bound it below by `OPT-epsilon/8`. Its absolute error is less than `epsilon`. Exact evaluation of the polynomial at the rational recovered point has polynomial bit length.

The algorithm returns an exactly feasible rational leader and an objective estimate. It need not return a rational vector that is exactly the follower optimum, or even an exactly feasible auxiliary approximate follower vector.

## 7. Complexity, source boundaries, and remaining audit status

Every coefficient and precision parameter above has polynomial bit length. Although `K` is a deliberately loose conditioning bound, only `log K` enters the branch construction and rounding precision. The factor `(tau/2)^(P+1)` likewise costs polynomially many bits for numerical degree `P`. The arrangement, polynomial expansions, algebraic optimization, and rational recovery have fixed dimension `r+k`. Thus all running times are polynomial in the stated input and accuracy parameters.

The sparse binary-power rational-output obstruction embeds in the diagonal special case. Dense arbitrary quadratic coupling is outside the separable model and retains the separately established hardness. Extra upper response constraints, leader-dependent resource matrices, and unbounded resource-row counts are not covered.

The KKT representation, Hoffman repair, polynomial convexity bounds, and fixed-dimensional real algebraic optimization are established tools. A focused primary-source comparison accompanies the one-resource closeout. The candidate contribution is the combined global bilevel accuracy-bit guarantee for arbitrarily many separable algebraic response coordinates and a fixed resource dimension, with exact rational feasible-leader output. Both independent full audits passed, with no substantive correction; the signed-marginal transfer has a separate explicit addendum in each review.

## 8. Extension to arbitrary strictly increasing polynomial marginals

The [general inverse lemma](../notes/certified-monotone-polynomial-inverse-approximation.md) has passed two independent full audits. Both fixed-resource auditors independently verified the transfer below and recorded PASS addenda.
It removes the nonnegative-coefficient restriction, without adding a new resource structure.

Suppose instead that each rational dense marginal `h_i` is strictly increasing on `[0,1]`, with arbitrary signed coefficients. Set `g_i=h_i-h_i(0)` and replace `ell_i(x)` by `ell_i(x)-h_i(0)`. The resulting `g_i` satisfies `g_i(0)=0`, `g_i(1)=G_i>0`, and `0<=g_i(z)<=G_i`. All feasibility, multiplier, Lipschitz, and rational-recovery arguments in Sections 2–3 and 5–6 are unchanged. Replace the positive-coefficient inverse lemma by the general inverse lemma; only a polynomial branch-count/degree/encoding guarantee is needed by the fixed-dimensional construction.

For completeness, the required replacement for uniform convexity follows from an elementary inverse modulus. Let `g:[0,1]->[0,1]` be a normalized strictly increasing polynomial of degree `d>=1`. On an interval `[a,a+s]`, put `v=g(a+s)-g(a)`. Lagrange interpolation of `g-g(a)` on the `d+1` equally spaced points of that interval gives, at each endpoint `t in {0,1}`,

```
|g(t)-g(a)| <= v (d/s)^d sum_(j=0)^d 1/[j!(d-j)!]
             <= v (2d/s)^d.
```

The numerator factors of every Lagrange basis polynomial have absolute value at most one, while the denominator has magnitude `(s/d)^d j!(d-j)!`. Subtracting the two endpoint values therefore gives

```
g(a+s)-g(a) >= (s/(2d))^d/2.                                  (14)
```

For the integral cost and displacement `s=|u-v|`, the Bregman integral has a subinterval of length `s/2` on which the marginal difference is at least `G_i (s/(4d))^d/2`. This works in both displacement directions: integrate the marginal difference at distances at least `s/2` from the base point. Consequently the coordinate Bregman divergence is at least

```
G_i s^(d+1)/[4(4d)^d]
 >= G_i s^(P+1)/[4(4P)^P].
```

Use `mu=min_i G_i/[4(4P)^P]` in (8) and (9). Its positive rational encoding has polynomial length in numerical `P` and the coefficient encodings. The entire error ledger and polynomial accuracy-bit conclusion follow unchanged.

Thus the theorem holds for arbitrary dense strictly convex univariate polynomial local follower costs, with fixed leader and resource dimensions. Their derivatives may vanish at interior points and their coefficients may have either sign. Strict monotonicity is a stated promise, not a quantitative strong-convexity assumption.

## 9. Verification and process-systems interpretation

The two independent standard-library exact checkers are
[the first review diagnostic](../code/bilevel_bounded_power/check_fixed_resource_first_review.py) and
[the second review diagnostic](../code/bilevel_bounded_power/check_fixed_resource_second.py).
They passed 512 exact projection/ray comparisons, 504 approximate-dual and repair cases, 6,542 positive-power Bregman inequalities, and 3,866 signed-polynomial modulus/Bregman cases. The cases include dependent resource rows, equalities, signed and tiny coefficients, box saturation, and strictly increasing marginals with stationary interior points. These checks supplement the general proofs; the quantifier-elimination optimizer is a proved complexity construction, not an implemented solver.

A process-systems interpretation is a population of independently operated units with strictly convex local operating costs and a fixed number of shared utility, material, or emission budgets. A fixed number of leader controls changes prices or resource availability. The upper objective can value individual unit responses with arbitrary signs. The theorem shows polynomial dependence on requested accuracy bits for that structural model, even though the induced upper objective need not be convex and the number of units grows. It does not model arbitrary nonlinear interactions among units or impose additional response-dependent upper feasibility constraints.
