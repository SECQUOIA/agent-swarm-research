\begin{abstract}
In pooling, every stream leaving a pool carries the pool's mixture, so
blending-network flows are coupled by bilinear quality equations.
Rational threshold decision is complete for the existential theory of
the reals ($\ETR$) with one two-sided attribute and no bypasses, even
with bounded degrees and fixed finite data apart from the threshold, and
also with one pool, bypasses, and a growing number of attributes. Hence
pooling lies in $\NP$ only if $\NP=\ETR$; the reductions also give
rational instances whose unique optimal flow has arbitrarily high
algebraic degree. If the number of pools times the affine quality rank,
or times the number of products, is fixed, LP basis indices give $\NP$
certificates. One-attribute pooling is strongly $\NP$-complete, and its
profit has no PTAS unless $\PP=\NP$, when all four layer degrees equal
two. With bypasses, one pool with one scalar quality, two feed arcs, two
outlet arcs, and fixed finite physical data is strongly $\NP$-complete.
For this interface with input degree at most two and positive flow lower
bounds allowed, feasibility is polynomial at product degree at most two and
strongly $\NP$-complete at degree three. Polynomial exact algorithms
follow from independent bounded blocks, planar composition along
degree-two bypass paths, and exact supply and demand contracts. For the
rank-one block used in pooling relaxations, we prove the general-cost
hardness conjectured by Dey, Kocuk and Santana, polynomial optimization
at fixed cost interaction rank, and exponential or superpolynomial lower
bounds on exact conic lifts of its unit-margin hull and on outer
approximations within inverse-quadratic $\ell_1$ error.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}
Pooling models blending networks in refining and other process
industries \citep{haverly1978-studies-of-the-behavior-of-recursion}.
Inputs of known quality are mixed in pools, every stream leaving a pool
carries the pool's mixture, and products must meet quality
specifications. Direct input-to-product arcs, called \emph{bypasses},
compete with pool feeds for source capacity. Once pool compositions are
fixed, flow conservation, capacities, and product specifications form a
linear system; choosing the compositions makes the problem a nonconvex
bilinear program. The classical p-, q-, and pq-formulations
\citep{haverly1978-studies-of-the-behavior-of-recursion,
bental1994-global-minimization-by-reducing,
tawarmalani2002-convexification-and-global-optimization-in} use pool
qualities or source proportions, and later work strengthens their
relaxations and discretizations
\citep{dey2015-analysis-of-milp-techniques-for,
gupte2017-relaxations-and-discretizations-for-the,
boland2016-new-multi-commodity-flow-formulations,
dey2020-convexifications-of-rank-one-based}. This paper asks which
restrictions on the network, data, and contracts make exact pooling
tractable, and what an exact answer must contain.

\paragraph{Known complexity.}
\citet{alfaki2013-strong-formulations-for-the-pooling} proved that
pooling is strongly NP-hard, even with one pool and no bypasses; their
reduction uses a growing number of attributes.
\citet{haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the} showed that
NP-hardness persists with one attribute, two sources, and two products;
that it becomes strong with one attribute and either two sources or two
products; and that strong NP-hardness also holds when all in-degrees,
or all out-degrees, are at most two. One pool without bypasses is
polynomial with a fixed number of attributes
\citep[Proposition~2]{alfaki2013-strong-formulations-for-the-pooling},
products \citep[Theorem~2]{haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the},
or inputs \citep{s3:haugland-hendrix2016,s4:boland2017}. \citet{s3:baltean2018}
give strongly polynomial algorithms for one-quality subclasses with
fixed positive demands and without feed-availability and pool-capacity
bounds. \citet[author manuscript, Section~4]{s4:boland2017} ask whether
scalar-quality pooling becomes polynomial when all in-degrees, or all
out-degrees, are at most two. They and
\citet[Section~6]{haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the} ask
which one-pool results extend to more pools, Haugland specifically for a
fixed number of pools with bounded numbers of inputs, products, or
qualities.

These results classify combinatorial hardness. A more basic question
remains: does exact pooling decision have short discrete certificates at
all? A rational instance need not have a rational optimal flow
(\Cref{s2:tiny-irrational}). The quadratic formulation places the
threshold problem in $\ETR$, the class of problems polynomially
reducible to deciding whether a system of rational polynomial equations
and inequalities has a real solution
\citep{schaefer2024-the-existential-theory-of-the}; here
$\NP\subseteq\ETR\subseteq\mathsf{PSPACE}$
\citep[Section~1.1]{abrahamsen2019-dynamic-toolbox-for-etrinv}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Table~\ref{s0:boundaries} summarizes the main physical boundaries.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Existential-real completeness} (Section~\ref{s2:algebraic}).
Threshold decision is $\ETR$-complete with one attribute having lower
and upper product specifications, binary source qualities, and no
bypasses (\Cref{s2:etr-complete}), also with in-degrees at most two,
out-degrees at most three, and all numbers except the threshold in fixed
finite sets (\Cref{s2:bounded}), and with one pool, bypasses, and a
growing attribute dimension (\Cref{s2:one-pool-etr}). Hence threshold
decision lies in $\NP$ only if $\NP=\ETR$. With exact positive node
contracts, feasibility is $\ETR$-complete in both classes
(\Cref{s2:variants} and the paragraph after \Cref{s2:one-pool-etr});
whether one upper-bounded attribute suffices remains open. The
reductions preserve solution sets up to rational bijections, so for
every irrational real algebraic number $\alpha$ some rational instance
has a unique optimal flow whose coordinates generate $\Q(\alpha)$
(\Cref{s2:algebraic-witness}). Irrationality alone would not exclude
$\NP$ membership, because a certificate need not list a flow. The
earlier reductions \citep{alfaki2013-strong-formulations-for-the-pooling,
haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the} encode combinatorial
problems; ours realize addition and inversion through exact product
quality specifications. To the best of our knowledge, the pooling
literature compared in Section~\ref{s2:algebraic} contains no previous
$\ETR$-completeness result for these two classes. Without bypasses,
one-pool pooling lies in $\NP$ for any number of attributes
(\Cref{s2:no-bypass-np}), so the one-pool hardness needs bypasses unless
$\NP=\ETR$. If the number of pools times the affine input-quality rank,
or times the number of products, is fixed, a guessed LP basis is a
polynomial-size certificate (\Cref{s2:pooling-np}); this gives $\NP$
membership, not a polynomial algorithm or rational optimal flows.

\item \emph{Hardness near the algorithmic regimes}
(Section~\ref{s3:hardness}). One-attribute pooling is strongly
NP-complete when all four layer degrees equal two, with unit capacities
and only upper specifications, and its profit has no PTAS unless
$\PP=\NP$ (\Cref{s3:all-two-thm}). If its zero upper quality bounds are
replaced by a suitable fixed positive bound, exact optimization remains strongly
NP-hard and the profit still has no PTAS unless $\PP=\NP$
(\Cref{s3:tolerance}). This answers both degree questions
of \citet{s4:boland2017} negatively. With two pools and two products on
an undirected tree, and growing input and attribute counts, threshold
decision is NP-complete in the ordinary sense (\Cref{s3:two-pools}),
which answers the product-bounded case of the two-pool question
negatively; the subfamily used in the reduction has an FPTAS in its
given representation (\Cref{s3:fptas}). With bypasses, one pool with one
upper-bounded scalar quality, two actual feed arcs, two actual outlet
arcs, and fixed finite physical data is strongly NP-complete
(\Cref{s3:constant-thm}); the many bypass sources are not mixing feeds.
Broad one-pool hardness with capacities was already stated by
\citet[Remark~4.6]{s3:baltean2018}; our contribution is the explicit
reduction under these simultaneous restrictions. Feasibility remains
strongly NP-complete when only five external nodes lack exact contracts
and ordinary products may have three bypass inlets (\Cref{s3:five-thm}).

\item \emph{Structural exact algorithms} (Section~\ref{s4:structural}).
These apply when the linear network can be eliminated without losing
pool balances or the objective. A fixed nonlinear core coupled to
independent polyhedral blocks of fixed dimension admits polynomial
global optimization with an exact algebraic optimizer
(\Cref{s4:fixed-core}). Consequences include fixed input and pool counts
with arbitrary bypasses, fixed pool and attribute counts without
bypasses (\Cref{s4:fixed-inputs} and \Cref{s4:fixed-qualities}), which answer the
input- and quality-bounded cases of Haugland's question, and fixed pool
count, affine quality rank, and bypass vertex integrity
(\Cref{s4:vertex-integrity}). On degree-two bypass graphs, composition
of planar relations along paths (\Cref{s4:path-projection}) gives
polynomial feasibility with a fixed number of pool attachments and
arbitrary attributes, and optimization of objectives on a fixed set of
retained arcs (\Cref{s4:bounded-attachments} and \Cref{s4:fixed-support}).
For one pool with two feed and two outlet arcs, input out-degree at most
two, and positive flow lower bounds allowed, feasibility is polynomial at product in-degree at most two and strongly
NP-complete at three (\Cref{s4:degree-boundary}). This boundary concerns
feasibility; the degree-two algorithm does not cover dense objectives
such as the economic threshold of \Cref{s3:constant-thm}.

\item \emph{Contract algorithms} (Section~\ref{s5:contracts}). Exact
contracts turn pool mass and attribute mass into conserved boundary
quantities, or into signed node divergences. For one pool with bypass
degree at most two this gives polynomial algorithms for exact supplies
with a fixed number of receiving products (\Cref{s5:fixed-products});
for feasibility under full contracts with scalar or affine-rank-one
qualities, with quadratic-field witnesses when the common pool bound has
lower bound zero and a redundant upper bound (\Cref{s5:quadratic-field}),
and with restrictive common bounds and throughput objectives
(\Cref{s5:restrictive-capacity} and \Cref{s5:throughput-optimization});
and for a fixed number of exceptions to otherwise exact contracts, with
arbitrary quality rank and redundant common bounds
(\Cref{s5:arbitrary-exceptions}), in contrast to the five-exception
hardness of \Cref{s3:five-thm}. Feasibility with two distinct full
input-quality vectors is polynomial on arbitrary bypass graphs
(\Cref{s5:two-vector-theorem}). Table~\ref{s5:scope-table} gives the
precise scopes; restrictions from different theorems do not combine
without an additional argument.

\item \emph{Response geometry and convexification}
(Section~\ref{s6:synthesis} and the appendices). As one product price
varies, a pool-free blending path has $2^n$ distinct unique optimal
flows (\Cref{s6:price-response}). A one-pool extension has $2^n$
isolated global optima and, after a price perturbation, at least $2^n$
strict local optima, yet it has a polynomial-size exact LP hull
(\Cref{s6:vertex-forcing}, \Cref{s6:local}, and \Cref{s6:lp-hull}). For the isolated
rank-one margin block used in pooling relaxations
\citep{dey2020-convexifications-of-rank-one-based}, we prove the
general-cost hardness conjectured by Dey, Kocuk, and Santana
(\Cref{app:margin-hardness} and \Cref{app:margin-certificates}) and polynomial
exact optimization for fixed cost interaction rank
(\Cref{s6:rank-theorem}). The unit-margin hull $K_{2m}$ has a
correlation-polytope face (\Cref{app:correlation-face}). With known
correlation-polytope bounds \citep{s6:fawzi2013,s6:lrs-full,s6:bfps2015},
this gives exponential or superpolynomial size lower bounds for its
exact conic lifts (\Cref{app:exact-conic}) and for outer approximations
within total entrywise $\ell_1$ error $1/A_m$ (LP) or $1/(2A_m)$ (SDP),
where $A_m=m(184m+6)$ (\Cref{app:approx-lp} and \Cref{app:approx-sdp}).
These uniform bounds do not concern ordinary additive operating costs or
a single objective, and are consistent with the exponential
second-order-cone representation of \citet{s6:jalilian2026}.

\item \emph{Physical foundations} (Section~\ref{s1:foundations}). We
give physical-flow proofs of established tools: destination
decomposition and the zero-optimum test of
\citet{xiang2026-dive-and-fix} (\Cref{s1:sign}), an exact uncapacitated
conic hull and its cyclic extension (\Cref{s1:cone-theorem} and
\Cref{s1:cyclic-sign}),
and a characterization of the product specifications that guarantee
integral optimal flows (\Cref{s1:facial}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Organization.}
Section~\ref{s1:foundations} fixes the model conventions (exact
contracts, standard economics) and exact arithmetic; fixed parameters
may enter polynomial exponents, and no fixed-parameter tractability
claim is implied. Sections~\ref{s2:algebraic} and~\ref{s3:hardness}
prove algebraic completeness and restricted hardness;
Sections~\ref{s4:structural} and~\ref{s5:contracts} develop structural
and contract algorithms. Section~\ref{s6:synthesis} discusses
representation consequences and the remaining degree-two bypass
question. Appendix~\ref{app:geometry} proves the response-geometry
results and limits of elimination, including dense-slab hardness.
Appendices~\ref{app:costs} and~\ref{app:convexification} treat costs and
convexification of the isolated margin block \eqref{s6:margin-block}
and can be read independently of
Sections~\ref{s2:algebraic}--\ref{s5:contracts}.
