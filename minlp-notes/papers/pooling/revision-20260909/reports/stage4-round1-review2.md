The principal mathematical checks supporting the verdict are these:

- **Fixed interaction rank, Appendix B:17–113.** The double-centering matrix really is the minimum attainable rank after additive row/column subtraction: the two annihilating linear maps preserve its value for every subtraction, and the displayed subtraction attains that rank. The projected boxes include both additive cost coordinates and the shared total. Alternating minimization on the fixed-total slices can choose a vertex in each projection while preserving a global optimum. A slice vertex lies on a vertex or edge of the original projected box. Enumerating all vertex pairs covers those edges; the additional chords only introduce feasible candidates. Stored corner interpolation gives original margins, including when the projection loses information. Equal-total edges cannot create omitted slice vertices. The scalar objective is exactly `alpha*S + beta + gamma/S`; positive endpoints, the positive-alpha/positive-gamma stationary point, constant pieces, and zero are sufficient. Comparing quadratic-field objective values does not require a common field of growing degree across the whole candidate list: pairwise comparisons have degree at most four. The stated fixed-rank polynomial bound and one-quadratic-field output are therefore supported.
- **Rank-one specialization and oracle, Appendix B:124–182.** The four scalar endpoint products cover all sign choices. Continuous knapsack supplies the breakpoint and recovery bounds. The factored-input proviso avoids understating the cost of reading a dense matrix or writing the full optimizer. Terminal upper/lower multipliers change interaction rank by at most the attribute rank; the remaining constraints must still be precisely the margin block. The text correctly makes no duality-gap or coupled-pooling claim.
- **Hardness, Appendix B:199–280.** Dummy margins remove the equal-total obstruction without changing the signed biclique numerator. A selected nonedge incurs at least one unit before the threshold constant; separate minimization over the two boxes permits a binary optimum. All cost magnitudes and dimensions are polynomially bounded, and the total is at most `2h+1`, so the positive no-instance gap is valid. The repair handles both `S>=1` and `0<=S<1`; `L=2B+1` strictly improves every point outside the required two-margin face. The shifted threshold is negative, so the otherwise feasible zero matrix does not invalidate the reduction.
- **NP membership and output fields, Appendix B:282–365.** Fixing the at-most-one-free-coordinate pattern on each margin slice gives affine margins on a closed rational interval. Minimizing the fractional objective produces the claimed rational or quadratic-field optimum. For a rational threshold, multiplication by positive total produces a rational quadratic whose minimizer on a rational interval can be chosen rational. The proof explicitly excludes a spurious zero-total witness and handles the constant case. Thus irrational exact optima do not contradict rational yes certificates. Enumeration of one small margin dimension, affine weight crossings, and greedy column fills supports the separate `d*2^d*poly(...)` bound.
- **Physical geometry, Appendix A.** I expanded the telescoping identity and checked the exposing objective. In the path realization, capacity gives the lower strip inequality and the midpoint specification gives the upper one. The relay resets preserve the same free coordinates. The exact penalty invokes a proved coefficient-controlled Hoffman constant and makes only polynomial encoding, not polynomial magnitude, claims. In the one-pool interface all actual flows and active quality are affine in `(x,z)`, while the sole new quality restriction gives `z>=t-t^2`. The stated economics yield `d^T*x-z`. The local-optimum derivative is strictly negative on every incident edge, and the denominator bound makes the chosen perturbation small enough. The LP hull proof returns an optimal vertex, rather than assuming every LP optimizer is physical. The parity lower bound concerns the finite optimal set, not its convex hull. The slab construction explicitly retains ordinary rather than strong hardness and makes no degree-two physical-realization claim.
- **Exact conic claims, Appendix C:29–114.** Trace equality first forces common binary margins. The nonnegative cross-pair section removes double selections, and mass `m` then forces exactly one member of every pair. The inverse is affine because the binary vector is read from the diagonal of its correlation matrix. The transfer therefore uses actual faces and an affine isomorphism. The Lorentz-chain conversion preserves total size up to the stated bound. The curved two-by-two unit-margin face establishes nonpolyhedrality separately; the paper correctly acknowledges that the exact finite-LP claim is then vacuous.
- **Approximation claims, Appendix C:126–378.** The generator bounds yield `8m B+116m D+5|S-m|`; averaging the excess-total bound gives precisely `28m B+156m D+5(m-T)`. The outer-set slice adds one further `eta`, and principal-block scaling produces `A_m=m(184m+6)`. The LP transfer uses the correct correlation inequalities and factor-two sandwich. For SDP, minimal-face restriction supplies relative strict feasibility, while the extra scalar PSD block accounts for a valid inequality's positive infimum. The shifted odd-cardinality slack stays in `[0,1]`, retains strictly negative pseudo-expectation for the selected delta, and gives the displayed `k^(-23/4)` prefactor and `k^(13/2)` denominator. Choosing `k` of order `(m/log m)^(2/13)` supports the approximate lower bound. The final discussion correctly stops short of an exponential-in-`m` approximate SOC claim.
- **Consistency of summaries.** The introduction and Section 6 distinguish positive-contract feasibility from zero-lower-bound profit thresholds, physical arc costs from arbitrary rank-one matrix costs, bounded attachments from unbounded attachments, fixed rank from two distinct full vectors, and redundant common bounds from restrictive ones. The degree-two/degree-three feasibility comparison points to the contracted network before its completion objective. The remaining degree-two question is not falsely left open for unrestricted bypass graphs. The common-field guarantee in the structural theorem is supported by joint algebraic sampling and algebraic LP recovery, rather than by combining unrelated algebraic leaf fields.
