Concrete findings requiring correction: none. The following records why several potentially vulnerable claims withstand scrutiny; it is not a list of requested stylistic changes.

- **Physical model and exact arithmetic, section 1 lines 16–190.** The text distinguishes arbitrary physical arc costs from standard source-cost/product-revenue economics, positive threshold hardness from trivial zero-lower-bound feasibility, and a scalar two-sided specification from one upper-only attribute. Zero-throughput qualities are explicitly irrelevant. The compact-lift argument is valid in the standard and acyclic models, and the source-free cyclic exception is explicitly withheld. The affine-coordinate reduction retains every original product inequality and has rational data of polynomial encoding length. No rational-flow implication is drawn merely from rational input data or NP membership.
- **Destination decomposition, single-product projection, sign and hull, section 1 lines 214–404.** Multiplying an arc by the destination fraction at its head scales every intake of a pool equally. This preserves the original pool quality; the recursion supplies the matching outgoing total. Upper node capacities survive coordinatewise domination. The converse single-product LP argument uses quality-mass conservation after reconstructing pool qualities, and does not incorrectly require that the original source routing be preserved. The cost inequalities have the correct direction for nonpositive minima. The shortest-path support bound follows from at most K independent active quality rows plus normalization. Positive common scaling respects all retained capacity rows with polynomial rational encoding. The conic hull statement concerns an uncapacitated scaling-closed set; the manuscript correctly warns that intersecting it with capacities does not generally recover the capacitated convex hull.
- **Cyclic reconstruction and decomposition, section 1 lines 419–547.** A source-free terminal component of the positive flow support is isolated by conservation. In a nonisolated strongly connected component, the backward transition matrix is substochastic and irreducible with an external-inflow deficit, giving a nonsingular mixing system. This supports rational reconstruction and the input-quality hull bound on nonisolated components. The forward absorption probabilities then give dominated terminal components. Negative directed cycles account for profitable isolated circulations. Removing nonnegative-cost cycles from a single-product flow preserves source withdrawals and product delivery; reconstructed qualities establish feasibility afterwards. Merging the isolated circulation into one terminal component justifies retaining the m-product approximation factor. The explicit nearly closed cycle example has determinant epsilon and residual (epsilon,0) for unit concentration error. The proof does not rely on source tracing that the declared algebraic semantics would lack.
- **Facial specifications, section 1 lines 568–715.** The face property gives the exact feed/outlet incompatibilities, including bypasses and empty product faces. Each compatible support branch is a bounded network flow polytope after node splitting; positive lower bounds on deleted arcs must make a branch infeasible, as the text requires. In the converse, nonfaciality supplies a feasible unit blend using some input outside the product region. Charging only those feeds makes a fractional flow strictly better than every integral flow at unit capacity. This proves necessity for the universal guarantee, not hardness. The recognition LP tests precisely whether an allowed blend can use a forbidden input. Fixed affine dimension bounds face enumeration through facet subsets; fixed pool count bounds face tuples. The endpoint disjunction and its throughput big-M bound are exact.
- **Many-pool arithmetic construction, section 2 lines 54–260.** Saturation counts no physical standard arc more than twice. The reciprocal emission has forced quality mass 1/B and hence emitted amount 1/a; the source/product totals copy that amount onto the other source arc. The conversion uses fresh sources and products, so it creates logical dependence without forbidden pool-to-pool arcs. Complementary variable feeds enforce the full [1/2,2] range, including unused variables. The inversion and addition pins reproduce xy=1 and x+y=z. Distinct emission gadgets handle repeated summand names. Storage throughput B=2M+3 leaves at least three slack units in the reverse construction. Given a source solution, all arc flows, including slack and diluent flows, are fixed; only irrelevant zero-flow qualities may remain free.
- **Bounded degrees, finite data and feasibility variants, section 2 lines 264–355.** Alternating reciprocal links supply the requested storage types and at most one equation use per position. At most two non-slack outlets make capacity seven sufficient. All listed local degree, capacity, quality and cost restrictions follow from the complete gadget interfaces. Mandatory first links enforce ranges even for unused variables. Complementary binary attributes correctly translate lower quality bounds to upper bounds. Exact marked-node contracts replace threshold saturation for feasibility. The text properly leaves one-attribute upper-only ETR-hardness unresolved.
- **One-pool construction, section 2 lines 359–481.** Replacing a repeated summand through two inversions has unique auxiliary values and keeps binary attribute data. At the auxiliary product, its own bypass has zero in each pinned attribute; the auxiliary attribute pin refers to a different auxiliary source. The three inversion pins imply Xx*tau1=tau1*tau2=Xy*tau2=1. Addition pins imply Xz*tau=(Xx+Xy)*tau=2. Range pins cover variables appearing in no inversion. All auxiliary intakes are at most two, so B=4N leaves enough filler and slack. There is no hidden extra flow choice, and the growing attribute dimension is explicit.
- **Algebraic witness and irrational optimum, section 2 lines 485–638.** The singleton argument starts from a compact basic closed set, adds uniquely determined nonnegative slack coordinates and polynomial gate values, and uses Toolbox Lemmas C–G. It does not invoke the non-preserving compactification step. Those transformations retain rational equivalence and coordinatewise affine recovery, and their extra interval promises constrain no additional solutions. This supports a singleton ETR-INV flow image with exactly Q(alpha) as its generated field. Saturation makes the threshold a global upper bound on profit, so uniqueness at that threshold gives a unique optimal arc flow. The small irrational example reduces to h(t)=t/(t−1)−2t, with the stated crossover and monotonicity; its throughput denominator is retained. The approximation paragraph promises small residuals for all formulation constraints, not exact feasibility or efficient construction.
- **Certificate theorem and pooling scopes, section 2 lines 644–839.** Bounded fibers have vertices even if lower dimensional. Their active normals contain an n-row basis. Multiplication by Delta squared handles either determinant sign, and Delta squared >0 excludes singular bases. Fixed parameter dimension and polynomial entry degree bound the dense determinant representation; coefficient lengths and interpolation complexity are polynomial. Fixed-dimensional real-algebraic decision therefore verifies only the guessed row indices in polynomial bit time. Including the objective row before taking a vertex is valid. Each pooling parameterization has bounded linear fibers; the outlet-fraction argument checks outlet bounds, no-outlet pools and zero throughput. The one-pool/no-bypass certificate is an active-product LP and does not require every guessed outlet to be positive.
