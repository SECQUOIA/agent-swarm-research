## Mathematical and bit-complexity checks

- **Foundations: model and encoding (Section 1, lines 15–197).** The distinction between physical arc costs and arbitrary rank-one matrix objectives is correct. Homogeneous product specifications and arbitrary finite qualities at inactive pools are handled consistently. The compact-lift argument is valid for standard and acyclic pooling, and the affine-rank substitution preserves every original quality row. It does not silently discard attributes after lowering the nonlinear dimension.
- **Destination decomposition, sign, paths, and cone (Section 1, lines 199–404).** Using the head multiplier in `f^j_uv = f_uv h_j(v)` preserves each pool's incoming quality ratios, while the recursion provides outgoing conservation. Each component is dominated coordinatewise, so both arc and node capacities survive. The single-product projection is exact in an acyclic network. The averaging inequalities have the correct directions for minimization with nonpositive optima. The path-mixture support bound is `K+1`, including simultaneous lower and upper coordinate bounds, because those active rows have rank at most `K`. Positive scaling restores all retained capacities with polynomial rational length. The conic-hull proof correctly uses finite conic combinations and derives closedness from the polyhedral extended formulation; it does not assume closedness of a general conic hull. The stated two-product example correctly separates convexification before and after imposing capacities.
- **Cyclic extension (Section 1, lines 406–547).** The strongly connected component argument correctly separates isolated circulations from components with boundary flow. On the latter, the backward substochastic matrix has spectral radius below one, and the resulting global rational system gives the stated determinant bit bound. In the forward decomposition, a terminal component without product outflow cannot have external inflow; removing it therefore leaves no upstream flow into a removed component. All remaining positive-support pools reach a product. The extra circulation component is physical, and merging it with a product component is justified by disjoint positive support. Negative cycles, empty product sets, attainment, and the near-singular residual example are treated correctly. The manuscript does not rely on an invertibility claim for an isolated circulation.
- **Faces and integrality (Section 1, lines 549–715).** The face property gives the exact support compatibility condition in both directions. Each support branch is a bounded network-flow polytope, and node splitting preserves integrality with integer bounds. The nonface construction really has a negative-cost fractional mixture while every integral feasible flow has cost zero. The recognition LP tests all representations of a feasible mixture, which is necessary for its converse. Enumerating facet candidates in the affine hull and intersections of at most `d` active facets yields the claimed polynomial bound for fixed pool count and affine dimension. Positive lower bounds on deleted arcs are explicitly checked. The endpoint disjunctions and the two big-M inequalities have consistent binary orientation.
- **Saturation and many-pool ETR reduction (Section 2, lines 31–260).** A standard arc participates in at most two marked groups, so the cost alphabet is correct. Both directions of the reciprocal emission, quality conversion, inversion, and addition constructions hold. Variable complements enforce the full `[1/2,2]` interval even for unused variables. Repeated variable names do not identify fresh gadget nodes. The bound `B=2M+3` covers every non-slack outlet and leaves positive slack. Each actual arc flow, including diluent and slack flows, is uniquely determined; inactive pool qualities are correctly excluded from the bijection claim.
- **Finite data and one-pool reductions (Section 2, lines 262–481).** Alternating chains supply the requested intake types with at most two non-slack outlets per storage pool; `B=7` suffices. Root advancement enforces ranges independently of equation occurrences. The finite alphabets, local degrees, linear graph size, and half-integer threshold bound agree with the construction. In the one-pool construction, repeated-summand normalization preserves the source set rationally; each pinned bypass has quality zero in its pinned coordinate. Inversion pins force both the source equation and variable ranges. Addition pins give `tau=2/z`, whose upper bound follows from `z=x+y>=1`. Filler and slack amounts preserve the marked total without an extra free flow. The dense quality table has quadratic, hence polynomial, size. Complementary attributes implement lower bounds without proving an unsupported one-upper-attribute hardness result.
- **Arbitrary algebraic degree (Section 2, lines 483–542).** The proof starts from a basic closed singleton and adds uniquely determined nonnegative slacks and polynomial gates. This is a compact ETR-CONJ instance, so Lemmas C–G of the cited toolbox apply without Lemma B. The actual transformations retain an original coordinate under a rational nonzero scaling and shift; they introduce rationally determined values, and their narrow intervals are promises already implied by the equations and ambient bounds. These facts support a singleton ETR-INV set and equality of generated fields, rather than merely preservation of satisfiability. The pooling bijections retain that coordinate. Saturation makes the unique threshold flow the unique optimum. The statement is existential in the chosen algebraic number and does not claim polynomial size in an unspecified encoding of that number.
- **Irrational optimum and approximate descriptions (Section 2, lines 544–631).** The elimination of the clean flow and the piecewise optimization in throughput are valid; the critical point and value satisfy the displayed quadratic exactly. The approximate-description paragraph claims only existence of short rational points with absolute polynomial residual tolerance, not exact rational feasibility or a polynomial construction algorithm.
- **Basis certificates and applications (Section 2, lines 633–839).** Bounded nonempty fibers have vertices even when lower-dimensional, whose active normals span the full flow-variable space. The certificate selects an invertible basis. Multiplication by `Delta^2` is sign-safe, and strict exclusion of the singular locus is essential and present. Degrees, coefficient heights, fixed-dimensional dense arrays, evaluation/interpolation, formula length, and rational arithmetic all have polynomial bounds under the stated explicit polynomial representation and degree assumption. The appended objective threshold preserves boundedness. The quality, affine-rank, and outlet-fraction applications preserve all contracts, bypasses, and actual arc costs. Zero-throughput pools and pools with no outlets are handled. The one-pool/no-bypass subset certificate remains sound if a selected outlet receives zero flow.
