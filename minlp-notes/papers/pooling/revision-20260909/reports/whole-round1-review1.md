**Findings.** There are no required major or minor findings. Two optional editorial changes would improve the entry points without changing the mathematics.

1. **WR1-O1 — Optional; make the abstract's degree refinement unambiguous.** Locator: `sections/00-introduction.tex:6–7`, read with `s2:bounded` and `s2:one-pool-etr`. “The reductions preserve small degrees and bounded data” is less precise than the body. The no-bypass reduction has a fixed finite data alphabet and bounds every local degree; the one-pool reduction bounds external degrees but explicitly lets the pool degrees and attribute count grow. A reader scanning the abstract could apply the stronger interpretation to both. The complete statements are correct, so this is not a mathematical defect. Suggested repair: replace the sentence with wording such as “The no-bypass reduction also has bounded local degrees and fixed finite data; both reductions transfer algebraic witness obstructions.” The one-pool external-degree refinement can remain in its theorem.

2. **WR1-O2 — Optional; distinguish the role of the general path tools from the contract classifications.** Locator: `sections/05-contract-algorithms.tex:99–315`, especially the paragraph at lines 102–108 and Theorems `s5:quasipoly-path`, `s5:strip`. These are mathematically useful results, but several pages of general projection analysis precede the first contract theorem. The subsequent physical algorithms primarily use the rational endpoint theorem, exact conservation, signed cuts, and the separate convex reduction. The text acknowledges the quasipolynomial tool's limits, yet the opening phrase about later “strip structure” can leave the reader expecting a direct theorem invocation that never arrives. Suggested repair: state explicitly which results are supporting context for the open boundary and which are used in the classifications; alternatively move the two general tools to a supporting appendix and retain their statements near the structural discussion. Their proofs should be preserved.

**Mathematical assessment.** The following records the substantive checks behind the verdict.

In Section 1, the use of homogeneous quality mass at zero product flow and the treatment of inactive pools are consistent throughout. Destination disaggregation multiplies arcs by the head's absorption fraction, which is the correct choice for preserving the mixture at a pool; multiplying by the tail fraction would not prove the same claim. Coordinatewise domination preserves precisely the upper-bound hypotheses stated. The single-product LP reconstructs qualities in topological order and aggregate quality conservation proves its converse. The cone identity is on the positive-capacity support, so its scaling argument does not silently restore removed zero capacities. Reimposing capacities after convexification is correctly excluded by the example.

The cyclic extension is explicitly an algebraic steady-state model. Its isolated circulations can be assigned a common quality, and the nonsingular components use a backward substochastic system with an exit. Terminal components without product outflow have no external inflow by conservation, so the separation into isolated circulations and absorbing components is sound. The negative-cycle alternative, approximation averaging, and bounded closed lift remain consistent with these semantics. They should not be advertised as a source-traced initialization model; the paper does not do so. The facial integrality theorem and converse are valid as universal guarantees over networks and objectives. Enumeration of faces uses fixed affine dimension and correctly checks positive lower bounds before deleting arcs.

In Section 2, the saturation threshold counts actual node totals and gives at most two contributions to a standard arc cost. Reciprocal emission uses a saturated product's exact quality mass to force a positive reciprocal. The complementary variable and mandatory initial emissions enforce both ends of the ETR-INV interval, including unused variables. The conversion creates an equal amount on a fresh input arc; it does not smuggle in a pool-to-pool arc or duplicate physical material. Addition, inversion, and repeated variable names are treated correctly. The storage capacity and slack estimates cover all simultaneous uses. The alternating chains preserve the initial variable flows and make the finite-data degree statement plausible at the exact stated alphabet.

The one-pool construction uses dedicated attributes with zero quality on the associated bypass source. Its three inversion pins enforce the intended reciprocal chain, and the addition pin has a positive multiplier. Repeated summands are normalized before a binary support attribute is created. The filler and slack bounds have enough room. These facts support the exact flow bijections, not merely equisatisfiability. For the arbitrary algebraic witness result I checked the compact-conjunctive entry into the cited toolbox: the paper deliberately avoids the general compactification step and uses uniquely determined gates. Its restricted singleton argument is supported by the primary source. The rational threshold is a saturation upper bound, so the claimed unique threshold flow is indeed the unique optimal arc flow.

The NP certificate proof does not confuse a fixed parameter dimension with an efficient enumeration of bases. At a vertex of a lower-dimensional bounded fiber there are still enough independent active rows in the ambient flow coordinates. Multiplying each reconstructed row by the determinant a second time gives the correct sign-independent inequality. Explicit fixed-dimensional polynomial arrays, determinant degree, coefficient growth, and interpolation cost are addressed. The outlet-fraction certificate handles arbitrary attributes and checks unusable pools before removal. The tiny irrational example has the correct throughput denominator, and its piecewise monotonicity gives the stated optimum.

In Section 3, the occurrence reduction and private triangles support the bipartite orientation source with the claimed degree and capacity restrictions. The all-degree-two construction has a stronger property than the bare hardness reduction needs: cleanup preserves profit and only reduces physical arcs, then a matching LP produces an integral selection. After merging lax products, the new pure-mode capacity is already enforced by the shared dirty input. The independent-set identity and its recovery direction are correct; the additive baseline in the approximation transfer is controlled by the cubic independent-set lower bound. The positive-quality tolerance proposition proves the weaker claims stated and does not falsely retain the zero-tolerance optimum identity.

The Matsui argument supplies its own large-base separation and does not rely on the overly broad estimate it identifies in the report. Polynomial bit length is distinct from bounded numerical magnitude. The two-pool tree uses output fractions for NP membership with a growing attribute count. The represented-family FPTAS only needs the stated affine polytope representation and does not contradict ordinary hardness. Copy, half-copy, separate cycles, and source splitting all retain the individual supply equations needed by the gadgets. Full and half occurrences are not incorrectly mixed in the same saturation cycle.

The exact-penalty argument repairs the copy polytope before making the primary intake radial repair. It bounds changes in the actual standard-economic objective separately from changes in intake reward after contracts have been relaxed. This distinction is necessary and is present. The finite-data circuit construction encodes large rational coefficients in topology; its physical completion objective forces every removed positive contract separately. The contracted feasibility version retains the encoded source threshold inside the network. The five-exception grouping uses disjoint unused mates and checks their qualities and exact supplies, preserving the two actual feeds and outlets. The matched degree-two/degree-three feasibility comparison therefore compares the same decision type and permits the necessary positive contracts.

Section 4's fixed-core theorem is more than an appeal to a fixed-dimensional LP. It enumerates sign conditions jointly in the nonlinear core and support directions, including zeros, and chooses local maximizing bases without an exponential Cartesian product. The support formula has polynomial rather than constant degree after denominator products, which is enough in fixed dimension. Empty and lower-dimensional blocks are accounted for. The global feasible set is compact because the core is compact and every block has a uniform finite box. Recovery is in one polynomial-degree field; the proof does not multiply degrees of unrelated algebraic coefficients. The cited algebraic LP result supports this common-field use.

The pooling ownership rules retain input, pool, and product balances. In particular, covered-product coordinate masses avoid an unbounded aggregate-row count when ambient attributes grow but affine rank is fixed. The planar composition proof uses convex and concave boundary envelopes to bound row growth, then balanced composition to bound both degree-free rational descriptions and bit length. Degenerate planar sets and duplicate-row deletion are considered. Lifting takes rational affine combinations of retained endpoints, so it needs no new field extension. The attachment algorithm retains every row involving pool-attached external nodes. Its optimization theorems correctly restrict objectives to retained coordinates or a supplied exact equality reduction.

In Section 5, fixed receiving products allow mass and full attribute mass to be computed from boundary flows; detached components can then be lifted independently without losing a pool aggregate. In the fully contracted model, the two global constant identities replace pool conservation but do not replace a restrictive common capacity. The signed-flow formulation handles negative transformed arcs by retaining orientation and changing bound order. Product outlet bounds at unequal inlet qualities give quadratic bounds on one transformed arc, while equal-quality and absent-inlet cases receive separate treatment. On paths and cycles, connected cuts have at most two boundary edges, which is what makes the candidate conjunction polynomial.

The quality-chart argument covers full-vector singularities by original fixed-quality LPs and uses an explicit polynomial list of directions away from them. It retains residual vector equations after scalar projection; a projected quality equation alone would not suffice. Active ordinary outlets confine quality to a plane, and the branch with only exceptional active outlets has its own conserved-boundary treatment. Charts are not closed across vanishing denominators. The objective reduction relies on exact ordinary contracts and properly excludes unrestricted dense arc costs.

The restrictive-capacity algorithm retains total unscaled bypass flow through a support calculation. Its box-truncated divergence base is correctly normalized at the empty and full sets under nonemptiness. The prefix support calculation uses a submodular binary path recurrence, and the combination over many prefixes takes a union of scalar breakpoints rather than a product of partitions. Interpolation of feasible signed flows at fixed quality recovers every intermediate throughput. The two-vector algorithm is a different result: its shared concave quadratic upper-bound term permits the auxiliary variable substitution, and exact convex QP is the invoked algorithm. Its treatment of an optimal QP value of zero and a spurious endpoint quality is sound. Positive outlet or common lower bounds have the wrong inequality direction for that reduction and are explicitly excluded.

Appendix A makes the separation between representation size and computational hardness concrete. The telescoping identity is exact, the physical path map respects capacities and quality inequalities, and the positive-revenue penalty removes only the linear path contracts. The nonlinear interface retains its own exact throughput requirement. Strict local maxima are justified through edge-ray derivatives and a quadratic remainder. The parity lower bound concerns an exact lift of the finite optimal set; the compact LP hull concerns a different set and returns a physically feasible vertex. The dense-slab hardness proposition does not assert an unavailable degree-two physical implementation. The rank-one constraint-perturbation reduction and nonlinear-leaf arithmetic cautions have appropriately limited scope.

Appendix B consistently distinguishes arbitrary matrix costs from additive physical operating costs. The interaction-rank definition is invariant under additive row and column terms. Projected-box slice vertices arise on original vertices or edges; enumerating extra segments is harmless because they remain feasible. A common total reduces every candidate to a rational expression with at most one square-root minimum. The biclique reduction and unit-margin penalty concern exactly the simultaneous-margin set in the earlier conjecture. Rational threshold witnesses are proved using a different quadratic inequality in the total, so they need not inherit an irrational optimizer. The one-fixed-margin-dimension enumeration is a genuine parameter bound separate from the fixed-interaction-rank result.

Appendix C's successive face equations first force equal binary margins, then one selected element per pair. The resulting inverse map is affine and the correlation face is exact. The no-finite-LP conclusion follows from nonpolyhedrality already at order two; it is distinguished from the positive-error LP size statement. I recalculated the coefficients in the generator and convex-combination rounding bounds and the factor (A_m=m(184m+6)). The approximation transfers use total entrywise error and its correct dual norm. The SDP argument uses valid shifted slacks rather than applying the LP sandwich theorem to PSD lifts, and the pseudo-density substitution gives the claimed exponent and polynomial prefactor. Exact exponential SOC dimension, exact unrestricted SDP order, and the weaker approximate SDP lower bound have different scopes; the text maintains them.

**Organization and readiness.** The paper tells a coherent mathematical story: real-algebraic obstruction, restricted combinatorial obstruction, and algorithms that preserve the needed global information. Section 1's background occupies substantial space, but its exact semantics and facial/support results are needed to interpret later claims. Section 3's long progression is justified by the distinct final restrictions: the ordinary-hard and value-preserving versions do not imply the finite-data or contracted-feasibility versions. Section 4 owns the independent-block and physical-boundary decompositions; Section 5 owns the conservation arguments. Moving the response, margin-cost, and conic proofs to appendices keeps those secondary contributions from obscuring the primary classification. Tables 1 and 2 are useful and consistent with the theorem hypotheses.

The proofs are more explicit than typical complexity papers about zero flow, exact versus residual feasibility, binary coefficient size, rational versus algebraic witnesses, and recovery of actual flows. These details are material here. I would retain them through any shortening. The optional changes above would improve the abstract and the transition into contracts; neither requires a new mathematical argument. On the evidence of this independent full review, there is no identified correctness blocker to proceeding with the manuscript, subject to normal external refereeing and the chosen journal's format and length requirements.

Section 2 (printed pp. 2–3) explicitly includes pool-to-pool arcs, finite node and arc upper capacities, and lower and upper terminal quality specifications. The source result is stated for its terminal-proportion formulation `(TP')`, with terminal indices based on reachability. The manuscript's separate explanation of algebraic circulation semantics is therefore an appropriate scope distinction; it does not claim priority for the already established zero-optimum test. This addendum changes neither the verdict nor findings WR1-O1 and WR1-O2.
