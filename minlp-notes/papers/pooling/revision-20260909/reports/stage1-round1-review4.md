## Proof and arithmetic checks

### Foundations

- **Model and encoding, lines 16–168.** The use of homogeneous quality masses at zero delivery is consistent throughout. Finite arc bounds and the source-quality box give the asserted compact projection in standard and acyclic generalized pooling. The affine-coordinate reduction retains every original quality row; rank zero and empty input sets are treated correctly. The distinction between arbitrary physical arc costs and source-cost/product-revenue economics is necessary and stated.
- **Rank-one block, lines 170–196.** The margin formula and converse are correct, including the zero block. The warning that physical feed/outlet costs induce an additive matrix cost, rather than an arbitrary matrix cost, prevents an invalid hardness transfer.
- **Destination decomposition and single-product projection, lines 198–308.** The multiplier uses the head of each arc. Thus all incoming streams at a pool receive a common factor, while the recursion provides the corresponding outgoing balance. This is sufficient to retain the original pool qualities. Coordinatewise domination preserves all stated capacities precisely because lower bounds are zero. The single-product LP reconstruction is valid and rational with polynomial encoding length. The negative-cost averaging inequalities have the correct direction.
- **Shortest paths and conic hull, lines 310–404.** Zero-capacity deletion, positive common scaling, and the distinction between signs and profit magnitudes are handled correctly. The support bound follows from at most K independent quality-coordinate rows plus normalization. The conic equality uses a polyhedral sum/projection, so closedness is established rather than assumed. The two-pure-product example correctly rules out reimposing capacities after convexification.
- **Cycles, lines 406–547.** The positive-flow support, rather than the original graph, is the correct object. The SCC argument separates isolated circulations from source-fed blocks; the latter have an invertible substochastic backward system. Forward absorption gives the destination decomposition on the remaining support. Removing nonnegative-cost cycles preserves source withdrawals and single-product aggregate quality mass. Choosing constant qualities on isolated circulations makes the negative-cycle test and compactness argument valid under the expressly stated algebraic steady-state semantics. The exact near-singular residual calculation also checks out.
- **Facial specifications, lines 549–715.** Both applications of the face property are needed and present. Each compatible support gives a bounded network polytope, and the lower-bound check on omitted arcs is explicit. The nonfacial converse supplies an actual negative-cost fractional mixture and shows that every integral flow has zero cost; it is correctly presented as a universal-integrality counterexample, not a hardness reduction. The LP recognition test, fixed-dimension face enumeration, and endpoint disjunction all have the claimed scope.

### Algebraic complexity

- **Source and saturation, lines 31–73.** Replacing x=1 by x²=1 is valid on [1/2,2]. The marking objective counts each prescribed total exactly once and each standard arc at most twice. Attaining its sum of upper bounds forces every marked total individually.
- **Many-pool reduction, lines 75–260.** I traced both directions through reciprocal emission, conversion, the four storage types, inversion, addition, and slack completion. The emission equations force a positive denominator. The two initial emissions impose both bounds on each original variable independently of equation occurrence. Repeated names are safe: repeated addition summands get distinct emissions, and x²=1 is a legitimate inversion gadget. Every connecting stream remains on an input-to-pool arc. The capacity choice B=2M+3 has the claimed slack margin and polynomial magnitude. All arc flows, including slack and diluent flows, are forced by the source assignment, so the bijection claim is stronger than equisatisfiability and is actually proved.
- **Fixed data and degree refinement, lines 262–355.** The alternating reciprocal chains can allocate each requested storage use to its own position using at most two links per request. An extra initial link handles an unused variable. A storage pool then has at most two non-slack outlets, so B=7 suffices. The stated local degree bounds and finite quality constants follow. Complementary attributes and exact marked-node contracts preserve the claimed variants without silently proving hardness for one upper-bounded attribute or for zero-lower-bound feasibility.
- **One pool, lines 357–481.** Saturated auxiliary sources and products force their pool intake and pool delivery to agree. The bypass is zero in each pinned attribute, including the pin involving a different auxiliary source. The three inversion pins therefore give the desired product equation; the two addition pins force a positive multiplier and equality of the sums. Repeated-summand normalization is a rational bijection. Range pins cover every variable outside inversion equations. B=4N bounds filler and slack flows. Attribute count grows, consistently with the later NP upper bound for fixed attribute count.
- **Algebraic witnesses and example, lines 483–631.** The revised argument begins with a compact basic-closed singleton, adds uniquely determined slacks/gates, and enters the toolbox at Lemma C. It does not rely on the non-preserving compactification in Lemma B. The later rational transformations and retained affine coordinate justify both singleton preservation and the exact generated field Q(alpha). This is an existence claim, with no unproved polynomial encoding bound on arbitrary prescribed alpha. The small irrational optimum retains the throughput denominator and has the printed value. The residual-rounding statement allows every constraint to be violated by the stated tolerance and does not claim exact rational feasibility.
- **Basis certificates and pooling scopes, lines 633–839.** A bounded nonempty fiber has a vertex even when lower-dimensional. Active row indices give a polynomial certificate. Multiplying the Cramer inequality by the determinant once after clearing its denominator is equivalent to multiplication by the positive determinant square, so both signs are treated correctly. Fixed parameter dimension and polynomial degree make dense determinant expansion/interpolation and subsequent algebraic decision polynomial in bit complexity. The output-fraction parameterization handles zero-throughput pools and preserves external contracts when removing pools without outlets. The one-pool/no-bypass support certificate is sound even when some guessed allowed outlets carry zero flow.

- Historical one-pool active-product LP and scalar-quality hardness: [[haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the]] p.7-10, Theorems 2–5. The manuscript accurately separates these combinatorial results from its algebraic completeness claims.

The [published article abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/opre.2015.1357) independently confirms the polynomial product-count approximation result; the local authors' slides describe the one-output selection mechanism.

Targeted web searches for pooling together with ETR or “existential theory of the reals” found no earlier pooling completeness result. This is supporting evidence for the manuscript's qualified novelty statement, not a proof of priority or an exhaustive literature search. The ETR-INV source universality is established prior work; its realization under the pooling restrictions is the distinct contribution here. The facial necessity/recognition results and explicit conic description are useful structural consequences, although individually more elementary than the algebraic reductions.
