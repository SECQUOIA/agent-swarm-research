## Physical models and structural results

- **Model and objective scope — `01-foundations.tex:16–196`.** The homogeneous quality convention correctly makes zero-demand product specifications vacuous. Removing a pool retains external contracts and checks positive incident lower bounds. The finite-capacity convention bounds every physical flow variable, including arcs with no separately supplied capacity. The rank-one correspondence is valid at positive throughput and at zero flow. The distinction between an arbitrary matrix objective and the additive coefficients induced by physical feed/outlet costs is correct. The compact-lift argument applies to standard and acyclic networks as stated, and does not silently assign source-derived quality bounds to isolated cycles.
- **Destination decomposition and sign test — `01-foundations.tex:218–358`.** The multiplier must use the head of each arc, as the manuscript does. This gives a common multiplier on every pool inflow; the absorption recursion gives the same multiplier on total outflow. Consequently the original quality remains feasible in each component. Product inflows to the selected destination are unchanged, and coordinatewise domination preserves every permitted capacity. Summing quality balances establishes the single-product LP projection. The averaging inequalities have the correct direction for negative costs. Shortest-path routing needs only a positive common scale after zero-capacity deletion, and the support argument gives at most (K+1) inputs even when each coordinate has both a lower and an upper bound. None of these arguments permits positive lower flow contracts.
- **Uncapacitated hull — `01-foundations.tex:360–404`.** Closure is proved through the LP projection, rather than assumed in the definition of the conic hull. Both inclusions in the finite Minkowski-sum representation follow from the decomposition and single-product reconstruction. The capacity-reimposition counterexample is correct: a physical pool cannot serve the exact-zero and exact-one products simultaneously, whereas their summed single-product solutions meet the listed capacities.
- **Cyclic and singular cases — `01-foundations.tex:406–547`.** For an SCC with external inflow, the backward matrix is substochastic with a deficient row, and strong connectivity supplies the required escape argument. An SCC with no external inflow has no external outflow by conservation. Every remaining component traces to source inflow; the constructed qualities therefore lie in the input hull. A terminal positive-support SCC with no product outlet is isolated, so removing these SCCs leaves an absorbing forward chain. The resulting destination components retain the original pool qualities. Isolated circulations can use one constant quality and be merged into a designated component without violating capacities or mixing. The negative-cycle disjunct is necessary under the expressly stated algebraic steady-state interpretation. With no negative cycle, ordinary cycle removal preserves source withdrawals and product delivery; reconstruction, rather than preservation of the old pool qualities, justifies the resulting path mixture. The near-singular two-pool example has determinant ε and the stated concentration/residual discrepancy. The bounded closed lift for cyclic arc-flow projections is also valid, including an empty input set.
- **Facial specifications and integrality — `01-foundations.tex:549–715`.** Applying the face property first at products and then at pools gives exactly the forbidden feed–outlet pairs. Fixing a compatible allowed support leaves an ordinary bounded network flow polytope; node splitting and the return arc preserve integer data. Positive lower bounds on deleted arcs must make a branch infeasible, as stated. The converse only asserts failure of a universal integral-optimum guarantee, and its unit-throughput construction proves that claim for every nonempty nonface. Recognition by maximizing forbidden-input weight is both necessary and sufficient, including empty and full faces. The fixed-dimension face enumeration is sufficient because a basis of active facet normals defines the same face. The endpoint disjunction and the resulting rational/integer certificates are exact. Neither fractional optima nor a finite union of integral branches is incorrectly used to infer computational hardness or polynomial-time optimization.

- **Finite data and one pool — `02-algebraic-complexity.tex:264–481`.** Alternating reciprocal chains supply each requested type with at most two links per request. Capacity seven leaves at least three units of slack and limits each storage pool to three outlets. In the one-pool construction, repeated-summand normalization preserves uniqueness; the bypass is zero in each pinned attribute; and the auxiliary equations force the intended inversion, addition, and range conditions. The filler and slack bounds hold with (B=4N). The growing attribute dimension is explicit and consistent with the later fixed-dimension NP upper bound. Complementary binary attributes correctly convert lower specifications, including bypass contributions and zero flow. Exact-contract feasibility and zero-lower-bound threshold hardness are kept distinct.
- **Algebraic degree — `02-algebraic-complexity.tex:485–630`.** The use of a compact basic-closed singleton avoids relying on the source's non-preserving compactification step. The approximate-description statement permits residual tolerance in all constraints and correctly avoids asserting exactly feasible rational approximations or a polynomial construction algorithm.
- **Basis certificates — `02-algebraic-complexity.tex:642–839`.** A bounded nonempty fiber has a vertex even when lower-dimensional, and its active normals span the full flow-variable space. The determinant-squared verifier is sound for both determinant signs and explicitly removes the singular locus.
