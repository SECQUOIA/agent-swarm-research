Required finding:

- **R4-1 — Minor: the synthesis assigns the fixed-mixing-variable mechanism to all restricted NP-hardness reductions.** Locator: `sections/06-synthesis.tex:5–7`, the sentence beginning “The restricted NP-hardness reductions retain only a few mixing variables”. The all-four-degrees-two construction in `sections/03-restricted-hardness.tex:217–316` has one pool per source-graph vertex. Its number of mixing variables grows; its reduction uses pure-mode replacement and independent-set selection. The current sentence therefore does not describe that prominent restricted hardness result, although the abstract and introduction correctly state that its pool count grows. Repair: say “The one- and two-pool NP-hardness reductions retain only a few mixing variables …”, or distinguish this mechanism from the growing-pool pure-mode construction. No theorem or proof needs changing.

Optional suggestions:

- **R4-O1 — Optional precision in the open-question comparison.** Locator: `sections/00-introduction.tex:68–71`. The sentence saying that the separate two-pool question “is answered negatively with two products” can be made more precise as “the two-pool, bounded-product case of that question is answered negatively”. Boland–Kalinowski–Rigterink, author manuscript Section 4, question 4 asks about two pools and “some bounds” on inputs, outputs and qualities. The manuscript supplies a negative result with two products and growing input/attribute counts, and also positive results when other parameters are fixed. The current sentence supplies the relevant qualification immediately, so I do not regard it as a substantive priority or scope error; the proposed wording would remove any suggestion that every version of their broader question receives a negative answer. Primary source: [author manuscript, Section 4](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/5059.pdf).
- **R4-O2 — Optional bibliography cleanup.** `bibliography.bib` retains `s6:deloera2006` and `s6:khademnia2024v2`, although neither is cited by the frozen manuscript after the tangential network–simplex comparisons were removed. They do not appear in the compiled bibliography and cause no correctness or build issue. Removing them would align the source bibliography with the stated disposition in `source-index.md`.

The principal contribution hierarchy is defensible. The introduction identifies the two physical ETR-completeness results as the principal advance, then places the restricted hardness boundaries and structural/contract algorithms alongside them. It correctly distinguishes ETR-completeness from irrationality and distinguishes a basis-index NP certificate from polynomial optimization. The matched degree-two/degree-three statement concerns contracted feasibility and preserves the same two-feed/two-outlet and input-degree restrictions. The constant-data economic threshold refinement is explicitly separate; no approximation or tolerance gap is inferred from strong NP-completeness alone.

The secondary contribution claims are also appropriately limited. The physical path construction supplies a physical realization and exact certificates for an established exponential shadow; it does not claim that exponential LP shadows are new or that the bare path is NP-hard. The rank-one block results distinguish arbitrary source-to-terminal matrix costs from additive physical feed/outlet economics. The uniform hull bounds concern every bounded linear objective simultaneously, with entrywise sum norm and inverse-quadratic absolute error, rather than a selected objective or an arbitrary approximation ratio. The manuscript correctly avoids inferring a zero Lagrangian duality gap, a physical multi-pool algorithm, an exponential approximate SOC bound, or practical solver behavior.

The following mathematical checks support the verdict:

- **Appendix A:** The telescoping identity, endpoint recursion, unique exposure, and coefficient conversion `d_j/s_j = 3s_j` are consistent. The two-quality relay copies preserve the path coordinates. The penalty rewards both source and reset-product completion, has polynomial encoding, and is uniform in the varied price. The explicit-response lower bound counts distinct supporting lines and applies only without auxiliary variables. The nonlinear interface forces `q = 2-t` and `z >= t-t^2`; the stated economics reduce exactly to `d^T x-z`. The strict-local-optimum perturbation is smaller than every nonzero terminal vertex separation. The parity lower bound and finite binary-label upper bound concern the optimal set, whereas the LP hull concerns its convex hull. The hull proof recovers a physical optimum by an optimal vertex, not by an arbitrary LP solution. The nongeometric-supply identity, slab encoding, rank-one matrix-parameter union of two LPs, and square-root-sum limitation preserve their stated scopes. In particular, the slab proof neither claims a physical degree-two realization nor strong hardness.
- **Appendix B:** Subtracting row/column effects gives the stated least interaction rank. Projected-box vertex enumeration and slice segments cover a fixed-total optimum; including nonedge segments adds only feasible candidates. The shared total leads to `alpha S + beta + gamma/S`, and zero total is handled separately. The biclique reduction uses sufficient dummy margins to equalize totals, gives a positive no-instance gap, and its coefficients remain polynomially bounded. The unit-margin repair and exact penalty retain that gap with zero lower bounds. The rational threshold certificate argument minimizes the multiplied quadratic on a rational interval; it does not incorrectly require the exact optimizer to be rational. The one-fixed-dimension algorithm has only exponentially many row patterns in that dimension and polynomially many greedy-column intervals per pattern.
- **Appendix C:** Trace equality forces identical binary margins of a generator. The nonnegative paired cross entries and maximal mass equation successively select a face, and the displayed affine inverse is correct. The nonpolyhedral two-by-two face is consistent with the exact SOC representation. I checked the exposure, rounding, and convex-combination estimates, including the possibly negative term `5[m-T(W)]`; the resulting coefficients are 28 and 156, and the face transfer constant is `184m+6`. The principal-block scaling gives `A_m=m(184m+6)`. The LP sandwich uses coefficient norm one. The SDP argument instead uses shifted sign-correlation slacks, the strict pseudo-density hypothesis, and the correct `k^(13/2)` denominator and `k^(-23/4)` prefactor. The support-gap interpretation uses the stated dual norms. The facial-reduction argument for valid-slack factorization accounts for a nonnegative constant with one extra scalar block.
- **Sections 01–05 dependencies:** The introduction’s arithmetic claims match the rational flow bijections, finite-alphabet chain refinement, and one-pool attribute encoding. The basis certificate handles both determinant signs and bounded lower-dimensional fibers. The fixed-core proof retains objective values in the support dimension and recovers all blocks in the same algebraic field. The endpoint algorithm does not retain a dense eliminated objective. The contract summaries retain the distinctions between exact mass/full-vector contracts, bounded exceptions, redundant versus restrictive common bounds, and two full input-quality vectors.
