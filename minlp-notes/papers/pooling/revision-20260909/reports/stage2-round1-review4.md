## Findings

### O1 — optional: identify the established degree-one LP result precisely

**Location:** lines 193–201, the paragraph beginning “At the other end of the degree boundary.”

**Reason:** The argument is correct, but this exact tractability boundary already appears as Proposition 3 in Haugland's computational-complexity paper. The introduction cites that paper generally for the hardness classification, while this local paragraph is uncited. A precise citation would distinguish the established positive side from the new simultaneous negative restrictions. This is an attribution improvement, not evidence that the new theorem is previously known.

**Fix:** Add a citation to Haugland (2016), Proposition 3, at this paragraph, retaining the short proof if desired. Primary evidence: [[haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the]] p.6.

### O2 — optional: add a compact final contract inventory

**Location:** lines 1296–1366, especially the final exception count at lines 1348–1356.

**Reason:** The five-exception count checks out, but verifying it requires tracing requested ports, their unused mates, grouped sources, and split collectors across several lemmas. The existing gate table gives the supply arithmetic but does not directly display the final external-node inventory. This is the most demanding physical-accounting step for a reader; a small inventory would improve auditability without adding another proof.

**Fix:** Add a short table or sentence grouping final inputs as middle/link, gate/split, conversion, two private conversion fillers, and anchor; group outputs as full/half, collectors, and the two primary outputs. State the exact versus variable contracts and maximum degrees. Keep the explicit statement that original signals receive no extra private reporting ports in this reduction. No change to the theorem is needed.

## Certificate scope

The fixed-parameter NP claims do not assume that an optimum has rational flows. I checked the basis-index certificate in Section 2: the threshold is inserted before choosing a bounded fiber vertex; the selected active matrix remains nonsingular at the witness; multiplying the Cramer inequalities by a second determinant handles either sign. For fixed parameter dimension, determinant coefficient arrays have polynomial size and can be computed by interpolation. Fixed-dimensional real-algebraic feasibility supplies a polynomial verifier.

The applications are properly separated. The tree construction has a fixed number of outlet fractions despite unbounded attributes. The one-pool scalar constructions have one pool-quality parameter despite arbitrary bypass complexity. The all-degree-two endpoint class instead has a polynomially specified disjunction with linear branch fibers. Finite vertex/arc bounds in the constructed networks bound every relevant flow. Exact supplies, exact qualities, and bypass arcs do not invalidate these uses.

## Primary literature, originality, and impact

The claimed comparison to earlier pooling complexity work is supported:

- [[haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the]] p.8–10 proves scalar hardness with separately restricted source/terminal counts; p.13–15 gives separate degree restrictions with growing attribute counts. Its p.16 discusses extensions to multiple pools. Those statements do not supply the simultaneous scalar all-four-degree-two theorem.
- [[boland2017-a-polynomially-solvable-case-of]] p.8 explicitly asks the scalar in-degree-two and out-degree-two questions, as well as the two-pool count question. The section's claim to answer the two degree questions in one common restriction class is justified. The [author manuscript](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/5059.pdf) supports that attribution.
- [[haugland2016-pooling-problems-with-polynomial-time]] p.17 expressly distinguishes its no-bypass convention from permitting one mixing pool and direct source-terminal arcs. Thus the bypass hardness is compared to the correct model, and two actual feeds are not mistaken for two external inputs.
- [[lugojan2018-piecewise-parametric-structure-in-the]] p.5 specifies the relaxed feed/pool capacities and fixed demands; p.24 asserts the broad one-pool hardness boundary in Remark 4.6. The manuscript correctly describes this as a prior assertion and does not claim priority for generic one-pool scalar capacity hardness. The published [Remark 4.6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6417401/) likewise does not establish the finite-data, two-feed, two-outlet, degree-bounded construction claimed here.
- [[asahiro2011-approximation-algorithms-for-the-graph]] p.15–17 supplies the bounded-literal satisfiability orientation pattern. Private triangles and subdivision give the additional degree/bipartite restrictions explicitly proved in this section.
- [[chlebik2006-complexity-of-approximating-bounded-variants]] p.25–26 is the appropriate primary source for an independent-set gap on cubic graphs with a supplied proper three-edge-coloring. I visually inspected original p.26: it explicitly says the constructed graph is edge-three-colored and three-regular and that the same source gap is preserved. Merely citing arbitrary cubic independent set would not have been enough, but that is not what this section does.
- [[matsui1996-np-hardness-of-linear-multiplicative]] p.3–7 supplies the product source, including the precise factor formulas. I visually inspected the original p.3 statement of Theorem 2.1 and verified its printed arbitrary-positive-integer-p claim against the counterexample. The correction is carefully restricted to the open report, not asserted to describe every published version.
- [[hoffman1952-on-approximate-solutions-of-systems]] p.1–3 is an appropriate historical reference for the polyhedral error-bound principle. The explicit coarse constant used here is proved independently.

The strongest scientific contributions are the simultaneous scalar all-degree-two approximation barrier and the explicit finite-data physical realization with a constant nonlinear interface. The five-exception theorem sharpens the role of exact auxiliary contracts. The tree theorem separates the one-pool and two-pool output-count boundaries cleanly. The explicit penalty and FPTAS supply useful supporting distinctions but should not be represented as general approximation barriers or a general algorithm for two-pool pooling; the current wording respects these limits.

Targeted online searches for recent scalar/degree pooling hardness and finite-data/two-feed results did not reveal a primary source establishing these simultaneous restrictions. A recent primary relaxation paper's literature discussion still points to the established Haugland, Boland, and Baltean-Lugojan/Misener results, but that observation is only corroboration, not a proof of priority. I did not conduct an exhaustive citation-network search or inspect every later pooling paper. The manuscript's qualified “to the best of our knowledge” formulation is appropriate.
