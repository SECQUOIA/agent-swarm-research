## Findings and concrete repairs

### M1 — Minor: the abstract leaves two simultaneous restrictions ambiguous

**Locator:** `sections/00-introduction.tex:6–7` and `:12–16`, PDF p. 1. Compare `s2:bounded`, `s2:one-pool-etr`, and `s4:degree-boundary`.

**Reason:** After naming both existential-real reductions, “The reductions preserve small degrees and bounded data” can be read as giving the full bounded-degree/fixed-data refinement for both. The one-pool theorem expressly allows its pool degrees and attribute dimension to grow; the fixed-finite-data refinement belongs to the many-pool construction. Later, “under the stated input degree bound” refers to a bound not actually stated in the abstract. Readers consulting only the abstract should not have to reconstruct which restrictions belong to which result. The body and tables already make the correct distinctions; this is not a counterexample to a theorem.

**Concrete repair:** Replace the first clause by “The many-pool reduction also admits bounded degrees and fixed finite data.” In the degree-boundary sentence, replace “under the stated input degree bound” by “when input out-degree is at most two.” If desired, also replace “every local degree” by “all four layer degrees.” Keep the separate contracted-feasibility qualification.

### O1 — Optional: trim repeated scope discussion while preserving theorem hypotheses

**Locator:** `sections/04-structural-algorithms.tex:1058–1070`; `sections/06-synthesis.tex`, subsection `s6:open`; `appendices/A-response-geometry.tex:489–498`.

**Reason:** Each passage correctly explains that endpoint elimination forgets dense objectives and that an abstract dense slab is not a physical degree-two hardness reduction. The repetition is understandable but contributes to the manuscript's 104-page length. No journal target or page limit was supplied, so I do not treat length alone as a mandatory revision.

**Concrete repair:** Keep the full explanation beside the path algorithm and the full slab proof in Appendix A. Shorten the corresponding synthesis paragraph to the retained-information issue plus exact cross-references. Preserve explicit restrictions in theorem statements and Tables 1–2; those are useful, not redundant.

There are no further mandatory findings from this review. In particular, I found no omitted lower-bound check, missing objective term, unsupported algebraic-field multiplication, or hidden degree-three bypass row in the stated positive degree-two algorithms.

## Primary-source and novelty assessment

The main contributions are substantial if this manuscript is otherwise unpublished: the two physical existential-real reductions, simultaneous local-degree hardness, fixed-data two-feed/two-outlet construction, uniform nonlinear-core aggregation, and contract-based algorithms each answer a concrete classification question. The isolated margin-cost and conic appendices have a different mathematical object, but the manuscript consistently marks that distinction. They resolve a stated cost conjecture and add explicit transfers, rather than treating general matrix cost as ordinary physical operating cost.

I independently inspected primary PDF passages, extracting text into `checks/whole-review3/primary/`, for the following central dependencies and comparisons:

- Abrahamsen–Adamaszek–Miltzow's ETR-INV source and Abrahamsen–Miltzow's compact-instance Lemmas C–G, including the definitions, unique gate extension, affine recovery, and promised intervals. The relevant compact basic-closed use is supported. The online [versioned toolbox record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08674v1) also identifies the precise source version.
- Haugland's final 2016 complexity article, Haugland–Hendrix's polynomial cases, and Boland–Kalinowski–Rigterink's one-pool/fixed-input result and closing degree/two-pool questions. Their source/terminal restrictions and no-bypass convention agree with the manuscript's comparisons. The local Boland author PDF's final section explicitly contains the two degree-two questions used here.
- Chlebík–Chlebíková, author-manuscript pp. 25–26: the constructed cubic instances have a supplied proper three-edge-coloring and retain the independent-set gap. This supports the stronger source needed by the all-four-degrees construction, rather than merely uncolored cubic independent set.
- Matsui's open report and Baltean-Lugojan–Misener's Assumption 2.2/Remark 4.6: the manuscript supplies its own corrected positive-product separation and appropriately credits the earlier broad one-pool capacity-hardness assertion. It does not treat that remark as an adequate substitute for its new simultaneous-restriction reduction.
- Basu–Pollack–Roy's Theorems 1.3.1/1.3.3 and bit-size convention; Adler–Beling's Section 5, Remark 1. Fixed total dimension, polynomial degree/length, and a polynomial-degree common coefficient field are the important restrictions, all supplied in the manuscript. - Dey–Kocuk–Santana's precise simultaneous-margin conjecture, independently visible after Theorem 4 in the [author PDF](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~sdey30/RankonePool.pdf). Jalilian–Kocuk's local archived preprint has Theorem 1, while the [published author-hosted PDF](https://research.sabanciuniv.edu/id/eprint/52174/1/Improved.pdf) has the cited Theorem 2 and explicitly discusses exponential representability. - Fawzi–Parrilo Theorem 1; Lee–Raghavendra–Steurer's 46-page author manuscript, Theorem 1.1, equation (3.11), and Theorem 5.3; Braun–Fiorini–Pokutta–Steurer's author manuscript, Theorem 6(i). I matched the quantitative exponents, pseudo-density norm and slack shift, and fixed dilation-two LP sandwich to the versions used. These sources support different conclusions and are not interchanged.

I also searched the public literature for pooling with existential-real/existential-theory terminology, degree-two restrictions, exact contracts, and rank-one simultaneous-margin costs. This did not identify a conflicting prior result within the reviewed primary-source scope. Such negative searches are not exhaustive evidence of priority. The manuscript's qualified novelty language should remain qualified.

The initial Xiang source-access limitation has been resolved by independent inspection of the original PDF and its supplied Zenodo metadata; the precise verification and remaining scope qualification are recorded in the addendum below. The manuscript's sign/decomposition results have complete proofs that I checked directly, and it gives the earlier work credit rather than claiming its priority. ## Primary-source verification addendum: Xiang et al. (9 September 2026)

After completing the review, I independently inspected the original twelve-page PDF supplied at `/tmp/pooling-xiang2026/paper.pdf` and its raw `metadata.json`. I read the model and proofs in Sections 2–3, including the terminal-proportion projection, restriction and augmentation arguments, Proposition 4, and Theorem 2. This supersedes the initial access limitation; it does not alter the verdict, mandatory finding M1, or optional finding O1.

The PDF's title, seven authors, proceedings identity, DOI, and printed pages 1–12 match the manuscript's bibliography entry `xiang2026-dive-and-fix`. Its 293,149-byte file has MD5 `b19bcb6c38fd643a76e3f8b7e55278b6`, matching the supplied Zenodo record's file checksum. The metadata gives DOI [10.5281/zenodo.19984634](https://zenodo.org/records/19984634), publication date 20 March 2026, and conference dates 20–22 March 2026; its May 2026 deposit date is distinct from the proceedings date. This verifies the supplied file against the supplied record; I did not independently retrieve the original bytes from Zenodo during this follow-up.

The specific mathematical attribution in `sections/01-foundations.tex:211` is supported:

- Pages 2–3 explicitly allow pool–pool arcs and use nonnegative flows, upper node/arc capacities, and terminal quality bounds. The objective is linear arc-cost minimization and zero flow is admitted in the intended formulation.
- Pages 4–6 give the terminal-proportion projection and restriction construction. For a single terminal, equation (4g) identifies the terminal-indexed flow with the physical arc-flow variable, rendering the proportionality equations redundant; the restriction is an LP.
- Proposition 4 on printed page 8 proves that the full optimum is at least the sum of single-terminal optima. Theorem 2 on the same page uses that inequality and Remark 1 to show that the optimum is zero exactly when every single-terminal optimum is zero. The supplied proof therefore supports the manuscript's credit for polynomial zero-optimum detection, including in the stated terminal-proportion formulation with pool–pool arcs.

A formulation-scope qualification remains, rather than an access limit. Xiang's Theorem 2 is stated for `(TP')`, indexed by terminals reachable in the underlying graph. On page 3, equation (1g) requires the terminal proportions at each pool to sum to one; this cannot hold when that pool's reachable-terminal set is empty. In `(TP')`, equation (3) on page 4 instead forces its incoming flows to zero. Consequently this source does not by itself establish the same theorem for the manuscript's separate physical equations allowing isolated positive pool circulations with no product path. The manuscript already says so in `sections/01-foundations.tex:412–415` and supplies its own circulation analysis. Reading the original source confirms that this is a useful and accurate qualification, not a reason to withdraw the earlier attribution or add a manuscript finding.

Persistent evidence is in `checks/whole-review3/primary/xiang2026-dive-and-fix.pdf`, the corresponding `.metadata.json` and extracted `.txt`, and `xiang2026-verification.json` (hashes, checksum comparison, and page locators). No manuscript file or other review report was changed.
