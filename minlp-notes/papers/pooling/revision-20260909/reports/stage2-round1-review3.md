**Primary literature, importance, and qualified novelty.** I consulted the local extracted texts and independently re-extracted the relevant originals with `pdftotext -layout` into the check directory. Important statements and equations were compared against these original-PDF extractions. These source checks support the manuscript's attributions:

- The orientation literal/clause construction is genuinely related to Asahiro et al., Section 5; their special saturation cycle and occurrence restriction are present in the original. The private triangles plus complete subdivision give the additional degree and bipartition properties proved here. See `[[asahiro2011-approximation-algorithms-for-the-graph]] p.15-17` (printed pp.92–94).
- The colored cubic independent-set gap is not being inferred from arbitrary cubic hardness. Chlebík and Chlebíková explicitly construct edge-three-colored three-regular instances while retaining the independent-set objective and gap in Section 5(A). See `[[chlebik2006-complexity-of-approximating-bounded-variants]] p.25-26`. Their initial definition uses simple graphs, and the construction is compatible with that convention. This is a valid primary source for the exact colored restriction used here.
- Haugland's separate scalar and degree restrictions, and his fixed-count questions, are documented in `[[haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the]] p.16` (printed p.214). Boland et al. explicitly list both scalar in-degree-two and scalar out-degree-two questions and the two-pool fixed-count question in `[[boland2017-a-polynomially-solvable-case-of]] p.8`; the [open author manuscript](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/5059.pdf) independently confirms these statements.
- The warning that suppressing direct-flow pools changes the one-pool algorithmic scope is explicit in `[[haugland2016-pooling-problems-with-polynomial-time]] p.17` (printed p.607). This supports the distinction between two actual feed arcs and a bounded total number of external sources.
- The original Matsui report's statement and proof are in `[[matsui1996-np-hardness-of-linear-multiplicative]] p.3-7`. The manuscript's correction is narrowly scoped to the needed parameter choice and preserves the source reduction; it does not claim to originate positive-product hardness.
- Baltean-Lugojan and Misener's unrestricted-availability assumptions and capacity-hardness assertion are present in `[[lugojan2018-piecewise-parametric-structure-in-the]] p.5` and `[[lugojan2018-piecewise-parametric-structure-in-the]] p.24`. The [published primary article, Assumption 2.2 and Remark 4.6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10898-017-0577-y) independently confirms them. “Asserted” is an appropriate description: the remark points to a bivariate-polynomial hardness argument and does not give the simultaneous finite-data/two-feed construction proved here.
- The polyhedral error-bound attribution is consistent with `[[hoffman1952-on-approximate-solutions-of-systems]] p.1-3`. The manuscript provides its own sufficient constant, so no precise best-constant claim depends on an external result.

The all-degree-two theorem answers specifically identified questions with simultaneous restrictions and a constant approximation gap. The two-pool tree isolates the failure of a fixed-output extension, although the growing attribute count must remain visible. The finite-data result is stronger in a different direction: it confines the nonlinear interface while allowing topology to encode high-precision linear relations. The five-exception theorem adds a meaningful exact-feasibility restriction. The introductory novelty disclaimer and concluding separation of these mechanisms are justified by the sources checked. I found no prior result in this focused search establishing the same simultaneous restrictions. This is evidence for the qualified “to the best of our knowledge” claim, not a comprehensive priority certification.
