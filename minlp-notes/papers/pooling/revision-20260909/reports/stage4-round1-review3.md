## Detailed mathematical assessment: Appendix C

**C.1, correlation face (`app:correlation-face`, pp. 96–97; source lines 29–75): sound.** For a nonzero generator, both margins have common total $S$, and the two trace-deficit expressions force both margins to be the same binary vector when the trace is one. This excludes zero from a trace-one convex decomposition. Nonnegativity then makes the cross-pair equation a face equation, and the final mass equation maximizes mass on that intermediate face. Thus the successive-section argument proves a face of the original hull, rather than merely an arbitrary section. The displayed affine inverse is correct on atoms and hence on their convex hull. In particular, the nonsymmetric ambient matrix space causes no problem: the selected face is symmetric.

**C.2, exact cone transfer (`app:exact-conic`, p. 97): sound.** Affine section and image do not increase the lifting cone. Padding smaller PSD blocks preserves the fixed-order bound. A Lorentz cone of total dimension $h\ge3$ uses $h-2$ three-dimensional Lorentz factors; the small-dimensional cases are separately counted. Therefore total SOC dimension, rather than only the number of SOC factors, is appropriately lower-bounded. Combining PSD blocks into one block of their total order is also legitimate. The nonpolyhedrality proof for the first-row/first-column-margin face of $K_2$ is correct: the strictly convex curve $q+1/q-2$ has infinitely many extreme graph points. Consequently the statement that $K_N$ has no finite LP lift for $N\ge2$ is stronger than a finite exponential LP count, and the manuscript explicitly acknowledges this.

**C.3, exposure and rounding (`app:face-rounding`, pp. 98–99): the constants check.** I independently followed every inequality, including small and zero totals. The margin estimate is $\|r-c\|_1\le2SD$; the nearest-binary rounding gives $u\le6SD$, $v\le8SD$. Counting double and empty pairs gives the claimed bound on $\|a-b\|_1$. Rescaling the paired vector to total $S$ avoids any unjustified lower bound on $S$. The resulting generator bound is $8mB+116mD+5|S-m|$. Averaging the positive-part identity, using the exposure inequality, adds $20mB+40mD+5(m-T)$, giving exactly $28mB+156mD+5(m-T)$. The possibility that the last summand is negative is correctly stated and handled; it is not silently replaced by a nonnegative deficit. Zero generators are included separately.

**C.4–C.5, outer transfer and approximate LP size (`app:outer-transfer`, `app:approx-lp`, p. 99): sound.** Each of $B,D,T$ has coefficient infinity norm one in the entrywise pairing, so distance $\eta$ to the hull gives the three error bounds used in the proof. The triangle inequality yields $184m+6$, followed by the additional factor $m$ in $A_m=m(184m+6)$. The inequalities with coefficient $2\operatorname{diag}(a)-aa^T$ have coefficient norm at most one and slack $(1-a^Tx)^2$. Thus an entrywise unit neighborhood lies in the factor-two outer polyhedron used by the cited sandwich theorem. No unsupported continuity of exact-face membership is being assumed.

**C.6, valid-slack factorization (`app:slack-factor`, p. 100): sound, including nonproper initial lifts.** Restricting to the smallest PSD face containing the feasible lifting matrices supplies a strictly feasible point on that face by a finite range-spanning convex combination. A valid affine functional has finite nonnegative infimum, so strict primal feasibility gives dual attainment. Its nonnegative constant remainder is absorbed by a scalar PSD block. This explains the necessary $q+1$, and avoids assuming that every valid inequality supports a face or that the original lift satisfies Slater's condition. The argument does not require the projected convex set to be closed.

**C.7–C.8, approximate SDP size (`app:sign-correlation`, `app:approx-sdp`, pp. 99–101): sound.** The use of signed correlation matrices is material. On sign atoms, the specified affine slack is exactly the odd-cardinality centered-square function. The coefficient norm is $1/(4k^2)$, so the uniform perturbation adds at most $1/(8k^2)$, while the pseudo-density remains strictly negative for the chosen $\delta=1/(16k^2)$. The shifted function stays in $[0,1]$, and its mean has the stated lower bound. Substitution into the quantitative input gives the polynomial prefactor $k^{-23/4}$ and denominator $k^{13/2}\log m$. Choosing odd $k\asymp a(m/\log m)^{2/13}$ with small $a$ produces the claimed superpolynomial order. The four-block map $J$ has entrywise operator norm at most $m$, not $4m$, because each input entry occurs in only one output entry. This gives the displayed $1/(2A_m)$ accuracy threshold.

The final support-gap interpretation is correct under the stated outer-containment condition. The closing scope exclusions are necessary and accurate: these are uniform guarantees over all bounded coefficient matrices, not objective-specific impossibility results. In particular, no exponential approximate-SOC conclusion is inferred from the weaker SDP order bound.

## Appendices A and B, and the synthesis

**Appendix A:** I found no defect in the telescoping identity, the path and two-quality relay realizations, or the contract-removal penalty. The Hoffman constant used by the price construction is proved in Section 3, and the coefficient normalization really does give $K_0=1$ there. The price bonus has polynomial binary length, although exponential magnitude; the text distinguishes these properties. Strict exposure gives an open price regime even for each endpoint vertex by one-sided persistence inside the chosen interval.

The physical interface forces the displayed seven new arc flows and active quality $q=2-t$; its only new effective quality restriction is $z\ge t-t^2$. The economic accounting yields the telescoping objective exactly. The strict-local-optimum perturbation uses a valid lower bound on distinct terminal-coordinate separation and strictly negative directional derivatives on the vertex tangent cone. The midpoint/parity integer-dimension proof concerns the finite optimal set. The LP hull proof concerns the convex hull of the full feasible set and returns an optimal hull vertex; these are different represented sets, and the manuscript keeps them distinct. The dense-slab reduction is ordinary NP-hardness with binary data, not strong hardness or physical degree-two hardness. The final two-LP parameter-rank argument handles the zero factor and both factor signs explicitly.

**Appendix B:** The centered matrix really has the minimum rank obtainable by removing additive row and column terms. The projected-box construction keeps polynomially many vertices for fixed dimension, and enumerating all vertex pairs covers slice edges without introducing infeasible candidates. Equal-total edges, singleton candidates, and the zero-total limit are accounted for. Each candidate objective is $\alpha S+\beta+\gamma/S$, and the stated stationary-point and comparison rules give polynomial encoding and one quadratic field. The rank-one specialization distinguishes arithmetic work from dense-input/output work.

The biclique reduction has sufficient independent dummy capacity to balance the two totals. Its nonedge penalty dominates every possible selected edge reward. Alternating minimization over the two boxes proves that the numerator's minimum is attained at a binary pair; the resulting unit numerator gap therefore applies to fractional pairs as well. The unit-margin repair is valid for both $S\ge1$ and $S<1$, and the exact penalty has the claimed sign and magnitude. The rational-certificate proof minimizes the threshold polynomial, rather than incorrectly demanding that the irrational optimizer itself be rational. The one-small-dimension algorithm enumerates slice patterns and greedy column orders with the claimed parameter dependence. The Lagrangian oracle is explicitly restricted to the isolated margin block and does not promise strong duality.

**Introduction and synthesis:** The summaries do not appear to enlarge these results into full physical-pooling claims. The distinctions between ordinary arc economics and arbitrary matrix costs, feasibility and positive profit thresholds, exact contracts and zero lower bounds, bounded attachments and unbounded attachments, and fixed-parameter polynomiality and FPT are consistently retained. In particular, the matched product-degree-two/three feasibility boundary traces to the contracted constant-data construction before economic completion; it does not confuse zero-flow feasibility with threshold hardness. The contract table retains the restrictive-common-bound and objective exceptions needed by the synthesis. The unbounded-attachment degree-two question is stated with the aggregate information that remains unresolved.

## Specific findings and repairs

**M1 — Minor factual correction: external results are not all described as published.** Locator: `README.md:7`, first sentence; compare `bibliography.bib` entries `abrahamsen2019-dynamic-toolbox-for-etrinv` and `s6:fawzi2013`. The README says all essential proofs are included or use “explicitly stated published results.” The manuscript instead expressly identifies some essential external results as preprints. In particular the algebraic-witness transformation uses Dynamic Toolbox, and the fixed-order conic bound cites the Fawzi–Parrilo preprint. This does not undermine the proofs, but the support description overstates publication status. **Repair:** change “published results” to “results from the cited primary literature,” or explicitly say “published papers and identified preprints.” Keep the existing version notes.

**O1 — Optional packaging clarification.** Locators: `README.md:3`, `README.md:21`, and its verification commands; `source-index.md` relative links. In the frozen directory supplied for this review, the manuscript builds from the included source files, but the promised verification directories and process records are not inside that directory, and repository-relative paths assume the original `papers/pooling/` location. This may be deliberate for an internal freeze. **Repair if this directory is distributed:** label those commands and links as repository-only evidence, or supply a submission README containing only runnable package instructions and point separately to the repository evidence. This is not a demand to include internal review reports with a journal submission.

**O2 — Optional explicit dimension convention.** Locator: Appendix B, source lines 5–19 (`s6:margin-block` and the interaction-rank definition). Declare $m,n\ge1$, as Appendix C does for its dimensions. The use of row and column 1 already presumes this. Alternatively, dispose of a zero-dimensional block before that definition. This is a notation/input-convention refinement, not a counterexample to the intended positive-dimensional theorem.

**O3 — Optional durable literature pinning.** Locators: bibliography entries `s6:lrs-full` and `s6:bfps2015`. Their URLs and full-manuscript descriptions currently identify the correct sources, as verified above. Recording a dated version or a hash in the reproducibility materials would protect the equation locators if an author-hosted PDF is replaced. This is particularly useful for the quantitative equation (3.11); a new mathematical citation is unnecessary.

The manuscript is long, but its central contribution and scope tables are usable. Further shortening should target repeated scope reminders and repository process prose before removing proofs or combining hypotheses. I do not recommend deleting the zero-total, singular-parameter or exact-arithmetic cases merely to reduce length.
