## Mathematical examination

I read every proof in both assigned sections and checked the following arguments directly, treating historical audits only as leads.

### Section 1

- **Physical model and certificates:** zero product or pool throughput forces all incident nonnegative flows to zero; qualities at inactive pools do not constrain the arc-flow projection. Compactness for acyclic networks follows from input-derived quality bounds and finite effective flow bounds. The cyclic lift requires the additional zero quality choice for isolated circulation components and is established separately. Affine quality rank elimination uses full-column independence and retains every original product row.
- **Rank-one correspondence:** nonnegative rank-one matrices are determined by their margins when total flow is positive; the zero block is handled separately. Physical feed/outlet costs induce additive matrix costs, so arbitrary matrix objectives must remain a separate model.
- **Destination decomposition:** multiplying an arc by the absorption fraction at its head scales every incoming quality mass at a pool by the same factor. The recursion ensures its outgoing component total agrees. Capacities survive coordinatewise domination. Lower bounds would invalidate this step and are explicitly excluded.
- **Single-product LP and sign:** topological quality reconstruction makes every conserved single-product LP flow physical; aggregate source mass is exactly terminal mass. Component averaging gives the signs of the minimization inequalities as written, including a negative optimum. Rational reconstruction is a triangular linear system with determinant bit bounds, not an assumption that repeated floating-point averaging is exact.
- **Shortest paths and conic hull:** deleting zero-capacity support permits a positive common scale for every uncapacitated finite flow. Path mixtures give the quality simplex; a vertex uses at most K+1 inputs. The hull is the sum of finitely many polyhedral cones. Reimposing capacities after convexification is correctly rejected by the two-pure-product counterexample.
- **Cycles:** positive-support strongly connected components with no external inflow have no external outflow. Other component equations have a substochastic internal matrix with eventual escape and hence an invertible I−Q. The forward absorption construction is valid after isolated circulations are separated. With no negative cycle, ordinary cycle removal preserves withdrawals and aggregate product quality. The compact lift including zero handles attainment. In particular, the original graph's reachability does not guarantee an invertible active mixing system.
- **Facial integrality:** the face property is applied first to product contributors and then to pool feeds. Compatible support branches are bounded network polytopes; integer bounds give integral vertices. For a nonface, a convex combination using an input outside the permitted region gives a negative-cost fractional unit mixture, whereas every integral feasible unit flow uses one permitted input. This proves necessity for the universal guarantee only.
- **Recognition and enumeration:** the forbidden-input-weight LP detects nonfaciality in both directions. In fixed affine dimension, each facet has d affinely independent input points, and at most d independent active facet hyperplanes define a face. Enumeration and containment therefore have polynomial size for fixed dimensions. Endpoint binary choices are exact support exclusions, including positive lower-bound branch checks, and produce rational LP certificates without any assumed integral-flow theorem for arbitrary quality specifications.

### Section 2

- **Saturation:** every standard arc belongs to at most two marked-node contribution groups, so costs are in {0,−1,−2}. Summed profit reaches its upper bound exactly when every marked total is attained.
- **Many-pool arithmetic:** storage throughput B gives quality a/B. Exact quality mass 1/B forces reciprocal outlet 1/a; source saturation copies it onto a different outgoing source arc. Conversion uses two relay pools and a saturated vacuous product, preserving the three-layer physical graph. Initial emissions impose both v and 5/2−v at least 1/2. Inversion pins give xy=1. Addition pins give (5/2−x−y)/(B(5/2−z))=1/B and hence x+y=z. Repeated variable names do not merge freshly generated gadget nodes. The sum of at most M outlets, each at most 2, leaves at least 3 units of storage slack with B=2M+3.
- **Finite data and degree:** a logical chain alternates an intake and its reciprocal; each location serves at most one equation request and one chain link. B=7 then leaves at least 3 slack units. At most two links per request and the initial root links yield O(n+m) size. All sources have outdegree at most 2, every storage pool indegree 2 and outdegree at most 3, and all other local degrees meet the stated bounds. Logical dependence introduces no pool–pool arcs.
- **Complementary attributes and feasibility:** complementary quality mass is t−m, including zero flow and bypasses. This trades a lower bound for a second upper-bounded coordinate. It does not prove hardness for one upper-bounded attribute. Exact marked-node contracts implement saturation for feasibility without relying on a nonzero objective.
- **One pool:** saturation makes auxiliary pool intake equal its corresponding pool outlet. Pinned bypass qualities are zero in every pin used. Three inversion pins give Xx·tau1=1, tau1·tau2=1, Xy·tau2=1; addition pins compare Xz and Xx+Xy using the same positive tau. Distinct-summand normalization adds uniquely determined reciprocal variables. Range pins cover unused and addition-only variables. B=4N supplies enough filler and slack. Every arc value is fixed by the source solution.
- **Algebraic witnesses:** the two pooling reductions preserve rational equivalence of arc-flow solution sets, with denominators bounded away from zero on the source variable box. A compact algebraic singleton transferred through the cited gates remains a singleton, retains an affine replacement of alpha, and generates exactly Q(alpha). The bounded-data construction preserves the designated variable feed. The forcing threshold is the global profit upper bound, hence the unique threshold flow is optimal.
- **Irrational optimum example:** the replacement z=max(0,t−1), c=1−z decreases cost and preserves the quality inequality. For t>1 the remaining quality bound is a≤t/(t−1)−2t, with maximum feasible t=3/2. The two objective branches meet at t=(1+sqrt(3))/2; their derivatives have opposite signs. The reported cost and all original capacities agree.
- **Residual approximation:** the argument is an existence bound for approximate residuals in a specified polynomial formulation. It allows violations of every constraint and does not imply exact rational feasible flows or a polynomial algorithm.
- **Basis-index certificates:** bounded nonempty fibers have vertices even when lower-dimensional; active normals span the full x-space. Cramer substitution multiplied by Delta squared correctly treats both determinant signs and excludes the singular locus. With fixed parameter count, determinant degrees, dense monomial counts, coefficient lengths, evaluation grids, and interpolation complexity are polynomial. The existential sign-decision result permits strict inequalities. All three pooling parametrizations preserve zero-throughput cases and finite linear-fiber bounds. One-pool no-bypass membership uses Haugland's active-product LP argument with contracts retained.

No mathematical gap requiring a new theorem or withdrawal of a stated result was identified in this author examination. The changes are principally attribution, precise scope, and clarification of the nontrivial external algebraic transfer.
