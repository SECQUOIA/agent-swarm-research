A star marks a node whose upper capacity b is attained at the profit threshold. Here λ is an input quality, q a pool quality, and µ a product quality specification; [0, 1] is vacuous. Arrow labels give the flows at saturation. Dashed arrows are physical input–pool arcs whose other endpoint is supplied by the calling gadget; they carry the logical continuation. No arrow joins two pools. The storage pool Q has quality-one intake a; its feed and diluent arcs, slack outlet, and other uses are omitted. The receiving pools’ other arcs and markings are determined by the caller. Thus a product’s exact quality turns an intake a into a copy of 1/a on another input’s outgoing arc. We also need to change that arc’s source quality without changing its amount. To convert a delivered quality-zero amount d ∈ [0, 2] to quality one, route it into a new pool H 0 of capacity 2, whose only outlet enters a marked vacuous product t 0 of capacity 2. A second pool H 1 of capacity 2 has only an outlet to t 0 and is fed by a new marked quality-one input r 1 of capacity 2; r 1 has one other outgoing arc. Conservation and saturation force f H 0 t 0 = d, f r 1 H 1 = f H 1 t 0 = 2 − d, f r 1 ,other = d. (15) These nonnegative flows respect all new capacities, and the product is vacuous. This proves both directions of the conversion. In particular, it is not a pool–pool arc: all physical arcs still have the standard input–pool–product form.
