\begin{abstract}
Pooling couples linear network flows through the requirement that every
outlet of a pool has the same mixture. We study which restrictions on
network structure, quality data and contracts make exact feasibility
and optimization tractable. Rational pooling threshold problems are
complete for the existential theory of the reals even with one attribute
and no bypasses, or with one pool and growing attribute dimension.
Fixed nonlinear parameter counts instead yield polynomial-size
basis-index certificates, but do not alone imply polynomial algorithms.
We give sparse and constant-data hardness constructions, exact algorithms
based on independent polyhedral blocks and planar path projections, and
contract algorithms using conserved quality masses and signed flow cuts.
The resulting boundaries distinguish product degree two from three under
specified pool interfaces and contract assumptions. Physical paths can
have exponentially many one-price responses using only two input
qualities. A one-pool extension has exponentially many isolated global
and strict local optima, yet admits a compact exact LP hull and polynomial
linear optimization. Finally, fixed interaction rank of an isolated
pool-block cost yields an exact quadratic-field algorithm and a
fixed-attribute Lagrangian oracle. All complexity and representation
claims distinguish feasibility, objective scope and exact arithmetic.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}
A pool combines incoming streams and sends the same mixture to each of
its outlets. Flow conservation and product specifications are linear
once pool compositions are fixed, but choosing those compositions
creates bilinear constraints. Direct input-to-product arcs, called
bypasses, add another interaction: source capacities couple the material
sent through pools with material blended directly at products.
The central question of this paper is which structural restrictions
control these interactions sufficiently for exact algorithms, and which
still permit algebraic or combinatorial hardness.

This question requires more than counting pools or bilinear equations.
The number of attributes and the affine rank of the complete input-quality
vectors can differ greatly. A bound on local degree permits arbitrarily
long bypass paths. Exact supplies and product contracts can turn global
pool balances into conserved quantities; general intervals need not.
A linear objective on a few retained arcs can behave differently from
one supported along an entire path. Finally, rational data do not imply
rational feasible points or optimizers. We work in the rational bit
model and specify exact algebraic output representations where needed.

Our principal contributions are organized around three boundaries.
First, we establish algebraic universality and then identify certificate
regimes inside it. Threshold feasibility is $\ETR$-complete with one
attribute and no bypasses, and also with one pool and bypasses when
attribute dimension grows. The reductions retain bounded-data and
degree refinements and give rational correspondences of solution sets,
which expose the possible algebraic degree of witnesses.
When pool count times attribute rank, or pool count times product count,
is fixed, a guessed LP basis supplies a short certificate verified by
fixed-dimensional algebraic decision. This proves $\NP$ membership
without claiming that an exact optimizer is rational.

Second, we locate combinatorial hardness and structural algorithms under
comparable physical restrictions. Strong $\NP$-completeness persists when
all four local degrees equal two and the number of pools grows. With
one pool, one upper-bound quality, two actual feeds and two actual
outlets, degree-three products already permit constant-data strong
hardness. For the same fixed pool interface and input degree bound,
degree-two products instead admit polynomial feasibility with arbitrary
attributes and positive lower bounds. More broadly, a fixed nonlinear
core coupled to independent bounded-dimensional polyhedral blocks gives
exact global optimization; this yields algorithms for fixed input and
pool counts and for fixed pool count, quality rank and bypass vertex
integrity. A separate planar-composition argument handles long degree-two
paths with a bounded retained boundary.

Third, exact contracts lead to algorithms beyond bounded attachments.
For one pool, conservation compresses unboundedly many feeds when only
a fixed number of products can receive pool flow. On fully contracted
degree-two bypass graphs, quality scaling produces a signed incidence
system with polynomially many connected-cut tests. Fixed exceptions
remain tractable even with unrestricted input-quality rank when common
pool bounds are redundant. A further support calculation handles a
restrictive common throughput interval in the fully contracted
rank-one-quality case. With only two distinct full input-quality vectors,
a different convex reduction gives rational feasibility witnesses on
arbitrary bypass graphs, under the specified zero outlet and common
lower bounds.

The final constructions explain why these hypotheses matter.
Exponential Klee--Minty shadows can be realized by ordinary physical
blending paths, including a two-quality relay construction with no
positive lower flow bounds. A one-pool nonlinear interface yields
$2^n$ isolated global optima and, after perturbation, $2^n$ strict local
optima with distinct values. Nevertheless, that family's full feasible
set has an explicit polynomial-size LP convex hull whose vertices are
physically feasible. These are response and geometry results, not
hardness arguments. A complementary cost theorem solves an isolated
rank-one margin block for fixed interaction rank and gives an exact
pool Lagrangian oracle with a fixed number of attributes.

The paper builds on established pooling complexity, formulation and
parametric analysis. Haugland
\cite{haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the} studies complexity
under restrictions on network cardinalities; Haugland and Hendrix
\cite{s3:haugland-hendrix2016} and Boland, Kalinowski and Rigterink
\cite{s4:boland2017} establish polynomial cases.
Baltean-Lugojan and Misener \cite{s3:baltean2018} develop piecewise
parametric structure under their supply, pool-capacity and demand
assumptions. These models must be compared with the actual bypass and
capacity assumptions: replacing bypasses by degree-one pools, for
example, does not preserve fixed pool count. Approximation and
convexification are developed by Dey and Gupte
\cite{dey2015-analysis-of-milp-techniques-for}, Gupte et al.
\cite{gupte2017-relaxations-and-discretizations-for-the}, and Dey, Kocuk
and Santana \cite{dey2020-convexifications-of-rank-one-based}.
We credit these foundations and distinguish physical pooling conclusions
from general rank-one matrix results. Comparisons with individual
theorems and source versions accompany the relevant proofs.

Section~\ref{s1:foundations} fixes the models and arithmetic conventions
and develops profitable-flow, conic-hull and facial-integrality tools.
Section~\ref{s2:algebraic} proves algebraic completeness and the
fixed-parameter certificate theorem. Section~\ref{s3:hardness} gives
restricted hardness, tolerance and approximation results, including an
FPTAS for the explicitly represented positive-product subfamily.
Section~\ref{s4:structural} develops structural exact algorithms, and
Section~\ref{s5:contracts} treats exact contracts and their exceptions.
Section~\ref{s6:synthesis} proves the physical response and geometry
constructions, the cost-rank theorem, and the remaining limitations.
The general degree-two bypass problem with fixed pools and fixed
quality rank but unbounded attachments and interval contracts remains
unclassified here.
