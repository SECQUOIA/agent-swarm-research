### Exact flows versus rational residual descriptions

Lines 585–605 correctly make an existence claim for simultaneous absolute residual tolerances. The finite rational flow bounds and the input-quality box give coordinate magnitudes bounded by `2^poly(N)`. Every degree-two polynomial has polynomially many terms of polynomial coefficient length, so the same type of bound applies to its gradient on a unit enlargement. Multiplying by the polynomial coordinate count gives a common bound for changes measured in the maximum coordinate norm. A dyadic mesh with exponent `O(poly(N)+log(1/epsilon))` can therefore round every lifted coordinate with the asserted total encoding length and error. This argument allows small violations of nonnegativity, capacities, conservation, mixing, and product specifications, consistently with the explicit all-constraints qualification.

The passage does not claim that rounding preserves exact feasibility, that a rational feasible flow exists, or that the rounded point is computable in polynomial time. It also correctly makes the metric depend on the chosen constraint scaling. I found no hidden exact-feasibility inference here.
