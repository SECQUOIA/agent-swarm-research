## Main construction and finite-data refinement

- **Saturation, lines 36–55 (`s2:saturation`).** The objective is exactly the sum of the marked node totals. Every summand has its own capacity upper bound, so meeting the sum forces every marked total separately. A feed arc can receive only an input contribution; a pool outlet can receive pool and product contributions; a bypass can receive input and product contributions. Thus the cost set is indeed `{0,-1,-2}` even when bypasses are present.
- **Base reduction, lines 59–241 (`s2:etr-complete`, `s2:emission`, `s2:bijection`).** The reciprocal product has quality mass `1/B`, so its storage outlet is `1/a`; the saturated relay source then delivers the same amount on its other arc. The conversion conserves the amount and changes only the source supplying it. The two initial emissions enforce both `v >= 1/2` and `5/2-v >= 1/2`, including unused variables. Inversion and addition gadgets recover respectively `xy=1` and `x+y=z`, and their converse flows satisfy the capacities. Repeated names do not identify newly created relay nodes or create parallel arcs. The maximum storage request count is at most `2m+1`; each requested storage outlet is at most two on a source solution. Thus `B=2M+3 <= 4m+5` and residual storage throughput is at least three. All physical arcs remain in the standard three-layer network. Every arc flow is determined by the source assignment, whereas unused zero-flow quality coordinates need not be determined; the bijection correctly concerns arc flows.
- **Allocation, lines 255–281 (`s2:bounded`).** A chain alternates its two required storage types under reciprocal emission followed by conversion. An inversion requests two positions and an addition requests three, counting repeated summands separately. The sequential rule uses zero links for a fresh position of the correct type, one link for the wrong type, and two for an already used position of the correct type. Therefore at most `2r` links serve `r` requests on a chain. Advancing each of the `2n` roots at least once adds at most `2n` links overall, giving `6m+2n`. The root advancement can occur after allocation when necessary, since an allocated root still has room for its one link outlet.
- **Bounds and degrees, lines 283–302.** Every chain intake is either a source variable, its complement, or their reciprocal, and therefore lies in `[1/2,2]`. Each storage pool has at most two non-slack storage outlets, so capacity seven gives slack at least three and diluent at least five. Its two inputs and at most three outputs meet the promise. Emission and conversion sources have two outputs; their relay pools have one input and one output. Addition pools have two inputs and one output, and the inversion collector has one input and one output. All products have at most two inputs. Distinct requests receive distinct positions even for repeated variables, so no hidden degree increase occurs. The capacities are `{2,5/2,4,7}` and quality endpoints are `{0,1,1/14,2/35}`. There are linearly many constant-size gadgets and marked nodes. Consequently the threshold is a half-integer of magnitude `O(n+m)`, with polynomial encoding length. Appending a link determines every new flow uniquely, preserving the rational correspondence in both directions.
- **Variants, lines 305–336 (`s2:variants`).** Complementary source qualities give complementary mass `t_j-m_j`, also at zero product flow. The lower specification therefore becomes exactly the stated upper specification on the second attribute. Zero-flow pools can be assigned consistent complementary qualities without restricting their flows. This changes neither graph degrees nor flow sets; it adds only complements of the finite quality constants. Replacing saturation objectives by lower node bounds equal to upper capacities at marked nodes gives the same feasible flow set and retains finite numerical data. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this contracted feasibility problem from trivial lower-free feasibility and does not infer one-upper-attribute ETR hardness.
