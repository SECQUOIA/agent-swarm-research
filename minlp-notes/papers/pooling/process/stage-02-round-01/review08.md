## Assigned emphasis: certificates and their scope

1. **Basis-index certificate, `s2:fiber-np`, lines 616–702.** The bounded nonempty fiber is a compact polyhedron, including when it has lower affine dimension. The active normals at an extreme point span the ambient flow space, so the guessed square nonsingular system exists. The verification inequality is correct for both signs of the determinant: multiplying the original inequality at `x=U/Delta` by `Delta^2` gives precisely `[A_j U-b_j Delta] Delta <= 0`. The strict determinant condition excludes spurious solutions at singular bases. Adding the threshold before selecting a vertex is necessary and is done.

   The degree, coefficient-length, and construction-time arguments are adequate. For fixed parameter dimension, the dense determinant arrays have polynomial size; the logarithm of the factorial number of determinant terms is polynomial. Exact evaluation and interpolation on the stated tensor grid computes the arrays in polynomial bit time. Constant entries, zero parameter dimension, and zero flow dimension do not invalidate the argument. Basu–Pollack–Roy's Theorem 1.3.3 and Section 1.3's definition of a well-behaved algorithm support the cited polynomial bit bound and the use of arbitrary sign conditions. This is a combinatorial NP certificate, not a claim that all feasible flows have small rational descriptions.

2. **Fixed pool-quality dimension, `s2:pooling-np` (i), lines 720–727.** At fixed pool qualities, both mixing and product mass constraints are linear in flows, as are every permitted bound and the arbitrary linear arc-cost threshold. Inactive pools contribute zero mass. Choosing their qualities in the input-quality box loses no feasible flow. Finite flow bounds provide the required bounded fibers. Empty-input instances are covered by the direct zero-flow treatment in the foundations and in this proof.

3. **Affine rank compression, `s2:pooling-np` (ii), lines 729–741.** The argument uses the affine hull of the full input quality vectors, not coordinatewise rank or the number of distinct vectors. After writing `lambda_i=lambda_0+B a_i`, mass conservation cancels the affine offset; independence of the columns of `B` makes the reduced blending balances equivalent to the original ones. Every original product attribute inequality remains present after substitution. No restriction on the number of original inequalities or on the bypass graph is needed. Active coordinates are convex combinations of the `a_i`, so their coordinatewise box is a valid rational parameter domain; inactive coordinates are free to be chosen there. Rational Gaussian elimination gives polynomial encoding length. Rank zero and the fixed-pool/fixed-input consequence are correctly handled.

4. **Outlet fractions, `s2:pooling-np` (iii), lines 743–767.** The simplex uses only allowed outlets. At fixed fractions the reconstructed outlet volume and each delivered attribute mass are linear in intake flows; in particular, the latter is `theta_lj sum_i lambda_ik y_il`, with no omitted throughput denominator. This preserves product totals, product specifications, outlet lower and upper bounds, arbitrary intake and bypass bounds, source contracts, pool bounds, and the objective. Conversely, positive throughput reconstructs the physical intake average. At zero throughput nonnegativity forces every intake and outlet to zero, so any allowed simplex point works without division. A pool with no outlet is separately checked against its incident and pool lower bounds before deletion; preserving external-node constraints is explicitly required. Thus deletion cannot silently remove a positive source or product contract. There are at most `|L||J|` parameters, even with arbitrary qualities. The final restriction to the standard three-layer model is correct.

5. **One pool without bypasses, `s2:no-bypass-np`, lines 772–799.** Guessing allowed positive products gives a polynomial-size subset certificate and a rational LP with polynomially many rows, even with arbitrary attribute count. For positive total throughput, multiplying each selected product's quality inequalities by the common pool throughput is equivalent to constraining the intake average. Completeness chooses the actual positive outlet set. Soundness only needs every positive outlet to be selected; selected outlets may themselves be zero. This distinction is stated and avoids unnecessary strict LP inequalities. Unselected outlets are fixed to zero while their original bounds and contracts remain, so required positive deliveries are not lost. At zero throughput all flows are zero, and the empty subset covers the physical solution whenever original constraints permit it. Arbitrary signs of rational qualities and arbitrary linear arc costs cause no problem. Haugland's Theorem 2 proof uses the same active-product linearization; the manuscript appropriately makes the lower-quality-bound and contract extensions explicit.

6. **Complexity scope, lines 328–336, 457–462, 578–583, and 801–813.** The distinctions are accurate: lower-free feasibility is trivial; a nonzero profit threshold is a different decision problem; complementing an attribute gives two upper-bounded attributes, not one; and the unresolved one-upper-attribute ETR boundary is not claimed solved.
