## Detailed check of the assigned focus

The fixed-core theorem (`sections/04-structural-algorithms.tex`, `s4:fixed-core`) is supported by the stated hypotheses. In particular:

- Bounded local polyhedra have vertices even when their affine dimension is smaller than their ambient dimension. The active-normal argument therefore justifies ambient-size bases; it does not silently assume full-dimensional blocks.
- The determinant, feasibility, and pairwise-score signs determine candidate validity and a maximizing choice in every block. The argument enumerates realizable joint signs in the fixed variables `(x,u)`, rather than the potentially exponential product of local basis sets. Selecting the first maximizer resolves ties without a connectedness assumption on sign realizations.
- The support formula includes empty-block rejection. For nonempty blocks, all-direction support inequalities characterize the compact convex Minkowski sum of the aggregate/objective images. This proves the required existential block statement in both directions.
- The clearing factor is strictly positive on its actual sign condition. The selected numerator term has the required extra determinant factor: dividing `n_j delta_j product_(i != j) delta_i^2` by `product_i delta_i^2` gives `n_j/delta_j`, including negative determinants.
- The degrees of the cleared support polynomials grow with the number of blocks. The proof acknowledges this and bounds the dense encoding using fixed total variable dimension. Polynomial degree and coefficient height, together with fixed dimension and formula length, suffice for polynomial bit quantifier elimination; no fixed-degree assertion is being used at that later step.
- The original feasible set is compact under the supplied hypotheses, so attainable objective values have an attained minimum. The joint optimality formula still uses a fixed total number of variables after renaming its bound variables.
- A joint algebraic sample puts the core and value in a single polynomial-degree field. The recovery LP uses that same field, and a vertex can be recovered without adjoining independent algebraic roots for the blocks. This is materially stronger than separately bounding degrees of unrelated coefficients, and the manuscript states the distinction.
- The aggregate-slack extension computes a core box even when the original compact-core formula supplies no explicit box. Coordinate projection, endpoint roots, Cauchy bounds, and fixed-degree interval arithmetic give polynomial-bit rational slack bounds. Empty cores, zero core dimension, and zero blocks are handled.

I checked the relevant source passages in Basu–Pollack–Roy, Section 1.3 (including the bit-size convention and Theorems 1.3.1/1.3.3) and Section 3.2 (realizable signs and univariate samples), and Adler–Beling, Section 5 Remark 1 (rational-machine complexity depending on problem degree). These support the uses in the manuscript. I also compared the substantive fixed-core and controlled-bypass canonical result files with the manuscript proof, without relying on their status lines.

For the pooling mappings (`s4:fixed-inputs`, `s4:fixed-qualities`, `s4:vertex-integrity`, and `s4:structural-corollaries`), I checked all arc categories, aggregate signs, dimensions, and inactive cases. Inlet fractions give the correct feed flows and feed-arc objective coefficients. Exact affine quality compression preserves every original product row. In the vertex-integrity decomposition each arc is owned once, no bypass joins two residual components, and local dimensions are bounded by the stated expression. Deleted-product totals are particularly important: the retained delivery and coordinate-mass variables put the unbounded family of original product-quality rows in the core, leaving only `t+1` aggregate equations per deleted product. Thus the proof does not incorrectly treat product specification rank as bounded. Its bounds on retained masses and its degree-two right-hand sides are valid. Recognition failure is appropriately distinguished from physical infeasibility.
