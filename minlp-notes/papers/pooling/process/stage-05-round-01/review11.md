## Two full source-quality vectors

**Locations: lines 1118–1340, `s5:two-vector-theorem` and `s5:two-vector-extensions`. No finding.**

The normalization restricts the full source vectors, rather than merely the number of attributes. With two distinct rational vectors, one differing coordinate supplies a rational scalar coordinate, and checking all coordinates of every positive-demand product establishes membership in the segment. Local mass conservation cancels the affine offset. Zero-demand qualities can be replaced because nonnegative incoming flows are then zero. The single-vector case is linear. Section 1 also explicitly handles the empty-input case, so the theorem does not need a separate zero-vector branch.

The class-total equations are necessary even with variable supplies: summing physical product mass and quality equations fixes total withdrawals from class 1 as `sum_j b_j B_j`, and total withdrawals from class 0 as `sum_j b_j(1-B_j)`. They do not fix an individual input withdrawal. On `0<q<1`, the intervals on `t_i`, `h_i`, and `w_ij` preserve the actual source, feed, and bypass bounds, with the sign reversed precisely for class 0. The local transformed source equation recovers the original source equation on division by the nonzero class factor.

For the reverse direction, the two class equations imply total recovered withdrawals equal total contracted product demand. Thus recovered feeds and outlets have equal total flow. The product equations and class equations also give `sum_i t_i=sum_ij w_ij`; summing the local source equations yields `sum_i h_i=0`. This is exactly `-q sum_(I_0)y_i+(1-q)sum_(I_1)y_i=0`, hence the physical pool quality balance. No extra aggregate equation is needed, and no bypass-degree assumption enters these deductions.

I checked the outlet identity directly from `s_j=-W_0j/q+W_1j/(1-q)` and the product residual equation. It gives

`W_0j=q b_j(B_j-1)+q(1-q)v_j`.

Consequently the stated lower affine bound enforces `v_j>=0`, the upper bound enforces the actual individual capacity, and their sum enforces the common upper capacity. An absent outlet has upper capacity zero and is enforced exactly, including at products missing one bypass class. Recovered outlets are defined by product mass conservation; the identity then verifies their bounds. At zero pool throughput, nonnegativity forces every incident flow to vanish.

The shared auxiliary variable has the correct direction: `r>=q^2` makes each `U(q-r)` upper bound no larger than `Uq(1-q)` when `U>=0`. Conversely choosing `r=q^2` retains every physical solution. Finite original capacities and `q,r in [0,1]` bound the affine polytope. The strict-interior procedure correctly excludes transformed endpoint artifacts: the epsilon LP tests the existence of any interior point of that polytope; a zero quadratic minimum forces every minimizer to have the same `q`; a negative minimum can be mixed with the interior point using the stated rational coefficient. The displayed bound on the resulting quadratic value is valid. This covers isolated feasible interior qualities as well as open feasible ranges. Endpoint LPs use the original physical rows.

Exact convex QP provides a polynomial-encoding rational optimizer and value. The manuscript's active-row KKT system gives an independent rationality argument; the algorithmic claim does not rely on enumerating active sets. Subsequent divisions by nonzero rational class factors preserve polynomial bit length.

For the additional resource rows, summing the outlet identity after multiplication by rational `alpha_j` is an equality regardless of the coefficient signs. Division is only by positive `q(1-q)`. The direction of auxiliary relaxation depends on the right-hand-side constant `U>=0`, not on the individual `alpha_j`. Thus signed resource coefficients, including `U=0`, are covered. Positive outlet and common lower bounds are explicitly excluded from this convex argument, and variable procurement optimization is correctly left unclaimed.
