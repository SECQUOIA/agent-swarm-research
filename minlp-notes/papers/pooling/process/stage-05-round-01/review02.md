## Parameterized projection and the assigned emphasis

**General planar paths, lines 30–129 (`s5:quasipoly-path`).** I checked the distinction between a small description for each parameter fiber and a uniform description on a parameter cell. The augmented minors supply the missing uniformity: they determine feasibility of every intersection of two independent boundary lines, including satisfaction of omitted rows. Keeping the box makes the vertex test valid for the selected system. Empty fibers, points, and segments have explicit handling. Including same-child Fourier–Motzkin pairs is necessary and is done. At singleton parameters the proof selects symbolic rows using their actual specialized signs; it does not accumulate independent algebraic coefficients.

The row bound from Section 4 can be used here even at algebraic samples: its geometric part works over real coefficients, while the stage-5 construction itself uses polynomial arithmetic. A bounded segment admits two opposite supporting rows for its affine hull and endpoint rows; a point admits at most four positively spanning normals. These facts justify selecting a small subset of candidates, rather than merely asserting that some unrelated small description exists. Balanced depth makes the retained row count, degree, and coefficient height polynomial. The additive overlay of one-dimensional partitions then gives the stated quasipolynomial cell count. The final original-path LP and Cramer determinants give a witness in the one chosen parameter field; factorially many determinant terms add only polynomially many coefficient bits.

**Equal-gain strips, lines 131–204 (`s5:strip`).** The same gain on both sides is essential and explicitly required. For nonzero gains, division by `A_i` reverses both interval endpoints whenever `A_i` is negative. This gives a genuine bounded difference system, not an inequality that only bounds a recursively defined state from one side. After the local lower/upper consistency conditions, every backtracking excursion has nonnegative length. Thus the unique simple path gives the directed shortest distance. The proposed assignment `y_i = min_j(U_j + d(j,i))` satisfies all lower bounds by the pairwise tests, all upper bounds using `j=i`, and both directions of each edge inequality by the directed triangle inequality.

Replacing an interval at a retained state by its prospective exact value, while keeping its old bounds separately, gives an exact projection. Additional bounds created by zero gains require no active-bound enumeration: every lower/upper pair is sufficient. A zero-gain edge must leave its interval at its original head and impose no condition on its original tail. The manuscript does precisely this before deleting the edge.

For an arbitrarily oriented tree, the equation `A_v = a_e A_u` is imposed in the original direction. An edge directed toward the chosen root therefore introduces a reciprocal gain during normalization. The proof never reverses a zero-gain relation. After removing zero edges, each component is a tree and the same unique-path recovery applies. Branching does not create exponentially many paths between a pair of vertices or require exponentially many choices of interval endpoints.

The encoding claim also survives these reciprocal factors. Along a root path every gain has exponent in `{-1,0,1}`. Normalized bounds and directed path sums therefore have denominators cleared by a product of gains with a fixed sufficient multiplicity. In a fixed number of parameters, the dense size of a polynomial whose degree is the sum of the input degrees is polynomial in the input length. Coefficient heights grow polynomially under these products and sums. The known signs justify clearing inequalities; gain-zero sign conditions are handled separately. This does not extend to succinct arithmetic-circuit input or a growing parameter dimension, and neither extension is claimed.

**Fixed total cycle rank and attainment, lines 205–214.** Removing `c` edges from the whole graph gives a spanning forest and at most `2c` additional retained states. Restoring the removed original strip rows checks the cycle conditions exactly, including any incompatible product of gains around a cycle. The argument needs fixed total cycle rank; a fixed bound on each block's rank would not bound the retained dimension. The stated theorem uses the correct quantity.

The objective must use retained coordinates, as stated. The projected relation and one objective-value coordinate have fixed dimension, so densely encoded polynomial degrees may grow while exact value-set elimination remains polynomial. The theorem correctly separates computing the infimum and deciding attainment from always returning an optimizer. For example, `x_1 = eta*x_0`, `x_1=1`, and `0<=x_0<=1` on the positive-gain cell allow `eta>=1`; minimizing retained `x_0` has infimum zero and no optimizer despite uniformly bounded states. This is compatible with the theorem's wording and its explicit warning about unbounded parameters. A nonempty compact closed original set does give attainment.
