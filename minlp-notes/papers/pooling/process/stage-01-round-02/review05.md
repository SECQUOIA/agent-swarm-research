## Mathematical checks

- **Model and encoding, lines 11–159.** The zero-flow conventions, distinction between threshold and feasibility, and finite-capacity hypothesis support the subsequent claims. For acyclic networks, a quality box gives a compact lift without removing any feasible arc flow. The affine-rank reduction is valid as a reduction of feasible flows: inactive qualities can be reassigned, and every original product inequality is retained after substitution. The stated ETR upper bound does not improperly imply NP membership.
- **Rank-one block, lines 163–187.** The positive-total margin identity and the separate zero matrix case are correct. Feed and outlet costs induce additive matrix coefficients, so the warning against transferring arbitrary-matrix objective hardness is justified.
- **Destination decomposition and single-product projection, lines 191–294.** Indexing the destination fraction by the arc head preserves the incoming mixture at each pool. The recursion also preserves flow balance; coordinatewise domination preserves all permitted capacity rows. Aggregate quality conservation and topological reconstruction establish both directions of the single-product LP projection. Averaging gives the stated cost inequalities with the correct signs, both for physical optimal flows and for the destination-flow relaxation.
- **Shortest-path criterion and witnesses, lines 295–340.** Deleting zero capacities before scaling is essential and is present. The support argument uses the rank of the active quality rows plus normalization. Rational LP vertices, simple shortest paths, and a minimum of finitely many positive capacity-to-load ratios give polynomial-size rational witnesses. Reconstruction can be treated as one triangular rational linear system, so its bit bound does not depend on an unjustified repeated-arithmetic argument.
- **Exact conic hull, lines 342–380.** Each containment is valid. Destination decomposition gives `S ⊆ Σ C_j`; exact single-product reconstruction gives `C_j ⊆ S`. Because `S` contains zero and is closed under nonnegative scaling, finite conic combinations can be converted into finite convex combinations. Finally, every fixed flow in `S` has a positive common scaling into `P` on the retained support. This proves `cone(P) = conv(S)` without assuming that a conic hull is closed. Closedness follows separately from the linear extended formulation: one flow copy per product, conservation and homogeneous product rows, and the sum equation. Both variable count and constraint count are polynomial in the explicitly encoded network and quality data. No claim of a polynomial number of inequalities in the original flow space is needed.
- **Capacity counterexample, lines 382–389.** With qualities 0 and 1 and exact pure product specifications, a shared pool can serve at most one product positively. That product's unit capacity makes total delivery at most one for every physical feasible flow and its convex hull. The sum of the two unit pure flows respects both feed capacities, both outlet capacities, both input and product capacities, and the pool capacity of two. It lies in the uncapacitated convex hull but has total delivery two. Thus the example proves exactly the stated failure of reimposing capacities after convexification.
- **Cycles, lines 391–526.** For a positive-flow strongly connected pool component, no external inflow implies no external outflow. Every remaining component has an invertible substochastic mixing block, while isolated circulation components admit constant qualities. The forward absorption argument after removing isolated terminal components yields the dominated destination decomposition. The circulation cone is physical and polyhedral, and scaling gives the cyclic hull identity as in the acyclic case. In the absence of negative cycles, removing cycles from a negative single-product ordinary flow preserves source withdrawals and product delivery and cannot increase cost. The converse uses reconstruction after routing, so it does not assume that the union of shortest paths is acyclic. Empty input/product sets, merging circulation into a designated component, attainment through a bounded quality lift, and the displayed near-singular residual example are handled consistently.
- **Facial specifications and endpoint class, lines 528–685.** Repeated use of the face property gives the compatibility rule and finite union of network-flow branches. Node splitting preserves the relevant bounds and allows the integrality argument. For a nonface, the proposed objective charges precisely forbidden feeds, giving a negative fractional mixture while every integral feasible flow costs zero. The faciality LP checks all possible input-mixture representations, which establishes the converse recognition argument. Fixed affine dimension gives polynomially many facet candidates and face descriptions; fixed pool count then gives polynomially many tuples. Positive lower bounds on omitted arcs are explicitly checked. The final endpoint disjunction and MILP use only upper quality bounds 0 or 1, as required; rational branch vertices also suffice for NP certificates with rational flow bounds.
