## Cyclic reconstruction, decomposition, approximation, and attainment

- **Lines 398–430, cyclic single-product reconstruction.** Summing conservation over a positive-support strongly connected component gives equality of external inflow and outflow. A component with no external inflow is therefore isolated, so a constant quality satisfies its equations without affecting a product. Every other component has a deficient row in its backward transition matrix. Reversing a strongly connected support preserves strong connectivity, which supplies a path to the deficiency from every row. The stated finite-state exit argument establishes invertibility. Nonnegativity of the inverse, together with its action on the boundary row sums, gives weights summing to one. The aggregate delivered-quality identity follows after all internal quality terms cancel. The global system outside isolated components is block triangular with nonsingular diagonal blocks, supporting the polynomial rational encoding claim.

- **Lines 446–466, cyclic destination decomposition and hull.** A terminal positive-support pool component without a product outlet cannot receive positive external flow, so it has no positive-flow connection to the rest of the network. Removing all such components leaves every active pool able to reach a product: otherwise a terminal component reachable from that pool would be one of the removed components, contradicting isolation. Consequently the forward chain is absorbed. Multiplication by the absorption probability at the head of each arc preserves incoming proportions, pool conservation, and the original pool qualities. Each destination component preserves the complete inflow to its designated product. This proves physicality and domination, rather than merely ordinary flow conservation.

- **Lines 487–498, circulation merging and the output-count factor.** The removed circulation has disjoint active pool vertices as well as disjoint positive arcs. It can therefore be combined with one destination component using the original qualities on the destination part and a constant quality on the isolated part. Their sum is dominated by the original flow and preserves node capacities. There are exactly `m` components after this operation, including when the designated product receives no flow. Thus averaging gives the stated factor `m`, including instances whose only profitable activity is an isolated circulation. The proof does not accidentally require `m+1` components or require every component to deliver positive product flow.

- **Lines 281–293 and 495–498, shared-capacity LP.** The objective is additive over the destination flows. Nonnegativity and upper capacities on their sum imply that each component independently respects every arc and node upper bound. The single-product reconstruction applies to each LP component even if its support contains a circulation. The merged physical decomposition supplies a feasible relaxed solution for every physical flow. Selecting the least-cost relaxed component then proves `z_rel <= z* <= z_best <= z_rel/m <= 0`. The signs and inequality directions are correct; this argument does not assume that summing independently physical LP components produces a physical pooling flow.

- **Lines 499–503, cyclic attainment.** The reconstruction's component argument is independent of the number of products. On every nonisolated active component, its unique quality vector is a convex combination of upstream boundary qualities and ultimately input qualities. Isolated components have no effect on any delivered quality and can use zero. Inactive pools can also use zero. Therefore every feasible arc flow admits a lift in the single fixed box containing zero and all input qualities. Intersecting the original polynomial constraints with that box and the finite flow bounds gives a compact lift with the same arc-flow projection. This establishes attainment, including when there are no inputs. It does not assume that every quality assignment on an isolated circulation is source-derived.

- **Lines 468–479 and 505–522.** A negative directed cycle can be scaled into all positive upper capacities. With no negative cycle, removing the cycles in an ordinary flow decomposition does not increase cost or change source withdrawals and deliveries; reconstructing the resulting single-product flow restores the mixing equations. The small-leakage example's determinant, solution, and residual are correct and illustrate why a small absolute residual is insufficient near an isolated circulation.
