## Checks and reasons

- **Physical model and decision problems, lines 11–104.** The conservation and quality-mass equations describe perfect mixing for the stated standard graph. Bypasses enter both delivery and attribute mass correctly. The homogeneous product inequalities give the stated zero-delivery convention, including empty product regions and exact quality contracts at demand zero: nonnegative incoming flows force the mass vector to zero. At a zero-throughput pool, nonnegativity and both sides of conservation force all incident flows to zero. The lower-bound checks required for removing a pool preserve feasibility, and the instruction to retain external contracts avoids discarding demands satisfiable by bypasses or other pools. The definitions distinguish exact throughput from exact product contracts and separate standard economic costs from arbitrary arc costs. Zero feasibility is asserted only under the stated absence of positive lower contracts. The generalized cyclic model explicitly permits isolated steady-state circulations; the later cycle results are consistent with this choice.

- **Encoding and affine reduction, lines 108–159.** The finite-capacity convention supplies the flow boundedness required for attainment. For an acyclic network, induction expresses every active pool quality as an input mixture; inactive qualities can be reset without changing flows. Thus a bounded closed lift can be selected even though the unrestricted set of inactive qualities is unbounded. The empty-input case is handled separately. The affine-coordinate equation follows from conservation and independence of the columns of `B`; resetting inactive qualities preserves the projected feasible flows. Substituting all original product rows preserves growing attribute counts. The rank-zero case reduces to linear restrictions. The section does not infer a general NP certificate from its existential-real formulation.

- **Rank-one block, lines 163–187.** Both directions of the margin factorization hold, including the explicitly separate zero matrix. The expression for delivered quality mass follows from the factorization. The objective warning is substantive and correct: physical feed/outlet costs produce additive coefficients, so arbitrary matrix costs define a broader model.

- **Acyclic decomposition, sign, and paths, lines 189–333.** The component multiplier must be indexed by the head of the arc; the manuscript does so. This simultaneously preserves each pool's incoming proportions and, by the backward recursion, conservation. Every component is dominated by the original flow, which preserves arc, source, pool, and product upper capacities under the stated zero lower bounds. In a single-product conserved acyclic flow, all input quality mass reaches that product, and topological reconstruction proves the converse LP projection. The objective inequalities have the correct direction for nonpositive minimization costs. Shortest-path mixture routing preserves aggregate composition; positive common scaling enforces all retained capacities. Zero-capacity node deletion is necessary and is included. The vertex-support argument yields at most `K+1` paths for coordinate quality specifications. LP vertices, path costs, scaling, and reconstructed qualities all have polynomial rational encoding. Empty product and unreachable-input cases are covered.

- **Uncapacitated hull, lines 335–385.** The argument uses finite conic combinations and explicitly establishes the otherwise potentially problematic closure conclusion through a linear extended formulation. Positive scaling from `S` into `P` is valid on the retained support. The stated two-quality example does show that reimposing capacities on the uncapacitated convex hull can yield a larger set than the capacitated convex hull.

- **Cyclic extension, lines 387–526.** Summing balances over a positive-support component shows that a component without external inflow also has no external outflow. Such components can receive constant qualities independently. Every other strongly connected component has an irreducible substochastic backward matrix with a deficient row, so the finite-step escape argument gives the required inverse. The full system on nonisolated pools is nonsingular, supporting the rational bit bound. For destination decomposition, terminal components with no product exit are isolated, so removing them does not strand an upstream component. The remaining forward routing chain is absorbed at products. Negative cycles are valid profitable circulations under the declared algebraic semantics. Removing nonnegative-cost cycles preserves source withdrawals and product delivery; single-product reconstruction supplies the mixing equations for the remaining path mixture. Merging the isolated circulation into one destination component preserves feasibility and domination. The bounded-lift attainment argument includes zero for isolated components and covers an empty input set. The displayed two-pool residual example has determinant and residual as claimed.

- **Facial specifications and endpoint class, lines 528–685.** Applying the face property first to product inflows and then to pool inflows gives exactly the forbidden direct arcs and feed–outlet pairs. Compatible supports therefore produce network-flow branches, including arbitrary bypasses and node bounds. Integral network branches supply an integral optimal flow for any rational linear objective. In the converse construction, a nonfacial feasible mixture uses positive mass from an input outside `F`; unit pool capacity prevents an integral flow from combining inputs, so every integral feasible flow costs zero while a fractional feasible flow has negative cost. The faciality recognition LP tests precisely this forbidden mass. The smallest-face enumeration retains all positive flows of any feasible solution, and the explicit lower-bound check prevents invalid deletion branches. Fixed affine dimension bounds the facet/face enumeration polynomially. The endpoint disjunction requires nonnegative attributes and only the stated 0/1 upper specifications; those hypotheses are present. Its big-M rows follow from pool throughput bounds, and the integer/rational certificate arguments follow from the corresponding branch network polytopes.
