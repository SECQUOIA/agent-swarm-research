## Checks supporting the verdict

- **Encoding and compactness, lines 108–159.** The bounds on every physical arc, together with a common input-quality box, give a compact lift in the standard and acyclic generalized models. Resetting inactive qualities preserves all constraints under the stated absence of intermediate quality restrictions. Thus the flow projection is compact and the linear optimum is attained, including instances with lower bounds. The empty-input case is separately handled. The affine-rank substitution preserves all original product rows; polynomial-size rational coordinates follow from determinant bounds for rational elimination. Its statement is properly about the physical projection, with inactive qualities freely reassigned. The text does not infer general NP membership or rational optimal solutions from the rational quadratic formulation. No algorithm in this stage depends on an unproved polynomial-size algebraic representation.

- **Destination decomposition and one-product reconstruction, lines 189–289.** The multiplier belongs at the head of an arc. With that choice both mass and incoming quality mass at a pool scale by the same factor, while the destination recursion gives outgoing conservation. Domination preserves the permitted upper bounds. Summing pool quality equations identifies the sole product's quality mass, so the projected single-product problem is indeed an LP. An optimal rational LP vertex has polynomial encoding length. Reconstruction uses a triangular system whose coefficients are rational sums of those flows, with nonzero diagonal at active pools. A determinant bound on the whole system justifies polynomial output length; repeated averaging is not being treated as a unit-cost arithmetic operation. The approximation inequalities have the correct direction for nonpositive minimized cost.

- **Path witness and conic hull, lines 291–389.** At a mixture vertex, normalization and at most K independent active quality rows yield support at most K+1. Shortest simple paths have polynomial length, and rational path costs, the mixture vertex, and the common capacity scale all have polynomial encoding length. Zero-capacity deletion ensures that every positive load has a positive scaling allowance. The conic-hull proof uses finite combinations and positive scaling; closedness follows from the polyhedral extended formulation rather than from an assumption that arbitrary conic hulls are closed. The capacitated counterexample has the stated individual and common capacity behavior.

- **Cyclic reconstruction and attainment, lines 398–503.** A positive-flow strongly connected pool component with no external inflow also has no external outflow. Every other component has a deficient row in its backward substochastic matrix, and irreducibility gives invertibility. More directly for the bit claim, all nonisolated active pools can be solved in one block triangular rational system; its determinant is nonzero and has polynomial bit length. The isolated components may use zero. Although conditioning can be arbitrarily poor, this does not violate exact rational bit bounds. For a feasible multiple-product flow, nonisolated qualities are forced by the source qualities, while isolated components cannot affect a product. Consequently resetting isolated qualities to zero preserves product feasibility, and one fixed box containing zero and all input qualities gives the closed bounded lift asserted at lines 499–503. The empty-input case and isolated negative circulations are included. Absorption-based destination decomposition, the negative-cycle alternative, and the merge of an isolated circulation into one product component are consistent with these semantics.

- **Facial specifications and exact algorithms, lines 529–686.** Compatibility on positive feeds and outlets gives exactly a finite union of bounded network-flow polytopes. The node-splitting construction preserves integer bounds, and the total-unimodularity argument supplies integral vertices and attained optimal flows. For the converse, a real negative mixture suffices initially because the resulting one-product rational LP is bounded and has a negative rational optimum. Faciality recognition is a polynomial-size rational LP test. In fixed affine dimension, supporting hyperplanes through input subsets and intersections of a basis of active facet normals give polynomially many face descriptions with polynomial rational encoding. Fixed pool count then gives polynomially many network problems. Checking removed arcs against positive lower bounds is explicit. In the endpoint class, a minimizing branch vertex supplies a short rational threshold witness, and integer bounds supply an integral one; exponential branch enumeration does not obstruct NP membership.
