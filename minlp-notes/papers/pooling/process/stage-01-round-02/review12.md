## Verification

- **Models and certificates (lines 1–159).** The distinctions between exact flow and product contracts, arbitrary arc costs and standard economic costs, feasibility and a negative-cost threshold, and standard versus cyclic steady-state semantics are sufficient for the subsequent statements. Zero-throughput qualities can be reassigned without altering flows or delivered masses. The compactness argument for acyclic networks correctly uses a bounded lift after reassignment, not boundedness of every original quality assignment. The affine-rank reduction uses mass conservation and injectivity of the column map; its treatment of inactive pools and rank zero preserves feasibility.
- **Rank-one block (lines 161–187).** The normalized outer product has the required margins. The converse factorization and its zero-total exception are correct. The restriction on which matrix costs correspond to physical arc costs prevents an invalid transfer of optimization hardness.
- **Acyclic destination decomposition and sign test (lines 189–279).** Multiplication by the fraction at the head of each arc gives a common multiplier to all inflows of a pool. The recursion supplies the same multiplier to total outflow, so both conservation and mixing hold with the original quality. Product inflows are preserved for the chosen destination. Nonnegativity and zero lower bounds are essential and are stated. The single-product LP converse reconstructs qualities topologically; aggregate quality conservation suffices because there is only one served product and no intermediate quality restriction. Averaging costs proves the displayed approximation inequality and the sign equivalence in the correct directions.
- **Relaxation and path criterion (lines 281–343).** Each relaxed component respects the original upper capacities by domination. Removing zero-capacity support before scaling is necessary and explicitly required. Ordinary path decomposition gives a lower bound from shortest-path costs. Conversely, routing a feasible source mixture and applying the single-product reconstruction gives a physical flow after the displayed positive scaling. The support-size argument correctly counts the normalization row in addition to at most K independent quality rows. Rational LP vertices, rational shortest-path costs, the capacity-ratio scale, and the rational linear reconstruction give polynomial-size witnesses.
- **Uncapacitated hull (lines 345–389).** Destination decomposition proves the containment in the sum of single-product cones; physicality of the individual cones proves the reverse containment after taking the conic hull. Scaling closure and inclusion of zero justify replacing the conic hull by the convex hull. Strictly positive retained capacities justify the equality with the cone generated by the capacitated set. The two-pure-product example correctly separates adding capacities to the uncapacitated convexification from convexifying the capacitated physical set.
- **Cycles (lines 391–526).** In a positive-flow strongly connected pool component, zero external inflow forces zero external outflow. Such a component is therefore an isolated circulation. For other components the normalized backward matrix is substochastic and irreducible, with a deficient row, which validates the finite-step escape argument and invertibility. The combined nonisolated linear system has rational data and nonsingular diagonal blocks, giving the claimed encoding bound. In the destination decomposition, removing terminal components that do not feed a product leaves every remaining active pool able to reach a product; those removed components have no positive external inflow and cannot support an upstream chain of additional discarded flow. Absorption probabilities therefore give the stated decomposition. Negative cycles yield physical circulations, while deleting nonnegative-cost cycles preserves withdrawals, delivery, and aggregate quality. The extension of the approximation inequality appropriately merges the isolated circulation component into one destination component. The bounded-lift existence argument and the near-singular two-pool calculation are consistent with these semantics.
- **Facial specifications and integrality (lines 528–601).** The face property applies first to product contributors and then to a contributing pool's input mixture. This proves the support compatibility condition in both directions, including the empty face. Fixing compatible supports yields ordinary bounded network-flow branches; splitting nodes and adding a bounded return arc permits the total-unimodularity argument. Projection preserves integrality. In the converse, nonfaciality supplies a feasible mixture containing positive mass from forbidden inputs; unit throughput makes every nonzero integral flow pure, so the negative feed costs distinguish the fractional feasible mixture from every integral feasible flow. The quantifier is correctly over a constructed network and objective on the same input qualities, not over every network with that product region.
- **Recognition and bounded-pool algorithm (lines 603–650).** Maximizing forbidden-input weight recognizes faciality: if the maximum is zero, expanding both endpoints of any relevant segment shows both belong to the region. Enumeration by pool faces is complete because a product face containing a pool quality contains its smallest containing face, and every positive pool feed lies in that face. The manuscript explicitly rejects branches omitting an arc with a positive lower bound. In fixed affine dimension, facet enumeration and intersections of at most d independent active facet hyperplanes provide polynomially many face descriptions; fixed pool count keeps tuple enumeration polynomial.
- **Endpoint formulation and certificates (lines 652–685).** Nonnegative input attributes make a zero product upper bound equivalent to exclusion of every positive contributing quality mass. This gives the feed/outlet disjunction exactly, while upper bound one is redundant. The throughput bound controls both disjunctive sums. Integral or rational branch vertices give polynomial-size threshold witnesses with directly checkable compatibility, without requiring a general NP certificate claim for pooling.
