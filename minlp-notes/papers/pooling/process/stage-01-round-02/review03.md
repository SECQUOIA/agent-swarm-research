Reviewed the complete `papers/pooling/sections/01-foundations.tex` (lines 1–685), with particular attention to destination disaggregation, the exact single-product LP projection, capacity preservation, and the approximation inequalities. I followed `reviewer-protocol.md`, did not inspect other reviewers' reports, and did not edit the manuscript.

No major or minor findings identified. The following records the mathematical checks underlying that verdict; these are verification notes, not requested changes.

1. Destination decomposition, lines 189–229. The head multiplier is the correct choice. At a pool, every incoming flow and its quality mass acquire the same factor, while the reverse recursion gives precisely the same outgoing total. Product inflows are preserved at the selected destination and vanish elsewhere. The fractions sum to one on every positive arc, including bypasses. Coordinatewise domination preserves input withdrawals, pool throughputs, product deliveries, and all individual arc upper bounds. This argument uses the stated zero lower bounds and absence of intermediate quality constraints; the manuscript does not extend it to positive flow contracts.

2. Single-product projection, lines 231–257. Aggregate source quality mass is necessary by cancellation of all internal quality flows. It is also sufficient for each particular LP arc-flow vector: topological averaging reconstructs qualities without changing that vector. Multiple routes and reconverging pools cause no additional quality condition because there is only one served product. The zero-delivery case follows from conservation and acyclicity. Thus the lemma proves equality of arc-flow projections, not merely equality of optimal values or an existence test with rerouting.

3. Polynomial sign test and approximation, lines 259–293. The inequality directions are correct for minimization with nonpositive optimum. Each single-product feasible set is contained in the full physical set, giving `z* <= z_best`. Decomposition of a physical optimum gives a component with cost at most `z*/m`. For the destination-flow relaxation, capacities imposed on the sum also bound every nonnegative component. An optimal relaxed solution therefore gives `z_best <= z_rel/m`, while physical decomposition gives `z_rel <= z*`. Neither averaging argument requires individual component costs to be nonpositive. Zero flow supplies the final nonpositivity and sign equivalence. The reconstruction bit bound follows from a rational triangular system of polynomial dimension and coefficient length; it does not require a bound obtained by repeatedly multiplying denominators along every route.

4. Shortest paths and conic hull, lines 295–389. Path decomposition preserves aggregate source withdrawals. The mixture LP has an optimal vertex supported on at most `K+1` inputs because opposite bounds on one quality coordinate add no independent row. The explicit common scale enforces all positive retained capacities with polynomial rational encoding. Removing zero-capacity nodes is necessary and is stated. Scaling then proves the capacity-dependent cone equality. The sum of the single-product cones has a polynomial linear formulation, so the closedness claim does not incorrectly assume that arbitrary conic hulls are closed. The two-product pure-quality example correctly disproves reimposing capacities after convexification.

5. Cyclic extension, lines 391–526. In an active strongly connected component, external inflow and outflow have equal totals. Components with no external inflow are isolated; other components yield an irreducible substochastic backward matrix with an exit, so the stated inverse and reconstruction are valid. Removing terminal components with no product exit leaves an absorbing forward chain. Its destination fractions preserve mixing by the same head-multiplier calculation. The isolated circulation flow can be merged into one destination component: there are no positive connecting arcs, and the merged flow remains dominated by the original. This justifies both approximation chains with denominator `m`, rather than `m+1`. The no-negative-cycle argument legitimately removes ordinary flow cycles before reconstructing a single-product physical flow. The bounded closed lift includes zero for isolated circulations, including when there are no inputs. I also checked the displayed determinant and residual example.

6. Other foundations, lines 1–188 and 528–685. The zero-flow conventions, finite-capacity assumption, exact-contract distinctions, compactness argument, ETR upper bound, affine-rank substitution, rank-one margins, and restriction on physical matrix costs are consistent. In the facial theorem, applying the face property twice yields the feed–outlet compatibility rule; compatible support branches are bounded network-flow polytopes. The nonfacial converse produces a negative-cost fractional mixture while every integral unit-throughput solution costs zero. Facial recognition correctly maximizes forbidden-input weight. Fixed-dimensional face enumeration and fixed-pool tuples give polynomial enumeration with polynomial-size rational data. Positive lower bounds on omitted arcs are explicitly retained as infeasibility checks. The endpoint disjunction and its MILP use bounds on both quantities no greater than pool throughput; their stated scope excludes additional nonredundant quality constraints. The NP certificates follow from integral or rational branch optima.
