## Mathematical checks

- **Facial sufficiency, lines 538–578.** Every active pool quality is in the input hull. Applying the face property first to a positive product blend and then to each contributing pool proves the claimed feed–outlet compatibility. Conversely, compatibility puts each contributing input and therefore the final blend in the required face. This handles arbitrary bypasses, multiple products, the whole-hull face, and the empty face. In particular, an outlet into an empty face cannot carry positive flow because conservation would require a positive feed, producing a forbidden pair.
- **Network representation and integrality, lines 558–578.** A branch fixes only permitted arcs; original bounds, including positive lower bounds on omitted arcs, remain constraints. Source withdrawal, pool throughput, and product delivery bounds can each be placed on an auxiliary node arc. Connecting the sources and products to common terminals and adding the bounded return arc gives a circulation formulation. No sum involving unrelated flows is introduced. With integer original bounds, effective bounds and the return-arc bound can also be integer. The incidence-matrix argument establishes an integral circulation polytope, and coordinate projection preserves integrality. Finiteness and boundedness justify choosing an integral minimizer from an optimal nonempty branch. The theorem asserts existence of an integral optimizer, not integrality of every feasible or optimal flow.
- **Universal necessity, lines 580–601.** For a nonempty nonface, the segment witness yields a feasible unit mixture with positive weight on an input outside the product region. Penalizing exactly those feeds gives negative cost. Unit pool capacity forces every nonzero integral flow to select one input, whose quality must lie in the product region, so all integral feasible flows have cost zero. The construction uses the same listed input qualities, integer capacities, and the stated two-valued costs. Rationality follows from the bounded mixture LP. The explicit qualification at lines 596–598 correctly limits the converse to existence of a network and objective; it makes no false claim about every network with a nonfacial region.
- **Facial recognition and fixed-parameter enumeration, lines 603–649.** The forbidden-weight LP detects every violation of the face property, including when the input list contains nonvertices or duplicates. Choosing the smallest face containing an active pool quality preserves all positive feed and outlet arcs. The inactive-pool choice is harmless. The explicit positive-lower-bound check prevents branch deletion from relaxing a contract. In fixed affine dimension, facets can be obtained from affinely independent input subsets, and a basis of active facet normals describes any nonempty proper face. Membership and containment can be checked using generating input points. The resulting enumeration and rational network solves have polynomial bit complexity for fixed pool count and affine dimension, without implying a uniform fixed-parameter exponent.
- **Endpoint specialization, lines 652–685.** The nonnegativity of the input coordinates is essential and is present. The stated absence of additional nonredundant quality restrictions makes the disjunction sufficient as well as necessary. Both sums in each disjunction are bounded by the pool throughput, validating the common big-M bound. Integral optimal flows with integer bounds, or optimal branch vertices with rational bounds, give polynomial-size threshold certificates. This conclusion concerns actual arc flows and does not require integer pool qualities or pure feeds.
- **Models and exact encodings, lines 1–174.** I checked zero-flow semantics, distinction between physical and arbitrary routing costs, exact contracts, compactness in the acyclic model, the existential-real upper bound, and affine-rank substitution. Inactive qualities do not obstruct equivalence of the flow projection. The input-empty case is separated where the source-derived box is used.
- **Rank-one block, lines 176–202.** The margin identity and its converse are correct for nonzero nonnegative rank-one matrices, with zero flow treated separately. Physical feed/outlet costs induce additive matrix coefficients; the warning about broader matrix objectives is justified.
- **Acyclic decomposition, sign, paths, and conic hull, lines 204–396.** Multiplying each arc by the absorption fraction at its head preserves incoming composition at every pool and conservation. Coordinatewise domination is sufficient for the stated upper-capacity constraints. The single-product LP is exact because all quality mass terminates at one product and intermediate qualities are unrestricted. Averaging yields the cost inequalities with the correct signs. The shortest-path construction requires zero lower bounds, positive retained capacities, and common scaling; all are stated. The vertex support bound and rational-size witness follow from rank and determinant bounds. The conic hull proof uses actual finite conic combinations and then a polyhedral extended formulation, so closedness is derived rather than assumed. The capacity counterexample separates the bounded convex hull from the capacity intersection with the unbounded hull.
- **Cyclic extension, lines 398–526.** Summing balances over a strongly connected component makes a component without external inflow an isolated circulation. For other active components, the backward substochastic matrix has an exit and is invertible. In the decomposition proof, a terminal component without a product outlet cannot have positive external inflow; all remaining active pools reach a product. Thus forward absorption, the head-weight construction, and the separate circulation component are valid. Pool-only directed cycles are physical under the expressly stated algebraic semantics. Removing nonnegative-cost cycles preserves source withdrawals and product delivery, which suffices for reconstruction. The bounded quality box may include zero for isolated circulations, giving compactness even with no inputs. I also checked the displayed two-pool determinant and residual calculation.
