## Mathematical checks

- **Model and certificates, lines 9–159.** The finite-capacity convention supplies the boundedness needed later. Zero-flow treatment, exact contracts, economic versus routing costs, and the distinction between profitable flow and feasibility are consistent. The acyclic input-quality box gives a compact lift without asserting that every original inactive quality is bounded. The affine-rank substitution preserves the flow projection, including inactive pools and rank zero. The polynomial formulation supplies the stated existential-real upper bound without an unwarranted NP conclusion.
- **Rank-one block, lines 161–187.** Both margin identities and the converse nonnegative factorization hold, including the separately handled zero matrix. Feed/outlet costs induce the additive coefficient form stated in the text.
- **Acyclic decomposition and sign test, lines 189–333.** The head multiplier is essential and is used correctly: every incoming flow and its quality mass receive the same multiplier, while the recursion establishes outgoing balance. Aggregate quality conservation gives the exact single-product LP projection. Coordinatewise domination preserves the stated capacities. The averaging inequalities have the correct direction for nonpositive minimization objectives. The shortest-path construction preserves source weights; positive support capacities permit rational common scaling. The vertex support argument gives at most K+1 inputs. Triangular rational reconstruction has polynomial bit size by a determinant argument; it does not require a numerical conditioning bound.
- **Uncapacitated hull, lines 335–387.** Destination decomposition and the inclusion of each single-product cone in the physical set establish both containments. Scaling explains why convex hull and conic hull coincide. A polynomial-size linear formulation establishes closedness without assuming arbitrary conic hulls are closed. The capacity counterexample distinguishes the bounded convex hull correctly.
- **Cyclic reconstruction, lines 396–430.** Summing conservation over a positive-support SCC proves equality of external inflow and outflow. A component with no external inflow consequently has no positive connection out of the component and can be assigned a common quality. In every other SCC the normalized backward matrix has a deficient row. Strong connectivity supplies a path from every row to some deficiency, so the finite-state uniform escape argument proves spectral radius less than one. The inverse is nonnegative. Applied to the external inflow weights, it also gives weights summing to one, which justifies the asserted boundary-quality average. Topological processing of the SCCs therefore reaches input qualities, since isolated components have no positive outgoing boundary flow. The combined nonisolated system is block triangular with nonsingular diagonal blocks. Its original rational coefficients have polynomial encoding, so Cramer's rule gives the claimed polynomial rational output size even when escape probabilities are very small.
- **Cyclic hull and sign criterion, lines 433–485.** A terminal SCC with no product outlet is isolated by conservation. No other positive-support SCC can feed it; thus removing all such SCCs leaves every remaining active pool able to reach a product. The forward chain is absorbed with probability one, and its head-weighted components retain physical mixing and sum to the original flow together with the isolated circulation. An arbitrary nonnegative circulation is physical under the explicitly chosen algebraic semantics. With no negative cycle, path-and-cycle decomposition removes only nonnegative-cost cycles while preserving source withdrawals and deliveries. Aggregate single-product quality conservation then justifies replacing paths by shortest paths. This argument does not require the retained paths to have an acyclic union.
- **Cyclic approximation and attainment, lines 487–503.** The isolated circulation has no positive arc connection to the remaining support. Merging it into one product component therefore preserves physical feasibility and coordinatewise domination, allowing the m-component averaging argument. Nonisolated qualities are input mixtures; replacing isolated qualities by zero preserves flows and product masses. A common box containing zero and all input qualities gives the required bounded closed lift, including the no-input case. Limits where an externally fed component becomes isolated do not invalidate this lift.
- **Singularity and conditioning, lines 505–526.** The unit closed two-pool circulation has one free common quality. For the perturbed example, direct multiplication gives determinant epsilon, solution `(lambda, lambda)`, and residual `(epsilon, 0)` at `(lambda+1, lambda+1)`. This establishes the stated failure of an absolute residual to control concentration error. It is consistent with polynomial exact bit complexity: small determinants can have short rational encodings.
- **Facial specifications and algorithms, lines 528–685.** The repeated face argument gives precisely the forbidden feed/outlet pairs and bypasses. Each compatible support branch is a network polytope, with original lower bounds retained; node splitting preserves integrality. The nonfacial converse produces a negative feasible mixture while all integral feasible flows have zero cost. Faciality recognition tests exactly whether any feasible mixture uses a forbidden input. The fixed-dimension face enumeration and smallest-face assignment cover every feasible pool support. Empty product faces, inactive pools, and removed positive-lower-bound arcs are handled. The endpoint disjunction and its throughput-based MILP follow from nonnegative quality masses. Integer or rational branch flows supply the claimed threshold certificates.
