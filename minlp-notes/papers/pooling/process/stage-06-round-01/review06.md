Interpreting “union” as a set removes duplicate projected points before testing whether a candidate lies in the convex hull of the others. Adding a segment introduces only the existing vertices and their translates as possible new vertices. Retaining an arbitrary original-box corner for each projected vertex suffices: subsequent interpolation needs a feasible preimage, not a uniquely determined one. Zero generators, fixed coordinates, and lower effective dimension do not invalidate the arrangement bound.

I checked the completeness argument at lines 563–585 carefully. At a fixed positive total, the two projected slices are independent, compact polytopes. Starting from a global optimum on their product, the first affine minimization can select a row-slice vertex without changing the optimum, and the second can select a column-slice vertex while retaining that row vertex. A slice vertex lies in the relative interior of a smallest original face of dimension at most one. If that face is an edge with equal endpoint totals, its interior cannot be extreme in the slice. Singleton original vertices and all unequal-total vertex segments therefore cover all required slice vertices. Additional nonedge segments create feasible candidates only, so they cannot lower the computed value below the true optimum.

At lines 587–614, interpolated projected coordinates are affine in the common total, giving exactly `alpha*S + beta + gamma/S`. The listed derivative cases exhaust minima: an interior minimum requires positive `alpha` and `gamma`; two negative coefficients give an interior maximum; the remaining nonconstant cases are monotone. Singleton intersections and a zero-only total are addressed. For a candidate interval reaching zero, nonnegative margins with total zero vanish, and the bound `|<C,W>| <= ||C||_infinity*S` ensures the correct zero extension. No unattained negative infimum is lost there.

Each projected box has at most a polynomial number of vertices for fixed `k`; taking all vertex pairs on each side and then pairing sides still gives `(m+n)^{O(k+1)}` candidates. Rational generator sums, interval intersections, and fractional coefficients retain polynomial bit length. Exact comparison of two candidate values need not place all candidates into a growing common field: each comparison has degree at most four, whereas the selected total, its interpolated margins, and `xy^T/S` lie in one quadratic field. Thus the proof supports the output claim as well as the running-time claim. The explicit warning that this is not an FPT bound is appropriate.
