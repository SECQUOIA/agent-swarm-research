# Instructions for a stage review

Review the entire assigned manuscript stage, with special attention to your assigned focus. Read the source proofs and relevant primary literature where necessary. Do not assume that an earlier PASS, a result status line, or a numerical experiment establishes a theorem.

Do not edit the manuscript or inspect the other reviewers' reports before writing your own. Save your report under the assigned round directory. Give exact manuscript locations, the mathematical reason for each concern, severity, and a proposed correction or a concrete counterexample when possible. Distinguish an actual defect from a request for explanation or an optional extension.

Major findings include a false or unsupported theorem, a missing essential hypothesis, an invalid reduction direction, a failure of polynomial bit complexity or certificate size, a material missing topic, or a source-attribution problem that changes the contribution. Minor findings include local ambiguities or repairable exposition that do not undermine the mathematical result. A difficult proof is not itself a defect; identify the unsupported inference.

End with a clear verdict: major findings present, minor findings only, or no findings. State what was checked and what remains outside your verification. Do not promise external-review acceptance or claim exhaustive correctness.

Root adjudicates every finding. A different agent implements accepted corrections. Any accepted major finding requires a fresh 15-reviewer round after repair. All accepted minor findings are also repaired and checked. Later changes that materially affect an accepted stage reopen its review.
