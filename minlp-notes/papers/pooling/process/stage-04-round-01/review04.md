## Finding 1 — check both endpoints of bounds before removing an unusable pool

**Severity: minor.** Locations: `04-structural-algorithms.tex` lines 10–12, 289–292, and 801–805 (`s4:fixed-inputs` and `s4:bounded-attachments`). The related convention in Section 1, `s1:model`, also mentions checking lower bounds only.

The section allows arbitrary rational lower and upper throughput bounds, but the removal instructions check only the pool and incident-arc lower bounds against zero. Nonnegativity of the flows does not imply that every *supplied upper bound* is nonnegative: an upper bound below zero specifies an infeasible instance. No explicit initial normalization or rejection of such bounds appears in these proofs or the referenced model definition.

For example, take one input, one pool with no incoming arc, one product, and one pool–product arc with bounds `[0,1]`. Give the pool throughput the rational interval `[-2,-1]`; give all other throughputs bounds `[0,1]` and all quality data zero. Conservation forces pool throughput and the outlet flow to zero, so the original instance is infeasible. Checking the pool's lower bound `-2` against zero succeeds. Removing the pool and its arc then leaves a feasible zero-flow instance. Thus the stated preprocessing, read literally, can discard the contradictory upper bound.

**Correction:** before deletion, check that zero belongs to every incident-arc and pool-throughput interval. Equivalently, state an initial normalization that intersects every flow interval with the nonnegative ray and rejects empty intersections. The former is the smaller local edit. Preserve adjacent external-node contracts as the manuscript already requires. This is a routine input-validation repair; it does not alter any parameter count, algebraic argument, or main theorem. If the intended input syntax already requires nonnegative flow-bound endpoints, make that restriction explicit and the example is outside the syntax.
