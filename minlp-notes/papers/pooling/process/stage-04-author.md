## Mathematical coverage and proof checks

- `s4:fixed-core` proves the entire rational-input supporting theorem, not just its pooling applications. Local bases are enumerated in fixed local dimension; validity is determined by determinant and residual signs, including lower-dimensional blocks, singular parameter values, singleton blocks, and empty blocks. Joint core/support sign conditions determine one maximizing vertex per nonempty block without enumerating the Cartesian product. Zero signs and disconnected sign realizations are expressly included.
- `s4:cleared-support` uses squared denominators and therefore works with either determinant sign. Its degree grows linearly with the number of blocks. The fixed number of variables, rather than a false constant-degree claim, gives polynomial dense expansion and coefficient size. `s4:universal-support` follows from support separation of the compact Minkowski sum and explicitly detects empty blocks. The fixed-variable formula defines all attainable objective values, and compactness gives attainment.
- `s4:optimal-sample` supplies a joint optimum/core sample in a common polynomial-degree real-algebraic field. The remaining bounded LP recovers all blocks over that field, using the precise common-extension-degree condition in Adler–Beling. Coordinate-box rows ensure full column rank. Zero-dimensional application blocks and an empty list of blocks can be padded by a scalar fixed to zero.
- The aggregate-inequality extension is fully developed beyond the canonical result's supplied-box alternative. For compact formula-defined C, coordinate quantifier elimination gives univariate descriptions; finite endpoints lie among roots of their nonzero polynomials. Cauchy bounds supply a polynomial-bit rational core box. Interval arithmetic then bounds each aggregate slack with polynomial encoding. The empty-core and zero-dimensional cases are explicit.
- `s4:core-limits` retains the fixed-core-binary qualification, the subset-sum obstruction for discrete local blocks, and the independent-radical exact-output obstruction for nonlinear convex blocks. None is recast as a numerical approximation hardness result, fixed-parameter tractability claim, or strongly polynomial runtime guarantee.
- `s4:fixed-inputs` gives the full source-fraction/product-block mapping, arbitrary qualities and bypasses, all node/arc throughput constraints, physical inlet costs, inactive pools, unusable-pool preprocessing, and both feasible-flow directions. `s4:fixed-qualities` gives the distinct input/product block mapping, full pool mass/quality aggregates, and the fixed-bypass extension. Both give exact optimization and recovery.
- `s4:vertex-integrity` proves the full affine-rank/deletion-set/component theorem. The proof assigns each arc exactly once, places all nondeleted-node constraints locally, tracks pool and deleted-input aggregate signs, and puts deleted-product total throughput and coordinate masses in the core. This last step is essential: arbitrary product specification vectors do not become a fixed number of aggregate rows merely by bounding the input-quality rank. The compact mass bounds, exact rank computation, inactive pools, objective identity, recognition algorithm, and dimension counts are explicit.
- `s4:structural-corollaries` retains the distinct fixed-quality vertex-cover precursor, sharper p+c singleton block dimension, unbounded hub bypasses, bounded components/unbounded matching, fixed distinct quality profiles, fixed-input recovery, no-bypass affine rank, no-pool LP, and rank-zero LP. Small bypass degree or treewidth is not substituted for vertex integrity.
- `s4:polygon-composition` gives the complete clamped convex/concave envelope identity, its piece-count proof, minimizer plateaus, singleton shared domains, vertical/other segments, points, and empty relations. The row bound uses a loose universal 64. Construction uses one exact Fourier–Motzkin elimination, then planar redundancy removal (each row tested over the system with that row removed) and explicit segment/point descriptions. Every coefficient uses a bounded number of operations on child coefficients. The proof does not invoke an unchecked general TVPI closure-size claim.
- `s4:path-projection` uses balanced composition for both row counts and coefficient lengths. A stored composition tree lifts shared coordinates by intersecting two closed intervals and taking a midpoint. After exact comparisons select active bounds, every recovered coordinate is a rational affine combination of parent endpoints, with divisions only by rational row coefficients. The polynomial-degree common field is preserved, including zero-length slices.
- `s4:bounded-attachments` proves the full multiple-pool extension with at most 4a retained variables and arbitrary many quality attributes. It preprocesses unusable pools without deleting neighboring external contracts, retains all flows at shared attachments, and treats every remaining path, isolated node, detached cycle, and attachment-to-attachment bypass. `s4:two-two-feasibility` retains the sharper thirteen-variable one-pool formulation. All fraction and product quality rows have degree at most two; zero throughput causes no division.
- `s4:fixed-support` retains designated actual arc coordinates and cuts paths/cycles there, including the repeated endpoint relation R(x,x) when a detached cycle is cut once. The theorem permits fixed-degree polynomial objectives and polynomially many closed fixed-degree constraints on the fixed retained core, uses compact attainment, and recovers all physical flows. `s4:equality-support` requires a supplied polynomial-bit exact rational equality reduction; `s4:price-changes` derives the revenue-change example and distinguishes the number of price changes from the number of distinct prices.
- `s4:degree-boundary` proves degree-two feasibility and strong degree-three feasibility NP-completeness in the precise two-feed/two-outlet class. The negative branch uses the accepted stage-3 physical network before contract completion: the original source threshold is already encoded by physical circuits. Exact nonzero contracts remain. Constant palettes, polynomial unary size, and fixed-pool/quality NP membership are justified by the accepted construction and certificate theorem.

## Source distinctions and developments

The general theorem is presented as uniform elimination over a polynomially varying core, using established support/Minkowski, real-algebraic, and algebraic-LP tools. No new general support-function or quantifier-elimination primitive, or established priority after a bounded search, is claimed.

The author inspected these primary source passages (local extracted primary text unless otherwise stated):

- Basu–Pollack–Roy, final article, Section 1.3 and Theorem 1.3.1 (printed pp. 1004–1005), plus the univariate sampling/sign construction. The fixed total variable count allows polynomially growing degree and coefficient bit complexity. Root additionally checked the original equation images independently.
- Adler–Beling, final article, Sections 4–5, especially Section 5 Remark 1, printed p. 455. The rational-machine extension is explicitly stated there, with a fuller exposition deferred by the authors. The manuscript cites this exact passage and keeps the common-field degree in the complexity statement. It does not infer polynomial time merely from individual algebraic coefficient encodings.
- Gritzmann–Sturmfels, final 1993 article, title/abstract and opening polytope conventions; Fukuda, January 11, 2003 author manuscript, introduction and Section 2 Proposition 2.1 (pp. 3–4). These establish the prior fixed-dimensional Minkowski computation and compatible common-support-face principle. The latter version is identified in its bibliography entry.
- Cslovjecsek et al., arXiv:2002.07745v2, introduction and Theorem 1. The block LP data are fixed linear polyhedra and linking matrices; the theorem does not optimize a nonlinear parameter changing every local matrix.
- Haugland's final 2016 article, Section 6, printed p. 214, states the bounded-pool question. The earlier MAGO 2014 two-pool claim is recorded in the source-audit notes as unproved and absent from the final paper; the manuscript uses the final paper and does not infer a formal retraction. The historical fixed-product branch must not remain labelled open in this manuscript: accepted `s3:two-pools` already supplies its negative answer for two pools/two products without bypasses. The source/result notes' older open-case language was corrected in the stage comparison.
- Boland–Kalinowski–Rigterink, author preprint dated August 13, 2015, Theorem 1 and Section 4 (p. 8); Haugland–Hendrix, final article, model and Theorem 4.1 plus the bypass discussion; Baltean-Lugojan–Misener, author manuscript, introduction and Assumption 2.2. The one-pool fixed-source predecessors, no-bypass convention, and single-quality/relaxed-feed-and-pool-capacity assumptions are distinguished from the new mappings. Existing bibliography entries for Haugland–Hendrix and Baltean-Lugojan–Misener were reused.
- Simon–King–Howe, revised manuscript dated October 27, 2010, actual title page and Section 3 (pp. 27–28); Hochbaum–Naor, final article, pp. 1179–1181 and Section 2. The local TVPI `paper.md` metadata omit Howe, whereas the actual inspected manuscript has three authors. An initial author message based on that metadata was withdrawn after direct title-page inspection. Root independently checked the primary Kent record and the published/revised-version discrepancy. The bibliography correctly identifies the three-author revised manuscript as such, without mixing in the different journal version's metadata. Pairwise resultant elimination and envelopes are prior tools; no disputed general normalized-closure size assertion is used as a premise.
