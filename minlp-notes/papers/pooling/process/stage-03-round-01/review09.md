The assigned focus checks are as follows.

- Lines 799–827, rational error bound: the Euclidean projection argument is valid for lower-dimensional polyhedra as well as full-dimensional ones. A nonnegative conic representation of the projection displacement can be reduced to independent active normals. For the resulting integral matrix B, det(BBᵀ) is a positive integer and its singular-value product is at least one. Together with the Frobenius bound NK₀, this gives the stated H after the two √N norm factors. Residuals must use the scaled representation, as the lemma explicitly requires. The separate treatment of a zero displacement avoids cancellation by zero.
- Lines 843–877, copy polytope and deficits: Q contains actual intakes and the linear bypass network, including the final collectors, but excludes the primary nonlinear constraints. The homogeneous source cone admits zero original signals even when the normalized source polytope is empty. Complementary ports and averages then fill the contracts, so the nonemptiness required by the error bound is justified. The retained upper halves of exact contracts give nonnegative deficits. Scaling satisfied A rows does not create positive residuals, while leaving the integral E rows unchanged makes the residual bound by δ valid. All positive lower flow bounds in this particular contracted construction are among its exact source supplies and auxiliary output demands; there is no omitted primary positive lower bound.
- Lines 879–892, primary feasible region: with T=Σxᵢ, S=Σaᵢxᵢ and all aᵢ>1, the condition F=S(T−1)−T≤0 is necessary and sufficient on x≥0, T≤2. At positive throughput q=S/T≥1, output 2 carries at most one, and output 1 carries at most 1/q; conversely the displayed construction uses y₂=1, y₁=T−1 and anchor (q−1)(T−1) when T>1. The condition q(T−1)≤1 bounds both total output-1 flow and anchor supply. T≤1 and T=0 are covered separately.
- Lines 894–914, repair: the derivative bound is valid throughout the convex intake simplex. If repair is needed, F(x′)>0 implies T′>1 and S′>1; both divisions are therefore safe. The proposed θ preserves S′/T′ and sets throughput to 1+T′/S′. The identity ‖x′−x″‖₁=F(x′)/S′ is exact. Rebuilding, rather than uniformly scaling all bypass flows, is essential because complementary ports have constants; the manuscript does rebuild them. Homogeneity of the original cone is sufficient to justify this reconstruction.
- Lines 916–942, penalty and encoding: the intake objective error is bounded by b_max(1+L)Hδ. With M₀=C+1, every positive deficit loses strictly more penalty reward than its repair can lose in base reward. This proves both inequalities in OPT_new=B₀+OPT_contract, including when the source polytope is empty. Clearing row denominators has polynomial bit cost, log H is O(log N+N log(NK₀)), and the subsequent products and threshold also have polynomial encoding length. No projection computation is needed to construct the reduction.
- Lines 944–958, production-cost realization: the private conversion positive port and the actual intake are equal only on Q. The proof correctly bounds the change of cᵀw during projection before using that identity, then bounds the radial change using the intake vector. Thus it does not assume copy identities in the relaxed network. Source and output throughput rewards are legal standard economic terms. Adding the same amount to every input cost and output revenue cancels by total external mass conservation. Global quality normalization does not change the flow set. The conclusion is correctly limited to ordinary hardness and exact value preservation.
- Lines 1131–1163 and 1173–1264, the other contract operations: completion profit removes lower bounds by exact saturation rather than by the preceding nonempty-Q penalty argument. Each term is individually capped, so deficits cannot cancel. The affine threshold circuit excludes zero original intake; the manuscript explicitly recognizes that its contracted network can be infeasible. The later feasibility theorem retains its positive contracts, groups complementary gate ports only after establishing copy identities independently of private supplies, and has the stated three variable inputs and two exceptional primary outputs. It does not inadvertently claim upper-bound-only feasibility hardness.
