# Literature review for *Exact Arithmetic in Polynomial Optimization*

**Reviewed 2026-10-05.** This report supports the manuscript specified in
`evidence/BRIEF.md`; the review is limited to the exact-arithmetic results and
their direct predecessors. It is a bounded source review, not a claim of
complete coverage or publication priority. The review distinguishes a source's
published version from an inspected preprint, exact decisions from approximate
outputs, and mathematical existence from an effective bit bound.

The source checks used the project literature KB, prior claim-driven primary
source audits under `research-20260927` and
`research-20261003-arithmetic/literature`, the theorem-interface sections of
the October 5 proof audits, five focused discovery rounds, and sequential
identified-source batches. Round artifacts, promotions, and retrieval failures
are preserved in the KB run. The only local manuscript
changes made by this review are this report and `literature.bib`; the manuscript
`references.bib` was not edited. No computational experiment was rerun.

## Theorem-by-theorem contribution and source map

“New in the manuscript” below means an additional proved statement relative to
the sources inspected here. It is not a priority claim. The exact input promise,
encoding, computation model, and output representation remain part of each
theorem; the table names only the points that determine its comparison with
prior work.

## Primary-source scope and version corrections

**Hesse's Redemption.** The ACM record confirms the STOC 2026 article (pp.410–419, DOI 10.1145/3798129.3800760), but its full text was inaccessible through the publisher endpoint and the repository copy timed out. The full version linked by the proceedings is arXiv:2511.03440; its history listed only v1 at the review date. All theorem statements here are therefore explicitly v1 claims: Theorem 1.1/Corollary 1.2 on objective-value approximation, Table 1's quartic/witness status, Appendix C Example C.1 (quartic irrational minimizer), Example C.2 (sextic with minimum zero at a unique irrational minimizer), and Lemma C.3 (for a univariate rational convex quartic, a rational minimum implies a rational minimizer). The manuscript's multivariate irrational quartic witness addresses a different degree/dimension case and does not contradict Lemma C.3. The proceedings table and theorem text remain unverified. `[[slot2025-hesse-s-redemption-efficient-convex]] p.3-7, p.30-31`; VOR metadata: `[[slot2026-hesse-s-redemption-efficient-convex]]` (no page locator available).

**Basu quantifier elimination.** The 2011 author manuscript, created May 23, 2011, has the block QE theorem as Theorem 2.16 on printed p.12. It gives output count/degree bounds and integer coefficient-bit bounds. The 2014 arXiv version has the same QE contract at Theorem 2.27 on p.16; its Theorem 2.16 is instead a sign-condition sample-points theorem. Cite the exact version whose numbering appears in a statement; the shared survey title does not remove the version distinction. `[[basu2011-algorithms-in-real-algebraic-geometry]] p.12`, `[[basu2014-algorithms-in-real-algebraic-geometry]] p.16`.

**Adaptive signs and Newton circuits.** Allender et al. and Bürgisser–Jindal establish an adaptive oracle/Turing bridge, not generic single-instance closure. ESY arXiv v2 contains the PPS-specific Corollary C.8; the conference paper lacks that appendix result, and the 2017 journal paper is recorded separately. The exact arithmetic-and-threshold compiler remains the manuscript's proof. The inspected closest compiler families have different predicates: approximate SU(2) synthesis (Dawson–Nielsen), compressed group identity for linear groups (König–Lohrey), and exact finite-group Boolean arithmetic over \\(\mathbb F_2\\) (Joye). These are prior for shared circuit reuse or commutators, but they do not state exact sign of a designated rational coordinate in a fixed compact quaternion group. `[[dawson2006-the-solovaykitaev-algorithm]] p.7-9`, `[[konig2015-evaluating-matrix-circuits]] p.9`, `[[joye2026-boolean-arithmetic-over-f2-from]] p.8-10`.

**Two source-formula cautions.** (i) Balister–Bollobás–Cutler–Pebody's general interlace identity is
\\[
q_H(-1)=(-1)^r 2^{m-r},\qquad r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}(I+A_H),
\\]
not \\((-1)^m\\) for every graph. The manuscript's application obtains (r=m) from oddness, so the needed specialization follows. (ii) Oertel–Wagner–Weismantel's 2014 accepted manuscript Observation 2 prints an incorrect equality for (P-P); the project audit replaces that step by vertex-difference width MILPs. Keep the repair attached if the article's oracle theorem is cited. The 2014 article text was not independently retrieved in this run. Its 2012 preprint is readable but states a different positive-tolerance interface; do not cite that preprint as the article's exact \\(\epsilon=0\\) theorem.

**SOS scope.** Hillar's field descent requires a finite totally real extension. The tower fields in the manuscript have nonreal embeddings, so that theorem neither proves nor refutes the least-field construction. Strict positivity of a polynomial is not the same as interiority in the SOS cone. Real chordal/block decompositions do not imply rational block certificates. The 2026 Gram-recovery theorem supplies explicit height bounds only with a positive eigenvalue margin as input. These distinctions resolve apparent conflicts between sources and the manuscript's format-specific lower bounds.

## Remaining limits on the novelty claim

The literature supports a conservative statement: the paper combines established real-algebraic separation, convex value optimization, Newton-circuit approximation, integer algorithms, violator-space sampling, and SOS techniques with additional proofs for exact output contracts and highly restricted strongly convex quartic classes. Broad architectures such as Newton approximation, arithmetic-circuit signs, semialgebraic elimination, block SOS existence, and fixed-dimensional integer optimization are imported. No absence claim is made. The highest-impact additional theorem is the certified quartic PosSLP-completeness result; the point-output, integer/projection, field, degree, and certificate-shape results supply independent output-format boundaries.

The following statements are not supported and should not appear in the manuscript:

- that exact convex-feasibility hardness, shared circuit reuse, Newton circuits, or commutator compilers are new in general;
- that \\(\mathrm P^{\mathrm{PosSLP}}\\) is unconditionally outside P, or that PosSLP-hardness gives unconditional NP-hardness;
- that exact equality is PosSLP-hard where only an upper bound is proved;
- that the STOC 2026 Hesse paper has exactly the arXiv v1 table or appendices;
- that a strong-convexity certificate gives a rational SOS, a short expanded optimizer, or a short feasible point at zero minimum;
- that the reported dimension-exponential lower bounds imply \\(2^{\Omega(L)}\\), NP nonmembership, or a lower bound on every circuit/root/implicit representation;
- that a real block decomposition theorem settles rational block-certificate existence or size;
- that Yang's degree exponent/effectivity conclusions have been checked from the primary paper.

The related-work section must identify precisely which manuscripts' statements have complete internal proofs and which are direct corollaries of the external contracts above. The exact five-variable degree-21 theorem should retain its finite complete-intersection hypothesis at the Cayley–Bacharach step and its separate positive-dimensional-base argument. The integer-dimensional algorithm should not cite a cut-oracle theorem as if it furnished rational whole-set cuts for an algebraic projected value. The forest QP theorem should display the admissible-vector verification, and the flow theorem should retain the source arithmetic model.
