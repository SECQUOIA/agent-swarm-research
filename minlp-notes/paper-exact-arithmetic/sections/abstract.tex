\begin{abstract}
A polynomial optimization problem can be asked for approximate values,
approximate optimizers, exact decisions about optimizers, or exact
descriptions of optimizers and optimality certificates. For convex quadratic
programs all four are available in polynomial time. We study higher degrees,
for explicit rational input in the bit model, under convexity hypotheses
stated for each result. For globally convex polynomials of unary degree,
polynomial-time value approximation is known; we add an effective error bound
and approximation of one fixed optimizer to precision $2^{-q}$ in time
polynomial in the input length $L$, the degree, and $q$. Convexity only on a
polytope suffices for cubics, whereas for quartics convex only on a box a
single constant-accuracy optimizer query can decide PosSLP, the sign problem
of integer circuits. For explicit globally strongly convex polynomials of
unary degree, every sign or equality test of a polynomial at the minimizer
reduces to one PosSLP instance. For quartics with a checkable strict
convexity certificate the order tests are PosSLP-complete, also for
coordinates of a rational minimizer with known minimum value; equality has
only the upper bound. For globally strongly convex quartics over rational
polyhedra, exact comparison lies in
$\mathrm{UP}^{\PosSLP}\cap\mathrm{coUP}^{\PosSLP}$, structured box and
network-flow problems reduce to one PosSLP instance per predicate, and small
nonlinear dimension admits ordinary fixed-parameter algorithms. The
coordinates of points isolated by rational convex polynomial inequalities are
exactly the real algebraic numbers with one real conjugate. For certified
strongly convex quartics of size polynomial in the dimension, the degree of
such coordinates, the bit length of rational optimizers and of interior Gram
matrices, and the degree of the least coefficient field of sum-of-squares
certificates can grow exponentially with the dimension, although each
exhibited family has a polynomial-size description in another format, such as
a shared rational circuit or a root circuit. Further results bound
common-field algebraic witnesses for nonconvex quadratic constraints with few
independent Hessians. For second-order cone systems over one explicitly
represented real number field, exact feasibility and witness recovery take
polynomial time when both the integer dimension and the dimension of the
span of the continuous Hessians of the squared cone residuals are fixed.
For cubics on product boxes that are convex in the residual
variables but possibly nonconvex in a core of dimension $k$, one random tilt
of the core yields the full selected optimizer of the tilted instance at
every precision, correct on every draw, with
expected work $k^{O(k)}(1+\Lambda/\sigma)^k\poly(L+q)$, where $\sigma$ is the
tilt width and $\Lambda$ is an upper bound on the coordinate second
derivatives in the core.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} polynomial optimization; convex optimization;
exact arithmetic; PosSLP; sums of squares; algebraic degree; bit complexity;
mixed-integer nonlinear programming.

\end{abstract}
