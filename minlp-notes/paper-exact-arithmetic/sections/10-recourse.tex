\section{Core noise: values, selected cores, and full recourse points}
\label{sec:recourse}

Many structured problems have a few complicating \emph{core} variables
$v$ and many \emph{residual} variables $z$, and the residual problem is
convex once $v$ is fixed. Two-stage models, where $z$ is the recourse
of a first-stage decision $v$, and decomposition methods that branch
only on $v$ have this form. At a rational core point the residual
problem is a convex program, so its value can be computed to any
certified accuracy in polynomial time (Lemma~\ref{lem:convex-value}).
The projected value $V(v)=\min_zf(v,z)$ is nevertheless nonconvex, and
the earlier sections show that approximating values, approximating
optimizer coordinates, and deciding exact relations are different
problems.

This section studies these outputs after one random linear
perturbation of the core coefficients. The topic belongs to the output
account of this paper for three reasons. First, the perturbation is
drawn once from a finite rational law that is fixed before any accuracy
is requested. The perturbed instance is therefore an ordinary rational
instance, and the contracts of Section~\ref{sec:models} apply to it
without change. Second, every draw is answered correctly. Probability
only bounds the expected cost; rare draws are solved by exact
real-algebraic computation on the same instance and with the same
selector. Third, the outputs separate sharply. On product boxes, values
and one selected core are available under residual convexity alone; on
coupled polytopes they need a supplied convexifier. Full optimizer
points need more structure: a supplied convexifier together with a
cubic objective or a convexified objective that is convex on all of
$\R^n$, or, for cubics on product boxes, exact certificates that we
construct. For residual quartics, core noise alone
does not help unless Square Root Sum and $\PosSLP$ have Las Vegas
algorithms with expected polynomial running time
(Remark~\ref{rem:recourse-quartic}).

The main results are Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-core} (values and
selected cores), Corollaries~\ref{cor:recourse-joint}
and~\ref{cor:recourse-qp} (joint convexity and exact quadratic output),
Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-convexified} (full points under a supplied
convexifier), Proposition~\ref{prop:recourse-fiber} together with
Proposition~\ref{prop:recourse-examples} (the structure of cubic
residual fibers and two obstructions), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-cubic} (full points for residual-convex cubics
on product boxes, with no convexifier). All proofs are in
Appendix~\ref{app:recourse}.

\subsection{Model, noise law, and selector}
\label{sec:recourse-model}

\begin{definition}[Core-noise instances]
\label{def:recourse-instance}
Fix a degree bound $D\ge1$. A \emph{core-noise instance} consists of
integers $k\ge1$ and $m\ge0$, a nonempty compact set
$X\subseteq[0,1]^k\times\R^m$, an explicitly encoded polynomial
$f\in\Q[v,z]$ of total degree at most $D$ in the core variables
$v\in\R^k$ and residual variables $z\in\R^m$, a rational curvature
parameter $\kappa\ge0$, and a rational noise width $\sigma>0$. The
instance has one of two types.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item[(B)] \emph{Product box.} $X=[0,1]^k\times[0,1]^m$; the function
$z\mapsto f(v,z)$ is convex on $[0,1]^m$ for every $v\in[0,1]^k$; and
$\kappa=\Lambda$ satisfies $\partial^2f/\partial v_i^2\le\Lambda$ on
$X$ for $i=1,\ldots,k$.
\item[(P)] \emph{Coupled polytope.} $X=\{x:Ex\le e\}$ is a bounded
rational polytope contained in $[0,1]^k\times\prod_{j=1}^m[l_j,u_j]$
for a supplied rational box, and $\kappa=\alpha$ makes
$f(v,z)+\frac\alpha2\norm v^2$ convex on $X$.
\end{enumerate}
We write $n=k+m$ and let $L$ be the total binary length of all these
data, including any supplied certificate for the convexity and
curvature promises.
\end{definition}

Core coordinates with other rational ranges can be mapped affinely onto
$[0,1]^k$. This rescales $\Lambda$ or $\alpha$ and changes the physical
noise directions, and the rescaled data are part of the input; no
invariance under such rescaling is claimed. Fixed coordinates are
substituted first, and empty or single-point feasible sets are detected
by linear programming.

Convexity and curvature are promises. The algorithms never test them;
when a certificate is supplied, the stated bounds assume that it can be
verified in time polynomial in $L$, and otherwise its verification cost
must be added. In type~(B) a valid $\Lambda$ can always be computed from
coefficient sums, but the numerical factor in our bounds depends on the
supplied value, which may be much smaller. Type~(P) asks for more than
convex residual fibers: it supplies a convex problem on every cell of
the core. For example, $f(v,z)=vz^2$ on the unit square has convex
fibers, but $f+\frac\alpha2v^2$ is convex for no $\alpha$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:recourse-examples}).

For a power of two $M\ge2$ put
\[
 G_M=\Bigl\{-\sigma+\frac{2\sigma j}{M-1}:\ j=0,1,\ldots,M-1\Bigr\},
\]
the endpoint-inclusive uniform grid in $[-\sigma,\sigma]$. A
\emph{draw} is a vector $\gamma$ with independent coordinates, each
uniform on $G_M$, and the perturbed objective is
\[
 f_\gamma(v,z)=f(v,z)+\gamma^{\mathsf T}v,\qquad
 f_\gamma^*=\min_Xf_\gamma .
\]
Only core coefficients are perturbed. The \emph{selected core}
$a=a(\gamma)$ is the lexicographically least core among the global
minimizers of $f_\gamma$ on $X$. The \emph{optimal fiber}
\[
 S(\gamma)=\{z:(a,z)\in X,\ f_\gamma(a,z)=f_\gamma^*\}
\]
is nonempty, compact, and convex in both types
(Lemma~\ref{lem:recourse-selected-point}). Let $p=p(\gamma)$ be its
point of least Euclidean norm. The \emph{selected point} is
$x^\sharp(\gamma)=(a,p)$. It is a global minimizer of $f_\gamma$, and
it does not depend on any requested accuracy.

An \emph{evaluator} is a deterministic algorithm that receives the
instance, the draw $\gamma$, and an integer $q\ge0$, and returns
rational numbers. We count its bit operations and the bit length of its
output. A quantity is \emph{base-computable} if it is computed from the
instance before sampling, in time polynomial in $L$; in particular the
grid size $M$ below has $\log M$ polynomial in $L$, so each sampled
coefficient has polynomial bit length. The notation $P_D$ stands for a
polynomial whose degree depends only on $D$; it never depends on $k$,
$m$, $\sigma$, the draw, or $q$.

The guarantees below have the same form. Correctness holds on every
draw and at every precision. The work bound is a single random factor
$\Omega(\gamma)$ times a polynomial in $L+q$: one factor controls all
precision queries at once, so there is no union bound over $q$ and no
resampling when the accuracy grows. The outputs are Cauchy names for
fixed numbers of the sampled instance: a value interval for
$f_\gamma^*$, and approximations to $a(\gamma)$ or $x^\sharp(\gamma)$.
They are not expanded algebraic representations, and they do not solve
the unperturbed instance.

\subsection{Values and selected cores}
\label{sec:recourse-core}

\begin{theorem}[Values and selected cores under one finite law]
\label{thm:recourse-core}
Fix $D$. For core-noise instances of type~(B) or~(P) there are a
base-computable power of two $M$ and an evaluator with the following
properties. For every draw $\gamma\in G_M^k$ and every integer $q\ge0$,
the evaluator returns a rational point $x_q=(v_q,z_q)\in X$ and a
rational number $\ell_q$, both of bit length at most $P_D(L+q)$, with
\begin{equation}
 \ell_q\le f_\gamma^*\le f_\gamma(x_q)\le\ell_q+2^{-q},
 \qquad
 \norm{v_q-a(\gamma)}\le2^{-q}.
 \label{eq:recourse-core-output}
\end{equation}
There is a function $\Omega:G_M^k\to[1,\infty)$ such that, for every
draw and every $q$, the bit work of the query is at most
$\Omega(\gamma)P_D(L+q)$, and
\[
 \E\,\Omega\le k^{O(k)}\Bigl(1+\frac\kappa\sigma\Bigr)^kP_D(L),
\]
with an absolute constant in $O(k)$.
\end{theorem}

The value part of \eqref{eq:recourse-core-output} is an exact Cauchy
name for $f_\gamma^*$ together with feasible points of certified
objective gap; the core part is a Cauchy name for the single vector
$a(\gamma)$. The residual coordinates $z_q$ need not approach any
particular residual optimizer. Ties among optimal cores, flat residual
fibers, and positive-dimensional optimal sets are allowed; no growth
constant, residual strong convexity, or interiority is supplied.

\paragraph{How the proof works.}
The evaluator refines dyadic cells of the core cube. On each cell it
computes a lower bound and a feasible witness by convex optimization:
in type~(B) at the cell corners, using the coordinate bound $\Lambda$
to control rounding, and in type~(P) by the secant underestimator
$f_\gamma+\frac{\alpha+\sigma}2\sum_i(v_i-l_i)(v_i-u_i)$ on the cell.
The latter is the classical $\alpha$BB construction
\citep{AndroulakisMaranasFloudas1995}. Pruning against the best
witness gives a certified value interval on every draw, and the
coordinate hull of the surviving cells contains every optimal core and
the incumbent. Small hull diameter therefore certifies the core output
without deciding uniqueness.

Three probabilistic estimates control the cost. (i) An
\emph{all-scale count}: the number of cells at every level of every
query is at most $4^kW(\gamma)$, where $W$ is defined from the
largest simplices in padded near-optimal sets. A convex-conjugate
pushforward measure and a covering argument give
$\Pr\{W>s\}\le a_k/s+C/M$ with $a_k=k^{O(k)}(1+\kappa/\sigma)^k$, in
every core dimension. (ii) A \emph{projected-growth tail}: the core
quadratic growth constant $g$ of the sampled objective satisfies
$\Pr\{g<t\}\le kt/\sigma+C/M$; when $g$ is not small the hull shrinks
at the expected rate. (iii) A \emph{finite-law transfer}: each event in
(i) and (ii) is defined by a formula with two quantified blocks, so a
block quantifier-elimination theorem bounds the number of intervals in
every one-dimensional section independently of coefficient heights.
Replacing continuous marginals by grid marginals one at a time costs
$C/M$, including atoms at ties and exact algebraic relations. Here $C$ is
the base-computable format bound from Appendix~\ref{app:recourse}, of size
$2^{\poly(L)}$; it is independent of the requested precision and sampled
coefficient heights.

When a cell count exceeds a base cap or the hull test fails, the
evaluator solves the same sampled instance exactly. Two-block singleton
formulas define the coordinates of the selected point; block
quantifier elimination and univariate real-root isolation give their
values. The exponential factor of this fallback depends only on the
format of the instance, and the sampled coefficient length and $q$
enter polynomially. This separation is what allows the grid to be
chosen after the fallback budget, with $\log M$ polynomial in $L$.

A simpler count based on growth alone gives at most
$(c(1+\sqrt{k\kappa/g}))^k$ cells per level, for an absolute constant
$c$. Its truncated moment under the growth
tail is bounded only for $k\le2$; for $k\ge3$ it grows as a positive
power of the cap, and that route fails. The all-scale weak first-moment
bound in (i) removes the restriction on $k$. It is an instance of the
weak-type estimate for a centered maximal function of a locally finite
measure; the conjugate construction makes that measure available.

\begin{corollary}[Joint convexity: no numerical smoothing factor]
\label{cor:recourse-joint}
Fix $D$. For instances of type~(P) with $\alpha=0$, that is, with $f$
convex on $X$, there are a base-computable power of two $M$ and an
evaluator with the outputs \eqref{eq:recourse-core-output} on every
draw and at every precision, and a function $\Omega$ with
$\E\,\Omega\le2$ such that every query costs at most
$\Omega(\gamma)P_D(L+q)$ bit operations.
\end{corollary}

The bound has no factor $k^{O(k)}$ and no numerical dependence on
$\sigma$; the noise width enters only through its bit length. The
proof uses no cells. It encloses every optimal core and the incumbent's
core by $2k$ binary searches whose probes are convex value queries on
$X\cap\{v_i\ge\theta\}$ and $X\cap\{v_i\le\theta\}$
(Appendix~\ref{app:recourse-joint}). Taking every variable as a core
variable ($m=0$) yields the lexicographically least optimizer of a
convex polynomial on a polytope inside the unit cube, in expected
polynomial time, under independent noise on every coordinate. This is
consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:points-quartic-lower}: there the hard
coordinate is not perturbed, and full-coordinate noise changes the
instance.

\begin{corollary}[Exact rational output for quadratic objectives]
\label{cor:recourse-qp}
Let $D=2$. For instances of type~(B) or~(P), there are a
base-computable $M$ and an algorithm that, on every draw, returns an
exact rational global minimizer of $f_\gamma$ on $X$ with core
$a(\gamma)$, together with the exact value $f_\gamma^*$. The output has
bit length polynomial in $L$ on every draw, and the expected bit work
is $k^{O(k)}(1+\kappa/\sigma)^k\poly(L)$; for type~(P) with $\alpha=0$
it is $\poly(L)$.
\end{corollary}

The proof shows that the core of the lexicographically first optimizer
of a rational quadratic program has denominators bounded by a
base-computable $U_Q$ with $\log U_Q=\poly(L)$, uniformly over all draws.
One query of Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-core} at accuracy
$1/(4U_Q^2)$ followed by continued-fraction reconstruction recovers that
core exactly, and one exact convex quadratic program
\citep{KozlovTarasovKhachiyan1980} completes it. The height argument is
specific to quadratics; it does not give exact output for cubic or
quartic objectives.

\subsection{Full points under a supplied convexifier}
\label{sec:recourse-convexified}

Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-core} says nothing about residual coordinates.
Substituting an approximate core into an exact residual solver does not
repair this: the residual optimizer set can jump at the selected core
(Proposition~\ref{prop:recourse-examples}). With a supplied convexifier
the jump is avoided by optimizing a convex surrogate on the fixed
domain instead.

\begin{theorem}[Full points with a supplied convexifier]
\label{thm:recourse-convexified}
Consider instances of type~(P) in which either
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $D=3$ and $f+\frac\alpha2\norm v^2$ is convex on $X$; or
\item $D$ is fixed and $f+\frac\alpha2\norm v^2$ is convex on all of
$\R^n$.
\end{enumerate}
There are a base-computable power of two $M$ and an evaluator that, for
every draw $\gamma$ and every $q\ge0$, returns a rational
$x_q\in X$ with $\norm{x_q-x^\sharp(\gamma)}\le2^{-q}$. Every query
costs at most $\Omega(\gamma)P_D(L+q)$ bit operations, where
$\E\,\Omega\le k^{O(k)}(1+\alpha/\sigma)^kP_D(L)$. If $\alpha=0$, then
$\E\,\Omega\le2$.
\end{theorem}

With $\beta=\alpha+1$ the surrogate is
$\psi_a(x)=f_\gamma(x)+\frac\beta2\norm{v-a}^2$. It is convex, its
minimum value is $f_\gamma^*$, and its optimal set is exactly the
selected fiber $\{a\}\times S(\gamma)$. Its linear coefficients involve
the unknown, possibly irrational core $a$. The key estimate is an error
bound $\dist(x,\{a\}\times S(\gamma))\le\Gamma(\psi_a(x)-f_\gamma^*)^{1/D_e}$
whose constant $\Gamma$ has polynomial bit length and is uniform in
$a$; here $D_e=4$ in case~(i) and $D_e=D$ in case~(ii). It is obtained
by describing the optimal set through a known rational matrix whose
right-hand side alone depends on $a$, and applying a Hoffman bound. In
case~(i) the matrix comes from the affine Hessian of a cubic, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-point}; in case~(ii) from gradient coefficient
rows, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:global-point}. The completion then minimizes
the rational convex function $\psi_b+\tau\norm x^2$ for a short rational
approximation $b$ of $a$. The search for $a$ uses $\alpha$; the larger
$\beta$ appears only in the polynomial-time completion and does not
change the numerical factor.

\begin{corollary}[Affine-power certificates]
\label{cor:recourse-affine-power}
Condition~(ii) holds, and can be verified in time polynomial in $L$
for fixed $D$, when
$f+\frac\alpha2\norm v^2$ is supplied in the form
$c_0+\ell^{\mathsf T}x+\frac12x^{\mathsf T}Ax
 +\sum_jw_j(a_j^{\mathsf T}x-b_j)^{p_j}$
with rational data, $A\succeq0$, $w_j>0$, and even $p_j\le D$.
\end{corollary}

This explicit class contains quartic and higher even-power terms with
dense rational affine forms and arbitrary rational coupling
constraints. It is a sufficient certificate, not a recognition
algorithm for global convexity.

\subsection{Residual-convex cubics without a convexifier}
\label{sec:recourse-cubic}

We now drop the convexifier and keep only type~(B) with total degree
three. Write $H(v,z)=\nabla^2_{zz}f(v,z)$, which is affine in $(v,z)$
and positive semidefinite on the product box. For a face $A$ of the
core cube $[0,1]^k$ let $F\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}$ be its set of free
coordinates, $c_A$ its relative center, and $c_z=(\frac12,\ldots,\frac12)$
the residual center. Put
\[
 H_A=H(c_A,c_z),\qquad g_A(v)=\nabla_zf(v,c_z),\qquad
 J_A(v)=\begin{bmatrix}H_A\\ g_A(v)^{\mathsf T}\\ I_m\end{bmatrix}.
\]
For $v\in[0,1]^k$ let $S_v=\argmin_{z\in[0,1]^m}f(v,z)$.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed face kernels and a conditional error bound]
\label{prop:recourse-fiber}
Let $f$ be a cubic of type~(B) and let $A$ be a face of $[0,1]^k$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item $0\preceq H(v,z)\preceq2H_A$ on $A\times[0,1]^m$. If the free
coordinates of $v\in A$ lie in $[\delta,1-\delta]$ with
$0<\delta\le\frac12$, then $H(v,c_z)\succeq2\delta H_A$. In particular
$\ker H(v,c_z)=\ker H_A$ for every $v$ in the relative interior of $A$.
\item For $v$ in the relative interior of $A$ and any $y\in S_v$,
\[
 S_v=\{z\in[0,1]^m:\ H_A(z-y)=0,\ g_A(v)^{\mathsf T}(z-y)=0\}.
\]
Every square minor of $J_A(v)$, restricted to $A$, is a polynomial of
degree at most two in the free coordinates.
\item Let $\delta\in(0,\frac12]$ and $\mu\in(0,1]$ be supplied rational
margins. Suppose the free coordinates of $v\in A$ lie in
$[\delta,1-\delta]$ and every nonzero square minor of $J_A(v)$ has
absolute value at least $\mu$. Then
\[
 \dist(z,S_v)\le\Gamma\bigl(f(v,z)-\min f(v,\cdot)\bigr)^{1/4}
 \quad\text{for } z\in[0,1]^m \text{ with gap at most }1,
\]
for a rational $\Gamma\ge1$ that is computed from $f$, $A$, $\delta$,
and $\mu$ in time polynomial in $L$ and the bit lengths of $\delta$
and $\mu$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The kernel is rational and constant on the relative interior of each
face, although it can grow on the boundary. Only one row of the
equality description of $S_v$ varies with the core. The family of
square minors of $J_A$ is exponentially large, but each member is a
quadratic in the free core coordinates. Part~(c) is conditional: the
margins $\delta$ and $\mu$ at an unknown, possibly irrational core
cannot be read from rational input heights, and computing a Hoffman
constant at a nearby rational core is unsound, since a minor can vanish
there while it is tiny but nonzero at the true core.

\begin{proposition}[Two obstructions]
\label{prop:recourse-examples}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item The cubic $f(v,z)=vz^2$ on $[0,1]^2$ has convex residual fibers,
but no constants $\Gamma,\theta>0$ satisfy
$\dist(z,S_v)\le\Gamma(f(v,z)-\min f(v,\cdot))^\theta$ for all
$v\in(0,1]$ and $z\in[0,1]$. For no $\alpha\ge0$ is
$f+\frac\alpha2v^2$ convex on the square.
\item For $\gamma>1$, the cubic $f_\gamma(v,z)=\gamma v-v^2z$ on
$[0,1]^2$ has affine residual fibers, optimal set $\{0\}\times[0,1]$,
and selected point $(0,0)$. For every $v>0$ the residual problem has
the unique minimizer $z=1$, so exact residual solutions at approximate
cores $b_j\downarrow0$ converge to $(0,1)$. For no $\alpha\ge0$ is
$f_\gamma+\frac\alpha2v^2$ convex on the square. Under uniform core
noise of width $\sigma>1$ added to $f(v,z)=-v^2z$, such draws have
positive probability.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Example~(b) defeats minimum-norm completion by approximate-core
substitution. The completion below avoids it by first certifying the
exact face of the selected core, here the vertex $v=0$, and then
regularizing the residual problem at that exact face.

\begin{theorem}[Full selected optimizer for residual-convex cubic recourse]
\label{thm:recourse-cubic}
Let $(X,f,\Lambda,\sigma)$ be a core-noise instance of type~(B) with
$D=3$ and $k,m\ge1$. There are a base-computable power of two $M$ and
an evaluator such that, for every draw $\gamma\in G_M^k$ and every
integer $q\ge0$, the evaluator returns a rational point
$(v_q,z_q)\in[0,1]^{k+m}$ with
\[
 \norm{(v_q,z_q)-x^\sharp(\gamma)}\le2^{-q}.
\]
There is a function $\Omega$ such that every query costs at most
$\Omega(\gamma)\poly(L+q)$ bit operations and output bits, and
\[
 \E\,\Omega\le k^{O(k)}\Bigl(1+\frac\Lambda\sigma\Bigr)^k\poly(L).
\]
The polynomials have absolute exponents. No joint convexifier, lower
bound on residual curvature, or uniqueness of residual optimizers is
assumed.
\end{theorem}

For $k=0$ the selected point is the minimum-norm minimizer of a cubic
convex on a box, and Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-point} gives it
deterministically. For $m=0$ Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-core} suffices.
The examples of Proposition~\ref{prop:recourse-examples} are covered.
The evaluator can also return a certified interval of width $2^{-q}$
for $f_\gamma^*$ at the same order of cost
(Remark~\ref{rem:recourse-value-certificate}).

The proof composes three certificates with
Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-core}, applied on the same draw to $f$ and to
the $2k$ baselines $f\pm tv_i$. Each certificate is sound on every draw;
probability only bounds how often one of them fails.

\begin{lemma}[Exact core endpoints from nearby tilts]
\label{lem:recourse-face}
Let $f$ be of type~(B), $\bar\Lambda=\max\{1,\Lambda\}$,
$0<t\le\frac12$, $\gamma\in\R^k$, and $1\le i\le k$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item If some global minimizer of $f_{\gamma-te_i}$ has core $b$ with
$b_i<t/\bar\Lambda$, then every global minimizer of $f_\gamma$ has
$v_i=0$. If some global minimizer of $f_{\gamma+te_i}$ has core $b$ with
$b_i>1-t/\bar\Lambda$, then every global minimizer of $f_\gamma$ has
$v_i=1$.
\item There are numbers $\theta_i^0,\theta_i^1$ depending only on
$\gamma_{-i}$ such that the following holds. If some global minimizer
of $f_\gamma$ has $v_i=0$ while some global minimizer of
$f_{\gamma-te_i}$ has core $b$ with $b_i>0$, then
$\gamma_i\in[\theta_i^0,\theta_i^0+t]$. If some global minimizer of
$f_\gamma$ has $v_i=1$ while some global minimizer of $f_{\gamma+te_i}$
has $b_i<1$, then $\gamma_i\in[\theta_i^1-t,\theta_i^1]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Part~(a) uses the one-dimensional projected value
$V_i(s)=\min\{f_\gamma(v,z):v_i=s\}-\gamma_is$, which becomes concave
after subtracting $\bar\Lambda s^2/2$. Optimal coordinates move
monotonically with the tilt, and two interior contacts for tilts that
differ by $t$ must be at distance at least $t/\bar\Lambda$. Applied with
certified approximations of the selected cores of $f_{\gamma\pm te_i}$
to accuracy $t/(8\bar\Lambda)$, the rules in~(a) fix a face $A$
containing $a(\gamma)$. By part~(b), the probability under the grid law
that the selected core has an endpoint coordinate that the rules miss
is at most $kt/\sigma+2k/M$.

\begin{lemma}[A lattice certificate for all bounded-height quadratic margins]
\label{lem:recourse-lattice}
Let $a\in[0,1]^r$, let $\chi(a)\in[0,1]^s$, $s=\binom{r+2}2$, list
the monomials of degree at most two in $a$, including $1$, and let
$B,T\ge1$ be integers. Given a rational $\hat a\in[0,1]^r$ with
$\norm{\hat a-a}_\infty\le1/(4T)$, put
$\omega_j=\operatorname{round}(T\chi_j(\hat a))$, and let
$\mathbf b_1$ be the first vector of an LLL-reduced basis, with
parameter $3/4$ \citep{LenstraLenstraLovasz1982}, of the lattice in
$\Z^{s+1}$ generated by the rows of $[I_s\mid\omega]$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item If $\norm{\mathbf b_1}^2>2^{s-1}(4sB)^2$, then
$\abs{h^{\mathsf T}\chi(a)}>sB/T$ for every $h\in\Z^s$ with
$0<\norm h_\infty\le B$.
\item Let $R_s=2^s4sB$. If $\abs{h^{\mathsf T}\chi(a)}\ge\eta_0$ for
every $h\in\Z^s$ with $0<\norm h_\infty\le R_s$, and
$T\eta_0>(s+1)R_s$, then the test in~(a) accepts.
\end{enumerate}
The test costs time polynomial in
$s+\log T+\log B+\langle\hat a\rangle$.
\end{lemma}

Apply the lattice lemma to $a_F(\gamma)$, where $F$ is the set of core
coordinates left provisionally free by the endpoint rules defining $A$,
after substituting the certified endpoint values into the minors. The tested
family contains $v_i$ and $1-v_i$ for $i\in F$, so acceptance proves that
these coordinates are truly interior. After clearing a
common denominator $\Delta$, every square minor of $J_A$ on every face
becomes an integer quadratic of height at most a base-computable $B$.
Acceptance therefore certifies $\delta=\min\{\frac12,sB/T\}$ and
$\mu=\min\{1,sB/(\Delta T)\}$ in
Proposition~\ref{prop:recourse-fiber}(c) for all exponentially many
minors at once, with no enumeration. Part~(b) needs the larger height
$R_s$, because a short lattice vector can have coefficients larger
than $B$.

\begin{proposition}[Small margins are rare]
\label{prop:recourse-margin-probability}
Let $f$ be a cubic of type~(B) and let $U\ge1$ be rational with
$\nabla^2_{vv}f\preceq UI_k$ on $X$. Let $A$ be a face of $[0,1]^k$
with $r\ge1$ free coordinates, let $\psi$ be a nonzero integer
polynomial of degree at most two in those coordinates, and let
$0<\eta<1$. Under continuous uniform noise on $[-\sigma,\sigma]^k$, the
probability that some global minimizer of $f_\gamma$ has core $a$ in
the relative interior of $A$ with $\abs{\psi(a_F)}\le\eta$ is at most
$8(U/(2\sigma))^r\sqrt\eta$. Under the grid law it is at most this
bound plus $C/M$, where $C=2^{\poly(L)}$ is base-computable and does not
depend on $\psi$ or $\eta$.
\end{proposition}

The proof does not use any continuity of residual optimizers. On a
face, the projected value is semiconcave; at a minimizer under a linear
tilt it has an affine lower support, and the slope of that support is a
locally $U$-Lipschitz function of the contact point. Hence the noise
must fall in the image of a small set under a Lipschitz map. An
elementary slicing argument bounds the volume of
$\{\abs{\psi}\le\eta\}$ by $8\sqrt\eta$ for every nonzero integer
quadratic. The constant $U$ can be much larger than $\Lambda$; it enters
only through bit lengths.

\begin{proposition}[Certified completion]
\label{prop:recourse-completion}
Let $f$ be a cubic of type~(B), and suppose that evaluators with the
guarantees of Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-core} are available for the
selected cores of $f_\gamma$ and $f_{\gamma\pm te_i}$, $1\le i\le k$, on
a fixed draw. For base-computable parameters $t$ and $T$, a base stage
makes $2k+1$ core queries at accuracy $\poly(L)$ bits, applies the rules
of Lemma~\ref{lem:recourse-face}, and runs the test of
Lemma~\ref{lem:recourse-lattice} once; it either rejects or accepts.
Acceptance certifies a face $A$ whose relative interior contains
$a(\gamma)$ and a rational $\Gamma$ of polynomial bit length with
$\dist(z,S(\gamma))\le\Gamma(f(a,z)-\min f(a,\cdot))^{1/4}$ for gaps at
most one. After acceptance, a query $q$ makes one core query at
accuracy $\poly(L)+O(q)$ bits and one convex minimization of the
rational cubic $z\mapsto f(b,z)+\tau\norm z^2$, and returns a point
within $2^{-q}$ of $x^\sharp(\gamma)$. Here $b$ is the rational core
approximation lying in $A$, with the certified endpoint coordinates imposed
exactly, and $\tau>0$ is the regularization parameter specified below.
Every acceptance is correct, whatever the draw.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-cubic} in outline.}
Choose the fallback budget $\Xi=2^{\poly(L)}$ first, then
$t=\min\{\frac12,\sigma/(16k\Xi)\}$, a margin threshold $\eta$ with
$A_*\sqrt\eta\le1/(16\Xi)$ for the union-bound constant $A_*$ over all
faces and all quadratics of height at most $R_{s_*}$,
$s_*=\binom{k+2}2$, and a lattice scale $T$ with
$T\eta>(s_*+1)R_{s_*}$. Finally choose one grid size $M$ that meets the
requirements of Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-core} for all $2k+1$
baselines and makes the finite-law error terms at most $1/(8\Xi)$. All
choices precede sampling and have polynomial bit length. If no endpoint
is missed and no tested quadratic has margin at most $\eta$, the lattice
test accepts by Lemma~\ref{lem:recourse-lattice}(b), so rejection has
probability at most $1/(4\Xi)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:recourse-face}(b) and
Proposition~\ref{prop:recourse-margin-probability}. On rejection the
evaluator computes $x^\sharp(\gamma)$ exactly from two-block singleton
formulas that encode the same selector: global optimality,
lexicographically least core, and least residual norm within that core.
The random factor is the sum of the $2k+1$ factors of
Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-core} plus $\Xi$ times the indicator of
rejection. The completion step is proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:recourse-cubic-proof}: regularization with
$\tau=e^6/(1024R_z^4\Gamma^4)$, where $e=2^{-q-1}$ and
$R_z\ge\max\{1,\sqrt m\}$, controls the distance to the least-norm point of $S(\gamma)$, and a
uniform objective perturbation bound controls the error from the
approximate core.

\subsection{Scope and relation to other work}
\label{sec:recourse-scope}

\begin{remark}[Degree four]
\label{rem:recourse-quartic}
Let $F$ be a quartic from Theorem~\ref{thm:points-quartic-lower}, convex
on a unit box, with a unique minimizer whose designated coordinate is
$0$ or $1$ according to an instance of Square Root Sum or $\PosSLP$.
Put $f(v,y)=v^2/2+F(y)$ with one core coordinate $v\in[0,1]$. Its
residual fibers are convex and $\Lambda=1$. For every draw the residual
part of the selected point is the minimizer of $F$. If the conclusion
of Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-cubic} held for this degree-four family,
its evaluator would decide the source problem by a Las Vegas algorithm
in expected polynomial time. Core noise cannot by itself make full points
of residual-convex quartics tractable; the cubic structure of
Proposition~\ref{prop:recourse-fiber} is essential. This is a
conditional statement, not a hardness theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Certified values for the sampled objective]
\label{rem:recourse-value-certificate}
To add a value interval to Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-cubic} or
Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-convexified}, query the point at accuracy
$2^{-q-1}/G$, where $G\ge1$ bounds $\norm{\nabla f_\gamma}$ on $X$
uniformly over the noise support, and pair its objective value with the
lower bound $\ell_{q+1}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-core}. This
certifies the sampled objective only; the unperturbed objective is not
solved at the same cost.
\end{remark}

The results have clear limits. All guarantees concern one sampled
rational instance. Theorem~\ref{thm:recourse-cubic} uses the product box
in the nearby-tilt argument, in the fixed face kernels, and in the
conditional error bound; it does not extend to arbitrary coupled
domains without a convexifier, and Remark~\ref{rem:recourse-quartic}
explains why degree four needs more than core noise. On accepted draws the
algorithm internally certifies the exact core face and the margins needed
for completion. Its output contract gives point and value approximations;
it does not include all active-constraint labels or expanded algebraic
coordinates, in line with Proposition~\ref{prop:points-active}.
The factor $k^{O(k)}$ is not
optimized, and the dependence on $\kappa/\sigma$ is numerical, not
through its bit length, except in Corollary~\ref{cor:recourse-joint}.

The individual tools are classical: branch-and-bound on core cells with
secant underestimators \citep{AndroulakisMaranasFloudas1995}; weak
convex optimization \citep{GroetschelLovaszSchrijver1988}; the
almost-everywhere differentiability of convex conjugates, which
underlies the growth tail \citep{Rockafellar1970}; covering arguments
for maximal functions \citep{EvansGariepy2015}; block quantifier
elimination \citep{Renegar1992QE,BasuPollackRoy2006}; Hoffman error
bounds \citep{Hoffman1952}; and lattice reduction for small integer
relations \citep{LenstraLenstraLovasz1982}. Smoothed analysis
\citep{SpielmanTeng2004} also perturbs instances randomly to bound
expected cost. What is shown here is a set of output guarantees for one
fixed finite rational law: certified values and one selected core for
nonconvex cores in every core dimension, with convex residuals on
product boxes or a supplied convexifier on coupled polytopes;
ordinary polynomial expected work under joint convexity; exact rational
output for quadratics; full selected points under a supplied
convexifier, for cubic objectives or when the convexified objective is
convex on all of $\R^n$; and, for residual-convex cubics on product
boxes, full selected points with no convexifier, obtained from exact
face and lattice-margin certificates. Kannan and Rademacher optimize a convex
objective plus a low-dimensional polynomial perturbation
\citep{KannanRademacher2009} to a
prescribed objective accuracy; that guarantee is an objective
approximation, whereas the guarantees here are Cauchy names for the value
and for a fixed selected point of one sampled instance.
