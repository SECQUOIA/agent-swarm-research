\begin{abstract}
Exact power-flow feasibility (network equations plus all operating limits)
is known to be NP-hard for AC networks. We prove that exact feasibility of
resistive direct-current networks (not the linear DC approximation) is complete for
the existential theory of the reals ($\ER$), so it is not in $\NP$ unless
$\NP=\ER$. Hardness holds even on connected, simple, planar, bipartite
graphs of maximum degree three with unit conductances and any fixed lower
bound on girth, and interval endpoints from fixed finite rational sets.
Voltage and signed injection intervals may both be singletons at the same
bus. The reduction simulates bounded arithmetic and preserves the entire
solution set by rational maps in both directions. Consequently, such
networks realize every compact semialgebraic set up to homeomorphism, and exactly the compact basic closed
sets over $\Q$ up to rational equivalence; a unique feasible voltage can
have any algebraic degree. Infeasible fixed-data networks of maximum degree
three with $O(k)$ buses can have injection residuals below $2^{-2^k}$,
the worst-case scale $2^{-2^{\Theta(n)}}$ for $n$-bus networks in
that class. For resistive-line AC networks with zero or one-sided reactive
bounds, hardness transfers under real bus angles, reference-fixed bus-angle
boxes, or principal windows shrinking with network size. For every rational
cosine limit in $(-1,1]$, we encode real-angle limits by a quadratic formula
with linearly many variables and predicates; principal line angles alone
admit winding solutions. We also give short rational certificates under a
gap promise and reactive-stability bounds.
\end{abstract}
