\begin{abstract}
Exact power-flow feasibility can remain difficult under severe graph and
coefficient restrictions. We prove that resistive feasibility is complete
for the existential theory of the reals, even on connected simple planar
bipartite graphs of maximum degree three, with unit conductance and any
prescribed fixed lower bound on girth. The model permits independent
positive voltage and signed injection intervals, including simultaneous
singletons; their endpoints belong to fixed finite rational sets.
The main construction realizes bounded addition and inversion while
preserving the entire solution set by rational maps in both directions.
It transfers to resistive-line AC networks with zero or one-sided reactive
injection bounds under explicit angle conventions. For rational cosine
limits in $(-1,1]$, we give a quadratic existential encoding of consistent
real bus angles with linearly many variables and predicates; principal line
angles alone can admit winding states.
The restricted resistive networks realize every compact semialgebraic
feasible-set topology. Rational equivalence is possible precisely for
compact basic closed sets defined over $\Q$, and unique voltages can have
arbitrary algebraic degree. Separately, the ordinary bounded-degree,
fixed-data construction yields explicit infeasible networks with doubly
exponentially small injection residuals, matching the general separation
scale for that class. Short rational certificates under a specified gap
promise and quantitative reactive stability delimit what numerical
feasibility evidence can certify.
\end{abstract}
