## Findings

1. **Minor — state the rotation quotient in the uniqueness credit.** Locator: `sections/00-introduction.tex:166–172`, especially “uniqueness within a cell for monotone flow networks on the torus.” A common rotation of every bus angle preserves every edge difference and winding number. The cited Jafarpour et al. result concerns uniqueness modulo this freedom (or after fixing a reference). The source defines the quotient by uniform rotations in Section 2.1, and its proof of Theorem 4.1 concludes equality modulo rotations. The broad introductory wording follows that source's own shorthand, but our manuscript carefully distinguishes unique magnitudes from unique reference-fixed rectangular voltages elsewhere; the credit should use the same precision. Proposed correction: “develop winding cells and uniqueness, modulo a common rotation, within each cell for monotone flow networks.” This is a missing qualification, not a substantive novelty error.

2. **Minor — broaden the Jeeninga locator to cover the convexity assertion.** Locator: `sections/00-introduction.tex:116–118`. The sentence attaches Theorem 3.22 to both the matrix-inequality alternatives and convexity. Theorem 3.22 supplies the alternatives; convexity and the closed-halfspace characterization are established in Theorem 3.18. Proposed correction: cite “Theorems 3.18 and 3.22,” or put the convexity statement in a separate clause with its own locator. The attribution to the authors is correct, and this does not affect the model comparison.

The qualification does not assert priority across all electrical models. The graph/data quantifiers match Theorem `thm:structural`; the finite endpoint sets may depend on the fixed girth bound, and there is no tree-hardness claim.

Its Gan–Low and Jeeninga comparison identifies actual differences in operating constraints, rather than incorrectly using signedness as the distinction. Jeeninga's primary text expressly allows signed constant-power demands and excludes voltage/line-current operating limits.
- The AC comparison does not claim to subsume Bienstock–Verma's lossless fixed-magnitude model or Lehmann et al.'s star/tree restriction. The table is an accurate selective list of simultaneous restrictions; it need not list every assumption of the cited theorems because the surrounding prose and model sections supply scope.
- The local cosine versus real zero-winding distinction is scientifically material and explained clearly. The statement covers rational cosines in `(-1,1]`, with unequal positive magnitudes, as in `thm:ac-membership`. The text separately warns that the shrinking-window cosines are not from a fixed alphabet.
- The rational-universality scope matches the compact basic-closed invariant. I checked the invariant argument and the three-quadrant obstruction: bounded nonvanishing denominators permit a basic-closed pullback, and the homogeneous leading-term argument excludes a conjunction representation of the three-quadrant germ. The Dynamic Toolbox comparison is justified by its actual Theorem 1 and Definition 4; integer versus rational coefficients do not distinguish the definitions. The manuscript properly avoids treating the disputed Boolean preprocessing as a dependency of its proof.
- Topological universality is clearly separated from rational equivalence and from generic complex root counts. The primitive-coordinate assertion is backed by affine recovery of the original algebraic coordinate; it does not merely infer a primitive coordinate from a field generated by several voltages.

It describes a limitation of residual thresholds, not a universal exact-algorithm running-time lower bound. It credits the general doubly exponential scale, and the gap promise remains explicit.
- Bibliographic additions are relevant and their checked metadata are consistent with the primary records. No unsupported all-model “first” claim or missing essential predecessor emerged from the searches described below. This is not proof of exhaustive publication priority.
