## Stage 2 AC development

The new formulation in `sections/03-ac.tex`, Lemma `lem:lift` and Theorem `thm:ac-membership`, is sound.

- **Crossing convention and signs.** With an oriented edge from j to i, the determinant in the manuscript is the determinant of the start and end phasors, and its sign matches counterclockwise short-arc motion. I reconstructed both wrap cases from arguments in `[0,2*pi)`. The conditions involving the open lower and closed upper half-planes correctly count arrival at and departure from the positive ray. The determinant sign excludes false crossings of the negative ray. Equal directions give zero; antipodal directions are excluded by the unsquared cosine test with `c > -1`. Unequal positive radii do not change any sign. The formula `delta_ij = alpha_i-alpha_j + 2*pi*k_ij` therefore has the correct sign.
- **Root-normalized potentials.** On a spanning forest, each edge equation determines the next potential by adding or subtracting an integer crossing count. One root fixed to zero in each component makes every potential uniquely determined and integral. The non-tree equations hold exactly when the fundamental-cycle sums vanish. Conversely, vertex differences telescope on every cycle. The displayed lift `theta_i = alpha_i + 2*pi*a_i` has precisely the selected short differences. Isolated vertices and the zero-vertex case are covered. Fixing a potential root to zero is not an additional restriction on feasible phasors.
- **Existential formula size.** Four variables per bus and one per edge suffice. The half-plane and determinant conditions require only constantly many atomic predicates per edge, even when the negations in the third branch are expanded. The difference equations are linear. Rectangular injections have linear total monomial-occurrence count when summed over buses, since each undirected edge occurs twice. Magnitude and cosine constraints are quadratic. Rational denominator clearing has polynomial bit cost even though it need not have linear total bit cost. The theorem deliberately distinguishes these claims and is correct.
- **Relationship to existing winding representations.** This is an elementary incidence-potential representation of a zero-cycle-sum condition, as the manuscript says. The new text does not claim the underlying potential or winding criterion as new. Jafarpour et al., inspected v4 Theorem 3.6, already give a potential description of reduced winding cells. The claimed additional use here is the rational polynomial branch encoding and its complexity consequence.

The new checker is a useful independent finite regression. Its `all_cycle_sums_zero` oracle enumerates simple cycles without using the propagation routine's forest; it is not merely repeating that implementation. Its arbitrary three-valued edge labels test a stronger algebraic domain than phasor-realizable labels alone. Orientation reversal, disconnected components, integer shifts across the branch cut, and rejection of the actual winding four-cycle all behave as stated. These tests supplement the general proof; neither the author nor the appendix mistakes them for a proof of all graph sizes.
