## Mathematical and physical review

1. **Complex-power signs and input convention.** Starting from `S_i=U_i conjugate(I_i)` with `y=g+i b`, I independently obtain active contribution `g(r_i^2-H_ij)-b D_ij` and reactive contribution `-b(r_i^2-H_ij)-g D_ij`. The shunt signs also agree. The manuscript specifies reciprocal lines, positive voltage magnitudes, signed injections, independently allowed singleton intervals, no transformers/phase shifters, and the rational cosine encoding. These are sufficient to define the claimed problem without a hidden assumption about bus types.
2. **Negative cosine limits.** Introducing positive magnitude variables removes the sign loss that squaring a cosine inequality would cause. `H_ij >= c_ij r_i r_j` is exactly the principal limit for the full specified interval `c_ij in (-1,1]`. At `c=1` only equal directions remain; `c>-1` excludes antipodes. Limits arbitrarily close to `pi` do not require large formulas beyond their already supplied rational bit length.
3. **Crossing rule, axes, and direction.** With line orientation `j -> i`, the determinant `D_ij` has the correct sign for the short arc. The proof's split on `alpha_i-alpha_j` rigorously identifies the wrap intervals `(-2pi,-pi)` and `(pi,2pi)`. Equal directions give zero. Arrival at and departure from the positive ray follow the stated half-open convention; the determinant test excludes the negative ray. Reversal negates the crossing count, including endpoint cases. Unequal radii preserve the relevant signs, unlike the older real-coordinate-sum test outside the acute-angle regime.
4. **Real lift.** The principal differences are uniquely defined because no edge is antipodal. The crossing identity telescopes with the stated traversal orientation. Choosing root arguments and integrating along a spanning forest gives a representation of every phasor, and the zero winding equation on each fundamental cycle supplies every remaining edge. Thus neither implication requires transcendental constraints in the existential formula.
5. **Polynomial encoding.** Each three-valued crossing variable is fixed by the Boolean formula itself; no integer quantifier is being hidden. There are polynomially many variables and total cycle-edge incidences, and all coefficients are rational. Strict comparisons and Boolean negations are permitted by the ETR convention established in section 1.
6. **Equal-angle transfer.** The paired reactive identity has the correct sign. The conclusion follows from `t sin(t)>0` for `0<|t|<pi`, positive magnitudes, and positive conductances. Disconnected graphs cause no issue: the conclusion is componentwise, with arbitrary component constants. This proof supports the entire claimed open interval below `pi` and does not assume stability or positive cosine.
7. **One-sided reactive intervals.** Purely resistive reciprocal lines make the sum of reactive injections zero before applying angle restrictions. Hence a common weak sign forces all injections to zero. The `[0,1]` substitution preserves the relevant feasible sets exactly. The prose correctly distinguishes this saturation identity from a symmetric tolerance or robustness theorem.
8. **Winding counterexamples.** The uniform cycle profile has the displayed injections and winding, including the triangle case. For arbitrary fixed rational `c<1`, sufficiently long cycles meet the window and rational active intervals around their common positive injection give rational data. This is an existence counterexample, not a claimed uniform polynomial-time construction in the bit length of variable `c`; no such complexity assertion is made. The explicitly given four-cycle has rational singleton injections and is directly checkable. The two-bus example at angle `pi` correctly shows why the strict endpoint in the equal-angle lemma matters.
9. **Small-cycle budgets and principal-window hardness.** A principal cycle sum is an integral multiple of `2pi`; bounding its absolute value strictly below `2pi` forces zero. The sine-concavity bound used for `c_n=1-1/n^2` is valid, gives `gamma_n <= pi/(sqrt(2)n)`, and leaves ample strict margin for cycles of at most `n` edges. Padding with feasible isolated buses preserves the reduction and its finite original alphabet. The text explicitly distinguishes the size-dependent cosine data from the fixed data in the rest of the construction.
10. **Bus-angle boxes.** Positive lower magnitudes plus `e_i >= |f_i|` force `e_i>0`, so there is a genuine real representative in `[-pi/4,pi/4]`. The line differences then satisfy the transfer lemma. One positive-real reference per component removes the residual constant angle, producing exactly the resistive feasible set in rectangular coordinates. This does not silently fix one reference for a disconnected graph.
11. **Abstract and accepted dependencies.** The stage-1 direction-of-bijection issue and certificate wording have been corrected. The updated abstract accurately distinguishes real line limits, principal windows, and bus boxes, and does not claim the result covers the lossless fixed-magnitude model.
