## Mathematical audit

1. **AC equations and data convention (Section 3.1): correct.** With (y=g+\mathrm i b), (U_i\overline{U_j}=H_{ij}+\mathrm i D_{ij}), multiplication by (g-\mathrm i b) gives the active term (g(r_i^2-H_{ij})-bD_{ij}) and reactive term (-b(r_i^2-H_{ij})-gD_{ij}). The shunt signs are also correct. Positivity of the magnitude variables ensures that (r_i^2=e_i^2+f_i^2) selects the intended root. Rational cosine inputs avoid treating transcendental angle bounds as rational numbers.

2. **Cosine test for the full stated range: correct.** The unsquared inequality (H_{ij}\ge c_{ij}r_ir_j) remains valid for negative (c_{ij}). The condition (c_{ij}>-1) excludes exactly the antipodal ambiguity needed later; (c_{ij}=1) is harmless and forces equal directions. All expressions remain quadratic.

3. **Short-arc crossing count (Lemma 3.1): correct, including axes and unequal magnitudes.** For principal endpoint arguments in ([0,2\pi)), the first branch is equivalent to (alpha_i-alpha_j<-pi), and the reverse branch to a difference greater than (pi). The determinant has the sign of the sine of this difference independent of either positive radius. Arrival at argument zero from below contributes (+1), departure below contributes (-1), and an endpoint at argument (pi) does not spuriously count a positive-ray crossing. Equal directions have determinant zero and crossing zero. Antipodal pairs are excluded rather than assigned an inconsistent convention. The manuscript proof covers these cases adequately.

4. **Cycle criterion and ETR membership (Lemma 3.2 and Theorem 3.3): correct.** The orientation (j\to i) matches both (delta_{ij}) and (D_{ij}). Summing the argument identity gives precisely the stated signed cycle count. Fundamental-cycle zero sums suffice: define a real lift along the forest and use each non-tree edge's cycle to enforce its difference. Necessity uses the strict upper bound (pi) to identify an allowed real difference with its unique principal representative. The encoded three-valued variables are real variables constrained by disjunctions, not hidden integer quantifiers. The number of cycle equations and their total length are polynomial, including disconnected graphs, isolated vertices, and forests.

5. **Equal-angle identity and hardness transfer (Lemma 3.4 and Theorem 3.5): correct.** Pairing (-\theta_iQ_i) over undirected edges gives (g_{ij}v_iv_j(\theta_i-\theta_j)\sin(\theta_i-\theta_j)) with a positive coefficient. Each term vanishes only at zero inside ((-pi,pi)). Thus the transfer with real limits (pi/2) is exact and preserves the resistive finite data and degree bound. The result does not assume positive cosine or stability. The NP consequence is properly conditional and explicitly scoped away from the lossless fixed-magnitude model.

6. **One-sided reactive intervals (Corollary 3.6): correct.** Pairwise antisymmetry gives total reactive injection zero for every resistive, shunt-free phasor profile, regardless of angle limits. Common-sign reactive intervals therefore force every reactive injection to zero. Replacing ([0,0]) by ([0,1]) changes no feasible profile in the restricted instances. The prose correctly explains why this is not a result about symmetric residual tolerances.

7. **Winding counterexample (Proposition 3.7): correct.** The uniformly spaced (n)-cycle has opposite sine contributions at each bus, active injection (2(1-cos(2\pi/n))), and winding one in the stated traversal. For every fixed rational (c<1), sufficiently large (n) works. The proof wisely chooses rational active intervals around the possibly irrational power rather than asserting the power itself is rational. The explicit four-cycle gives rational singleton injections 2. Its corresponding unit-voltage resistive network has zero injections. This is an existence counterexample, so the density argument does not need to supply a polynomial-time rational approximation algorithm. The two-bus endpoint example establishes sharpness of the strict (pi) hypothesis.

8. **Positive principal windows (Lemma 3.8 and Corollary 3.9): correct.** A cycle's principal-difference sum is a multiple of (2\pi), and a strict budget below (2\pi) makes it zero. The inequality (1-cos t\ge2t^2/\pi^2) on ([0,pi]) yields (gamma_n\le\pi/(\sqrt2 n)). A fundamental cycle has at most (n) edges, leaving ample strict margin below (2\pi). The cosine (1-1/n^2) has logarithmic bit length and a strictly positive angle window. Isolated padding buses preserve feasibility and the original finite data. The distinction between fixed finite data and the size-dependent cosines is explicit.

9. **Bus-angle boxes (Corollary 3.10): correct.** The two linear inequalities imply (e_i\ge|f_i|). Positive magnitude then excludes (e_i=0), supplying a unique angle in ([-pi/4,pi/4]). All edge differences admit the equal-angle lemma; the component reference removes the remaining common rotation. This gives exactly (f=0) and (e=v), with no separate line limits needed.
