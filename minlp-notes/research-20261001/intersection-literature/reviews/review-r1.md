# Review r1: stream `intersection-literature`

Reviewer: independent research agent (did not write the material). Date: 2026-10-01/02.
Object: `note.md`, `code/`, `logs/`, `sources/` of this stream.
Verdict: **major problems** (three major, four minor, two optional issues).

Most of the audit holds up. I checked these against the sources and reproduced them:
the SCIP baseline (source defaults, CHANGELOG lines, quotes and pages in the SCIP 8/9
papers, the ZIB 20-29 numbers), the BCM locators and the "deepest cut" quote, the CCDLM
Thm. 6.3 construction and its "more nonlinear" variant of Ex. 6.1, the CWY statement
(thesis Thm. 4.1, p. 66), the Eckstein-Nediak locators, the Theorem 14 corner facts,
Proposition B's identity, and the 0/47 and 0/73 cone-span counts. The problems are in

## Major issues

### M1. KY Examples 4.2-4.5 are missed. Proposition 2(b) is essentially KY Example 4.3, and KY already show that their strict condition cannot be dropped

Location: note Summary item 1 (lines 37-44), Section 4.4 ("The equality case", lines
306-339), Section 7 rows "Thm 1(4)" and "Prop 2(b)", Section 8 item 1.

The note cites KY Section 4 (Prop. 4.1, 4.3, Cor. 4.4) but not the four examples that
close that section (draft pp. 21-23, `sources/kilinc-yang2015-sufficiency-draft.txt`
lines ~990-1100). KY state there: "Examples 4.2 and 4.4 show that our sufficient
condition stated in Corollary 3.12 has room for improvement ... Examples 4.3 and 4.5
demonstrate cases of an extreme inequality ... that cannot be generated by any CGF when
... σ_{D_c}(d) = 1 or ... = 0. The main difference in these examples is in the way the
sequence of points in B_2 approach to a point in cone(A)."

- **KY Example 4.3** (A = I, B = {e_1, e_2} ∪ {(1 − 1/√n, −1/n)}, c = (1, 1)): the points
  of B_2 are `(1 − s, −s²)` with `s = 1/√n`. They approach the corner minimizer `e_1` along
  a parabola tangent to the ray, and c is not generated. Proposition 2(b) of the sfree
  note uses the points `(−s², 1 − s)`, which accumulate at the minimizer `e_2`, with
  `w = (1, 1)`. This is the same example with the coordinates swapped; the sfree version
  only replaces the discrete sequence by a quadratic sublevel set.
- **KY Example 4.5** gives the same failure for σ = 0 along a limit direction.
- **KY Example 4.2** (points `(1, −1/n)`, a transversal approach) shows that the cut is
  generated in the equality case σ = 1. Example 4.3 shows that it is not generated under
  a tangential approach. This is exactly the distinction that the transversality
  hypothesis `∇q(t*)^T(x̄ − t*) > 0` of Theorem 1(4) formalizes.

Consequences:

1. These statements are wrong as priority claims: "Proposition 2(b) shows that the strict
   inequality in their condition cannot be dropped in general" (Summary and §4.4), and
   "Prop 2(b): Not found as an example; elementary" (§7). KY already show this and use
   the same parabolic mechanism. Proposition 2(b) should be cited as a quadratic-set
   instance of KY Ex. 4.3.
2. The Theorem 1(4) verdict ("no prior statement found; covers an equality case left open
   by KY") must be qualified. KY observe that attainment in the equality case depends on
   how B_2 approaches the limit point, and give one example of each kind. What remains is
   a *general* sufficient condition (transversality at every minimizer) for closed `S`
   given by a `C^1` function. That is a modest but defensible claim; the novelty table and
   Correction 1 should say so.

### M2. KY Corollary 3.12 as printed is false. The note's Section 4.4 argument depends on an unstated corrected reading

Location: Remark 4.4 ("This derivation assumes the draft's Cor. 3.12 as stated",
lines 281-283) and the Theorem 14 check (lines 335-337: "By the minimality argument in
Remark 4.4, `t* − s̄` must be the `D_1` representative of its ray, and condition (iii)
fails").

In the printed corollary (draft p. 17), condition (ii) constrains only `D_1`, and `D_2`
is any subset of `(cl(R_{++}(B_2)) \ cl(B_2)) ∩ cone(A)`. A point of `D_2` may therefore
lie on a ray that meets `cl(B_2)` farther out. Then (iii) for that ray only asks for
σ > 0, which always holds for `c > 0`. Two counterexamples to the printed statement:

- **KY's own Example 4.3.** Take `D_1 = ∅`, `D_2 = {(1/2, 0)}`. Then (i) holds (the only
  ray is `cone(e_1)`), (ii) is vacuous, and σ_{D_c}((1/2, 0)) = 1/2 > 0. The corollary
  would say that c = (1, 1) is generated, which KY prove false in the same example.
- **sfree Proposition 2(b).** `cl(R_{++}B_2) ∩ R^2_+` consists of the rays of `e_1` and
  `e_2`. Ray `e_1` meets `cl(B_2)` first at `φ e_1`, where φ is the golden ratio, so
  σ = φ > 1. For ray `e_2`, take `D_2 = {(0, 1/2)}`: `q(0, 1/2) = 1/4 > 0`, so this point
  is not in `cl(B_2)`, and σ = 1/2 > 0. The printed corollary would give attainment of
  `z_K = 1`, which Proposition 2(b) refutes.

KY's proof replaces each `d ∈ D_2` by `2d/σ(d)` and applies Prop. 3.11, whose condition
(ii) then needs the whole segment `(0, 2d/σ(d))` to avoid `cl(B_2)`. So the intended
hypothesis is that **the ray of each `D_2` point does not meet `cl(B_2)`**.

- Remark 4.4's own choice of `D_1` and `D_2` satisfies this corrected hypothesis, so the
  derivation of Theorem 1(2) survives.
- But "as stated" is wrong.
- The Theorem 14 inference "`t* − s̄` must be the `D_1` representative" holds only under
  the corrected reading. Under the printed reading one could put `(t* − s̄)/2` into
  `D_2`, and the corollary's hypotheses would hold.
- The claim that Theorem 1(4) "covers the equality case KY's strict conditions leave open"
  likewise needs the corrected reading.

Fix: state the corrected form of Cor. 3.12, explain why it is needed (with one of the
counterexamples above), and say that the note's conclusions use the corrected form.

### M3. Proposition B's general last claim is proved only for one `F`

Location: §5.2, the statement "For `F` with `det F > 0` not a multiple of a rotation,
`C_F` is neither a (14a) nor a (14b) set", labeled *proved*; also the summary item 3.
`code/check_bcm_orbit.py` Part 3 prints this general conclusion from the single witness
`F^T = diag(2, 1/2)`.

The proof only treats `F^T = diag(2, 1/2)`. The general claim is true, and a short proof
is available:

- *(14a).* The lineality space of `C_A = {M : sym(AM) ⪰ 0}` is `A^{-1} span(J)`. A (14a)
  set is `C_{G}` for a rotation `G`, with lineality `G^{-1} span(J)`. Equal cones force
  `A^{-1} J = μ G^{-1} J`, so `A = G/μ`. Then `μ > 0`, since `C_{−G} = −C_G ≠ C_G`.
- *(14b).* This part is immediate. By BCM's own proof of Lemma 22 (p. 21), every (14b)
  set lies in `{ad ≤ bc}`, while `int C_F ⊆ {det M > 0}` for `det F > 0`.

Either add this argument or restrict the statement to the example. Under the rubric for
this program, a stated general claim without proof is a missing proof step.

### m2. Kılınç-Karzan-Steffy (Math. Program. 159, 2016, 585-605) is missing

KY's B ⊆ cone(A) result is a restatement of "[20, Proposition 5](b)". Yıldız's thesis
(p. 66) says that Theorem 4.1 (CWY) "has been generalized in [88, 89]", where [88] is
Kılınç-Karzan-Steffy. KS also prove that relaxed CGFs (support functions, possibly
infinite) generate all necessary inequalities without assumptions. That is a refereed
result close to Theorem 1(5). It is the published counterpart to the unrefereed KY
draft, and it belongs in §3, in the §7 rows for Thm 1(2) and 1(5), and in the §9
must-cite list.

### m3. The solver baseline is stated more strongly than the sources read allow

§6 "Correct baseline statement" ("there is no published branch-and-bound comparison"),
Correction 8, and the summary all say this flatly. But the note did not read the
computations of the monoidal paper, which the SCIP 9 report cites ("As [15] showed, the
strengthened intersection cuts significantly outperform ..."), or the Math. Program. 197
version of Chmiela et al. (Open question 3 admits this). Replace the statement by "no
branch-and-bound comparison in the sources I could read (ZIB 20-29 gives only
preliminary LP-speed remarks); the monoidal paper's computations and the journal version
were not accessible". My own attempt found no open copy either. The IPCO slides URL now
redirects, and Springer requires login, which I did not bypass.

### m4. §4.3 tests only part (1) of Proposition A but concludes that Proposition A "does not apply to any corner studied"

Part (2) (`S_k − s̄ ⊆ cone(P)`) was not tested. The conclusion is still true, for a
reason the note should state:

- `{w ≤ xy}` and `{w ≥ xy}` lie in no closed halfspace;
- a convex cone `≠ R^3` lies in one.

So for bilinear `S`, part (2) holds iff part (1) does. In general, part (1) is a special
case of part (2), since `cone(P) = R^k` implies `S_k − s̄ ⊆ cone(P)`.
