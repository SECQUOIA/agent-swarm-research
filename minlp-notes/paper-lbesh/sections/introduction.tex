\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Generalized disjunctive programming (GDP) represents discrete alternatives by logical statements and associates each alternative with a set of algebraic constraints. In a convex minimization GDP, the objective and inequality functions are convex and equality constraints are affine, both globally and within each alternative; the union of alternatives is generally nonconvex. This structure is useful for process selection and design because constraints belonging to an inactive alternative need not constrain the selected process. Logic-based outer approximation exploits that distinction when constructing mixed-integer linear masters and continuous subproblems \citep{turkay1996}.

For a bounded convex disjunction, a hull formulation distributes the original variables among alternatives and scales each copy by its selection weight. Convex nonlinear rows then admit perspective representations. An outer approximation can enforce these rows through affine perspective cuts \citep{frangioni2006,bestuzheva2023}. The choice of the point at which to construct a cut remains consequential even when the formulation and the family of valid inequalities are fixed. A point tangent uses the current violated point. A radial supporting cut first searches the segment from a strict interior anchor to that point, then linearizes at the boundary. The latter incurs extra function evaluations but can remove a different portion of the current relaxation.

This paper studies a specific computational question: does that additional separation work repay its cost in a GDP solver? We compare the extended supporting hyperplane (ESH) policy with the extended cutting plane (ECP) policy in one implementation, holding the master construction, initialization, incumbent recovery, validation rules, and solver settings fixed within each pair. We retain both hull and big-$M$ masters and both single-tree and repeated-master execution. The name logic-based ESH (LB-ESH) describes the implementation's use of the original disjunct structure; it does not denote a new family of inequalities. The tested solver uses nonlinear programming (NLP) both to seek interior anchors and to recover feasible incumbents at integer selections.

Our contribution is the controlled comparison, its explanatory diagnostics, and the explicit mathematical and numerical conditions needed to interpret it. First, we give a self-contained account of cut validity and fixed-residual separation, including zero selection weights, approximate boundary points, small-weight omission, and the distinction between a valid relaxation bound and an objective-gap certificate. We derive a residual-calibrated fractional rule and a per-disjunction geometric repair bound, and give counterexamples to general cut dominance and to a linear objective-error inference after intersecting disjunctions with global constraints. These results clarify the method's scope; we do not assert priority for the underlying convexity and compactness arguments. The residual-calibrated rule is analyzed separately from the fixed weight cutoff used in the experiments.

Second, an executable oracle diagnostic isolates dependence on equivalent constraint representations and measures the function evaluations required by radial searches. Third, the computational study combines 51 related generated controls, of which 33 are held out from the pilot split, with 27 external models, two additional schedules for the held-out single-tree comparisons, pilot ablations, and independent conic references. Every supplied incumbent is checked against the original model. Failed interfaces, invalid witnesses, and open gaps remain in their declared cohorts. Supplementary tolerance and initialization studies are reported separately from the original runs.

The results support a limited practical benefit. On the held-out controls, single-tree ESH meets the numerical solve criterion on all 33 instances with either formulation; ECP does so on 32 with hull and 31 with big-$M$. Common-solved shifted geometric mean wall times favor ESH by approximately 4--6\% in the single-tree comparisons across the three schedules. ESH has lower arithmetic mean cut counts and LP iteration counts on the matched cohorts, but its recorded row-separation time per run is substantially greater. Formulation and tree policy have larger measured timing effects. The external models give equal ESH and ECP accepted solve counts in every matched configuration, and exact conic formulations provide a strong alternative where available. Disabling integer-point NLP recovery reduces accepted outcomes from 69 of 72 pilot runs to one of 72, exposing an important limitation of this implementation.

The matched design also limits the interpretation. ECP shares interior initialization even though point tangents do not require it; the comparison therefore measures a policy change inside this solver, rather than independently optimized implementations. The controls share model structures and parameters, and repetition changes scheduling but retains the solver seed. Reported solves satisfy stated numerical feasibility and gap tests, rather than exact-arithmetic certificates. Within these limits, the study provides evidence for a concrete solver-design decision and documents circumstances in which the extra radial work is not rewarded.
