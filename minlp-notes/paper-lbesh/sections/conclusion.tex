\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Radial separation offers a small but repeatable computational benefit over point tangents in the matched NLP-assisted GDP implementation studied here. It generates fewer cuts on average while incurring substantially greater recorded row-separation time per run. The advantage is conditional on the formulation, tree policy, shared initialization and tested instances; it does not transfer to improved external accepted counts. Supported exact conic alternatives have lower common-solved mean times in these comparisons, while external coverage is mixed.

The mathematical analysis explains what can be guaranteed independently of these timings: valid perspective inequalities, finite fixed-residual separation under explicit compactness and enforcement conditions, and a safe residual-calibrated treatment of small weights. The diagnostic and counterexamples explain why these guarantees imply neither universal cut dominance nor a general linear objective-error bound without additional joint regularity. Together with the complete model specifications, retained negative outcomes, original-model validation and reproducible artifacts, the study provides a concrete basis for deciding whether the cost of radial separation is justified in a particular GDP solver.
