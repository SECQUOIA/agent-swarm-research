\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
The controlled comparison answers a practical implementation question. Once a GDP solver already maintains term-specific interiors, recovers integer-point incumbents with NLPs, and supports the same master formulations, replacing point tangents by radial cuts can reduce outer work enough to repay the extra function evaluations. The benefit is modest: the single-tree conditional timing advantage stays at about 4--6\% across three schedules. The larger formulation and tree effects suggest that separator choice should be assessed within the complete solver architecture. Aggregate means alone would overstate how consistently ESH reduces search, because several node and NLP medians favor ECP.

The contribution is the matched GDP experiment, the representation and oracle-cost diagnostics, and the reconciliation of exact separation statements with recorded numerical behavior; the ingredients are established (\cref{sec:related}). The residual-calibrated omission rule and the counterexamples make that interpretation more precise, and their proofs expose the assumptions needed to use the method.

Because of the scope limits stated in \cref{sec:introduction}, small aggregate differences describe these matched schedules, repeated with changed execution order but the same search seed, not an independent population of applications. The preliminary cone pass also means that the held-out set was untuned rather than wholly unseen.

The external and conic results show where the positive finding stops. Equal external accepted counts give no evidence of wider ESH coverage. Missing interface values, original-model witness violations and initial-value export failures affect the external baseline rankings, as the separate follow-ups show. On supported quadratic and external comparisons, exact cone formulations can be substantially faster. The study includes no general mixed-integer conic OA solver, so it cannot rank the prototype against that approach. Nonquadratic functions also need not be nonconic: the exponential, logarithmic and reciprocal controls have explicit cone representations.

The recovery ablation is especially informative: the tested implementation derives much of its practical success from solving selected continuous subproblems. Finite fixed-residual separation does not ensure that finite-tolerance callbacks will produce usable incumbents or close a gap within a time limit. The retained logs establish the observed stalls and open gaps but lack the complete candidate and cut histories needed to identify a single cause for every failure. Likewise, the tested fractional user cuts add work without improving accepted counts. These outcomes delimit the present implementation. They do not invalidate separation-based convex optimization or the residual-calibrated rule, which the frozen study did not implement.

Finally, validity and numerical certification are different obligations. The exact proofs require in-domain evaluations, compact candidate sets and enforcement of old cuts. The implementation retains full tangent offsets and validates recovered incumbents, but provides no interval-certified coefficients, certified cone duals, or certified normalization domain near zero selection weights. Original-model checks and independent arithmetic and reference comparisons strengthen the empirical evidence but remain numerical checks.
