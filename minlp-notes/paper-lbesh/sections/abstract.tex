\begin{abstract}
Radial supporting cuts require a boundary search before linearization, whereas point tangents linearize directly at the violated point. We ask whether this extra work pays off in outer approximation of convex generalized disjunctive programs (GDPs). In one solver that uses nonlinear programming (NLP) for interior anchors and integer-point incumbent recovery, we compare radial (extended supporting hyperplane, ESH) and point (extended cutting plane, ECP) separation with all other components matched, for hull masters with perspective cuts and big-$M$ masters, in single- and multiple-tree execution. The study retains 1464 benchmark outcomes on 51 generated controls and 27 external models, plus 420 continuous conic reference calls. On 33 held-out controls, single-tree ESH solves all instances with either formulation; ECP solves 32 with hull and 31 with big-$M$. Across three schedules with unchanged search seeds, single-tree common-solved shifted mean wall times favor ESH by 4--6\%. In the four primary matched configurations, ESH generates 6--18\% fewer cuts on average, but its mean recorded row-separation time per run is 5.5--6.6 times that of ECP. Formulation and tree organization have larger timing effects. On external models, ESH and ECP have equal accepted counts; where exact conic formulations are supported, they have lower common-solved mean times. Disabling integer-point NLP recovery reduces accepted single-tree pilot runs from 69/72 to 1/72. Proofs give conditions for valid cuts and finite fixed-residual separation and separate them from numerical gap acceptance. The evidence supports a modest separator benefit in this implementation, not a new cut family or general solver superiority.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} generalized disjunctive programming; convex mixed-integer nonlinear programming; perspective cuts; extended supporting hyperplanes; outer approximation; computational reproducibility.
