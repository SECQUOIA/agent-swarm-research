## Mandatory distinctions and negative findings

- Perspective cuts are established; radial selection changes the generation point. No stronger limiting relaxation or general finite-cut dominance is claimed.
- The proposed residual-calibrated fractional policy is proved under stated assumptions; the frozen solver uses a fixed threshold. A cap/stall is not a complete residual certificate. Big-M fractional separation has no corresponding hull theorem.
- Separation at a fixed residual tolerance is not exact primal feasibility or guaranteed finite closure of an objective gap. Valid lower bounds and independently validated incumbents have distinct roles.
- A failed anchor/reduced NLP is not proof of model or assignment infeasibility. ECP fallback covers lack of strict interiors in theory, but the prototype requires finite differentiable evaluations.
- Cut validity for approximate boundary points requires retaining the function offset; a floating-point cut is not automatically an exact separating certificate.
- Individual-disjunction repair cannot imply a linear intersection objective-error bound without joint regularity. Epigraph compactness needs a separate argument.
- No-NLP ablation means no integer-point NLP recovery, not zero NLP work: its 72 records still contain interior NLP work, with 62 stalls, nine time limits and one solve, versus 69 default solves. Explain what the source and retained states establish, without diagnosing all stalls from absent histories. See `notes/lbesh-nonlp-ablation-diagnosis.md`.
- Fractional user cuts add no accepted-count gain in the declared pilot comparison. Report actual cuts and costs, not only option settings.
- Exact quadratic conic controls and general cone root/enumeration references are different comparisons. Numerical cone duals and reported infeasibilities remain uncertified floating-point evidence.
- External legacy scope must exclude all six norm-objective cases from the smooth no-norm stratum, including the two successful ones. The 25 conic-supported models exclude two exponential batch models. No unsupported cases may create an artificial conic speed advantage/penalty.
- Original baseline interface failures do not show search inferiority. Keep Gurobi trig tolerance followup (nine runs) and GAMS initialization followup (24 runs) separate; report missing exported batch bounds despite native closed-gap logs. See `notes/lbesh-baseline-numerics.md`, `notes/lbesh-review-gurobi-sensitivity.md`, `notes/lbesh-review-legacy-initialization.md`.
- Recorded row-separation, NLP and wall times may overlap. Arithmetic mean node/NLP changes concentrate in hard cases and are not typical-case causal explanations. Report appropriate medians and cohort membership.
