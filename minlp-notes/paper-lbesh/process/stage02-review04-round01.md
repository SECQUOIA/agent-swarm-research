## Minor findings

1. **Declare all row sets finite.** `model-and-cuts.tex:3–15` explicitly makes `I` and `K_i` finite but does not explicitly do so for the global row set `H` and term row sets `J_{ik}`. The proofs in `separation-theory.tex:48,67` use the finiteness of the collection of rows to sum the rowwise packing bounds. This is plainly the intended finite algebraic problem class; say that `H` and every `J_{ik}` are finite as well. No new assumption beyond the intended scope is needed.

2. **Say that the whole candidate sequence lies in one compact set.** `separation-theory.tex:114` currently says “a sequence of compact lifted candidates.” Compactness is a property of the common candidate domain or closure of the sequence, not of individual points. Replace this with “a sequence of lifted candidates contained in a common compact set” (or explicitly the bounded lifted domain defined earlier). The surrounding text and proof make the intended uniform bound clear, so this is a precision correction rather than a counterexample to the intended proposition. It especially helps readers understand why merely bounded original variables do not settle the artificial-epigraph issue.

3. **Narrow the description of nonfinite-error detection to match the implementation.** `algorithm-and-implementation.tex:16` says that “Nonfinite violations or fallback coefficients raise an evaluation error.” `_esh_cuts` explicitly rejects a nonfinite row value at the exterior candidate (`solver.py:316–317`) and nonfinite fallback coefficients (`:341–343`). It does not explicitly test finiteness of every bisection row evaluation (`:327–332`) or of the computed affine violation `viol` (`:337–338`). For example, a NaN midpoint comparison is treated as the nonpositive branch. State specifically that nonfinite *candidate row values* and nonfinite fallback coefficients raise errors; do not imply certified or exhaustive checks of intermediate arithmetic. It would be accurate to add that intermediate bisection evaluations receive no separate finiteness guard. The existing certified-arithmetic qualification is good but does not make this broad procedural claim literal.

4. **Distinguish the exact boundary target from the implemented exterior bracket endpoint.** `algorithm-and-implementation.tex:20` says that the line search's boundary point belongs to the row boundary. In the preceding procedure (`:15`), the code instead linearizes at an exterior bracket endpoint, which is generally outside that boundary. Write “The exact boundary target belongs to …” or “With an exact root, the resulting point belongs to …”. Keep the useful explanation that rowwise boundary search need not return a point feasible for all rows of the disjunct. The approximate-point validity discussion already gives the correct mathematics.

## Independent proof reconstruction

- **Perspective identity and zero weight.** Normalizing a convex combination of positive-weight blocks gives convexity; boundedness of `g` gives continuity at `(0,0)`. The differentiated perspective has gradient `(a_z,b_z)` and zero affine constant by homogeneity. The point tangent at `z=p` establishes the reverse inequality in the supremum identity. The empty-term convention is explicitly separated from the closure of the empty positive-weight feasible lift, avoiding a common incorrect closure claim.
- **Separate-disjunction hull.** The forward map is a convex combination of normalized positive-weight blocks; grouping any convex combination by alternative proves the reverse map. Empty terms cause no obstruction at zero weight. The paper restricts this result to separate disjunctions, appropriately leaving original integer restrictions, cross-disjunction interactions and logic out of a full-hull claim. The omitted-coordinate completion also works: set each omitted coordinate to its weight times the original coordinate.
- **Big-M and epigraph.** Box maximization of an affine function gives a valid finite deactivation constant. An objective tangent supplies the lower epigraph bound, and the gradient/diameter inequality supplies the upper bound. Because the artificial variable has no other role, lowering it to the original objective preserves the optimization problem. The prototype's lack of automatic compact epigraph bounds is disclosed.
- **ESH separation margin.** At the negative anchor, the boundary supporting inequality gives `a_z·(p-anchor) >= delta/s`. The Lipschitz inequality on the final segment gives `v <= LD(1-s)`. Multiplication yields the displayed lower bound; the additional division by `s<=1` only strengthens it. The normal bound follows from `|b_z| <= F+LR`.
- **Integral and fractional packing.** Later candidates satisfy old cuts. Therefore the difference between the generating cut violation and the later nonpositive violation, divided by a uniform normal bound, gives positive pairwise distance for each assigned-row subsequence. With policy mixtures use the smaller of the ECP/ESH distance bounds. Term activation removes big-M deactivation or sets the active hull copy equal to `x`. Fractional packing instead uses lifted block distance and the weighted residual. The safe-skip bound and fixed-cutoff residual bound follow directly from `r<=lambda U_r` and `lambda<=1`. The big-M limitation is correctly stated.
- **Geometric repair.** The repair uses convex coefficients `delta/(delta+v)` and `v/(delta+v)`, which cancel the upper row residual against the anchor margin. Box and affine rows are preserved. The weighted sum of displacement bounds proves the one-disjunction estimate, and `lambda v <= epsilon_p` proves the calibrated estimate. The global-row counterexample has exact feasible set `{(0,0)}`, residual `epsilon`, and objective underestimation `sqrt(epsilon)`, so it invalidates a uniform linear rate from term interiors alone.
- **Value convergence.** Common compactness supplies cluster points; residual continuity supplies feasibility of every cluster point. Relaxation optimality (or vanishing suboptimality) supplies the upper limit bound; feasibility supplies the lower limit bound. Closed bounded integer coordinate sets preserve integrality for the GDP version. The proof does not improperly infer a finite rate.
- **Single-tree contract.** The result is conditional on old-cut enforcement, finite repeated callbacks, and finite or uniformly separating fractional refinement. Under those explicit conditions it reduces to the packing results plus complete finite-MILP solution. It claims residual acceptance separately from an exactly feasible objective certificate. It does not claim the callback implementation automatically satisfies the contract.
- **Certified arithmetic.** Uniform coefficient-expression error `E` makes relaxation by `E` valid. At the generating point one loses at most another `E`; a later allowed violation `rho` yields `(kappa-2E-rho)/Ktilde`. The coefficientwise enclosure formula is correct.
- **Representation invariance and scalar recurrence.** Positive boundary derivative scales all affine coefficients by the same positive factor. At an exterior point the chain rule yields the stated ratio; convexity at zero gives `phi(v)<=v phi'(v)`. The exponential tangent recurrence is exactly Newton's update and produces a strictly tighter bound above one at each step. Decrements are less than `1/a`, proving the transient lower bound; the limiting fixed-point equation forces limit one. The quadratic Taylor coefficient is `a/2`.
- **Ellipsoid dominance and counterexample.** Direct differentiation gives the centered ECP offset `(r^2+1)/(2r)>1`. The off-center circle root formula, its bounds, and both witness inequalities check out, so neither halfspace generally contains the other. Setting weight one is enough to transfer failure of general dominance to the perspective setting.
