## Required minor corrections

1. **Describe the actual finiteness checks precisely.** `sections/algorithm-and-implementation.tex:15` says radial use requires a “finite usable strict anchor,” and `:16` says “Nonfinite violations or fallback coefficients raise an evaluation error.” The source `code/minlp_solver_lab/lbesh/solver.py:314`–`:345` explicitly tests finiteness of the candidate row value and tangent coefficients. It does **not** explicitly test finiteness of the anchor row value, intermediate bisection values, or the scalar radial tangent violation. The anchor condition is only `r.violation(interior) < -1e-9`; bisection branches on `> 0`; the computed radial violation branches on `> self.cut_tol * 0.1`. For example, negative infinity passes the anchor comparison, and positive infinity passes the radial violation comparison. Exceptions may propagate, but NaN/infinity return values are not uniformly rejected by these branches. State the specific checked quantities and note that intermediate anchor/root evaluations have no separate finiteness guard. This is a manuscript-versus-source accuracy issue, not evidence that any recorded experiment encountered such values. The existing caveats on noncertified finite arithmetic should remain. No source change or rerun is required for honest reporting of the frozen implementation.

2. **Make the single-tree contract explicitly exhaustive about new cuts at old-cut-infeasible callbacks.** `sections/separation-theory.tex:125` excludes such points from the count of “fresh” rejection iterations, but does not directly say whether new cuts can still be added there; `:133` then infers that finitely many additions leave a fixed finite master. The intended sufficient contract is defensible, especially with the finite “other optional refinement” condition at `:128`, but the partition should be explicit. Say that callbacks already violating an old lazy cut re-enforce an old cut without generating fresh cuts, or explicitly count every new cut generated there under the finite optional-refinement allowance. State that each intervening old-cut resolution episode finishes in finite time. Merely excluding points from the packing count must not appear to imply a bound on cuts generated at them. This is a minor clarification of an already strongly conditional theorem, not a request to prove an undocumented property of the actual solver or to alter it.

3. **State the common compact domain in the value-convergence proposition.** `sections/separation-theory.tex:114` starts with “a sequence of compact lifted candidates.” Compactness is a property of a set, and individual candidate points are trivially compact; the proof at `:117` requires all candidates to lie in one fixed compact lifted domain. Replace this with “a sequence of lifted candidates in the common compact domain defined above” (or equivalent). The model and scaled-box assumptions already supply the intended domain, so this is a statement-level precision fix, not a missing substantive model restriction.

## Proof reconstruction and scientific assessment

- **Perspective identity and zero weights:** `model-and-cuts.tex:38`–`:48` is correct. The positive-weight derivative yields `(a_z,b_z)` and homogeneity cancels the tangent constant. Boundedness of g on C gives continuity at the unique zero-weight point. The inactive-origin convention is explicitly distinguished from the closure of an empty positive-weight feasible lift; the latter is indeed empty. Positive constant infeasible rows can therefore deactivate a term without excluding other alternatives.
- **Separate-disjunction hull:** `model-and-cuts.tex:50`–`:56` gives both inclusions. Grouping a finite convex combination by alternative uses convexity correctly, and all-empty alternatives produce empty sets on both sides. The all-tangents statement is restricted to continuous term hulls, with original integer coordinates and global/logic coupling treated separately. Sparse-copy extension is valid because omitted coordinates have only their common box restrictions inside that disjunction.
- **Epigraph compactness:** `model-and-cuts.tex:65`–`:78` correctly distinguishes bounded original coordinates from an upper-bounded artificial epigraph. The tangent minimum is a valid lower bound and the gradient-diameter construction is a valid upper bound. Optimal lifts are preserved because the artificial variable has no other role. The prototype's lack of automatic epigraph upper bounds is prominently acknowledged.
- **ESH and ECP margins:** `separation-theory.tex:13`–`:35` has the correct sign in the anchor supporting inequality. The two inequalities `a_z^T(p-anchor) >= delta/s` and `v <= LD(1-s)` yield the stated positive uniform margin. Gradient and perspective-normal bounds follow directly. The use of the joint `(x,y)` box for global nonlinear rows is consistently carried into the packing proof.
- **Integral and fractional packing:** Each row-specific rejected subsequence has a positive pairwise distance because later candidates satisfy the older cut. Finitely many rows and compactness then bound the total fresh rejections. Term activation cancels big-M in the integral proof. Weighted fractional margins and bounded transformed normals correctly avoid a denominator-dependent distance bound; that proof is appropriately confined to hull separation.
- **Skipping:** The residual-calibrated omission test follows from `r <= lambda U_r`. The actual fixed-cutoff guarantee is the maximum of the checked unscaled tolerance and skipped-row bound, not their sum. The strict positive cutoff also supplies a uniform transformed margin for retained-cut termination. The text appropriately does not equate a capped or stalled LP phase with a successful residual test.
- **Repair and objective inference:** The convex combination in the repair proposition satisfies each nonlinear row, preserves affine rows, and gives the stated displacement. Weighting and the triangle inequality yield the two hull-distance bounds. The square-root example obeys all stated term assumptions and disproves an O(epsilon) objective-error inference after adding a global constraint. It does not accidentally claim failure of every error bound or of limiting value convergence.
- **Value convergence:** With the common compact domain made explicit as requested above, continuity of weighted residuals and optimality up to o(1) give the asserted subsequence proof. Integer coordinates retain integrality at limits. If the target set is empty, such a sequence cannot exist. No finite gap-closure conclusion is taken from this limiting proposition.
- **Callbacks:** The theorem correctly requires complete finite MILP solves, original-model acceptance tests, independently validated exact incumbents, and bounded optional cut work. It explicitly does not certify the frozen callback implementation. Clarifying the accounting of old-cut-infeasible callbacks will make the finite-additions argument unambiguous.
- **Approximate roots and arithmetic:** Keeping the full function offset preserves a true tangent at a nonboundary point. The one-dimensional counterexample has the correct artificial bound `x <= 1/2` and valid tangent bound `x <= 5/4`. The certified coefficient perturbation calculation loses E once in the computed residual and E again in the validity relaxation, hence the `kappa-2E-rho` margin is correct. The coordinatewise sufficient bound for E is valid. The text does not claim ordinary solver output provides such enclosures.
- **Representation invariance:** The chain rule at an exact common root multiplies both affine coefficients by positive `phi'(0)`, so the original and lifted halfspaces agree. The same-point ECP weakening follows from the tangent inequality for phi at v evaluated at zero. The same-anchor and exact-root qualifications are necessary and present.
- **Scalar recurrence:** The anchor tangent is redundant for a >= 2. Each new point tangent supplies an upper bound strictly between one and the current master optimum, making older scalar upper bounds redundant. The decrement is strictly less than 1/a, proving the cut-count lower bound. Monotonic convergence and the fixed-a Newton quadratic expansion are correct. The manuscript separates iteration count from oracle runtime and row-residual accuracy from geometric accuracy.
- **Cut non-dominance:** The off-center disk boundary formula and both witnesses are correct. The witnesses compare outer halfspaces, not feasible disk points. The centered ellipsoid calculation gives dominance only in the declared centered case. Setting the perspective weight to one correctly carries the counterexample into the perspective formulation.
