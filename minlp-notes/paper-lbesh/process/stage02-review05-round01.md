## Actionable minor findings

1. **State common compact containment precisely.** Location: `sections/separation-theory.tex:114`, the first sentence of Proposition `prop:value`: “a sequence of compact lifted candidates” is not standard mathematical wording and, read literally, assigns compactness to individual points. Replace with “a sequence of lifted candidates contained in one compact set” (or explicitly say the common compact lifted domain defined above). The proof uses a convergent subsequence from every subsequence, which requires common compact containment. The intended condition is already supported by the surrounding model assumptions, so this is a statement clarification rather than a gap in the intended result.

2. **Distinguish the exact row boundary from the returned exterior endpoint.** Location: `sections/algorithm-and-implementation.tex:20`, sentence “Its boundary point belongs to the boundary of that row's sublevel set”. This is inside the implementation description immediately after the routine says it linearizes at an exterior bracket endpoint. That returned point is generally outside the row's sublevel set, not on its boundary. Say “The exact segment intersection lies on the boundary of that row's sublevel set ...; the implemented bisection returns an approximate point on the exterior side.” Preserve the useful explanation that a row boundary need not be feasible for the whole term. The full tangent constant already makes the implemented cut valid under the idealized exact-evaluation assumptions.

3. **Make the counterexample's constant positive.** Location: `sections/separation-theory.tex:110`, “For every fixed $K$ ... $0<\eps<1/K^2$”. Write “For every fixed $K>0$”. As written, $K=0$ makes the displayed threshold undefined. This is a minor quantifier correction; the square-root counterexample is otherwise correct.

## Independent mathematical assessment

- **Bounded perspective and zero weights.** The finite-box assumptions ensure bounded row values, hence continuity at the inactive origin. The tangent supremum identity holds even for a lower-dimensional box because the function and ambient gradient are defined on a neighborhood and the tangent at the evaluated point attains equality. The manuscript correctly distinguishes the closure of a nonempty term's feasible lift from the intentionally added inactive origin for an empty term. A positive constant term row forces its weight to zero, while a globally positive constant row excludes the global model.
- **Separate hull and sparse copies.** Grouping a finite convex combination by alternative proves the reverse inclusion without a hidden nonemptiness assumption. The stated sparse-copy extension is valid because omitted coordinates have no term constraint beyond their box and the weights sum to one. The hull assertion is about continuous term sets and explicitly does not convexify the original integer coordinates or all interdisjunction interactions.
- **Epigraph boundedness.** The tangent lower bound and gradient-based upper bound preserve the lift $t=f(x)$ when the epigraph has no other role. The numerical implementation does not automatically satisfy this bounded-domain hypothesis, and the manuscript says so. A bounded LP optimum alone is correctly not treated as compactness of arbitrary callback candidates.
- **Margin and packing.** The anchor supporting inequality gives $a_z^T(p-\bar x)\ge\delta/s$; combining $v\le LD(1-s)$ yields the stated ESH margin. The rowwise distance bounds require old-cut feasibility and a fixed positive anchor margin, both stated. At fractional weights, using $(\nu,\lambda)$ rather than the normalized point gives a bounded coefficient norm independent of the denominator. The positive-weight omission rule and the weaker fixed-cutoff bound follow directly. Zero-weight terms and constant rows do not create an infinite rejection sequence.
- **Geometric repair and objective claims.** Convex interpolation cancels the maximum row violation against the common negative anchor margin. The weighted repair estimate sums distances to one disjunction's hull only, as claimed. The intersection example has a unique exact feasible point and objective error $\sqrt\eps$, so it rules out a uniform linear residual-to-objective bound under merely separate-term margins. The value convergence proof is sound under common compact containment and asymptotically optimal valid relaxations; it does not imply finite gap closure.
- **Single-tree contract and arithmetic.** The conditional theorem explicitly requires resolution of old-cut repetitions and finite optional refinement. Its objective conclusion additionally requires an exactly feasible incumbent and a valid lower bound. The computed-cut relaxation loses at most $2E$ in generating margin and an additional stored-cut tolerance $\rho$; the signs and distance bound are correct.
- **Representation and explicit examples.** A monotone convex composition preserves row signs, and its positive boundary derivative scales the full tangent/perspective coefficients. The inequality $\phi(v)\le v\phi'(v)$ follows from the supporting inequality for $\phi$ at $v$ evaluated at zero. The scalar master really takes the new tangent bound because it lies strictly between one and its previous bound; this proves the recurrence, the strict transient lower bound, and convergence. The centered ellipsoid formulas and off-center disk witnesses are correct and establish only the claimed conditional dominance/non-dominance.
