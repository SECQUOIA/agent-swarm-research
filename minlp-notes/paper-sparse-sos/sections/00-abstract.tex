\begin{abstract}
Sparse moment-sum-of-squares hierarchies exchange finitely many separator
moments between the bags of a junction tree. We quantify how this restricted
information affects convergence. For box problems, common polynomial
kernels and a density correction turn every feasible moment family into
probability laws with exactly consistent separator marginals. For the
ordinary sparse quadratic module, the order-$r$ gap is bounded by the sum
of the absolute nonconstant local Chebyshev coefficients times
$O(\log^3 r/r^2)$, with constants depending on width and local degree and
no additional factor in the number of bags. For the full sparse
preordering, we give an explicit rounding proof of the inverse-square rate
previously stated in lecture slides. A fixed quadratic with two bags has a
preordering gap of exact order $r^{-2}$ even with exact local measures,
showing a limitation caused solely by finite separator information.

We also study large private continuous vectors entering each bag objective
as a convex quadratic. Their degree remains two while the shared degree
grows; at fixed shared width, SDP block sizes are polynomial in private
dimension and relaxation order. Nonempty fixed private polytopes give
$O(r^{-2})$ gaps. Affine fibers with a fixed constraint matrix and complete
recourse give the sharp general rate $O(1/r)$. The inverse-square bound
returns when projected optimal multipliers have summable weighted
Chebyshev coefficients, or each component is a Lipschitz function of its
corresponding shared coordinate.
Extensions cover polynomial constraints under a global error bound,
finite states with common continuous boxes, and exact rational box
certificates with positive slack and size polynomial in expanded SDP
dimensions and rational input length.
\end{abstract}
