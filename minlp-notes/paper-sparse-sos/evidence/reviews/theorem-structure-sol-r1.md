## Independent reconstruction of the theorem chain

### 1. Scalar cones, normalization, and finite SDP certificates

`02-setting.tex:47–66,189–208,259–310` supplies the correct sparse coefficient quotient, compactness, and Slater arguments. At a leaf, a polynomial that cancels against the other bags can contain no coordinate outside the parent separator. Repeating this reduction preserves the degree and identifies every zero decomposition with signed edge-separator polynomials. This is what makes weak duality and the global sparse certificate definition agree with the finite conic dual; equality of raw local expectations alone would not suffice.

For compactness, singleton box tests give `0 <= L(x^(2 alpha)) <= 1` for `|alpha|<=r`. Every exponent through degree `2r` splits into two exponents through degree `r`; moment Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the remaining moments. Closed PSD and equality constraints then give a compact feasible set. Product arcsine moments satisfy the separator equalities and make every retained moment/localizing matrix positive definite. Thus finite SDP Slater gives attained real box certificates at the finite moment optimum. This does not assert an exact polynomial separator at the true optimum of an infinite local-measure problem.

The even-cap interval theorem is stated and proved with the needed odd-degree padding at `02-setting.tex:319–337`. Zeros are allowed; an odd polynomial of degree `2j-1` uses an ordinary interval certificate through degree `2j`. This extra degree is accounted for rather than silently identifying positivity degree with certificate degree.

### 2. Full preordering and exact separator laws

The source/output normalization identity, rather than approximate preservation of moments, gives the exact separator laws in `03-kernels.tex:135–194`. A univariate Jackson factor has an interval certificate through degree `2(m-1)`. Integrating an omitted output coordinate replaces its source factor by the polynomial one. The autocorrelation identity gives `1-gamma_1=3/(2m^2+1)`; integrating `1-cos(kt)<=k^2(1-cos t)` gives the multiplier bound even beyond kernel support. ### 3. Ordinary-module signed correction

I checked `04-ordinary.tex:123–231,326–470,506–551` against `sparse-putinar-kernel.md`, `sparse-putinar-exact-consistency.md:66–288`, and `signed-kernel-final-audit.md` without using their acceptance verdicts as premises.

Evenness of `N` makes the geometric multiplier globally SOS. The squared Fejér source kernel is therefore globally SOS for each fixed output, with source degree `D=2(s-1)(N+1)`. Its mass is `omega=1-z_s^(N+1)` and its residual is interval nonnegative, bounded by `delta`. Adding that residual gives a polynomial identity of mass one. The resulting tensor densities have mass one and exactly matching separator marginals before they are made nonnegative.

The important residual estimate does not assume positivity of products of interval-positive polynomials. For a residual subset of size `j`, write `G` for the product of the other SOS factors and `H` for the product of the residuals. Telescope `delta^(2j)-H^2`. Each summand contains only one interval certificate for `delta^2-varrho_i^2`; every other multiplier is SOS. After multiplication by `G`, the maximum representation degree is `(v+j)D<=2r`. The square roots in `G(a+cH)^2` have degree at most `(v+j)D/2<=r`. Hence weighted Cauchy–Schwarz proves

`|L(GH)| <= delta^j L(G) <= delta^j Lambda^(v-j)`.

The last inequality uses a coefficient estimate only on `G`, whose degree is at most `(v-j)D<=r`. The objective error is also evaluated only after its combined polynomial has degree at most `vD<=r`; its intermediate univariate terms are allowed to have higher degree because they are never separately passed to `L`. These degree ranges are not interchangeable.

Zero- and one-residual terms are nonnegative under the ordinary module. Summing the higher terms gives `hbar_b>=-Delta_w`. The same scalar correction in every bag preserves every separator marginal. The objective correction identity compares the corrected law with the reference probability law, not with a signed probability law. It costs at most `Delta_w osc(f_b)`. ### 6. Recourse duality and projected regularity
