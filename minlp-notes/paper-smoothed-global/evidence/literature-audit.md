## Finding

The manuscript has a defensible, narrow contribution: it composes a base-instance-selected **finite rational perturbation law**, quantitative growth and algebraic section-count bounds, and exact search on the ordinary event. In routes that use a fallback, a deterministic exact fallback preserves correctness on the remaining draws; integer-lattice and strong-field routes also include direct exact procedures. The theorems state distinct aligned, ambient, sparse, constrained, recourse, and integer regimes with their own input, arithmetic, and output contracts. The main novelty claim belongs to these stated compositions and bounds, not to smoothed global optimization, exact polynomial optimization, finite tie isolation, sparse optimization, or treewidth methods in general.

The final source audit does not identify a literature gap that invalidates that scoped claim. The unresolved full-text gaps below are recorded as gaps. The manuscript either cites those works only as general background, gives no pinpoint dependent on an unavailable text, or now supplies the needed proof internally. There is no basis here for “first” or priority language.

## Local companion work: exact overlap and remaining contribution

The three local companions are direct antecedents, not external priority claims. The final bibliography identifies them by exact title as unpublished, undated manuscripts, without invented authorship. The final introduction and limitations now credit the following overlap.

### Exact arithmetic in polynomial optimization

The companion already proves a full-point cubic recourse result (`thm:recourse-cubic`): degree-three objectives on product boxes, convex residual fibers, and a core-coordinate curvature bound admit a base-computable finite uniform core-noise grid fixed before sampling. Every draw and every requested precision (q) are covered; the selector is the lexicographically least optimal core followed by the least-norm point in that core’s optimal residual fiber. No residual curvature lower bound, joint convexifier, or unique residual optimizer is assumed. Its one-draw factor applies uniformly over all (q). Do **not** claim a new cubic core-only full-point time bound, a new canonical selector, or a new finite law independent of requested (q).

The companion also has a full-point theorem when a core-supported convexifier is supplied (`thm:recourse-convexified`). Under (H_{RR}\succeq\mu I), a rank-at-most-core-dimension Schur-complement convexifier exists with scale (M+M^2/\mu). The present direct recourse count retains (L/\sigma) rather than this potentially larger derived convexifier scale. That is a rate distinction, not a claim that no earlier full-point method exists. The companion’s quartic conditional obstruction is tied to Square Root Sum/PosSLP; it does not extend to cubics, for which the positive theorem above is explicit. It also does not defeat the distinct supplied-convexifier result.

Output contracts differ: the companion returns a Cauchy name for a fixed selected point and sampled optimum value; this manuscript’s ordinary branch returns an implicit patch descriptor plus a separate global proof record. The descriptor alone is not a global certificate. The manuscript’s general output does not promise a canonical optimizer on ordinary draws; its algebraic fallback uses the lexicographically least global minimizer. These interface differences do not by themselves establish a new optimization-time theorem.

### Decomposition-aware deterministic global optimization

This companion already supplies curvature-corrected coordinate grids, min-marginal filtering, and recomputable path certificates. Its point-growth parameter κ and weighted-growth parameter ̅κ are different: point growth implies weighted growth with ̅κ ≤ κ, but not conversely. Its approximation theorem has cost (f(p,\bar\kappa)(I+q+1)^5), with
\[
f(p,t)=c_0(c_1p\sqrt t)^p t(1+\log_2 t)^2.
\]
The constants are not algorithm inputs; certificate-driven trials stop without knowing them. Under point growth and a unique minimizer a separate exact theorem gives (f_1(p,\kappa)(I+1)^{C_1}).

The retained-node lower bound √((n−2)κ)+1 applies to the specified filtered common uniform-grid method, not to all discretizations or global optimization algorithms. Under rETH the companion rules out (o(p)) in the κ exponent in its stated product-time model; it does not prove a matching (p/2+O(1)) lower bound and does not cover every smaller positive linear exponent. The manuscript credits these deterministic antecedents and does not transfer their lower bound to a different sparse or smoothed model.

### Sparse indicator quadratics

This companion perturbs only the indicator penalties λ_i on a finite equally spaced grid, with at least (2n) values. At fixed treewidth it solves each draw exactly using message dictionaries. The stated expected bit work is polynomial in numerical quantities such as (C,H,\mu^{-1},R,\sigma^{-1}), not in their binary lengths. Its hardness corollary yields (NP\subseteq ZPP) only under the stated every-draw exact-value and expected-bit-work model, for the fixed matrix/noise family constructed there. It is not a lower bound for the manuscript’s broader linear-objective perturbation law. The manuscript now credits the finite-noise/treewidth overlap and retains the numerical-dependence boundary.

## Contribution and use recommendations

Describe the contribution as the theorem-specific composition of (i) a finite rational law selected from the unperturbed instance, (ii) quantitative growth and algebraic counting that bound the ordinary search, and (iii) a deterministic exact fallback preserving correctness for every perturbation atom. Keep each regime’s structural assumptions, bit model, and output representation with its statement. State rational point/value output only in the quadratic/polyhedral classes that provide it; for general polynomial and graph classes retain their declared implicit, charted, algebraic, or component outputs. Do not claim the fallback itself is a polynomial-time construction where only polynomial-bit witnesses plus exact checking are proved.

The results can be presented as theoretical exact search/certification tools and as a framework for using a finite randomized perturbation to make a hard global search typically smaller while retaining a complete certificate path. They do not establish practical speedups, a universal smoothing principle, or an exogenous-noise model. Keep the paper’s stated regret and modeling consequences as formal consequences of its assumptions, not as empirical recommendations.
