**NEW-1: affine margins.** In the initial Appendix F:945–952, the proof chooses an arbitrary cube vertex with \(p\le0\) and asserts that moving toward it coordinate by coordinate decreases \(p\) throughout. That assertion is false. Opus's example \(p(x)=x_1-2x_2+1/2\), \(x=(0,0)\), and target \((1,1)\) demonstrates it: moving coordinate 1 first increases \(p\), and the path first reaches zero after length \(7/4\), exceeding \(p(x)/\pi_{\min}=1/2\).

The lemma at F:937–941 is nevertheless true with its stated constant. The complete repair is as follows. In the nonempty-zero-set branch, a nonzero affine \(p\) cannot have \(\pi=0\). Define \(\pi_{\min}\) only in that branch, as the least nonzero \(|\pi_i|\), which is at least \(2^{-H_0}\). If \(p(x)=0\), the distance assertion is immediate. If \(p(x)>0\), choose the coordinatewise minimizer
\[
v_i=0\ (\pi_i>0),\qquad v_i=1\ (\pi_i<0),\qquad v_i=x_i\ (\pi_i=0).
\]
This point minimizes \(p\) on the cube and has \(p(v)\le0\). It need not be a vertex when a zero-slope coordinate is left at \(x_i\). Move only the nonzero-slope coordinates toward \(v\), one at a time, stopping at the first zero. Every nonconstant leg decreases \(p\) at rate \(|\pi_i|\ge\pi_{\min}\). If the traversed length is \(s\), then \(p(x)\ge\pi_{\min}s\), and Euclidean distance to the zero set is at most \(s\). Thus \(|p(x)|\ge2^{-H_0}\operatorname{dist}(x,Z_p)\). Apply the same argument to \(-p\) when \(p(x)<0\).

If \(Z_p\) is empty, connectedness makes \(p\) have one sign, and the minimum of its absolute value is attained at a cube vertex. That nonzero rational has denominator dividing a product of at most \(q+1\) base denominators, so its magnitude is at least \(2^{-(q+1)H_0}\). This branch covers every nonzero constant polynomial and \(q=0\).

The actual repaired Appendix F:945–957, hash `8c2208bc...`, contains precisely these branches and the coordinatewise minimizer construction. I verified its proof, not just the root's proposed wording. NEW-1 is resolved in that observed state.

Its application to `cor:int:bilinear` is also valid. The boundary proof chooses \(\delta\le1/4\) at initial F:894. At distance \(\delta/2\) from a nonempty chart zero set, the distance branch dominates \(2^{-(k+1)H_0}\delta/2\); for an empty zero set the vertex bound also dominates it, since \(\delta/2\le1\) and the face dimension is at most \(k\). The repaired proof therefore restores the polynomial-length \(\mu_0\) used at initial F:957–967 and in bilinear TU recourse. No threshold, sampler, theorem statement or work factor needs to change. My earlier reports' affirmative disposition of the arbitrary-vertex argument was incorrect; this report supersedes that local proof assessment.

**NEW-2: common aligned marginal versus row-specific lattice scales.** The perturbation-model definition, Section 02:110–123, defines aligned noise using one marginal law \(\mathcal D\). Section 08:656 calls the lattice theorem an instance of that definition, but 08:676–682 supplies independent laws \(U_{\sigma_i,M}\) with row-specific positive scales. This is a literal contract mismatch. The theorem and its actual proof are internally correct: the local-count theorem permits independent unequal marginals at 03:104–114, and the level-count corollary expressly permits \(U_{\sigma_i,M}\) at 03:151–160. Appendix F:1134–1167 uses those laws and one common \(M\).

The smallest robust repair is to name this as a row-scaled extension of the aligned model, without enlarging every theorem's hypotheses. For example, add after the perturbation-model definition: “The integer-lattice theorem also uses a row-scaled aligned model: the coordinates of \(\xi\) are independent with laws \(U_{\sigma_i,M}\), where the supplied positive scales may differ and one common resolution \(M\) is chosen before sampling. The other theorems use the common scale stated in their hypotheses.” Qualify 08:656 to refer to that extension. The base-chosen-law convention should say that it chooses one common \(M\) in this extension; each row uses its supplied scale. The universal-resolution wording can likewise call \(U_{1,M}\) the common normalized grid, rescaled by the scale or row scales stated in the theorem.

The common \(M\) is essential, not optional. The original sampled values have denominator dividing \(D_0(M-1)\); independently selected resolutions would generally replace this factor by a product or least common multiple. The proof does not use a lattice for the auxiliary squared-noise values. All \(\sigma_i\) are base rational data included in \(Q_{\rm den}\) and \(I\); each row still uses exactly \(\log_2M\) random bits and the polynomial-bit contract is unchanged. Do not attempt to remove the mismatch by rescaling \(T\): unequal row scaling generally changes the concave term \(-\alpha\|Tx\|^2/2\).

Section 02:483–484 already gives the correct row-specific grid regret bound \(\sum_i\sigma_i\omega_X(T_{i\cdot})\). No broader row-specific Gaussian or ambient theorem is needed or inferred. With the explicit extension, NEW-2 is a contract repair and not a new mathematical theorem.

**NEW-6: ambient regret widths.** Confirmed at Section 04:635–640. The first inequality correctly invokes `prop:model:regret`, but the bound \(\bar\sigma\sum_iw_i\) uses \(w_i\) already assigned to auxiliary factor-box widths, most recently at 04:619. Ambient noise needs widths of the original perturbed coordinates, or valid upper bounds from the continuous polyhedral relaxation. This is a false displayed bound in the current notation.

It can fail for actual regret, not only for a loose width estimate. Take
\[
X=[0,1],\qquad F(x)=-x^2+\frac{201}{200}x,\qquad
\sigma=\frac1{100},\qquad \alpha=200,\qquad T=\frac1{10}.
\]
The quadratic matrix is \(A=-2\), and \(A+\alpha T^{\mathsf T}T=0\), so this is a valid supplied convexifying factorization. The original minimum is at \(x=0\). The ambient grid draw \(\gamma=-\sigma\) makes \(x=1\) the sampled minimizer, with original regret \(1/200\). The aligned auxiliary width is \(1001/10000\); the uniform-ambient construction uses \(\Psi=10\) and auxiliary width \(101/1000\). Multiplying either by \(\sigma\) gives less than \(1/200\). The original coordinate width is 1 and the correct support bound is \(\sigma\), which covers that regret.

Replace the ambient sentence by \(\bar\sigma W_{\rm noise}\), referring to the model's definition at 02:479–482, or explicitly by \(\bar\sigma\sum_{j\text{ original perturbed}}(U_j-L_j)\) on a box. For a mixed polytope use coordinate widths of its continuous relaxation as upper bounds. Keep \(\bar\sigma=\sigma\) for the grid and \(\bar\sigma=(b+20)\sigma\) for the Gaussian-like law; the support factor is already correct. The aligned sentence uses the unexpanded range \(u_i-\ell_i\) of \((Tx)_i\), so it is valid for a common support scale; the lattice extension has the weighted row-specific version already in the model. These are law-specific width bounds, not the widths of the auxiliary search box. The general regret proposition, its calibration proof and the optimizer/runtime theorems remain valid.

It retains the full Gaussian-like support factor and the correct aligned sentence. I checked this actual diff against the counterexample and the model proposition. NEW-6 is resolved in those bytes.
