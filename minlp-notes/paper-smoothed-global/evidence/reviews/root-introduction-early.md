# Root early introduction review

This is a review of the current incomplete introduction, not approval of
the final scientific claims. Literature identity and priority review belong
to Luna.

1. The opening sentence about hard instances being degenerate in specified
   ways is too general. The manuscript's explicit examples exhibit ties,
   flat optimal sets and small threshold gaps. Present these as mechanisms
   that the developed analysis handles, rather than a characterization of
   all hard instances or an unsupported explanation of practical speed.
2. The integer lattice theorem is in Section 8 (label thm:int:lowrank), not
   Section 4. Its model is being extended to fixed-degree explicit convex
   piecewise-polynomial unaries; do not freeze the intro to quartic-only
   scope after that extension is incorporated.
3. Independent coefficient intervals give the direct local product count,
   but uniform ambient factor and residual noises are dependent. The
   mechanism paragraph must qualify this simplification and refer to the
   complementary-minor conditional-volume argument for that route. A
   blanket claim of independence after conditioning contradicts Section 4.
4. Original-objective approximation uses the actual support width. For the
   finite Gaussian-like law this is (b+20) sigma, and b depends on the base
   scale choices. The calibration cannot simply be stated as sigma
   proportional to epsilon for all routes. Give the certified actual-width
   calibration and its necessary precision/scale choice, with exactly
   feasible algebraic chart lifts where rational full feasibility fails.
5. Contributions must be tied to the completed Luna source comparison;
   the finite-law model, classical isolation, rational univariate
   representations and percolation are not by themselves originality
   claims. Name the actual certificates, bounds, structural compositions,
   extensions and restrictions established here, and identify precise
   overlap with the related unpublished manuscripts.
6. The actual finite-law Gaussian and general nonlinear flow/TU precision
   contracts and output evaluation bounds must be reflected in the final
   table. In particular, a parameter-dependent algebraic core evaluation
   or fallback is not uniformly polynomial per draw merely because its
   expected contribution is polynomial.

No literature search, experiment rerun, project-wide check or TeX edit was
performed for this report.
