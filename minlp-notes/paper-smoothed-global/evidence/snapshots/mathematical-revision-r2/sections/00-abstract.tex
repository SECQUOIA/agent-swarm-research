\begin{abstract}
We study exact global optimization of nonconvex quadratic and fixed-degree
polynomial problems with continuous and integer variables after one linear
perturbation from a finite rational law computed before sampling. The
algorithms return an exact global optimizer on every draw and never
resample. Their expected bit work is polynomial in the input length and
explicit curvature, width, and noise ratios for fixed structural parameters.
For quadratic programs with few negative directions, aligned noise for
continuous problems and Gaussian-like noise for mixed problems give FPT
bounds jointly in negative inertia, integer dimension where applicable,
and numerical ratios; uniform ambient
noise gives a dimension-dependent exponent. Sparse polynomial interactions
admit polynomial bounds for each fixed treewidth, including graph, simplex,
and order constraints. A small core with tractable recourse gives FPT bounds
jointly in core size and numerical ratios. Core-only noise suffices for
uniformly strongly convex continuous recourse and structured convex integer
recourse, with distinct sampling-precision bounds. Strong noise gives a
separate component algorithm without a width parameter. The proofs combine
curvature-corrected refinement, noise-dependent counting, and exact
certificates; most routes use a rare same-draw fallback, while lattice and
component methods finish directly. We prove limits of the sparse retention
rule, a hardness implication for arbitrary sparse affine constraints, and
arithmetic implications for convex quartic residual point evaluation under
core-only noise. Sampled solutions give certified original-objective
approximations with the numerical factors retained. Practical performance
is unestablished.
\end{abstract}
