\begin{abstract}
We study exact global optimization of nonconvex quadratic and fixed-degree
polynomial problems with continuous and integer variables when the linear
part of the objective is perturbed once by a random vector from a finite
rational law computed from the instance. The algorithms return an exact
global optimizer of every sampled instance, never resample, and have
expected bit complexity polynomial in the input length and in explicit
curvature-to-noise ratios for fixed structural parameters. Four structures
give such bounds. For quadratic and mixed-integer quadratic programs with few
negative curvature directions, the bound is fixed-parameter tractable in the
negative inertia and the integer dimension under Gaussian-like noise on all
coefficients, and for continuous problems also under noise aligned with
those directions, but it has a dimension-dependent exponent under uniform
noise. For polynomials whose interactions have bounded treewidth, it
is polynomial for each fixed width, also under simplex, order, and graph
constraints. For a small continuous core whose fixing leaves tractable
continuous or integer recourse, it is fixed-parameter tractable in the core
size, and noise on the core alone suffices for strongly convex continuous
recourse and for convex integer flow and totally unimodular recourse. Under
strong noise, random components are solved exactly without any width
parameter. The common mechanism is curvature-corrected refinement, counting
under independent noise, exact closure certificates, and an exact fallback
on the same draw that the finite law makes rare. We also prove limits: the
width exponent of the sparse retention rule is necessary, objective noise
cannot handle arbitrary sparse constraints unless
$\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{ZPP}$, and efficient residual point evaluation
under core-only noise with merely convex recourse would give Las Vegas
algorithms for Square Root Sum and PosSLP. Sampled solutions certify approximate solutions of the
original instance; no practical speedup is claimed.
\end{abstract}
