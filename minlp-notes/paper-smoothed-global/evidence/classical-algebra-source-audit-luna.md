## Renegar: block-sensitive quantifier elimination

**Key:** `renegar1992-on-the-computational-complexity-and`. The identity is James Renegar, “On the Computational Complexity and Geometry of the First-Order Theory of the Reals. Part III: Quantifier Elimination,” *Journal of Symbolic Computation* 13(3), 329–352 (1992), DOI [10.1016/S0747-7171(10)80005-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0747-7171(10)80005-7). The local primary copy is `literature/papers/renegar1992-on-the-computational-complexity-and/original.pdf`; its metadata matches the article and marks it read.

**Directly checked locator and contract:** Theorem 1.1, printed p. 330. It takes a first-order formula with `ω` quantified blocks of sizes `n_k`, `ℓ` free variables, `m` atomic polynomial predicates of degree at most `d ≥ 2`, and outputs an equivalent disjunction of conjunctions of polynomial sign conditions. The number of conjunctions, atoms per conjunction, and output degrees are bounded by `(md)^{2^{O(ω)} ℓ ∏_k n_k}`. For integer input coefficients of bit length `L`, output coefficient lengths are bounded by `(L+1)` times that format factor, and sequential bit work is bounded by `L log L log log L` times the same factor, in addition to `(md)^{O(ℓ+Σ_k n_k)}` calls to the input Boolean predicate. The real-number-model format bound permits arbitrary real coefficients. The theorem’s algorithm is constructive and uses no divisions.

This is the right part of Renegar’s series and it supports the displayed format/height/work statement in Appendix A and the two-block section-count use in Section 03. The manuscript’s positive-block, positive-free-variable, `m ≥ 1`, `d ≥ 2` formulation is compatible with the theorem. For the section-count application, fixing the number of blocks makes the hidden format exponent an absolute constant; the resulting count is independent of coefficient heights, while the bit-work claim still depends on the coefficient lengths as stated.

**Limit:** The printed theorem uses `O(ω)` in the exponent and does not give a numerical exponent constant. Section 03:457–465 accurately treats `a_R` as one fixed admissible choice and says its value is not extracted. This audit verifies the asymptotic block-sensitive contract; it does not certify a numerical value for `a_R`. Do not describe such a value as extracted from Renegar.

## Basu–Pollack–Roy: Bézout count and univariate operations

**Key:** `basu2006-algorithms-in-real-algebraic-geometry`. The identity is Saugata Basu, Richard Pollack, and Marie-Françoise Roy, *Algorithms in Real Algebraic Geometry*, 2nd ed., Algorithms and Computation in Mathematics 10, Springer (2006), DOI [10.1007/3-540-33099-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-33099-2). Springer confirms the second edition and bibliographic data. The primary text is the authors’ posted second-edition version `book-bpr-posted1` (11/03/06): [author-hosted PDF](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/bpr-posted1.pdf), [author version page](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/bpr-posted1.html). The local package `literature/papers/basu2006-algorithms-in-real-algebraic-geometry/` contains that PDF and is marked read. Its PDF text layer is garbled, so I checked the locators below against page images.

**Exact root-count substitute for Fulton:** Theorem 4.106, “Weak Bézout,” printed p. 165, states that for `P_1,...,P_k ∈ C[X_1,...,X_k]` of degrees `d_1,...,d_k`, the number of nonsingular common zeros is at most `∏ d_i`. Proposition 4.105, printed p. 164, gives the corresponding projective statement. Theorem 4.106 directly supports the `D^k` count in `lem:sp:bezout` and the use in Appendix A for nonsingular stationary points. It does not require the broader sum-of-degrees-of-irreducible-components statement attributed to refined Bézout.

**Univariate contract:** The local primary text directly supports the operations cited in Appendices A and F: Algorithm 10.1 on printed p. 366 computes a gcd and gcd-free part using extended signed subresultants; p. 367 gives its arithmetic and integer-coefficient bit-size bounds. Chapter 8, especially pp. 326 and 330–332, supplies subresultant/resultant arithmetic and coefficient-size bounds. Proposition 10.20 and Corollary 10.22 on pp. 368–369 give an effective root-separation lower bound for integer polynomials in terms of degree and coefficient bitsize. Algorithm 10.4 on p. 383 returns isolating lists of rational intervals for real roots and states a polynomial binary-complexity bound. Algorithm 10.6 on p. 390 determines the sign of another integer polynomial at an isolated real root, again with a polynomial binary bound. Exact quotients and modular inverses can be obtained through the same gcd/Bezout route once the relevant coprimality is established; make that reduction explicit if the broad list at F:10 is retained. Refinement to `q` bits can be done by exact bisection with Sturm/root-count tests; the number of bisections is charged to `q`. The source uses asymptotic O-bounds; they are not extracted constants for a particular implementation.

Appendix F also cites BPR at F:103 for algebraic closedness of the Puiseux-series field; the direct locator for that use is recorded in the completion note below.

## Mehlhorn–Sagraloff–Wang: integer-polynomial root isolation and refinement

**Key:** `mehlhorn2015-from-approximate-factorization-to-root`. The identity is Kurt Mehlhorn, Michael Sagraloff, and Pengming Wang, “From Approximate Factorization to Root Isolation with Application to Cylindrical Algebraic Decomposition,” *Journal of Symbolic Computation* 66, 34–69 (2015), DOI [10.1016/j.jsc.2014.02.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2014.02.001). The checked primary text is [arXiv:1301.4870v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.4870v2), also stored as `literature/papers/mehlhorn2015-from-approximate-factorization-to-root/original.pdf`; the local metadata identifies the journal article but still marks the package unread. The locators below are arXiv PDF pages, not journal pagination.

Theorem 5, arXiv pp. 19–20, treats integer polynomials of degree `n` and coefficient magnitude below `2^τ`. It computes isolating **complex disks** for all roots, with multiplicities, in `Õ(n^3+n^2τ)` bit operations and refines them to radius below `2^-κ` in `Õ(n^3+n^2τ+nκ)` bit operations. Its proof first computes the squarefree part. Theorem 3 on p. 18 is the broader coefficient-oracle result and assumes the number of distinct roots is supplied; the current integer-polynomial use can rely on Theorem 5 instead.

**Scope correction for the draft:** Mehlhorn et al. directly support integer-input disk isolation/refinement, including multiple roots. Their output is not an exact rational interval list and Theorem 5 does not supply the manuscript’s gcd/resultant/modular-inverse or sign-at-real-root contracts. BPR is the direct source for those exact real-univariate operations and for the explicit separation bound. The paired BPR/Mehlhorn citations in F:10 and F:622–626 should be read or split that way. The tilde over O hides logarithmic factors; do not infer explicit constants from it.

## Completion note: BPR Puiseux field and constant-term limit in Appendix F

I directly checked the second-edition primary PDF at the additional live citation F:103–110. Section 2.6 defines the field of Puiseux series with coefficients in a field `K` as formal Laurent series with rational exponents, equivalently a union over denominators of Laurent-series fields; this matches the manuscript’s `P = ⋃_{m≥1} Q̄((ε^{1/m}))`. On printed p. 83, Theorem 2.91 says that `R⟨ε⟩` is real closed when `R` is real closed, and Theorem 2.92 says that `C⟨ε⟩` is algebraically closed when `C` is algebraically closed of characteristic zero. Thus Theorem 2.92 applies with `C = Q̄`, justifying the algebraically closed field used for simultaneous triangularization and eigenvalues in F:116–125.

On printed p. 89, Proposition 2.99 identifies the nonnegative-order elements as the valuation ring of bounded Puiseux series. Notation 2.100 defines `lim_ε` on that ring as the homomorphism that sends a series to its constant coefficient. This directly supports taking the `ε^0` coefficients as limits of bounded roots in F:128–135. These are direct primary-source matches; no theorem number or stronger field claim is inferred.
