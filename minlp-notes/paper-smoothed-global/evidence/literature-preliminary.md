## Defensible novelty boundary for the authors

Do not claim “first smoothed global optimization,” “noise makes exact global optimization easy,” “first exact sparse polynomial optimization,” or a general polynomial region-count theorem. The literature already contains smoothed global optimization for structured quasi-concave problems, finite-set isolation/Pareto analyses, deterministic exact sparse-box classes, and exact fixed-dimension IQP/MIQP results. The ReLU hardness result also rules out an unqualified universal smoothing claim.

The narrow contribution supported by the present comparison is the **specific composition** in the draft: a base-instance-selected finite rational perturbation law; quantitative global-growth tails and semialgebraic finite-grid counting; exact optimization on every draw through a same-draw deterministic fallback; and the stated aligned, ambient, sparse, or recourse regimes. State each theorem’s assumptions and output model. The fallback returns the declared rational, implicit-patch, charted, algebraic, or component representation for that class; rational point/value output is limited to the classes that state it, including the quadratic results. The fixed law, dimension/width factors, and output contract are part of the claim. This bounded search does not establish publication priority.

### Exact arithmetic in polynomial optimization

The companion’s Section 10 already proves two full-point results that constrain novelty. Theorem `thm:recourse-cubic` gives, for degree-three polynomials on product boxes with convex residual fibers and core-coordinate curvature bound `Lambda`, a base-computable finite uniform core-noise grid fixed before sampling. For every draw and every requested `q`, it returns a rational point within `2^-q` of a canonical selected optimizer, at expected cost `k^{O(k)}(1+Lambda/sigma)^k poly(L)`. It assumes no residual-curvature lower bound, no joint convexifier, and no unique residual optimizer. The selector is the lexicographically least optimal core, followed by the least-Euclidean-norm point in that core’s optimal residual fiber; it is independent of `q`.

The same theorem supplies a per-draw random factor `Omega(gamma)` whose bound applies to **all** `q` queries using that one draw. The law size `M` is base-computable and chosen before sampling, so the finite law and sampled optimizer do not change with requested precision. These law/query properties and the cubic core-only full-point evaluator are already in the companion; do not present them as new here.

The companion’s Theorem `thm:recourse-convexified` also gives full-point `q`-bit evaluation on every draw for fixed-degree objectives when a core-supported convexifier is supplied; for degree three it only needs convexity on the feasible polytope. Its numerical factor depends on `(1+alpha/sigma)^k`. In the current manuscript notation, the row-sum Hessian bound `K_2` bounds both the negative core-Hessian part and the core–residual cross block. Under uniformly strongly convex residual Hessian `H_RR >= mu I`, the Schur complement supplies the core-supported rank-at-most-`k` convexifier `alpha = K_2 + K_2^2/mu` (the generic form is `M+M^2/mu`). Therefore full-point core-only optimization does not become a new result merely because this draft packages it as an implicit patch plus global proof record. The meaningful rate distinction to audit is whether the new direct count keeps the factor at `L/sigma` instead of paying for the potentially much larger derived `alpha/sigma`.

The distinction is degree-sensitive. The companion’s Remark `rem:recourse-quartic` already connects extending its core-only full-point conclusion to the stated convex quartic family with a Las Vegas algorithm for Square Root Sum or `PosSLP`. This is conditional complexity evidence, not an unconditional hardness theorem. Do not generalize it to cubics: the companion proves the positive cubic theorem above. Nor should the quartic point obstruction be described as excluding the supplied-convexifier theorem, whose premise is different.

The companion and current draft also have different output contracts. The companion computes a Cauchy name for its fixed canonical point and the sampled optimum value; it does not return a compact finite patch descriptor or a pruning/global-proof record. The current draft’s ordinary branch returns an implicit patch descriptor and a separate global proof record; the descriptor alone does not certify global optimality. The general output definition does not promise a canonical optimizer on ordinary draws; its fallback algebraic output selects the lexicographically least global minimizer. Its theorem bounds expected evaluation cost for each `q` using the same base-selected law/draw, while the companion states a common per-draw `Omega(gamma)` factor uniformly over all `q`. Describe the finite descriptor-plus-proof interface as an output distinction, not by itself as a new optimization-time bound.

### Decomposition-aware global optimization

This deterministic companion is a direct antecedent for curvature-corrected coordinate grids, min-marginal filtering, and recomputable path certificates. Its parameters are not interchangeable:

- Let `L_i = max(0, partial_ii F)` on the continuous hull and `L=max_i L_i`. Point growth means `F(x)-F(x*) >= g ||x-x*||^2`, with a unique minimizer, and `kappa=max(1,L/g)`. Weighted growth means `F(x)-OPT >= gamma ||x-x*||_L^2`, where `||d||_L^2=sum_i L_i d_i^2`, and `bar-kappa=max(1,1/gamma*)` for the largest valid `gamma*`. Point growth implies weighted growth with `bar-kappa <= kappa`; the reverse need not hold. `bar-kappa` is scale-invariant and puts no growth demand on coordinates with `L_i=0`.
- Theorem `thm:approx` gives certified `2^-q` approximation in `f(p,bar-kappa)(I+q+1)^5` bit operations, with `f(p,t)=c0(c1 p sqrt(t))^p t(1+log_2 t)^2`. The growth constant and `bar-kappa` are not algorithm inputs; trials stop on a valid certificate. Under point growth the larger `kappa` can be substituted. Theorem `thm:exact` separately gives an exact minimizer in `f_1(p,kappa)(I+1)^{C_1}` under point growth and a unique minimizer.
- Proposition `prop:sharp` and Corollary `cor:uniformgrid` exhibit a family for the **filtered common uniform-grid method** that retains at least `sqrt((n-2)kappa)+1` nodes per coordinate in the stated stages. This is a lower bound on that localization/filtering behavior, not on all grid methods or all global optimizers. Graded grids instead give `O(sqrt(bar-kappa) log(n+2))` nodes per coordinate in the weighted-growth analysis.
- The approximation upper bound has `kappa` exponent `p/2+O(1)` (and fixed input-degree exponent 5). Under rETH, Proposition `prop:lbproduct` rules out a sublinear `o(p)` exponent in its stated product-time model `f(p)(kappa I)^{p/psi(p)}` for integer box quadratics with a supplied tree decomposition and unique minimizer. The reduction does **not** prove a matching lower bound of `p/2+O(1)` and does not exclude every smaller linear exponent, nor bounds such as `kappa^3 I^p`; on the hard instances `kappa` and `I` are polynomially linked. This is a deterministic approximation lower bound, not a smoothed lower bound.

The finite perturbation and expected exact-fallback composition in the current draft are separate claims. Do not transfer the companion’s exponent lower bound to the sparse-SOS paper without a proof that its reduction satisfies the sparse paper’s model and runtime parameters.

### Sparse indicator quadratics

This companion is a close finite-noise/treewidth precedent with a different model. It perturbs **only the indicator penalties** `lambda_i`, independently and uniformly on a finite equally spaced grid in `[-sigma,sigma]` (at least `2n` values). For fixed treewidth it exactly solves the perturbed positive-definite indicator quadratic and computes exact message dictionaries on the stated bounded boundary box on every draw. The expected bit work is polynomial in the input length and in numerical values such as `C,H,mu^{-1},R,sigma^{-1}`; it is not polynomial in their bit lengths.

Its scaled hardness corollary is also precise: if a solver returns the exact optimal value for every draw and has expected bit complexity polynomial in encoding length on every instance, then `NP subseteq ZPP`, even for the fixed matrix family and fixed `sigma` specified in the corollary. The proof scales the linear data and penalties while perturbing only those penalties. This is not a lower bound for the global draft’s broader linear-objective perturbation model, nor a blanket obstruction to its structured results. Credit the overlapping finite-noise/treewidth/dictionary ideas and keep this numerical-dependence boundary attached.
