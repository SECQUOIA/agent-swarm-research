\begin{abstract}
Sparse global optimization can admit small lower certificates even when a
single spatial partition would be large. We address the construction of
such certificates without knowing a global minimizer. We study
certificates formed by convex bag models and affine
minorants on separator cells of a tree decomposition. Under global
quadratic growth at a unique minimizer, Lipschitz bag gradients, and
quadratic local-model error, we give an algorithm that repeatedly rebuilds
graded partitions around a point recovered from its current certificate.
A bound on aggregate copy disagreement gives contraction on arbitrary
branching trees. For fixed structural parameters, conditioning, and
numerical scales, the final partition has $O(N(1+\log_+(N/\eps)))$ boxes
and cells, and construction uses $O(N(1+\log_+(N/\eps))^3)$ local convex
minimizations, where $\log_+z=\max\{0,\log z\}$.
The minimizer may be on the boundary, and no nonconvex local search is
required. Certified inexact solves and gradient errors preserve
these orders. For rational polynomial objectives of fixed degree, affine
Taylor models give an exact rational implementation whose bit cost is
polynomial in the input length and requested precision for fixed structural
and conditioning parameters. We also characterize separator inconsistency
through an exact distance-to-band identity and prove limitations of
pointwise localization and unadjusted slopes. For contracting nonlinear
dynamics, graph strips, adjoint-adjusted slopes, and forward repair yield
analogous guarantees; a finite-precision implementation returns a
compressed exactly feasible trajectory and certified objective bounds.
\end{abstract}
