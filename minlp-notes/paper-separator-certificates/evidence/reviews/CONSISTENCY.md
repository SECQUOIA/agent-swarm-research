# Independent referee review: separator consistency and scalar covering

Reviewed the manuscript versions of `sections/model.tex`, `sections/consistency.tex`, and `appendices/scalar-covering.tex` on 2026-10-06. This review derived the statements directly from the manuscript definitions. It did not conduct literature research, alter the literature knowledge base, rerun experiments, or edit manuscript files. A second focused independent check of the scalar concentration, endpoint conventions, and dyadic counting agreed with the conclusions below.

## Verdict

The mathematical results reviewed are sound. No serious mathematical defect or incomplete proof remains in this part of the paper. Two small statement-scope repairs were sent to the consistency author and have been integrated: explicitly declare the edge-count notation in the discounted-bag-error proposition, and handle the one-bag case in the tree sandwich. The author had already separated `n_T=N-1` from the coordinate dimension `n`, stated that local cell classes are linear spaces containing constants, and covered the zero-dimensional quotient in the attainment proof.

## Derivations checked

1. **Certificate and value-function definitions.** Running intersection makes the private variables on the two sides of an edge disjoint. Product boxes make the domains of private-variable minimization independent of the fixed separator point. Continuity and compactness therefore give attained continuous conditional value functions. Every touching cell intersection is included in the certificate inequalities. The fixed-slope dynamic program unfolds over finitely many compact configuration domains, and its signs agree with the first-order telescoping identity.

2. **Restricted split duality.** The product of the weak-star compact convex bag-functional sets is compact. The Lagrangian is continuous affine in the functionals and continuous linear in the split functions with the uniform topology. Compact minimax applies even when the split spaces are not complete or finite dimensional. The multiplier supremum is finite precisely for agreement on every prescribed test function. Its value is then zero; otherwise it is positive infinity. Consistent families form a closed compact subset, giving attainment whenever nonempty. For probability measures the local minimizations are the original continuous bag minima. The subsequent tree-gluing argument correctly accounts for running intersection.

3. **Closed convex envelopes.** For a bounded bag function the closed convex hull of its graph is compact and projects onto the entire bag box. Its lower fiber boundary is a finite closed convex minorant and is greater than every other closed convex minorant. A linear objective has the same infimum over the graph and this compact hull. Consequently the envelope attains the bag-function infimum even for bounded discontinuous split functions. The product-hull minimax argument imposes agreement of neighboring separator copies and proves the stated affine-envelope identity.

4. **Finite-dimensional attainment.** The only directions for which the sum of bagwise minima of the pure shift is zero are constant edge functions: nonnegative deviations must vanish at every globally consistent point, every bag point extends to the product box, and leaf elimination then makes each edge function constant. Away from the constant quotient, continuity on its unit sphere gives strict uniform decay and coercivity. The zero-dimensional quotient is handled separately in the current version. Exactness is equivalent to common shifted-bag minimizers because their nonnegative objective deviations sum to zero.

5. **One-separator band identity.** A split has gap `sup(chi-U)+sup(L-chi)`. The sum is nonnegative because the band margin has minimum zero. Balancing the two suprema by a constant and clipping into the band proves twice the uniform distance is at most the gap. Conversely any band member bounds the bracket by an oscillation, and constant shifts turn the optimized oscillation into twice the optimized uniform distance. The independent-cell identity follows by balancing each cell separately. Negative individual cell brackets are permissible; the largest global bracket remains nonnegative. The single-valued convention is needed only to define the split at common endpoints.

6. **Tree sandwich and joint obstruction.** Enlarging all but one edge class to arbitrary bounded functions permits infimum-based dynamic programming on both sides of that edge, yielding its band lower bound. Perturbing one joint exact split incurs at most the sum of edge oscillations, proving the upper bound. The three-bag smooth quadratic obstruction is correct: both original bands contain zero, but the middle shifted bag is at most `-beta-|lambda_2-lambda_1|`, and the endpoint bag minima are at most zero. The zero split attains the gap `beta`. Thus separate exact edge bands do not suffice for joint affine exactness.

7. **Graded exact split.** The conditioned bag margin is `W_t=g_t+w_t` away from the root and `W_r=g_r`; it dominates every incident separator margin. With `tau=1/(2n_T)` and `theta_t=(2b_t+1)tau`, the nonroot margin coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one, and the root child coefficients also sum to one. This makes the leftover bag expression nonpositive and proves the discounted edge bracket. Setting the perturbation to zero proves exactness. The inequalities `tau<=theta_t<=1-tau` place the narrower band inside the original band. Constant balancing gives the distance and cell-sandwich bounds.

8. **Discounted bag errors.** With `tau'=1/(3n_T+1)` and `theta'_t=(3b_t+2)tau'`, the retained margin coefficients sum to `1-tau'`. Reserving `err_t-tau'W_t` therefore leaves a nonpositive remainder at every bag. The perturbation signs and the two occurrences of each edge are correct. Bounded nonnegative errors need not be continuous for this argument.

9. **Scalar concentration and chord bounds.** For the convex function `varphi=L+M(s-c)^2/2-U+M(s-c)^2/2`, the sum of slope masses on the inner half-interval equals `varphi'(b-)-varphi'(a+)`. Secant inequalities and `varphi(a)+varphi(b)>=2varphi(c)` give the stated `4w(c)/h+4Mh` bound. All derivatives used in this concentration step are interior and finite. The chord residual of a concave function is bounded by the minimum of its endpoint supporting lines, whose maximum is at most one quarter of interval length times slope drop. The weighted concave and convex residuals plus the quadratic correction give the stated oscillation bound. Smooth bag data supply the required semiconcavity and Lipschitz constants because taking a pointwise infimum preserves uniform semiconcavity and uniform Lipschitz continuity on a fixed private-variable box.

10. **Scalar covering construction and count.** For an interior cell of radius `r`, take `h=4n_T r` about a point minimizing the margin. The cell lies in the inner half-interval and the outer interval remains in the separator range. Concentration and chord approximation yield oscillation at most `w_min/(2n_T)+(8n_T+1/2)Mr^2`; the bracket subtracts `w_min/n_T`. At boundary cells, finite Lipschitz endpoint slopes give the stated `2Gr+(5/2)Mr^2` bound before discounting. Both bounds tend uniformly to zero. Affine cell minimizers exist by coercivity in slope, with a free intercept used for balancing. Interior split cells meet the projected sublevel set at `eta_j=(16n_T^2+n_T)Mr_j^2-2epsilon`; strict dyadic inequalities give exactly the ceiling formulas for `J_t` and `J'_t`. A cover interval intersects at most `floor(d/(2r))+2` closed dyadic cells, including touching boundaries. At most `2n_T` cells occur near each interval endpoint at any level. Each bisection increases the final number of cells by one. Finally `g_D<=B(D)` proves the bracket-refinement partition is a coarsening of the bound-rule partition by induction on ancestors.

## Resolved small repairs and optional clarity

- The discounted-bag-error proposition now explicitly states `n_T=N-1>=1` and repeats the definition of `b_t`, making its inherited scope explicit.
- The tree-sandwich theorem now assumes `N>=2`, avoiding a maximum over the empty set of nonroot bags. The one-bag gap itself is zero.
- Optional clarification: the generalized normalized-functional duality identity alone does not guarantee that its objective is a lower bound for the original problem. Such validity follows, for example, if each functional set contains all point evaluations. The probability-measure case already has this property. The current theorem is mathematically correct without that validity assumption, but the term “bag relaxations” may encourage readers to infer it.
