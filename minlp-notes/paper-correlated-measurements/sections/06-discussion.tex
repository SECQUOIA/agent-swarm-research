\section{Discussion and conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What improves a usable design certificate}

What can be certified depends on keeping the statistical information model
separate from the optimization relaxation. The public-source enumeration
shows that full-inverse gating can change the selected experiment even in a
small, exactly enumerable model, and that in this model the effect depends
on the criterion: D-optimal and conventional A-optimal choices agree across
all eleven tested budgets, while one trace-information choice changes. Because gating never
understates information (Proposition~\ref{prop:gating}), a gated global upper
bound can still be reused once the incumbent is evaluated under the selected
covariance, and the condition-dependent comparison bounds the possible
efficiency loss.

For temporal designs, finite histories are useful when their conditional
structure admits a tractable price over the full feasible family and their
uniform error is small enough for the desired certificate. The all-split
comparisons are stronger evidence of relaxation strength than a timed solver
contest: their common fractional witnesses prove that better continuous
optimization or scalar split selection cannot remove the measured
separations. The kinetic all-diagonal witness extends this conclusion to the
diagonal family and its specified affine upper supports; it says nothing
about branching or other valid cuts. Conversely, in the complete-packet
examples the tested dense bound is tighter and all methods select the same
design. No universal dominance is demonstrated.

The results suggest allocating work according to the part of the bound that
remains loose. A large continuous support residual calls for a better reference
or further numerical optimization. A large locality correction calls for a
stronger model-specific bound, a longer history, or a different representation.
A remaining mixture integrality gap requires restrictions that distinguish
one feasible experiment from a fractional mixture. More accurate arithmetic
can reduce enclosure loss, as the spacing experiment illustrates, but cannot
remove a statistical truncation bound or a mixture gap. Because the parts are
separate, inexpensive heuristics can supply incumbents while independently
chosen witnesses certify them.

Latent separators expose a second tradeoff. For nested anchors and the same
complete feasible family, removing anchors improves the mathematical hull
bound; the exact eight-time study verifies strict improvements for nested
sets, not for unrelated partitions or unfinished solver values. Fewer anchors,
however, require larger conditional-block patterns, and more anchors increase
the nuisance dimension. The larger fixed-physical experiments show these
costs: the best reported 192-point separator certificate still misses the
.01 log-gap target and exceeds the nominal 30-second allowance once
certification is included. The hierarchy orders the bounds; it does not
identify a uniformly efficient representation.

\subsection{Scope of the statistical and complexity conclusions}

Fixed covariance and nominal sensitivities make the information objective
precise but limit its interpretation. The kinetic examples are local designs
for stipulated error models. Independent balance-equation and high-precision
checks support their sensitivity implementation, and the exact certificates
apply to the stored rational arrays; neither validates a physical noise law.

Finite-scenario standardization answers a different question from raw maximin
information. It measures each scenario against its own attainable optimum,
and its certificate must therefore include uncertainty in those optima. The
shared support bound prices one design over all scenarios and improves on
separate scenario caps in the reported tests.

The approximation theorems identify tractable promised classes; they do not
predict the runtime of the numerical prototypes. Weighted trace permits
growing packet and parameter dimensions because its local support is linear.
Nonlinear criteria use a fixed-dimensional relative PSD cover with large
polynomial exponents. The correlated temporal schemes require complete
packets and the stated fixed covariance promises, while the abstract additive
cover requires fixed information dimension and an explicit rational
feasibility representation. This restriction is consistent with the
hardness result of \citet{BansalXu2026} for growing information dimension
(Section~\ref{subsec:intro-prior}).
A finer grid for one physical process makes the per-step correlation
approach one, and the measured calendar-price failures are consistent with
this loss of uniform promises. The conservative sufficient windows do not
prove a lower bound for every possible algorithm. The represented-matroid
result concerns additive information atoms and gives no automatic way to
impose arbitrary matroid constraints on correlated histories.

\subsection{Conclusion}

The paper gives a route from a specified correlated experiment to a
checkable global design bound. Explicit, schedule-uniform errors of local
conditionals support finite-state certificates and approximation guarantees;
normalization on exact ranges extends the approximation to several matrix
criteria; and Schur elimination gives a nested alternative when calendar
histories are costly. The computations establish strict separations from
virtual-noise relaxation families and useful finite-scenario guarantees, and
also report cases with weaker bounds or unfavorable cost. Every guarantee
consists of a feasible experiment and a verified upper witness for the same
selected-covariance objective, so it can be checked independently of the
numerical method that proposed the design.

\subsection*{Data and code availability}

The standalone computational supplement contains the model arrays, selected
schedules, rational witnesses, exact checkers, numerical producers, a
dependency lock and file-hash manifests. Its README maps each reported study
and table to the saved artifacts and gives separate commands for certificate
validation and numerical reproduction. Full validation replays 46 principal
saved certificates, independently checks every public-source schedule, checks
the kinetic sensitivities and verifies the fresh exhaustive studies. Once
dependencies are installed, validation needs no network access or commercial
solver. Optional reproduction of the historical mixed-integer proposals uses
Gurobi under its license. The public sensitivity CSV keeps its immutable
source reference \citep{MeasurementOpt2025} and hash, with no new licensing
assertion about that third-party data; third-party literature
and uninspected stored author selections are not included. The Lean
formalizations of Theorems~\ref{thm:dag-spectral-set}
and~\ref{thm:matroid-spectral-set} are distributed with the accompanying
source repository, not with the computational supplement.
