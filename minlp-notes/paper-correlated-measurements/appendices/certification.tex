\section{Exact arithmetic for the support witnesses}
\label{app:certificate-arithmetic}

For rational encoded data, rational elimination evaluates the selected
covariance inverse and the conditional regressions used by the local and
separator certificates. Gaussian innovation formulas are an efficient
alternative, not a different information model. The exact checker also uses
rational representations of its reference matrices $N$ or $W=N^{-1}$,
separator nuisance coefficients $G$, split and query witnesses, and scenario
simplex weights. Their stated positivity, feasibility, and normalization
conditions are checked exactly; a numerical proposal alone is not a witness.
The rational upper enclosures $\widehat\delta<1$ of
Section~\ref{subsec:local-certificates} replace analytic locality errors in
all local scores, scenario by scenario when needed; the weight $H$ below
includes this rational inflation factor when applicable. These
representation choices restrict the checker, not the real-valued theorems.
All covariance matrices inverted must be SPD under the stated hypotheses.
Symmetry and exact positive leading principal minors check an SPD reference;
exact pivoted rational $LDL^T$ or all principal minors can check a PSD split.
A nonpositive pivot rejects an SPD claim. Floating-point eigenvalues alone
certify neither assertion.

For $x\in\Q$, $x>0$, choose an integer $h$ with $x=2^h y$,
$1\le y<2$. Put $u=(y-1)/(y+1)\in[0,1/3)$, and for $m\ge1$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-log}
 s_m(u)=2\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{u^{2j+1}}{2j+1},\qquad
 0\le\log y-s_m(u)\le
 \frac{2u^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)(1-u^2)}.
\end{equation}
The series follows by integrating $2/(1-u^2)$; replacing the remaining
odd denominators by $2m+1$ proves its geometric remainder bound.
The same formula at $u=1/3$ encloses $\log2$. Combine the enclosures as
$\log x=h\log2+\log y$, reversing the endpoints for the first term when
$h<0$. Thus determinants below one and negative log determinants cause no
sign ambiguity. Exact positive rational determinants need no eigenvalue
calculation. Refinement to any prescribed rational interval width terminates
geometrically; the bit length of $h$ and the requested interval width control
the number of terms, and the arithmetic bit cost also depends on the rational
input encoding. Integer floor and ceiling then yield outward decimal
displays. A displayed efficiency percentage additionally needs an exponential
enclosure if it is itself asserted as a rigorous bound.

\subsection{Signed integer interval scores}
\label{app:integer-scores}
For a scalar local innovation let
\[
 u=(F_t-\sum_j b_jF_{t-a_j})^T,\qquad d>0,\qquad s=u^THu/d,
\]
where $H$ is any symmetric rational weight matrix. In our logdet certificates
$H\succeq0$; allowing indefinite $H$ makes the arithmetic contract explicit.
Choose positive integer grids $G_F,G,G_s$. Enclose sensitivities on grid
$G_F^{-1}$, and $b_j,H_{ij}$ on grid $G^{-1}$, using exact floors and
ceilings for signed rationals. For integer intervals, multiplication uses
the minimum and maximum of all four endpoint products; subtraction uses
$[a,b]-[c,d]=[a-d,b-c]$. A square interval has lower endpoint zero if it
contains zero, and otherwise the smaller endpoint square; its upper endpoint
is the larger endpoint square.

These rules enclose each component $u_i$ by endpoints divided by $GG_F$.
Compute diagonal terms $H_{ii}u_i^2$ by the square rule and off-diagonal
terms $2H_{ij}u_iu_j$ for $i<j$ by interval multiplication. Let $Q^+$
be the integer upper endpoint after summation, so
$u^THu\le Q^+/(G^3G_F^2)$. Repeated variables need not be independent;
interval inclusion holds term by term and dependence can only widen the
result. Rounded weight entries need not form a PSD matrix.

Require $d_\ell=\lfloor Gd\rfloor>0$, and put $Q_+=\max(0,Q^+)$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer-arc-bound}
 s\le\frac{Q_+}{G^2G_F^2d_\ell}
 \le\frac1{G_s}\left\lceil
 \frac{G_s Q_+}{G^2G_F^2d_\ell}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
For a nonnegative numerator, division by the positive lower variance gives
the first inequality. For a negative numerator, the nonnegative right side
already dominates it. The clamp is therefore necessary for this general
indefinite-weight contract. A nonpositive variance floor causes rejection
or grid refinement; it is never replaced by a positive number.
The statistical innovation lower bound $d_*$ used to establish $\delta$
and the arithmetic floor $d_\ell/G$ serve different purposes. Both must
be valid for their respective conditional variances.

An exact integer path DP applied to these upper scores bounds the rational
support price. If every reachable arc exceeds its direct rational ceiling
by at most $e$ integer units and paths have at most $k$ scoring innovations,
the additional price is at most $ke/G_s$. A separator pattern scored as a
sum of scalar conditional innovations obeys the same bound with the total
number of selected observations. A finite-state spacing restriction changes
the feasible price, not this inclusion argument. For fixed positive variances
and PSD weights, refining $G_F,G$ shrinks the interval error. At a score-grid
boundary one excess ceiling unit may persist at every finite interval grid;
refining $G_s$ too removes this final scale, so no universal
negligible-error claim is needed. General block scores can instead use exact rational
conditional inverses and an outward ceiling directly; the scalar formula
above does not assume an unproved entrywise bound on a block inverse.

\section{Structured evaluation of the established dense relaxation}
\label{app:dense-oracle}

Let $K_{ij}=P\rho^{|i-j|}$, $R=K+rI$, $P\ge0$, $r>0$,
$|\rho|<1$, and choose an admissible scalar split $a>0$.
For $P>0$ and $n\ge2$, put $c=P(1-\rho^2)>0$. The scaled latent precision
$M$ has endpoint diagonals $1$, interior diagonals $1+\rho^2$, and adjacent
off-diagonals $-\rho$, so $K^{-1}=M/c$. Defining
$\beta_i=a(1-z_i)+rz_i>0$ gives the exact tridiagonal identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dense-tridiagonal}
 [M+c\diag(z_i/\beta_i)]U=MF,\quad
 V_i=(a/\beta_i)U_i,\quad
 J_D=J_0+F^T\diag(z_i/\beta_i)U.
\end{equation}
To derive them, multiply $(I+(K+(r-a)I)Z)V=F$ by $K^{-1}$,
set $U=\diag(\beta_i/a)V$, and collect terms. The coefficient matrix
is SPD. These are instances of established fast latent-precision plus
diagonal-noise algebra \citep{RybickiPress1995}. Tridiagonal solves require
$O(np)$ field operations, but information formation and all gradient
quadratics bring the complete value/gradient cost to $O(np^2+p^3)$,
with $O(np+p^2+n)$ storage. Field-operation counts are distinct from
rational denominator growth.

An exact split check follows from the congruence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split-tridiagonal-check}
 R-aI=M^{-1/2}[cI+(r-a)M]M^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
Strict positive pivots in rational tridiagonal elimination of
$cI+(r-a)M$ prove $a<\lambda_{\min}(R)$. Conversely, its first nonpositive
pivot, with all earlier pivots positive, proves it is not SPD by Sylvester's
criterion, and hence $a\ge\lambda_{\min}(R)$. Elimination stops at that
pivot: no division by a zero or negative pivot is needed. This is the scalar
all-split witness in \eqref{eq:scalar-barrier}. A PSD boundary split can
alternatively be checked by a complete PSD test. For $n=1$, use variance
$P+r$ directly; the endpoint formula $M/c$ does not apply. For $P=0$ or
$\rho=0$, use the corresponding diagonal covariance directly.

\subsection{Covariance innovations and the reverse adjoint}
Precision coordinates can be ill-conditioned when $|\rho|$ approaches one,
even if the observation nugget makes $R$ moderately conditioned. The
following exact covariance formulas avoid the identified precision solve.
Start $P_1^-=P$, $m_1^-=0$, where each $m_i^-$ is a row with $p$ entries.
For $i=1,\ldots,n$ set
\begin{align}
 e_i&=F_i-m_i^-,& q_i&=z_iP_i^-+\beta_i,& w_i&=z_i/q_i,\nonumber\\
 m_i&=m_i^-+P_i^-w_i e_i,&
 P_i^+&=P_i^-\beta_i/q_i,\nonumber\\
 m_{i+1}^-&=\rho m_i,&
 P_{i+1}^-&=\rho^2P_i^++P(1-\rho^2).
 \label{eq:dense-covariance-filter}
\end{align}
The final unused prediction can be omitted. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dense-innovation-info}
 J_D(z)=J_0+\sum_i w_i e_i^Te_i.
\end{equation}
On $T=\{i:z_i>0\}$ these are ordinary Kalman innovations for covariance
$K_{TT}+\diag(\beta_i/z_i)$ and deterministic observation rows $F_T$.
This is the covariance in \eqref{eq:vn-covariance}. At $z_i=0$ the update
and information are zero, with no infinite variance formed. Classical Kalman
filtering and RTS smoothing are described by
\citet[Theorems 6.6 and 12.2]{Sarkka2023}; the present equations apply their
linear algebra to the design sensitivity right-hand sides.

All rows of $V$ are needed in the selection gradient, including those at zero
weights. Initialize a row $b_{n+1}=0$ and sweep backward:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dense-reverse-adjoint}
 V_i=\frac{a}{q_i}(e_i-\rho P_i^-b_{i+1}),\qquad
 b_i=w_ie_i+\rho\frac{\beta_i}{q_i}b_{i+1}.
\end{equation}
For one right-hand side consider
$Q(F)=\frac12\sum_i w_ie_i^2$ with the variances and weights fixed.
The forward mean map in \eqref{eq:dense-covariance-filter} is
$m_{i+1}^-=\rho[(1-P_i^-w_i)m_i^-+P_i^-w_iF_i]$.
Its negative adjoint with respect to $m_i^-$ is
$b_i=w_ie_i+\rho(1-P_i^-w_i)b_{i+1}$.
Since $1-P_i^-w_i=\beta_i/q_i$,
$\partial Q/\partial F_i=w_i(e_i-\rho P_i^-b_{i+1})$.
By \eqref{eq:vn-covariance} this derivative is row $i$ of $ZV$.
Cancel $z_i/a$ when $z_i>0$ to prove the first identity in
\eqref{eq:dense-reverse-adjoint}; continuity of the resolvent and the
positive denominators proves it also at $z_i=0$. Finally apply
\eqref{eq:vn-gradient}. This is an ordinary reverse derivative of the
innovation quadratic, not a new filter or relaxation.

In exact arithmetic, $a/q_i\le1$ for an admissible scalar split.
The conditional variance $P_i^-+r$ arises by predicting the original
observation from previous observations with additional independent
nonnegative virtual noise. For every predictor coefficient vector $c_0$,
the original residual variance is at least
$\lambda_{\min}(R)(1+\norm{c_0}^2)$; added noise cannot reduce it.
Minimization gives $P_i^-+r\ge\lambda_{\min}(R)\ge a$, and therefore
$q_i=a(1-z_i)+(P_i^-+r)z_i\ge a$.
Also $|\rho\beta_i/q_i|\le|\rho|$.
These bounds explain why the reverse sweep avoids one amplification mechanism;
they are not a complete floating-point error analysis.

Two implementation failures have different causes. The algebraically
correct tridiagonal precision formulation can lose accuracy near unit
correlation because it works in a poorly conditioned latent precision.
A first covariance implementation instead computed
$V_i=a(F_i-m_i^{\rm smooth})/\beta_i$ from RTS smoothed means. That identity
is exact, but a large latent-to-nugget ratio can cause catastrophic
cancellation in $F_i-m_i^{\rm smooth}$ before scaling. The reverse sweep
\eqref{eq:dense-reverse-adjoint} avoids that particular subtraction and
uses the bounded factor $a/q_i$. Remaining small-residual cancellation,
ill-conditioned parameter information, and finite floating-point range can
still cause error. Neither successful numerical evaluation nor observed
agreement replaces the rational certificate.

The boundary cases follow directly. When all $z_i=0$, $J_D=J_0$ and $V=F$,
so the gradient generally is nonzero. Negative $\rho$ changes the signs of
mean transitions and adjoints but not variance positivity. A split $a>r$
is valid whenever $a\le\lambda_{\min}(R)$; positivity of $\beta_i$ does
not require $a\le r$. In floating point the process variance is evaluated
as $P[(1-\rho)(1+\rho)]$ to avoid an unnecessary intermediate overflow
for negative $\rho$. Exact certificates are unaffected by expression order.

\section{Robust certificate witnesses and normalization limits}
\label{app:robust-witnesses}

Consider two scalar scenarios with prior one and two feasible paths whose
information pairs are $(4,1)$ and $(1,4)$. This is an additive two-arc graph
with increments $(3,0)$ and $(0,3)$. Both individual optima are four and each
feasible standardized efficiency is $1/4$. At $N_1=N_2=5/2$ and equal
scenario weights, the common linear support price is $3/5$, whereas the
average of the separate support maxima is $6/5$. The shared certificate is
$\log(5/8)$, a strict improvement of $3/5$ over that separate-price bound.
It still exceeds the true optimum $\log(1/4)$: the equal mixture has
information $(5/2,5/2)$ and must satisfy every such tangent upper bound.
Thus zero memory error does not remove a common-mixture integrality gap.

The extra factor in Corollary~\ref{cor:robust-fptas} cannot be omitted for
an arbitrary maximizing tie choice in the stated same-set recipe. Let
$a=3/4$, $p=1$, and let the returned set of two-scenario information pairs be
\[
 \mathcal C=\{(3/4,1/10),(1/10,1),(3/8,3/8),(9/32,3/8)\}.
\]
Add the feasible pairs $(1/2,1/2)$ and $(1,1/10)$ to form $\calF$.
Every pair in $\calF$ has a representative in $\mathcal C$ between
simultaneous factors $3/4$ and $5/4$: use itself for retained pairs,
$(3/8,3/8)$ for $(1/2,1/2)$, and $(3/4,1/10)$ for $(1,1/10)$.
All pairs are realizable on an additive choice graph with common positive
prior $1/20$ in each scenario and nonnegative scalar increments.
The true scenario maxima are $(1,1)$ and the set maxima are $(3/4,1)$.
The last two retained pairs tie for best set-normalized minimum, $3/8$.
Choosing $(9/32,3/8)$ yields true efficiency $9/32$, while the true robust
optimum is $1/2$. Their ratio is $9/16=a^2$.
This example limits that comparison procedure; it is not a lower bound
against all possible algorithms for robust normalization.

\section{Changing the latent representation and stationary bridges}
\label{app:separator-hierarchy}

\subsection{The cross-representation Schur identity}
We prove \eqref{eq:separator-cross-identity} algebraically so that no Gaussian
prior cross term is implicit. Fix $A\subseteq B$ and put $C=B\setminus A$.
Write $U=X_A$, $V=X_C$. The anchor prior can be expressed as
\[
 V=L U+\xi,\qquad L=K_{CA}K_A^{-1},\qquad
 \Sigma=K_{CC}-K_{CA}K_A^{-1}K_{AC}\succ0,
\]
where $\xi$ is independent of $U$. Empty blocks have their usual omitted
interpretation. Partition the observation loading given $(U,V)$ as
$H_B=[H_U,H_V]$. For the selected rows and the $B$-conditional covariance
$D=(D_B)_{SS}$, the joint quadratic, including the parameter prior, is
\begin{align}\label{eq:anchor-joint-quadratic}
 &\theta^TJ_0\theta+U^TK_A^{-1}U
 +(V-LU)^T\Sigma^{-1}(V-LU)\nonumber\\
 &\hspace{20mm}+(F_S\theta+H_{U,S}U+H_{V,S}V)^T
 D^{-1}(F_S\theta+H_{U,S}U+H_{V,S}V).
\end{align}
Its coefficient matrix in $(\theta,U,V)$ is precisely $M_B(S)$.
In particular the nuisance prior is
\[
 K_B^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}
 K_A^{-1}+L^T\Sigma^{-1}L&-L^T\Sigma^{-1}\\
 -\Sigma^{-1}L&\Sigma^{-1}
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
which is generally not block diagonal between retained and removed anchors.
This positive observation quadratic is the quadratic part of the residual
likelihood in $(\theta,U,V)$: both mean terms enter its residual with the
same minus sign.

Substitute $V=LU+\xi$. For fixed $(\theta,U)$ this is a bijection of the
minimized coordinates, so it leaves their Schur complement unchanged.
The retained observation loading becomes
$\widetilde H=H_U+H_VL=H_A$, by iterated Gaussian conditioning (or directly
by multiplying covariance blocks). Minimizing the remaining terms over $\xi$
gives the quadratic
\[
 \theta^TJ_0\theta+U^TK_A^{-1}U
 +(F_S\theta+H_{A,S}U)^T
 [D+H_{V,S}\Sigma H_{V,S}^T]^{-1}
 (F_S\theta+H_{A,S}U).
\]
Indeed, completing the square in $\xi$ gives the inverse in brackets by
Woodbury's identity. Conditional covariance decomposition gives
$D+H_{V,S}\Sigma H_{V,S}^T=(D_A)_{SS}$.
The resulting coefficient matrix is $M_A(S)$, proving
\eqref{eq:separator-cross-identity} for every selected set, including the
empty set. This proof needs only $K\succ0$ and positive observation noise;
Markov structure is used for cheap block pattern enumeration, not the
identity itself.

For completeness, if $M\preceq N$ are SPD with the same coordinate split,
then for every parameter vector $x$,
\[
 x^T\mathcal S_\theta(M)x
 =\min_y[x;y]^TM[x;y]
 \le\min_y[x;y]^TN[x;y]
 =x^T\mathcal S_\theta(N)x.
\]
The minimum of the average is at least the average of the minima, proving
Schur concavity in the same way. Eliminating two nuisance groups in either
order is the same joint minimum over their vectors. These facts justify
every matrix-order step in Theorem~\ref{thm:separator-hierarchy}.

The hierarchy can be strict even when the empty-anchor hull has no integer
gap. Take $n=2$, $p=1$, $k=1$, $J_0=1$, $F=(1,-1)^T$,
$K=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
and $r=1$. Both true singleton informations equal $3/2$, so
$U_\varnothing=\log(3/2)$. For all anchors the symmetric concave scalar-split
relaxation attains its maximum at equal weights, with information $9/5$;
thus $U_{\{1,2\}}=\log(9/5)>U_\varnothing$.
The equal mixture with one anchor has information $75/44$; this value
illustrates an intermediate representation, and we do not claim that this
asymmetric level is optimized at equal weights.

\subsection{Singleton patterns recover the actual diagonal split}
Suppose $D_A=D$ is diagonal. Set $L_0=H_AK_A^{1/2}$, so
$R=D+L_0L_0^T$. At a distribution over schedules the augmented data
matrix depends only on $Z=\diag(z_i/D_{ii})$:
\[
 \overline M_A=\diag(J_0,K_A^{-1})+[F,H_A]^TZ[F,H_A].
\]
Eliminating the nuisance variables gives
\[
 J_0+F^T\{Z-ZL_0(I+L_0^TZL_0)^{-1}L_0^TZ\}F
 =J_0+F^TZ(I+(R-D)Z)^{-1}F.
\]
The inverse identity follows by multiplying by $I+(R-D)Z$ and is valid
at zero weights and with rank-deficient $R-D$. Hence the objective is the
virtual-noise extension for this exact $D$. For cardinality alone, the
marginals of mixtures over size-$k$ subsets fill $\mathcal Z_k$; more generally
they fill the actual indicator hull. Replacing that hull by a larger
polytope gives a further relaxation, not an equality of feasible sets.
For no anchors the same formulas have no nuisance columns and apply when
$R$ itself is diagonal.

\subsection{Signed stationary bridge formulas}
\label{app:stationary-bridges}
For $K_{ij}=P\rho^{|i-j|}$ with $P>0$ and $|\rho|<1$, two neighboring
anchors $a<c$ give, at an interior time $i$, the nonzero loadings
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bridge-loadings}
 H_{i,a}=\rho^{i-a}\frac{1-\rho^{2(c-i)}}{1-\rho^{2(c-a)}},\qquad
 H_{i,c}=\rho^{c-i}\frac{1-\rho^{2(i-a)}}{1-\rho^{2(c-a)}}.
\end{equation}
At an anchor its loading is the corresponding unit vector. Before the first
or after the last anchor the only loading is the appropriate power of
$\rho$. For $a<i\le j<c$ the conditional latent covariance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bridge-covariance}
 \Cov(X_i,X_j\mid X_A)
 =P\rho^{j-i}
 \frac{(1-\rho^{2(i-a)})(1-\rho^{2(c-j)})}{1-\rho^{2(c-a)}}.
\end{equation}
With only a left anchor, omit the right numerator factor and denominator;
with only a right anchor, omit the left factor and denominator. With no
anchors use $P\rho^{j-i}$. Rows and columns at an anchor have zero latent
conditional covariance, and $r$ is added to every observation diagonal.
These identities follow by inverting the two-by-two anchor covariance and
subtracting $K_{:A}K_A^{-1}K_{A:}$; the Markov property makes all other anchor
loadings zero and distinct residual blocks independent. They retain signs
for negative $\rho$. Rational input yields rational expressions.

One can price a block pattern by scalar filtering rather than repeatedly
inverting its selected bridge covariance. Write $V_t$ for its unconditional
conditional-latent variance, obtained by setting $i=j=t$ in
\eqref{eq:bridge-covariance} or its endpoint versions. For successive selected
nonanchor times $s<t$ in a block with right anchor $c$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bridge-transition}
 A_{ts}=\rho^{t-s}\frac{1-\rho^{2(c-t)}}{1-\rho^{2(c-s)}};
\end{equation}
with no right anchor set $A_{ts}=\rho^{t-s}$. The denominator is positive
because $s<c$. Dividing the conditional cross covariance by $V_s>0$
proves this transition, and its process variance is
$V_t-A_{ts}^2V_s\ge0$ by conditional covariance positivity. Starting the
first selected time at prediction variance $V_t$, propagate
$P_t^-=V_t-A_{ts}^2V_s+A_{ts}^2P_s^+$ and update with observation-noise
variance $r$. Filter the deterministic rows $F_t+H_{A,t}G$ in parallel.
At a selected anchor the residual state is deterministically zero; its
information contribution is the row outer product divided by $r$, and the
latent filter is reset. This explicit reset also handles partitions assigning
an anchor observation to a block that continues on its right. Thus no formula
divides by its zero residual state variance.

For ordered anchors $a_1<\cdots<a_m$, let
$c_j=\rho^{a_j-a_{j-1}}$. The anchor-prior trace in
\eqref{eq:separator-certificate} has the sparse form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchor-prior-score}
 \tr(WG^TK_A^{-1}G)=\frac{G_1WG_1^T}{P}
 +\sum_{j=2}^m
 \frac{(G_j-c_jG_{j-1})W(G_j-c_jG_{j-1})^T}{P(1-c_j^2)}.
\end{equation}
This is the ordinary Markov density factorization. Hence the exact
support calculation needs neither a dense anchor-prior inverse nor a
long-window covariance approximation. Enumerating $2^b$ block patterns and
handling $m$ nuisance coordinates still have real computational costs.
