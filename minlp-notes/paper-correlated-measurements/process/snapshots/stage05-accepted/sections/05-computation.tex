\section{Computational investigation}\label{sec:computation}

The strongest empirical result is a certified separation between bounds for
\emph{the same discrete problem}. On ten rational input instances, a feasible
point in every admissible scalar virtual-noise relaxation has a value larger
than a finite-history upper bound on the integer optimum. One kinetic instance
also separates every admissible diagonal split. These comparisons do not
require a claim that a competing numerical solver has converged. Other tests
show the limits of the approach: full-packet examples favor the tested dense
bound, and fine physical grids defeat the present calendar implementation.
All observation-error models in the temporal experiments are stipulated;
none is fitted to experimental residuals.

\subsection{Evidence and reproducibility}\label{subsec:computational-evidence}

We distinguish four kinds of evidence: exact rational identities and rankings;
rational global certificates with outward logarithm bounds; floating-point
solver results; and numerical checks of sensitivity generation. A JSON decimal
is treated as its exact rational value when issuing a certificate. This convention
certifies the supplied design instance, without asserting that its decimal
sensitivities equal exact real derivatives of an exponential mean model.
Reported log gaps are $U-\ell$; relative information gaps are
$(U-\ell)/\ell$ when $\ell>0$. A log gap $g$ gives determinant-root efficiency
at least $\exp(-g/p)$. Decimal bounds in the tables are rounded outward when
presented as certified inequalities; ordinary numerical comparisons are labeled
as such.

The portable supplement contains model arrays, feasible selections, tangent
and dual witnesses, exact checkers, numerical producers, and an explicit
file-hash manifest. Its validation command uses no commercial solver and
recomputes the mathematical certificate quantities rather than trusting saved
solver objectives. It also independently checks all source-model rankings,
864 temporal sensitivity entries against an augmented mass-balance ODE, and
16 sensitivity rows against 70-digit matrix exponentials. The largest observed
absolute sensitivity differences in the latter two checks were
$1.65\,10^{-13}$ and $1.48\,10^{-16}$, respectively. These are numerical
consistency tests, not validated ODE enclosures. Tiny exact tests exercise the
locality, approximation-set, Schur, robust, and interval-score arguments.
The spectral-set algorithms of Section~\ref{sec:approximation} are theoretical
schemes; they are not the practical large-instance solvers used below.

Historical runs were made on a shared machine with Python 3.13.11,
NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1, SymPy 1.14.0 and, for the mixed-integer
outer-approximation comparisons, Gurobi 13.0.3. BLAS and Gurobi used one thread
per invocation. Several certificate batches ran concurrently; individual
timings are retained as observed, rather than interpreted as controlled runtime
comparisons. The new exhaustive studies use the same recorded Python and
scientific-library versions, with no mixed-integer solver. Setup, incumbent
generation, numerical witness search, and exact certification are distinguished.
A sum of historical stage times is an accounted cost, not a newly timed pipeline.
The archive retains time limits, iteration caps, unsuccessful status messages,
input hashes, and earlier results superseded by stronger certificates.

\subsection{Reanalysis of the inspected public kinetics implementation}
\label{subsec:source-computation}

The source comparison of Section~\ref{subsec:source-comparison} uses the public
\texttt{measurement-opt} revision identified there and the supplied
$24\times4$ sensitivity file \texttt{kinetics\_Q\_drop0.csv}.
The eight times are $7.5,15,\ldots,60$, using the source's dropped-zero
sensitivity labels. Its internal spacing map is shifted by $-7.5$; this
leaves every pairwise time difference and feasible schedule unchanged
(see the supplement's source-data provenance note).
Three species may each use either a
static-cost measurement (SCM), acquiring all eight times for cost 2000,
or a dynamic-cost measurement (DCM), with installation cost 200 and cost 400 per
sample. The two modalities cannot be combined on one species. There is at
most one DCM at a time, no DCM at adjacent times, and at most four DCM samples.
A DCM installation without any sample is omitted as a redundant description
of the same acquired data. The prior is $10^{-4}I_4$.

At each time the six modality/species responses have covariance
\[
 R_6=\begin{pmatrix}B&B/2\\B/2&B\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/10&1/10\\1/10&4&1/2\\1/10&1/2&8\end{pmatrix};
\]
different times are independent. The same species sensitivity is used for
both modalities, as in the inspected code. Thus this example concerns
\emph{within-time channel selection}; it is separate from the complete-packet
temporal locality experiments.

Two independent enumerations produce the same 2347 nonempty acquisition
schedules. We evaluate budgets $1000,1400,\ldots,5000$ for trace information,
D-optimality, and conventional A-optimality. Exact rational arithmetic gives
all 66 formula--criterion--budget optima, each unique, under selected-covariance
information and full-inverse gating. A separate implementation constructs the
full $48\times48$ covariance in a different row order, directly inverts each
selected covariance, and verifies all 14,082 objective values. Comparing
rational determinants suffices for D-optimality; conventional A-optimality is
ranked by the rational trace of the inverse. No floating tie decision remains.

Exactly one of the 33 criterion--budget choices changes: trace information
at budget 3000. The correct choice is SCM for species B and DCM for species C
at times 45 and 60. Gating instead chooses SCM for B and DCM for A at times
7.5 and 22.5. Their true information traces are approximately 83.03029588 and
80.44572850, respectively, giving a certified relative loss of
$3.112800396788\%$ to the displayed precision. The gated traces are approximately
108.08074141 and 108.30708633, explaining the reversal. The D-optimal and
conventional A-optimal choices agree between the two formulas at every tested
budget; this agreement of optimizers does not make their information values
equal. The supplement retains every objective and runner-up margin.
This is a reanalysis of the pinned code's feasible family and supplied data,
not a reconstruction of uninspected stored published solutions or a validation
of how the sensitivity CSV was originally generated.

\subsection{Scalar temporal designs and relaxation separation}
\label{subsec:scalar-experiments}

The six synthetic cases use $p=3$, $k=n/3$, $J_0=0.1I_3$,
$R_{ij}=(2/5)^{|i-j|}+\ind_{i=j}$, and $n\in\{48,96\}$.
Each sensitivity array has independent standard-normal entries generated
by NumPy's default generator with seed 0, 1 or 2. The 48-row array is the
prefix of the 96-row array with the same seed; the six cases are not six
independent replications. The saved decimals specify the certified instances.
The numerical finite-history method optimizes a mixture of complete feasible
paths and evaluates its proposed integer selections under the true covariance.
Its $L=8$ runs close the surrogate numerical gap, while the rational
certificates in Table~\ref{tab:scalar-separations} retain a positive gap to the
true discrete optimum.

The kinetic mean is the consecutive first-order model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:computational-kinetics}
 B(t)=\frac{A_0k_1}{k_2-k_1}
       \bigl(e^{-k_1t}-e^{-k_2t}\bigr),\qquad A_0=1,
\end{equation}
with parameters $(\log A_0,\log k_1,\log k_2)$ and prior $0.01I_3$.
The early and late regimes have $(k_1,k_2)=(0.7,0.2)$ and $(0.18,0.045)$.
Times are $12j/n$, $j=1,\ldots,n$, $k=n/3$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:temporal-probe-covariance}
 R_{ij}=\frac1{800}\bigl((2/5)^{|i-j|}+\ind_{i=j}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The total pointwise noise standard deviation is $0.05$ in normalized
concentration units; the observed first-lag correlation is $0.2$.
Holding the per-grid correlation fixed while changing $n$ changes the physical
correlation scale. These four cases are therefore separate stipulated instances.
The analytic derivatives are given and checked in the supplement. The
rate-swap ambiguity discussed in \eqref{eq:rate-swap} remains;
these are local-information designs, not global parameter-recovery guarantees.

Proposition~\ref{prop:diagonal-barrier} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kinetic-diagonal-separation}
 \inf_{\text{admissible diagonal splits}}
    \phi_{z^0}(a)>15.043116838,
 \qquad \max_{S\in\calF}\log\det J(S)<14.950572423,
\end{equation}

certificate times are 1.56 and 0.226 seconds. In contrast, the analogous
synthetic seed-0 fixed-point probe gives a numerical lower estimate 7.68927318,

diagonal splits; it does not establish the optimum of the diagonal-split family.

\subsection{Spacing, full packets, and partial observations}
\label{subsec:packet-experiments}

A spacing-constrained kinetic case uses $n=96$, $k=32$, $g=2$, $L=13$ and
the exact decimal correlation $0.6324555320336759$, chosen near $\sqrt{0.4}$.
Its covariance approximately preserves the earlier 48-point physical decay,
but its observation budget is doubled, so it is not the fixed-budget grid study
below. The compact producer uses 610 feasible masks instead of 8192
unrestricted masks and takes 3.238 seconds. Direct rational arc evaluation
certifies gap $<0.01155765$ in 36.609 seconds. The integer-interval mode
increases the bound by exactly $10^{-8}$ and takes 1.087 seconds. This is an
observed arithmetic tradeoff for the same instance and reference, not a generic
speedup factor. Across its 31,900 reachable arcs, 418 upper scores increase
by one integer unit, none decrease, and the maximum path price increases by
one unit. Recomputing with the refined stationary pair bound reduces
$\delta$ from approximately .00379560 to .00318692 and gives a new certificate
gap $<0.00972559$ in 1.088 seconds, with the same incumbent. Reference and
coefficient grids are $10^8$ and $10^{12}$; the sensitivity and score grids are
$10^{18}$ and $10^8$. A nonpositive rounded innovation floor causes rejection.

The complete-packet examples acquire all $d=4$ or $d=16$ spectral channels at
a selected time. They have $n=24$, $k=8$, $L=6$, one unknown parameter
$\log k_1$, and scalar prior .01. Their stylized Beer--Lambert mean combines
the three concentrations of \eqref{eq:computational-kinetics} with fixed
Gaussian channel profiles specified in Appendix~\ref{app:computational-models}.
The latent and observation-noise covariances are both $I_d$, and the transition
is $A=(1/5)(C+\diag(1,-1,\ldots))$, where $C$ is the cyclic shift.
Thus $\norm A\le2/5$, while $AA^T\ne A^TA$ in the tested dimensions.
Increasing $d$ adds observations at the same per-channel noise level, so
information values across $d$ do not have a common normalization.

All three tested methods---finite-history scalar-information pricing,
greedy with single exchanges, and dense continuous rounding---select the
first eight times in both examples. The archived numerical memory relative
gaps are .70469\% and .72802\%, whereas the dense tangent gaps are tighter,
.24301\% and .20574\%. Memory pricing takes about .022 and .026 seconds;
greedy/exchange takes .009 and .032 seconds; dense optimization takes .006 and
.061 seconds, excluding generation of its supplied common incumbent.
These floating-point results demonstrate no advantage in selected designs and
no upper-bound advantage for memory on these examples.

The new complete-block far-pair argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:block-locality}
reduces the dimension-free $L=6$ constant from .007274322 to .006754203
(upward displays) for the same transition/noise promises. This is a fresh
comparison of theoretical constants, not a relabeling of old certificates.
We also perform a new exact experiment with $n=8$, $d=3$, $k=3$, $L=3$,
the same transition construction, prior .01, and rational sensitivity
$F_{t,j}=((7t+3j)\bmod13-6)/10$, using zero-based $t,j$.
All 56 schedules and their local regressions are enumerated exactly. The
old and sharp-far errors are .111957334 and .103863638 (upward displays),
and the same local-optimal schedule has true information exceeding .90391527.
The respective true-information upper bounds are below 1.01644959 and
1.00735957; relative gaps decrease from 12.4497\% to 11.4441\% (upward
rounding). The direct selected-inverse checks verify every sandwich and the
exhaustive optimum lies below both bounds. The single fresh run takes .011
seconds for model construction and .095 seconds for exact enumeration and
certification. This confirms a useful but modest constant improvement,
without claiming that the uniform bound is sharp for these sensitivities.

For partial observation, two independent latent modes have transition
$\diag(2/5,1/5)$, stationary covariance $I_2/800$, observation row
$(3/5,4/5)$, and independent measurement variance $1/800$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-mode-probe}
 R_{ij}=\frac1{800}\left\{
   \frac9{25}(2/5)^{|i-j|}+\frac{16}{25}(1/5)^{|i-j|}+\ind_{i=j}\right\}.
\end{equation}
This is not a single-mode scalar covariance. Using the four kinetic
sensitivity arrays above, $W=I_3$, $J_0=.01I_3$, and $L=8$, one exact
integer price per case gives relative trace-information gaps bounded by
.059924\%, .059732\%, .060361\%, and .059925\%, in order
48/early, 48/late, 96/early, 96/late. The normalized error bound uses
$\gamma=2/5$, $s=1$, $\kappa=1/2$, and the rational upper enclosure
$\sqrt{1/2}\le707106781187/10^{12}$; the resulting $\delta$ is below
.000583886. Certificate times are .617, .777, 1.314 and 1.262 seconds,
additional to numerical design generation.
The numerical memory schedules improve on completed greedy/exchange search
in all four cases. The dense numerical comparator is faster, .046--.176
seconds versus .224--.685 for the $L=8$ numerical price, but its matched-incumbent
relative gaps are .9438--1.8741\%. Its 96/late solve reaches the iteration cap;
all reported dense upper bounds come from evaluated tangents, not a success
flag. The two $n=48$, $L=6$ preliminary prices and the weaker original partial
bound remain in the archive; the normalized $L=8$ certificates supersede them
for the principal guarantee.
