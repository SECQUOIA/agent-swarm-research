\section{Computational models, implementation, and retained evidence}
\label{app:computational-models}

\subsection{Kinetic sensitivities and packet spectra}

For \eqref{eq:computational-kinetics}, put $d=k_2-k_1$ and
$u=e^{-k_1t}-e^{-k_2t}$. In the declared log coordinates the sensitivity row is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kinetic-sensitivity-row}
 \left(
 B(t),\quad
 A_0k_1\left\{\frac{k_2u}{d^2}-\frac{k_1t e^{-k_1t}}d\right\},\quad
 A_0k_2\left\{-\frac{k_1u}{d^2}+\frac{k_1t e^{-k_2t}}d\right\}
 \right).
\end{equation}
The tested rates are separated; no near-equal-rate numerical stability claim
is made for this expression. An independently assembled linear mass-balance
system for $(A,B,C)$ has generator
\[
 G=\begin{pmatrix}-k_1&0&0\\k_1&-k_2&0\\0&k_2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For each log parameter $\theta_j$, its sensitivity obeys
$\dot s_j=Gs_j+(\partial_{\theta_j}G)x$.
Initial conditions are $x(0)=(A_0,0,0)^T$,
$s_{\log A_0}(0)=x(0)$, and zero for the rate sensitivities.
We integrate this augmented twelve-dimensional linear system with DOP853
(relative tolerance $2\,10^{-13}$, absolute tolerance $2\,10^{-14}$)
and separately evaluate its matrix exponential at 70 digits. This comparison
uses the balances, not numerical differentiation of the same closed form.
The supplement additionally records complex-step checks, mass conservation,
and the rate-swap identity. All large-instance certificates continue to refer
to the saved rational decimal arrays.

For the complete-packet spectra, let channel coordinates be equally spaced
on $[0,1]$. The three profiles are
$h_m(\lambda)=\exp\{-(\lambda-c_m)^2/(2w_m^2)\}$ with
$(c_1,c_2,c_3)=(.15,.50,.85)$ and $(w_1,w_2,w_3)=(.16,.19,.16)$.
The mean is $h_1A(t)+h_2B(t)+h_3C(t)$, with unit path length,
$C=A_0-A-B$, $A_0=1$, $k_1=.7$, $k_2=.2$, and only $\log k_1$
unknown. Times are $.5,1,\ldots,12$. The latent recursion starts in its
stationary covariance $I_d$, with process covariance $I_d-AA^T$ and
independent observation covariance $I_d$. Its complete observation covariance
has diagonal blocks $2I_d$, lower blocks $A^{t-s}$ and transposed upper blocks.
These are normalized synthetic spectra, without measured-noise or calibration
claims.

\subsection{Numerical proposals and exact checks}

The practical finite-history logdet solver optimizes convex combinations of
complete path information matrices, using a cardinality-layered support oracle
and fully corrective numerical mixture steps. It retains true-covariance
integer incumbents separately. A support tangent bounds the entire path hull,
while the locality correction bounds the true selected information. These are
not the large-exponent approximation-set algorithms of
Section~\ref{sec:approximation}. For fixed weighted trace, a single support
price suffices. Minimum spacing is encoded in feasibility state as well as
used in the covariance bound.

The dense comparator uses the selected-covariance extension
\eqref{eq:vn-covariance}. Numerical continuous proposals use SLSQP; tangent
support prices over an exact-cardinality cube use the $k$ largest gradient
coordinates (block sums for complete packets). Robust versions use common
visits, an epigraph, and nonnegative scenario weights before a common price.
The early mixed-integer comparisons add up to 200 root outer-approximation
rounds and Gurobi branching, within the recorded per-invocation time allowance.
The archive retains the actual optimizer options and tolerances, completed
iteration counts, cap outcomes, selected schedules and tangent points.
A numerical success flag is never a premise of a rational certificate.

The repaired covariance oracle computes virtual-noise information by scalar
filtering and its derivative by the reverse recursions in
Appendix~\ref{app:dense-oracle}. The archival precision-space prototype remains
available to reconstruct historical proposals and its documented instability;
it is not used to assert robust near-unit-correlation numerical evaluation.
The diagonal-split proposal uses SLSQP eigenvalue constraints and a separate
linear dual SDP. The unsuccessful kinetic proposal and the synthetic
\texttt{optimal\_inaccurate} dual status are preserved. Exact factor repair
supplies a valid PSD dual independently of either status. Optional proposal
reproduction uses CVXPY 1.9.2 and Clarabel 0.11.1; verification of its saved
rational dual requires neither package.

Ordinary scalar log certificates use $10^8$ reference and score grids and
rational logarithm enclosures with explicit remainder. Spacing and partial
certificates use coefficient grid $10^{12}$ and feature grid $10^{18}$.
Robust certificates use exact simplex weights and $10^{12}$ final log grids;
all individual normalizer certificates are recomputed. Separator certificates
use grid $10^8$ for both the reference matrix and nuisance witness,
$10^{12}$ for bridge regression coefficients, $10^{18}$ for features,
$10^8$ for scores, and $10^{12}$ for final logarithms. Their exact feasibility
checks include cardinality, covariance promises, positive reference matrices,
positive variance floors and declared pattern caps. The supplement saves
all fractions, not just decimal summaries.

The fresh nested-anchor study uses SLSQP tolerance $10^{-12}$ and at most
1000 iterations, starting from the uniform mixture over all 56 schedules.
Proposed nonnegative weights are floored on a $10^9$ grid and divided by their
integer sum; this creates an exactly feasible mixture. Its exact Schur
information supplies $W=J^{-1}$ and its exact optimizing nuisance matrix
supplies $G$. The support maximum is evaluated over every actual schedule,
so the certified mixture gap is independent of SLSQP convergence. The dense
fractional visits are rounded and adjusted on a $10^8$ grid to retain exact
cardinality. Every stored split is checked by rational leading principal
minors. The fresh block experiment uses exhaustive exact pricing, with no
numerical optimization. All indices in these two small fixtures are zero-based;
the temporal kinetic times in the main experiment are one-based grid multiples.

\subsection{What the archive retains and what each result establishes}

The archive is a frozen record of the relevant investigation, not an additional
set of unproved manuscript claims. Earlier $L=6$ scalar studies, the small
12- and 24-candidate certificates, and the initial $L=8$ run are retained as
precursors; the six-case exact scalar table and four kinetic certificates
provide the main unconstrained evidence. All fixed-split dense and all-scalar
comparisons retain exact common-model checks. The all-diagonal kinetic witness
is the stronger single-case separation, while the unsuccessful synthetic
fixed-point witness remains a negative result.

The first spacing certificate, its integer-interval replay, and its refined
pair-bound certificate are separate artifacts. Their improved guarantees do
not imply a change in incumbent. The full-packet numerical studies retain
both dimensions, their identical greedy schedules, tighter dense bounds,
workspace estimates, and tiny exhaustive diagnostics. The fresh block
certificate is stored separately and does not replace their numerical bounds.
Partial-observation records retain the original weaker bounds and preliminary
$L=6$ cases; the four normalized $L=8$ certificates establish the reported
trace guarantees.

The robust archive retains original hull, greedy, nominal, dense rounding,
independent exchange-polishing, and exact-certification records, including all
scenario values and individual-reference costs. Earlier bounds are not
retroactively assigned to later incumbents. In the fixed-physical study,
primary timeouts and the separately budgeted $L=12$ diagnostic remain distinct.
The refined transfer is a separate numerical postprocessing record. Separator
block-size sweeps retain smaller blocks, five exact certificates, nonnested
partitions, generation and certification costs, and the missed .01 target.
Neither an internal software check nor an independent research-agent review
is described as external peer review or formal proof-assistant verification.

The standalone supplement's README maps every table to its saved artifacts,
provides a complete validation command and separate numerical reproduction
commands, and lists dependency and input provenance. Its validation can be
run after copying the supplement outside the repository. It neither needs
the original repository's module paths nor fetches or redistributes literature
PDFs. Public sensitivity data retain their pinned source and hash; we make no
new licensing assertion about that third-party data.
