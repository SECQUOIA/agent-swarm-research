## Mathematical and scientific assessment

The foundations correctly distinguish selected covariance from full-inverse gating. The equality condition, rank-sensitive Kantorovich consequence, objective-specific approximation chains and scalar sharpness example are consistent. The manuscript correctly distinguishes trace information from conventional A-optimality, local Fisher rank from global identifiability, and a declared regularizer from actual prior evidence. The rate-swap example makes that distinction concrete rather than leaving a generic caveat.

I followed the locality proofs through the residual normalization, near/far covariance terms and row bound. The scalar stationary refinement is restricted to the required constant marginal/noise variances. The full-block proof transports covariance in whitened coordinates and does not commute gains or transitions. The partial-observation argument uses covariance order and range factorization instead of assuming an invalid Euclidean contraction. The general-decay proof establishes the needed property for actual local regressions and explicitly acknowledges both dummy-block and diagonal-normalization reductions. The KL discussion correctly recognizes that a global Gaussian KL bound already implies relative precision control. These points keep the asserted development separate from established localization theory.

The graph FPTAS proof charges the calendar history, cardinality and cooldown states and treats fixed covariance promises as restrictions on an instance sequence. The rational normalization lemma retains exact ranges and forces all basis-factor owners, so the absolute signed-profile error really does imply a relative PSD bound. I checked the maximum-volume argument, rectangular congruence, rank-zero case and profile-count dependence. The matroid proof preserves original rank on restriction, contracts forced owners and uses squared minors with exact coefficient recovery. It does not infer the same result for finite-field inputs, independence oracles, matroid intersections or correlated histories. The criterion consequences and finite-scenario double approximation factor respect those quantifiers.

The practical logdet certificates have a consistent contract: the incumbent is a feasible schedule, the support is over the full feasible family, and the rational reference, locality enclosure and logarithm bounds are checked. A fractional lower bound is never used as a discrete incumbent. The all-diagonal witness follows from a convex lower tangent and weak PSD duality; its common fractional point justifies the intersection-of-upper-supports claim without a minimax interchange. The robust construction treats unknown individual optima with the correct lower/upper directions and prices one common schedule.

The changing-anchor Schur identity retains every nuisance-prior cross block. Its concavity argument establishes the direction of the complete-hull ordering and does not order nonnested partitions or arbitrary unfinished optimizer values. The singleton endpoint discussion uses the actual residual diagonal split, so it does not accidentally equate the last-unanchored convention with the physical-nugget split. I also independently checked this algebra on five rational, non-Markov SPD latent covariance models: all 16 schedules per model, both parameter Schur identities, the cross-representation identity, and nonuniform mixture orderings. All **250 exact matrix checks passed**. See `verification/stage07-review4/check.py` and `results.json`. These finite checks support the audit; the general argument remains the manuscript proof.

## Integration, importance and evidence limits

The paper's unifying question is clear: how to turn a specified correlated experiment into a checked bound on the best feasible schedule. The progression from statistical correctness to relative local control, approximation consequences and practical witnesses is coherent. The contribution table separates old ingredients from the specific developments. The standalone proofs are long, but their detail addresses genuine singularity, representation and arithmetic issues, and fits the user's requested comprehensive scope.

The most consequential experimental claims are comparisons of mathematical relaxation families on common inputs, rather than incompletely controlled timing contests. Negative full-packet results, failed proposals, fixed-physical-grid timeouts, nonnested separator comparisons and certification costs are retained. The new nested study isolates the hierarchy on identical complete schedules. The source reanalysis reports one changed trace choice and unchanged tested D/A choices without extrapolating to all published results. Physical noise validation, exact real ODE sensitivities, global nonlinear identifiability, continuous scenario robustness and efficient implementation of the theoretical spectral sets are all expressly outside the evidence. The READMEs maintain those distinctions.

This review does not revalidate all 46 archived certificates, inspect every code branch or read all 58 cited works in full. Those are limits of this independent review, not newly identified manuscript defects. I found no remaining required revision in my scope.
