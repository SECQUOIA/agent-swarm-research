## Mathematical assessment

- Section 4, lines 281–307: the common feasible family, common information dimension, and SPD priors make both raw and standardized criteria well-defined. The scenario-dependent congruence calculation is correct, including the requirement that priors transform. Only standardized efficiency has the asserted independent-coordinate invariance.
- Lines 309–325: nonnegative weights summing to one justify the minimum-to-average step. The support maximization correctly uses one common feasible path. Scenario-specific references and error bounds do not break that argument. Exact clipping and normalization are legitimate witness construction; negative weights and approximately normalized weights are not silently admitted.
- Proposition `prop:robust-normalizers`, lines 327–353: the standardizer interval directions are correct. Upper bounds on each individual optimum are subtracted in the incumbent lower certificate; lower bounds are subtracted in the robust upper certificate. Clipping the final upper bound at zero is valid. The two efficiency statements distinguish absolute standardized performance from performance relative to the robust optimum. Lower references must come from the same feasible family; the text correctly warns about using a larger reference family. No continuous-uncertainty guarantee is claimed.
- Corollary `cor:robust-fptas`, lines 355–397: the block-diagonal order is fixed at `qp`; the common graph can use the largest required calendar memory while retaining the original common feasibility constraints. The spectral representative is simultaneous across scenarios. Transferring local and true information gives the stated factor `a`. Exact rational determinants permit ranking without a logarithm or root oracle. The set-based standardizers cost a second factor: `a^p D_s^* <= d_s <= D_s^*` gives `E_* <= E_C <= E_*/a`, and hence the returned schedule attains `a^2` of the true robust optimum. The epsilon allocation and polynomial bit-complexity conclusion follow from the referenced fixed-promise results, including the full-history cap. This is explicitly not a practical solver runtime claim.
- Appendix `app:robust-witnesses`, lines 206–234: both examples are correct. The shared and separate linear support prices are respectively `3/5` and `6/5`, yielding the claimed improvement. The `9/16` sharp ratio arises for the displayed maximizing tie, and the text confines this limitation to the same-set comparison recipe. The true versus fractional-hull gap is not conflated with memory error or with infeasible independent scenario choices.
- The broader section consistently separates feasible-experiment lower bounds, continuous-relaxation lower bounds, and certified global upper witnesses. Its all-split argument fixes a common fractional point and does not interchange max and min. The nested-anchor statement concerns full feasible-schedule hull optima, with appropriate caveats for unfinished numerical witnesses and nonnested partitions.

## Primary-source assessment

I read `literature/AGENTS.md` before source inspection. Important local equations were checked through fresh extraction of the original PDFs, not only KB notes. Temporary extracts were removed rather than packaged as manuscript material.

- Burclová–Pázman, primary preprint <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.06226>, Section 3.2, PDF pages 6–7: separately optimized criteria normalize the minimum efficiency and are handled with affine supports. This is criterion robustness, and the manuscript does not misdescribe it as the present covariance-scenario theorem. The bibliography explicitly identifies the inspected preprint. A requested web screenshot failed; the original PDF text was available and inspected.
- Maus et al. (2010), local original PDF pages 2–3, especially equations (11)–(12): relative-efficiency maximin under AR(1) correlation uncertainty is explicit. Their computational candidate designs come from nominal genetic-algorithm runs. The manuscript's limited attribution is supported and does not imply certified global standardizers in that paper.
- Duarte–Sagnol–Wong (2018), local original PDF pages 1–7, Sections 2–3 and reaction-study context: minimax design, scenario exchange, and efficiency bounds are genuine predecessors. The manuscript does not claim that these generic ingredients are new or inherit an unconditional continuous-uncertainty certificate from numerical inner solves.
- Wang–Yue (2026), local original PDF pages 2–4, equation (6) and Section 3.1: its information is additive over time, with exact-count subset refinement and robust scenario strategies. Describing it as independent-trial sampling information is supported. The manuscript does not inherit its questionable expectation equivalence for E-optimality.
- Chowdhary–Attia–Alexanderian, <https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09137v2>, Section 3.1 and Section 4.2 equations (47a)–(47b): exact-budget conditional-Bernoulli policy optimization and correlated observation noise are explicit. The manuscript appropriately recognizes this direct robust binary-design predecessor while distinguishing the present rational finite-scenario certificate.

No corrections are requested from this review. The later integrated introduction should retain these scoped attributions rather than elevate generic normalization, maximin design, or scenario exchange into novelty claims.
