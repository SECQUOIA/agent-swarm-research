## Mathematical assessment

1. **Weighted-trace FPTAS (`thm:trace-fptas`).** The rational envelopes follow from the stated locality bounds. In the partial-observation case the replacement of the square root by the upper signal bound is conservative. The cap at `L=n-1` gives exact histories. The minimality argument bounding `2^L` has the correct direction, including an early full-history cap. The information comparison with a PSD weight and prior proves the factor `(1-delta)/(1+delta)` without requiring a positive optimum. The explicit cooldown state resolves the otherwise problematic cases `L=0` and `g>L`; clamping `g` makes a binary-encoded enormous gap harmless. The rational covariance construction, principal solves, and accumulated path comparisons give a credible Turing proof, rather than merely an arithmetic operation count. The fixed contraction/variance promises and explicit input sensitivities are necessary restrictions and are stated.

2. **Individual-channel boundary (`prop:channel-hardness`).** The proposed cubic-graph covariance has eigenvalues in `[3/4,5/4]`, while its latent component is positive definite and bounded by `(7/8)V`. The Neumann estimate supplies `x_i >= 2/3`; any internal edge contributes twice to the summed degree term, giving the claimed `1/9` information gap. The chosen epsilon remains inverse-polynomial and forces an independent set when one exists. This reduction does not contradict the complete-packet theorem.

3. **Normalization (`lem:spectral-normalization`).** Rational LDL terms avoid irrational data, and the weighted maximum-volume basis is only a coverage argument, not an algorithmic oracle. Requiring the distinct owners ensures that the selected labeled rank-one contributions really occur in every accepted object, including multiple basis factors belonging to one atom. The exact range filters and rectangular reconstruction are essential and correct. Filtering each atom's diagonal at `4p` preserves the target trial because every atom has at most `p` factors. Dyadic normalization has polynomial bit length even with very small nonzero rational pivots.

4. **DAG cover (`thm:dag-spectral-set`).** The quantifier is genuinely one representative matrix for each entire target matrix, not a direction-dependent lower envelope. Signed floors are handled correctly: accumulated residuals lie in a common interval even if path lengths differ. Equal profiles therefore give an absolute entry error below `Nh`, and the forced identity floor converts this to two-sided relative PSD order. The state coordinate bound accommodates negative sums; fixed `p` makes both the trial count and profile count polynomial. Zero information, singular priors, exact kernels, infeasibility, and the empty path are covered.

5. **Criterion and locality transfer (`subsec:spectral-consequences`, `cor:true-spectral-cover`).** Determinant, determinant root, and log determinant are not conflated. The inverse comparisons occur on a common range. Estimability of contrasts is retained, and singular full-parameter A cost is handled explicitly. The true-information transfer uses the local sandwich on both schedules; its upper and lower factors are correct. The specified epsilon allocation satisfies the stated relative cover. Objective evaluation is appropriately qualified, and the fixed-degree algebraic comparison for E-optimality is valid.

6. **Represented matroids (`app:matroid-proof`).** Restriction retains the original rank, and contraction is performed only after checking forced-owner independence. The resulting bases lift to actual original bases. Nonnegative squared minors certify exact profile attainability in characteristic zero. The tensor interpolation is an alternative implementation of the cited established method; its degree, grid size, and evaluation bit lengths remain polynomial at fixed matrix dimension even when matroid rank grows. Deletion recovery keeps the original row dimension during rank drops, which is crucial. A failed deletion remains impossible after subsequent deletions; this proves that the final witness is exactly a base. The zero-information case and `q'=0` case are valid. The finite-field and mixed-determinant counterexamples correctly limit the proof, without claiming hardness of all broader algorithms.

7. **Focused scalar predecessor (`app:scalar-prior`).** The normalization preserves information rankings and removes sensitivity/noise scale from the joint condition-number bound. The positive process regularization has the stated selected-covariance sandwich. The augmented Gaussian graph has constant-width bags containing the target. Keeping the only positive variance cost until the singleton target bag avoids the printed private-block inverse problem. The posterior-variance ratio converts to a relative information bound because normalization provides a constant lower bound on the best nonempty information. Padding is explicitly restricted to unconstrained cardinality. The text does not misrepresent the predecessor's spectral-rounding model as an implemented rational algorithm.
