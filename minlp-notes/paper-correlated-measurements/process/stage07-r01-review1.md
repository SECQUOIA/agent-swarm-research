## Mathematical assessment

The statistical experiment is specified sufficiently precisely: fixed known parameter-independent covariance, mean sensitivities at a nominal parameter, nonadaptive complete packets, and a declared fixed prior or regularizer. The paper distinguishes these assumptions from nonlinear posterior claims, global identifiability, estimated covariance and arbitrary within-packet channel selection. The selected-covariance information, conditional-response sensitivity correction, gating Schur identity, equality criterion and rank refinement are correct. The Kantorovich proof gives the claimed uniform comparison without requiring a new matrix inequality. Its design consequences account for the prior and singular kernels correctly. The scalar sharpness sequence is valid as an infimum, rather than asserting an attained rational equality.

I followed every locality derivation. The residual-to-information transfer correctly uses equal singular-value spectra of the two Gram matrices, rather than incorrectly inverting the residual sandwich. The overlapping-history near terms are retained, and the geometric double sum and combined row bound agree. Fresh local regressions are essential and consistently used. The signed scalar transport and constant-variance first-gain refinement are justified; the nonstationary counterexample correctly prevents applying that refinement under only an upper variance bound. The complete-block far-pair identity uses ordered transition/update products, so it does not require commutativity. Whitening invariance is established by block orthogonal congruence. The spacing floor accounts for the maximum number of observations and the last gap; the row-packing recurrence is an upper bound even though separate pair bounds need not be attained by one schedule.

The general-decay weighted-conjugation proof controls both row and column sums of a nonsymmetric perturbation, uses a valid inverse Neumann bound, and then bounds the actual local predictor. It requires no hidden upper diagonal bound. The supplied-metric reduction is a valid rational PSD condition. The partial-observation proof transports covariances in their own metrics; the singular-state factorization avoids inverting a latent covariance. Its gain and endpoint constants are compatible and yield the displayed near and far terms. Exact-history, zero-signal and zero-contraction cases are handled directly.

I also read the approximation and certification developments in full. The graph feasibility state retains spacing cooldown beyond the information window. The weighted-trace complexity argument separates fixed promises from growing dimensions and addresses rational bit lengths. The spectral cover preserves exact ranges, forces the basis owners, bounds each retained atom after rational normalization, and uses signed floor profiles without assuming nonnegative off-diagonal entries. The matroid argument preserves original rank under restriction and deletion; characteristic-zero squared minors prevent cancellation. Criterion transfer, singular-domain treatment and the two-factor robust-normalizer argument have the required quantifiers.

The practical support bounds price the complete feasible family and inflate only the data term. Rational witness feasibility and outward locality-error enclosures are explicit. The all-diagonal lower witness uses a convex tangent in the split variables and a valid PSD dual bound; it is not an unsupported interchange of maximum and minimum. The separator hierarchy retains the nuisance prior cross terms and applies Schur concavity across changing representations. The restrictions to nested anchor sets and complete-schedule hulls are correctly stated. The dense filter/adjoint derivation is exact, and its numerical stability discussion does not substitute floating evaluation for a certificate.

## Literature and scientific positioning

I checked the local primary Liu et al. article, particularly its Section IV distinction between truncating covariance before inversion and gating the full inverse. The manuscript credits this established distinction and does not inherit the source’s overly broad wording that weak correlation is necessary for equality.

I checked the original PDF of the 2025 Kaminetz thesis, pp. 13–14, including Theorem 4.3 and its displayed marginal/supermodularity argument. The manuscript’s exact counterexample targets the stated claim and carefully separates fixed-order and pivot-first interpretations. I checked the distinct [March 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05709v1) and its original local PDF: its Kaporin normalization and Definition 1.1 match the manuscript, and the thesis supermodularity assertion is not transferred to that paper. The local Schäfer et al. article supports the distinction between its geometric KL guarantee and its added-noise construction; the manuscript correctly acknowledges that a global KL bound can itself imply relative precision control.

I also verified the recent [Bansal–Xu preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05468v1), Theorem 1.1 and its invertibility setup. The discussion’s growing-dimension A/E partition-constraint hardness statement is a conservative consequence, not an overstatement of that source.

The contribution table and detailed prior discussions consistently separate inherited factorization, filtering, convexity, generalized design, profile interpolation and normalization ideas from the precisely qualified combined guarantees. The computational claims distinguish rational input certificates, numerical sensitivity consistency, solver proposals, relaxation strength and runtime observations. Positive and negative experiments are retained, with no universal dominance or physical-noise-validation claim.
