\title{Rounding switching controls under a hard switch budget:\\sharp minimax bounds and exact algorithms}

\begin{abstract}
Combinatorial integral approximation rounds a relaxed control on $n$ modes to
an integer control that activates one mode at a time; the error is the
largest deviation between cumulative mode allocations. We study this rounding
when the integer control may switch at most $s$ times, so that grid
refinement cannot remove the error. For measurable controls on $[0,T]$ with
$0\le s\le3$ and $n\ge s+2$, the worst-case error is exactly
$T\max\{1/(s+2),\,1/(n((n/(n-1))^{s+1}-1))\}$; hence the lower bound
$T/(s+2)$ of Sager and Zeile is exact only up to $n=4,7,11$ for $s=1,2,3$.
The proof combines heavy-mode rounding with sharp one-sided reach bounds,
using exact dual certificates for three switches. For more modes than
activation blocks, mode removal gives exact plateau ranges and the sharp
first mode-count correction to the limit $T/(s+1)$. On finite grids we
derive an explicit one-switch minimax formula for every grid when $n\ge3$,
and exact three-mode values on five to seven cells, which settle the
continuous three-mode, two-switch value $T/5$ and disprove specific published
finite-grid claims. For a given input on $N$ cells, exact algorithms use
$O(nN)$ operations for one switch and, for each fixed budget, operations
polynomial in $N$ and linear in $n$. A switch-preserving uniform-grid
transfer with sharp error below one cell width turns exact coarse-grid optima
into additive certificates for the continuous optimum, illustrated on a
public Lotka--Volterra relaxed control. Lean formalizations verify the
arbitrary-grid one-switch formula, the transfer and coarsening results, and
the exact three-mode values.
\end{abstract}
