## Proof and scope audit

### Instance algorithms

**Three-candidate theorem, `10-instance-algorithms.tex:19–49`.** The largest-terminal-mass dominance step is valid: replacing the final mode cannot worsen the omitted-mode maximum or the final deficit term. The treatment of the two mass leaders and the best remaining prefix allocation exhausts initial-mode choices, including ties. The constant schedule is correctly compared separately. The third mass leader suffices for constant-time omitted-maximum evaluations. The stated arithmetic cost includes reading and accumulating all data; selecting a constant number of leaders requires no full sort. Fixed initial modes and subsets of permitted boundaries have the claimed scope.

**Crossing queries and residual completion, lines 51–89.** The difference of the varying terms increases by at least the elapsed time, so the crossing is unique when present. Before it the decreasing term controls, and afterward the nondecreasing term controls. Consequently the neighboring permitted boundaries suffice, including empty and extreme-boundary cases as specified. The random-access complexity properly excludes input preprocessing. The final-residual formula remains valid when residuals are negative: a negative endpoint value that cannot control a signed term is already covered by the earlier discrepancy or the opposite endpoint. The exchange among a fixed eligible set is sound and does not imply a greedy prefix algorithm.

**Subset assignment and fixed budget, lines 91–156.** A mode's entire disconnected block subset determines its full-horizon component error. Checking block endpoints is sufficient by monotonicity, and the empty subset correctly retains the terminal mass of an unused mode. The recurrence partitions the assigned block set exactly once among modes. Its transition count is `3^k` per mode; the stated `k 2^k` preprocessing and `n 2^k` traceback storage are sufficient. Enumeration of exactly `min(s+1,N)` nominal blocks covers shorter schedules because equal-labeled subdivision preserves the physical control. This also verifies the large-budget and no-switch boundary cases. The result is polynomial for fixed budget, with no unsupported fixed-parameter tractability claim in the budget alone. Rational bit lengths remain polynomial under the stated sums, differences, and comparisons.

**Dwell constraints and candidate labels, lines 158–205.** Dwell is imposed on maximal consecutive assigned strings, so separated appearances cannot pool their durations and artificial adjacent subdivisions do not create new dwell obligations. Empty assignments remain eligible. This makes admissibility separable by mode and preserves the full-budget coverage proof. Infeasibility is handled explicitly. The candidate-label exchange correctly accounts for newly omitted mass: an outside label forces one of the top `d` masses to be unused, and one of the role's `d` cheapest labels is available for replacement. Each replacement removes an outside label and introduces none. The special case `n=d`, arbitrary ties, and unrestricted-role scope are addressed.

**Continuous enumeration, lines 207–244.** The LP includes every word and a nondecreasing list of closed input cells for switch times. Repeated labels and zero-length blocks ensure complete coverage of schedules with fewer switches; allowing either incident input cell covers boundary switches. It is correct not to truncate the number of blocks at the number of input cells. The cumulative service formula is affine in the switch variables. Monotonicity between actual switches justifies checking only those endpoints, even when an interval crosses input breakpoints. The one-sided variant keeps the correct sign and is distinct from the full objective.

The bounded polytope has `k` variables and the claimed order of inequalities. A vertex of a bounded polytope has active normals spanning the ambient variable space, even when the feasible set is lower-dimensional. Thus enumerating nonsingular `k`-row active systems is complete. The word/cell count and fixed-budget bit-complexity statement are valid. The text accurately presents this as a small-instance exact benchmark rather than a scalable general solver.

### Transfer and coarsening

**Supported uniform transfer, `11-transfer-and-coarsening.tex:11–53`.** The original occupations give a feasible fractional flow with integer chain bounds. Integral flow produces exactly one supported selection per cell and floor/ceiling prefix counts. Every endpoint error is strictly below the mesh width, including the integral-prefix case. Selected and inactive component errors are monotone within each grid cell even though the original integer control may switch there, so the endpoint estimate is a whole-horizon estimate.

The chronology argument separately establishes switch preservation: positive occupation supplies a source time inside each successive cell, and its source block index cannot decrease. Deleting repeated selections of a block and merging equal labels gives the stated subsequence property. This is the necessary extra argument beyond unrestricted support rounding. The polynomial construction claim is compatible with rational occupations, integer capacities, and explicit grid length.

**Instance/minimax transfer and binary case, lines 55–103.** Attainment is used correctly in both strict upper inequalities. For minimax transfer, selecting a grid maximizer avoids the invalid step of taking a supremum of pointwise strict inequalities. The first minimax inequality also uses cell averaging, appropriately accounting for different input classes. For binary nonuniform transfer the two possible next discrepancies are at distance at most the permitted interval's width; the interval argument ensures a supported feasible choice. Pure cells preserve the previous discrepancy. The one-cell example proves sharpness for transfer of a supplied schedule, which is the stated claim.

**Universal sharpness, lines 105–137.** The first-cell discrepancy gives the grid lower bound, and the constant final-mode schedule attains it. Direct integration of the continuous competitor gives `(n-1)/n^2` and the repeated-cycle value `(n-1)/(n^2 m)`. Their switch counts agree with the specified budgets, including the final continuation. Subtracting a feasible continuous competitor gives a lower bound on the instance-optimum gap, which is enough for the universal coefficient claim. The four-mode example yields at least `9/16`, and the source interpretation concerns an instance comparison, not a difference of minimax values. Those distinctions are explicit and correct.

**Certified coarsening, lines 141–205.** Exact coarse cumulative data suffice to evaluate the returned schedule for the original input. The transfer theorem gives the open lower endpoint; the coarse optimum supplies a feasible upper endpoint. A common-refinement sweep integrates nonaligned rational piecewise-constant input in the stated arithmetic count. Fixed-budget bit complexity and the separate parameter-only dependence on budget and requested resolution are justified. The sharper binary/one-switch certificates have correctly non-strict half-mesh lower bounds. Zero switches, clipping negative lower endpoints, equality before clipping, and nested versus nonnested grid feasibility are distinguished correctly.

**Dwell obstruction and perturbation, lines 207–241.** On odd grids both dwell-constrained runs would have to have length exactly one half, which is impossible. Constants remain feasible and have error one half, whereas the continuous input itself has zero error. Thus this is an actual nonconvergence example under refinement. The perturbation certificate follows from the one-Lipschitz optimum map and the triangle inequality. Its strict suboptimality bound is `h+2 delta`, and a componentwise rate bound integrates to `delta=rho T`.
