## Independent mathematical assessment

- **Foundations:** The cumulative-allocation characterization is correct for measurable controls; the derivative can be assigned any simplex value on its null exceptional set. The schedule parameterization correctly handles vanishing blocks and repeated modes. The telescoping formula for occupations makes schedule compactness valid even when blocks collapse. Minima and maxima are attained, and both discrepancy criteria satisfy the claimed Lipschitz estimate. Endpoint monotonicity requires only almost-everywhere derivative signs, so averaging inputs really does preserve the grid instance problem. The resulting minimax domination has the correct direction despite the different input classes. The no-switch baseline follows directly from terminal masses.
- **Uniform continuous input, Theorem `thm:uniform`:** Starting from the original objective, the negative discrepancy at the end of a block yields `(n-1)t_j <= n t_{j-1}+nE` even when the selected mode occurred earlier. The omitted-mode lower bound is necessary precisely because `k<n`. Summing the geometric recurrence gives the printed denominator `n(r^k-1)`. The distinct-mode construction saturates negative block-end error and bounds all positive errors by `T/n`; it does not incorrectly exclude repeated-mode competitors.
- **Uniform grid, Theorem `thm:uniform-grid`:** Error integrality and the floor recurrence are consistent with the original objective. Truncating an iterate at `N` preserves the negative discrepancy inequality; the positive discrepancy is bounded by `N/n`. The proof that a successful recurrence starts with a positive increment covers `N < n-1` and zero-length padding. The floor/ceiling identity in the strict-gap proof is valid, including `n=2`. Since `C=ceil(nE)<nE+1`, the final inequality is strictly less than `E+(n-1)/n` as claimed.
- **Three-term formula and constructive one-switch theorem:** I derived the full endpoint error before eliminating redundant terms. The terminal positive error of the first mode and activation error of the second mode are indeed bounded by the displayed last and middle terms, respectively. Endpoints and the binary empty-omitted-set convention are sound. In the large-mass branch, mass conservation permits at most one additional mass above `E`; the proposed switch time is at most `E`. In the all-small branch, failure of the candidates yields exactly `(2n-1)E < n-1-(n-2)m_q-m_r`. The ordering inequality for the top two masses has the stated sign and supplies the contradiction. The threshold at five modes is correct. The algorithm's `O(n)` arithmetic statement explicitly excludes cumulative integration and is justified.
- **Grid corollary:** Moving a single switch changes each occupation by at most the displacement and cannot add switches. The refinement argument establishes only the leading coefficient, which is exactly what the corollary claims.
- **Three-cell proposition:** Choosing the two largest masses makes every omitted mass at most one, because otherwise the largest three would sum to more than three. The active occupations at time two are both one, and the final occupations and rank bounds give the claimed `2-3/n`. The uniform lower bound applies to every admissible schedule, including a constant one. The published-conjecture examples have correct constants and satisfy `1 <= s <= N-2`; the source page confirms both printed branches and restrictions.

## Tests actually performed

All independently implemented mathematical checks passed. Code: `verification/reviewer03/stage01-round01/independent.py`; exact counts: `results.json` and `results.log` in the same directory. This program imports **no author verification code**.

1. **70 uniform-grid instances**, covering `2 <= n <= 5`, every `1 <= k < n`, and `1 <= N <= 7`. Enumerated **35,581 schedules** across all admissible shorter block counts, all integer boundary sets, and all words, including repeated modes. Computed the original maximum absolute discrepancy using scaled integers. Every minimum equals the printed scalar recurrence; every strict gap inequality passes using rational arithmetic.
2. **878 continuous fixed-word LPs**, for `2 <= n <= 5` and every `1 <= k < n`. Variables are the ordered switch times and error. Constraints directly impose both signs of every coordinate's cumulative discrepancy at every block endpoint. Taking the minimum over every word, including repeated words and zero-length blocks, agrees with the printed uniform formula within `1e-8`.
3. **5,057 rational piecewise-constant profiles** for the one-switch construction: exhaustive half-simplex three-cell profiles for `n=3,4,5`; deterministic random rational profiles for `3 <= n <= 15` and one to seven cells; and eleven deliberately concentrated profiles that force the second all-small-mass candidate. All schedules satisfy the theorem's error bound. Branch counts: 3,181 large-mass cases, 1,865 first-candidate small-mass cases, 11 second-candidate small-mass cases.
4. **40,456 direct three-term identities**, with errors evaluated on the union of input-cell boundaries and the actual switch time, including switch times zero and `T`. All comparisons use exact fractions.
5. **4,667 three-cell upper-bound constructions** checked directly and **48 uniform sharpness cases**, `3 <= n <= 50`, checked by minimization over the four possible switch boundaries.
6. Verified every frozen manifest hash. Executed the frozen boundary checker and captured its startup failure, supporting R03-01.
