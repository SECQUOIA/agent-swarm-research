## Mathematical audit: no-issue findings

1. **One-switch dominance and crossing search:** `10-instance-algorithms.tex:19–68`, Theorem 13.1. Independently substituting the terminal masses into the three-term error expression confirms that replacing the final mode by the largest available mass cannot worsen the omitted maximum. The three initial candidates then cover every case, with the binary case treated separately. Constants, ties, prescribed initial modes, and an empty permitted-boundary set are covered. The strict increase of `2t-A_p(t)` justifies the crossing search even for flat pieces of `A_p`. Input reading and preparing the permitted-boundary list are expressly excluded from the additional query bound. There is no unsupported claim that label-dependent dwell restrictions preserve dominance.

2. **Prefix completion:** `10-instance-algorithms.tex:70–89`, Lemma 13.2. The starting error covers the negative endpoint of every subsequently inactive coordinate and the positive endpoint of the subsequently active coordinate. This explains why the final formula uses signed residuals, not their absolute values. The exchange argument remains valid when some residuals are negative and when the fixed eligible set excludes the prefix's last label. The limitation to a supplied prefix is explicit.

3. **Subset assignment and full grid enumeration:** `10-instance-algorithms.tex:91–156`, Proposition 13.3 and Theorem 13.4. The subset costs include both disconnected occurrences and unused-mode terminal errors. The recurrence correctly partitions the nominal blocks among modes. Its transition count is exactly the familiar sum `sum_S 2^|S|=3^k`; storing a minimizing subset for each mode/state permits the claimed traceback. Subdividing maximal runs proves coverage for *at most* the budget, including `s>=N-1`, without disallowing repeats. Intermediate values are selected among rational component costs, so no recurrence creates an exponential rational denominator growth. The stated arithmetic and fixed-budget bit-complexity claims are appropriate; no fixed-parameter claim in the budget alone is made.

4. **Dwell and candidate labels:** `10-instance-algorithms.tex:158–205`, Lemma 13.5 and preceding extension. Dwell applies to maximal physical runs after merging adjacent nominal blocks, including the horizon-end runs. This is a property of one mode's subset and survives subdivision. Consequently the separation argument is valid, unlike an arbitrary transition constraint. The candidate-set exchange frees a label among the role's `d` cheapest modes while controlling the newly omitted mass using an omitted top-`d` mass label. The proof handles ties and the case `n=d`. Its restriction to the unrestricted role-assignment problem is clear.

5. **Continuous exact benchmark:** `10-instance-algorithms.tex:207–244`, equation (119). I independently formed the constraints using block-duration variables rather than switch-time variables. The number of closed time-cell assignments is the stated combinations-with-replacement count. Repeated labels and zero-length blocks provide coverage of shorter schedules, even when the number of blocks exceeds the number of input cells. No input-breakpoint inequalities are missing: error is monotone throughout each schedule block, despite input slope changes inside it. For the one-sided objective, retaining only `W-A<=E` has the correct sign. The bounded-polytope vertex argument works in lower affine dimension: an extreme point still has a full-rank active set in the ambient variable space. Bounds on rational linear-system solutions justify polynomial bit complexity for fixed block count. The complete enumerator is consistently described as a small-instance benchmark.

6. **Supported uniform transfer:** `11-transfer-and-coarsening.tex:11–53`, Theorem 14.1. The fractional occupations satisfy conservation and the integer floor/ceiling capacities on the mode chains. Integral flow therefore supplies a supported one-hot selection per cell. Integer prefixes agree exactly at integral cumulative values and differ by strictly less than one otherwise. Cellwise monotonicity transfers the strict endpoint bound to the entire interval. Choosing a positive-occupation interior time in every cell yields nondecreasing original block indices; removing repeat selections and merging equal labels then cannot increase the number of changes. The finitely switching assumption suffices for choosing times away from switches. The network and rational occupation calculations have polynomial size in the explicitly specified grid and schedule data.

7. **Strict minimax transfer and binary nonuniform transfer:** `11-transfer-and-coarsening.tex:55–103`, Corollary 14.2 and Proposition 14.3. The minimax proof correctly uses an attained *grid* maximizer and the previously established cell-averaging comparison. Thus it does not incorrectly exchange a supremum with a pointwise strict inequality. In the binary construction, the two candidate endpoint errors have distance at most the maximum cell width, one is no greater than the upper limit and the other no smaller than the lower limit. At least one is feasible. The pure-cell choices preserve support and leave the discrepancy unchanged. The one-cell half/half schedule proves the stated supplied-schedule sharpness, with a non-strict half-width guarantee.

8. **Sharp coefficient and source correction:** `11-transfer-and-coarsening.tex:105–137`, Proposition 14.4. The first grid cell forces error `(n-1)/n`; the all-final-mode schedule attains it over the remaining horizon. Independently integrating the sequential equal-duration continuous competitor gives `(n-1)/n^2`, and repeating the cycle gives `(n-1)/(n^2*m)`. The competitor has `nm-1` actual changes because its final label continues into the long pure tail. With `N=nm+1`, it satisfies the cited budget range. A continuous upper bound is sufficient for the claimed lower bound on the instance gap; no unproved exact continuous optimum is used. The four-mode example has gap at least `9/16`, and the text correctly refrains from turning that fact into a difference of minimax values.

9. **Coarsening, boundary conventions, and perturbation:** `11-transfer-and-coarsening.tex:141–241`, Theorem 14.5, Example 14.6, and Corollary 14.7. The actual coarse cumulative values determine the returned schedule's error even for a measurable input. Integration of rational piecewise-constant supplied data on the common refinement has the claimed linear arithmetic cost and polynomial bit lengths. The distinction between such data and arbitrary behavior inside an input cell is explicit in the implementation documentation. The chosen cell count yields strictly sub-`epsilon*T` error with the universal transfer theorem. The half-width specializations appropriately use non-strict inequalities. Negative lower bounds may be clipped only with a closed endpoint; a raw zero lower bound stays strict. Nested and nonnested fine grids are distinguished correctly. The input perturbation contributes one `delta` to each end of the interval, giving the stated strict suboptimality bound `h+2*delta`. Finally, the odd-grid dwell example really has only constant feasible schedules under the stipulated maximal-run dwell convention, so its nonconvergence is substantive.
