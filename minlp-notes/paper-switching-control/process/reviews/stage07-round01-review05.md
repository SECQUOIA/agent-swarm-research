## Mathematical assessment

- **Foundations and uniform inputs (Sections 2–3).** The distinction between the continuous adversary class and the grid-constant adversary class is explicit. Endpoint monotonicity proves exact cell averaging for every grid schedule, supplying the otherwise nontrivial comparison between minimax values. Compactness establishes attainment and justifies the later strict minimax transfer. The geometric uniform lower bound applies to repeated-mode competitors. The scalar grid recurrence handles zero-length padding, early arrival, and the integer threshold domain. The one-switch formula includes constants through the stated endpoint convention.

- **Heavy modes and distinct reach (Sections 4–6).** Maximizing terminal flow for a heavy mode guarantees some repetition; the deadline reordering then needs only the first repeated prefix and does not silently prescribe the repeated mode. Extension and restriction preserve the switch bound. The latest feasible inverse convention covers flat cumulative-complement functions. The two- and three-block arguments correctly use distinct available labels and uncapped identities only under the contradiction hypothesis. For four blocks, the event LP is used in the necessary-condition direction. Its larger feasible set is harmless for the certified lower bound. The ten symmetry types, the exceptional five-mode availability, and stabilization for dimensions at least nine are justified. The matrix identities and multiplier signs establish the claimed weighted bound without numerical optimization.

- **Exact small budgets and structural counterexamples (Sections 7–8).** The full minimax formula has the stated spare-mode range. The equal-terminal-mass result is kept separate from unrestricted minimax. The transition counts 5, 8, and 12 and their polynomial sign arguments agree with the formulas. In Proposition 8.1 the capped inverse estimate, threshold perturbations, attaining word, and exclusion of repeated words establish the exact instance optimum `18673/18396`. Its scaled consequence is only a minimax lower bound. The two adjacent-pair counterexamples concern stronger assertions than the heavy-mode theorem and do not contradict it.

- **General bounds and boundary consequences (Sections 9–10).** Both terminal-mass choices in Lemma 9.1 have the correct monotonicity direction and positive remaining threshold. The constant-mode branch is covered. The transformed negative seed must remain negative, as the text requires. The elementary and seeded coefficients, their plateau tests, and first-order asymptotic conclusions are consistent. Neither a sufficient plateau test nor the seeded second-order upper coefficient is promoted to an exact characterization. The all-light proof reaches the horizon in the asserted equal-mass band, and the omitted-mode argument supplies the matching instance lower bound. The classical small-mode comparison is properly attributed and does not inherit an unsupported hard-budget tightness claim.

- **Finite-grid minimax (Sections 11–12).** Both obstruction families are needed and represented. In particular, the all-initial-modes family is not restricted by an erroneous second-mass upper bound. The limiting coverage argument, compressed realization, interval elimination, and rational operation/bit-complexity statements agree. Boundary cases in the three-mode residue formula include the separate one-cell value. The floor-history transition characterization proves realizability for every length, rather than assuming every enumerated floor array is physical. The five-, six-, and seven-cell enumerations cover their full classes. The exceptional six-history orbit and its three repair words provide the seven-cell upper bound, and the displayed rational input supplies the matching lower bound. The continuous consequences `F_{3,2}=T/5` and `T/7 <= F_{3,3} <= T/6` use the correct normalization and have matching stated scope.

- **Algorithms and transfer (Sections 13–14).** The dominance and subset-partition arguments permit repeated labels and unused modes. Subdivision preserves maximal runs, which is essential for the full-budget dwell extension. The complexity claims distinguish arithmetic cost, rational bit length, fixed budget, and input preprocessing. The continuous LP enumeration covers coincident switches, input-knot ties, repeated labels, and block counts exceeding the number of input cells. Supported grid rounding gives chronological occurrences in the original word, hence cannot increase its switch count. The universal coefficient-one sharpness is an instance-optimum statement; it is not presented as a sharp minimax gap. Coarsening strictness, nonaligned integration, nested-grid scope, input perturbation, and the odd-grid dwell obstruction are consistent.

- **Computations and higher reach (Section 15 and Appendix A).** The public profile is explicitly normalized and quantized; the exact assertions concern that specified input. Same-grid budget comparisons and nested-grid certificates use the correct admissible sets. The continuous public optimum is evaluated off the fine grid, not inferred from a fine-grid minimum. The appendix's conditional exclusion inequality has an explicitly unproved weighted premise. The negative event witness is demonstrably nonphysical. The interpolation lemma establishes precisely the chronology requirement it states, without imposing inverse identities. The final primal/dual result proves the optimum of one specified chamber only. None of these appendix limitations is hidden in an earlier theorem dependency.

## Independent verification

The following checks passed in this review. The exact checks do not rely on the manuscript's numerical discovery outputs.

1. **Clean build and complete offline suite.** A relocated build from source, with the copied PDF and bibliography output removed first, produced 56 pages. There were no undefined-reference, overfull-box, or underfull-box warnings. `verification/run_all.py` completed successfully, including all stage proof, integrity, algorithm, derived-data, and generated-table checks.

2. **Independent all-dimension four-block certificates.** `independent_four.py` imports no project checker. It rebuilds the quotient rows from labeled excluded-set events and its own orbit canonicalization. It verifies all 179 finite certificates. For each of the ten polynomial cases, it checks enough exact integer evaluations to establish the identities using an explicit degree bound, separately checks the denominator factorization and shifted coefficient signs, and performs additional direct matrix evaluation. All ten cases passed; the log records 69 exact polynomial-system evaluations. This is a separate arithmetic reconstruction from the author's symbolic-polynomial implementation, with the manuscript's stabilization proof still essential.

3. **Independent all-word structural optimum.** `independent_optimum.py` forms and solves exact three-variable vertex systems directly from the input table, without importing the reach code or author checker. All 27 words and 972 affine switching cells give global one-sided optimum `18673/18396`, attained by word `(0,2,1)` at the printed switch times. Every word using fewer than three labels has strictly larger optimum. The scaled coefficient is `37346/262143`.

4. **Independent floor-history coverage.** `independent_floor_words.py` intersects bit sets of all complete words rather than using the author's nine-state recurrence. It obtains 396, 1872, and 8856 realizable histories for five, six, and seven cells, with the printed switch-count distributions. For each history and each prefix coordinate, it verifies that both floor and ceiling occur in some compatible full word, corroborating strict realizability by averaging. The six exceptional histories are exactly the full permutation orbit printed in the manuscript.

5. **Independent chronological program.** `independent_chamber.py` reconstructs 3660 base inequalities, 378 chronology inequalities, and 64 equalities. It checks the old witness, its 239 chronological violations, the canonical certificate ordering, every exact dual residual, and a freshly constructed uniform primal. The matching optimum is `13104/125`, with 254 nonzero dual multipliers. This check does not imply anything about other chambers.

6. **Independent arbitrary-grid checks.** `independent_grid_lp.py` uses full cell allocations, independently forms both LP families, and compares 320 programs across 18 rational grids with the closed formula. It also reconstructs compressed extremizers and evaluates every one-switch schedule exactly. The LP solutions are floating-point corroboration; the realization/error calculations are rational. The analytic elimination remains the proof of the general formula.

7. **New instance-algorithm cases.** `independent_algorithms.py` uses a new random seed for 136 exhaustive rational nonuniform-grid comparisons, including mode-dependent dwell, infeasibility, and budgets exceeding the grid's internal-boundary count. It independently forms duration-variable continuous LPs for six full/one-sided cases, totaling 100 programs, and compares their results with the rational solver. Nine coarsening certificates are checked against continuous optima. The numerical LP comparisons supplement the exact enumeration and printed proof; they do not replace either.

8. **Independent source-to-public-result reconstruction.** `independent_public.py` imports no manuscript algorithm. It verifies the pinned CSV hash, independently normalizes and quantizes the decimal data, reconstructs all 84 archived coarse-cell mass vectors, evaluates all six ordered-pair continuous candidates using complete vector endpoint errors, and checks all fine switching boundaries. It reproduces continuous optimum `4721469/2500000`, fine-grid optimum `1889/1000`, and the resulting gap. It independently checks the displayed uniform convergence values, transition dimensions, and stored timing medians. I did not repeat timing measurements on the shared machine.
