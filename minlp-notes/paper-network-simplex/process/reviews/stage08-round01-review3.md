## Mathematical assessment

**Foundations and block reduction (Sections 2–3).** The disaggregation equivalence handles the empty flow domain correctly: positive weights exist, zero-weight scaled systems equal `{0}`, and finite capacities force their flows to vanish. The integral-flow corollary is a hull equality and expressly does not retain the continuous decomposition-size bound. The fundamental-cycle proof establishes direct block independence, including articulation vertices, bridges, parallel edges, and loops. Proportional refinement respects different local mergers and zero merged weights. I independently checked that a nonfeasible reference flow causes no problem once the block domains and bridge bounds are enforced. The separate treatment of constraints added after convexification is necessary and present.

**Compression (Section 4).** The fixed-arc lemma correctly identifies the remaining feasible affine hull after every constant coordinate is removed. The path suppression counts use within-block degree and preserve cycle rank. In the observation-elimination proof, the replacement-minor and bordered-minor arguments give the claimed unit Schur coefficients. Distinct selected observations, distinct labels, and one representative aggregate arc prevent accidental coefficient accumulation. Rank–nullity gives the cycle rank of the unobserved subgraph with isolated vertices included. The minimum completion claim is restricted to individual products and ambient linear reconstruction; the manuscript does not confuse it with optimal extension complexity or conditional slice dimension.

**Structured oracles (Section 5).** I checked the five theta feasibility comparisons, tight support formulas, degenerate segment/point argument, and recovery choices. The parallel-path proof shifts the bounded matrix correctly; the complementary subset gives nonnegative row targets, the source-side row cut converts back to the stated subset inequality, and active branches have the correct majorant direction for a globally valid separating cut. Arbitrary orientation is handled by deviation signs. The nonempty-state prechecks and zero-weight cases are retained.

**Bounded rank and sharp K4 (Section 6).** The positive circuits are unit dependencies because the signed network normal matrix is totally unimodular. The edge list covers lower-dimensional state domains by including rows tight throughout those domains. Chamber supports remain valid for lower-dimensional sums. The stronger dual-multiplier bound uses the transformed ternary edge directions and unimodularity, rather than an unjustified bound on the inverse matrix alone. Enumeration sizes support the distinct `2^{O(r^2)}` separation and `2^{O(r^3)}` recovery bounds. The sequential denominator argument is additive in encoding length, as required. The explicit five-product K4 section supplies local feasible witnesses and a necessary support inequality, rather than only exhibiting a multiplier two in one dual basis.

**Actual coordinate sections and transportation transfer (Section 7).** I challenged the key distinction between projection and coordinate restriction. Every original coordinate except the designated first-layer products really is fixed in the constructed sparse hull. Other table cells are auxiliary state-flow entries and were never original product coordinates. The two observed boundary layers and aggregate boundaries determine the third layer margins by subtraction. Padding makes all layer totals positive without changing old cells. A nonnegative layer flow on the acyclic network is bounded on every arc by its layer value, which gives the scaled capacities used in the proof.

The source theorem is strong enough: De Loera–Onn's coordinate-erasing representation preserves designated coordinates, and its Section 3.3 injection puts every retained entry into layer one. I checked the local primary text and the [author-hosted published article](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/researchsummary/universalitytransportation.pdf), particularly Theorem 1.1 and printed page 816. Thus the manuscript's further sparse bilinear transfer does not rest on an unsupported arbitrary-layer assumption.

The section-to-facet lemma in Section 6.6 is also sufficient. Two-dimensional local section interior forces every valid affine equation to have zero coefficients on the two free coordinates. An active nonconstant restricted inequality must have the local half-plane normal. This establishes both the existence of a relative facet representative with the required ratio and invariance under affine equations. The normalized triangle example therefore forces a coefficient ratio `M` in any finite description. Polynomial model size in `log M` yields superpolynomial coefficient magnitudes, while leaving polynomial coefficient encoding possible; no separation-hardness or extension-complexity conclusion is asserted.

**Sparse Fibonacci and simple degree-three realization (Section 8).** I verified the incidence equations, invertibility of `D`, complement invertibility, positivity and sum of the balancing weights, and the local recovery construction. The slack reduction in one nonempty allowed row preserves all state capacities. The sparse observation count is the number of ones in `D`, not in its complement. The selected free products remain actual coordinates throughout. The graph input length `O(q log q)` is consistent with exponentially growing `F_q`; the lower bound is superpolynomial in that input length and does not claim exponential bit length. Join splitting and subdivision preserve the flow and state-flow correspondence, and fixing the added aggregate coordinates preserves the same two-coordinate section. The stated vertex, arc, degree, and rank counts are correct.

**Few observed labels (Section 9).** The profile formulation retains a common state vector across all serial gadgets. Residual elimination gives positive subset rows, negative singletons, and the negative full row as stated. I independently enumerated the reduced positive circuits and recovered counts 1, 5, and 16. I checked both occurrence arguments and the two three-label bypass repairs. The exceptional rows selected for a repair belong to distinct gadgets, so the chosen gadget flow coefficient has no additional same-gadget contribution. The four-label example uses the coordinate-section obstruction, not merely a nonunit unrepaired certificate. Global observation merging retains every original simplex variable and provides the stated compact refinement; it correctly changes the parameter from all labels to observed labels. The flat-chain scope is not extrapolated to nested series–parallel networks.
