The following records the substantive checks behind the verdict. References to result labels identify the exact manuscript arguments examined.

1. **Disaggregation and the base domain.** In `prop:disaggregation`, the forward construction is linear after setting the state weights, and the reverse construction preserves every individual observed coordinate at the appropriate simplex vertex. Finite capacities are essential at weight zero and are present. At least one state weight is positive, so an allegedly feasible extension for an empty base would supply a point in that empty base; the explicit empty-domain claim is sound. The `m=0`, isolated-vertex, and loop cases are consistent with the definitions. In `cor:integral-flows`, the nonintegral-arc subgraph argument produces a signed cycle perturbation because a lone nonintegral incident arc cannot satisfy an integral balance. Integral bounds make both sufficiently small perturbations feasible. Refining each positive-weight state into integral flow vertices preserves the products at its fixed simplex vertex. The warning that this can require more than `m+1` integral graph points is necessary and correct.

2. **Block factorization and a reference outside the capacity box.** For `lem:block-factorization`, subtracting the chord expansion leaves a circulation supported on a forest, hence zero. Every fundamental simple cycle lies in one cyclic block; extending its restriction by zero therefore preserves all vertex balances, including articulation balances. This verifies both directions of the direct-product statement, rather than merely verifying a dimension count. Loops as individual blocks and parallel-edge cycles fit the same proof. The reference need only satisfy `Av=b`. Its bridge coordinates are fixed throughout the balance affine space, while the block domains can be translated away from zero. Consequently an infeasible reference does not undermine the factorization or the later scaled domains.

3. **Merging and global refinement.** In `thm:block-state-reduction`, all constituents of a merged local state are scaled copies of the same nonempty convex domain. Their sum identity therefore applies even when that domain does not contain zero. Observed slices are kept separate. At a zero merged weight, nonnegativity of all global weights implies that each constituent weight is zero. At a positive merged weight, proportional refinement preserves its scaled domain and its sum. Combining the refined block deviations with the same reference flow gives global flows satisfying the same `b` for each normalized state. No additional articulation coupling is needed, and different blocks may merge different labels. I checked this distinction especially carefully because independent choices of simplex vectors for the blocks would have been invalid. The manuscript makes no such choice.

4. **Fixed arcs, suppression, and ambient dimension.** `lem:fixed-arcs` uses the section's nonempty-base assumption. After all constant coordinates are removed, averaging feasible points that are individually strict on each remaining bound produces one point strict on every bound. Thus the remaining balance affine space is exactly the feasible affine hull. This is different from the dimension of a particular conditional observation slice, as the manuscript explains. In path suppression, signed circulation deviations are constant along degree-two paths even if arc orientations reverse or reference flows differ along the path. Intersecting signed capacity offsets retains every original bound. Rank-one loops/cycles are handled separately; the higher-rank core's minimum degree gives `k_B <= 3 r_B - 3`. The identity chord rows imply boundedness and force all cycle coordinates to zero at zero state weight.

5. **Total unimodularity and observation elimination.** I checked the incidence-minor induction, tree-column replacement identity, row-replacement identity for `W`, and bordered determinant identity for `R` in `lem:cycle-tu`. They prove the Schur coefficients individually belong to `{0,±1}`; a general claim that an inverse has small entries would not alone have established the needed product. In `thm:observed-compression`, pivot reconstruction is reversible and all unselected observation equations remain. The selected observed coordinates are distinct, and distinct labels use distinct product columns. A residual bound includes one original flow coordinate and one expression from each label. Therefore collecting coefficients does not invalidate the unit bound. The kernel of restriction to observed arcs is exactly the circulation space supported on the unobserved subgraph, with isolated vertices included in the component count. `prop:minimum-completion` proves necessity only for individual-coordinate determination on that ambient space and attains it with the nonforest unobserved edges. It does not claim an extension-complexity lower bound. The row, nonzero, and rational-coefficient qualifications match these constructions.

6. **Structured supports, transportation, and recovery.** The five theta feasibility tests characterize the intersection of the attainable interval sum with the third interval. The six tight-support formulas attain their extrema even for segments and points. In `lem:theta-minkowski`, an exposed edge of a two-dimensional sum must contain a nontrivial segment from a summand in that edge's direction; segment and point sums receive separate treatment. Suffix support sums therefore describe the exact recovery intersection, and the stated point-choice formula stays within its six bounds. The changed default-vector wording now accurately describes the two scalar coordinates needed for a theta block. For `thm:parallel-hull`, I checked the lower-bound shift, derivation of nonnegative row targets from complementary subset inequalities, equality of row/column totals, and minimization over column sides of a cut. They give the complete transportation feasibility condition, including a zero total. Affine branch selection yields a global valid separating inequality because it chooses an upper bound on the concave right-hand side that is exact at the candidate. Observations occur in disjoint path/state positions, so the unit-coefficient assertion follows.

7. **Bounded-rank libraries and coefficient control.** A minimally supported positive dependence has an ordinary one-dimensional dependence space: any additional dependence could perturb the positive vector in both directions. This justifies the cofactor/TU proof of unit local circuits. In `lem:rank-edges`, the active rows at a relative interior point of an edge have rank `s-1`, including any rows tight throughout a lower-dimensional slice. Adjoining an arbitrary row to its defining rows establishes the stronger property `Md` is ternary. The coordinate directions make arrangement chambers pointed, so their extreme rays suffice to test every objective; constancy of each maximizing vertex within a chamber proves support linearity there. For `lem:rank-duals`, transforming the defining directions by the unimodular basis first is the essential step: `B^{-T}h` is primitive and orthogonal to the ternary vectors `Bd_i`. Its cofactors obey the same `H_s` bound, without an unjustified extra factor of `s`. Opposite row normals cannot occur together in a nonsingular basis, preventing repeated product coefficients. The finite subset counts give `2^{O(r^2)}` separation after the stated grouping.

8. **General recovery and encoding.** In `thm:rank-recovery`, the permissible next state is exactly its slice intersected with the translated negative suffix sum. It is nonempty and bounded by the induction invariant, even when it has no ambient interior. A vertex still has full ambient active-normal rank, justifying basis enumeration. The enlarged normal library and all candidate checks fit `2^{O(r^3)}`. The common-denominator argument adds at most one parameter-bounded determinant's bit length per selected state; it does not repeatedly square the previous denominator. Bounds on accepted state vectors and fixed inverse matrices also control rejected candidates. Compact block defaults and exceptions avoid falsely charging dense `m|E|` output to a sparse linear bound; the dense output cost is stated separately.

9. **Necessary coefficients and universality.** `lem:section-facet` uses a locally two-dimensional coordinate section. This forces valid affine equations to have zero coefficients on both free coordinates. At least one nonconstant restricted inequality is active along the section boundary, and tangent directions force the required coefficient ratio. I checked the five-product K4 balances, free coordinates, residual support, and witness neighborhood, including the fourth arc's different reference offset. The seven-product variant also has the stated sum and capacity ranges. The universality transfer fixes all original coordinates except designated actual products, retains the first-layer coordinate injection of the cited transportation theorem, and pads every layer total to be positive. Nonnegative acyclic state flows have arc values bounded by their total flow, so the common capacity is sufficient in both directions. Uniform normalization preserves ratios. The superpolynomial magnitude conclusion is consistent with polynomial encoding length and makes no separation-hardness claim. The deep imported transportation theorem remains an imported theorem; its required coordinate-erasing and first-layer properties were checked against the primary source in my Stage 1 review and are unchanged here.

10. **Sparse series–parallel and flat-chain results.** I checked the balancing identity and inverse-based local witness in `lem:balanced-incidence`: the profile perturbation has zero sum, the residual row excess is nonnegative, and reducing one allowed cell corrects that row without crossing its bounds. The Fibonacci column recurrence proves invertibility of the incidence matrix; the total-weight identity proves invertibility of its complement. The complement makes the observed set sparse while preserving the balancing weights. Splitting joins and subdividing the unobserved parallel arcs preserves the free product section and yields the stated degree and graph counts. For the flat chain, direct elimination of the residual profile gives precisely the positive subset, negative singleton, and negative full normals. Original nonnegativity handles doubly observed cells. The general circuit/minor bound and finite-basis recovery remain valid at zero weights. The expanded three-label classification correctly rules out the other weighted covers. Product occurrences obey the claimed exclusions, and the only possible bypass coefficients outside `{0,±1}` are repaired using a selected gadget balance without introducing another doubled flow coefficient. The four-label section obstruction then makes the three-label threshold sharp for this topology. The manuscript correctly refrains from transferring that threshold to general nested series–parallel graphs.
