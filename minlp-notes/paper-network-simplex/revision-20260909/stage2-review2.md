The main checks of the bounded-rank development were as follows.

1. **Local feasibility and edge directions.** In `lem:rank-circuits`, an extreme nonnegative dependence cannot have a proper linearly dependent subset of its support: such a dependence would permit both signs of a sufficiently small perturbation while retaining nonnegativity. Thus the support is a circuit in the ordinary linear-algebra sense, and the selected-column cofactor formula is applicable. Total unimodularity forces its positive primitive coefficients to equal one. In `lem:rank-edges`, the stronger condition `Md` being ternary follows from adjoining a row to the cofactor determinant, not merely from `d` being ternary. This stronger property is subsequently used and is justified. At a relative interior point of an edge of a lower-dimensional slice, the active rows include the equations defining its affine hull; their rank is still `s-1`. The edge-direction conclusion therefore does cover degenerate slices.

2. **Completeness of support rays.** In `lem:rank-support-library`, the presence of coordinate directions ensures that every full-dimensional closed arrangement chamber is pointed. An objective in its interior cannot expose a positive-dimensional face of any state polytope, because such a face has an edge whose direction would be orthogonal to that objective. Along a segment inside a chamber, a change in the maximizing vertex would therefore be impossible. Support linearity extends to chamber closures by continuity, so their extreme rays suffice. This applies to point and segment summands, and the signed rays also correctly describe the empty suffix as the singleton zero. I found no unproved assumption of full-dimensional state domains or sums.

3. **The same `H_s` bound for primal normals and dual multipliers.** The crucial step at `sections/04-bounded-rank.tex:218–227` is valid. For a nonsingular normal basis `B`, the vectors `Bd_i` remain independent and ternary. Since `B` is unimodular, `B^{-T}h` is primitive integral and is the primitive normal of those transformed directions. Applying the cofactor/Hadamard argument there yields `H_s` directly. A triangle inequality on the entries of `B^{-T}h` would introduce an unnecessary factor of `s`, but the manuscript does not use that weaker argument. The optimal dual-support reduction also works for a lower-dimensional primal: perturbing a dependent positive optimal support must have zero objective change, so a coefficient can be removed and the remaining independent support extended to a basis with zero extra multipliers.

4. **Cuts in actual original coordinates.** In `thm:bounded-rank`, local feasibility is checked before support values are used. Selecting minimizing bases and endpoint branches gives an affine majorant equal to the concave support sum at the query. It is therefore a global valid cut, rather than a local approximation. A normal basis cannot contain an opposite pair; a retained product occurs at most once in each state's chosen basis expression, and products for distinct states have different indices. Local opposite-pair certificates cancel a repeated product. The aggregate expression uses distinct chord flows. These facts justify the coefficient bound after collecting original-coordinate terms. The manuscript consistently excludes simplex denominator clearing from its unit/integer flow-product normalization.

5. **Sequential recovery and rational encoding.** `thm:rank-recovery` uses exactly the intersection of the next state domain with the reflected suffix sum. Its nonempty bounded intersection has a vertex whose active rows span the full ambient cycle space, even if the intersection has empty ambient interior. Basis enumeration therefore finds a feasible state; induction preserves the suffix membership, and the final empty suffix enforces exact zero remainder. At `sections/04-bounded-rank.tex:348–362`, a common denominator for the next aggregate can be multiplied by the selected basis determinant. Its bit length increases additively with each recovery step. Initial endpoint denominators, support sums, bounded state coordinates, and fixed inverse matrices also control numerator lengths and rejected candidates. This establishes the stated encoding qualification; it does not assume that all integers have constant bit cost. Normalizing only positive weights is safe, and the default/exception output is compact rather than dense.

6. **Operation counts.** With at most exponentially many signed normals and directions in rank `s`, selecting subsets of size `O(s)` gives the claimed `2^{O(s^2)}` circuit, ray, and support-basis work. The recovery normal list has `2^{O(s^2)}` entries; selecting and testing its `s`-row bases gives `2^{O(s^3)}` work. Products of these parameter factors have the same stated orders. The global sum is controlled by the number of blocks and `sum_B a_B <= |Obs|`, with reference-flow and original-domain work included. The grouping/sorting qualification and separate dense-output cost are explicit. I found no hidden factor in the number of unobserved labels or state-by-arc output that invalidates the advertised compact bound.
