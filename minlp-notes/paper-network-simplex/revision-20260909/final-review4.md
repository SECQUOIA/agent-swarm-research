## Correctness and scope findings

**R4-C1 — Confirmed; no defect. Disaggregation, block gluing, and residual-cycle compression.** Locations: `01-foundations.tex`, Propositions/lemmas on disaggregation and block factorization and Theorem `thm:block-state-reduction`; `02-compression.tex`, Theorem `thm:observed-compression` and Proposition `prop:minimum-completion`.

The state construction includes the residual capacity bounds, forces zero-weight states to zero using finite upper bounds, and handles an empty original flow polytope correctly. Block independence is a circulation statement around a reference satisfying the balances; it does not incorrectly impose independent balances on the original block flows. Proportional refinement of locally merged states produces the same global weights on all blocks. The deletion lemma correctly distinguishes the ambient circulation dimension from conditional degeneracy.

For observed elimination, total unimodularity controls both the row-replacement coefficients and the bordered Schur-complement coefficients. Selected products are distinct, unselected observations introduce a different product, and different labels use different columns, so the residual sum does not double an individual coefficient. The dimension of the restriction kernel is precisely the cycle rank of the unobserved multigraph, including isolated vertices and loops. The individual-product completion minimum is expressly restricted to ambient reconstruction; it is not incorrectly asserted to be an extension-complexity lower bound. No correction is needed.

**R4-C2 — Confirmed; no defect. Complete positive structural oracles and coefficient bounds.** Locations: `03-structured-oracles.tex`; `04-bounded-rank.tex`, through Theorem `thm:rank-recovery`; `07-fixed-state-chains.tex`.

The theta Minkowski argument covers segments and points as well as full polygons. Its branch expansions use each observation at most once. The parallel-path transportation shift derives nonnegative row targets from complementary subset inequalities, so the max-flow sufficiency argument does not omit a feasibility hypothesis. Extracted active branches are valid affine majorants of concave supports, which is the correct direction for global separating cuts.

The bounded-rank arrangement refines every possible state normal fan because every edge direction lies in the fixed library, including lower-dimensional slices. Signed coordinate hyperplanes make chambers pointed. The stronger bound on dual weights uses the unimodular transformation of the defining directions, rather than an invalid inference from the size of the normal alone. Recovery enumerates vertices in the full ambient coordinate space and gives a credible denominator-growth argument. The large parameter dependence is acknowledged and distinguished from input bit complexity and dense-output costs.

The chain profile inequalities are necessary and sufficient before projection. Residual elimination yields the smaller signed-subset universe stated in the paper. I checked the four classes of three-label circuits and the occurrence argument. The two exceptional bypass coefficients are removable using a gadget flow equation without changing product coefficients; the selected endpoint gadgets are distinct in precisely the cases where the repair is needed. These claims are compatible with zero weights and arbitrarily many globally unused labels. No correction is needed.

**R4-C3 — Confirmed; no defect. Actual-coordinate facet transfer and the K4 example.** Location: `04-bounded-rank.tex`, Lemma `lem:section-facet`, equations `eq:sharp-k4-states` through `eq:sharp-k4-residual-arcs`, and Corollary `cor:rank-three-sharp`.

The local half-plane has two-dimensional interior in a plane fixing all other original coordinates. Consequently every ambient affine equation has zero coefficients on the two free coordinates. An active nonconstant restricted inequality must contain the boundary tangent in both directions, which forces the asserted normal ratio. This justifies the conclusion for an ambient relative facet and for every finite inequality description; no assumption that facets survive a general projection is used.

The five-product K4 construction fixes all other original coordinates, obtains the necessary support inequality, and supplies a feasible witness on the entire local feasible side. The asymmetric bound on arc 01 is carried through the residual witness. Thus the ratio two is forced on actual products and cannot be removed by balance equations. This establishes sharpness of the rank-three integer coefficient bound in the stated rational scaling. No correction is needed.

**R4-C4 — Confirmed; no defect. Transportation universality hypotheses and sparse coordinate transfer.** Location: `05-universality.tex`, Theorem `thm:universality` and Corollary `cor:universal-coefficients`.

I checked the imported construction in the primary [De Loera–Onn article](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/researchsummary/universalitytransportation.pdf). Its coordinate-erasing representation, first-layer injection on printed page 816, and polynomial output bound support the hypotheses used here. A bounded rational inequality polytope in the nonnegative orthant has a bounded standard-form slack extension. Clearing equation denominators preserves the original free coordinates. Padding one corner makes all three layer totals positive without altering designated old entries.

In the four-layer network, fixed aggregate interior flows impose the cell margins; observed boundary flows impose the first two layer margins; the third follows by subtraction. Each layer flow has total D_k and therefore obeys the scaled common capacity D_k. The section retains only the designated interior products as free coordinates. Uniform normalization changes Q to Q/B and preserves coefficient ratios. The statement that original model data become unit data correctly excludes the rational constants used only to define the section. Large coefficient magnitudes do not imply superpolynomial encoding lengths or separation hardness. No correction is needed.

**R4-C5 — Confirmed; no defect. Sparse Fibonacci construction and maximum-degree-three realization.** Location: `06-series-parallel.tex`, Lemma `lem:balanced-incidence`, Theorem `thm:fibonacci`, and Corollary `cor:fibonacci-simple`.

The balancing identity gives the necessary local inequality. In the converse, the proposed perturbation has zero profile sum, positive profiles strictly below their weights, and sufficiently small excess in the selected row to remove it from one unobserved cell. This proves local sufficiency while fixing every other original coordinate. Invertibility of both D and its complement follows from the printed recurrence and balancing calculation. Observation count 5q−4, graph counts, and O(q log q) sparse encoding are consistent.

Splitting internal joins and subdividing one arc of each pair produces 3N vertices and 4N arcs. The graph is simple, acyclic, and has maximum undirected degree three; it remains two-terminal series–parallel. Every original flow and every state flow extends uniquely, and the added capacities are redundant for unit source–sink flows. Fixing added aggregate coordinates therefore preserves the same two-dimensional section in the unchanged observed products.

The construction grows both block rank and observed-label count. It does not contradict the bounded-rank or at-most-three-observed-label positive theorems, nor does the flat chain belong to the unrestricted parallel-path block family. The aggregate/full-state distinction is accurately credited to [Almoghrabi–Skutella–Warode, Remark 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-026-02392-8). No correction is needed.

## Novelty, importance, and presentation

The paper does not claim the general hull, simplex disaggregation, polynomial separation, common-factor elimination, transportation projection, or Minkowski support refinement as new. Its source-specific discussion is materially accurate. In particular, I inspected the published [Khademnia–Davarnia article](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10546393): its complete EC&R hull theorem, equality-balance convention, and Example 2 with a nonunit aggregation weight are all properly distinguished from the present finite graph libraries and unavoidable actual-product ratios. The Cartesian gluing and elimination attributions to Davarnia's dissertation and Liberti–Pantelides, and the transportation-cut attribution to Kis–Horváth, also match the primary passages inspected.

The strongest original developments are the exact observation-complement formulation with coefficient control, the bounded-rank certificate and recovery bounds, and the sharp label threshold together with sparse coefficient obstructions. The elementary constituent arguments do not diminish the value of their combined structural conclusions. The universality transfer is a useful restriction of a classical result; its novelty is narrower than the explicit sparse series–parallel construction, as the paper makes clear.

The manuscript is long at 50 pages, but has a coherent progression from the known hull to reductions, positive coefficient descriptions, obstructions, and fixed-label recovery. Tables of scope and prior work help distinguish superficially similar hypotheses. Terminology and coefficient normalization are consistent. The application and aggregate-budget discussion correctly describe component-hull relaxations after side rows, rather than claiming convexification commutes with intersection.

The caveats about rational row scaling, generic LP availability, dense output costs, and side rows recur several times. A final copy edit could shorten some repetitions by cross-reference. Preserve each theorem's local hypothesis and normalization. No mathematical revision is requested.
