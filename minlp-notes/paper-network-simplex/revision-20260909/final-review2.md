# Independent whole-manuscript referee report 2

**Verdict: accept.** I found no major or minor defect requiring a revision. The paper makes a credible, sufficiently distinct contribution to the polyhedral study of sparse flow–simplex products. Its strongest results are the restricted coefficient obstructions and the sharp three-versus-four observed-label threshold. The compression and separation results provide a useful constructive framework around them. This verdict concerns the frozen `final-round1` manuscript and delivery artifacts, not earlier internal decisions.

I read the complete manuscript, including every proof, example, computational section, conclusion, bibliography, and the packaged reproduction instructions. I did not read other reviewers’ reports or adjudications. I checked primary-source passages independently of the literature-audit narrative and ran the checks listed below in private extractions. The evidence directory is `final-review2-evidence/`; `reviewed-artifacts.json` identifies the actual artifacts by SHA-256.

## Originality, relationship to prior work, and significance

**FR2-A1 — Established convexification is correctly separated from the contribution.** Section 2.2 and the introduction expressly recognize that the full hull and polynomial-size disaggregation are known. I checked Khademnia–Davarnia’s published Theorem 1 and Example 2 in the local published-paper text, and checked the publisher’s record online. Theorem 1 supplies a complete EC&R description; the special forest construction should not be mistaken for the scope of that completeness theorem. The manuscript does not make that mistake. Example 2 establishes a dual aggregation weight of two. It does not itself establish the claimed invariant ratio of coefficients on two retained products. The manuscript’s local coordinate-plane lemma supplies the missing distinction. The attribution to simultaneous convexification is also consistent with the scope stated in Davarnia’s dissertation and the 2017 paper’s publisher record. [Khademnia–Davarnia](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/moor.2023.0001), [Davarnia–Richard–Tawarmalani](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/16M1066166).

**FR2-A2 — The compression is a specific network development of established elimination and gluing.** I checked Davarnia’s dissertation Proposition 2.6 and Liberti–Pantelides Theorem 3.1, including the latter’s warning that redundancy of nonlinear equations does not imply redundancy of their McCormick relaxations. Section 4 properly identifies the residual nullity with circulations on the unobserved arcs, retains state and residual bounds, and proves unit coefficients through total unimodularity. Neither the linear-algebra nullity principle nor common-simplex gluing is presented as new. The minimum completion statement is explicitly about individual missing products on the ambient circulation space. The forest criterion, sparse blockwise construction, and coefficient guarantee are the meaningful development here, rather than an extension-complexity lower bound. [Dissertation](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/05/02/79/00001/DAVARNIA_D.pdf), [Liberti–Pantelides](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10898-006-9005-4).

**FR2-A3 — Transportation and support geometry are appropriately credited.** I checked Kis–Horváth Section 5.9, Proposition 22, and equations (30)–(31); they explicitly use a lower-bound shift and cut projection. The paper accurately treats its parallel-path theorem as a specialization of those mechanisms. Onn–Rothblum’s edge-direction/zonotope refinement supports the classical status of the geometric ingredient in Section 6. The finite uniform libraries, the use of the cycle matrix to bound both directions and dual multipliers, and the explicit recovery complexity are the more specific results. I found no claim that general Minkowski support addition or fixed-dimensional normal refinement originated here. [Kis–Horváth](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-021-01652-z), [Onn–Rothblum](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0309083).

**FR2-A4 — The coefficient results are the clearest contribution.** The two-label K4 local half-plane, balanced-incidence lemma, sparse Fibonacci complement construction, and complete three-label chain occurrence analysis go beyond finding a large determinant or a nonunit dual multiplier. Their restrictions concern actual products, graph topology, sparsity, and invariance under affine equations. I checked De Loera–Onn Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 and the explicit first-layer injection on printed page 816: the injection needed by the manuscript is indeed present. The ensuing bilinear coordinate-section transfer is justified, while transportation and two-commodity universality are correctly attributed. I also checked the recent multiflow paper’s Remark 1 and the binary-simplex preprint’s statement of its projected target space; both distinctions in the literature discussion are accurate. [De Loera–Onn](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/researchsummary/universalitytransportation.pdf), [Almoghrabi–Skutella–Warode](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-026-02392-8), [Davarnia–Rahimian v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15861v2).

These source checks support the qualified novelty claim; they cannot establish exhaustive priority across all literature. The unrestricted universality corollary is closer to a transfer of an existing theorem than the chain threshold and sparse Fibonacci family. The paper’s present framing makes that difference clear. Its likely impact is structural understanding and smaller exact formulations, rather than an immediately demonstrated improvement in industrial optimization runtime.

## Mathematical review

**FR2-A5 — Foundations and compression.** The disaggregation proof handles empty flow sets and zero weights. The integral-flow hull equality does not inherit the continuous at-most- m+1 decomposition count, and the paper explicitly warns of this. Block factorization uses circulation deviations, which explains why articulation balances impose no additional coupling. Local merged states are refined consistently with the common global weights. Path suppression, fixed-arc deletion, cycle-matrix total unimodularity, Schur-complement coefficient control, and the rank–nullity completion argument are sound. Distinct selected products and distinct label columns prevent unnoticed coefficient addition in the residual rows.

**FR2-A6 — Structured descriptions and bounded rank.** I checked both directions of theta feasibility, support attainment, and the Minkowski argument for points and segments. In the parallel-path theorem, complementary subset inequalities supply nonnegative shifted row targets before max-flow is invoked. Affine branches are upper bounds on concave right-hand sides, so the emitted cuts are globally valid. In Section 6, active rows on a lower-dimensional edge still have ambient rank s−1; this justifies the uniform direction library. Pointed arrangement chambers cover objective space, the nonnegative support bases remain sufficient on degenerate slices, and the unimodular transformation in the multiplier bound preserves primitivity. The recovery argument correctly uses full-rank active bases at vertices even in lower dimension. Its denominator-growth argument charges one parameter-bounded determinant per sequential state, rather than assuming fixed precision.

**FR2-A7 — Necessary coefficients and chains.** The coordinate-section lemma requires genuine two-dimensional local interior and therefore makes the free coefficients immune to affine-hull changes. The K4 proof establishes necessity and local sufficiency, not merely a support bound. The balanced-incidence witness preserves the common profile total and repairs only an unobserved entry; positivity follows from the stated neighborhood. The Fibonacci balancing and complement-invertibility arguments check out, as do the simple degree-three graph counts and unique state-flow extension. The profile formulation includes nonnegative observed cells and the unobserved residual state. The three-label proof checks product occurrences separately from circuit weights and repairs the two possible bypass coefficient exceptions with a valid flow equation. Thus the four-label obstruction does not conflict with the three-label positive result.

The upper coefficient bounds consistently refer to the displayed rational row scaling; the lower ratios are scaling invariant. Magnitude, encoding length, extension size, and separation complexity are kept distinct. I found no illicit transfer of a facet through a projection and no use of aggregate-flow integrality to assert integrality of prescribed state coordinates.

## Computation, presentation, and delivery

**FR2-A8 — Evidence and claims are proportionate.** The computation section distinguishes exact assembly, numerical feasibility, exactly checked Farkas cuts, and exact decomposition. It compares global unused-label merging and elementary one-flow reductions, explains why independent state optimization is easy without coupling, and acknowledges cases where conventional LPs win. The resource-budget experiment is correctly an intersection with the component hull; it does not claim the hull of the product graph after adding the budget. The synthetic scope and shared-host timing limits are explicit. The implementations are described with their actual supported scope, including the bounded-rank prototype limitation.

The 50-page manuscript is long but coherent. The scope and prior-work tables help, terminology is consistent, and the proof details are justified by the degeneracy and coefficient issues being addressed. The reproduced PDF has an empty author field. I found no missing reference or TeX warning in the clean standalone build. I have no mandatory editorial finding.

**Actual executions.** Both archive manifests passed in private extractions. The standalone LaTeX source built successfully, and its PDF SHA-256 exactly matched the supplied submission PDF. All 23 principal tests passed. I personally ran the supplied exact-contract audit: 2,196 queries, 989 exact acceptances, 1,207 rejections, and 17,584 exact vertex cut checks. The elimination audit checked 1,180 cycle minors and 1,408 observation/completion sets. The repair audit covered all 512 negative-two and 27 positive-two endpoint patterns. The Fibonacci audit verified facets and coefficient ratios exactly against enumerated vertices at q=3 and q=4. The mathematical audit checked 1,681 theta Minkowski pairs, 8,360 recoveries, the 16 reduced circuits, and Fibonacci witnesses through q=10. Bounded-rank recovery, 60 padding/normalization cases, and the integral-hull program passed. Table regeneration reproduced all five files and preserved the supplement manifest. Separately, I wrote a direct Fraction calculation checking the printed K4 balance vector and 488 local feasible witnesses without importing the supplied implementation.

**Limits.** Running supplied audit programs is independent execution, not independent authorship of those programs. I did not rerun the complete timing experiment or exhaustively inspect all production code paths. The finite checks do not prove arbitrary-size results or reimplement transportation universality. The mathematical verdict rests on reading the proofs and checking their imported hypotheses as well as on the reported executions. I did not conduct an exhaustive visual review of every rendered PDF page.

No correction is required for acceptance on the evidence examined.
