The main substantive checks were as follows.

1. **Exact versus numerical semantics are accurate.** The implementations and `delivery/API.md` distinguish the standard-library rational separators from the general compressed formulation's numerical solver. In `code/network_simplex_compressed/certificate.py`, a returned Farkas cut is supported by nonnegative rational multipliers, exact cancellation of every auxiliary coefficient, and negative combined residual at the candidate; the final original-coordinate cut is also checked for strictly positive exact violation. Unsuccessful multiplier reconstruction returns `uncertified_outside`, and LP failure returns `solver_failure`. Neither is converted into a mathematical infeasibility certificate. A nonpositive numerical phase-I optimum returns `numerically_feasible`, which the paper and API explicitly decline to call exact membership.

   I tested that distinction directly on a one-loop, unit-capacity model with `x=y=1/2` and observed product `z=-1/10^20`. The exact graph separator returns a cut with violation exactly `1/10^20`. Both initial and observed-eliminated numerical-certificate interfaces return `numerically_feasible` without a cut on this instance. This is consistent with the documented contract, rather than a defect: numerical feasibility tolerance is not an exact membership test. `api-semantics-probe.json` records the result. It demonstrates why the qualifications in the implemented-scope table and API should remain explicit.

2. **The strengthened baselines implement the stated comparisons.** I checked `strong_baselines.py` and its use in `paper_stage06.py`. Fixed-weight full optimization uses positive state flows and native scaled bounds, substitutes the original flow/product objective and side rows, and includes the fixed simplex-objective constant. Global merging retains only globally observed labels plus a merged default. With free weights, all original simplex variables and their contributions are retained. The benchmark's full membership call explicitly disables the optional one-flow reduction, making the separately reported one-flow column distinct; full still removes zero states and uses sparse state/aggregate matrices. The one-flow construction intersects both scaled bounds and all observations of both positive states, while the second balance follows from the aggregate. The independent-network baseline is used only without a coupling row.

   The common budget row is supplied to the full, globally merged, initial, observed-eliminated, and cutting-plane formulations. The manuscript describes their common feasible region as the component hull intersected with that row; it does not claim the hull of the newly constrained nonlinear graph. The all-labels-observed control correctly prevents global merging from explaining the local-compression saving. These controls and the reported LP wins materially improve baseline fairness.

3. **Timing and output costs are not conflated.** In `paper_stage06.py:95–119`, exact membership-only and decomposition-inclusive calls are timed independently. The latter includes the query work again, as the documentation says, and is not inferred by subtraction. Constructor time is included in both reported totals. In `member_case` and `opt_case`, warmup certificate/support audits are outside the timed components; repeated numerical statuses and objective agreement are checked separately. Method order rotates over measured runs, and each run constructs its model. General compressed symbolic construction is counted before matrix assembly/solution. Repeated-cut total time includes separation and rational reconstruction, while the table caption explains their exclusion from its build/solve component columns.

   The reported operation bounds concern structural algorithms, not observed wall-clock superiority. The manuscript separately discloses tuple-normal dimension overhead, constructor observation sorting, and dense compatibility-accessor output cost. It calls the bounded-rank implementation a verification prototype, not a production general-graph oracle. Rows exclude variable bounds; CSR counts do not purport to measure peak memory. I found no mismatch that changes the interpretation of the total-time or size comparisons.

4. **Numerical objective evidence is not presented as exact optimization.** The reconstruction adapter in `run.py` uses small-denominator rational approximation, checks its error and exact aggregate balances, and the warmup audits then check the recovered decomposition. The text correctly restricts this adapter to the synthetic instances and does not claim an exact general LP optimizer or exact rational optimality from a floating-point objective. Numerical full-hull support audits are identified as numerical, while exact multiplier checks and exact path/integer-flow cut checks are identified separately.

5. **The quantitative prose matches the preserved measurements.** I checked the model-size and timing claims against the raw records and generator, including the 5,547-to-516-to-255-to-222 ordering, the all-labels-observed 7,215/495/367 counts, the distinction between two positive states and two observed labels, and the flat-chain boundary/interior cases. The tables retain the large-boundary cases where the positive-state and one-flow LPs outperform the exact oracle. The paper states that only the smaller flat lengths were timed with the general compressed variants, and that five runs on a shared host do not establish universal speed ordering. Deleting comparisons dependent on omitted historical data does not erase the current negative runtime results.

6. **The mathematical meaning is preserved.** I rechecked how the computational statements use the Stage 2 results: exact cuts concern retained original coordinates; compact recovery may share defaults across unused labels; normalization uses positive weights; the coefficient-three-label threshold is for the flat topology; and the two-supplier interpretation is a parallel-path component with signed traversals. The elementary two-state feasibility formula and fixed-weight independent-objective identity are correct under their stated assumptions. No broader graph, extension-complexity, or exact coupled-model conclusion is introduced by the Stage 3 exposition. The unchanged full proof audit remains recorded in my Stage 2 report; I found no new contradiction while checking the code-facing interpretations.
