## Findings

1. **R3-01 — Minor: qualify the introduction's claim about recovering “all state products.”** Location: `sections/00-introduction.tex:63–65`, compared with `sections/02-compression.tex:314–322` and `sections/01-foundations.tex:380–386`.

   The introduction says the sum of the residual cycle ranks is the minimum number of added individual products needed to recover “all state products on the ambient circulation space.” The proved minimum is for each **locally observed** block/label pair. Labels absent from a block are merged and then assigned a feasible proportional refinement. Their original individual state flows are generally not determined by the retained coordinates and balance equations.

   A concrete example is a single self-loop of capacity one with balance zero, two explicit labels, and only `(e,1)` observed. Take `x_e=1/2`, `y_1=y_2=1/3`, and `z_e1=1/6`. The sum in the introduction is zero: the only observed label has an empty unobserved subgraph. Nevertheless, in global state order `(0,1,2)`, both `(1/6,1/6,1/6)` and `(1/12,1/6,1/4)` are feasible state-flow completions of exactly these original coordinates. They satisfy the scaled bounds, aggregate equation, observation, and balances, but disagree on the unobserved label-2 product. Thus those individual products are not uniquely recoverable from the claimed information. The exact Fraction check is in `stage1-review3-evidence/completion_scope.py`, with output in `completion_scope.json`.

   Repair the sentence to say that the count is the minimum for determining the full state circulation **for every locally observed block/label pair** by adding individual products and using balance equations on the ambient circulation space. If useful, distinguish this determination from choosing a feasible refinement for merged labels. This is minor because the formal proposition has the correct quantifiers and the formulation and recovery proofs are unaffected.

## Literature comparison and originality assessment

- **General hull and EC&R.** I inspected the published Khademnia–Davarnia paper's Theorem 1, the equality-balance setup in Section 3, Theorems 2 and 3, and Example 2, using the primary PDF already retained in the revision literature directory and a fresh private text extraction. The revised `01-foundations.tex:66–77` accurately distinguishes the complete EC&R description from the particular tree/forest constructions. Example 2 establishes a nonunit aggregation weight and a projection-cone extreme point; it does not itself establish the invariant ratio claim proved here by a two-coordinate section. Thus the comparison at lines 106–114 is justified. [Published primary PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10546393).

- **RRLT and shared-simplex gluing.** Liberti–Pantelides Theorem 3.1 explicitly obtains the product-elimination result through the nullspace of a full-row-rank linear system. Its following discussion explicitly warns that an eliminated bilinear relation's McCormick relaxation need not be redundant. Davarnia's dissertation Proposition 2.6 gives the shared-simplex Cartesian-component gluing result. The manuscript's treatment at `01-foundations.tex:79–92` credits both, and the compression proof really does preserve the state and residual capacity inequalities. Its graph complement criterion and unit minors are a specific implementation and coefficient guarantee, rather than a new general rank-nullity principle. [Liberti–Pantelides](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10898-006-9005-4), [Davarnia dissertation](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/05/02/79/00001/DAVARNIA_D.pdf).

- **Cayley/network cuts.** I checked the publisher's full text of Kis–Horváth, particularly Section 5.9, Proposition 22 and equations (30)–(31). The lower-bound shift and complete transportation projection are genuine predecessors. The current text at `01-foundations.tex:94–104` and the end of the parallel-path proof credits them directly. The manuscript's parallel-path construction is presented as a specialization to observed state slices, with separation and recovery. It does not claim a new max-flow/min-cut theorem. [Kis–Horváth full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-021-01652-z).

- **Support geometry.** I inspected Gritzmann–Sturmfels Lemmas 2.1.4–2.1.5 and Onn–Rothblum Lemma 2.3 in primary-paper text. These supply support-face addition, common refinement of normal fans, and refinement by a zonotope generated from edge directions. The bounded-rank manuscript proof uses this established geometry, but additionally establishes its uniform TU direction bounds, dual-multiplier bound, and finite recovery basis construction. The introduction accurately distinguishes the `2^{O(r^2)}` separation factor from the `2^{O(r^3)}` recovery factor. [Gritzmann–Sturmfels](https://doi.org/10.1137/0406019), [Onn–Rothblum preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309083).

- **Transportation universality.** I checked De Loera–Onn's primary PDF, including Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 and the coordinate injection in Section 3.3. The retained coordinates are indeed embedded in one layer. The manuscript explicitly credits both slim-table universality and its two-commodity interpretation, then supplies the further sparse bilinear section construction. Fixing boundary products for two labels determines all three layers' margins, while only the designated interior products remain free. The acyclic common-capacity normalization preserves the necessary scaled state bounds. The two-coordinate section lemma explains why the coefficient ratio survives affine-hull equations; this is materially stronger than asserting that a facet survives arbitrary projection. [De Loera–Onn primary PDF](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/researchsummary/universalitytransportation.pdf).

- **Series-parallel integrality.** The published Almoghrabi–Skutella–Warode Remark 1 explicitly separates aggregate arc-flow integrality from integrality of the full commodity-flow vector. Its use at `01-foundations.tex:121–125` is accurate and does not purport to prove the manuscript's coefficient obstruction. The conclusion correctly keeps the flat-chain threshold separate from general nested series-parallel networks. [Published Remark 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-026-02392-8).

- **New preprint.** The versioned arXiv record confirms the title, authors, version 2, and 11 August 2026 date in `references.bib`. The primary text's introduction and Section 3 support the comparison: binary simplex variables occur in bilinear constraints, and the target hull eliminates those variables into x-space. That differs from retaining continuous y and selected product coordinates here. The revised paragraph does not dismiss the paper merely because it studies chance constraints. [Versioned record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15861v2), [primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15861v2).

The author literature audit was used to locate primary files, not as evidence of correctness or priority. I also performed independent online searches for network/simplex residual-cycle hulls, series-parallel bilinear coefficient results, and current work related to the nearest network convexification paper. No directly matching earlier structural theorem emerged. This is a qualified search result, not proof of universal priority.
