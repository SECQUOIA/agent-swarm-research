## Findings and mathematical assessment

There are **no required corrections**. The following stable assessment IDs record the particular potential gaps I challenged; they are not open findings.

**R3-A01 — Block gluing and observation elimination: sound.** In `01-foundations.tex` and `02-compression.tex`, the spanning-forest argument correctly separates the ambient circulation space across cyclic blocks, including bridges, loops, parallel edges, and articulation vertices. The reference vector need not be feasible. Proportional refinement uses the actual local merged weight and correctly handles weight zero. Theorem 4.4 retains both individual-state and residual capacity bounds. Its coefficient conclusion follows from the two distinct TU minor arguments for the reconstruction coefficients and Schur complement. A remaining observation introduces a distinct product column, while products belonging to different labels are distinct; hence the residual sum does not silently double a product coefficient. The unobserved-subgraph nullity is the right count, and the text does not confuse its individual-coordinate completion interpretation with an extension-complexity lower bound. The fixed-arc lemma also correctly separates ambient nullity from capacity-induced degeneracy.

**R3-A02 — Cycle, theta, and parallel-path sufficiency: sound.** In `03-structured-oracles.tex`, local interval feasibility is imposed before Minkowski membership. The six-direction argument includes segments and points rather than assuming full dimension. For unrestricted parallel-path counts, the lower-bound transportation shift and all subset cuts establish sufficiency; the proof derives nonnegative row targets from complementary subsets instead of assuming them. Selecting active affine branches produces globally valid cuts because the appropriate support expressions are minima or maxima of affine functions. The transportation construction is properly attributed and not represented as a new general max-flow principle.

**R3-A03 — Bounded-rank support library and coefficient bound: sound.** In `04-bounded-rank.tex`, particularly Theorem 6.5, I checked the following essential steps. Minimal nonnegative row dependencies are unit positive circuits because the cycle matrix is TU. At an edge of a lower-dimensional slice, the active defining rows still have ambient rank `s-1`; this supplies the same direction library. The arrangement is pointed because coordinate directions are included, and its chamber closures cover objective space. Linear support on every chamber therefore reduces all support inequalities to the finite ray list, including lower-dimensional sums. The stronger multiplier bound is justified by applying the unimodular transformation to the defining edge directions: bounding `B^{-T}h` merely by a coordinatewise triangle inequality would be insufficient, but the manuscript does not make that mistake. Nonsingular bases cannot contain opposite normals, preventing a retained product from occurring twice in a support branch.

My independently written probe reconstructed the rank-three K4 libraries: 7 edge directions, 18 rays, and 128 normal bases. The largest ray entry is 1, while the largest retained dual multiplier is 2, illustrating why the multiplier argument matters. Exact library checks passed. In 35 families containing up to five state slices, including point slices, 1,890 support values and 280 sum-membership queries agreed with independently assembled numerical LPs. This finite numerical comparison supplements the proof; it does not replace it.

**R3-A04 — Recovery and arithmetic qualifications: sound.** Theorem 6.6 uses the exact intersection of the next state domain with the translated remaining Minkowski sum. Its finite basis enumeration succeeds at an ambient-rank active set even when that intersection is lower-dimensional. The denominator argument is adequate: each sequential step introduces only a determinant from a fixed parameter-dependent library, so denominator *bit length* grows additively with the number of states. The claimed polynomial encoding bound includes the candidate and library encodings. It is not a claim of parameter-independent polynomial runtime. The separate dense-output cost and compact default/exception representation are necessary and correctly stated. I reran the supplied recovery verification: 64 cases and 334 recovered states passed, with zero-weight states, equality slices, and 25-state cases. The tested systems were a cycle, theta, K4, and rank-four box; this execution does not validate all rank-four graph cores.

**R3-A05 — Fixed-label circuits and coefficient repairs: sound.** In `07-fixed-state-chains.tex`, the profile formulation preserves one common state vector across all gadgets, and residual elimination yields the stated smaller normal universe. I independently enumerated all 16 positive circuits for three explicit labels and checked 3,072 product-coefficient extrema across all 64 local observation patterns. These exact checks confirm that circuit weight two on the negative full normal does not become a product coefficient two. I also checked the two exceptional bypass-flow cases in the proof of Theorem 9.5. Three positive singleton upper endpoints must come from distinct gadgets; the selected gadget balance removes coefficient `-2` without doubling another flow coefficient. Three zero-bypass positive pair rows similarly must be distinct lower endpoints and permit the opposite repair. The supplied exhaustive repair program passed all 512 and 27 exceptional endpoint patterns, covering 1,617 exact repair choices. The two printed examples have the stated violations. The four-label obstruction and the merger from total labels to observed labels are compatible with the positive result.

**R3-A06 — Necessary coefficients and local sufficiency: sound.** The coordinate-section lemma requires and uses local two-dimensional interior. Thus every ambient affine equation has zero coefficients on the two free coordinates, and an active nonconstant restricted inequality must have the specified local normal. The five-product K4 construction supplies an actual feasible local half-plane, not just a necessary support bound; its rational witness and all six residual arc bounds check out. The balanced-incidence construction similarly provides a sufficiently small feasible neighborhood with controlled profile changes and a valid single-entry adjustment. The Fibonacci complement remains invertible, preserves positive balancing weights, and gives the claimed linear observation count. The simple degree-three lift uniquely extends every state flow and preserves the free products. I reran exact facet checks at `q=3,4`: the supporting faces have dimensions 21 and 30 in affine dimensions 22 and 31, with ratios 2 and 3 and zero free-product coefficients in the affine equations.

**R3-A07 — Universality and prior-work positioning: justified within the checked sources.** Theorem 7.1 needs more than an unspecified polytope isomorphism. I checked De Loera and Onn's primary paper online: Theorem 1.1 uses coordinate-erasing representation, and printed page 816 explicitly embeds the earlier coordinates in layer 1. The positive-layer padding and the state-flow capacity bound then establish the manuscript's retained-coordinate transfer. Normalization changes the free section to `Q/B`, preserving ratios. The model's unit data and the rational constants selecting a section are correctly distinguished. I also downloaded and checked the primary Khademnia–Davarnia article: its Theorem 1 is a complete EC&R description, its network section explicitly duplicates equality balances with opposite signs, and Example 2 demonstrates a nonunit aggregation weight. The manuscript accurately acknowledges this predecessor and states the stronger retained-product ratio claim separately. These checks support the stated contribution boundary; I have not proved a universal negative claim that no other publication contains any of these specializations.

Primary sources checked: [De Loera and Onn](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/researchsummary/universalitytransportation.pdf), [Khademnia and Davarnia](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10546393).

## Overall assessment

The paper is long, but its ordering and scope tables make the progression from classical disaggregation to the specific structural results understandable. The repeated boundaries about equality flows, side constraints, rational row scaling, and observed-label counts protect against material misinterpretations. I have no presentation change to require as a condition of acceptance. The finite libraries are primarily structural results with large parameter factors; the paper says so. The sharp thresholds, invariant product-coefficient obstructions, and observation-sensitive exact formulation provide sufficient theoretical substance beyond the established hull and transportation ingredients.
