Independent whole-manuscript referee report, reviewer 1

Manuscript: *Sparse convex hulls for network flows coupled to a simplex*, frozen `final-round1/paper-network-simplex` submission. Review date: September 9, 2026.

**Verdict: accept.** I found no major or minor defect requiring correction. The optional editorial suggestion R1-O1 below is not a condition of acceptance. This judgment follows a fresh reading of every manuscript section, theorem, proof, example, computational claim, table, conclusion, and bibliography, followed by primary-source checks and private execution of the standalone deliverables. I did not read another reviewer’s report, an earlier report, or an adjudication.

The paper makes a substantial structural contribution within an established convexification framework. The most useful positive result is the observation-sensitive extended formulation with a precise residual-cycle count and controlled coefficients. The bounded-rank libraries explain how graph structure limits the projected inequalities, while the coordinate-section constructions show that the coefficient restrictions are real. The sharp three-versus-four observed-label threshold on flat chains and the sparse Fibonacci family are especially convincing complements. The numerical experiments support a model-size and certificate contribution; they correctly acknowledge that elementary state merging and standard LPs often win on runtime. The paper does not need a universal speed advantage to justify its mathematical contribution.

**R1-C1 — Mathematical foundations and compression: no defect found.** Locations: Sections 2–4, in particular simplex-vertex disaggregation, block factorization, exact blockwise state reduction, fixed-arc deletion, unit-minor elimination, and observation-sensitive compression. I checked both directions of each equivalence rather than using the existence of full disaggregation as a substitute for the compression proof. A circulation restricted to an individual cyclic block is balanced even at an articulation vertex because each fundamental cycle belongs to a single block. This establishes the Cartesian factorization used by the local merger. The proportional refinement uses one global simplex vector, preserves each global state’s capacity bounds, and divides only when the merged weight is positive. The local set is nonempty wherever scalar multiplication of it is used. Empty base domains are separately handled.

For elimination, the selected observed coordinates are distinct product coordinates; the Schur entries are minors of a totally unimodular cycle matrix. Thus combining the reconstructed expressions does not accidentally double a selected product coefficient: other observations introduce distinct coordinates and different labels have disjoint columns. The restriction kernel consists exactly of circulations supported on unobserved arcs, including loops and isolated vertices in the rank formula. The completion proposition measures individual-coordinate reconstruction on the ambient circulation space. It explicitly avoids the false claim that this is an extension-complexity lower bound or a dimension count on a zero-weight slice. The fixed-arc lemma correctly removes all affine restrictions arising from bound degeneracy when every fixed coordinate is removed. Retaining the state and residual bounds is essential, and the manuscript retains them.

**R1-C2 — Structured oracles, rank bounds, and degeneracies: no defect found.** Locations: Sections 5–6. The theta feasibility tests separately establish local nonemptiness before support addition. The planar Minkowski argument covers segments and points; it does not incorrectly generalize the edge argument to higher dimension. The parallel-path lower-bound shift produces nonnegative row targets using complementary subset inequalities, and the displayed min-cut expression yields the claimed sufficient conditions. Choosing an active affine majorant of the concave right-hand side returns a globally valid cut, including in the presence of ties.

For bounded rank, the positive circuits are unit because the row configuration is totally unimodular. Edge directions remain covered for lower-dimensional states because affine-hull rows count among the active rows. The objective-space arrangement is pointed because it includes the coordinate hyperplanes, so testing its extreme rays suffices. The stronger dual multiplier bound is justified by applying the unimodular basis transform to the defining edge directions; a direct triangle-inequality estimate would not suffice, but that is not the argument used. Recovery enumerates bases of the intersection with the remaining Minkowski sum, so it handles lower-dimensional feasible sets. The denominator-growth argument is additive in encoding length over successive states. The stated arithmetic bounds, parameter dependence, observation grouping proviso, and separate dense-output cost are consistent.

**R1-C3 — Coefficient obstructions and observed-label scope: no defect found.** Locations: the local-section lemma and K4 construction in Section 6, Sections 7–9. I recomputed the K4 state sums and residual arc formulas and checked that the two potentially tight residual bounds are equivalent to the claimed local half-plane. The supplied witness establishes sufficiency on an open neighborhood, which is needed for the ambient coefficient-ratio conclusion. An affine equation valid on the whole hull restricts to the zero equation on this two-dimensional coordinate section, so adding affine-hull equations cannot change either free product coefficient.

The transportation transfer uses designated coordinates in one common table layer. I checked the cited injection in De Loera–Onn, Section 3.3, printed page 816: it does retain every input cell in layer 1. The boundary observations fix the first two layers’ margins; subtraction fixes the third. The common capacity follows from acyclic nonnegative state flow of value D_k. Padding ensures positive layer weights and does not alter old coordinates. Uniform normalization preserves the ratio. These points justify the two-label statement without assuming facets survive arbitrary projection.

For the balanced-incidence construction, multiplying the profile inequalities by the positive balancing vector proves necessity, while the explicit inverse-matrix correction preserves the total profile and supplies local sufficiency. The Fibonacci recurrence, complement invertibility, observation count, and degree-three transformation are consistent. In the fixed-state results, the residual state is always unobserved and is eliminated before constructing the reduced normal universe. I checked the four forms of the sixteen three-label circuits and the two bypass-coefficient repairs. The repairs change only coordinates belonging to a valid gadget balance and leave product coefficients unchanged. Finally, the observed-label corollary merges only globally unobserved labels and preserves all original simplex coordinates. It does not discard a structurally observed label merely because its query weight is zero. The flat-chain threshold is not presented as a threshold for general series–parallel graphs.

**R1-C4 — Prior work and novelty: no defect found within the sources examined.** Locations: Section 2.2, comparison table, and the local attributions in Sections 5–9. I inspected the local primary texts of Khademnia–Davarnia (Theorems 1–3, equality handling, Example 2), Davarnia’s dissertation (Proposition 2.6), Liberti–Pantelides (Theorem 3.1 and the following relaxation warning), Kis–Horváth (Section 5.9, Proposition 22 and its transportation projection), De Loera–Onn (Theorems 1.1–1.2 and the first-layer injection), and Onn–Rothblum (the edge-direction zonotope refinement). The paper accurately identifies the established ingredients and does not claim general hull existence, common-factor elimination, transportation projection, or polynomial separation as new. The distinctions from nonunit aggregation weights and aggregate multiflow integrality are meaningful. I also verified the current bibliographic dates on the primary pages for [Davarnia–Rahimian v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15861v2) and [Almoghrabi–Skutella–Warode](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-026-02392-8). This is a targeted prior-work audit, not proof that no uncited predecessor exists.

**R1-O1 — Optional minor presentation preference; no correction required.** Locations: the introduction, the ends of Sections 4–9, and the conclusion. The repeated qualifications about generic polynomial separation, rational coefficient scaling, affine-hull invariance, side-row exactness, and the flat-chain scope are correct and useful. At fifty pages, however, the exposition could be shortened slightly by retaining each qualification beside its theorem and replacing some later repetitions with cross-references. Preserve all mathematical hypotheses and the existing strong-baseline discussion. This is an editorial preference, not a correctness or novelty objection.

No required corrections are requested. The acceptance recommendation rests on the proofs and the qualified contribution, with the validation limits stated above.
