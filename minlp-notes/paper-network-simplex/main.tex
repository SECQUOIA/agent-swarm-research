\begin{abstract}
We study the convex hull of selected products $x_ey_j$, where $x$ is a
bounded network flow with equality balances and $y$ lies in a simplex.
Classical simplex-vertex disaggregation gives an exact polynomial-size
extended formulation, but it copies the network for every simplex vertex.
For each cyclic block and each label observed in it, we give an exact
formulation with one additional variable per independent cycle of the arcs
on which that label is unobserved, with all flow, product, and auxiliary
coefficients in $\{0,\pm1\}$. This count equals the minimum number of missing
individual products whose addition determines these observed-label state
flows through the balance equations; if the unobserved arcs form forests, no
additional variables are needed. If every block has cycle rank at most $r$,
finite libraries give exact separation and recovery without online linear
programming, and some description has flow and product coefficients bounded
by a function of $r$ that is sharp at rank three. On chains of parallel arc
pairs with one bypass, at most three observed labels guarantee flow and
product coefficients in $\{0,\pm1\}$, whereas four can force a ratio of two.
In general, coefficients are unbounded: with growing label count, Fibonacci
ratios arise on simple series--parallel graphs of maximum degree three, and
a transfer of transportation universality gives every integer ratio with two
labels. In synthetic experiments, the new formulations are much smaller than
disaggregation, but a conventional formulation with unused labels merged is
often faster.
\end{abstract}
