# Exact convex QP with rational matrices and algebraic affine data

Date: 2026-09-28. Status: a direct reduction to rational QP and algebraic
recognition, with a completed
[fresh independent review](number-field-psd-qp-fresh-review.md).
This is a supporting theorem; no novelty claim is made.

The constant-matrix QP charts give the required field and size bounds,
but those charts alone are not a polynomial-time algorithm. A norm
regularization and two linear error bounds supply the missing algorithm.

## 1. Input and conclusion

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\subset\mathbb R\) be given by a dense
irreducible integer polynomial of degree \(D\), an isolating interval
for its selected real root, and rational power-basis representations of
the input elements. Consider
\[
 P=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:Cx\le b\},\qquad
 f(x)=\tfrac12x^TQx+c^Tx,                                  \tag{1}
\]
where \(Q\succeq0\) and \(C\) are rational, while \(b,c\) have
entries in \(K\). Equalities can be represented by both signs. The
number of variables and rows is unrestricted. Let \(N\) include the
entire explicit input, in particular the dense field representation.
If there are no variables, evaluate the constant constraints directly;
the proof below concerns \(n\ge1\).

There is a deterministic algorithm polynomial in \(N\) that classifies
infeasibility and unboundedness below and, in the remaining case, returns
the least-Euclidean-norm optimizer \(x_*\) in the original field \(K\).
Its rational power-basis coefficients have polynomial total bit length.
Consequently the weaker requested bound \(f(D)N^C\), with an absolute
input exponent, also holds. This conclusion uses the standard exact
rational convex QP and certified algebraic recognition algorithms.

The main construction below assumes \(P\ne\varnothing\) and a finite
infimum. Section 6 removes that promise using rational LPs and the same
number-field separation estimates.

## 2. Charts give uniform bounds without being enumerated

A convex quadratic bounded below on a nonempty polyhedron attains its
minimum. Its optimal set is closed and convex, so it has a unique
least-norm point \(x_*\). The
[constant-matrix chart lemma](common-range-optimizer-witness.md#2-constant-matrix-quadratic-programming-charts)
applies with \(b,c\) as real parameters. For a basis \(I\) of all
active row normals at \(x_*\), it gives
\[
 \binom{x_*}{\lambda}
 =\begin{pmatrix}Q&C_I^T\\C_I&0\end{pmatrix}^{+}
                      \binom{-c}{b_I}.                     \tag{2}
\]
The matrix on the right is rational and has polynomial coefficient
length, including when it is singular. The basis multipliers here may
have either sign; this formula is a chart identity, not a KKT certificate
using a nonnegative multiplier support.

Every coordinate of \(x_*\) is therefore a rational linear combination
of the input coordinates of \(b,c\), with uniformly polynomial-size
rational coefficients. Thus \(x_*\in K^n\), and its power-basis
representation has polynomial size. Rational determinant bounds and a
Cauchy bound for every conjugate of \(\alpha\) give an effectively
computable integer \(R\ge1\), of polynomial bit length, such that
\(x_*\in[-R,R]^n\). They also give a polynomial bound on the
coefficient bits of every coordinate's absolute minimal polynomial.
One can obtain the latter bound by clearing denominators, multiplying
by an appropriate power of the leading coefficient of the polynomial
of \(\alpha\), and bounding all conjugates of the resulting algebraic
integer. Its field norm gives an integer annihilator of degree at most
\(D\); passing to an irreducible factor preserves a polynomial bound.

These are uniform bounds over the possible row subsets. Computing a
conservative bound needs no active-set search. No chart is generated by
the algorithm.

Replace \(P\) by \(P_R=P\cap[-R,R]^n\), which has the same optimum
and the same least-norm optimizer. Write its rows as \(Ax\le d\),
where \(A\) is rational. Choose a rational \(U\ge1\), of polynomial
bit length, bounding the Euclidean norm of every point of the box.

## 3. Two computable Hoffman constants

For a fixed real matrix \(M\), Hoffman's bound is
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(y,\{x:Mx\le r\})
 \le H_M\|(My-r)_+\|_\infty                             \tag{3}
\]
whenever the fiber is nonempty. An upper bound for \(H_M\) is the
maximum of \(\sqrt{|I|}/\sigma_{\min}(M_I)\) over independent
row subsets. To see this, project \(y\) onto the polyhedron and express
the displacement as a nonnegative combination of independent active
normals. Taking its inner product with that displacement proves (3).

For rational \(A\), rational minor bounds give
\(1\le H_A\le2^{N^{O(1)}}\), with a computable upper bound.
We also need such a bound for
\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}A\\Q\\-Q\\c^T\\-c^T\end{pmatrix}.    \tag{4}
\]
This matrix lies in \(K\). Its entries have polynomial power-basis
length and uniformly bounded conjugates. Every nonzero square minor
has magnitude at least \(2^{-N^{O(1)}}\) in the selected embedding:
clear denominators and the required power of the leading coefficient
of the polynomial of \(\alpha\); the resulting nonzero algebraic
integer has nonzero integral norm, while its other conjugates have
the usual determinant upper bounds. At most one of the two proportional
rows \(c^T,-c^T\) occurs in a nonzero minor, although a general
number-field determinant estimate would also suffice.

For any independent row submatrix, select a nonsingular square column
minor. Its determinant lower bound and a matrix norm upper bound give
a lower bound for that row submatrix's smallest singular value. Hence
one can print \(1\le H_M\le2^{N^{O(1)}}\) satisfying (3), again
without finding the row subset attaining the bound. The right-hand side
of the polyhedron does not enter a Hoffman constant.

Let \(O=\operatorname{argmin}_{P_R}f\), and put
\(q_*=Qx_*\), \(a_*=c^Tx_*\). Then
\[
 O=\{x\in P_R:Qx=q_*,\ c^Tx=a_*\}.                       \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, any two minimizers have the same \(Qx\), by the midpoint
identity and positive semidefiniteness. Their quadratic values and then
their linear values agree. The converse follows by substitution into
\(f\). Although the right-hand sides in (5) are unknown, its matrix
is exactly (4), so its Hoffman bound is available.

Let \(q\ge\|Q\|_2\) be a computable rational upper bound. For
\(z\in P_R\), put \(\gamma=f(z)-f(x_*)\). The variational
inequality at \(x_*\) gives
\[
 \|Q(z-x_*)\|\le\sqrt{2q\gamma},\qquad
 |c^T(z-x_*)|\le\gamma+U\sqrt{2q\gamma}.                    \tag{6}
\]
The second inequality uses
\(\tfrac12(z^TQz-x_*^TQx_*)
 =\tfrac12(z+x_*)^TQ(z-x_*)\).
It follows from (3)--(6) that, for \(0\le\gamma\le1\),
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(z,O)\le T\sqrt\gamma,\qquad
 T=H_M[1+2(U+1)(q+1)]\ge1.                                 \tag{7}
\]
Only \(\log T=N^{O(1)}\) matters for the algorithm.

## 4. Approximate one specified optimizer using rational QPs

For rational \(\varepsilon,\delta>0\), compute rational data with
\[
 b\le b^+\le b+\varepsilon\mathbf1,\qquad
                  \|\widetilde c-c\|_2\le\varepsilon.
\]
Keep the rational box rows exact. Exact number-field root refinement
and rational polynomial evaluation produce these data with polynomial
cost in \(N\) and \(\log(1/\varepsilon)\). Solve the rational QP
\[
 y=\operatorname{argmin}_{Cx\le b^+,\ x\in[-R,R]^n}
 \left[\tfrac12x^TQx+\widetilde c^Tx
                              +\tfrac\delta2\|x\|^2\right]. \tag{8}
\]
Its feasible set contains \(P_R\); its objective is strictly convex.
The classical exact rational QP algorithm returns its unique rational
minimizer in polynomial bit time.

Choose a rational Lipschitz bound
\(L\ge qU+\|c\|_2\) for \(f\) on the box, and put
\(W=2U+LH_A\). By (3), some \(z\in P_R\) satisfies
\(\|z-y\|\le\zeta:=H_A\varepsilon\). Comparison with \(x_*\)
in (8) yields
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|y\|^2&\le\|x_*\|^2+2W\varepsilon/\delta,\\
 0\le f(z)-f(x_*)&\le W\varepsilon+\delta U^2/2=:\Gamma.
 \end{split}                                               \tag{9}
\]
For the first line, use
\(f(y)\ge f(z)-L\zeta\ge f(x_*)-L\zeta\); for the second,
drop the nonnegative term \(\delta\|y\|^2/2\) in the comparison
and repair the objective by at most \(L\zeta\).

Assume \(\Gamma\le1\), and set
\(e=H_A\varepsilon+T\sqrt\Gamma\). By (7),
\(\operatorname{dist}(y,O)\le e\). Let \(w\) be its projection
onto \(O\). Since \(x_*\) is the projection of zero onto \(O\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|w-x_*\|^2
 &\le\|w\|^2-\|x_*\|^2
 \le2W\varepsilon/\delta+2Ue,\\
 \|y-x_*\|&\le e+\sqrt{2W\varepsilon/\delta+2Ue}.          \tag{10}
 \end{split}
\]
Both \(w\) and \(y\) lie in the box, which justifies the factor
\(2U\). The repair point and projections occur only in the proof.
They are not computed by the algorithm.

Here is one fully rational precision choice. For \(\tau=2^{-p}\le1\),
set
\[
 E=\frac{\tau^2}{16(U+1)},\qquad
 \delta=\frac{E^2}{8T^2(U^2+1)},
\]
and choose
\[
 0<\varepsilon\le
 \min\left\{\frac{E}{2H_A},\frac{E^2}{16WT^2},
                         \frac{\delta\tau^2}{32W}\right\}. \tag{11}
\]
Then \(\Gamma\le E^2/(8T^2)<1\), \(e\le E\), and
\(2W\varepsilon/\delta\le\tau^2/16\). The right-hand side of
(10) is at most
\(\tau/16+\sqrt3\,\tau/4<\tau\).
All printed precision bits are \(O(p)+N^{O(1)}\).
Thus (8) is a certified approximation oracle for the same point
\(x_*\) at every precision, with polynomial bit complexity.

The norm penalty is essential to this argument. Merely solving rounded
unregularized QPs need not converge to the least-norm optimizer:
minimizing \(-\varepsilon x\) on \([0,1]\) always selects \(1\),
whereas the least-norm optimizer of the limiting zero objective is \(0\).

## 5. Exact reconstruction inside the input field

Apply the existing
[constructive common-field recovery theorem](constructive-common-field-recovery.md)
to the tuple \((\alpha,x_*)\), with degree bound \(D\), the
polynomial height bound from Section 2, and the approximation oracle
from Section 4. Its common field is exactly \(K\), since all output
coordinates belong to \(K\) and the tuple includes \(\alpha\).
The theorem's Kannan--Lenstra--Lovasz recognition and rational linear
algebra therefore have polynomial cost and produce one degree-\(D\)
representation. There is no product of coordinate degrees.

If recovery uses a new primitive generator \(\beta\), it also returns
\(\alpha=a(\beta)\). In \(\mathbb Q[\beta]\), the vectors
\(1,a(\beta),\ldots,a(\beta)^{D-1}\) form a basis. Rational
linear algebra expresses every recovered coordinate in this basis,
giving the requested original power-basis representation in \(\alpha\).
The known isolating interval fixes its embedding.

Exact substitution checks primal feasibility. An optional exact
optimality certificate is a vector \(\lambda\ge0\) satisfying
\(Qx_*+c+C^T\lambda=0\), supported only on the exactly active
constraints. This is a feasible rational-matrix affine system over
\(K\). Its least-norm point can be recovered by the existing
[outward-rounding affine-fiber argument](common-range-witness-recovery.md#5-recover-the-affine-fiber-through-rational-quadratic-programs),
which uses rational minimum-norm QPs and one Hoffman bound. It has
polynomial bit complexity. The certificate is optional because the
certified recognition theorem already identifies the specified optimizer.

## 6. Removing the feasibility and boundedness promise

Feasibility of any rational-matrix system \(Bx\le a\), with
\(a\in K^s\), can be decided by one sufficiently accurate rational
outer approximation. First compute a conditional box containing its
least-norm feasible point, using the chart argument with objective
\(\|x\|^2\). The box has polynomial bit length.

On that box, the minimum common violation
\[
 t_* = \min\{t:t\ge0,\ Bx\le a+t\mathbf1,
                                      \ x\in[-R,R]^n\}
\]
is attained. This LP has an optimal vertex, and its matrix is rational,
so vertex determinant bounds put \(t_*\) in \(K\) with polynomial
power-basis length. If the original system is infeasible, compactness
gives \(t_*>0\). The algebraic norm estimate from Section 3 gives
a uniform computable positive lower bound \(2^{-N^{O(1)}}\).
Round \(a\) outward by less than that bound and test the boxed
rational system by exact rational LP. It is feasible exactly when the
original system is feasible. This is a gap argument, not an assumption
that rational feasibility survives arbitrary approximation.

For nonempty \(P\), boundedness below of (1) is equivalent to
feasibility of the rational-matrix system
\[
                 Qv+C^T\lambda=-c,\qquad\lambda\ge0.       \tag{12}
\]
If (12) holds, completing the square gives
\[
 f(x)=\tfrac12(x-v)^TQ(x-v)-\tfrac12v^TQv-\lambda^TCx
       \ge-\tfrac12v^TQv-\lambda^Tb\quad(x\in P).
\]
If (12) fails, separation from the closed polyhedral cone
\(\operatorname{range}Q+\operatorname{cone}(C^T)\) gives
\(h\) with \(Qh=0\), \(Ch\le0\), and \(c^Th<0\).
Every feasible point then starts a ray on which \(f\) tends to
\(-\infty\). Thus the same affine feasibility routine classifies
boundedness without solving an algebraic QP or enumerating active sets.

## 7. Sources, scope, and verification limits

The primary paper of
[Kozlov, Tarasov and Khachiyan (1980)](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/zvmmf5189)
explicitly states exact polynomial-time classification, value computation,
and optimizer recovery for convex QP with integer input, hence rational
input after clearing denominators. Its Russian full text was inspected:
the definition of exact solution and complexity claim occur on page 1320;
optimizer recovery occurs in Section 6 on page 1323. This is the only
QP solver imported by the construction above.

[Hoffman (1952)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/049/4/V49.N04.A05.pdf)
is the classical linear error-bound source; Section 3 above supplies the
particular determinant bound needed for the algebraic row in (4).
The reconstruction import and its precise KLL assumptions are stated
in the linked common-field recovery note.

As a related primary result,
[Adler and Beling (1994)](https://adler.ieor.berkeley.edu/ilans_pubs/lp_algebraic_1994.pdf)
prove polynomial algebraic-coefficient LP algorithms in the common-field
degree and size. Their Section 5, Remark 1 discusses binary symbolic
arithmetic and finite-precision ellipsoids; it explicitly leaves fuller
details to another paper. The proof here uses the direct rational-matrix
LP gap argument instead, so it does not need to import an unstated
algebraic QP extension from that LP result.

This argument addresses one dense, explicitly supplied real field.
Independently encoded algebraic coefficients with an uncontrolled
compositum degree are outside its input model. It also does not justify
using an existential active-set chart as an algorithm. No numerical
solver implementation or practical precision claim is made.
