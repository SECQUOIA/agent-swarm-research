# Convex quartic realization of monotone cube-root circuits

Date: 2026-09-28. Status: quantitative proof independently reviewed in
[the adversarial review](monotone-cube-root-circuit-review.md).
Publication priority remains unestablished.

The quartic construction also applies to a compact class of algebraic
circuits. It does not require an expanded minimal polynomial for the
output numbers. The restriction below gives explicit positive lower
bounds for intermediate values and Jacobian singular values, with
polynomially many bits.

**Theorem.** Let $k\geq1$. Suppose a directed acyclic circuit, ordered by its gates,
defines real numbers $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_k$ by

\[
 \xi_i^3=c_i+\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}\xi_j,
 \qquad c_i\geq1,\quad a_{ij}\geq0,
 \tag{1}
\]

where the coefficients are rational and encoded in binary. There is a
deterministic polynomial-bit-time construction of a rational quartic in
$n=2k$ variables with

\[
 \nabla^2F\succeq I,\qquad
 F^{-1}(0)=\{(\xi_1,\xi_1^2,\ldots,\xi_k,\xi_k^2)\}.
 \tag{2}
\]

The output includes an SOS expression with $2k+1$ rational quadratic
squares and a positive definite rational Hessian Gram matrix on the
full basis $(y,x\otimes y)$. All have polynomial bit length in the
circuit input. Signed affine gates, multiplication gates, and general
root circuits are outside the theorem.

The algebraic field generated by the circuit can have degree as large
as $3^k$, already for independent prime cube roots; see
[the exact field-degree proof](quartic-span-boundary-review.md#2-exact-algebraic-degree).
Expanding a defining polynomial is not part of the algorithm. The proof uses only rational arithmetic and certified
approximations of the circuit's real gate values.

## Bounded values and a nonsingular quadratic description

Put

\[
 A=2+\sum_{i=1}^k\left(c_i+\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}\right),
 \qquad n=2k.
 \tag{3}
\]

Then $A\geq3$. Induction in (1) gives $1\leq\xi_i\leq A$:
if predecessors satisfy this bound, the radicand is at most
$A(c_i+\sum a_{ij})\leq A^2$, and at least one.
Use variables $(X_i,Y_i)$ and define

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 b_i(X)&=c_i+\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}X_j,\\
 q_i&=X_i^2-Y_i,\\
 r_i&=X_iY_i-b_i(X),\\
 s_i&=Y_i^2-X_i b_i(X).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]

These are rational quadratics, all vanishing at the point $p$ in (2).
The equations $q_i=r_i=0$ already have $p$ as their unique real solution:
each $Y_i=X_i^2$, followed by the unique real cube root in (1).

Let $J$ be the Jacobian of the $2k$ residuals $(q_i,r_i)$ at $p$,
ordered by gates. It is block lower triangular with diagonal blocks

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}2\xi_i&-1\\\xi_i^2&\xi_i\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \det J=\prod_{i=1}^k3\xi_i^2\geq3^k\geq1.
 \tag{5}
\]

Every matrix entry has magnitude at most $2A^2$. Thus the following
rational bounds suffice:

\[
 \|J\|_2\leq\|J\|_F\leq V:=2nA^2,
 \qquad J^{\mathsf T}J\succeq\nu^2I,
 \qquad \nu:=V^{-(n-1)}.
 \tag{6}
\]

The quadratic parts of all residuals $q_i,r_i$ have norm at most one.
Their gradient norms at $p$ are at most $V$. The logarithms of $V$ and
$\nu^{-1}$ are polynomial in the input length.

## An exposing quadratic for the full circuit

For the proof, define the real quadratic

\[
 E_i=\xi_i^2q_i+s_i-\xi_i r_i.
 \tag{7}
\]

Write $u_i=X_i-\xi_i$ and $v_i=Y_i-\xi_i^2$. Direct expansion gives

\[
 E_i(p+(u,v))
 =\xi_i^2u_i^2+v_i^2-\xi_i u_i v_i
                  -u_i\sum_{j<i}a_{ij}u_j.
 \tag{8}
\]

In particular the constant and the entire gradient vanish, including
derivatives in predecessor variables. This cancellation is essential;
freezing the gate radicand while differentiating would not prove it.

Choose rational weights

\[
 \rho=(8kA)^{-2},\qquad \omega_i=\rho^{i-1},\qquad
 E_* =\sum_i\omega_i E_i.
 \tag{9}
\]

We claim that $E_*(p+z)=z^{\mathsf T}H_*z$, where

\[
 H_*\succeq\gamma I,\qquad
 \gamma:=\omega_k/4,
 \qquad \|H_*\|_2\leq W:=3kA^2.
 \tag{10}
\]

To prove the lower bound, scale each gate's two displacement coordinates
by $\sqrt{\omega_i}$. The local diagonal block becomes

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}\xi_i^2&-\xi_i/2\\-\xi_i/2&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant is $3\xi_i^2/4$ and its trace is $\xi_i^2+1$,
so its smaller eigenvalue is at least $3/8$, using $\xi_i\geq1$.
Each off-diagonal coupling between gates $j<i$ becomes
$-a_{ij}\rho^{(i-j)/2}/2$. The maximum absolute row sum of all such
couplings is at most

\[
 \frac{kA\sqrt\rho}{2}=\frac1{16}.
\]

The scaled matrix is therefore at least $(3/8-1/16)I\succeq I/4$.
Scaling back proves the gap in (10). For the upper bound, the unscaled
diagonal blocks have norm at most $2A^2$, and the coupling matrix has
absolute row sums at most $kA/2$. Their sum is at most $3kA^2$.
All weights and the reciprocal gap have polynomial bit length.

## Rational coefficients with an arbitrarily small gradient

Set $m=\gamma/2$ and $L=W+1$. For a positive rational tolerance
$\varepsilon\leq1$, put

\[
 \delta=\min\left\{1,\frac\gamma{4k},
                       \frac\varepsilon{2kV}\right\},
 \qquad \theta=\frac\delta{4A}.
 \tag{11}
\]

Compute rational approximations $\widehat\xi_i$ with absolute error
at most $\theta$ and set

\[
 G=\sum_i\omega_i
           \left(\widehat\xi_i^2q_i+s_i-\widehat\xi_i r_i\right).
 \tag{12}
\]

Every summand in (12) still vanishes at $p$ exactly. This follows from
the exact residual identities (4), not from the accuracy of the
approximations. Since $|\widehat\xi_i|\leq A+1$,

\[
 |\widehat\xi_i^2-\xi_i^2|\leq3A\theta\leq\delta,
 \qquad |\widehat\xi_i-\xi_i|\leq\delta.
\]

The differences in the affine quadratic block and gradient between
$G$ and $E_*$ are therefore at most $2k\delta$ and $2kV\delta$,
respectively. Consequently

\[
 G(p+z)=\ell^{\mathsf T}z+z^{\mathsf T}Hz,
 \qquad H\succeq mI,\quad\|H\|_2\leq L,
 \quad\|\ell\|_2\leq\varepsilon.
 \tag{13}
\]

These estimates use only rational combinations of the circuit residuals.
They require neither a primitive element for the generated field nor a
congruence projection in that field.

## Polynomial-time certified gate approximation

For completeness, given a positive rational target $0<\theta\leq1$,
all gate values can be approximated within error $\theta$ in time
polynomial in the circuit input, the encoding length of $\theta$, and
$\log(1/\theta)$. Equivalently, a dyadic target $2^{-P}$ costs
polynomial time in the circuit input and $P$. Maintain rational enclosing intervals for each
predecessor, clipped to $[1,A]$. Nonnegative coefficients give exact
radicand bounds by interval evaluation. The cube-root map has derivative
at most $1/3$ on $[1,\infty)$.

Choose endpoint approximation accuracy

\[
 \eta=\frac{\theta}{4k(A+1)^k}.
 \tag{14}
\]

Use rational bisection, with exact rational cubing comparisons, to
enclose the cube roots of both radicand endpoints within outward error
$\eta$. If $D_i$ denotes the maximum interval width through gate $i$,
then the row-sum bound $\sum a_{ij}\leq A$ gives

\[
 D_i\leq A D_{i-1}+2\eta,
 \qquad D_0=0,
 \qquad D_k\leq2\eta\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}A^j<\theta/2.
 \tag{15}
\]

Midpoints supply the required approximations. The number of bisection
bits is $O(\log(1/\theta)+k\log(A+1)+\log k)$, plus the rational
input lengths. Fixed common dyadic precision can be used for all endpoint
approximations; exact affine evaluations involve only polynomially many
input coefficients and these endpoints. Thus rational numerator and
denominator lengths, and the total bit-operation count, remain
polynomial. There is no hidden algebraic-field-degree factor.

## The quartic and its rational Hessian certificate

Choose a positive dyadic square $\varepsilon=t^2$ satisfying

\[
 \varepsilon\leq
 \min\left\{1,\frac{m^2}{2n},
            \frac{\nu^2m^2}{36n(L+nV)^2}\right\}.
 \tag{16}
\]

Construct $G$ at that tolerance by (11)--(12), and define

\[
 F=\left(\frac{G}{t\nu}\right)^2+
                  \sum_{i=1}^k\left[
                      \left(\frac{q_i}\nu\right)^2+
                      \left(\frac{r_i}\nu\right)^2\right].
 \tag{17}
\]

Equations (6) and (13) give exactly the quantitative hypotheses of the
[reviewed small-square construction](general-strongly-convex-quartic-singleton.md).
It follows that $\nabla^2F\succeq(3/2)I$. The polynomial vanishes at
$p$ and is a sum of rational squares, so $p$ is its unique zero. Its
degree is four because the positive definite quadratic part of $G$
contributes a nonzero leading quartic part.

The stronger condition (16) supplies a positive definite Hessian Gram
matrix by the [reviewed block-Gram argument](sos-convex-quartic-realization.md).
That argument is an algebraic identity in the point coordinates and the
rational quadratic data; it does not require the point to be presented
by a single minimal polynomial. Here the point is bounded by
$\|p\|\leq nA^2$ and can be approximated with polynomial precision
cost by (14)--(15). After the rational quadratics are fixed, the real
Gram entries are polynomials of degree at most two in the formal center
coordinates: the constant Hessian block is quadratic, the linear block
is affine, and the quadratic block is constant; conjugating by the
affine shift preserves degree at most two. Let $N_g=n+n^2$, let
$R_p=nA^2+1$, and compute a rational $C\geq1$ bounding the coefficient
absolute sum of every entry polynomial. This takes polynomial bit time.
If the center is approximated with coordinate error $\zeta\leq1$, a
bound on partial derivatives gives Frobenius error at most
$2N_g n C R_p\zeta$. For the explicit positive block-Gram gap $\mu$
from that certificate note, choose

\[
 0<\zeta<\min\left\{1,\frac\mu{8N_g n C R_p}\right\}.
\]

Approximating each gate to $\zeta/(2A+1)$ controls both its value and
its square to within $\zeta$, so (14)--(15) supply this center precision
in polynomial bit time. The Gram error is then less than $\mu/4$.
The same exact rational coefficient projection produces a positive
definite rational certificate. No expanded common-field representation
is needed.

All input-dependent rational constants above have polynomial bit length.
In particular $\log(1/\gamma)=O(k\log(kA))$ and
$\log(1/\nu)=O(k\log(kA))$; (11) and (16) require only polynomially
many further approximation bits. The rational quartic, its $2k+1$ SOS
factors, and its Hessian certificate have polynomial size. An expanded
quartic has only $O(k^4)$ possible monomials.

## Consequences and scope

A real embedding of the generated field preserves (4) and hence fixes
the unique real solution. Thus this circuit class lies within the
one-real-embedding restriction of the earlier singleton theorem. The
new input representation permits nested cube-root gates without paying
for an exponentially large expanded minimal polynomial.

For any rational linear form in the gate values, exact comparison to a
rational threshold reduces to adding that affine inequality to
$F\leq0$. A rational box containing $p$ can be added if compactness is
wanted. This is an arithmetic-comparison reduction, not an NP-hardness
claim or a lower bound for exact decision.

The assumptions $c_i\geq1$ and $a_{ij}\geq0$ are used for both the
uniform lower bound on gate values and the stable sequential
approximation. The proof does not justify replacing them by arbitrary
signed arithmetic circuits, nor does it represent general multiplication
gates. The [independent review](monotone-cube-root-circuit-review.md)
checks the quantitative proof and certificate construction. A broader
literature comparison remains necessary to assess publication priority.
