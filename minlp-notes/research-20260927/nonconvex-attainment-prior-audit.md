# Prior-art audit: attainment and algebraic optimizers

Date: 2026-09-28. Scope: attained minima of rational quadratic systems
with arbitrary affine rows and fixed native constraint Hessian-span
dimension. This is a literature and reduction audit, not a review of the
ordered-limit proof under development.

**Assessment.** Once the finite-infimum value bound in
[the finite-infimum note](nonconvex-finite-infimum.md) is available,
Grigoriev--Pasechnik sampling and the minimum-face lemma already give
polynomial-size optimizer certificates, an attainment test, and optimizer
recovery with an NP oracle, for every fixed Hessian span. A fully rational
reduction gives the weaker uniform bound
\(N^{O((h+1)^2)}\). Consequently, the fixed-parameter complexity class
alone should not be presented as the main new step in the attainment
extension. A direct \(N^{O(h+1)}\) height bound and a bound for a
minimum-norm optimizer remain more precise claims requiring proof.

Working over the algebraic number field generated by the infimum gives
the sharper degree bound immediately from the same sampling theorem.
The corresponding coefficient-height bound is plausible by following the
sampling proof over that field, but its published theorem states its bit
bound only for integer coefficients. This audit does not silently identify
an arithmetic-operation bound with the needed bit bound.

## 1. What the main sampling source actually states

[Grigoriev--Pasechnik, *Polynomial-time computing over quadratic maps I:
sampling in real algebraic sets*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0403008),
Theorem 1.2, applies over a computable subring of a real closed field. For
the zero set of \(p(Q(x))\), with \(Q\) having \(k\) quadratic
components and \(\deg p=d\), it gives component samples in real
univariate representation (RUR), of degree \((dn)^{O(k)}\). The stated
coefficient-bit estimate applies when the coefficient ring is
\(\mathbb Z\).

Theorem 1.5 also states exact minimization, minimizer computation, and
nonattainment detection for a polynomial in the same quadratic map, with
the same bounds. Its proof is deferred to a continuation. This statement
concerns the whole algebraic zero set; arbitrary additional affine
inequalities are not included at no cost.

The source proof uses special quotient bases, determinant computations,
infinitesimal limits, and restricted elimination. In particular,
Proposition 4.8 controls parameter degrees and coefficient bits in the
critical-point reduction. These are promising tools for a number-field
extension, but are not themselves its complete proof.

The current status of the announced optimization proof deserves explicit
qualification. [Kamminga--Rudolph, *The Pure-State Consistency of Local
Density Matrices Problem: In PSPACE and Complete for a Class Between QMA
and QMA(2)*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol362-itcs2026/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.83/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.83.pdf),
ITCS 2026, Definition 1.14 and Theorem 1.15, give parallel approximation
of the optimum and an optimizer on a **bounded** quadratic-map zero set.
Their processor bound has a quadratic exponent in the number of map
components. Immediately after that theorem, they report that a proof of
Grigoriev--Pasechnik Theorem 1.5 is unavailable to their knowledge. Thus
the distinction between the proved sampling theorem and the announced
optimization theorem is also made in recent primary literature. Their
result does not provide an arbitrary-affine-row attainment theorem.

## 2. An unconditional rational reduction for optimizer certificates

Let the original problem have input length \(N\), constraint Hessian
span \(h\), and finite infimum \(\theta\). The existing value theorem
gives a defining polynomial \(f_\theta\in\mathbb Z[T]\), together
with an isolating interval \([a,b]\), whose degrees and bit lengths are
\(N^{O(h+1)}\). Choose the endpoints not to be roots. This section
uses that value theorem as an input; it does not derive it from earlier
sampling results.

The rational Hessian-basis lift writes the original feasible set as the
projection of

\[
 P\cap\{F_1(w)=\cdots=F_h(w)=0\},
\]

where \(P\) is a rational polyhedron, \(w=(x,y)\), and the
\(F_j\) are rational quadratics. Introduce one further coordinate
\(t\). Set

\[
 \widehat P=P\times[a,b],\qquad
 Q(w,t)=(F_1(w),\ldots,F_h(w),q_0(x)-t,t),
\]

and define

\[
 p(Y)=\sum_{j=1}^{h+1}Y_j^2+f_\theta(Y_{h+2})^2.
                                                        \tag{1}
\]

The set \(\widehat P\cap Z(p\circ Q)\) is exactly the lifted
optimizer set, with the extra coordinate equal to \(\theta\).
In particular, it is nonempty exactly when the infimum is attained.
The interval prevents other real conjugates of \(\theta\) from
creating false optimizer witnesses.

If this set is nonempty, choose a face of \(\widehat P\) of smallest
dimension that meets the variety. The
[reviewed minimum-face lemma](nonconvex-hessian-span-prior-audit.md)
shows that an entire connected component of the variety restricted to
the affine hull of this face lies in the face. A rational affine chart
has polynomial bit size in the description of \(\widehat P\).
On that chart, \(Q\) still has at most \(h+2\) quadratic components.
Grigoriev--Pasechnik Theorem 1.2 therefore returns a sample in the face.
Its coordinates contain an optimizer of the original problem.

Writing \(D=\deg f_\theta\), the outer degree in (1) is at most
\(2\max(D,1)\). Hence the sampling bound is

\[
 (DN)^{O(h+1)}=N^{O((h+1)^2)}.                          \tag{2}
\]

The same bound holds for the coefficient bits, including the chart,
isolating endpoints, and conversion to a common rational RUR. Clearing
denominators does not change the zero set. A zero-dimensional face is
handled directly. Unbounded faces and lineality cause no exception.

This establishes a rational box of radius
\(2^{N^{O((h+1)^2)}}\) containing an optimizer whenever one exists.
The box need not contain every optimizer. It also says nothing about
whether the sampled optimizer has minimum Euclidean norm.

### Complexity consequence

For fixed \(h\), use the already established
\(\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}\) finite-value procedure to obtain
\(f_\theta,[a,b]\). The existence of a short RUR satisfying all
original rows, (1), and \(a\le t\le b\) is an NP predicate.
Polynomial-time univariate sign determination checks a proposed RUR.
Bound (2) proves completeness of this certificate system.

One NP query decides attainment. Standard prefix search on a bounded
certificate encoding then recovers an optimizer RUR with polynomially
many NP queries. This search uses predicates asking whether an accepted
certificate has a given prefix; it is not restricted to rational
objective-threshold queries. There is no claim that a witness itself
certifies global optimality without the previously computed exact value.

Thus exact attainment and recovery of **some** optimizer belong to
\(\mathrm{FP}^{\mathrm{NP}}\) for fixed \(h\) by this shorter route.
This is a conditional corollary of the new finite-value bound and an
established sampling theorem, not evidence that the full statement was
previously published.

## 3. What working over the optimal-value field adds

Let \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\theta)\), using the selected real embedding.
Instead of (1), use the \(h+1\) quadratic equations

\[
 F_1(w)=\cdots=F_h(w)=0,\qquad q_0(x)-\theta=0.
                                                        \tag{3}
\]

The polyhedron \(P\) is still rational, so the same minimum-face
argument applies. Use Theorem 1.2 with coefficient ring \(K_0\), a
computable subring of \(\mathbb R\), and outer polynomial equal to a
sum of squares. It yields a relative RUR over \(K_0\) of degree
\(E=N^{O(h+1)}\). If \(\alpha\) is its selected root, every output
coordinate is in \(K_0(\alpha)\). Therefore

\[
 [\mathbb Q(x^*):\mathbb Q]
 \le [K_0(\alpha):K_0]\,[K_0:\mathbb Q]
 \le E D=N^{O(h+1)}.                                    \tag{4}
\]

This is a proved degree conclusion from the published theorem. Products
of two bounds with exponents linear in \(h\) still have an exponent
linear in \(h\). No quadratic exponent is necessary for degree.

The height conclusion needs a further step. To obtain the desired full
RUR bound by this route, one needs relative output coefficient heights
bounded by

\[
 \operatorname{poly}(D,H)\,N^{O(h+1)},                  \tag{5}
\]

where \(H\) bounds the defining and input field coefficient bits and
the degree of the polynomial in \(D,H\) is independent of \(h\).
The arithmetic-operation count alone does not establish (5): repeated
squaring is an elementary example of short arithmetic computations
whose intermediate bit lengths grow much faster than the operation
count.

Once (5) is known, passage to rational defining polynomials, selection
of the real embedding, and a common primitive element do not require a
quadratic exponent in \(h\). Resultants and field norms multiply
the relative degree by \(D\), and their coefficient bits are polynomial
in the degrees and coefficient bits. For the univariate isolation part,
[Strzeboński--Tsigaridas, *Univariate real root isolation in an extension
field*](https://who.paris.inria.fr/Elias.Tsigaridas/files/st-issac-2011.pdf),
Problem 1 and Sections 3--4, provide polynomial bit algorithms and
separation bounds with the degree and defining height of the simple
extension included in the input. This removes a possible isolation
obstruction; it does not supply (5) for the earlier sampling stage.

The proof architecture in Grigoriev--Pasechnik suggests that (5) should
follow by tracking coefficient heights through bounded-size quotient
matrices, determinants, and infinitesimal removal over \(K_0\).
This audit has not completed that extension or located an explicit
primary statement with the required dependence. Accordingly:

- the sharper optimizer **degree** bound is an established-theorem
  corollary;
- a number-field route to the sharper **height** bound is credible but
  is not counted here as a verified black-box corollary;
- this unresolved audit step provides no evidence of publication
  novelty for a direct height proof.

The minimum-norm claim is separate again. Sampling (3) gives some
optimizer, not necessarily a minimum-norm one. Applying a value theorem
to \(\|x\|^2\) on (3), over \(K_0\), followed by sampling at that
second value would give the desired point. This requires the
corresponding number-field extension for the value theorem as well.
In a nonconvex problem a minimum-norm optimizer need not be unique, so
the term “canonical optimizer” needs an additional uniqueness argument.

## 4. A sharper hardness boundary: one quadratic inequality

The primary general attainment-hardness reference is
[Ahmadi--Zhang, *On the Complexity of Testing Attainment of the Optimal
Value in Nonlinear Optimization*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.07683),
Theorem 2.2. It proves strong NP-hardness for a linear objective and
quadratic constraints on instances that are feasible and bounded below.
Its displayed reduction uses separate Boolean quadratic equations, so
the native Hessian span grows with the SAT instance. The following
simple compression gives a stronger parameter restriction. It is an
elementary refinement of familiar Boolean-quadratic and escape-to-infinity
constructions; priority is not claimed.

**Proposition.** Testing attainment is strongly NP-hard for a linear
objective, arbitrary rational affine inequalities, and exactly one
quadratic inequality. This holds on a family with known infimum zero,
constant-size coefficients, and native Hessian span exactly one.

**Proof.** Given a 3SAT instance on \(n\ge1\) variables, use
\(x\in[0,1]^n\). Replace each positive literal by \(x_i\) and
each negative literal by \(1-x_i\); let \(L_C(x)\) be the sum of
the three literals in clause \(C\). Introduce \(t,y\ge0\) and
consider

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \inf\quad &t\\
 \text{subject to}\quad
 &0\le x_i\le1\quad(i=1,\ldots,n),\quad t,y\ge0,\\
 &L_C(x)\ge1\quad\text{for every clause }C,\\
 &\sum_{i=1}^n x_i(1-x_i)-ty\le0.
 \end{aligned}                                          \tag{6}
\]

For every \(t>0\), the point
\(x_i=1/2\), \(y=n/(4t)\) is feasible: each clause sum is
\(3/2\), and the quadratic row is zero. Thus (6) is feasible and
has infimum zero, regardless of satisfiability.

At \(t=0\), every term \(x_i(1-x_i)\) is nonnegative. The
quadratic inequality then forces every coordinate to be Boolean.
The affine clause inequalities are exactly the SAT clauses on Boolean
points. Hence zero is attained if and only if the instance is
satisfiable; a satisfying assignment gives a point with \(t=y=0\).

The Hessian of the sole quadratic row is nonzero, so its span has
dimension one. Its block on \(x\) is \(-2I_n\), and its block on
\((t,y)\) is
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
It is indefinite. All coefficients in (6) have bounded absolute value,
including clauses with repeated literals, so this is a strong
NP-hardness reduction. \(\square\)

The affine rows are essential to the scope of this statement. It does
not assert hardness for unconstrained optimization with only one
quadratic inequality. It also does not apply to systems whose native
quadratic rows are all convex.

On the promise that the infimum is zero, attainment is feasibility with
the additional affine row \(t=0\). The known short feasible-point
certificate bound places this promised problem in NP for fixed \(h\).
Thus the promised-known-zero attainment problem is strongly NP-complete
already with one quadratic inequality and arbitrary affine rows.

## 5. Sources, review, and remaining work

Primary material examined directly:

- Grigoriev--Pasechnik: Theorems 1.2, 1.5, 1.10, and 2.1;
  Proposition 4.8; the corresponding coefficient estimates and
  infinitesimal-removal steps in Sections 2, 4, and 5. The local copy is
  [the source text](nonconvex-prior-sources/grigoriev-pasechnik-2005.txt).
- Kamminga--Rudolph: Definition 1.14, Theorem 1.15, the paragraph about
  the unavailable Grigoriev--Pasechnik optimization proof, and the
  approximation and optimization discussion in Section 6.
- Ahmadi--Zhang: the introduction and Theorem 2.2 with its reduction.
- Strzeboński--Tsigaridas: Problem 1, the overview of the two isolation
  methods, and their polynomial complexity dependence.

Searches covered the announced continuation of quadratic-map sampling,
attainment with one quadratic inequality, exact optimizer encoding, and
sampling with algebraic coefficient fields. Grigoriev's publication list
was also inspected. No conclusion of novelty is drawn from unsuccessful
searches. The number-field height step remains an identified proof
obligation rather than a literature gap claim.

The attainment-proof reviewer independently reconstructed the rational
optimal-level reduction in Section 2. That reviewer and the parent
researcher independently verified the one-quadratic hardness reduction,
including fractional feasibility, the Boolean boundary, the Hessian
count, and bounded coefficients. The prior audit does not validate the
forthcoming ordered-limit proof. No numerical experiment is needed for
these algebraic reductions.
