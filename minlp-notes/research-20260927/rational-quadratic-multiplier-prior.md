# Scope and prior work for quadratic ideal multiplier certificates

Date: 2026-09-28. Status: primary-source comparison and elementary
scope checks. This note does not establish publication priority.

The companion
[affine-membership theorem](rational-quadratic-perturbation-multipliers.md)
has passed fresh review. Its
[higher-membership extension](rational-quadratic-multipliers-higher-membership.md)
has also passed a separate fresh proof review. Neither mathematical
review establishes publication priority.

## What the certificate operation adds

Write \(F_0=\sum_i q_i^2\), where the rational \(q_i\) have degree
at most two. Assume their constant span contains
\(G=X^{\mathsf T}HX+\ell^{\mathsf T}X+c\), with \(H\succ0\).
The targets \(R_a\) are quadratic and have supplied polynomial
ideal representations in the \(q_i\). For a rational symmetric
\(J\), put \(P_\lambda=\lambda F_0-R^{\mathsf T}JR\).

The existence of a scale making \(P_\lambda\) nonnegative is already
elementary under these assumptions. On a sufficiently large compact
ball, the supplied membership coefficient polynomials are bounded.
Cauchy--Schwarz then bounds \(\|R\|^2\) by a constant times \(F_0\).
Outside that ball, \(G\) is at least a positive constant times
\(\|X\|^2\), and the quadratic targets have magnitude
\(O(\|X\|^2)\). If \(G=\sum_i g_iq_i\), then
\(G^2\le(\sum_i g_i^2)F_0\), so the same domination follows there.
Finally, \(|R^{\mathsf T}JR|\le\|J\|_{\rm op}\|R\|^2\).
This establishes global domination without an SOS certificate.

The reviewed contribution is the more specific algebraic operation:
affine membership suffices for the prescribed multiplier
\(h=1+\|X\|^2\), with a directly constructed rational positive
definite Gram on a supplied polynomial list. The construction uses
rational linear algebra and has polynomial bit complexity in the
explicit input. It requires no algebraic expansion of common zeros
and no semidefinite feasibility oracle. The higher-membership extension
replaces \(h\) by \(h^d\), where \(d\) bounds the degree of the
ideal-membership coefficients, with polynomial complexity in the
expanded monomial count.

These claims concern a selectable scale \(\lambda\). They do not
give a multiplier of that degree for every already-fixed nonnegative
quartic. They also do not solve general ideal membership, discover the
required positive definite \(G\), or assert practical speedups.

## Closest primary frameworks examined

[Burgdorf, Scheiderer, and Schweighofer, *Pure states, nonnegative
polynomials and sums of squares*](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43280),
Proposition 5.3(a) and Remark 5.5(1), establish an order-unit property:
for an archimedean quadratic module and an ideal generated by
\(b_i\), the element \(\sum_i b_i^2\) dominates the ideal square
in the module order. This is a direct conceptual predecessor for
domination by an SOS baseline. The proposition does not specify the
present truncated radial multiplier, its degree from supplied ideal
membership, or the explicit rational Gram construction. Our setting
starts in a polynomial ring whose full SOS cone is not archimedean.
The positive quadratic in the factor span supplies the additional
finite-degree identity. Sections 3--5 of the published paper were
examined, particularly the stated proposition and remark.

[Lombardi, Perrucci, and Roy, *An elementary recursive bound for
effective Positivstellensatz and Hilbert 17-th
problem*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.2338), Theorem 1.4.4, give general
effective rational-function SOS certificates with degree bounds.
Their denominator can vanish only where the original polynomial
vanishes, but it may vanish at those zeros. This is broader in the
input class and does not give the fixed everywhere-positive
denominator or membership-sensitive degree at issue here. Theorem
1.4.3 also treats vanishing through a Real Nullstellensatz identity;
that is not the ordinary ideal membership used in our hypotheses.
The theorem statements and their stated certificate forms were
examined; their long constructive proof was not independently audited.

[Reznick, *On the absence of uniform denominators in Hilbert's 17th
problem*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0306163), Theorem 1 and Corollary 2,
rule out a universal fixed multiplier, or a finite predetermined
multiplier list, for a full nonnegative-form cone outside the Hilbert
SOS cases. This prevents reading our restricted construction as a
general fixed-degree certificate theorem for quartics. Our baseline,
ideal-membership, and adjustable-scale hypotheses specify a narrower
class. The paper's theorem, corollary, proof, and discussion of
positive definite forms were examined.

[Bender, Kozhasov, Tsigaridas, and Zhu, *Certificates for nonnegativity
of multivariate integer polynomials under
perturbations*](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.09808v1), December 2025,
provide a much broader certification framework using stereographic
transformation, explicit perturbations, and SOS modulo gradient
ideals. Their introduction and algorithm/complexity statements give
singly exponential dependence on dimension for that framework.
These certificate types do not themselves assert a degree-two
pole-free rational-function certificate for the original polynomial.
Their Section 7 concerns adding an explicit SOS perturbation, again
a different operation from the multiplier identity here. The
introduction and relevant stated certificate types were examined,
not every proof. The distinction is between certificate forms and
structural assumptions, not a claimed improvement of their general
algorithm.

Searches also used the terms “ideal membership,” “order unit,”
“coercive quadratic,” “multiplier degree,” and “rational SOS.”
They found these close frameworks but did not resolve priority for
the particular finite-degree statement. Failure to locate an
equivalent theorem is not evidence that none exists.

## Assumptions that cannot simply be removed

Three elementary examples explain important limits.

* Mere vanishing is insufficient. In one variable take \(q_1=x^2\)
  and \(R=x\). The factor span contains the positive definite
  quadratic \(G=x^2\), and \(R\) vanishes at the common zero.
  Nevertheless, \(\lambda x^4-x^2<0\) for sufficiently small
  nonzero \(x\), for every fixed \(\lambda\). Here \(R\) is not
  in the ordinary ideal \((x^2)\).
* A merely positive semidefinite quadratic part is insufficient.
  In two variables let \(q_1=x\), \(q_2=x^2\), \(G=x^2\), and
  \(R=xy=yq_1\). The affine membership holds, but
  \(\lambda(x^2+x^4)-x^2y^2<0\) at \(x=1\) and sufficiently
  large \(|y|\).
* Genuine higher-degree targets are not covered. For
  \(q_1=x^2+1\), take \(R=xq_1\). Then
  \(\lambda F_0-R^2=(\lambda-x^2)(x^2+1)^2\) is negative
  for large \(|x|\), although the target has an affine ideal
  representation.

Every multiplier considered in the companion notes is positive on
real space. Such multiplication cannot repair these negative values.
The examples therefore rule out the respective proposed
generalizations of the conclusion, not just this proof technique.

## Potential optimization use and remaining work

A quadratic lifting of a polynomial MINLP may supply quadratic
residuals and additional low-degree relations obtained by exact
elimination. The theorem can turn those identities into globally
valid rational SOS certificates for selected quartic penalties when
the positive quadratic span condition holds. For fixed membership
degree, constructing this certificate requires only polynomial-size
rational algebra. This is a plausible certificate-generation
operation, not yet an implemented solver improvement.

Practical value requires finding informative residual systems and
perturbations, controlling the conservative scale, retaining
sparsity, and evaluating whether the resulting bounds help an actual
relaxation or verification workflow. The supplied tower examples
demonstrate exact coefficient-field avoidance. They do not by
themselves establish usefulness on application instances.

The elementary domination argument and three counterexamples were
checked algebraically. No new computational or Lean verification was
needed for this scope note.
