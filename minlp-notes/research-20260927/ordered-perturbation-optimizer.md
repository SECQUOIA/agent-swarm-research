# The optimizer field from ordered quadratic perturbations

Date: 2026-09-27. Status: complete proof; two independent adversarial reviews
and targeted exact checks found no gap. Publication priority remains
unestablished.

The canonical optimizer of a rational convex quadratic program satisfies the
same explicit algebraic degree bound as its optimal value. Two ordered
perturbations separate minimum-norm selection from strict feasibility. This
avoids introducing an algebraic optimal-value constant into the constraint
system. In particular, the objective Hessian does not enter the parameter.

## 1. Statement

Consider

\[
 \theta=\min\{q_0(x):x\in F\},\qquad
 F=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:Ax\le b,\ Ex=e,\ q_i(x)\le0\ (1\le i\le m)\},
\]

with rational input, positive semidefinite quadratic Hessians for the
objective and all constraint polynomials, and explicit binary input length
\(N\ge2\). Let

\[
 h=\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}
               \{\nabla^2q_i:1\le i\le m\}.
\]

Assume the feasible set is nonempty and the optimal value is finite. Finite
attainment for this class is classical; the source and precise assumptions
are recorded in [the unbounded-value note](unbounded-value-optimization.md).
Let \(x^*\) be the unique minimum-norm point of the optimal set.

**Theorem.** The field generated by all coordinates of \(x^*\), and hence
also by those coordinates together with \(\theta\), has degree at most

\[
 [\mathbb Q(x_1^*,\ldots,x_n^*,\theta):\mathbb Q]
 \le (2n+1)^{\min(h,n)}.                              \tag{1}
\]

Each coordinate has a nonzero integer annihilating polynomial whose
coefficient bit lengths are \(N^{O(h+1)}\). The same asymptotic height
bound holds for its primitive minimal polynomial. The conclusions require
neither bounded domains, strict feasibility, a rational optimizer, nor a
zero-dimensional full complex KKT system.

The degree formula and height estimate come from
[the explicit elimination lemma](explicit-span-separation.md). The new step
is to obtain one common ordered limit for all optimizer coordinates while
keeping at most `h` multiplier variables. The earlier
[optimizer-recovery proof](exact-convex-optimizer-recovery.md) gives the
weaker point degree and height bound \(N^{O((h+1)^2)}\) by adjoining the
optimal value as an algebraic parameter. Its exact recovery algorithm
remains applicable with the improved bounds here. We do not infer that
its complete running-time exponent improves by the same amount.

## 2. A rational restriction preserving the selected optimizer

Write \(F^*=\{x\in F:q_0(x)=\theta\}\). This is a nonempty closed
convex set. The squared norm has a unique minimizer on it: existence follows
by restricting to any nonempty bounded norm sublevel set, and uniqueness
follows from strict convexity.

At \(x^*\), retain every affine equality, replace active affine
inequalities by equalities, retain active quadratic inequalities, and delete
all inactive inequalities. Let \(F_1\) be the resulting closed convex set.
Then \(x^*\in F_1\), and the lexicographic minimum of

\[
             (q_0(x),\|x\|_2^2)                              \tag{2}
\]

on \(F_1\) is still attained uniquely at \(x^*\). Indeed, if a point
\(y\in F_1\) had \(q_0(y)<\theta\), a sufficiently short segment
from \(x^*\) towards \(y\) would satisfy every deleted strictly slack
inequality and improve the original objective, a contradiction. Thus
\(q_0\ge\theta\) on \(F_1\). If instead \(q_0(y)=\theta\) and
\(\|y\|<\|x^*\|\), the same segment would contain an original
optimizer of smaller norm. Finally, a different point with equal objective
and equal norm would, by strict convexity of the norm, give the same
contradiction on the open segment. These arguments use convexity to preserve
all retained inequalities along the segment.

Choose a basis of the retained quadratic Hessians, indexed by \(B\),
and write

\[
 \nabla^2q_i=\sum_{j\in B}c_{ij}\nabla^2q_j.
\]

The coefficients \(c_{ij}\) are rational and have polynomial bit length
in \(N\), by solving a rational linear system with minors of the input
matrix of Hessian entries. Add the affine equations

\[
                q_i(x)-\sum_{j\in B}c_{ij}q_j(x)=0.          \tag{3}
\]

All hold at \(x^*\), because every retained quadratic row is active
there. Their intersection with \(F_1\), denoted \(F_2\), still has
\(x^*\) as its unique lexicographic minimizer. A rational parametrization
of the complete affine equations is

\[
                     x=x_0+Vu,\qquad u\in\mathbb R^d,         \tag{4}
\]

where \(V\) has full column rank, \(d\le n\), and the coefficients
have polynomial bit length in \(N\). The consistency of these equations
is witnessed by \(x^*\); rational Gaussian elimination then provides a
rational particular solution and nullspace basis. No coordinate of
\(x^*\) is used as an input coefficient.

Let \(r_i(u)=q_i(x_0+Vu)\) denote the retained constraint polynomials
and \(f(u)=q_0(x_0+Vu)\). Identities (3) make the span of the retained
\(r_i\) as **whole polynomials**, including the affine and constant
terms, have dimension at most `h`. Every \(r_i\) vanishes at the
parameter \(u^*\) of \(x^*\).
The reduced exact feasible set

\[
                    C=\{u:r_i(u)\le0\text{ for every retained }i\}
\]

takes account of all retained inequalities, not merely the eventual KKT
support. On `C`, \(f\ge\theta\), and \(u^*\) uniquely minimizes
\(\|x_0+Vu\|^2\) among points with \(f=\theta\).

If \(d=0\), (4) is the rational point \(x_0=x^*\), with polynomial
bit length. All conclusions follow directly. Assume \(d>0\) below.

## 3. The two limits

For \(\varepsilon>0\), define the strictly convex coercive objective

\[
             f_\varepsilon(u)=f(u)+\varepsilon\|x_0+Vu\|^2.  \tag{5}
\]

It is essential to use the norm in the original coordinates: replacing it
by \(\|u\|^2\) would generally select a different optimizer. Its
Hessian is \(\nabla^2 f+2\varepsilon V^TV\succ0\).

First minimize (5) over the exact set `C`, obtaining the unique point
\(u_\varepsilon\); write \(x_\varepsilon=x_0+Vu_\varepsilon\).
Comparison with \(u^*\) gives

\[
 \theta\le f(u_\varepsilon),\qquad
 f(u_\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\|x_\varepsilon\|^2
                   \le\theta+\varepsilon\|x^*\|^2.
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \|x_\varepsilon\|\le\|x^*\|,\qquad
 0\le f(u_\varepsilon)-\theta
                   \le\varepsilon\|x^*\|^2.                 \tag{6}
\]

Every accumulation point of \(x_\varepsilon\) as
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) belongs to \(F_2\), attains \(\theta\),
and has norm at most \(\|x^*\|\). Uniqueness of the lexicographic
minimizer proves

\[
                       x_\varepsilon\longrightarrow x^*.   \tag{7}
\]

Second, for each **fixed** \(\varepsilon>0\), relax the retained rows:

\[
 \min_u f_\varepsilon(u)
               \quad\text{subject to}\quad r_i(u)\le\delta,
                 \qquad \delta>0.                           \tag{8}
\]

There is a unique optimizer \(u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\). The point
\(u^*\) is strictly feasible because all retained rows vanish there.
The optimizers of (8) lie in the compact sublevel set
\(\{u:f_\varepsilon(u)\le f_\varepsilon(u_\varepsilon)\}\),
since \(u_\varepsilon\) is an available competitor. For this fixed
\(\varepsilon\), every accumulation point as \(\delta\downarrow0\)
is exact-feasible and minimizes \(f_\varepsilon\) on `C`. Uniqueness
therefore gives

\[
 u_{\varepsilon,\delta}\longrightarrow u_\varepsilon
                         \quad(\delta\downarrow0).           \tag{9}
\]

The compact set used for (9) may depend on \(\varepsilon\). We make no
claim about uniform convergence in (9), a quantitative choice of
\(\delta(\varepsilon)\), or convergence along an arbitrary diagonal
sequence. Only the ordered limits (9), then (7), are used.

## 4. A nonsingular system with at most `h` multipliers

Slater's condition and the positive definite objective Hessian in (8) give
KKT multipliers. Represent the negative objective gradient by a nonnegative
combination of active constraint gradients and choose such a representation
with smallest support `J`. Its positive-weight gradient columns are
linearly independent: a dependence permits moving the weights in one of
the two directions until a positive weight becomes zero while all weights
remain nonnegative. Since the retained whole polynomials span at most `h`,

\[
                         s=|J|\le\min(h,d).                  \tag{10}
\]

The support can be empty. Reduced multipliers preserve stationarity and
complementarity. We use minimal support of the **active gradients**, rather
than merely a spanning set of Hessian matrices.

Choose any sequence \(\varepsilon_\ell>0\) decreasing to zero. For
each \(\ell\), choose \(\delta_{\ell,\nu}>0\) decreasing to zero
along which one support \(J_\ell\) occurs. This is possible because
there are finitely many row subsets. Pass to an outer subsequence of
\(\varepsilon_\ell\) on which \(J_\ell=J\) is also fixed. This
one nested support is now fixed for every coordinate and every linear
combination considered below. Multipliers may vary and may be unbounded.

Write

\[
 r_i(u)=\tfrac12u^TQ_i u+a_i^Tu+c_i,\qquad
 f(u)=\tfrac12u^TQ_0u+a_0^Tu+c_0.
\]

For this fixed `J`, put

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M&=Q_0+2\varepsilon V^TV+\sum_{i\in J}\lambda_iQ_i,\\
 v&=a_0+2\varepsilon V^Tx_0+\sum_{i\in J}\lambda_i a_i,\\
 \Delta&=\det M,\qquad p=-\operatorname{adj}(M)v,\\
 G_i&=\tfrac12p^TQ_i p+\Delta a_i^Tp+(c_i-\delta)\Delta^2
                                                   &&(i\in J),\\
 A&=\Delta^2,\\
 B_j&=\bigl(x_{0j}\Delta+(Vp)_j\bigr)\Delta
                                                   &&(1\le j\le n).
 \end{aligned}                                               \tag{11}
\]

At the selected KKT roots, \(M\succ0\), \(u=p/\Delta\),
\(G_i=0\), \(A>0\), and \(B_j/A=x_j\). The Jacobian of the
`G` equations in the selected multipliers is nonsingular:

\[
 \frac{\partial G_i}{\partial\lambda_j}
       =-\Delta^2\nabla r_i(u)^TM^{-1}\nabla r_j(u).           \tag{12}
\]

This follows by differentiating stationarity,
\(\partial u/\partial\lambda_j=-M^{-1}\nabla r_j(u)\), and using
\(r_i(u)-\delta=0\) to remove the derivative of \(\Delta^2\).
Independent selected gradients make (12) negative definite. Other complex
components of the same polynomial system need not be isolated or regular.

All polynomials in (11) have degree at most \(a=2d\) in
\(\lambda\). Their total degree in the coefficient parameters
\((\varepsilon,\delta)\) is at most \(2d+1\). Clearing all rational
coefficients by one common positive integer gives integer polynomials of
coefficient \(\ell_1\)-norm at most \(2^\tau\), where
\(\tau=N^{O(1)}\). Multiply every `A,B_j` by the same integer so their
ratios are unchanged. The polynomial bound on \(\tau\) follows from
the rational affine restriction, determinant expansions, and denominator
clearing: the logarithm of the number of monomials is polynomial in `N`,
even though that number need not itself be polynomial.

## 5. Elimination with ordered coefficient parameters

The following extension of the
[one-parameter lemma](explicit-span-separation.md#2-an-elementary-elimination-lemma)
is the only extra elimination fact required. Its proof is included to fix
the order of specialization.

Let \(G_1,\ldots,G_s,A,B\) be integer polynomials in
\((\varepsilon,\delta,\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_s)\), of degree at
most \(a\ge1\) in \(\lambda\), and coefficient \(\ell_1\)-norm
at most \(2^\tau\), \(\tau\ge1\). Suppose there are nested parameter
sequences as in Section 4, with nonsingular `G` roots and \(A\ne0\),
such that for each fixed \(\ell\)

\[
 \frac{B(\varepsilon_\ell,\delta_{\ell,\nu},\lambda_{\ell,\nu})}
      {A(\varepsilon_\ell,\delta_{\ell,\nu},\lambda_{\ell,\nu})}
          \longrightarrow w_\ell\quad(\nu\to\infty),
 \qquad w_\ell\longrightarrow w^*\quad(\ell\to\infty).       \tag{13}
\]

Put

\[
 D=a+1,\quad L=D^s,\quad T=a(s+1),\quad
 K=L\bigl[\tau(T+1)+2+\lceil\log_2L\rceil\bigr].             \tag{14}
\]

**Ordered-limit lemma.** A nonzero \(P\in\mathbb Z[w]\) satisfies
\(P(w^*)=0\), \(\deg P\le L\), and \(\|P\|_1\le2^K\).

*Proof.* Introduce a new deformation parameter \(\beta\) and equations

\[
                       F_i=G_i+\beta\lambda_i^D.             \tag{15}
\]

Over \(\mathbb Q(\varepsilon,\delta,\beta)\), their leading
monomials for a degree-first order are the pairwise coprime
\(\lambda_i^D\). They form a Groebner basis, with quotient basis
\(\mathcal B=\{\lambda^\alpha:0\le\alpha_i<D\}\) of size `L`.
This remains a basis after every real specialization of
\((\varepsilon,\delta)\) and every nonzero \(\beta\).

Let \(M_A,M_B\) denote multiplication by `A,B` in this quotient.
A product of a basis monomial with `A` or `B` has multiplier degree at
most `T`. Replacing \(\lambda_i^D\) by \(-G_i/\beta\) strictly
reduces that degree, so each reduction branch has at most `T` replacements.
Hence \(\widetilde M_A=\beta^T M_A\) and
\(\widetilde M_B=\beta^T M_B\) have integer-polynomial entries in
\((\varepsilon,\delta,\beta)\), each of coefficient norm at most
\(2^{\tau(T+1)}\). The coefficient parameters are treated simply as
indeterminates in this norm estimate; adding a second one introduces no
new factor in the exponent. Form

\[
 H=\det\bigl(w\widetilde M_A-\widetilde M_B
                                       -\zeta\beta^T I_L\bigr).
                                                               \tag{16}
\]

This is nonzero, since its leading \(\zeta\) coefficient is
\((-\beta^T)^L\). Its `w` degree is at most `L`, and
\(\|H\|_1\le2^K\), by determinant expansion.

Let \(H_k(\varepsilon,\delta,\beta,w)\ne0\) be the lowest nonzero
\(\zeta\) coefficient. This removes permanent factors arising from
other roots with `A=B=0`. Fix any selected parameter pair and its selected
nonsingular multiplier root. The real implicit function theorem continues
that root to (15) for small \(\beta\), with \(A\ne0\). Evaluation
at it is a common left eigenvector of the multiplication matrices. Thus the
specialized determinant has the factor
\(\beta^T(Aw-B-\zeta)\). The parenthesized factor is coprime to
\(\zeta\), because \(A\ne0\). Dividing out the global lowest
power of \(\zeta\) therefore shows that \(H_k\) vanishes at the
branch value \(w(\beta)=B/A\). This remains true if specialization
makes \(H_k\) identically zero.

Let \(R(\varepsilon,\delta,w)\ne0\) be the lowest nonzero
\(\beta\) coefficient of \(H_k\). Divide by that power of
\(\beta\) and take \(\beta\to0\) on the branch. For **every**
selected pair \((\ell,\nu)\),

\[
 R\left(\varepsilon_\ell,\delta_{\ell,\nu},
                          B/A\right)=0.                     \tag{17}
\]

Write the global lowest nonzero \(\delta\) coefficient as

\[
 R(\varepsilon,\delta,w)=\delta^r S(\varepsilon,w)
                                      +O(\delta^{r+1}),
 \qquad S\ne0.
\]

For fixed \(\ell\), divide (17) by
\(\delta_{\ell,\nu}^r\) and use the finite inner limit in (13).
This gives \(S(\varepsilon_\ell,w_\ell)=0\). The coefficient
\(S(\varepsilon_\ell,w)\) may be the zero polynomial at a particular
outer parameter; the stated equality is still valid and no exclusion is
needed. Finally write

\[
 S(\varepsilon,w)=\varepsilon^t P(w)+O(\varepsilon^{t+1}),
 \qquad P\ne0.
\]

Divide by \(\varepsilon_\ell^t\) and take the finite outer limit.
Then \(P(w^*)=0\). Every coefficient extraction preserves the degree
bound in `w` and cannot increase coefficient norm. The proof also covers
\(s=0\): the quotient has dimension one and there are no root equations.
\(\square\)

## 6. Coordinate height and a single field degree

Apply the ordered-limit lemma to (11). Limits (9) and (7) give (13) for
each coordinate, on the one support fixed in Section 4. Therefore every
coordinate has an integer annihilator of degree at most
\(L=(2d+1)^s\), with

\[
 \log_2\|P_j\|_1\le
 (2d+1)^s\left[\tau\bigl(2d(s+1)+1\bigr)+2+
                     \left\lceil s\log_2(2d+1)\right\rceil\right].
                                                               \tag{18}
\]

Since \(d,s\le N\), \(s\le h\), and \(\tau=N^{O(1)}\), this
is \(N^{O(h+1)}\). Passing to a primitive minimal-polynomial factor
preserves that asymptotic bound. For example, Cauchy's bound controls all
roots of an annihilator, and expanding the corresponding factor bounds
its coefficient bit lengths by a polynomial in its degree and the original
coefficient bit length. The factor's leading coefficient divides the
annihilator's primitive leading coefficient by Gauss's lemma.

A product of the separate coordinate degrees is unnecessary. For every
\(c\in\mathbb Q^n\), use \(B_c=\sum_jc_j B_j\), clearing its
rational denominators along with `A`. The multiplier degree stays at most
\(2d\), and the ordered limits give \(c^Tx^*\). The degree bound
`L` is independent of the size of `c`. Thus every rational linear
combination of the coordinates has algebraic degree at most `L`.
The coordinates are algebraic, and the primitive element theorem in
characteristic zero supplies such a linear combination generating their
joint field. Its degree is at most `L`. Finally, \(\theta=q_0(x^*)\)
belongs to this same field because the objective has rational coefficients.
This proves (1), using \(s\le\min(h,d)\) and \(d\le n\).

When `h=0`, this yields degree one: the canonical optimizer is rational.
When `n=0`, the empty coordinate field and the objective value are rational,
and the degree bound is also one.

## 7. Consequences and limits

The point bound gives an effective radius
\(2^{N^{O(h+1)}}\) containing the canonical optimizer. Combined with the
rational optimal-face intersection oracle and algebraic recognition in
[the exact recovery note](exact-convex-optimizer-recovery.md), it improves
the degree and height bounds used to recover that one fixed optimizer.
This argument is existential about the active restriction and support;
the recovery algorithm need not discover them.

For a mixed-integer problem with rational data, fix any optimal integer
assignment \(z^*\) whose continuous fiber has a finite attained convex
quadratic optimum. If its native continuous Hessian span is `h`, the
minimum-norm continuous optimizer in that fiber, together with the global
optimal value, lies in a field of degree at most (1), independently of the
number of integer variables. The height bound uses the substituted input
length \(N(z^*)\); without a bound on the bit length of \(z^*\), it is
not a uniform height bound in the original input. Existence and the search
for an optimal integer assignment are separate issues, addressed under
joint convexity and fixed integer dimension in
[the mixed-integer attainment and optimization note](mixed-integer-attainment-frontier.md).

Equation (1) bounds a common field's degree; it does not by itself construct
a primitive generator and all coordinate expressions in that field. Nor
does it automatically supply an efficient independently checkable
multivariate certificate. Coordinatewise recovery is established by the
linked algorithm, whereas a complete common-field certificate construction
requires further work. The result does not prove a practical numerical
speedup or sharpness of the displayed degree bound.

Minimum-norm selection by strictly convex regularization, nested
perturbations, and primitive elements are established tools. The
multiplication-matrix perturbation mechanism is also classical; the
[elimination note's source comparison](explicit-span-separation.md#4-prior-results-contribution-and-limitations)
examines Canny's generalized characteristic polynomials and Nie--Ranestad's
algebraic degrees. The candidate contribution here is the combined
Hessian-span bound for the whole optimizer field without adjoining the
objective value or assuming strict feasibility. It is a refinement of the
current research package, not an independent claim of priority for any
individual ingredient. A broader novelty assessment still requires the
package's literature comparisons.

There is also close prior machinery in Grigoriev--Pasechnik,
[*Polynomial-time computing over quadratic maps I*, Theorem 1.10 and
Section 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0403008v3). Theorem 1.10 bounds degrees
and integer coefficient sizes for representatives of iterated limits of
rational images over ordered infinitesimals. Section 2 uses a parametrized
special Groebner basis, a finite staircase quotient, and multiplication
tables; Theorem 2.1 bounds representations of finite limit images by the
quotient dimension. We inspected the primary text on printed pages 5--8.
Thus neither the transfer of algebraic bounds through ordered limits nor
the special quotient construction is new here. Theorem 1.5 also states
optimization over algebraic sets defined through few quadratic maps,
with its proof deferred to a continuation. These results are stronger
algorithmic machinery in their own setting. The present proof supplies a
direct convex KKT route with an explicit bound and native Hessian-span
compression; it does not establish that this refinement could not also be
derived from that earlier machinery.
