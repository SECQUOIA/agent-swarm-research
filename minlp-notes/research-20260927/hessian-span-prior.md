# Prior work for Hessian-span value bounds and exact feasibility

Date: 2026-09-27. Status: independent literature audit, with a limited novelty
conclusion. This note compares [the candidate value theorem](hessian-span-reduction.md)
and its possible mixed-integer extension with primary sources. It is not a
second proof of the candidate theorem.

The inspected sources do not state the candidate bound in terms of the linear
span of the native Hessian **matrices**. The relevant claimed advance is a
uniform degree-and-coefficient-height bound with exponent depending on that
span dimension, despite arbitrarily many affine and quadratic rows and without
Slater or genericity assumptions. An unsuccessful search is not evidence of
priority. Fixed-count quadratic algebraic algorithms and existing weak convex
optimization algorithms remain essential prior work.

## What the parameter does and does not measure

Write each native constraint as

\[
q_i(x)=\tfrac12x^TQ_ix+a_i^Tx+c_i,\qquad Q_i\succeq0,
\quad h=\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}\{Q_1,\ldots,Q_m\}.
\]

The parameter is not the dimension of the subspace generated by the ranges of
the matrices, not their aggregate rank, not the number of quadratic rows, and
not the dimension of the span of the complete polynomials. The affine terms
are unrestricted. All dimensions in the candidate input may grow except the
parameter being fixed in a complexity corollary.

This distinction is material. One positive-definite matrix already has range
dimension equal to the ambient dimension while its matrix span has dimension
one. Conversely, a three-dimensional matrix span can require arbitrarily
many common PSD generators. The example now used in the candidate note is
\(Q_r(t)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I_r&tI_r\\tI_r&t^2I_r\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
Its ranges for distinct parameters intersect only at zero. A PSD generator
used with positive weight must have its range inside that of its target,
so a common family generating \(m\) distinct inputs needs at least \(m\)
members. This remains true for generators outside the matrix span.
The [package assessment](hessian-package-assessment.md) supplies the proof.
The earlier diagonal moment-curve example established only the weaker
input-cone extreme-ray distinction and admits three external PSD generators.
Thus a bounded-generator epigraph lift explains the cases h<=2, but does
not explain the whole h=3 class.

## General exact convex quadratic feasibility is not routine weak optimization

A direct reduction makes the precision issue concrete. Given nonnegative
integers a_1,...,a_n and integer b, consider

\[
0\le x_i\le a_i+1,\qquad x_i^2\le a_i\ (i=1,\ldots,n),
\qquad \sum_i x_i\ge b.
\]

This is an explicitly bounded rational convex quadratic system. It is feasible
if and only if

\[
\sum_i\sqrt{a_i}\ge b.
\]

Indeed, each feasible coordinate is at most sqrt(a_i); in the reverse direction
choose x_i=sqrt(a_i). Its Hessians are the n independent coordinate matrices,
so h=n. This elementary reduction shows why a uniform exact polynomial-time
algorithm for unrestricted h would solve the threshold version of Square-Root
Sum. It is compatible with a polynomial-time algorithm for every fixed h.

[Eisenbrand, Haeberle and Singer, *An Improved Bound on Sums of Square Roots via
the Subspace Theorem*, SoCG 2024](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol293-socg2024/LIPIcs.SoCG.2024.54/LIPIcs.SoCG.2024.54.pdf),
pp. 54:1–54:2 and Section 4, discuss unresolved Turing complexity and improve
separation bounds in a fixed-radicand regime. Their constant depends on the
radicands and is ineffective; the result does not provide a uniform
polynomial-precision algorithm for arbitrary binary input. Their signed-sum
formulation is broader than the direct threshold reduction above. No new
classification of unrestricted convex QCQP in P or NP is asserted here.

There is also an elementary gap obstruction with bounded variables. On
[0,1]^n impose x_1=1/2, x_{i+1}>=x_i^2, and x_n<=0. The system is infeasible,
but the point x_i=2^{-2^{i-1}} violates only the final row, by
2^{-2^{n-1}}. Compactness makes the minimum maximum violation positive, while
this example bounds it above by that doubly exponentially small number. Thus
arbitrary-dimensional bounded convex quadratic systems do not admit a general
polynomial-bit positive-violation gap. This is a direct calculation, not a
novel lower-bound claim; repeated-squaring constructions are classical.

The parameter h excludes this chain when fixed: its n-1 nonzero native
Hessians are independent.

## Exact rational witnesses require a separate claim

[Bienstock, Del Pia and Hildebrand, *Complexity, Exactness, and Rationality in
Polynomial Optimization*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08347), Introduction and
Section 6, explicitly separate exact feasibility, rational witnesses, and
near-feasible witnesses. Their discussion records the polynomial-size rational
witness theorem for **one** quadratic inequality plus affine rows, and the
fixed-count quadratic feasibility precedents. Example 6.1 gives an unbounded
convex repeated-squaring system requiring large coordinates. Example 6.3 gives
a rational SOCP with no rational feasible point. The latter is not, by itself,
a counterexample for systems presented solely by globally convex quadratic
polynomials: an SOC inequality with a variable right-hand side has an
indefinite squared polynomial representation. Example 6.2 also must not be
cited as a bounded globally convex quadratic example without checking its
individual Hessians.

The local full text examined was
[literature/papers/bienstock2023-complexity-exactness-and-rationality-in/fulltext.md](../literature/papers/bienstock2023-complexity-exactness-and-rationality-in/fulltext.md).
This source audit alone did not settle rational-point existence in the full
class. Subsequent work in this batch supplied an independently checked
[three-quadratic singleton](hessian-span-reduction.md): on `[0,2]^2`, the
convex inequalities `x^2-y<=0`, `y^2-2x<=0`, and
`(x-y)^2-y-2x+4<=0` have the unique feasible point
`(cuberoot(2),cuberoot(4))`. Its Hessians are PSD and span dimension three.
Thus an exact rational feasible witness need not exist even in this fixed-span
class. The [independent review](hessian-span-review.md) checks the positive
quadratic-combination certificate. This example is a result of the current
batch, not an attribution to the inspected source.

An exactly feasible rational witness is unnecessary for the **decision**
consequence. A polynomial-size rational point with sufficiently small
residual, together with a proved positive-gap threshold, can certify the
correct yes/no answer without itself being exactly feasible.

Optimization witnesses are a different matter even in the simplest class:
minimizing x over x^2<=2 has irrational minimizer -sqrt(2), although the
feasible set contains many rational points.

## Algebraic degree: Nie–Ranestad is strong nearby precedent

[Nie and Ranestad, *Algebraic Degree of Polynomial Optimization*, SIAM Journal
on Optimization 20 (2009), 485–502](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.1233), Theorem 2.2,
Corollary 2.5, and Section 3.2 give degree formulas from complex KKT critical
points. With m active quadratic rows in n variables the generic QCQP formula
is 2^m binomial(n,m). The same bound applies to specified nongeneric cases
when the KKT system remains zero-dimensional. Affine active equations can be
eliminated before making this comparison. The result is about algebraic
degree; it is not the candidate's uniform coefficient-height statement and
its hypotheses do not cover all degeneracies used in the candidate note.

The candidate's reduction to a bounded number of quadratic degrees of freedom
is consistent with this classical n^{O(m)} degree scale. Therefore the mere
fact that fixed-count QCQP has polynomial algebraic degree is not new. The
proposed addition is the active affine reduction that makes the count depend
on h even when m is unbounded, combined with an effective height estimate and
regularization that allows nonisolated KKT systems and failure of Slater.

Author/arXiv copies were downloaded to
[sources-hessian-span-prior/nie-ranestad-2009.pdf](sources-hessian-span-prior/nie-ranestad-2009.pdf)
and converted to text for theorem inspection. No assertion about a sharp
h-dependent degree formula is supported by the present candidate proof.

## Few-quadratic algorithms: parameter and affine-row boundaries

[Grigoriev and Pasechnik, *Polynomial-time computing over quadratic maps I:
sampling in real algebraic sets*](https://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~grigorev/pub/quadric_cc.pdf)
and [Kamminga and Rudolph, arXiv:2411.03096v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03096v2)
are the strongest directly relevant few-quadratic precedents already inspected
in [the separate source audit](few-quadratic-value-bound-evidence.md). This note
does not repeat that audit or independently verify every cited elimination
bound. Those results concern a bounded number of quadratic-map components;
affine rows cannot silently be counted as free components in their general
nonconvex algorithms. The candidate instead exploits convexity and the active
system to establish an encoding bound, without enumerating all affine active
sets algorithmically.

A direct nonconvex polynomial-time feasibility claim with arbitrary affine
rows would be false already for standard quadratic optimization sublevel
sets. This is another reason to distinguish an algebraic **value encoding**
claim from a polynomial-time **discovery** claim.

## Mixed-integer comparison: Del Pia gives an exact one-row result

[Del Pia, *Convex quadratic sets and the complexity of mixed integer convex
quadratic programming*, arXiv:2311.00099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00099),
Theorems 2–3 and Section 4.1, handles convex quadratic sets consisting of a
polyhedron plus one convex quadratic inequality. Its exact MICQP theorem is
fixed parameter tractable in the number of integer variables. Here MICQP
means a convex quadratic **objective over linear constraints**; the paper's
notion of accurate solution includes exact feasibility, boundedness, optimal
value, and an optimizer. Equivalently, its decision machinery handles one
convex quadratic sublevel constraint. The paper develops rational affine
reductions and rounding of projections; it is not a theorem for arbitrarily
many independently curved quadratic constraints.

The downloaded current text is
[sources-hessian-span-prior/delpia-2025.pdf](sources-hessian-span-prior/delpia-2025.pdf).
Its Theorem 3 is a stronger parameterized complexity statement than a generic
N^{O(k+h)} result in the special one-row case: FPT fixes the polynomial input
exponent independently of k. A new result polynomial for fixed k and h should
be called that, or XP in the combined parameters, unless a bound of the form
f(k,h)N^{O(1)} is actually proved. It should not be presented as improving
Del Pia's running time on the one-row class.

[Khachiyan and Porkolab, *Integer Optimization on Convex Semialgebraic Sets*,
Discrete & Computational Geometry 23 (2000), 207–224](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Khachiyan/00230207.pdf),
Theorem 1.2, is a broader exact structural precedent. Its polynomial-time
specialization fixes the total number of free **and quantified** variables.
Thus describing a projection onto k integer coordinates with n existential
continuous variables does not by itself put arbitrary n in that theorem's
fixed-dimensional polynomial case. It does not subsume fixed k and fixed h
with an unbounded number of continuous variables by that direct argument.

## Weak mixed convex optimization as an ingredient

[Basu, *Complexity of optimizing over the integers*, arXiv:2110.06172v6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.06172v6),
Theorem 5.8, supplies an approximation algorithm with a separation oracle for
a bounded convex domain and a first-order oracle for a convex objective. Its
hypothesis requires a full-dimensional ball of known radius centered at a
point in the same integer fiber as an optimum. The Lipschitz bound is in the
full joint space. The dimensional dependence is
2^{O(k log(k+n))}, polynomial for fixed integer dimension k, with precision
entering logarithmically. The discussion following Theorem 5.7 addresses
finite-precision implementations; Remark 5.12 distinguishes this dependence
from a stronger FPT target.

A plausible application here moves every original row into the convex
maximum-violation objective and optimizes it over a product box. The new
optimization domain can then have the required radius regardless of whether
the original feasible set is thin or empty. For integer coordinates, a box
with endpoints half a unit outside the allowed integer interval has the same
integer points and a uniform interior margin in each eligible fiber.
Continuous coordinates can be affinely normalized after fixed coordinates
are eliminated. The candidate value theorem would supply a uniform
integer-slice positive-gap threshold; applying weak optimization below this
threshold would then decide exact original feasibility.

This route is different from assuming that a continuous yes/no feasibility
oracle automatically yields a suitable separation oracle for a projected
convex set. The row-to-objective reduction, uniformity of the threshold over
integer assignments, the full joint convexity assumption, coordinate and
Lipschitz bit bounds, and the rational oracle implementation each still need
explicit verification. A separate independent review is handling that
proposed extension. No mixed-integer theorem is claimed proved by this
literature note.

## Search scope, conclusions, and verification limits

Queries covered exact convex quadratic feasibility; rational and irrational
QCQP/SOCP witnesses; Hessian span and linearly dependent Hessians; independent
quadratic forms; quadratic maps with affine rows; algebraic degree; and fixed
integer dimension convex optimization. Search terms involving Hessian spans
also returned range-span dimension-reduction work. Those are different
parameters and do not establish an overlap.

The strongest comparisons found are:

- Few-quadratic algebraic methods already give the underlying fixed-count
  degree/height phenomenon.
- Nie–Ranestad gives precise generic algebraic degree and a nongeneric
  zero-dimensional upper bound.
- Del Pia gives exact FPT mixed-integer algorithms for one convex quadratic
  sublevel row, including substantial thin-set and projection analysis.
- Khachiyan–Porkolab gives exact convex semialgebraic integer optimization at
  fixed total logical dimension.
- Basu gives weak mixed convex optimization under explicit geometric and
  oracle assumptions; it can be an ingredient, not a novelty claim.

No equivalent bounded rational convex QCQP theorem parameterized by the
matrix-span dimension h was identified. This conclusion remains qualified:
the search was not exhaustive, and an equivalent result may appear under a
different formulation or follow from an older theorem more directly than the
candidate proof currently recognizes.

Targeted work performed: read the named local literature note and full texts;
downloaded the two named arXiv PDFs; ran `pdftotext -layout` on those PDFs;
inspected exact theorem statements and definitions with `rg` and `sed`;
and checked the elementary Square-Root Sum reduction and bounded squaring
chain symbolically. No repository-wide verification, CI inspection, Lean,
or numerical solver experiment was run. Source reading verifies the stated
assumption comparisons; it does not establish publication priority or prove
the candidate's new claims.
