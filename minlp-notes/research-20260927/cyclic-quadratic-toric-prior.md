# Cyclic quadratic systems: binomial and sandpile prior audit

Date: 2026-09-28. Scope: the proposed cyclic quadrics, their lattice degree,
real solutions, and rational coefficient choice. This is a targeted literature
audit, not a claim of novelty.

The normalized system is exactly a directed sandpile firing system. Its complex
root count, finite character-group orbit, and lattice determinant belong to
established binomial and sandpile theory. The proposed rational coefficients
turn that finite orbit into one irreducible rational algebraic point. The
additional convex exposure must be assessed separately: none of the primary
sources checked below states the combined high-degree singleton and exposing
quadratic construction.

## Exact identification

Take \(m=n+1\geq3\), \(x_0=1\), and cyclic indices. Put

\[
d=\frac{2^m-(-1)^m}{3},\qquad
s_i=\frac{(-2)^i-1}{3},\qquad
\alpha^d=2,\quad\alpha>0,\qquad p_i=\alpha^{s_i}.
\]

The proposed equations are

\[
q_i=x_i^2-2^{k_i}x_{i+1}x_{i+2}=0,
\qquad
k_{m-2}=(-1)^m,\quad k_{m-1}=-(-1)^m,
\]

with all other \(k_i=0\). The substitution \(x_i=p_i y_i\) gives

\[
p_i^{-2}q_i=y_i^2-y_{i+1}y_{i+2}.
\]

These are the firing binomials of the strongly connected Eulerian directed
circulant with arcs \(i\to i+1,i+2\). Its row Laplacian is

\[
B=2I-P-P^2,
\]

where \(P\) shifts a row to its successor. Thus the exponent vectors are the
rows of \(B\), or the columns of \(B^\mathsf T\). State this transpose convention
when applying sources.

One should call this a **binomial system** or a **translated lattice system**.
For \(d>1\), its complex zero set contains several points, so its ideal over
the algebraic closure is not a prime toric ideal. Also distinguish the ideal
generated by the homogeneous firing binomials from its saturation; equality is
not automatic.

## Primary sources and exact usable results

**Eisenbud and Sturmfels, “Binomial Ideals,” Duke Mathematical Journal 84
(1996), 1–45.** Theorem 2.1(a)–(b), printed p. 11, treats arbitrary fields and
proper ideals in a Laurent polynomial ring. It identifies each such binomial
ideal with a partial character \(\rho:L\to k^\times\). An integer basis change
reduces its equations to powers \(z_j^{a_j}=c_j\). The requirement that the
field be algebraically closed starts in part (c), so the partial-character
description itself applies over \(\mathbb Q\). This is the direct framework for
the rational coefficients \(2^{k_i}\), rather than merely for coefficient-one
equations. [Author-hosted article](https://eisenbud.github.io/papers/pdfs/1996-002.pdf).

**Perkinson, Perlman and Wilmes, “Primer for the algebraic geometry of
sandpiles” (2013; arXiv version 2011).** Their sandpile graph is a finite weighted
directed graph with an accessible sink. Theorem 3.1(2) describes saturation of
the binomials from lattice generators. Definition 4.1 and Proposition 4.2 give
the reduced toppling ideal. Theorem 6.5 and Corollary 6.6 identify its zeros over
\(\mathbb C\) with a faithful orbit of the character group of the sandpile group.
The discussion immediately before Theorem 6.5 explicitly allows any initial
point with nonzero coordinates, then rescales it to the all-ones vector.
Proposition 4.8 and Corollary 6.10 identify full-lattice homogenization when the
sink column lies in the integer span of the others; that condition holds here
because the columns sum to zero. These results cover the orbit description
after diagonal rescaling. [Author-hosted article](https://people.reed.edu/~davidp/homepage/publications/primer.pdf),
[arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.6163).

**O’Carroll, Planas-Vilanova and Villarreal, “Degree and Algebraic Properties of
Lattice and Matrix Ideals,” SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics 28 (2014),
394–427.** Use their directed-graph statements rather than Proposition 7.1,
which assumes a connected weighted simple undirected graph. Proposition 7.6
applies to a generalized critical binomial matrix with strongly connected
underlying digraph: its row-binomial ideal meets each coordinate hyperplane
only at the origin. Proposition 5.7, for a positively graded matrix ideal with
that property, identifies its saturation with its unmixed part. Corollary 5.9
gives its degree from lattice torsion; for the standard grading this is the gcd
of maximal-rank minors. Here all cofactors equal \(d\), so the degree is \(d\).
These results prevent conflating the homogeneous matrix ideal with the
saturated lattice ideal. [Primary preprint, v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.5382),
[published article](https://doi.org/10.1137/130922094).

**Rojas, “Counting Real Roots in Polynomial-Time for Systems Supported on
Circuits” (2021 conference manuscript), §2.2.** Proposition 2.7 gives the
Smith-form reduction for square Laurent binomial systems with nonzero real
constants and integer exponents. Proposition 2.8 counts real torus roots using
the exponent matrix rank modulo two. Consequently, an odd nonsingular
determinant yields exactly one real torus solution; positive constants give
one positive solution. The author explicitly describes these binomial facts as
folklore. Here \(d\) is odd. [Primary conference PDF](https://puremath.no/Contributed%20MEGA/papers/MEGA_2021_paper_50.pdf).

**Yong, Zhang and Golin, “The number of spanning trees in a class of double
fixed-step loop networks.”** Lemma 1, equation (1), gives the product
\(\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(2-\zeta^{pj}-\zeta^{qj})\), counting rooted directed spanning
trees over all roots. Divide by \(m\) for one root in this vertex-transitive
graph. For \(p=1,q=2\), factoring \(2-z-z^2=(1-z)(2+z)\) gives the cofactor
\(d\). The introduction, PDF p. 2, explicitly attributes the
closed formula for this exact graph to Lonc, Parol and Wojciechowski,
“On the number of spanning trees in directed circulant graphs,” Networks 37
(2001), 129–133. This attribution was verified in the Yong–Zhang–Golin paper;
the 2001 article was not independently read in this audit.
[Author-hosted article](https://www.cse.ust.hk/~golin/pubs/Networks_Double_Loop.pdf).

## What the rational coefficients add

The following are direct deductions for the proposed construction, not claims
that the cited papers state this particular coefficient choice.

Let \(L\subset\mathbb Z^n\) be the row lattice after deleting coordinate zero.
The coefficients define the partial character

\[
\rho(b_i)=2^{k_i}=p^{b_i}.
\]

Its values are rational, although the extension \(p\in(\mathbb R_{>0})^n\)
used to trivialize it is algebraic. Over \(\mathbb C\), the zero set is the
translate \(p\cdot\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb Z^n/L,\mathbb C^\times)\).

The passage from torus roots to all affine roots needs justification.
In the quotient by the dehomogenized equations, \(x_1x_2=1\), and subsequent
equations make every coordinate a unit. Thus dehomogenization removes the
saturation issue, including any homogeneous component supported at the origin.

The coordinate ring over \(\mathbb Q\) is

\[
\mathbb Q[t,t^{-1}]/(t^d-2),\qquad x_i=t^{s_i}.
\]

For the inverse map, set \(t=x_1^{-1}\), which exists in the quotient.
The equations indexed \(0,\ldots,m-3\) give \(x_i=t^{s_i}\) successively.
The equation indexed \(m-2\) then gives
\(t^{(-1)^m d}=2^{(-1)^m}\), equivalently \(t^d=2\).
Conversely, those powers of any invertible \(t\) with \(t^d=2\) satisfy all
the cyclic equations, including the final one. The two maps are inverse.

Since \(s_1=-1\), the coordinates recover \(t\). Eisenstein at 2 makes this a
field of degree \(d\). This arithmetic argument is necessary: a complex root
count of \(d\) alone would not prove the degree of a selected real root. With
all coefficients equal to one, the same lattice has a rational real root.
The proposed twist instead makes all \(d\) complex solutions conjugate over
\(\mathbb Q\). The cyclicity of the finite quotient is also visible from the
same recurrence; no separately verified source for its Smith form was found.

## Convex exposure and defensible attribution

The specific additional identity is

\[
\sum_i p_i^{-2}q_i(x)
=\sum_i(y_i^2-y_{i+1}y_{i+2})
=\frac12\sum_i(y_i-y_{i+1})^2.
\]

After fixing \(y_0=1\), this has positive definite quadratic part and vanishes
only at the all-ones vector. Its ingredients are elementary, but the cited
binomial, sandpile, and tree-count papers do not state their use as an exposing
positive combination for this rational quadratic system. This audit found no
primary source stating the exact rational twist together with this exposure.
That limited search result is not evidence sufficient to claim novelty.
The companion [grounded-cycle audit](cyclic-grounded-energy-prior.md) examines
the graph-energy identity and the exact Jacobsthal tree-count source.

The safe presentation is an explicit construction based on established
binomial-lattice and directed-sandpile structures, with the rational
irreducibility argument and the exposing identity proved directly. Do not
present the Jacobsthal degree, the torus root count, or the translated finite
orbit as new.
