## Construction and field obstruction

The baseline has exactly one exposing quadratic square and three
residual squares per quintic gate. The boxes \([1,2]\) satisfy the
realization theorem's input condition. Fixing every gate except the
last leaves only four positive squares. The exposing quadratic's
positive definite homogeneous matrix ensures a nonzero \(z^2\)
coefficient in this slice.

The root independently checked that the five last-gate relations
\[
 x^2-y,\quad xy-z,\quad y^2-xz,\quad yz-b,\quad z^2-bx
\]
span the quadratic vanishing space whenever \(T^5-b\) is irreducible
over the real base field. Their fifteen products are independent:
their leading forms already have independent products among the
fifteen ternary quartic monomials. The root supplied this simpler
leading-form argument during development, so it is a contribution,
not a fresh noncontributor review. The author checked its integer
determinant independently.

Write the restricted exposing factor as \(g=\sum c_iq_i\) in this
basis. Since \(c_5>0\), the vector
\((0,0,c_5,0,-c_3)\) kills all four positive factors and has negative
value on the subtracted square. Independence of products makes the
Gram matrix unique on this vanishing space. The fresh reviewer
supplied an equivalent explicit negative vector.

The root independently reconstructed the real-field lemma before
reading the final proof. If \(a^D=2\), with \(D=5^k\), the constant
term of the minimal polynomial over any \(E\subseteq\mathbb R\)
shows \(a^r\in E\), where \(r=[E(a):E]\). The root-of-unity factor
must be one because its order is odd and it is real. Bézout's
identity then shows \(r=\gcd(r,D)\); in particular \(r\mid D\).
If \(a\notin E\), adjoining \(a^5\) leaves degree exactly five.
All earlier gate coordinates lie in that enlarged field. This
justifies the specialization even when \(E\) is transcendental.

The separate PSD Gram argument is necessary. Positive semidefiniteness
over a real field does not by itself give square factors in that field.
After specialization, \(Qm(p)=0\) puts the rows of \(Q\) in the
five-dimensional vanishing space. If \(B\) is its coefficient matrix
and \(C\) is any left inverse over the same field, then
\[
 Q=B(CQC^{\mathsf T})B^{\mathsf T},\qquad CQC^{\mathsf T}\succeq0.
\]
This contradicts the unique indefinite matrix just described.

Conversely, the rational Hessian SOS can be Taylor-integrated at
the zero over \(\mathbb Q(a)\). The identity
\[
 \int_0^1(1-t)(U+tV)^2\,dt
 =\tfrac12(U+V/3)^2+(V/6)^2
\]
uses only positive rational weights, each a sum of rational squares.
It introduces no additional field extension. Thus the same field
is necessary and sufficient for both polynomial SOS and PSD Grams.

## Quantitative convexity

The root checked the scaling independently. If the rational baseline
Hessian Gram is \(M\succ0\) of size \(h=N+N^2\), then
\[
 \mu=\det(M)/\operatorname{tr}(M)^{h-1}
 \le\lambda_{\min}(M).
\]
For \(R=y_k^2-x_kz_k=X^{\mathsf T}TX\), one has
\(\|T\|=1\) and \(\|T\|_F^2=3/2\). The canonical Hessian Gram
of \(R^2\) has norm at most \(8(3/2)+4=16\).
Consequently \(\lambda=\lceil17/\mu\rceil\) makes the perturbed
Gram at least the identity. The determinant and trace have polynomial
bit length in this polynomial-size matrix, so this scaling does not
expand the degree-\(5^k\) field or require exponential coefficient
length. It is a polynomial-time construction in \(k\).

The Hessian bound and stationarity establish global nonnegativity
and the unique zero. The argument does not infer nonnegativity from
the signed-square formula alone.

## An individual coefficient must also have large degree

The following strengthening was proposed by the root and reconstructed
independently by the fresh reviewer. It concerns finite lists of
algebraic coefficients; a transcendental coefficient has no finite
minimal-polynomial encoding.

**Lemma.** If a field generated by finitely many algebraic numbers
\(c_1,\ldots,c_s\) contains \(a=2^{1/5^k}\), then at least one
\(c_i\) has degree at least \(5^k\) over \(\mathbb Q\).

**Proof.** Put \(D=5^k\), let \(\zeta\) be a primitive \(D\)-th root
of unity, and write
\[
 Z=\mathbb Q(\zeta),\qquad Z^+=\mathbb Q(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}).
\]
The field \(Z^+\) is totally real. The real-field argument above
says that the minimal polynomial of \(a\) over \(Z^+\) is
\(T^r-a^r\) for some \(r\mid D\). If \(r<D\), the element
\(a^r\in Z^+\) has rational minimal polynomial
\(T^{D/r}-2\), by Eisenstein. That polynomial has nonreal roots,
contradicting total reality. Hence \([Z^+(a):Z^+]=D\).
The extension \(Z/Z^+\) has degree two and is nonreal, whereas
\(Z^+(a)\) is real. Thus
\([Z(a):Z^+(a)]=2\) and \([Z(a):Z]=D\).
The splitting field \(S=Z(a)\) consequently has an automorphism
\(a\mapsto\zeta a\), fixing \(Z\), of order \(D\).

Let \(T\) be the compositum of the normal closures of the fields
\(\mathbb Q(c_i)\). It is a finite Galois extension containing \(a\),
so it contains every conjugate of \(a\) and hence \(S\).
Restriction gives a surjection
\(\operatorname{Gal}(T/\mathbb Q)\to\operatorname{Gal}(S/\mathbb Q)\).
On the other hand the faithful action on the conjugates of all
\(c_i\) embeds \(\operatorname{Gal}(T/\mathbb Q)\) into
\(\prod_i\mathfrak S_{d_i}\), where \(d_i=[\mathbb Q(c_i):\mathbb Q]\).
If every \(d_i<D\), every element of this product has order whose
5-primary part is at most \(5^{k-1}\): a permutation on fewer than
\(5^k\) letters has no cycle with length divisible by \(5^k\).
An element mapping to the order-\(5^k\) automorphism of \(S\)
must have order divisible by \(5^k\), a contradiction. \(\square\)

The fresh reviewer supplied a different check of the order-\(D\)
automorphism using Eisenstein at an unramified prime above two in
the cyclotomic field. The proof above avoids that ramification input.
The main SOS theorem therefore forces an individual entry of every
algebraic Gram certificate, and an individual coefficient of every
algebraic polynomial SOS certificate, to have degree at least \(5^k\).
This strengthens the obstruction to a single common-field encoding.
It also applies to separate dense coordinate minimal polynomials.
It gives no lower bound for sparse polynomials, arithmetic circuits,
radical expressions, or tower encodings.
