# A quantitative terminal Slater margin over one number field

Date: 2026-09-27. Status: independent audit of the terminal-margin step;
the argument below supports that step. It does not audit the preceding
facial reductions or claim that their coefficients have the required height.

## Statement

Let a nonempty bounded polyhedron \(P\subseteq\mathbb R^n\) and convex
quadratics \(q_1,\ldots,q_m\), with \(m\ge1\), have coefficients in a
fixed real embedding of a number field \(K\). Let \(s\ge2\) bound the
number of variables, rows, and explicitly listed scalar coefficients. Suppose

\[
 [K:\mathbb Q]\le D,\qquad h_{\rm W}(a)\le B
 \quad\text{for every coefficient }a,
\]

where \(h_{\rm W}\) is absolute logarithmic Weil height and \(B\ge1\).
Let the span of the quadratic Hessians have dimension at most \(h\).
The span may be taken over \(K\) or over \(\mathbb R\): these dimensions
agree for matrices over \(K\). Suppose there is an \(x\in P\) with
every \(q_i(x)<0\).

If only the Hessian span after restriction to \(\operatorname{aff}P\)
is bounded by \(h\), take a free-coordinate affine chart first. The affine
hull is obtained from rows of the description of \(P\) that are equalities
throughout \(P\). Linear algebra over \(K\) gives a chart with coefficients
of polynomially bounded height. Restriction preserves positive
semidefiniteness and boundedness, so the same conclusion applies.

There are \(y\in P\) and \(\sigma>0\) such that

\[
 q_i(y)\le-\sigma\quad(1\le i\le m),\qquad
 \log_2(1/\sigma)\le (s+D+B)^{O(h+1)}.             \tag{1}
\]

When the best margin exceeds one, take \(\sigma=1\). Thus, in the
application \(s\le N^{\operatorname{poly}(h)}\),

\[
 D\le N^{O(h+1)},\qquad B\le N^{\operatorname{poly}(h)}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sigma\ge2^{-N^{\operatorname{poly}(h)}}.
\]

This is an existence and encoding statement. No explicit field presentation
is required as input to the statement. The proof supplies a presentation of
controlled size existentially. If no quadratic rows remain, no terminal
Slater margin is needed; one can set the otherwise unused \(\sigma=1\).

## A controlled common-field presentation

Replace \(K\) by the field generated by the finitely many input coefficients,
which only decreases its degree. Write these generators as

\[
 a_1,\ldots,a_v,\qquad v\le s.
\]

There is an integer \(t\) of magnitude at most
\(1+\binom D2(v-1)\) such that

\[
 \alpha=\sum_{j=1}^v t^{j-1}a_j
\]

generates this field. Indeed, for any pair of distinct embeddings of \(K\)
into its normal closure, equality of their images of this expression is a
nonzero polynomial equation in \(t\) of degree at most \(v-1\). There
are at most \(\binom D2\) pairs, so at least one integer in a set with
more than \(\binom D2(v-1)\) elements avoids all collisions. The
height inequalities for sums and products give

\[
 H:=h_{\rm W}(\alpha)
 \le vB+O(v^2\log(D+v)).                           \tag{2}
\]

The primitive integer minimal polynomial \(p_\alpha\) has degree
\(d\le D\) and coefficient bit lengths polynomial in
\(D+H\). This follows directly from
\(\log M(p_\alpha)=d h_{\rm W}(\alpha)\), where \(M\) is Mahler
measure, and the elementary coefficient bound
\(\|p_\alpha\|_\infty\le2^d M(p_\alpha)\).

Every \(\beta\in K\) of height at most \(B'\) has a unique expression

\[
 \beta=\sum_{r=0}^{d-1}c_r\alpha^r,\qquad c_r\in\mathbb Q,          \tag{3}
\]

whose rational coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in
\(D+H+B'\). To check this without a hidden normal-closure factor,
apply all \(d\) embeddings to (3). Its coefficient matrix is the
Vandermonde matrix with entries \(\alpha_j^r\), where
\(\alpha_j\) are the distinct conjugates of \(\alpha\). Cramer's
rule expresses each \(c_r\) as a quotient of two determinants.
For any \(d\times d\) matrix \(A\) of algebraic numbers,

\[
 h_{\rm W}(\det A)
 \le d\sum_{i,j}h_{\rm W}(A_{ij})+\log(d!).         \tag{4}
\]

This follows place by place from the determinant polynomial and the
product formula. Heights are absolute, so computing these determinants
in a normal closure introduces no multiplier equal to that closure's
degree. Each Vandermonde entry has height at most \(dH\); replacing
one column by the conjugates of \(\beta\) adds entries of height at
most \(B'\). The resulting Cramer quotient has height polynomial in
\(D+H+B'\). Since the quotient lies in \(\mathbb Q\), this is
exactly a bound on the logarithms of its reduced numerator and denominator.

Finally, a rational interval \((l,u)\) of polynomial bit length in
\(D+H\) isolates the intended real root of \(p_\alpha\). For example,
a Cauchy bound bounds all roots, and the nonzero integer discriminant
bounds the separation of distinct roots below by
\(2^{-\operatorname{poly}(D+H)}\). A sufficiently fine dyadic interval
then isolates the desired root. Thus

\[
 p_\alpha(T)=0,\qquad l<T<u                         \tag{5}
\]

selects the intended embedding with one real variable and polynomial-size
rational data. Other conjugates need not preserve any sign or convexity
property. They are excluded by (5).

## Boundedness supplies a controlled rational box

Even if the description of \(P\) contains no coordinate box, its boundedness
supplies one with polynomial bit length in \(s+D+B\). Every vertex is
the solution of \(n\) independent active affine equations. Cramer's rule
and (4) bound each vertex coordinate's Weil height by
\(\operatorname{poly}(s+B)\). For a nonzero \(\gamma\in K\),

\[
 |\log |\gamma||\le D h_{\rm W}(\gamma)
\]

at the selected real embedding. This bounds every vertex coordinate in
absolute value by \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(s+D+B)}\). A nonempty
bounded polyhedron, including a lower-dimensional one, is the convex hull
of its vertices. Hence \(P\subseteq[-R,R]^n\) for a rational integer

\[
 R\le2^{\operatorname{poly}(s+D+B)}.                \tag{6}
\]

The same coefficient and coordinate magnitude bounds give a rational
integer \(M\) of polynomial bit length with
\(|q_i(x)|<M\) on \(P\).

## Compressed KKT elimination over the selected embedding

Set

\[
 \beta=\min_{x\in P}\max_i q_i(x)
      =\min\{t:x\in P,\ -M\le t\le M,\ q_i(x)-t\le0\ \forall i\}.
                                                               \tag{7}
\]

The feasible epigraph slice is compact, and strict feasibility gives
\(\beta<0\). Appending the coordinate \(t\) does not increase the
native Hessian span, since its new row and column are zero in every
Hessian. The objective \(t\) is convex.

Repeat the active affine restriction, whole-polynomial span reduction,
regularization, sparse multiplier selection, and adjugate elimination in
[the Hessian-span value proof](hessian-span-reduction.md). These steps
work over the embedded field \(K\). All new affine coefficients are
obtained by linear algebra over \(K\); Cramer's rule and (4) bound
their heights by \(\operatorname{poly}(s+B+\log R+\log M)\).
Use a free-coordinate affine chart, so the auxiliary ball used by that
proof also has controlled rational radius. If the chart has dimension zero,
the optimizer is already a point over \(K\) of controlled height, and
the conclusion follows directly.

Otherwise choose a native multiplier support of size at most \(h\) along
a regularization sequence. Together with the auxiliary ball multiplier,
the KKT determinant formula contains at most \(h+1\) multipliers.
The limit-value singleton formula contains quantifier blocks of sizes

\[
 1\quad\text{and}\quad h+3,
\]

with one free scalar for the value. Primal dimension enters its polynomial
degrees and coefficients, not its number of quantified variables.

Before forming determinants, encode each restricted scalar coefficient
by (3), in the same primitive element \(\alpha\). Replace \(\alpha\)
by \(T\) and put (5) in the existential block. This gives a rational
formula with block sizes \(1\) and at most \(h+4\). Its polynomial
degrees and coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in \(s+D+B\).
For the determinant expansion, the degree in \(T\) is at most the
matrix dimension times \(D-1\); the determinant coefficient bound
uses at most a factorial number of products, whose logarithm is polynomial
in the matrix dimension. Clearing the finitely many rational input
denominators preserves polynomial bit length. The number of expanded
monomials can depend on \(h\), but need not be used as a multiplier
in a bound on individual coefficient bit lengths.

Coefficient-sensitive block quantifier elimination now gives an integer
annihilator of \(\beta\) whose degree and coefficient bit lengths are

\[
 (s+D+B)^{O(h+1)}.                                 \tag{8}
\]

The theorem used here is Basu--Pollack--Roy, *Algorithms in Real Algebraic
Geometry*, Theorem 14.16. The integer coefficient conclusion is also
stated in [Basu's 2011 survey, Theorem 2.16](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011.pdf).
The theorem is applied to rational data after (5), not directly to
unencoded algebraic coefficients.

To pass from the singleton formula to an annihilator, discard identically
zero output polynomials. Some remaining polynomial must vanish at
\(\beta\); otherwise all its signs are constant on a neighborhood of
\(\beta\), contradicting the singleton property. Remove any power of
the indeterminate from that polynomial. The reciprocal Cauchy root bound
then gives

\[
 |\beta|\ge2^{-(s+D+B)^{O(h+1)}}.
\]

A minimizer of (7) and \(\sigma=\min(1,-\beta)\) prove (1).

## Audit conclusion and limits

The terminal margin has the requested bound if the preceding facial
reductions place all polyhedral coefficients in one field with the stated
degree and absolute-height bounds. Independently bounded degrees for
individual coefficients would not suffice: their compositum could have
exponential degree. A common-field degree bound is essential here.

No step transfers convexity to conjugates, introduces one variable per
coefficient, or invokes general elimination in the full primal dimension.
The proof also works when the quadratic coefficients themselves lie in
the controlled common field, so rational native quadratics are a special
case. This audit uses symbolic arguments and the cited elimination theorem;
no numerical test, Lean proof, or project-wide verification was run.
