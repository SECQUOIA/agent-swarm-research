# Rational witness size in a strictly feasible convex quartic set

Date: 2026-09-28. Status: focused primary-literature comparison with
[independent source checks](posslp-convex-quartic-prior-review.md); no
priority claim. The construction's mathematical proof has its own
[fresh independent review](strict-quartic-rational-witness-independent-review.md).

Exponential rational witness length is already known for strictly
feasible convex quadratic systems. The
[reviewed quartic construction](strict-convex-quartic-rational-witness-lower-bound.md)
adds a more restrictive setting: one globally strongly SOS-convex
quartic inequality, a supplied positive definite rational Hessian Gram,
and a compact full-dimensional feasible set in a small bounded region.
The large bit length comes from denominators, not large coordinate
magnitudes. The checked sources do not supply this combined restriction.
That search finding does not establish novelty.

## 1. Exact scope of the result

The family has a rational quartic \(G_k\) in \(N=O(k)\)
variables with polynomial total input size, including a full rational
positive definite Gram for
\(v^T\nabla^2G_k(x)v\). Its claimed properties are

\[
 \nabla^2G_k(x)\succeq I,\qquad
 K_k=\{x:G_k(x)\le0\}\text{ compact},\qquad
 \{x:G_k(x)<0\}\ne\varnothing,
\]

and every rational point in \(K_k\) has exponential bit length in
\(k\). The feasible sets lie near bounded algebraic points. These
are ordinary expanded rational polynomials at fixed degree; a tiny
parameter is generated by a short root circuit and realized by the
quartic rather than printed as a rational with exponentially many bits.

The construction uses a chain
\(\delta_i=(1+3\delta_{i-1}^2)^{1/3}-1\), followed by a
negative-square perturbation of a strongly SOS-convex singleton
realization. Localization around a cubic irrational coordinate
then forces the denominator lower bound by elementary rational
approximation. This paragraph identifies the target for comparison;
it does not verify that construction or its quantitative constants.

The following distinctions matter:

- Strict feasibility ensures rational points exist by density. It does
  not bound the binary length of the first such point.
- A polynomial bound on coordinate magnitudes is a norm bound. It is
  different from a polynomial bound on rational numerators and
  denominators.
- Exponential bit length in \(N\) and polynomial input size imply a
  superpolynomial witness-size obstruction in input length. They do not
  automatically imply a bound of \(2^{\Omega(L)}\) for total input
  length \(L\).
- A lower bound for explicitly written rational witnesses says nothing
  by itself about all algebraic, circuit, or proof-system certificates,
  or about membership of a decision problem in NP.

## 2. Old lower bound: a strictly feasible convex quadratic system

[Pataki and Touzov, *How Do Exponential Size Solutions Arise in
Semidefinite Programming?*, arXiv:2103.00041v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.00041v2),
pages 2--3, recalls Khachiyan's system

\[
 x_i\ge x_{i+1}^2\quad(1\le i<m),\qquad x_m\ge2.
 \tag{1}
\]

It forces \(x_1\ge2^{2^{m-1}}\), hence at least \(2^{m-1}\)
bits in every rational solution. Each row is a native convex quadratic
inequality, and each quadratic row is also a two-by-two PSD constraint.
The paper's later theory relates variable-growth hierarchies to dual
singularity degree. Its general theorem requires fixing trailing
coordinates and making a specified coordinate sufficiently large; it
does not say that every strictly feasible SDP has only large points.

Two elementary observations make the comparison precise. System (1)
is strictly feasible: choose \(x_m=3\), then recursively choose
\(x_i=x_{i+1}^2+1\). It is unbounded, since increasing \(x_1\)
preserves feasibility. Its compulsory large bit length arises from
large magnitudes. It therefore already refutes a general claim that
strictly feasible convex polynomial systems must have short rational
witnesses. It does not by itself establish the proposed one-row,
compact, small-norm quartic example.

The author's direct reading and an independent source check agree on
this scope. In particular, relying on the abstract's broad wording
without the hypotheses of Theorem 1 would overstate the result.

## 3. Old upper bound: exponential rational sampling suffices

[Safey El Din and Zhi, *Computing Rational Points in Convex
Semialgebraic Sets and SOS Decompositions*, arXiv:0910.2973v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.2973v1),
Proposition 2.5, PDF page 9, records the bound

\[
                   \operatorname{bits}(x_i)\le \ell D^{O(n)}
 \tag{2}
\]

for a rational point in a nonempty set given by strict polynomial
inequalities of degree at most \(D\) and coefficient bit length at
most \(\ell\). No convexity assumption is used for this strict-set
bound. It attributes the statement to Basu--Pollack--Roy (1996),
Theorem 4.1.2, proof on page 1032.
[Basu, Pollack, and Roy, *On the Combinatorial and Algebraic Complexity
of Quantifier Elimination*](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/jacm95.ps),
pages 1031--1032, treats a basic open set and supplies such a point in
each connected component. Both readers inspected that primary theorem.
The general convex rational-point theorem in Safey El Din--Zhi has
the larger bound \(\ell D^{O(n^3)}\), which is unnecessary here.

Use (2) for a conjunction of strict inequalities, or a positive Boolean
combination reduced to nonempty such pieces. An arbitrary Boolean
formula with negated strict atoms can encode equalities, so the phrase
"strict atoms" alone is insufficient to guarantee an open set. For
the present application, the single inequality \(G_k<0\) is a basic
open set and this issue does not arise. Nonemptiness must also be stated
explicitly; it appears in the proposition's preceding discussion.

After clearing denominators of \(G_k\), one has \(D=4\) and
\(\ell\) polynomial in the family parameter. Thus (2) gives a
rational strict feasible point with at most \(\operatorname{poly}(N)
2^{O(N)}\) coordinate bits. The exponential lower bound in \(N\)
matches the exponential dependence on dimension qualitatively.
This is not a claim to match the constants, polynomial factors, or
every encoding convention in the upper bound.

## 4. The closest recent convex-quartic comparison

[Slot, Steurer, and Wiedmer, *Hesse's Redemption: Efficient Convex
Polynomial Programming*, arXiv:2511.03440v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03440v1),
Section 1.3 and Table 1, distinguishes compact rational witnesses for
exact \(f\le0\) from approximate witnesses. Its convex-quartic
exact-witness entry is unknown in that version. Its principal norm bound
and approximation theorem do not give a polynomial bound on denominators
needed to satisfy an exact threshold. The paper gives irrational
minimizers and higher-degree examples without rational exact witnesses;
those are different from a strict feasible set with only long rational
witnesses.

The family shows that strict feasibility and a supplied
strong convexity certificate do not restore polynomial-size rational
exact witnesses at degree four. It complements the repository's
irrational quartic singleton examples by retaining full dimension and
rational feasibility. The dated open-entry comparison is specifically
to the inspected preprint, not an assertion about an uninspected final
proceedings version or proof of priority. The
[broader exact-decision audit](posslp-convex-quartic-prior.md) records
publication-status checks and the older SDP/SOCP hardness comparison.

This lower bound is unconditional as a statement about rational
output length, unlike an interpretation of PosSLP-hardness as evidence
against polynomial-time decision. It would not rule out a short
symbolic witness, a decision algorithm that avoids printing a point, or
efficient approximate feasibility under a specified tolerance.

## 5. Searches and verification limits

Searches combined `rational witness`, `rational point`, `bit length`,
`denominator`, `strictly feasible`, `bounded`, `compact`, `convex
polynomial`, `quartic`, `spectrahedron`, and `semidefinite`. The search
also checked general rational-point algorithms and norm bounds, because
those can be mistaken for exact rational encoding bounds.

Primary text was read for the Pataki--Touzov and Safey El Din--Zhi
statements above. A fresh reviewer independently checked those sources,
and read the cited Basu--Pollack--Roy theorem, including the open-set
hypothesis and per-component conclusion. The author then independently
read that theorem in the repository's primary PDF using
`pdftotext -f 30 -l 31 -layout literature/papers/basu1996-on-the-combinatorial-and-algebraic/original.pdf -`.
The author has not rederived
the full rational-sampling algorithm. The new quartic construction is
not proved by this literature audit.

No inspected source was found with all the proposed restrictions.
Equivalent bounded constructions, nonstandard formulations, or later
unindexed work remain possible. General spectrahedral examples with
large bit complexity would still need comparison of boundedness,
strict feasibility, the input model, and native polynomial convexity
before being claimed equivalent.
