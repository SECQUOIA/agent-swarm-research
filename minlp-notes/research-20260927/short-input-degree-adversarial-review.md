# Independent review of the short-input QCQP degree construction

Date: 2026-09-27. Scope: the original Sections 1–5 and the significance and literature
claims in [the construction note](short-input-qcqp-degree-lower-bound.md),
as read before any constructive primitive-weight extension. This reviewer
did not develop those proofs. A separate fresh subreviewer searched for
equivalent prior results. The review found no mathematical correction
needed in the version examined. This assessment is evidence from a proof
audit, not a formal verification or a proof of novelty.

## 1. Arithmetic and optimization audit

The one-block problem is strictly convex, has a compact ball constraint,
and satisfies Slater's condition at zero. The unconstrained minimizer is
the constant vector with entry (K), whose squared norm exceeds one.
The positive solution of the secular equation therefore gives the unique
optimizer. This step does not rely merely on an arbitrary stationary root.

The modular argument was checked directly. With (r=p-1), the identity

\[
 (r-1)/2=r\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p
\]

is the reason the derivative calculation works. At every nonzero residue
\(\alpha=-j\), both \(P-T^{2r}\) and its derivative vanish. Their
degree is below \(2r\), which proves the asserted polynomial identity.
For the constant term, if \(\kappa^2+1=kp\), then
\(1\le k\le p-2\); hence \(1+k\) is nonzero modulo \(p\).
This excludes the otherwise important possibility that the constant term
is divisible by \(p^2\). The prescribed congruence modulo \(p^2\)
preserves that conclusion for the larger block coefficients.

The optimal-value valuation also checks out. In its convergent power
series in the Eisenstein root, the constant term has valuation one;
the terms of orders one through \(r\) have valuation greater than one.
The order-\(r+1\) coefficient is a unit, so its valuation is
\((r+1)/(2r)<1\). All later terms have strictly larger valuation.
There is consequently no cancellation at the smallest valuation. Since
\(r\) is even, the reduced denominator is \(2r\). The local
ramification index of the field generated by the value is at least this
denominator, and its global degree is at least its local degree. The
value thus generates the whole degree-\(2r\) multiplier field. The
reverse degree inequality follows from the displayed rational expression
for the value, rather than an unstated genericity argument.

The splitting lemma correctly handles the repeated roots of \(P\bmod q\).
After the substitution \(T=-i+Ki u\), the other denominators are units,
and the reduced polynomial has the two simple roots \(u=\pm1\).
The two lifted roots at the same pole are distinct; roots at different
poles have distinct residues modulo \(q\). Thus the proof finds all
\(2r\) roots in \(\mathbb Q_q\), although the unscaled reduction is
not squarefree.

The induction establishing product degree is valid. At the next prime,
all earlier polynomials split over the same field \(\mathbb Q_{p_i}\).
The splitting field of their product can therefore be realized inside
that field. This supplies a simultaneous embedding of the earlier
compositum; independent choices of embeddings are not being tacitly
assumed compatible. The next Eisenstein polynomial is irreducible over
\(\mathbb Q_{p_i}\), and consequently over its embedded earlier
compositum. This proves the full degree product. Pairwise trivial field
intersections alone would not have sufficed.

Positive objective weights preserve every block optimizer. Disjoint
nonempty supports make the constraint Hessians linearly independent,
so the Hessian-span parameter is exactly \(h\), rather than merely
bounded by \(h\).

### Subsequently proposed positive-definite mixing

The author subsequently supplied the following strengthening, which was
checked independently before its inclusion in the construction note.
Write \(w_i=t^{i-1}\), \(P=p_h\),
\(K_{\max}=\max_i K_i\), and \(L=K_{\max}P^2\). The secular
equation implies

\[
 1<\lambda_i<K_i\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{r_i}j^2}<L.
\]

For the first inequality, evaluating its decreasing left side at one
gives at least \(r_iK_i^2/4\ge4\). For \(h\ge2\), set
\(\eta=1/(2h w_hL)\) and replace the ball constraints by

\[
 g_i(x)=\|x_i\|^2+\eta\sum_{j\ne i}\|x_j\|^2
             -1-\eta(h-1)\le0.
\]

Each Hessian is positive definite. Their coefficient mixing matrix is
\(M=(1-\eta)I+\eta\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\), which is invertible,
so their span still has dimension \(h\). At the original optimizer,
all rows are active. Put \(a_i=w_i\lambda_i\); stationarity for the
Lagrangian \(f+\tfrac12\sum_i\mu_i g_i\) requires \(M\mu=a\).
The explicit inverse gives

\[
 \mu_i=
 \frac{a_i-\eta\sum_j a_j/[1+\eta(h-1)]}{1-\eta}>0,
\]

because the subtracted term is less than
\(\eta h w_hL=1/2\), whereas \(a_i>1\). These are sufficient
KKT conditions for a convex problem. Strict convexity of the objective
preserves the same unique optimizer and therefore the same optimal value.
The origin remains strictly feasible and the feasible set is compact.
The rational \(\eta\) has bit length
\(O(\log h+\log w_h+\log L)\). For the existential weight (14),
this is \(O(h^2\log P)\). For a larger constructive weight, this
formula must use that weight's actual bit length; it does not inherit
the sharper estimate automatically. For \(h=1\), the original single
ball already has a positive definite Hessian.

## 2. Primitive weighting and the parameter consequence

The bounded-weight existence argument is correct. Distinct embeddings
of the joint field differ on at least one block value because these
values generate that field. Each pair therefore excludes at most
\(h-1\) integer choices of \(t\). Counting unordered pairs and using
one more positive integer than the total exclusion count gives a
primitive weighted sum. This proves existence with the stated bit
length. It does not find a good weight in polynomial time, a distinction
the examined version explicitly makes.

The dense-input bound is conservative and sufficient. For fixed \(h\),
choosing the primes in \([R,2R]\) gives

\[
 N=O_h(R^2\log R),\qquad D\ge[2(R-1)]^h.
\]

If a universal upper bound \(D\le f(h)N^C\) existed with a fixed
exponent \(C\), first fixing any integer \(h>2C\), and then sending
\(R\) to infinity, gives a contradiction. The constants depending on
this fixed \(h\), including an arbitrarily large but finite \(f(h)\),
cannot offset the excess power of \(R\). The existence of arbitrarily
large suitable intervals follows from the prime number theorem in the
stated arithmetic progression.

The conclusion concerns algebraic degree and an output format containing
an expanded minimal polynomial. It gives no lower bound for deciding
feasibility, comparing two optima, or approximating them. In this example
each separate optimizer coordinate still lies in a single block field
of degree \(2r_i\). Thus a list of separate coordinate descriptions is
short even when their joint field has degree \(D\). The weighted value
also has a compact expression as a sum of separately described block
values. No obstruction to such output formats follows.

The fresh prior-art subreviewer also examined the squared-pole secular
equation in Forsythe–Golub, Sections 2 and 5

The examined passages concern secular equations or geometric critical
point counts; they did not yield an equivalent rational block-field
construction with the coefficient bounds at issue here. This records
the subreviewer's inspection, not a second complete source audit by this
reviewer.

The arithmetic ingredients—Eisenstein irreducibility, local ramification,
simple-root Hensel lifting, and primitive-element collision counting—are
classical. The candidate addition is their explicit realization in a
strictly convex trust-region family, with simultaneous splitting conditions
and controlled input size. The solver-related implication is a limitation
on expanded exact algebraic output, not an optimization hardness result.
It usefully explains why a succinct exact representation matters, but
does not independently establish a faster solver or a broader tractable
problem class.

Searches combined “trust region” or “trust-region” with “algebraic degree,”
“minimal polynomial,” “irreducible,” “irreducibility,” “Galois,”
“Eisenstein,” and “degree 2n”; broader searches included convex quadratic
optimization, secular equations, and short rational coefficients.
Search results about irreducible tridiagonal matrices and quantum-graph
secular polynomials concern different notions and do not establish
equivalence. No equivalent explicit arithmetic family was located in
the material inspected. This limited search does not establish novelty.
