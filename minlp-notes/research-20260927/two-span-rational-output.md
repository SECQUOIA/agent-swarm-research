# Polynomial-time rational output at Hessian span two

Date: 2026-09-28. Status: complete proof, independently reviewed.
This is a constructive consequence of the
[two-span rationality theorem](two-span-rationality-frontier.md) and the
package's exact fixed-span convex quadratic optimization algorithm.
It is a supporting result, not a new feasibility complexity classification.
Publication priority is not established.

## 1. Result and dependencies

Consider a rational polyhedron \(P\) and rational convex quadratics

\[
 q_i(x)=\tfrac12x^TQ_ix+a_i^Tx+c_i,\qquad Q_i\succeq0.
\]

Let \(N\) be their explicit binary input length and assume

\[
 h=\dim\operatorname{span}\{Q_i:1\le i\le m\}\le2.
\]

**Theorem.** A deterministic algorithm runs in time polynomial in \(N\)
and either reports that
\(F=P\cap\{q_i\le0:1\le i\le m\}\) is empty or returns a rational
point of \(F\), with polynomial total binary length.

The number of variables and affine rows is unrestricted. No box, strict
feasibility, rational affine-hull promise, or positive definiteness is
required. The conclusion concerns the native PSD representation.

The proof uses the following inputs, with their distinct roles.

- The package's [exact fixed-span algorithm](exact-convex-optimizer-recovery.md)
  decides feasibility and minimizes a rational affine objective over a
  rational native PSD system. For span at most two its running time and
  finite-value encoding length are polynomial. Only its feasibility and
  optimal-value outputs are needed below.
- [Lemma 2 of the rationality note](two-span-rationality-frontier.md)
  proves that a positive tangency multiplier for two rational convex
  quadratics can be chosen rational with polynomial bit length. Its proof
  also makes that multiplier computable by rational linear algebra and
  univariate polynomial gcds.
- Classical exact rational convex quadratic programming handles one
  remaining quadratic inequality over a polyhedron. Classical short-point
  bounds control a radius search; they are not inferred merely from
  qualitative rational density.

Thus this theorem turns the existing exact decision and value procedures
into rational feasible output. It does not construct rational points by
rounding arbitrary boundary points.

## 2. Reduce the native system to two quadratics

Use the epigraph construction in Section 4 of the rationality note. When
\(h=2\), the cone generated by the input PSD Hessians has two extreme
rays, generated by input matrices \(B_1,B_2\). They can be found in
polynomial time: test pairs of independent input matrices and check
whether every input Hessian is their nonnegative rational combination.
There are at most \(m^2\) pairs. All coordinates follow from rational
linear algebra of polynomial bit complexity.

Writing \(Q_i=\alpha_iB_1+\beta_iB_2\), with
\(\alpha_i,\beta_i\ge0\), gives the equivalent lift

\[
 \tfrac12x^TB_1x-u_1\le0,\qquad
 \tfrac12x^TB_2x-u_2\le0,
 \qquad \alpha_i u_1+\beta_i u_2+a_i^Tx+c_i\le0.
\]

The lift has polynomial size and just two PSD quadratic inequalities.
A rational feasible lifted point projects to a rational original point.
For \(h=0\), use linear programming. For \(h=1\), the analogous
one-variable epigraph lift leaves one quadratic, handled by Section 6.

It therefore suffices to treat

\[
 F=\{w:Ew=e,\ a_j(w)\le0\ (1\le j\le M),\ q_1(w)\le0,
 q_2(w)\le0\},                                      \tag{1}
\]

where each \(a_j\) is affine and rational. Decide emptiness first.
For the rest of the proof assume \(F\ne\varnothing\).

## 3. Identify the relevant affine face

For every affine inequality minimize \(a_j\) over \(F\), using the
exact value algorithm. Because \(a_j\le0\) on \(F\), the row is
identically tight exactly when this minimum is zero. A negative finite
value or an unbounded-below objective means that the row is not
identically tight. Put

\[
 I=\{j:a_j=0\text{ throughout }F\},\qquad
 L=\{w:Ew=e,\ a_j(w)=0\ (j\in I)\}.                \tag{2}
\]

The space \(L\) is the affine hull of the smallest face of the
polyhedron in (1) containing \(F\). To see this directly, choose, for
each \(j\notin I\), a point of \(F\) at which that row is strict.
Their average lies in \(F\) and makes every such row strict; if there
are no such rows, choose any feasible point. This argument establishes
existence only; the algorithm does not form an
average of algebraic points. All equalities implicit in the original
polyhedron occur among (2), since a row tight throughout the polyhedron
is also tight throughout \(F\).

Compute a rational affine chart \(w=w_0+Vu\) of \(L\). Gaussian
elimination gives polynomial-size rational coefficients. Substitute in
both quadratics and the remaining affine inequalities. Convexity is
preserved and the Hessian span does not increase. If the chart has
dimension zero, return its rational point. Henceforth write the
restricted polynomials as \(q_i(u)\) and \(a_j(u)\).

## 4. Detect and use a strict margin

Compute the exact optimum \(s^*\) of

\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 \text{maximize}&s\\
 \text{subject to}&q_i(u)+s\le0\quad(i=1,2),\\
                 &a_j(u)+s\le0\quad(j\notin I),\\
                 &0\le s\le1.
 \end{array}                                                   \tag{3}
\]

This is a rational native PSD problem with Hessian span at most two.
It is feasible at \(s=0\), and its finite optimum belongs to \([0,1]\).
The exact value algorithm determines whether \(s^*=0\).

If \(s^*>0\), choose a rational \(0<\delta<s^*\). Its binary
length can be polynomial: refine the isolating interval of the positive
algebraic value until it has a positive lower endpoint, and choose a
smaller positive dyadic rational. The polynomial degree and coefficient
height bounds for \(s^*\) bound the required refinement. Define the
nonempty closed set

\[
 C_\delta=\{u:q_i(u)\le-\delta\ (i=1,2),\quad
                  a_j(u)\le-\delta\ (j\notin I)\}.            \tag{4}
\]

Starting at \(R=1\), double the integer \(R\) until the exact
feasibility oracle reports
\(C_\delta\cap[-R,R]^d\ne\varnothing\). There are polynomially
many doublings. For example, the fixed-span small-point bound obtained
from Grigoriev--Pasechnik's quadratic-map sampling theorem, recorded in
[the prior-work audit](nonconvex-hessian-span-prior-audit.md), gives a
point of magnitude \(2^{N^{O(1)}}\) for (4), whose rational input length
is polynomial in the original \(N\). Alternatively Theorem 4 of the
rationality note gives a short rational witness for (4). The former
justification makes this algorithm independent of that quantitative
addendum.

For a quadratic \(q(u)=\tfrac12u^TAu+b^Tu+c\), a valid Lipschitz
constant in the infinity norm on \([-R,R]^d\) is

\[
       L_q=R\sum_{r,t}|A_{rt}|+\sum_r|b_r|.                    \tag{5}
\]

For an affine row use the sum of the absolute values of its linear
coefficients. Let \(L_0\ge1\) be the maximum of these rational bounds,
and put \(\eta=\min\{1,\delta/(2L_0)\}\). All have polynomial
binary length.

Bisect the current coordinate intervals, retaining a closed half-box
whose intersection with the same set \(C_\delta\) is nonempty, until
every interval has width at most \(2\eta\). A lower-half feasibility
query suffices: retain the lower half if feasible and otherwise the upper
half. The current feasible intersection guarantees that the latter is
nonempty when necessary. Only the current interval endpoints are retained.

Let \(\widehat u\) be the rational midpoint of the final box. Some
\(u\in C_\delta\) lies in that box, so
\(\|\widehat u-u\|_\infty\le\eta\). The segment between them
stays in \([-R,R]^d\); (5) shows that every quadratic and remaining
affine row has value at most

\[
                    -\delta+L_0\eta\le-\delta/2<0
\]

at \(\widehat u\). The rational image \(w_0+V\widehat u\)
therefore belongs to (1), including all equalities exactly. The number
of bisections is \(O(d\log(1+R/\eta))\), hence polynomial. The
oracle queries add only affine bounds and preserve span at most two.

## 5. The zero-margin case

Assume \(s^*=0\). There is no point in \(L\) at which both
quadratics are strictly negative. Indeed, the averaging argument in
Section 3 supplies a feasible point strict in every remaining affine
row. A sufficiently short segment from it toward any such hypothetical
point would make both quadratics and all those affine rows strict,
contradicting \(s^*=0\).

The face restriction is essential. For example, take
\(q_1(x,y)=x^2-1\), \(q_2(x,y)=y^2-1\), and the affine row
\(x\ge1\). The full system has no positive common margin, but the two
quadratics are both strictly negative at the origin. No nonzero
nonnegative aggregate of them is globally nonnegative. Section 3 first
restricts to \(x=1\); there \(q_1\) is identically zero, so the
individual-zero branch below applies.

On the rational chart write

\[
 q_i(u)=\tfrac12u^TA_i u+b_i^Tu+c_i.
\]

Compute the unconstrained infimum of each quadratic by rational linear
algebra. If \(b_i\notin\operatorname{range}A_i\), it is
\(-\infty\). Otherwise solve \(A_i v_i=-b_i\), and the rational
minimum is \(\mu_i=c_i+\tfrac12b_i^Tv_i\). Feasibility gives
\(\mu_i\le0\) whenever it is finite.

If some \(\mu_i=0\), then every feasible point satisfies
\(A_i u=-b_i\). Restrict to this rational affine space and remove
that quadratic inequality. Its value is identically zero there. Exactly
one quadratic remains, so Section 6 returns a rational point. This also
handles an identically zero quadratic: remove the row even when its
gradient equations have no rank.

Otherwise both quadratics take negative values somewhere. The convex
alternative in Proposition 3 of the rationality note then gives
**both** multipliers positive; an aggregate with only one positive
multiplier would make that individual quadratic globally nonnegative.
Thus for some \(t_0>0\),

\[
 g_{t_0}=q_1+t_0q_2\ge0\quad\text{everywhere},
 \qquad q_1(p)=q_2(p)=0\quad\text{at every }p\in F.            \tag{6}
\]

The proof of the tangency lemma computes a suitable rational \(t>0\)
as follows.

1. Compute a rational basis of \(W=\ker A_1\cap\ker A_2\).
   If the restrictions of the linear terms to \(W\) are not both
   zero, choose a basis vector \(v\) with \(b_2^Tv\ne0\) and set
   \(t=-b_1^Tv/(b_2^Tv)\). Existence of (6) guarantees that such a
   vector exists, that this is \(t_0>0\), and that it works for all
   basis vectors.
2. Otherwise restrict to a rational complement of \(W\), on which
   \(A_1+A_2\) is positive definite. In quotient dimension zero take
   \(t=1\). In positive dimension form

   \[
   f(T)=c_1+Tc_2-\tfrac12(b_1+Tb_2)^T
                    (A_1+TA_2)^{-1}(b_1+Tb_2).                \tag{7}
   \]

   If this rational function is identically zero, take \(t=1\).
   Otherwise cancel common factors of its numerator and denominator,
   obtaining the reduced numerator \(N(T)\). The tangency lemma proves
   that \(\gcd(N,N')\) has degree one and its root is \(t_0>0\).
   Return this rational root.

Determinants and adjugates in (7) give univariate polynomials of degree
\(O(d)\) and polynomial coefficient bit length. They can be computed
by polynomial-time rational polynomial arithmetic, as can cancellation
and gcd. The bound on the rational root also follows from Lemma 2.
**Cancellation must precede the derivative gcd**: raw determinant factors
can create spurious repeated nonpositive roots.

Now compute a rational chart of

\[
 M=\{u:(A_1+tA_2)u=-(b_1+tb_2)\}.                            \tag{8}
\]

It is nonempty and contains all feasible points, because (6) and
feasibility force the aggregate to attain its zero minimum. Since
\(t>0\) and both matrices are PSD,
\(\ker(A_1+tA_2)=\ker A_1\cap\ker A_2\). Each quadratic
therefore becomes affine on (8). The original affine inequalities and
these two affine restrictions define a nonempty rational polyhedron.
Rational linear programming returns the desired point.

There is at most one such curved restriction. In the individual-zero
branch there is instead one remaining quadratic. Polynomial coefficient
bounds are composed only a fixed number of times.

## 6. One remaining quadratic

For completeness, let \(P'\cap\{q\le0\}\ne\varnothing\), where
\(P'\) is rational polyhedral and \(q\) is rational PSD quadratic.
Starting with \(R=1\), double \(R\) and solve the rational convex
quadratic program

\[
                   \min\{q(u):u\in P'\cap[-R,R]^d\}.        \tag{9}
\]

Skip an empty box. When the minimum is at most zero, return an exact
rational minimizer of (9). Classical convex quadratic programming provides
this minimizer in polynomial time, with polynomial encoding length. The
box makes the nonempty problem compact, so there is no unbounded-objective
case in the call.

The classical source is Kozlov, Tarasov, and Khachiyan, *The polynomial
solvability of convex quadratic programming*, 1980. The original paper
defines exact solution as including the exact rational optimal value and
an optimizer, and gives polynomial Turing complexity. The inspected source
and page references are recorded in
[the SOCP prior audit](socp-hessian-span-prior.md#one-cone-reduces-to-exact-convex-qp),
with the [primary full text](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=zvmmf&option_lang=eng&paperid=5189&what=fullt).

Vavasis's short rational witness theorem for one rational quadratic
inequality and arbitrary rational affine rows bounds the size of a
feasible point, even without convexity. Consequently some polynomial-bit
radius succeeds, and the doubling loop has polynomial length. This use
of Vavasis is a size bound; the polynomial-time subproblem algorithm uses
the PSD assumption. The same routine covers constant or affine \(q\).

## 7. Consequences and limits

The output can be checked using rational arithmetic alone. In mixed-integer
applications, once an integer assignment of polynomial encoding length
has been found, the algorithm returns rational continuous coordinates
whenever the continuous native PSD Hessian span is at most two. Finding
that integer assignment remains the responsibility of the relevant
mixed-integer algorithm.

If a rational convex quadratic objective has rational attained value
\(\theta\), and its Hessian together with all constraint Hessians spans
at most two dimensions, append \(q_0-\theta\le0\) and apply the
algorithm. This returns a rational optimizer in polynomial time in the
input and the encoding of \(\theta\). The optimal value can first be
obtained with the existing exact fixed-span optimization algorithm;
whether its algebraic representation has degree one is decidable.

The distinction between rational output and algebraic output is material:
the canonical minimum-norm feasible point used by an algebraic recovery
procedure need not be rational even when rational feasible points are
dense. The present construction selects a point suited to rational
output. For example, \((x-2)^2\le2\) has the rational feasible point
\(x=1\), but its minimum-norm point is \(2-\sqrt2\). The construction
does not supply rational output at span three, where an
irrational singleton is already possible, or for arbitrary squared SOC
representations.

The construction gives a theoretical exact-output guarantee. The general
fixed-span oracle and the worst-case radius and precision bounds are not
claimed to be practical. A practical implementation would need usable
constants, reliable rational face detection, and efficient implementations
of the exact oracle calls. No computational speedup is established here.

## 8. Prior work and verification

The one-quadratic certificate result, polynomial-time rational convex QP,
rational Gaussian elimination, box bisection, and Lipschitz rounding are
established tools. The additional ingredient is the special rational
tangency structure for two native PSD quadratics, as proved and compared
with prior work in the [rationality note](two-span-rationality-frontier.md).
This note claims no independent novelty for the exact fixed-span decision
algorithm or its algebraic value bounds.

The [independent literature audit](two-span-rational-output-prior.md)
identifies Safey El Din and Zhi's *Computing rational points in convex
semi-algebraic sets and SOS decompositions*, 2010, as the strongest general
rational-output predecessor located. Its Theorem 1.1 computes a rational
point when one exists in a convex semialgebraic set, in
\(\sigma^{O(1)}(sD)^{O(n^3)}\) bit operations, where \(n\) is the
ambient dimension. That bound does not directly imply polynomial time
here because \(n\) grows. Its use of affine restrictions is also prior
methodology. The algorithm author independently inspected Theorem 1.1
and Corollary 1.2 in the
[primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.2973). The audit also compares
exact SDP algorithms and ellipsoid-contact algorithms. No direct subsumer
was located in this limited search; this does not establish priority.

The proof checks polynomial input growth at each stage, retention of one
fixed positive-margin system throughout bisection, exact preservation of
affine equalities, and cancellation before tangency-root extraction.
The [independent adversarial review](two-span-rational-output-review.md)
reconstructs every branch and found no mathematical obstruction. It
identified a malformed math command and an omitted empty-average case;
both were corrected. The author reread the complete review and checked
the resulting affine-face and rounding arguments independently.

A targeted inline Python command (`python -`) checked this note and the
prior-audit note for local-link existence, trailing whitespace, control
characters, final newline, and paired math delimiters; it passed. After
adding the review link, the same check was repeated on all three new
documents. No numerical experiment or Lean formalization is asserted to
verify this theorem. No project-wide verification or CI inspection was
performed.
