# A PosSLP reduction to certified cubic-root circuits

Date: 2026-09-28. Status: proof passed
[fresh adversarial review](posslp-certified-cubic-root-independent-review.md)
and an independent root reconstruction. The scoped
[primary literature comparison](posslp-convex-quartic-prior.md)
also has an independent source review; publication priority is
unestablished.

The reduction encodes the sign of an integer arithmetic
circuit using only cubic-root gates whose radicands are rational affine
combinations of previous roots and their squares. All roots lie near
one, and rational interval certificates have polynomial bit length.
Combined with the reviewed
[signed odd-root quartic construction](signed-odd-root-circuit-quartic.md),
this proves PosSLP-hardness of exact feasibility for one rational
strongly SOS-convex quartic inequality and one rational affine inequality.
The quartic has a supplied positive definite rational Hessian Gram.
It also proves PosSLP-hardness of deciding whether the minimum of a
rational strongly SOS-convex quartic on a fixed unit cube is zero,
even when its global Hessian is at least \(36I\). A
[separate reviewed upper bound](posslp-certified-cubic-root-upper.md)
makes the certified positive cubic-root comparison language
PosSLP-complete. That upper bound is for the root-circuit language,
not for arbitrary convex quartic optimization.

The proof is a sign simulation by analytic functions. It does not
represent intermediate integer values exactly as root outputs.
Its small parameter is generated by a short circuit; its exponentially
small value is never printed as a rational constant.

## Input problem and the conclusion

PosSLP asks whether the integer output \(V\) of a straight-line
program with constants zero and one and gates \(+,-,\times\)
is positive. Replace its output by \(W=2V-1\), adding two gates.
Then \(W\ne0\) and
\[
                         W>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V>0.
\]
Binary integer constants, if allowed in the input convention, can first
be built by polynomially many additions from zero and one.

**Theorem.** In deterministic polynomial time one can map
this input to a circuit
\[
 \xi_i^3=c_i+\sum_{j<i}(a_{ij}\xi_j+b_{ij}\xi_j^2)
 \tag{1}
\]
with rational coefficients, and positive rational boxes
\([L_i,U_i]\), such that:

1. Direct signed interval evaluation of each right-hand side on all
   predecessor boxes lies in \([L_i^3,U_i^3]\).
2. All coefficients and interval endpoints have polynomial bit length.
3. A designated output satisfies \(\xi_o\ne1\), and
   \(\xi_o>1\) if and only if \(V>0\).

Thus the boxes satisfy the actual input promise of the signed-root
quartic realization. Their existence is proved below by explicit
formulas, rather than assumed from the true gate values.

## Analytic arithmetic gates

For real arguments close to zero, put
\[
 A(z)=(1+3z)^{1/3}-1,\qquad S(z)=A(z^2),
\]
\[
 H(z)=\frac{S(A(z))+S(A(-z))}{2},\qquad
 P(x,y)=A\!\left(\frac{H(x+y)-H(x-y)}4\right).
 \tag{2}
\]
The real cube root is used. In a small complex neighborhood of zero,
use the analytic branch with value one before subtracting one.
Then \(H\) is even,
\[
 H(z)=z^2+O(z^4),\qquad P(x,0)=P(0,y)=0,
 \qquad P(x,y)=xy+\text{terms of higher total degree}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The exact vanishing on both coordinate axes matters: it prevents errors
of smaller order than \(xy\) when the two inputs have different
orders in the small parameter.

An addition or subtraction macro is \(A(x\pm y)\). A product
macro is \(P(x,y)\). If a signal \(x\) is stored as a root
\(1+x\), each macro has form (1). Addition or subtraction uses
one root gate. A product uses nine root gates:

- Four gates for \(A(x+y),A(-x-y),A(x-y),A(-x+y)\).
- Four gates applying \(S\) to those four signals.
- One gate applying \(A\) to the signed sum of these four results,
  divided by eight.

For example, \(S(x)\), stored as a root \(1+S(x)\), has
radicand \(3\xi^2-6\xi+4\), where \(\xi=1+x\).
An addition radicand is \(3\xi+3\eta-5\); the other signs
change only its rational affine coefficients. The final product gate
has radicand
\[
                 1+\frac38(\zeta_1+\zeta_2-\zeta_3-\zeta_4).
 \tag{4}
\]
No radicand contains a product of distinct predecessor roots.

## Explicit analytic bounds

Fix
\[
                         K=2^{24},\qquad r=1/100.
\]
For real \(|z|\le1/12\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 |A(z)|\le2|z|,\qquad |A(z)-z|\le2z^2.
 \tag{5}
\]
Indeed \(A'(z)=(1+3z)^{-2/3}\) and
\(A''(z)=-2(1+3z)^{-5/3}\), whose absolute values are below
two and four, respectively, on this interval.

For \(|x|,|y|\le r/4\),
\[
                 |P(x,y)-xy|\le K|xy|(|x|+|y|).
 \tag{6}
\]
Here is a quantitative proof. On the complex disk \(|z|\le1/12\),
the same derivative estimate gives \(|A(z)|\le2|z|\).
On the closed polydisk \(|x|,|y|\le r\), the arguments
\(x\pm y\) have modulus at most \(0.02\), their images
under \(A\) at most \(0.04\), and their images under
\(S\circ A\) at most \(0.0032\). The argument of the
outer \(A\) in \(P\) has modulus at most \(0.0016\),
so \(|P|\le0.0032<1\). Every branch used in these expressions
is analytic on a neighborhood of that polydisk.

Cauchy's coefficient estimate bounds the coefficient of \(x^iy^j\)
in \(P\) by \(r^{-i-j}\). By (3), the coefficients on the
axes vanish and the \(xy\) coefficient is one. Writing
\(s=|x|/r\) and \(t=|y|/r\), the remaining series is bounded by
\[
 \frac{|xy|}{r^2}
 \left(\frac1{(1-s)(1-t)}-1\right)
 \le \frac{2}{r^3}|xy|(|x|+|y|).
\]
The last inequality uses \(s,t\le1/4\). Since
\(2/r^3=2{,}000{,}000<K\), this proves (6), including cases
where one input is zero.

## Homogenization by numerator and denominator signals

For each integer circuit value \(V_i\), construct two analytic
signals \(f_i(\delta),g_i(\delta)\) with an assigned common
positive integer order \(d_i\). Their leading coefficients will
be \(V_i\) and one:
\[
 f_i(\delta)=V_i\delta^{d_i}+O(\delta^{d_i+1}),\qquad
 g_i(\delta)=\delta^{d_i}+O(\delta^{d_i+1}).
 \tag{7}
\]
For the constant one use \((\delta,\delta)\) of order one.
For zero use \((A(\delta-\delta),\delta)\), again of order
one. Every displayed signal is a root output minus one.

An addition or subtraction \(V_i=V_a\pm V_b\) becomes
\[
 f_i=A\bigl(P(f_a,g_b)\pm P(f_b,g_a)\bigr),\qquad
 g_i=P(g_a,g_b).
 \tag{8}
\]
A multiplication becomes
\[
                 f_i=P(f_a,f_b),\qquad g_i=P(g_a,g_b).
 \tag{9}
\]
In both cases set \(d_i=d_a+d_b\). The inputs to the addition
macro in (8) have equal assigned order. There is no need to create a
separate circuit for a power \(\delta^{d_i}\). These orders
are proof annotations of at most exponential value and polynomial bit
length. The construction uses only a constant number of macros per
integer gate, with shared predecessor outputs.

Let \(T\) be the actual number of macros after this transformation,
before generating the small parameter in (14)--(15).
It is polynomial, in fact linear, in the unit-constant integer circuit
size. Count all zero-initialization macros as well. The topology fixes
\(T\) without knowing \(\delta\); consequently the later choices
of \(q,Q,\delta_0\) are not circular.

## A uniform finite error estimate

Set
\[
 B_0=2,\qquad B_j=64K B_{j-1}^3\quad(1\le j\le T).
 \tag{10}
\]
These large numbers are used only in the proof. Their logarithms satisfy
\[
                       \log_2 B_j=16\cdot3^j-15.
 \tag{11}
\]
Suppose for now that
\[
                         0<\delta\le 2^{-30}/B_T.
 \tag{12}
\]
After macro \(j\), every available signal \(f\) of assigned
order \(d\), with its prescribed integer coefficient \(C\),
satisfies
\[
 |C|\le B_j,\qquad
 |f-C\delta^d|\le B_j\delta^{d+1},\qquad
 |f|\le2B_j\delta^d.
 \tag{13}
\]
Initially these assertions hold for \(f=\delta,C=1,d=1\).
For the inductive step write \(B=B_{j-1}\). All signal inputs
have absolute value at most \(2B_T\delta\le2^{-29}\),
so (5)--(6) apply.

For addition of equal-order inputs, the leading coefficient has
absolute value at most \(2B\). The inherited error is at most
\(2B\delta^{d+1}\), and (5) adds at most
\(32B^2\delta^{2d}\). Since \(d\ge1\), their sum is at
most \(34B^2\delta^{d+1}\), below the bound in (10).

For a product of orders \(a,b\), the leading coefficient has
absolute value at most \(B^2\). Multiplying the two approximations
in (13) creates error at most \(3B^2\delta^{a+b+1}\).
Equation (6) adds at most
\(16K B^3\delta^{a+b+1}\). Their sum is below
\(64K B^3\delta^{a+b+1}\). Finally the last bound in (13)
follows from its first two and \(\delta\le1\). This completes
the induction, including nodes whose prescribed leading coefficient is
zero.

For the final numerator, the coefficient is the nonzero integer
\(W\). Its error is at most
\(2^{-30}\delta^{d_o}\), so its sign is exactly the sign of
\(W\). This establishes the desired sign comparison once (12)
has been realized by a small circuit.

## Generating the small parameter and its rational boxes

Put
\[
 q=2T+5,\qquad Q=q+9T+1,\qquad \delta_0=1000^{-(Q+3)}.
 \tag{14}
\]
Generate \(q\) roots using
\[
                 \delta_{j+1}=S(\delta_j),\qquad \delta=\delta_q.
 \tag{15}
\]
The first radicand is the rational number \(1+3\delta_0^2\).
Every later one has the quadratic form given after (4).
For positive \(z\), one has \(0<S(z)\le z^2\), because
\((1+t)^3\ge1+3t\) for \(t\ge0\). Therefore
\[
          0<\delta\le\delta_0^{2^q}<2^{-4\cdot2^q}.
\]
Since
\[
 4\cdot2^q=2^{2T+7}>16\cdot3^T+30>\log_2 B_T+30,
\]
condition (12) holds. The printed rational \(\delta_0\) has
only \(O(Q)\) bits; the tiny number \(\delta\) is represented
by its \(q\) root gates.

Expand every macro into the raw root gates already specified, placing
the \(q\) parameter gates first. There are at most \(Q\)
raw root gates. Number them topologically and assign
\[
 w_i=1000^i\delta_0,\qquad L_i=1-w_i,\qquad U_i=1+w_i.
 \tag{16}
\]
For all these gates, \(w_i\le1000^{-3}<1/3\), so the boxes
are positive and their endpoints have \(O(Q)\) bits.

Except for the first parameter gate, every radicand equals one when
all predecessor roots equal one. On predecessor boxes with width at
most \(w_{i-1}\), direct signed interval evaluation gives deviation
from one of at most \(13w_{i-1}\). For an addition or subtraction
gate the coefficient absolute sum is at most six. For a small-signal
squaring gate \(3\xi^2-6\xi+4\), the bound is
\(12w_{i-1}+3w_{i-1}^2\le13w_{i-1}\). For (4) the
coefficient absolute sum is \(3/2\). Shared or repeated inputs
do not invalidate these interval bounds.

On the other hand,
\[
 (1-w_i)^3\le1-2w_i\le1-13w_{i-1},\qquad
 (1+w_i)^3\ge1+3w_i\ge1+13w_{i-1}.
\]
Thus the required interval inclusion holds. For the first gate it
follows directly from \(3\delta_0^2\le3w_1\) and its
radicand being greater than one. This proves all box promises without
knowing any root value. The raw gate coefficients have constant bit
length apart from the first parameter gate, whose length is \(O(Q)\).
The complete circuit and all its boxes have polynomial total size and
are constructed by rational arithmetic in polynomial time.

## Consequence for exact convex quartic feasibility

Apply the reviewed signed-root theorem to (1) and (16), with degree
three at every gate. Its normalization multiplies every positive root
by a common rational \(\kappa\). It returns a rational strongly
SOS-convex quartic \(F\), of polynomial bit length, whose unique
zero has coordinates
\((\kappa\xi_i,(\kappa\xi_i)^2)_i\). It also supplies a
polynomial-size rational positive definite Hessian Gram and proves
\(\nabla^2F\succeq I\) globally. The polynomial is explicitly a
sum of rational quadratic squares, so \(F\ge0\) everywhere.

Use the two convex inequalities
\[
                        F(X)\le0,\qquad X_{o,1}\ge\kappa.
 \tag{17}
\]
The first inequality restricts \(X\) to that unique zero. The
second holds there exactly when \(\xi_o>1\), because equality
has been excluded by the nonzero integer leading coefficient. Thus
(17) is feasible exactly when the original PosSLP instance is positive.

The feasible set is either empty or a singleton. This is an exact,
degenerate feasibility result: no Slater condition or inverse-polynomial
gap is claimed. The positive Hessian certificate and all coefficients
remain small. The argument gives no obstruction to polynomial-time
approximate minimization and no claim that PosSLP is NP-hard.

## Exact optimization on a fixed unit cube

The degeneracy in (17) concerns feasibility, and can be separated from
the optimization domain. Put \(w_{\max}=\max_i w_i<1/3\).
The signed-root normalization has
\[
 \kappa=\frac1{1-w_{\max}}<2,\qquad
 0<\kappa\xi_i\le\frac{1+w_{\max}}{1-w_{\max}}<2.
\]
Thus every coordinate of the quartic's unique zero lies in \((0,4)\).
Minimize \(F\) on the box with designated coordinate
\(X_{o,1}\in[\kappa,8]\) and all other coordinates in \([0,8]\).
Its minimum is zero exactly when the original instance is positive.
In the other case the zero lies outside the box, so compactness and
nonnegativity give a strictly positive minimum.

Use the rational affine transformation
\[
 X_{o,1}=\kappa+(8-\kappa)Y_{o,1},\qquad
 X_j=8Y_j\quad(j\ne(o,1)).
\]
It maps the fixed cube \([0,1]^N\) onto this box. The transformed
quartic \(\widetilde F(Y)=F(X(Y))\) remains a sum of rational
quadratic squares, and
\[
 \nabla^2\widetilde F(Y)\succeq36I,
 \qquad
 \min_{Y\in[0,1]^N}\widetilde F(Y)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V>0.
\]
Indeed the affine diagonal scales are eight or \(8-\kappa>6\).
The Hessian Gram transforms by an invertible rational block map from
\((v,Y\otimes v)\) to \((Dv,(DY+b)\otimes Dv)\), so its
positive definiteness and polynomial bit size are preserved.

The cube has the fixed rational strict interior point
\((1/2,\ldots,1/2)\). Thus this optimization statement uses an
elementary compact domain with a supplied Slater point. In the no case
the optimum is positive; no inverse-polynomial lower bound on that
positive value is asserted. Adding the threshold inequality
\(\widetilde F\le0\) would again make feasible instances
singletons, so the domain Slater statement is not a Slater statement
for that threshold feasibility problem.

## Verification, prior comparison, and scope

The [independent review](posslp-certified-cubic-root-independent-review.md)
reconstructs the analytic bounds, projective orders, finite error
recurrence, parameter size, signed interval arithmetic, and both
quartic transfers. An independent root reconstruction also checked these
steps and the normalization interface with the signed-root theorem.
The focused exact command was

```text
python research-20260927/check_posslp_cubic_root_reduction.py
```

It passed the product's bivariate series through degree six, projective
degree and leading-coefficient annotations, and exact signed interval
checks for five compiled circuits with positive, zero, and negative
integer outputs, including cancellation. Those cases have 77 to 207
raw root gates. These finite calculations do not prove the universal
Cauchy estimate or all-input complexity bound. No project-wide checks,
CI inspection, or Lean verification were used.

The [primary root-circuit audit](posslp-root-circuit-prior.md) records
older PosSLP reductions to restricted arithmetic and bounded arithmetic
circuits. In particular, the present result does not claim that bounded
arithmetic simulation is new. Exact SDP feasibility also has established
PosSLP-hardness. The [convex-optimization prior audit](posslp-convex-quartic-prior.md)
records the older SDP reduction, checks its direct SOCP consequence,
and compares the precise quartic restrictions with the November 2025
version of *Hesse's Redemption*. That version leaves exact convex-quartic
threshold complexity unclassified; the present lower bound does not
settle every complexity-class question in that table.
The contribution proved here is the mandatory cubic-root compilation
with explicit rational interval certificates and its transfer to the
stated strict-Hessian quartics. Its publication priority remains
unestablished. PosSLP-hardness is not asserted to imply NP-hardness.

A [separate reviewed perturbation](unconstrained-quartic-posslp-reduction.md)
uses this circuit to obtain PosSLP-hardness of unconstrained exact
minimum-sign decision for strictly SOS-convex quartics. Its two branches
have strictly negative or strictly positive minima, respectively.
