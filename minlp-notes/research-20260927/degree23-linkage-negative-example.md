# A degree-23 residual with an unavoidable extra quadratic base point

Status: exact algebraic example verified;
[independent checker review](degree23-linkage-checker-review.md) and
[fresh full review](degree23-linkage-exact-review.md) completed.
This is a negative construction result, not a new convex quartic example
or a priority claim.

The proposed construction does produce the expected degree-23 number field in
five affine coordinates. Its quadratic equations retain an additional rational
projective point. This prevents this linkage pattern from producing a globally
convex rational SOS quartic with a unique degree-23 zero and positive definite
Hessian there, in every rational affine chart. The particular example below
also has three real embeddings, an independent obstruction to a singleton.

## 1. The nine-point residual configuration

Work over $\mathbb Q$ in $\mathbb P^5$ with coordinates $x_0,\ldots,x_5$.
Let

\[
 A=\{[1:a:b:c:0:0]:a,b,c\in\{-1,1\}\},\qquad
 b_0=[0:0:0:0:1:0],\qquad R=A\sqcup\{b_0\}.
\]

The saturated homogeneous ideal is

\[
 I_R=(x_5,\ x_1^2-x_0^2,\ x_2^2-x_0^2,\ x_3^2-x_0^2,
             \ x_4x_0,\ldots,x_4x_3).
\]

In particular, its degree-two part has dimension $13$, with basis the three
displayed square differences, the four $x_4x_j$ for $0\leq j\leq3$, and
the six $x_5x_j$ for $0\leq j\leq5$. The ideal generated by these thirteen
quadrics must be saturated before identifying it with $I_R$: the latter also
contains the linear form $x_5$.

The eight-point complete intersection $A\subset\mathbb P^3$ has Hilbert
function $(1,4,7,8,8,\ldots)$. The extra point increases each positive-degree
value by one, since $x_4^d$ separates it from $A$. Thus

\[
 H_R=(1,5,8,9,9,\ldots),\qquad h_R=(1,4,3,1).
\]

The only relation among degree-two evaluations on $R$ is supported on $A$.
Ordering the signs lexicographically, one relation is

\[
 (-1,1,1,-1,1,-1,-1,1,0).
\]

Its zero coefficient at $b_0$ will matter below.

## 2. Five explicit quadrics and their linked residual

Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
 q_1&=x_1^2-x_0^2+x_4x_2+2x_5x_0,\\
 q_2&=x_2^2-x_0^2+x_4x_3+3x_5x_1,\\
 q_3&=x_3^2-x_0^2+x_4x_1+5x_5x_2,\\
 q_4&=x_4x_0+x_5(x_3+x_4+x_5),\\
 q_5&=x_4(x_1+2x_2+3x_3)
       +x_5(17x_0+2x_1+3x_2+5x_3+7x_4+11x_5).
\end{aligned}
\]

Let $I_C=(q_1,\ldots,q_5)$ and $I_\Gamma=I_C:I_R$.
The exact retained computation establishes the following statements over
$\mathbb Q$:

- $C$ is a proper complete intersection of five quadrics, of degree $32$.
- $I_\Gamma$ is saturated, has degree $23$, and
  $I_C:I_\Gamma=I_R$.
- $\Gamma$ lies entirely in the affine chart $x_5=1$.
- Its Hilbert numerator is $h_\Gamma=(1,5,9,7,1)$.
- Its affine coordinate algebra is a field of degree $23$.

Here a Hilbert numerator $h$ means that the homogeneous coordinate ring has
Hilbert series $\sum_j h_jz^j/(1-z)$. Classical complete-intersection
liaison predicts

\[
 h_\Gamma(j)=\binom5j-h_R(5-j).
\]

The Artinian socle degree in this formula is $5$. The cohomological
Cayley–Bacharach shift used below is $4$; they differ by one.
In degree two, $H_\Gamma(2)=1+5+9=15$, so
$\dim I_\Gamma(2)=21-15=6$.

For the field claim, the affine lexicographic Gröbner basis has shape

\[
 p(t),\quad x_3-r_3(t),\quad x_2-r_2(t),\quad
 x_1-r_1(t),\quad x_0-r_0(t),\qquad t=x_4,
\]

where the $r_i$ are rational polynomials and $p$ has degree $23$.
The complete coefficient list for $p$, beginning with $35322$ and ending with
$22501016846117$, is retained in both
[the Singular script](check_degree23_linkage.sing) and
[the Python checker](check_degree23_linkage.py). The Python checker proves
irreducibility modulo $293$ by verifying

\[
 t^{293^{23}}\equiv t\pmod p,\qquad
 \gcd(p,t^{293}-t)=1\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_{293}[t].
\]

Because $23$ is prime, the first identity permits only irreducible factor
degrees $1$ or $23$; the second excludes degree one. The leading coefficient
is nonzero modulo $293$, so irreducibility lifts to $\mathbb Q$.
Exact Sturm root counting gives **three** real roots. Consequently this
field has three real embeddings, not one.

The five-by-six Jacobian has rank five at all nine points of $R$.
The field calculation gives $23$ distinct geometric residual points in
characteristic zero. These account for all $32$ points of $C$, which is
therefore reduced.

## 3. The six-dimensional quadratic space retains a rational point

A convenient sixth quadric is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 g={}&17x_0^2+2x_0x_1+3x_0x_2+5x_0x_3
      -x_1x_3-2x_2x_3-3x_3^2\\
    &+28x_0x_5+x_1x_5+x_2x_5-5x_3x_5+4x_5^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Writing $L=x_1+2x_2+3x_3$, direct expansion gives

\[
 x_5g=(x_0+x_5)q_5-(L+7x_5)q_4.
\]

Since $x_5$ is invertible on $\Gamma$, this proves $g\in I_\Gamma(2)$.
At a cube point,

\[
 g(1,a,b,c,0,0)=14+2a+3b+5c-ac-2bc\geq1.
\]

Thus $g$ is independent of the five quadrics defining $C$, and

\[
 I_\Gamma(2)=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{q_1,\ldots,q_5,g\}.
\]

Every member vanishes at $b_0$. More precisely, the checker establishes the
homogeneous ideal equality

\[
 (q_1,\ldots,q_5,g)=I_\Gamma\cap I_{b_0}.
\]

The quadratic base scheme is reduced of degree $24$, with Hilbert numerator
$(1,5,9,7,2)$. This gives a concrete example where the field has degree $23$
and six independent quadratic relations, but those relations do not define
the field point projectively.

## 4. Why this entire linkage pattern fails for convex rational SOS

The extra point is forced for any reduced complete intersection
$C=\Gamma\sqcup A\sqcup\{b_0\}$ of five quadrics having the configuration
in Section 1. Indeed, the classical Cayley–Bacharach identity with shift
$s=10-5-1=4$ gives

\[
 \dim I_\Gamma(2)-\dim I_C(2)=9-H_R(2)=1,
\]

whereas, after moving $b_0$ to the other part,

\[
 \dim I_{\Gamma\cup\{b_0\}}(2)-\dim I_C(2)
      =8-H_A(2)=1.
\]

The containment between these two spaces is therefore equality.
Equivalently, for $q\in I_\Gamma(2)$, the quartic $qx_4^2$ vanishes at
every point of $C$ except possibly $b_0$; Cayley–Bacharach in degree four
forces it to vanish there as well.

Suppose $\Gamma$ is one degree-23 closed point and a rational SOS quartic
$F=\sum_j f_j^2$ vanishes at one real point of $\Gamma$. Each rational
quadratic $f_j$ vanishes at all conjugates, so its homogenization belongs to
$I_\Gamma(2)$ and vanishes at $b_0$.

If $b_0$ lies in the chosen rational affine chart, it is a second real zero
of $F$. If $b_0$ lies at infinity, it gives a nonzero rational direction
where the leading quartic form of $F$ vanishes. For a globally convex
quartic with positive definite Hessian at its zero, the
[flat-direction reduction](quartic-zero-degree-adversarial.md) removes this
direction by a rational affine fiber minimizer. It preserves rational SOS,
the zero's coordinate field, and positive definiteness of its Hessian.
If $m\leq4$ variables remain, $m$ quadratic residuals with independent
gradients at its regular zero give field degree at most
$2^m\leq16$ by isolated-point Bézout, contradicting $23$. If no variables
remain, the zero is rational, giving the same contradiction.

The projective base point alone does **not** forbid an arbitrary affine SOS
singleton when the point is at infinity. The global convexity and regularity
assumptions are essential to this second part of the argument. The explicit
example above has three real embeddings, so it already fails uniqueness
without needing convexity.

## 5. Prior results, review, and verification scope

The liaison and Cayley–Bacharach formulas used here are established results.
[Gold, Little, and Schenck, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311129)
states the residual-scheme identity with shift
$s=\sum d_i-m-1$ and attributes it to Davis, Geramita, and Orecchia.
It applies to the residual schemes of a zero-dimensional complete
intersection. The dimension calculations here specialize that theorem;
they do not resolve an Eisenbud–Green–Harris conjecture. The full theorem
and its residual-scheme definition were inspected directly. An independent
reviewer also checked Davis–Geramita–Orecchia's 1985 Theorem 3(b),
“Hilbert Functions under Liaison,” and independently reconstructed the
flat-direction argument in the local note.

The value of this computation is a concrete counterexample to the inference
that the expected Hilbert function and an irreducible degree-23 residual
suffice for the intended convex construction. No novelty is claimed for
the liaison principle or for this style of residual construction.
