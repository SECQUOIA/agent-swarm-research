# Independent proof review of the algebraic certificate theorem

Date: 2026-09-27. Reviewed manuscript:
[algebraic-primal-dual-certificates.md](algebraic-primal-dual-certificates.md).

The stated certificate theorem is supported by the proof. I found no
mathematical gap after checking the complete manuscript, including the
soundness identities, the number of curved reductions, multiplier support,
the common-field claim, and the encoding bound. This conclusion concerns
the stated existence and verification theorem. It does not establish an
algorithm for constructing all certificate data or a priority claim.

The main reviewer read the complete manuscript independently of its author.
A separate focused reviewer checked the infeasibility branch and found the
support limitation below. The main reviewer independently rechecked that
branch, its attainment source, and the support example. Both reviewers
communicated corrections and qualifications while the draft was being
written; the final result therefore incorporates adversarial feedback.

## 1. Preservation and soundness

The native tangent at the proposed primal point is a valid necessary
inequality because each native quadratic is convex. Adding all those
tangents at the start is legitimate even if the primal point is irrational.
Their coefficients belong to the field generated by that point.

For an exposing aggregate `g`, complementarity is essential. It gives
`g(x*)=0`; stationarity and polyhedral complementarity give

\[
 \nabla g(x^*)^T(x-x^*)
   =\sum_r\mu_r(b_r-A_rx)\ge0\qquad(x\in P_j).
\]

The exact quadratic Taylor formula then writes `g` as the sum of this
nonnegative linear term and a nonnegative quadratic term. On the original
feasible set, the aggregate is also nonpositive. Both terms vanish there.
Positive semidefiniteness converts vanishing quadratic form into the
matrix equation used by the next record. Thus every listed section
preserves every original feasible point.

The final identity in manuscript equation (11) has the correct signs. Its
three terms are respectively a positive semidefinite quadratic form,
negative multiples of native feasible inequalities, and positive
polyhedral slacks. They prove global optimality of the supplied feasible
point. No Slater test, affine-hull calculation, or minimum-norm test is
needed by the verifier. An unnecessary exposing record cannot make the
certificate unsound if its local identities are checked.

The word *facial* should be interpreted with care. The successive sets are
polyhedral sections containing the feasible set; they need not be faces of
the current polyhedron. For example, `P = R` and `g(x) = x^2` produce the
section `{0}`, which is not a face of `R`. This terminology issue does not
affect the proof.

## 2. Completeness, support, and the reduction count

On the current affine hull, a native row with zero restricted Hessian is
identical to its already-present tangent. Discarding that row from the
nonlinear Slater test is therefore justified. If the remaining rows are
strictly feasible, relative Slater and the polyhedral normal cone give the
final KKT record. If there are no remaining rows, ordinary polyhedral
first-order optimality gives the same record.

If strict feasibility fails, convex separation gives a normalized
nonnegative aggregate nonnegative throughout the current polyhedron. Its
value at the feasible point is zero. The aggregate therefore admits
stationarity with active polyhedral normals. The multiplier equations
have `n+1` rows, including normalization. A nonnegative solution of minimum
support has independent columns and at most `n+1` positive coordinates.
A nonsingular subsystem expresses those coordinates over `K` by Cramer's
rule. The same argument with only the `n` stationarity equations proves
the final support bound `n`.

The restriction to native rows with nonzero restricted Hessians is
necessary for the stated descent argument. The manuscript makes this
restriction before choosing the basic solution. A normalized positive
combination of such positive semidefinite Hessians is nonzero. Restricting
to the next section kills that nonzero member of the current Hessian span.
The restriction map on the span therefore has a nontrivial kernel, and
its image dimension falls by at least one. This proves the bound of `h`
curved records even when one record removes several dimensions.

There is no hidden field extension: every linear system uses gradients at
the same primal point and previously generated coefficients in its field.

## 3. Height accounting

A potentially incorrect argument would repeatedly bound arithmetic by
the degree of the number field. A factor polynomial in the field degree
at every reduction could yield only an exponent quadratic in `h`.
The manuscript avoids that argument. It tracks absolute logarithmic Weil
height through the reductions and converts to a power-basis encoding only
once at the end.

The determinant estimate used in the manuscript is valid. In fact, the
slightly sharper bound

\[
 H(\det M)\le r^2 B+\log(r!)
\]

holds when every entry of the `r` by `r` matrix has height at most `B`.
The determinant polynomial has partial degree at most one in each of its
`r^2` entries and coefficient length `r!`. Apply the standard polynomial
evaluation bound for absolute height. The manuscript's `r^3 B` estimate
is a harmless weakening. The needed evaluation bound, together with
`H(a^{-1})=H(a)`, is stated in
[Min Sha, Section 2.1 and Lemma 2.1, printed page 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.07147).
The primary PDF was inspected during this review.

Cramer quotients, evaluated gradients, and the appended linear equations
therefore cost a factor polynomial in the original input dimension per
round, independent of the number-field degree. At most `h` rounds preserve
the asserted height order `N^{O(h+1)}`. The number of rows is polynomial in
`N`, since `h <= N`.

For the final conversion, the Vandermonde system over the embeddings of
`K` has dimension `D`. Its coefficient heights are polynomial in `D` and
the generator height. Cramer's rule and invariance of absolute height
under conjugation give rational power-basis coefficients of height
polynomial in `D`, the generator height, and the element height. Working
in a normal closure for this estimate does not introduce its degree into
absolute-height inequalities. Applying this conversion once proves the
claimed total bit bound. This is an existence argument; it need not
construct all embeddings.

Exact verification at one isolated real root uses only univariate
arithmetic. The claim would not follow merely from separate coordinate
polynomials and unrelated isolating intervals. The common-field format
is a substantive part of the theorem.

## 4. Infeasibility and its attainment hypothesis

If the polyhedron is nonempty and the native system is infeasible, every
value of `max_i q_i` on the polyhedron is strictly positive. Its infimum
is finite and nonnegative, but strict positivity of the infimum requires
attainment. The epigraph program has affine objective and positive
semidefinite quadratic constraint Hessians, so
[Luo--Zhang, Corollary 2, printed pages 6--7](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/97122.pdf)
applies. The primary statement explicitly assumes positive semidefinite
objective and constraint Hessians and a finite lower bound. It provides
the missing attainment step and hence a positive attained value.

The epigraph admits relative Slater: choose a point in the relative
interior of the nonempty polyhedron and then choose its epigraph coordinate
sufficiently large. Strictness of every listed affine inequality is not
needed. The manuscript's original phrase choosing any point in the
polyhedron was replaced by this explicit relative-interior choice. The
corrected text was independently rechecked.

The epigraph canonical optimizer lies in a field satisfying the stated
degree bound. Its active KKT system has `n+1` equations, including the
epigraph stationarity equation `sum lambda = 1`. A basic multiplier
solution lies in the same field. Expanding its stationary quadratic gives
manuscript equation (19), whose right-hand side is strictly positive on
the polyhedron. This proves infeasibility without facial reductions.

## 5. Exact stress examples and limits

For a two-step singular example, take

\[
 q_1=x_1^2-x_2\le0,\qquad q_2=x_2^2\le0,
 \qquad\min x_1.
\]

The feasible set is `{0}`. Ordinary native KKT stationarity fails there:
the available constraint gradients are `(0,-1)` and zero, whereas the
objective gradient is `(1,0)`. The certificate first uses `g=q_2` to
append `x_2=0`. It then uses `g=q_1`, with the normal of that equality,
to append `x_1=0`. The final linear objective is certified by the latter
equality. Each record is checked by direct exact expansion.

The chain `q_i=x_i^2-x_{i+1}` for `i<h`, followed by `q_h=x_h^2`,
with objective `min x_1`, shows why this reduction scheme can need all
`h` steps. At zero, its
initial native tangents require `x_2,...,x_h >= 0`. A nonnegative
aggregate minimized there cannot use any of the first `h-1` rows:
its derivative in the allowed positive `x_{i+1}` direction would be
negative. Thus the first curved aggregate uses only the last row.
After its variable is fixed to zero, the same argument repeats. This is
a sharp example for the recorded reduction mechanism, not a novelty claim
about classical singularity chains.

Original native multiplier support cannot be bounded by `h` alone. For
any integer `m >= 3`, let

\[
 P=\{x\in\mathbb R^m:\sum_jx_j=1\},\qquad
 q_i(x)=\|x-e_i\|^2-r^2,
 \quad
 \frac{m-2}{m-1}\le r^2<\frac{m-1}{m}.
\]

All native Hessians are `2I`, so `h=1`. Averaging their values gives

\[
 \frac1m\sum_i q_i(x)
 =\|x\|^2+1-\frac2m-r^2
 \ge\frac{m-1}{m}-r^2>0\qquad(x\in P).
\]

The full system is infeasible. Every proper subsystem is feasible: for
a subset of `s <= m-1` indices, use the average of its `s` centers.
Its squared distance to each included center is `1-1/s`, at most
`(m-2)/(m-1)`. Therefore any positive aggregate infeasibility certificate
must use all `m` native rows. The theorem's dimension-dependent support
bound is consistent with this example. The smaller support internal to
the optimizer-degree proof does not transfer to the original rows.

The manuscript uses a simpler variant with `P = R^n`, `m = n`, and
`r^2 = (n-2)/(n-1)`. Completing the square in the averaged quadratic
proves positivity on all of `R^n`, so the hyperplane constraint above is
unnecessary. I independently rechecked this variant, including `n=2`.

## 6. Verification scope

The review used complete symbolic proof reading, exact hand calculations
for the two examples, and direct primary-source checks for attainment and
height arithmetic. It did not run a project-wide test suite, inspect CI,
perform numerical experiments, or use Lean. A targeted `python -` command
checked this review's final newline, trailing whitespace, control
characters, paired display and inline math delimiters, and its one local
link; it passed. Positive review is additional evidence, not a formal proof
assistant guarantee. Broader novelty comparisons belong to the separate
prior-art audit.
