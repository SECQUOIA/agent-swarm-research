\begin{abstract}
Many nonconvex mixed-integer optimization problems have objectives that are
sums of local terms whose interaction graph has small treewidth. Small
treewidth alone does not make such problems tractable: box-constrained
nonconvex quadratic programming is strongly NP-hard at treewidth two. We
identify checkable quantities that make tree decompositions useful for
certified global optimization, and we show where they stop working. If $L_i$
bounds the curvature of the objective along coordinate $i$, subtracting
$L_iw^2/8$ at every grid node, where $w$ is the largest adjacent grid interval,
turns exact dynamic programming over a product grid into a valid lower bound
for the continuous mixed-integer problem; the bound equals the best vertex
bound over all grid cells but needs no enumeration of cells. Coordinate
min-marginals of the corrected problem discard intervals that cannot contain
an improving point, and the grids of the successive stages form a certificate
that is checked by recomputation. If the minimizer is unique and the objective
grows quadratically away from it, grids graded around the current corrected
minimizer and filtered in this way keep $O(\sqrt{\bar\kappa}\log n)$ nodes per
coordinate at every accuracy, where $\bar\kappa$ is a scale-invariant ratio of
curvature to growth. For explicit rational mixed-integer quadratic programs on
a box with a tree decomposition of bag size $p$, this gives certified
$2^{-q}$-approximation in $f(p,\bar\kappa)(I+q)^{O(1)}$ bit operations and the
exact minimizer in $f(p,\kappa)I^{O(1)}$, where $I$ is the input length; the
growth constant need not be known. We show that the dependence on $\kappa$
cannot be polylogarithmic unless P${}={}$NP and that its exponent must grow
linearly with $p$ under the randomized exponential-time hypothesis. Exact
recourse lowers the curvature that must be discretized: private convex blocks
with certified piecewise-affine responses, convex value factors, minimum-cut
oracles for separately concave submodular residuals, and cut-based grid
oracles for balanced quadratics all fit the framework, whereas bag-local
corrections of grid messages are invalid. Totally unimodular coupling
constraints admit exactly feasible correlated rounding with polynomial
complexity for fixed width, the Bellman residuals of an endpoint program
describe all minimizers of coordinatewise concave quadratics, and
explicit families show exponentially large exact messages at bounded
conditioning, the failure of point localization under set growth, and the
limits of local moment matching. An exact-arithmetic implementation with an
independent certificate checker exhibits the predicted accuracy-independent
grid sizes.
\end{abstract}
