\begin{abstract}
Many nonconvex mixed-integer problems have objectives that are sums of local
terms with an interaction graph of small treewidth. Small treewidth alone is
not enough: box-constrained nonconvex quadratic programming is strongly
NP-hard at treewidth two. Two quantities make tree decompositions useful for
certified global optimization: upper bounds $L_i$ on the curvature along each
coordinate, and quadratic growth. Subtracting $L_iw^2/8$ at each grid node,
where $w$ is the length of the longest adjacent grid interval, turns exact dynamic
programming over a product grid into a valid lower bound. Coordinate
min-marginals then discard intervals that cannot contain an improving point, and the stage
grids form a certificate that is checked by recomputation and needs no growth
assumption. Under growth in the norm weighted by the $L_i$, filtered grids
graded around the current corrected minimizer keep
$O(\sqrt{\bar\kappa}\log(n+2))$ nodes per coordinate at every accuracy, where
$\bar\kappa$ is a scale-invariant ratio of curvature to growth. For rational
mixed-integer quadratic programs on a box, with a tree decomposition of bag
size $p$ and input length $I$, this gives a certified $2^{-q}$-approximation
in $f(p,\bar\kappa)(I+q+1)^5$ bit operations and, under growth at a unique
minimizer, the exact minimizer in $f_1(p,\kappa)I^{O(1)}$ with the Euclidean ratio
$\kappa\ge\bar\kappa$, without knowledge of the growth constant. Unless
P${}={}$NP, neither parameter can be dropped and the dependence on $\kappa$
cannot be polylogarithmic; under the randomized exponential-time hypothesis
the exponent of $\kappa$ cannot be $o(p)$. We extend the method to exact
partial minimization, totally unimodular coupling constraints and several
minimizers, and test an exact-arithmetic implementation with an independent
checker.
\end{abstract}
