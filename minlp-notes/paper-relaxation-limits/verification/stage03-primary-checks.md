# Stage 3 direct primary-source checks

Date: 2026-09-05. This records the author's actual source inspection, supplementing the coordinator's records. Relevant portions were checked; no claim is made to have audited every theorem in the cited books or papers. This is not an exhaustive priority survey.

- Cornuéjols, July 2000 author manuscript, *Combinatorial Optimization: Packing and Covering*: read the original extracted passages and visually inspected printed p.76, Theorem 6.5. Its rectangular Eulerian-submatrix criterion is the version of Camion used in `thm:width-two-gap`. Read printed p.82, Theorem 6.13 for mixed packing/equality/covering integrality of balanced matrices, including its unit right-hand sides and unit bounds. The latter is used only for the separate monomial argument. Original: https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/gc0v/webpub/notes.pdf.

- Hassin–Tamir, original author scan: visually read printed p.381, Theorem 3.1 and the series/parallel terminal recursion, and the printed p.379 title and journal metadata. The decomposition theorem is credited by that paper to Dirac and Duffin. The exact source title is *Efficient Algorithms for Optimization and Selection on Series-Parallel Graphs*, SIAM Journal on Algebraic and Discrete Methods 7(3), 379–389 (1986), DOI https://doi.org/10.1137/0607043. Original: https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~hassin/sp.pdf. The paper-local proof supplies the full coloring invariant beyond this classical decomposition input.

- Barrus, *On fractional realizations of graph degree sequences*, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 21(2), P2.18 (2014): opened the original PDF and read Theorem 2.1 and its setup concerning fractional realizations and vertex-disjoint odd cycles. Its fixed-degree graphic statement is related background. The manuscript separately proves the required slab form with dummy and parallel edges and does not silently apply the source outside its statement. Original: https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v21i2p18/pdf.

- Edmonds, *Maximum Matching and a Polyhedron with 0,1-Vertices*, Journal of Research of the National Bureau of Standards 69B, 125–130 (1965): opened the NIST original and read its abstract and Section 1 weighted-matching statement. The established polynomial weighted-matching primitive is cited; both objective-specific reductions are proved in the manuscript. Original DOI: https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.069B.013.

- Deza–Onn, *Optimization over degree sequences*: read Theorem 1.2 and the complete Section 3 matching construction in the primary author manuscript https://arxiv.org/html/1908.09278v1. This confirms attribution of separable convex degree optimization to an established matching reduction. The manuscript proves its specific private/parallel edge and signed-cost gadget. Publisher metadata verified: Discrete Applied Mathematics 296, 2–8 (2021); DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2019.12.016. An initially incorrect DOI was corrected before the final build.

- Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver, *Geometric Algorithms and Combinatorial Optimization*, first edition (1988): downloaded the complete author-hosted original and inspected the definition of well-described rational polyhedra and Theorem 6.4.9, printed p.179, with the relevant separation-to-optimization proof. It covers rational polyhedra without an implicit full-dimensionality assumption. The manuscript gives its coefficient/bit bounds and cites this exact theorem for the remaining classical oracle equivalence. Original: https://www.zib.de/userpage/groetschel/pubnew/paper/groetschellovaszschrijver1988.pdf. The browser could not process the 37 MB document, but a direct open download and text extraction succeeded; this is a resolved size limitation, not an access-control bypass. Publisher first-edition DOI is https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-97881-4; the initially selected later-edition DOI was corrected.

- Adams–Gupte–Xu, *Error bounds for monomial convexification in polynomial optimization*: read the local primary fulltext and visually inspected the original manuscript PDF p.22, Proposition 4.1. This confirms the classical common-ratio positive-product and elementary-symmetric envelope formulas and their stated antecedents. The manuscript proves the particular cardinality and common-aspect formulas it needs. Bibliography uses the published issue year 2019, Mathematical Programming 175, 355–393, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-018-1246-8; the local package's 2018 date is the online publication year.

- Del Pia–Khajavirad, *The multilinear polytope for acyclic hypergraphs*: read the local primary fulltext, Section 3, Theorem 7 and proof. The scalar forest gluing proof is supplied independently; this source is contextual polyhedral attribution rather than a substitute for the manuscript's exact scalar claim. Published metadata: SIAM Journal on Optimization 28(2), 1049–1076 (2018), DOI https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1095998.

- Karp, *Reducibility among Combinatorial Problems* (1972): opened the primary authorized reprint. Its scanned text is not available through the browser extraction. The classical PARTITION completeness input is cited with its original proceedings metadata, pp.85–103, DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9. The narrow-positive-box reduction, remainder and NP certificate are proved completely in the manuscript; no unsupported stronger classification of PARTITION or of this reduction is inferred. The coordinator separately checked the original PARTITION statement and reduction. This record does not claim that the author independently read the entire scanned reprint.

- Altschuler–Boix-Adserà, *Polynomial-time algorithms for multimarginal optimal transport problems with structure*: read the local primary text's Section 2 rational-entry convention and Section 7.2 low-rank definition, Theorem 7.4, the subsequent accuracy question and Corollary 7.5; visually inspected the original manuscript's PDF p.55 containing the question. The historical question concerns logarithmic inverse-accuracy dependence for constant-rank costs and the weighted dual oracle. The present reduction states its rational-bit scope, explicit cost factorization and padding, and does not prohibit fixed-accuracy approximation or unrestricted unit-cost real arithmetic. Published metadata: Mathematical Programming 199, 1107–1178 (2023), DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-022-01868-7. The source confirms the historical question; it does not establish the absence of a later answer.
