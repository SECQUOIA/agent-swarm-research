\title{Convex relaxation gaps and spatial certificates in nonlinear optimization}
\author{}

\begin{abstract}
Spatial branch-and-bound methods relax each nonlinear term separately and
then subdivide the domain. We quantify two limits of this approach. First,
using probability laws on box vertices, we compare the termwise gap $T$,
the sum of the exact envelope widths of the terms, with the envelope gap
$H$ of their sum. For signed bilinear functions, the worst ratio $T/H$ on a
graph lies between $\max\{1,\sqrt{\rho_G/(2\log2)}\}$ and
$4\sqrt{\rho_G}$, where $\rho_G$ is the maximum induced edge density; the
order also follows from Schur-multiplier bounds of Davidson and Donsig.
For positive multilinear functions on nonnegative boxes, the worst ratio
grows as $\log d/\log\log d$ in the degree $d$, disproving a conjectured
constant bound of Luedtke, Namazifar and Linderoth; for cubics it lies
between $1610000/743033>2.166$ and $31/12$. With $f$ feedback variables
the ratio is at most $2^f$; on the unit cube, frequency two with odd
girth $g$ gives the sharp constant $g/(g-1)$, and incidence treewidth two
gives exactly two. On positive boxes with aspect ratios at most $\rho_B$
the worst ratio lies between $\max\{2,\rho_B\}$ and $\rho_B+2$, and a
threshold test for the exact midpoint envelope of one product is
NP-complete, even on narrow boxes with unequal sides. Second, under
specified node oracles and at fixed tolerance, certificates by
coordinate-product regions need exponentially many regions: for an
explicitly solved fractional-cardinality quadratic with chord (McCormick,
$\alpha$BB), SDP--RLT or box-preordering bounds of order up to a constant
multiple of $n$, and for random cubic XOR objectives even with the exact
quadratic moment hull of every region and after bounded monomial
reformulation. Certificates with $2^{O(n)}$ regions exist in both cases.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Global solvers for nonconvex factorable problems bound each nonlinear term
by a convex relaxation, starting with McCormick's recursive
convexification \citep{McCormick1976}, and refine these bounds by
branching on coordinates. We study two limits of that design: the loss from
convexifying terms separately, since a relaxation can be exact for every
term and still ignore that the terms share variables; and the number of
regions a specified spatial bounding rule must certify to prove a desired
tolerance.

For a scalar function $f$ on a box $B$, write
$H_B(f;x)=\cav_B f(x)-\vex_B f(x)$ for the width of its graph hull. For a
specified decomposition $f=\sum_e f_e$, write
$T_B(f;x)=\sum_e[\cav_B f_e(x)-\vex_B f_e(x)]$ for the width obtained by
giving each term its exact graph hull. Always $T_B\ge H_B\ge0$, and only
$T_B$ depends on the decomposition. We study the ratio $T_B/H_B$ where
$H_B>0$. It compares pointwise widths; it is not an approximation ratio for
a constrained problem and by itself implies no lower bound on a spatial
tree (Section~\ref{subsec:certificate-model}).

\paragraph{Prior work on envelope gaps.}
For multiaffine functions, both envelopes are extrema of expectations over
probability laws on box vertices with the prescribed means; this
representation and earlier gap comparisons appear in \citet{Luedtke2012}.
Luedtke, Namazifar and Linderoth bounded the positive bilinear ratio by a
chromatic-number expression at most two, and asked whether positive
multilinear functions on nonnegative boxes admit a uniform constant
(Conjecture~1 of their author manuscript). \citet{Boland2017} related the
signed bilinear gap to cut ranges and showed that the worst ratio in $n$
variables grows as $\sqrt n$, with upper bound $600\sqrt n$ for every
bilinear function in $n$ variables. \citet{Sherali1997} gave envelope
formulas for symmetric multilinear functions, \citet{AdamsGupteXu2019}
error bounds for single monomials on boxes, and
\citet{DelPiaKhajavirad2018} exactness of the standard linearization on
Berge-acyclic hypergraphs.

\paragraph{Prior work on spatial methods.}
\citet{Jarre2018} gave an exponential branch-and-bound lower bound for a
binary knapsack with a semidefinite bound, and \citet{Coniglio2026} a
related spatial obstruction under midpoint branching on symmetric
coordinate domains. Our moment constructions build on the
fractional-knapsack lower bounds of \citet{Grigoriev2001} and
\citet{Potechin2019} and on the signed XOR construction of
\citet{Schoenebeck2008}. Convergence analyses of spatial branch-and-bound
study the cluster problem: near a nondegenerate minimizer, the number of
boxes that cannot be fathomed depends on the convergence order and
prefactor of the lower bounds
\citep{DuKearfott1994,WechsungEtAl2014,KannanBarton2017}. Termination
results guarantee finite trees at every positive tolerance, as in the
disjunctive sum-of-squares framework of \citet{AhmadiEtAl2026}; such
results allow exponentially many regions. For feasibility-based bound
tightening (FBBT), \citet{BelottiEtAl2012} showed that, when the limiting
box is nonempty, linear FBBT limits can be computed by an LP although
primitive iteration need not terminate.

\paragraph{Simultaneous envelopes.}
The common tool is Lemma~\ref{lem:vertex-law}: $\vex f$ needs \emph{one}
law for the whole sum, whereas $T$ lets every term choose its own law.
Our main gap results are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Signed bilinear functions}
(Section~\ref{sec:bilinear}). For every graph $G$, the worst ratio over
real coefficients satisfies
$\max\{1,\sqrt{\rho_G/(2\log2)}\}\le\Gamma(G)\le4\sqrt{\rho_G}$, where
$\rho_G$ is the maximum edge-to-vertex ratio of an induced subgraph
(Theorem~\ref{thm:graph-density}). Thus bounded signed gaps on a graph
family are equivalent to bounded induced density. For every bilinear
function in $n$ variables our bound gives $2\sqrt{n-1}$, compared with
$600\sqrt n$ in \citet[Theorem 2]{Boland2017}. The upper order also
follows from Schur-multiplier bounds of \citet{DavidsonDonsig2007}, as we
show in Section~\ref{subsec:schur}; our proof is elementary and gives the
constant four.
\item \emph{Positive multilinear functions}
(Section~\ref{sec:positive-universal}). The worst ratios in degree $d$ and
in dimension $n$ satisfy $R_d\sim\log d/\log\log d$ and
$C_n\sim\log n/\log\log n$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-growth}). The lower
bound is an exact multiscale family with $T=L$ and $H=O(\log L)$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:dyadic-exact}); it disproves the conjecture of Luedtke
et al. The upper bound uses one mixture of rounding laws, built from the
means and the degree bound alone, that serves all terms at once; we give
explicit finite bounds (Theorem~\ref{thm:harmonic-fixed-point}). Unit
coefficients and interior points do not change $R_d$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:coefficient-removal}).
\item \emph{Cubics and equal means} (Sections~\ref{sec:cubic}
and~\ref{sec:equal-means}). We prove $1610000/743033\le R_3\le31/12$, so
degree three already exceeds the positive bilinear constant two
(Theorems~\ref{thm:cubic-upper} and~\ref{thm:cubic-analytic}). An
explicit homogeneous example with 52 variables and unit coefficients has
ratio above two (Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-two-level}). At equal means,
Sherali's symmetric envelopes give an exact formula whose supremum is at
most two (Theorem~\ref{thm:equal-finite}, Corollary~\ref{cor:equal-infinite}),
so on the unit cube ratios above two require unequal means.
\item \emph{Incidence orientation and marginal floors}
(Section~\ref{sec:incidence}). On the unit cube, an incidence orientation
with variable outdegree $r$ and factor outdegree $s$ gives
$T\le[r+\min\{s,R_{s+1}\}+\kappa]H$ with $\kappa=2/(1-e^{-1})$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:incidence-orientation}); the worst ratios under
incidence treewidth, degeneracy, or orientation parameter $k$ are all
asymptotic to $k$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:structural-growth}). A lower bound
$\delta$ on all normalized means gives growth
$\log(1/\delta)/\log\log(1/\delta)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:marginal-floor}).
\item \emph{Feedback, frequency two and width two}
(Sections~\ref{sec:frequency} and~\ref{sec:width-two}). If deleting $f_F$
variables leaves an incidence forest, $T\le2^{f_F}H$ on every nonnegative
box (Theorem~\ref{thm:feedback}); $f_F=0$ recovers acyclic exactness, and
$f_F=1$ is sharp. For positive monomials on the unit cube with variable
frequency at most two and odd girth $g$ of the dual graph,
$T\le\frac{g}{g-1}H$, and $T=H$ (bipartite exactness) when the dual graph
is bipartite; these constants are sharp and the frequency-two supremum is
$3/2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:frequency-two}). The same holds for convex
cardinality factors (Theorem~\ref{thm:cardinality-frequency}), and the
scalar envelopes of these classes are computable in polynomial time
(Corollary~\ref{cor:scalar-envelope-algorithm}). For incidence treewidth
two the exact constant is two on unit cubes and zero-lower boxes, and for
common-aspect products (Theorem~\ref{thm:width-two-gap}). Its proof
partitions the factors into two classes with totally unimodular incidence
matrices (Theorem~\ref{thm:one-sided-coloring}).
\item \emph{Positive physical boxes and complexity}
(Sections~\ref{sec:positive-boxes} and~\ref{sec:single-product}). On
strictly positive boxes with aspect ratios at most $\rho_B$, the worst
ratio satisfies $\max\{2,\rho_B\}\le C_{\rm box}(\rho_B)\le\rho_B+2$, with
upper bound $\rho_B+\beta_N$, $\beta_N<2$, in $N$ coordinates
(Theorems~\ref{thm:positive-box-upper} and~\ref{thm:positive-box-lower}).
Bipartite exactness fails on unequal boxes
(Example~\ref{ex:unequal-seven-six}). For a single product, where $T=H$,
deciding whether the exact convex envelope at the box midpoint is at most
$q$ is NP-complete, even on arbitrarily narrow unequal boxes
(Theorem~\ref{thm:single-product-hardness}); a fixed number of distinct
aspect ratios is tractable (Proposition~\ref{prop:ratio-types}). The
reduction also answers, negatively under P${}\ne{}$NP, the
logarithmic-accuracy question of \citet{AltschulerBoixAdsera2023} for
rank-one multimarginal transport (Corollary~\ref{cor:rank-one-precision}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Spatial certificates.}
A certificate is a finite cover of the feasible set by regions whose oracle
bounds meet a target; a lower bound on its size applies to every complete
coordinate split tree, whatever its split points and branching rules. The fractional-cardinality problems are solved
explicitly, so their bounds measure certificate size rather than the
difficulty of finding an optimizer. Each proof constructs, in every region
that restricts few coordinates, a feasible solution of the node relaxation
with a low objective value, and then counts how many witness points one
certified region can contain.
\begin{enumerate}\setcounter{enumi}{6}
\item \emph{Fractional cardinality} (Sections~\ref{sec:cardinality-spatial}
to~\ref{sec:relative-blocks}). Minimize $\sum_ix_i(1-x_i)$ over
$[0,1]^n$ with $\sum_ix_i=k+\tfrac12$. Covers certified by chord bounds,
which include the McCormick and $\alpha$BB underestimators, need
$(3/2)^{n/24}$ boxes at absolute tolerance $1/8$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:chord-cover}); $2^{n+2}-3$ nodes suffice
(Proposition~\ref{prop:chord-upper}). The bound extends to SDP--RLT
(Theorem~\ref{thm:sdp-cover}) and to the full box preordering of order
$r$, with exponential size for $r$ up to a constant multiple of $n$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:preordering-cover}). It further extends to arbitrary coordinate domains
(Theorem~\ref{thm:product-domain-cover}) and to coordinatewise graph
lifts with no loss in order (Theorem~\ref{thm:local-graph-lift}). Unique
optimizers without symmetry (Corollary~\ref{cor:unique-cardinality}) and
fixed relative tolerance, via independent blocks, are also covered
(Theorem~\ref{thm:relative-cover}). For instance, full global SDP--RLT at
relative tolerance $1/32$ needs $(3/2)^{17n/384}$ regions. Padberg's
clique inequality closes this family at the root
(Proposition~\ref{prop:clique-escape}).
\item \emph{Signed cubic XOR} (Sections~\ref{sec:xor-quadratic}
to~\ref{sec:xor-upper-affine}). For bounded-occurrence cubic XOR
objectives on $[-1,1]^n$ obtained from random instances, with
$\OPT\ge1/8$, certificates at absolute tolerance $1/16$ or relative
tolerance $1/2$ need at least $2^{7n/1024}$ regions. This holds at every
order $r\ge2$ up to a constant multiple of $n$, even when the node oracle
also requires the first and second moments to lie in the exact convex hull
of $\{(x,xx^{\mathsf T}):x\in S\}$ for each region $S$
(Theorems~\ref{thm:xor-transfer} and~\ref{thm:xor-family}). After
reformulation with signed monomials of degree at most $D$ as new
coordinates, the bound is $2^{7n/(1024D)}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:monomial-transfer}, Corollary~\ref{cor:degree-regions}).
A quadratic formulation with a linear objective needs $2^{7n/3072}$
regions already at order one
(Corollary~\ref{cor:xor-quadratic-formulation}). Grid certificates with
$48^n$ regions give upper bounds of the same exponential order
(Propositions~\ref{prop:xor-bernstein-upper}
and~\ref{prop:xor-order-one-upper}).
\end{enumerate}
These bounds hold at a fixed tolerance and grow with dimension. They are
lower bounds for every cover, obtained by counting witnesses. They differ
from the cluster-problem analyses, which bound the number of boxes near a
nondegenerate minimizer as the tolerance shrinks; here the chord error is
already quadratic in the box width \eqref{eq:second-order-chord}. Affine branching, additional
coupled cuts, and methods that combine separate component certificates
are outside the stated oracles; for affine branching we show only that our
proof technique fails (Propositions~\ref{prop:affine-quadratic-obstruction}
and~\ref{prop:affine-substitution-obstruction}).
