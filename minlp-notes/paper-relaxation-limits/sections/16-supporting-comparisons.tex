\section{Representation and other computational resources}\label{sec:comparisons}
The preceding lower bounds count regions under a specified node oracle.
Other resources need different statements: global lift size counts cone
factors or matrix order, integer precision counts discrete coordinates, and
bound propagation concerns a limit or a sequence of local updates. None of
these counts can replace a count of certified spatial regions.

Several short geometric comparisons show why the representation must be
fixed. Appendix~\ref{app:point-packing} derives the four known
point-packing RLT and SDP values, including symmetry-adjusted secants and
attaining covariances. This benchmark shows the gain from moment
consistency and the care needed when the underlying box changes.
Appendix~\ref{app:scaling} gives exact scale and multiplicity hulls by
classical disaggregation, with a retained common intensive variable.
It separates this hull identity from cost separation, which needs
additional structure. Appendix~\ref{app:p-split} gives a retained-domain
counterexample to the universal nonexactness assertion in the published
P-split theorem, a sufficient directional repair, and exact translated-ball
projection and distance formulas. A rational change of coordinates can turn
a growing gap into a bounded
one, and in aligned coordinates the hull of the exact auxiliary image can be
exact. These claims concern the stated epigraph model and retained domain.

Appendix~\ref{app:rank-one} exhibits a stable correlation-polytope face
inside the unit-capacity rank-one hull. Known correlation-polytope lower
bounds transfer to exact conic lifts, and
an explicit stability estimate transfers them to entrywise-$\ell_1$
approximations of accuracy of order $m^{-2}$. The exponential bound for
fixed PSD block size and the superpolynomial bound for unrestricted PSD
lifts are separate conclusions. Appendix~\ref{app:fbbt}
transfers least monotone fixed points to fair forward FBBT, proves
PosSLP-hard constant-error approximation of the limiting lower bound,
and gives a system that needs doubly exponentially many primitive updates
under every update schedule; fairness is needed only for convergence. The
restriction to primitive contractors is essential.
Finally, Appendix~\ref{app:integer-precision} contrasts scalar and
simultaneous-bilinear approximation accuracy with spatial certification.
It gives short midpoint and cover proofs and does not treat the broader
theory of mixed-integer representability.
