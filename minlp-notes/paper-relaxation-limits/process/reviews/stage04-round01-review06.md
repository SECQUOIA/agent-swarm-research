**Independent verification**

1. **Optimum, counting, upper certificates, and tolerance.** A feasible-slice vertex has one half coordinate and k unit coordinates, hence penalty 1/4. The count uses marginal avoidance probabilities, not independence of the two endpoint events. From `|A|+|D| >= |R|`, taking the larger marginal avoidance base and then its reciprocal gives the stated minimum of the two exponential bounds. Empty endpoint classes, impossible avoidance, overlapping covers, and zero thresholds are handled correctly. Chord domination follows from convexity between actual endpoints, including singleton intervals. The upper tree adds four nodes per surviving prefix; its counts are `2^(n+2)-3` and `2^(n+1)-1`. The exact midpoint recurrence starts at `(k+1,n-k)` and gives `binomial(n+1,k+1)` leaves. The tolerance optimum is `1/4-delta^2` precisely in the stated `0 <= delta < 1/2` range, and the target must remain positive. Certified removed regions must be included in the cover; closure of an open removed slab is not silently assumed.

2. **Complete PSD and every RLT pair.** With `c=t/s` and `d=t(t-1)/(s(s-1))`, the identities

   `c+(s-1)d=tc`, `d-c^2=-(c-d)/s`, and `c-d=t(s-t)/(s(s-1))`

   give covariance `(c-d)(I-J/s)` on free coordinates and zero covariance on every restricted row and column. The positive constant entry in the augmented matrix makes this sufficient for full augmented PSD. For distinct free indices the four RLT expressions are `d,c-d,c-d,(s-t)(s-t-1)/(s(s-1))`; for repeated free indices they are `c,0,0,1-c`. If either coordinate is restricted, the relevant slack product factors through its deterministic slack. This includes repeated restricted coordinates and singleton intervals. Every equality-product row has sum `K mu_i`. The objective is exactly the restricted middle-coordinate penalty. The diagonal mixed RLT constraint implies chord domination.

3. **Affine closure and order one.** On a nonempty box/balance polytope, affine LP duality represents a valid affine slack as a nonnegative constant plus nonnegative bound-slack coefficients plus a multiple of the balance. No strict-feasibility hypothesis is needed, including for degenerate boxes. Expanding two such representations uses first moments, all pairwise slack products, and `L[e v]=0` for affine v, including `v=e`. At higher order the same expansion with the permitted square stays within degree 2r. Constant affine functions can use their constant representations. This closure concerns original-coordinate affine inequalities; it does not include the lifted clique cut. At order one, affine-square positivity, the first-moment bounds, pairwise slacks, and affine balance products are exactly the displayed SDP–RLT model.

4. **Higher moments and localizers.** The cardinality recurrence is valid through multiplier degree `2d-1 <= s-1`, so it does not request an undefined denominator. Homogenization replaces a degree-a monomial by its degree-d completion with a positive denominator; its difference is the balance times a multiplier of degree at most d-1 in the Boolean quotient. Thus the comparison of squares uses equality multiplier degree at most 2d-1. In the Gram formula, falling-factorial Vandermonde gives the required entry without cancelling a possibly zero numerator. All coefficients are nonnegative in the stated sufficient range. For localizers, conflicting assignments vanish and repeated factors reduce to one assignment indicator. The uncancelled indicator formula gives a nonnegative weight. A nonconstant original square permits at most 2r-2 assigned coordinates, making this weight strictly positive; constant, zero-weight, and all-assigned cases need no invalid conditional denominator. Conditioning leaves `s-v >= 2d` and both residual cardinalities at least `2d-1`. The square may couple all remaining coordinates. The integer exclusion count leaves at least 2r-1 witnesses of both endpoint types, giving exactly q_r. The strict below-target estimate includes `R` empty, and positive q_r is properly distinguished from a linear q_r.
