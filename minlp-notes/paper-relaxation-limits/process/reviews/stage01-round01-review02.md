**Independent verification**

All derivations below are mathematical checks. The separate finite computation is identified explicitly at the end.

- **Envelope and deficiency identities.** Independent endpoint sampling represents each multiaffine graph point as a convex combination of endpoint graph points. Hence optimization over vertex laws gives the two envelopes. At a unit-cube mean, a monomial has upper probability `u=min_i x_i` and lower probability `max(0,u-S)`, so its width is `min(u,S)`. The common threshold attains every upper probability at once. Therefore `H=max_P sum_e a_e E_P D_e`, with one common admissible law. Independence gives `u(1-product_j x_j)`. Two low coordinates give at least `u/2`; all-high terms give at least `(1-exp(-1))min(u,S)/2`. This confirms both the inequality direction and the factor two in the preliminary rounding lemma. Affine terms must be discarded as directed before the displayed formula for the absolute upper envelope.

- **Box transfer.** For each transformed original monomial `g=sum_q g_q`, `vex g >= sum_q vex g_q` and `cav g <= sum_q cav g_q`. Thus its width is at most the sum of expanded widths, establishing `T_original <= T_expanded`. The whole graph hull is transported by the affine bijection. Nonnegative expansion coefficients permit one threshold law to attain all expanded upper envelopes simultaneously. Merging duplicate expanded supports also preserves their total width because the merged coefficients have the same sign. The manuscript correctly avoids claiming preservation of factor incidence or equality with a recursive relaxation on a positive box.

- **Induced cuts and extension off the half grid.** For a half-valued set `W`, `X=(1+s)/2` and zero singleton sign means yield `E b=(sum a+E Q)/4`. Symmetrizing an extremizing sign vector with its negative gives both extrema, hence `H=(max Q-min Q)/4=R/2`, while `T=L/2`. Fixed-one coordinates add an affine function on `W`. In the cell arrangement, active equalities and complementations either propagate a fixed coordinate, force one half through an odd complementation cycle, or leave a perturbable parameter. This proves the asserted half-integral vertex property. Since `H` is concave and `T` is affine on each cell, `T-cH` is convex, with the required inequality direction at convex combinations of cell vertices.

- **Cut polarization and row constants.** With `Q(s)=s^TAs/2`, `u=(s+t)/2`, and `v=(s-t)/2`, direct expansion gives `Q(s)-Q(t)=2v^TAu`. The two supports are complementary, so `R=max_S ||A_(S,S^c)||_(infinity->1)` with no missing factor two. The real Khinchin factor is `1/sqrt(2)`. At a locally maximal squared-weight cut, every crossing row norm is at least its full row norm divided by `sqrt(2)`. Combining the two side estimates gives `R >= (sum_i ||a_i||_2)/4`. Fractional orientation then gives `L <= sqrt(rho)sum_i ||a_i||_2`, proving constant four. The degree calculation counts each edge twice: `sum_i ||a_i||_1=2L`, proving constant two for `sqrt(Delta)`. The direct degeneracy argument instead counts each outgoing edge once and yields the stated constant four.

- **Bipartite, lower-order, and exactness constants.** In an inherited bipartition, the row estimate gives `R>=L/sqrt(2 Delta_P)` and likewise for the other side. The four-cycle with one negative unit edge has `Q` extrema `2,-2`, hence `L=4`, `R=2`, density one, and maximum degree two; all sharpness conclusions stated for this example follow. For random signs, there are `2^(h-1)` representatives and two exponential tails, so the logarithmic term is exactly `h log(2)`. Minimizing `h log(2)/lambda + m lambda/2` gives `sqrt(2mh log(2))`. Localizing to a densest induced face gives the claimed `sqrt(rho/(2 log(2)))` lower factor with a full signing. Compactness is invoked only for the full-center ratio on `L=1`; perturbation therefore does not presume continuity of the maximum of induced ratios at disappearing supports. The positive-coloring probabilities reciprocate to the displayed even and odd formulas. The exactness criterion follows because equality `R=L` requires the two extremal cuts to realize the positive and negative edge sets separately; the cycle parity criterion characterizes those cuts.

- **Schur transfer, including real conventions.** I checked Davidson–Donsig's original PDF pages 4, 6, and 7. Theorem 1.2 gives the projective-norm inequality in the required direction and explicitly distinguishes the real constant. The weighted Theorem 2.4 gives `2 sqrt(beta)`, whereas the integer pattern theorem would introduce a ceiling. For a real matrix, the real projective convention used in the manuscript is appropriate. Equivalently, the Hilbert-vector formulation can be made real by replacing complex vectors with their real and imaginary parts: real entries equal the resulting real inner products, with unchanged vector lengths.

  The rectangle count for a symmetric pattern is bounded by `|E(R union C)|+|E(R intersect C)|`; taking equal densest sets proves `beta=rho`. For `M=sign(A)`, the pairing is `2L`, because both matrix orientations are included. Rank-one decomposition and the definition of the real infinity-to-one norm yield `2L <= pi(M)||A||_(infinity->1)`. For independent sign vectors `u,v`, direct expansion with `p=(u+v)/2`, `q=(u-v)/2` gives `u^TAv=2[Q(p)-Q(q)]`. Both points lie in the sign cube's convex hull; multiaffinity bounds each value by the vertex extrema. Thus `||A||_(infinity->1)<=4R`, and the complete chain is `2L <= K_G(2sqrt(rho))(4R)`, or `L<=4K_Gsqrt(rho)R`. The Sidon comparison has the correct direction because mean zero gives `||Q||_infinity/2 <= R <= ||Q||_infinity`. Bipartite sign reversal makes both extrema symmetric.
