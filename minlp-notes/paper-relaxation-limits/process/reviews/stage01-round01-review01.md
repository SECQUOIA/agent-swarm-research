**Independent verification.** The checks below are exact analytic derivations, not finite numerical evidence for universal theorems. I found no central computation claim in the frozen manuscript that requires a numerical reproduction.

1. **Envelope definitions and vertex reduction.** On a compact box, the graph is compact and its convex hull has closed interval vertical fibers. Its lower and upper endpoints are respectively the greatest convex minorant and least concave majorant. For a multiaffine function, independent endpoint rounding preserves both the full mean vector and the expected function value; thus every graph point lies in the convex hull of the vertex graph. The reverse inclusion is immediate. The resulting finite polytope gives attained minima/maxima and continuous piecewise-affine envelopes, including at box faces. The termwise relaxation projects, at a fixed common `x`, to the sum of the factor intervals. This verifies the stated width, factorization dependence, affine invariance, and `0 <= H <= T`.

2. **Unit-cube lower and upper product envelopes.** The consecutive failure-interval construction has the claimed union measure: intervals are disjoint up to endpoints before total length one, and the continuous walk around the circle covers the whole circle once total length reaches one. Each failure interval has length at most one, so its marginal is preserved even when it wraps. Outside coordinates can be completed independently. Common threshold rounding preserves every singleton mean simultaneously and attains every positive monomial's upper bound. Subtracting `max(0,u-S)` from `u` gives `min(u,S)`, exactly the manuscript's deficiency budget. The two independence cases also retain their stated constants at means equal to one, at zero budgets, and when two means equal `1/2`.

3. **A strict exact-versus-expanded-versus-recursive example.** Consider the one factor `f(x)=x1*x2*x3` on `[1,2]^3` at `x=(3/2,3/2,3/2)`. The exact lower envelope is `3`: the affine minorant `2(x1+x2+x3)-6` lies below all eight vertex values, and equal mass on the three vertices with one upper endpoint and the three with two upper endpoints attains `3`. The upper envelope is `9/2`, attained by equal mass at the all-lower and all-upper vertices. Therefore both the original exact termwise gap and full gap are `3/2`.

   Under `x=1+p`, the expansion has three bilinear factors and one cubic factor, besides affine terms. At `p=(1/2,1/2,1/2)`, each nonlinear factor has width `1/2`, so the expanded termwise gap is `2`, strictly greater than `3/2`. Meanwhile the ordinary recursive relaxation with `z=x1*x2` permits `z=2` and the final product value `5/2`; its lower bound is `5/2`, below the exact lower value `3`. Its upper bound is `9/2`. These exact values support both distinctions the manuscript makes and verify the direction of the nonnegative-box comparison. In the general proof, affine expansion preserves degree and positivity but changes factor incidence; the manuscript properly limits its transfer accordingly.

4. **The induced-cut equality and its extension off the grid.** At half-valued coordinates, the law assigning equal mass to `s` and `-s` preserves all means and attains either extremum of the homogeneous quadratic sign polynomial. Coordinates fixed at one only add an affine function of the remaining coordinates, so the full `{0,1/2,1}` identities hold. For each cell, the active equalities form components linked by equality or complementation. A component with neither a fixed value nor an odd-complementation cycle has a genuine small feasible perturbation; it cannot occur at a vertex. Thus every cell vertex is half-integral. `T-cH` is convex on the cell, since `T` is affine there and `H` is concave. Its bound at the vertices extends with the required direction to the whole cell. Zero weighted support is handled consistently.

5. **Cut and graph constants.** With `Q=s^TAs/2`, the polarization calculation is `Q(s)-Q(t)=2v^TAu`. This gives exactly the defined half-range, without a missing factor of two. A locally optimal cut for squared weights retains at least half the squared incident weight at each vertex. The two Khinchin estimates then give `R >= (1/4) sum_i ||a_i||_2`. The flow network has the stated minimum-cut expression, and its fractions count each absolute edge weight once. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz consequently yields `L <= sqrt(rho) sum_i ||a_i||_2`, giving the constant four. Degree summation yields the constant two; using the bipartition itself yields the claimed `sqrt(2 min Delta)` bound. The distinct degeneracy-coloring proof has the same valid constants. A four-cycle with one negative unit edge has `Q` extrema `2,-2`, hence `L=4`, `R=2`, density one, and center ratio two. This checks the finite sharpness claims and their distinction from growing-density asymptotics.

6. **Fixed-graph lower bound and support.** The random-sign argument uses `2^(h-1)` representatives and both exponential signs, hence the logarithmic term is `h log 2`; its optimization gives `sqrt(2mh log 2)`. Independence between values at different vertex sign vectors is unnecessary. Restriction to a densest induced face and extension to a full signing are valid. Zero extension of an induced coefficient vector proves equality with the whole-center supremum. On `L=1`, positivity and continuity of `R` justify the compact maximization and subsequent full-support perturbation without assuming continuity of `c*`. The graph-family quantifiers, leaf-dilution example, scaling objection to unnormalized weighted density, and arboricity comparison all follow as stated.

7. **Positive coefficients, exactness, and prior transfer.** The balanced random cut of color classes has crossing probability `2 floor(chi/2) ceil(chi/2)/(chi(chi-1))`; its reciprocal gives both displayed coloring constants. The positive complete-graph ratio and the signed-cycle criterion follow exactly from the cut range. In the Schur transfer, rectangle counting gives `beta=rho`; tensor duality counts the symmetric edges twice; and `||A||_(infinity->1) <= 4R` gives `L <= 4 K_G sqrt(rho) R`. The primary source explicitly discusses real and complex Grothendieck constants; the manuscript chooses the real projective norm and real constant consistently. Finally, mean zero gives `||Q||_infinity/2 <= R <= ||Q||_infinity`, with equality of the latter two quantities for bipartite graphs. These checks support the stated older attribution and Sidon comparison.
