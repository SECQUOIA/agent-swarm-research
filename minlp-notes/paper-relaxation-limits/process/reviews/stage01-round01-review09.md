## Independent verification

### Envelope and certificate foundations

The vertex-law proof correctly replaces any graph point by independent endpoint rounding before taking the convex hull; this gives both vertical endpoints and their attainment. The circle construction for a single monomial preserves each failure marginal even when the consecutive intervals wrap around the circle. Positivity then makes one threshold law attain every upper envelope. The deficiency identity correctly converts the full lower-envelope minimization into one joint-law maximization; the two independent-rounding cases preserve the same complete mean vector.

The box-transfer inequality has the necessary direction: the exact hull width of an original factor is no greater than the sum of widths of its expanded monomials. The full function's hull width is invariant under the affine bijection. The warning that incidence properties need not survive expansion is appropriate. Fixed coordinates and affine summands are accounted for.

The certificate definition counts a cover of the feasible set and requires the stated oracle to certify every final region. It therefore does not infer a tree bound from a gap ratio or silently grant unmodeled cuts. The ordinary nonnegative absolute-tolerance and relative-tolerance-in-[0,1] conventions give the stated monotonicity in the incumbent; spelling out those parameter ranges would be harmless editorial clarification.

### Cut formulas and constants

At a half-integral point the sign-pair law gives `H=R_W/2`, while exact factor widths give `T=L_W/2`; fixed-one coordinates contribute only affine terms. The cell partition covers the cube, and every active relation is equality, complementation, or a fixed coordinate. A component without a fixed value or odd complementation cycle has a nonzero feasible tangent direction, so it cannot occur at a cell vertex. Thus the asserted half-integrality and the convexity argument extending the inequality to each cell are sound.

The polarization normalization is correct: `Q(s)-Q(t)=2 v^T A u`, giving `R=max_S ||A_(S,T)||_(infinity->1)`. A local maximum for squared edge weights gives at least half the squared incident weight across each vertex. Applying real Khinchin separately on each side gives `R >= (sum_i ||a_i||_2)/4`. The fractional-orientation max-flow calculation gives precisely the induced density, without integer rounding. Consequently the constants `4 sqrt(rho)`, `4 sqrt(d)`, `2 sqrt(Delta)`, and the bipartite `sqrt(2 min(Delta_P,Delta_Q))` follow. The frustrated four-cycle has `L=4`, `R=2`, `rho=1`, and degree two, so the claimed finite bipartite sharpness and lower endpoints for the universal constants are valid.

The random-sign proof counts at most `2^h` exponential terms after fixing one vertex sign. Jensen and the optimized positive parameter give the finite bound `sqrt(2mh log 2)`. Exposing a densest induced face makes extension to a full signing valid. The equality of the full-center supremum with the induced supremum is a statement about varying the coefficients, and the text correctly avoids claiming it for every fixed coefficient vector. Full-support perturbation uses continuity of the full-center ratio on `L=1`, not continuity of `c*` across zero coefficients.

I also checked the coloring probabilities, cycle-cut parity proof, arboricity comparison, diluted-core example, and scale-invariance objection. Their qualifications and stated constants are consistent.

### Schur-multiplier and Khinchin source audit

The original local Davidson–Donsig PDF was rendered and inspected at p.4 and p.6-7. Its Theorem 1.2 has the projective norm inequality in the required direction and explicitly distinguishes the real and complex Grothendieck constants. Its weighted Theorem 2.4 gives the continuous square-root rectangle-density upper bound; using the indicator matrix of the pattern gives `2 sqrt(beta)`. The manuscript correctly uses this theorem instead of the integer-rounded pattern theorem. These claims are supported by [[davidson2007-norms-of-schur-multipliers]] p.4 and p.6-7. The newer [author-hosted version](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~krdavids/Preprints/DavDon_schur.pdf) was also checked: it adds operator-valued material and shifts pagination, but retains Theorem 2.4 and the relevant upper bound.

Independently, counting the two orientations of every undirected edge gives `beta(P)=rho(G)`. Real projective duality gives `2L <= pi(sign(A)) ||A||_(infinity->1)`. The manuscript's second polarization identity has the correct factor two, and multiaffinity bounds both partially zero sign vectors by the same vertex extrema, giving `||A||_(infinity->1) <= 4R`. The resulting transfer constant is indeed `4 K_G`, with no missing real/complex conversion. The final text claims improvement only over this particular transfer and does not claim that its constant is the best in earlier literature. That is an accurate novelty boundary.

The normalized sharp real Khinchin inequality, including equality for two equal coefficients, is explicitly stated as equation (1) in [Eskenazis–Nayar–Tkocz, arXiv:2301.09380v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.09380v2). Homogeneity gives exactly the manuscript's displayed inequality, including the zero coefficient vector. The [Szarek publisher record](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/studia-mathematica/all/58/2/101277/on-the-best-constants-in-the-khinchin-inequality) confirms title, year, volume, pages, and DOI. The manuscript cites an established external theorem and does not misrepresent it as an elementary proof supplied here.
