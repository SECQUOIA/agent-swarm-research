**Independent verification.** The following derivations describe the mathematical checks, independently of the finite checker.

1. **Original transfer and complete local oracle** (`13`, 35–156). For a witness-containing product, a restricted coordinate permits exactly its witnessed Boolean endpoint; every other set contains both endpoints. Deterministic substitution is defined whether or not the original pseudoexpectation assigns the signs positive mass. With generator degree sum `a` and square multiplier degree `d`, `a+2d<=2r`. Endpoint interpolation of each local univariate factor gives nonnegative coefficients. After removing constants, there are at most `a` nonconstant factors. Their products are Boolean indicators, including zero for conflicting factors; repetitions preserve idempotence. Thus `I P^2=(I P)^2` in the Boolean quotient, with square degree at most `2(a+d)<=4r`. Both the original expression and its reduction stay in the source domain. Local equalities vanish after substitution and reduction, including all allowed coupled multipliers.

   Marginalizing the actual source quadratic law and fixing the restricted coordinates preserves precisely every required diagonal and cross entry. Its finite endpoint support lies in the actual node product even when that product is not closed. The proof therefore establishes hull membership, not just all separating halfspaces of its closure. On compact boxes, linear separation gives the claimed equivalence with every valid quadratic moment inequality. Products of coupled quadratic cuts do not follow from this and are correctly excluded.

2. **Clause normalization and witness count** (`13`, 11–24, 132–148). Multiaffinity moves any original point to a Boolean vertex without increasing the objective. All clause costs are in `[0,1]`. The source square tests `(1±chi_A)^2` are available through degree six since `r>=2`; their reduced values imply absolute character mean at most one. Untouched clauses keep zero pseudo-cost, and no more than `Delta|R|` clauses are touched, counting multiplicity. Therefore `LB<=Delta|R|/m`. A certified product contains at most fraction `2^(-mT_*/Delta)` of the Boolean witnesses. The union bound applies to overlapping arbitrary covers and does not require a tree. The earlier certificate definition and `13`, 151–156 correctly charge certified discarded regions and make worse incumbents require at least the best-incumbent target.

3. **Signed realization, especially the constant class** (`13`, 159–179; `14`, 112–125). A PSD unit-diagonal matrix has unit Gram vectors. Entries of absolute value one identify vectors up to sign; different resulting classes are orthogonal. Taking the constant vector as the representative of its class fixes its associated sign to one. Independent unbiased signs on every other class then reproduce all means and pair moments, including correlations with deterministic coordinates. This proves the general lemma, not only the zero-mean special case.

   The distinction from graph support is substantive. For a single clause of sign `b`, take independent uniform `X,Y,Z` and set `U=bZ,V=b`. This law has exactly the same augmented first/second matrix as the uniform satisfying graph law with `XYZ=b,U=XY,V=b`. It satisfies both graph equations in expectation, but `U=XY` fails on half its atoms. Its expectation of `(U-XY)^2` is two, whereas the graph law gives zero. Hence the two laws are not interchangeable at higher degrees. The manuscript never interchanges them. This example also explains why graph equalities on degree-two moments cannot silently be promoted to graph support.

4. **Classical width construction** (`13`, 181–243). The bounded-width closure has a unique sign for each derived support: opposite signs would resolve to a contradiction of width zero. For supports `I,J,K` of size at most `floor(w/2)`, multiplication of the two derived differences yields the third within width `w`, proving transitivity with consistent orientations. The matrix `y_(I triangle J)` is the signed-class Gram matrix. Its quadratic form proves positivity for every polynomial square of allowed degree, even when the union of the polynomial's monomial supports is large. Define every moment through degree `w` directly by the derived-sign-or-zero rule; odd top-degree moments do not need a split into two half-width supports. The constant character is normalized to one. Thus original degree `4r`, and later `4rD`, is available whenever it fits in the source width. The source's typographical norm-zero sentence does not infect this independently written proof.

6. **Lifted lower bound at order one** (`14`, 7–168). Literal pullback costs a factor at most `D` in total degree. Restricted supports are fixed in their entire union `C`; every restricted lifted coordinate is then deterministic. The local-factor expansion yields products of parity indicators, which are idempotent even with overlap, duplicate factors, dependent parities, or an inconsistent product. With `a+2d<=2r`, `deg(I P_hat)<=D(a+d)<=2rD`, so its square fits through `4rD`. Graph identities vanish as real polynomials before applying the functional, and every allowed multiplied identity has pullback degree at most `2rD`. A lifted linear square pulls back to square-polynomial degree at most `D`, its diagonal entries reduce to one, and its other entries are signed source character moments of degree at most `2D`. The signed realization lemma therefore supplies the required actual full-box law. An endpoint-valued mean forces each restricted coordinate almost surely, so arbitrary nonclosed sets remain covered.

   Objective equality is a real polynomial identity, not merely Boolean agreement. A changed clause has support at most three; square-polynomial degree three is available because the integer condition `rD>=2` implies `2rD>=4`. No part of positivity, graph equality, realizability, or counting requires a separate `r>=2`. The lifted objective still must have degree at most `2r`; this allows the linear factorable objective at order one and excludes evaluating a written cubic original objective at that order.

   Separately, an order-one law on the entire lifted box bounds `|E[v]-a_i a_j a_k|` by `h+2h=3h` using only `E[u]=E[x_i x_j]` and `E[v]=E[u x_k]`. Each expectation involved is degree at most two.
