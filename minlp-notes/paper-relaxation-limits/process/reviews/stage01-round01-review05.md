**Independent verification.**

The following analytic checks were performed directly against the manuscript.

- **Vertex laws and positive deficiencies.** Independent endpoint sampling represents every multiaffine graph point without changing its scalar value. The finite vertex polytope therefore supplies attained envelopes, including boundary means. Consecutive failure arcs attain the single-product lower probability even when their total length exceeds one. The common threshold law attains every positive upper term simultaneously. The identity `u-max(0,u-S)=min(u,S)` gives the stated term deficiency. Affine terms must be understood as discarded when writing the displayed upper-envelope identity, as the proposition directs. The independence estimates have the correct inequality direction and handle zero deficiency. Nonnegative affine expansion increases the specified termwise width while preserving the full width; the text does not wrongly preserve original incidence.
- **Half-integral cells.** Every active relation is an equality or a complementation, together with possible fixed-coordinate equations. In a relation component, an odd complementation cycle forces one half; an anchored component propagates an anchor in `{0,1/2,1}`. A component with neither has an actual one-dimensional perturbation. All inactive inequalities have strict slack at the candidate vertex, so a sufficiently small perturbation preserves them. This proves the asserted cell-vertex restriction. On each cell the signed termwise width is affine, while `H=cav-vex` is concave. Hence `T-cH` is convex for the required `c>=0`, and its grid-vertex bound extends to the cell. Fixed-one outside coordinates add only affine terms, so the grid identities also hold on all boundary faces.
- **Polarization and row estimates.** With `Q=s^T A s/2`, the identity is `Q(s)-Q(t)=2v^T A u`, giving precisely the displayed half-range and rectangular norm, without an extra factor two. The squared-weight local cut guarantees at least half of each full squared row mass across the cut. Khinchin on both sides then gives `R >= (sum_i ||a_i||_2)/4`. The maximum-degree bound uses the full row `l1` sum `2L`, yielding `2 sqrt(Delta)` as stated.
- **Fractional orientation.** At density `rho`, every finite cut in the network has capacity at least `m`; an endpoint arc has capacity strictly greater than `m`, and the remaining minimum cut for fixed vertex set `U` has capacity `m-|E(U)|+rho|U|`. The edgeless case is separately handled. The direct degeneracy proof is valid with reverse processing of an acyclic orientation, and induced restrictions preserve all parameters used.
