**Independent verification.** The following derivations were reconstructed from the definitions, with special attention to constants, inequality directions, exponents and truncation.

1. **Optimum, chords and upper certificates.** A nonvertex on the balance slice has two fractional coordinates that can be varied oppositely. Every vertex therefore has `k` ones and one half, giving penalty `1/4`; concavity gives the global optimum. The difference between the quadratic and its chord expands to `(x-a)(b-x)`, and convexity puts any convex underestimator below the chord, including a degenerate interval. Both McCormick lower planes reduce to this chord after `y=1-x`; the minimal convexifying alphaBB coefficient is one because the second derivative is `2(alpha-1)`. The midpoint high/low stopping thresholds are respectively `k+1` and `n-k`, so Pascal recursion gives exactly `binomial(n+1,k+1)` leaves. Symmetry of that binomial coefficient gives the stated exponent `min{k+1,n-k}`. For positive tolerance, each surviving prefix contributes four nodes, including the intermediate upper child: `1+4(2^n-1)=2^(n+2)-3`; the full binary tree has `2^(n+1)-1` leaves. The terminal corner lower bound `(1-alpha)/2` is strictly larger than `1/4`. The second-order error bound is `sum_i (b_i-a_i)^2/4`, in the correct direction.

2. **Avoidance, thresholds and charging.** The witness classes are distinct because `p=1/(2m)` lies strictly between zero and one. Marginal avoidance gives the two upper bounds with bases `(n-z)/n` and `(n-k)/n`; the events need not be independent. Since at least one exclusion set has size at least `|R|/2`, the uniform upper bound uses the larger of those two powers. Its reciprocal is consequently the manuscript's **minimum**, not a maximum. Empty endpoint classes give base one, and impossible avoidance gives zero probability. The count uses only a finite cover and the union bound, so overlapping regions and arbitrary real split points pose no problem. At positive target, the chord witness estimate gives `|R|>2m(1/4-epsilon)`. Charging at most `a` discarded certified boxes per processed node gives at most `(a+1)N` cover members. Closed-slab certification is an explicit sufficient condition, not an unsupported closure assertion. For `0<=delta<1/2`, every tolerated sum remains in `(k,k+1)`, and the minimum penalty is `1/4-delta^2`; hence the replacement tolerance is exactly `epsilon+delta^2`. The positivity condition on the resulting target is necessary and present.

3. **Full SDP–RLT.** For the free coordinates, the covariance is `(c-d)(I-J/s)`, with `c-d=t(s-t)/(s(s-1))>=0`. Restricted rows have zero covariance, so the entire augmented matrix is PSD by the Schur complement. I checked both free and deterministic equality-product rows. The four distinct-free-index slack products are `d`, `c-d`, `c-d`, and `(s-t)(s-t-1)/(s(s-1))`; the repeated-free-index cases are `c,0,0,1-c`. A restricted index factors out a nonnegative deterministic slack, including singleton boxes. The only nonzero penalty terms are the restricted middle coordinates. The diagonal mixed RLT constraint has the correct sign to imply SDP domination of the chord bound. At order one, these constraints are exactly the stated full preordering. Affine Farkas representations need a nonnegative constant, box-slack combinations and a balance multiple; the manuscript includes all three and retains the distinction between original-space affine inequalities and lifted clique cuts.

4. **Fractional moments and homogeneous Gram proof.** For a squarefree multiplier of degree `a<=2d-1`, the cardinality recurrence uses the moment of degree `a+1<=s`; there is no zero denominator. In the homogeneous replacement, `binomial(t-a,d-a)>0` under the stated hypotheses. The replacement difference is the balance polynomial times a polynomial of degree at most `d-1` in the Boolean quotient. Multiplication by `P+H` then needs balance multipliers of degree at most `2d-1`, exactly the proved range. At intersection size `ell`, the Gram identity reduces to

   `sum_j binomial(ell,j)(t)_(2d-j)(s-t)_j = (t)_(2d-ell)(s-2d+ell)_ell`.

   This polynomial Vandermonde identity yields the displayed moment entry after division by `(s)_(2d)`. No possibly zero numerator is divided out. Every incidence matrix is a Gram matrix, and the coefficients are nonnegative under `t,s-t>=2d-1`, including the equality endpoints. This supplies universal square positivity independently of finite eigenvalue calculations.

5. **All assignment localizers and exact order range.** Expanding upper slacks gives the uncancelled identity

   `E[I_(A,B)u_C]=(t)_(a+|C|)(s-t)_b/(s)_(a+b+|C|)`.

   The indicator weight is nonnegative. If the original square polynomial is nonconstant, the budget gives `a+b<=2r-2`, making that weight strictly positive; cancellation is therefore legitimate when the conditional functional is needed. If the restricted polynomial is constant or all coordinates are assigned, direct evaluation avoids a zero-variable denominator. The remaining nonconstant degree `d` satisfies `s-a-b>=2d`, `t-a>=2d-1` and `s-t-b>=2d-1`. Repetitions collapse and conflicting slacks give zero. Thus the proof includes arbitrary global square multipliers. For noninteger `t<2r-1`, the product of `floor(t)+2` distinct lower slacks has exactly one negative falling-factorial factor, confirming why stronger square positivity alone cannot extend this full-preordering construction.

   Integer rounding of `|R|<k-2r+2` leaves at least `2r-1` unit coordinates, and likewise for zeros. The separate `r=1` dimension check is valid. The objective is strictly below the non-strict pruning target, including `R` empty. This proves exactly `q_r=min{k-2r+2,z-2r+2,m(1/2-2epsilon)}`. For balanced parts, positivity is `r<(t+2)/2`, while retaining the `n/24` exponent at `epsilon=1/8` requires `r<=3t/8+1`. These are distinct conditions, as stated.

6. **Perturbations and cost shift.** Strict concavity excludes nonvertex minimizers, and an exchange sorts coordinate values against the strictly increasing coefficients. The mass-transfer argument also proves the same vertex uniquely minimizes the linear term over the entire slice; that stronger fact is needed by the clique-cut proof. First moments are in `[0,1]` by the actual node constraints, so the perturbation is at most `eta`. This gives the exact `-2eta` threshold change. With `epsilon=1/8` and `eta=1/32`, the restriction term is `3t/16`, the exponent is `n/32`, and the order condition is `r<=13t/32+1`. The explicit linear coefficients sum to `eta`. A permutation preserving the single balance slice can change the coefficient vector only by a constant vector; comparing sums makes that constant zero. Adding the conserved flow shifts each functional by exactly `K`, and the derivatives and absolute-versus-relative distinction are correct.

7. **Arbitrary coordinate sets and degree-free graph lifts.** The proof uses actual membership of the two endpoints on free coordinates and of the witness on fixed coordinates. It never assumes a closed, connected or compact coordinate set. Every local valid polynomial reduces to a nonnegative combination of the two endpoint indicators. Grouping factors by original coordinate costs at most their original total degree, even when factors repeat or their reductions become constant.

   In the lift, every original or auxiliary variable maps to an affine polynomial in one Boolean coordinate, or to its finite actual witness value. This is a degree-nonincreasing algebra homomorphism before Boolean reduction. The retained affine balance has multiplier budget `2r-1`; local equality products have zero Boolean reduction through their available degrees. Every allowed localizer reduces to a nonnegative sum of assignment indicators times the substituted global square, within the original order `r`. Every Boolean assignment maps to an actual point of the **full product graph**. Consequently a full-graph identity, including a coupled identity, is annihilated. Full-graph objective agreement and uniqueness of multilinear reduction give the original objective value even when the written lifted objective couples coordinates. This reasoning concerns a moment functional, not an equality of a nonlinear function with its affine endpoint interpolant on the continuous interval. Finite values on all of `[0,1]`, retained originals, available lifted objective degree, and full-graph agreement are all explicitly required. No compact graph or hidden bound on the number of auxiliaries is used.

   Literal polynomial substitution separately costs at most `D` per lifted degree. The retained affine balance needs `1+D(2r-1)<=2rD`, and objective equality on the whole cube is a polynomial identity. Thus the `rD` comparison is valid and weaker.

8. **Global tensor and relative transfer.** For each tested product `g` and square degree `d`, every block matrix indexed by all block monomials through `d` is available because `deg(g_b)+2d<=2r`. Tensoring those PSD matrices, then taking the principal submatrix of total degree at most `d`, gives exactly the required global localizing matrix. Unused tensor entries do not need globally defined moments. Equality products factor monomial by monomial. This proves the assertion for squares coupling every block and for lifted variables.

   Light blocks have penalty at most `|R_b|/(2t)<=|R_b|/(2q_0)` since `1<=q_0<=t`. Heavy blocks use actual witness evaluation and penalty `1/2-1/(4t)<=1/2<=|R_b|/(2q_0)`. Certification therefore forces `sum_b |R_b|>=2q_0G tau`, with precisely `tau=1/4-theta(K+1/4)-eta`. Independent block partitions then give region fraction at most `(2/3)^(q_0G tau)`. The reciprocal exponent is `q_0 tau n/(3t)`. The explicit parameters give `tau=17/128` and exponent `17n/384`. The argument only asserts an exponential bound when `tau>0` and the block parameters are fixed. In the lift, the global substituted objective agrees with `C` on every remaining Boolean assignment, including when some blocks use deterministic evaluation; tensor Boolean reduction proves objective agreement without assuming the written objective is block-separable.

9. **Symmetry and escape certificates.** A permutation preserving the feasible set preserves the balance normal space. A permuted block indicator has exactly one block's number of ones, so it must be a whole block indicator. Objective preservation requires translated coefficient multisets. For the squared-index coefficients, successive differences are `c(2((b-1)ell+i)+1)`, whose first entry distinguishes blocks. Each block is therefore fixed, and a finite distinct set cannot equal a nonzero translate of itself. The coefficient upper bound `eta/(3t)` gives the required block mass bound. The argument correctly does more than appeal to distinctness.

   The clique expression at a Boolean vector with `s` ones is `(s-k)(s-k-1)>=0`. Multiaffinity extends this validity to the continuous cube. The balance products give total second moment `K^2`; the cut then gives trace at most `k+1/4`, hence penalty at least `1/4`. Combining this with the independently attained linear-program minimum proves exactness for the perturbed objectives; PSD is unnecessary. Exact evaluation provides attainment. The `k=0` boundary is supplied directly. One cut per relative block or reuse of separate component certificates escapes the global-cover obstruction.
