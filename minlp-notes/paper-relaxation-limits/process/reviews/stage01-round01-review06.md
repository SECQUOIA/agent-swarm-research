**Independent verification.** The following are analytic checks unless explicitly described as computation.

- **Graph geometry and boundaries.** Independent coordinate endpoint sampling expresses every multiaffine graph point as a convex combination of vertex graph points with the same value. A finite vertex graph polytope has lower and upper surfaces given by finitely many affine pieces; hence the envelope continuity claim includes box faces and corners. If the graph is itself affine, its envelope is that affine function, so this degenerate case also fits. The initial continuous-function scalar graph-hull characterization is valid because the graph is compact and its finite-dimensional convex hull is compact.
- **Termwise model and domain transformations.** At fixed `x`, the independent factor hulls give intervals, whose Minkowski sum has exactly the stated sum of widths. Adding an affine summand shifts both endpoints equally. Affine box bijections act on the full `(x,y)` graph. Positive affine monomial expansion increases or preserves the termwise width, while preserving the full polynomial width. Common-threshold rounding attains every expanded positive monomial upper envelope, hence the original factors' upper envelopes. This justifies the direction of the transfer and the warning that incidence structure can change. The different status of recursive products on the unit cube and positive boxes agrees with the primary source.
- **Joint laws and deficiencies.** The consecutive failure-interval construction has the prescribed individual failure probabilities and union measure equal to the total length capped at one, including lengths zero or one. The same threshold law works for every positive support simultaneously. Subtracting the lower single-term envelope gives `min(u_e,S_e)`, and the common upper value converts the full hull gap to the maximum expected weighted deficiency. Both cases in the independence lemma preserve the complete mean vector and give its stated constant.
- **Half-integral cell proof.** On each cell the minimum of `x_i, x_j, 1-x_i, 1-x_j` chooses a fixed affine piece. Active equality/complementation relations have either an anchored component, a component forced to `1/2` by an odd complementation cycle, or a free parameter. In the last case a sufficiently small perturbation preserves inactive strict inequalities as well, so it cannot be a vertex. Thus all cell vertices are half-integral. Since `H` is concave, `T-cH` is convex on a cell for `c >= 0`; grid-vertex comparison therefore extends to the whole cell. Fixing outside coordinates to one adds only affine terms and does not invalidate the grid identity.
- **Cut constants and upper bounds.** With `Q(s)=s^T A s/2`, writing `u=(s+t)/2`, `v=(s-t)/2` gives `Q(s)-Q(t)=2v^T A u`; this yields the exact rectangular-block maximum. The real Khinchin factor is `1/sqrt(2)`. A locally maximal squared-weight cut preserves half of every squared row norm, and using both sides gives `R >= sum_i ||a_i||_2/4`. Fractional orientation and weighted Cauchy–Schwarz yield `L <= sqrt(rho) sum_i ||a_i||_2`. The resulting density constant four, degree constant two, and bipartite factor `sqrt(2)` are consistent. I also checked the distinct reverse-order coloring argument for degeneracy and the flow cut expression `m-|E(U)|+t|U|`.
- **Lower order and coefficient support.** The sign-supremum estimate counts at most `2^h` signed exponential terms and needs no independence across vertex configurations. Jensen and the optimized parameter yield `sqrt(2mh log 2)`. The densest induced face is unaffected by arbitrary full-support signs outside it. The whole-center supremum identity follows by zero extension of each induced coefficient vector. Positivity and continuity of `R` on `L=1` justify perturbation to full support without assuming continuity of `c*` at disappearing coefficients.
- **Exactness and structural consequences.** The two extremal cuts must attain all positive and all negative weight separately when `R=L`; the cycle-parity characterization gives precisely the stated two cuts. The coloring crossing probability, all-positive complete-graph limit, frustrated four-cycle constants, leaf dilution example, and density/degeneracy/arboricity comparisons agree with direct counting. Parameter monotonicity is sufficient for all listed structural consequences. The constant intervals are correctly distinguished from growing-parameter asymptotics.
- **Schur transfer.** The definition of the real projective norm directly gives `|<A,M>| <= pi(M)||A||_(infinity->1)` by bounding each rank-one summand; for `M=sign(A)`, the pairing is `2L`. Davidson–Donsig p.4 states the projective comparison and explicitly distinguishes the real constant. Its weighted Theorem 2.4, applied to the pattern indicator, gives `2 sqrt(beta)` without integer rounding. For the symmetric adjacency pattern an edge is counted at most once in a rectangle unless both endpoints are in the intersection; this proves `beta=rho` after taking `R=C` on a densest set. Finally, `u^TAv = 2[Q(p)-Q(q)]` and multiaffinity on the sign cube give the factor `4R`. Combining the three steps gives exactly `L <= 4 K_G sqrt(rho) R`. For nonempty support, the mean-zero Sidon comparison and the bipartite equality are valid. The attribution is appropriately limited to the implication from prior norm theory.
