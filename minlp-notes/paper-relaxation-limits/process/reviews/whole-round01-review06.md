**Independent verification.** I reconstructed the main arguments and tried to invalidate them at their assumptions, endpoint cases, and transitions between relaxation models. The following records the substantive checks, rather than only their outcomes.

1. **Signed bilinear widths and positive common laws.** I checked the reduction of a signed bilinear gap to half-integral cut data, including the half-range normalization, polarization, the squared-row local cut estimate, and the weighted density/Schur transfer constants. In the positive setting I reconstructed the single-law requirement and the marginal-preserving quantile construction. In particular, the profile counts, dyadic cutoff, harmonic normalization, inactive mass, and tangent mixture must all refer to one joint law. They do. I checked the large-radix qualification and that optimizing the cutoff sharpens the upper constant without claiming a matching refined lower expansion. The coefficient-cloning argument uses its degree restriction where needed.

2. **Cubic and equal-mean results.** I checked the cases in the cubic mixture, the constrained and unrestricted scalar minimizations, the vertex-count interpretation of the finite constructions, and the fixed-cardinality law for the equal-mean formula. The proof does not incorrectly substitute count-polynomial formulas at nonbinary points. My separate exact symbolic checker verifies both scalar elimination identities and all five Bernstein identities, including the minimum coefficient `901/120000`. I also executed the complete printed finite-cubic program, covering the 18-, 24-, and 192-variable certificates, and the printed finite-signing program. Their exact integer/rational outputs agree with the manuscript.

3. **Incidence and structural restrictions.** I checked ownership of the high coordinates, the compatibility of shared low anchors, the incidence-degree alternative, and the feedback-component gluing. For frequency two I checked the doubled formulation, including repeated/parallel edges and dummy variables, and the nonnegative baseline needed for the multiplicative conclusion. For treewidth two I reconstructed the serial and parallel cases of the active-terminal/block invariant. 4. **Positive boxes and exact complexity.** The spreading argument preserves the fixed ambient joint law while comparing the extrema and controlling the loss. The aspect-ratio statements distinguish original factors from their expansion and retain their coefficient assumptions. I checked the limiting constructions and the predecessor examples. For the exact single-product problem I reconstructed the PARTITION encoding and the separation of the two product ranges. The inverse-polynomial-in-the-encoded-integers separation is not presented as a fixed-error hardness theorem, and the rational witness-size argument matches the exact decision model.

5. **Cardinality, coordinate domains, and reformulations.** I reconstructed the falling-factorial Gram identity and the nonnegative coefficients in its degree range, then checked how conditioning transfers it to box localizers. The tensor argument uses a principal submatrix with the correct total-degree restriction. I checked repeated indices and zero-probability assignments, endpoint product domains, and the polynomial degree needed for local graph interpolation. The distinction between cuts valid on a full graph domain and cuts valid only on the finite hard instance is maintained. The spatial counting claims, hierarchy-order claims, and global extension claims refer to different mathematical objects throughout.

6. **XOR and monomial lifts.** I traced the signed-character Gram construction through the source degree requirement, the source width parameter, the `4r` and `4rD` degree budgets, and the deterministic monomial substitution. The proof does not confuse substitution with conditioning. The Boolean support-union argument gives the stated rank bound, and the relative/full-domain distinction is retained in the quadratic objective upper bound. I checked the first-order case against the degree restrictions rather than extrapolating the higher-order statement without them.

7. **Point packing, the additional focus.** I derived the four relaxation values directly from the specified interval models. The RLT pairwise bound follows by taking diagonal secants and the larger lower plane; these choices are simultaneously feasible because the pair entries are independent in that model. The SDP bound follows from
   `sum_{i<j}(X_ii - 2 X_ij + X_jj) = k tr(X) - e^T X e <= k L s - s^2 <= k^2 L^2/4`.
   This is applied to the correct common interval subset, with `k >= 2`, and separately to the two coordinate matrices. For SYM, I independently obtained all six RLT distances `(1/2, 7/8, 5/4, 5/4, 13/8, 2)` and all six SDP distances `(k/[4(k-1)], 1/2, 3/4, 5/8, 7/8, 1)` in the printed group-pair order. The covariance is a positive multiple of the orthogonal projection on the tight group and nonnegative diagonal blocks elsewhere. Thus PSD holds exactly, without an eigenvalue tolerance. On the tight group the off-diagonal value lies in the actual tightened RLT interval; other off-diagonal entries are products of the means. Repeated-index RLT inequalities also hold. The bounds `n >= 5`, `k = ceil(n/4)`, and `k-1 = floor((n-1)/4)` are correct. The dimension-scaled statement and `L = 0` case follow from the same identity. None of this is a claim about every symmetry, ordering, strengthening, or spatial algorithm.

I checked the P-split retained-domain counterexample and exact ball projection, the rank-one exposed face and stability estimates, and the distinction between exact fixed-block lifts and approximate PSD bounds.
