**Independent verification.** The following are direct proof reconstructions and targeted adversarial checks.

1. **Optimum, covers and upper certificates.** Opposite perturbations exclude vertices with two fractional coordinates. The balance then forces one half-coordinate, giving penalty `1/4`; the argument also covers `k=0` and `k=n-1`. For a containing box, the only potentially positive witness chord contributions come from `M intersect R`, each below `1/(2m)`. A certified box therefore requires `|R|>m(1/2-2epsilon)`. For asymmetric witness classes the two endpoint-avoidance events need not be independent: the smaller marginal bound controls their intersection, and inversion gives the minimum of the two reciprocal expressions. Overlap between cover members is harmless. Empty endpoint classes give base one, and `q=0` gives one region.

   The positive-tolerance tree adds four nodes at each surviving prefix, giving `2^(n+2)-3` nodes. The exact midpoint tree satisfies the Pascal recurrence with stopping counts `(k+1,n-k)`, giving `binomial(n+1,k+1)` leaves and the complementary-demand exponent `min(k+1,n-k)`. For tolerance `delta<1/2`, every allowed sum remains strictly between `k` and `k+1`; minimizing its residual penalty gives `1/4-delta^2`. Exact-balance witnesses remain available at target `1/4-epsilon-delta^2`. This reasoning would fail outside the stated range, and the manuscript explicitly says so. Charging discarded certified regions gives at most `(a+1)N` cover members without assuming an open slab's closure is certified.

2. **Full SDP–RLT.** With `c=t/s` and `d=t(t-1)/(s(s-1))`, direct subtraction gives covariance `(c-d)(I-J/s)`, where `c-d=t(s-t)/(s(s-1))`. Deterministic rows have zero covariance. The row identity `c+(s-1)d=tc` proves all balance products. For two distinct free indices the four slack moments are `d,c-d,c-d,(s-t)(s-t-1)/(s(s-1))`; for a repeated free index they are `c,0,0,1-c`. A restricted index factors off a nonnegative deterministic slack, including singleton intervals. The diagonal mixed inequality yields the chord lower bound. At order one, the stipulated full preordering supplies exactly these constraints and augmented PSD. The affine-Farkas closure uses identities in original coordinates and maintains the degree bound, including balance-square terms; it does not supply the lifted clique cut.

3. **Moment positivity and localizers.** The cardinality recurrence only reaches subset size `2d<=s`. In the Boolean quotient, each degree-`a` monomial differs from its homogeneous replacement by the cardinality polynomial times a polynomial of degree at most `d-1`. Multiplication by `P+H` therefore uses exactly the allowed multiplier degree `2d-1`. The Gram entry identity follows from Vandermonde after factoring, without dividing by a potentially zero numerator. Its coefficients are nonnegative when `t,s-t>=2d-1`.

   A repeated slack product reduces to an assignment indicator or zero. Direct expansion gives weight `(t)_a(s-t)_b/(s)_(a+b)`. If the original square polynomial is nonconstant, the assigned count is at most `2r-2`, so this weight is strictly positive; conditional dimension and residual cardinalities meet the lemma's hypotheses. Constant, zero-weight and all-assigned cases use direct evaluation instead of an undefined conditional functional. This proves positivity for the full globally coupled square, not just selected monomials. The negative product of `floor(t)+2` lower slacks shows why the noninteger full-preordering range cannot be extended by citing stronger moment-matrix positivity alone.

4. **Order, perturbation and endpoints.** The integer count `|R|<k-2r+2` retains at least `2r-1` unit coordinates; the zero-coordinate count is identical. The `r=1` remaining-dimension case is handled separately and correctly. The constructed penalty is strictly below the non-strict pruning target, including `R` empty. This gives exactly `q_r=min(k-2r+2,z-2r+2,m(1/2-2epsilon))`. At `epsilon=1/8`, preserving `q_r=t/4` is equivalent to `r<=3t/8+1`. Positive but bounded `q_r` is not misdescribed as a uniform exponential bound.

   Strict concavity excludes nonvertex minimizers, and a mass transfer from a higher-cost coordinate to a cheaper unfilled coordinate proves the unique sorted linear-cost minimum as well. The node slacks imply first moments in `[0,1]`, giving perturbation at most `eta`. The perturbed constants, stated upper certificate and increasing-cost shift follow. The absolute-versus-relative limitation is necessary and explicit.

5. **Adversarial reconstruction of no-order-loss lifting.** For every unrestricted original coordinate, substitute the actual two graph endpoint values affinely; fix restricted coordinates at their actual witness graph values. Every lifted variable then has degree at most one. A local polynomial inequality reduces to an affine endpoint indicator with nonnegative coefficients. After grouping factors, each distinct nonconstant Boolean coordinate consumes at least one original lifted degree. Hence an indicator times the substituted global square still satisfies `|J|+2 deg(Phi(P))<=2r`, even with repeated constraints, several auxiliaries for one coordinate, or squares coupling all coordinates.

   A local graph equality has zero reduction; multiplication by an arbitrary allowed global polynomial preserves this. The same argument respects a full-product-graph identity. The retained affine balance maps to the original fractional-cardinality relation with multiplier degree at most `2r-1`. Finally, every Boolean assignment maps to a true full graph point. Full-graph objective agreement therefore implies equality of multilinear reductions and objective moments. Those assignments generally do not satisfy the balance: this is why agreement on the balance-feasible graph alone is not being substituted for the actual hypothesis. No continuous interpolation identity is assumed. Functions finite everywhere on `[0,1]` preserve all witness tuples and the original attaining optimizer even for a noncompact lifted graph. Literal polynomial substitution remains valid with order `rD`, by `1+D(2r-1)<=2rD` for balance products.

6. **Relative tensor transfer.** For a fixed global generator product and square degree `d`, every block localizing matrix on monomials of degree at most `d` exists because `deg(g_b)+2d<=2r`. Tensor its Gram representations and restrict to total-degree-at-most-`d` indices. The retained entries are exactly the available global localizer moments; unused tensor entries need not be global moments. Thus all coupled squares are covered. Balance and local equality products factor one global monomial at a time.

   Light blocks give penalty at most `|R_b|/(2q_0)`; heavy blocks can be evaluated at their true witnesses and satisfy the same estimate. Rearranging the objective upper bound against `(1-theta)C*` gives `sum_b |R_b|>=2q_0 G tau`. Independent block partitions then give exponent `q_0 G tau`; the explicit example reduces to `17n/384`. With graph auxiliaries, apply affine substitution in light blocks and actual evaluation in heavy blocks before tensoring. A globally coupled written objective agreeing on the full graph still has the same Boolean reduction as the original sum, so no separability assumption on its written form is needed.

7. **Symmetry and escape certificates.** A feasible-set permutation preserves the block-constant normal space and therefore maps whole blocks to whole blocks. Objective preservation forces translated coefficient lists. Squared consecutive indices give different sorted successive differences in different blocks, excluding these translations; distinctness alone would not suffice. The clique polynomial's value at a Boolean point with `s` ones is `(s-k)(s-k-1)>=0`. Multiaffinity gives continuous validity. Equality products turn the cut into trace at most `k+1/4`, and the separately attained linear-cost minimum gives exact perturbed root values. One cut per relative block or reusable exact component certificates defeats the specified global-cover obstruction.
