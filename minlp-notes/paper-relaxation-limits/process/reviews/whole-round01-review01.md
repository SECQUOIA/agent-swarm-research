## Independent verification

### Foundations and common probability laws

I reconstructed the endpoint interpolation argument: independently replacing each coordinate by a Bernoulli endpoint preserves every multiaffine monomial's expectation. Consequently the finite vertex-law LP gives the exact graph hull, including boundary marginals. The common upper envelope and the nonnegative expansion comparison are used in the correct direction. Fixed coordinates, affine terms and zero widths do not require division by zero or change the comparisons.

For signed bilinear functions I checked the induced-cut identity at half-grid points, the half-integral cell vertices, and the concavity interpolation that extends the bound. Polarization contributes the stated factor two. The row estimate, weighted orientation load, maximum-degree and bipartite specializations use consistent constants. Full-center weighted coefficients and optimization over full signings remain distinct assertions.

For the positive common laws I checked the marginal integrals rather than assuming that separate monomial witnesses combine. The harmonic truncation has the claimed mean; inactive cutoff mass gives the stated loss. The one-low cases include coordinates exactly at one half. The tangent-mixture bound uses convexity of the correct scalar function, includes the `d-1=1` case, and the finite Lambert bound implies the stated asymptotics with the required parameter range. The dyadic and radix constructions have nonnegative mixing weights and the correct mean; their bit/digit reversal events provide simultaneous hits. Padding does not change the claimed degree/dimension quantifiers.

For the cubic law I rederived the deficiency combination `18 O + 6 I + 7 B` and its case split. The mixture-only optimality claim is appropriately restricted. The Bernstein argument and the finite rational witnesses support their respective, different conclusions. In the equal-means transfer, group averaging preserves the two hull endpoints, while concentration dominates the finite union over assignments; the error divided by the degree-scale quantity vanishes for the stated degrees.

The owner/anchor construction is compatible across edges: disjoint owned sets prevent the apparent conflict from shared anchors. The marginal-floor completion normalizes and also handles endpoint probabilities. In feedback gluing, the common law dominates every prescribed cell by the factor `2^-f`; subtracting the same common component permits the remaining forest laws to glue without paying the loss again. For positive boxes, balancing is performed in the ambient dimension before restriction. I checked the cancellation in coefficient spreading and the endpoint/overflow binomial terms; balancing afresh inside each monomial would not be the same proof. The regularity hypothesis is only needed for the stated coefficient orders.
