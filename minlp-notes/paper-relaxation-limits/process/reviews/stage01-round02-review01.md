**Independent verification.**

1. **Envelopes and simultaneous laws.** Multiaffinity makes independent endpoint rounding preserve both the mean and the scalar function value, so the full graph hull equals the vertex graph hull. The fixed-mean probability polytope is nonempty and compact. This establishes attained lower and upper envelope formulas, including boundary points after fixed coordinates are removed. The termwise relaxation permits a separate law for each factor; its upper and lower vertical bounds are sums of the corresponding factor bounds, giving `0 <= H <= T` with the stated direction. A convex mixture of globally admissible laws preserves the entire mean vector. For the positive-monomial deficiency formulas, the common threshold law simultaneously attains every upper envelope, and each deficiency is pointwise nonnegative. Thus summing law guarantees is legitimate; independently optimizing individual factors would not establish the full-envelope bound.

2. **Expansion and domains.** On nonnegative boxes the affine expansion has nonnegative coefficients. The scalar graph hull is preserved by the coordinate bijection, while each original factor hull is contained in the relaxation obtained by separately convexifying its expansion. Hence the original termwise gap is at most the expanded termwise gap. The manuscript uses this inequality in the correct direction and warns that incidence structure changes. The separate warning about recursive McCormick relaxations is warranted by the original positive-box example in Luedtke et al. The repaired introduction includes the necessary nonnegative-box qualifier.

3. **Induced-cut characterization.** Pairing any sign vector with its global negative attains each quadratic extremum with zero sign means. This gives `H = R_W/2` and `T = L_W/2` at the required half-valued face points. Fixing outside coordinates to one only adds affine terms. I checked the cell argument: active equality/complement relations either fix values in `{0,1/2,1}` or leave a feasible local direction, so every cell vertex is half-integral. Since `H` is concave and `T` is affine on a cell, `T-cH` is convex there for `c >= 0`; the interpolation inequality has the correct direction. The zero-support convention is consistent with this proof.

4. **Graph bounds and quantifiers.** Polarization gives the exact rectangular sign norm after dividing the quadratic oscillation by two. A locally maximal cut for squared coefficients makes every crossing row norm at least its full row norm divided by `sqrt(2)`. Applying the sharp Khinchin bound on each side then yields `R >= sum_i ||a_i||_2/4`. The fractional-orientation network proves precisely the load bound `rho`, including edgeless graphs. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz and the row inequality give the constant 4; direct row degree bounds give `2 sqrt(Delta)`. The bipartite bound is attained by the stated frustrated four-cycle. The random-sign calculation uses `2^h` exponential terms after identifying global sign reversals, yielding `sqrt(2mh log 2)`. Extension off a densest induced face retains full support without changing the witness. The whole-center supremum and nonzero-coefficient approximation argument avoid assuming continuity of `c*` when support disappears.
