**Independent verification.** The following are analytic reconstructions, supplemented where indicated by finite exact checks.

1. **Optimum, chord model, witnesses, and counting.** A nonvertex of the balance slice with two fractional coordinates admits opposite perturbations. Its vertices have exactly one half coordinate, giving penalty optimum `1/4`. A convex coordinate underestimator lies below the endpoint chord, including on a singleton interval. The manuscript correctly uses an infimum for arbitrary underestimators. The McCormick substitution and the minimum convexifying alphaBB parameter both give that chord.

   In a witness-containing box, the zero and unit witness coordinates contribute zero chord value; unrestricted intervals are exactly `[0,1]` and contribute zero. Certification at positive target forces `|M intersect R| > 2m T_*`. Endpoint exclusions imply two avoidance events, whose joint probability is bounded by the smaller marginal probability without any independence assumption. Since `|A|+|D| >= |R|`, the reciprocal uniform cover bound is the **minimum** in `N(q)`, not the obsolete maximum. Empty endpoint classes give a base of one; `q=0` is harmless. Overlap and arbitrary real split points do not affect the union bound.

   The positive-tolerance construction has four new nodes per surviving prefix, hence `2^(n+2)-3` nodes and `2^(n+1)-1` leaves. The exact midpoint stopping counts are `k+1` high intervals or `n-k` low intervals. Pascal's recurrence gives `binomial(n+1,k+1)` leaves, with fixed-exponent polynomial size `O(n^min(k+1,n-k))`. The second-order error follows from maximizing each product `(x-a)(b-x)` at the interval midpoint.

   Charging is stated for final and discarded certified regions, which is what preserves the witness cover. It does not silently replace an uncertified open slab by its closure. For `0 <= delta < 1/2`, every allowed total lies strictly between `k` and `k+1`; minimizing its fractional-coordinate penalty gives `1/4-delta^2`. The lower bound then uses positive target `1/4-epsilon-delta^2`. The excluded integer-sum boundary is explicitly discussed.

2. **Full SDP–RLT.** On free coordinates, the covariance is exactly
   `t(s-t)/(s(s-1)) * (I-J/s)`; all restricted covariance rows vanish. The hypotheses leave at least one coordinate of each endpoint type, so `s>=2` and `1<=t<=s-1`. This proves the complete augmented PSD condition. For distinct free indices, the four RLT expectations are `d`, `c-d`, `c-d`, and `(s-t)(s-t-1)/(s(s-1))`. Repeated free indices give `c,0,0,1-c`. A restricted index makes the relevant slack deterministic, covering repeated restricted indices and degenerate boxes. Row sums are `K mu_i`, and the objective is precisely `|M intersect R|p(1-p)`.

   Order one of the defined box preordering supplies exactly these moment, RLT, first-bound and balance-product conditions. The affine-Farkas closure is valid on a nonempty box/balance polytope, with a nonnegative constant included and no strict-feasibility assumption. Expanding products uses only granted inequalities and equality products. This closure concerns original-space affine inequalities; it does not include the lifted clique cut.

3. **Fractional moments and every localizer.** The cardinality recurrence holds for squarefree multiplier size at most `2d-1 <= s-1`, so it never requests a zero denominator. The homogeneous replacement has positive denominator under `t>=2d-1`, including `d=1,t=1`. Its difference from the original monomial is the cardinality polynomial times a quotient of degree at most `d-1` in the Boolean quotient. Multiplying by `P+H` stays within the proved multiplier degree `2d-1`.

   For intersection size `h`, factoring the Gram numerator as `(t)_(2d-h)` and applying falling-factorial Vandermonde gives the displayed matrix entry. No cancellation by this possibly zero factor is needed. Each incidence matrix is a Gram matrix and every coefficient is nonnegative in the stated sufficient range.

   The uncancelled assignment formula is
   `E[I_(A,B) u_C] = (t)_(a+|C|)(s-t)_b/(s)_(a+b+|C|)`.
   Repeated slacks reduce to an indicator; conflicting slacks reduce to zero. If the square polynomial is nonconstant, the original degree budget forces `a+b<=2r-2`, making the conditioning weight strictly positive. After fixing assignments, remaining square degree `d` satisfies `s-a-b>=2d`, `t-a>=2d-1`, and `s-t-b>=2d-1`. Thus the Gram lemma applies even to globally coupled squares. Constant, zero-weight, and all-assigned cases are treated without undefined conditional moments. The negative product of `floor(t)+2` lower slacks verifies why stronger classical square positivity alone does not enlarge the noninteger full-preordering range.

4. **Threshold, perturbation, and increasing costs.** The integer comparison `|R|<k-2r+2` leaves at least `2r-1` unit coordinates; the same holds for zero coordinates. The separate `r=1` dimension argument is correct. The constructed objective is strictly below the target even when `R` is empty. This yields exactly
   `q_r=min(k-2r+2,z-2r+2,m(1/2-2epsilon))`.
   The positivity boundary, linear-order regime, `epsilon=1/8` specialization, and trivial nonpositive-threshold cases all agree with the formulas.

   Strict concavity puts every minimizer at a vertex. Strictly increasing coefficients select the unique sorted vertex, and the mass-transfer argument separately establishes its linear-cost optimality on the whole slice. First moments remain in `[0,1]`. Adding perturbations of total mass at most `eta` therefore costs at most `eta` in the witness functional, giving the stated perturbed threshold and `n/32` example. On a single balance slice a preserved coefficient vector can change only by a constant vector, and equal coefficient sums force that constant to vanish. Adding conserved flow changes absolute bounds by exactly `K`; the manuscript correctly declines to infer a fixed relative bound from this shift.

5. **Arbitrary coordinate sets and no order loss.** The proof uses actual membership of witness values and endpoints, so disconnectedness, nonclosedness and noncompact lifted graphs do not obstruct it. After fixing restricted coordinates, every unrestricted local inequality has nonnegative values at zero and one. Its Boolean reduction is their affine interpolation. Grouping repeated factors gives nonnegative coefficients on assignment indicators. Every coordinate with a nonconstant reduced factor consumes at least one original factor degree, preserving the localizer budget for every global square.

   For graph auxiliaries, each lifted variable has an affine or constant image under `Phi`, independently of its defining function's degree or regularity. Hence `deg Phi(P)<=deg P`. Retained original coordinates make the balance image exactly the earlier affine cardinality equation. Every local equality has zero Boolean reduction, as do its allowed global multiples. Full-product-graph identities have the same property, because every substituted Boolean assignment is a true graph tuple.

   Full-graph objective agreement is the decisive objective step: the substituted lifted objective and the intended substituted quadratic agree at every Boolean point, so their unique multilinear reductions agree. Both are within degree `2r`. This establishes objective equality as a statement about the functional; it asserts no affine graph identity at continuous interior points. Functions finite on all of `[0,1]` preserve all actual witness tuples and the original optimization problem. The exponent counts originals. The literal-substitution comparison is independently valid since `1+D(2r-1)<=2rD`, and remains coarser.

6. **Global tensor and relative model.** For any product of local factors and a global square of degree `d`, each block localizing matrix indexed through degree `d` is available because `deg(g_b)+2d<=2r`. Tensoring these PSD matrices and taking the principal submatrix with total index degree at most `d` proves the entire global localizer. Entries outside that principal submatrix need not be defined as global moments. Equality products vanish by factoring monomials blockwise. The proof remains valid with graph auxiliaries.

   Light blocks supply fractional moments; heavy blocks supply actual witness evaluation. Both have penalty at most `|R_b|/(2q_0)`. Adding conserved flow and perturbation gives `GK+sum_b|R_b|/(2q_0)+G eta`, so certification forces `sum_b|R_b|>=2q_0 G tau`. Independence across blocks, unlike the within-block avoidance events, is present and justifies multiplying witness fractions. Thus the exponent is `q_0 G tau`; the explicit example gives `tau=17/128` and `17n/384`. The positive-tau regime and fixed-order/fixed-tolerance quantifiers are correct.

   A coupled written lifted objective is covered: its full Boolean reduction equals that of the original separable objective after the block substitutions. The tensor functional annihilates Boolean identities in every remaining formal coordinate. No written block-separability hypothesis is needed.

   A feasible-set permutation preserves the balance row space and consequently maps whole blocks to whole blocks. Squared-index coefficient lists have different successive differences across blocks; they cannot be translates. A finite list cannot be its own nonzero translate. This proves the stated stronger asymmetry, for which distinctness alone would not suffice.

7. **Escape cuts and scope.** Padberg's cut becomes `(s-k)(s-k-1)` at Boolean vertices; multiaffinity gives validity throughout the continuous cube. Balance products imply `sum_ij X_ij=K^2`, and the cut then gives `trace(X)<=k+1/4`. Adding the separately attained linear-cost minimum proves exactness for the perturbed root. Actual optimizer evaluation proves attainment. PSD is unnecessary for this deduction. One cut per block closes the relative root. The alternative fixed-dimensional component certificates and the explicit optimizer prevent any reading of the cover theorem as inherent optimization hardness.
