\begin{abstract}
Separate convexification of nonlinear terms can lose information about their
shared variables. We develop a probability formulation of this loss and
explain its relation to spatial optimality certificates. For bilinear
functions with arbitrary coefficient signs, an elementary cut argument bounds
the ratio of the McCormick gap to the scalar graph-hull gap by four times the
square root of the largest induced edge density. A random-sign construction
gives the matching order on each individual interaction graph. Sharper
maximum-degree and bipartite bounds, exactness criteria, and the distinction
between exact termwise convexification and recursive product relaxations
identify the scope of these comparisons. The density order is also a
consequence of earlier Schur-multiplier theory; the explicit transfer and
proof are included to make that attribution precise.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
Convex relaxations support global optimization by providing bounds on
nonlinear functions over a region. Their strength depends on how the model
is divided into pieces. Even when each piece is replaced by its exact convex
hull, the resulting bounds can be weaker than the hull of their sum: separate
pieces may use incompatible representations of their common variables.
Spatial subdivision introduces a second question, namely how many regions
are needed before a specified bounding rule certifies a desired tolerance.
These questions concern different mathematical objects. A pointwise envelope
gap comparison is not an approximation ratio for a constrained optimization
problem, and it does not by itself bound a branch-and-bound tree.

McCormick's construction provides the basic recursive mechanism for
factorable functions \citep{McCormick1976}. For multilinear functions, the
convex hull of the scalar graph has a finite representation using box
vertices. Luedtke, Namazifar, and Linderoth \citep{Luedtke2012} compare this
hull with termwise and recursive relaxations, and establish several exactness
and positive-coefficient bounds. Boland et al.\ \citep{Boland2017} show that
signed bilinear gaps can grow with the square root of dimension and relate
the comparison to cuts in induced interaction graphs.

We use the vertex representation to state the comparisons consistently.
For positive multilinear functions on nonnegative boxes, a common probability distribution
attains all upper term envelopes, so the entire gap question reduces to a
single simultaneous rounding problem. For signed bilinear functions, an
exact cut-range characterization instead permits graph density and degree
bounds. The elementary density proof makes the constants explicit, while
the accompanying Schur-multiplier transfer explains which asymptotic content
was already implicit in established theory \citep{DavidsonDonsig2007}.
The framework also specifies the bounding oracle and the certified-region
count needed for spatial lower bounds.
