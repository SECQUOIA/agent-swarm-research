## Minor finding

1. **Exclude the zero vector from the witness assertion.** In `sections/08-four-aggregation.tex:349`–351, “any multiplier in [the four-ray cone] is strictly negative unless it is a positive multiple of r_j” includes the zero vector, since the displayed cone includes zero. Its polynomial is zero at the witness, and it is not a positive multiple of r_j. Change this to “any **nonzero** multiplier in [the cone] …” or “any good multiplier …”. This does not affect the theorem: good multipliers were expressly defined to be nonzero, and only those are used in the ensuing indispensability argument.

## Mathematical checks

- Checked the external theorem against BD arXiv:2405.18282v1, Introduction and Section 8, including the standing independence assumption. The paper correctly requires nonempty interior, closure of the interior, signed homogeneous PDLC, and no nonstrict points at infinity. The source uses closed convex hull notation; the manuscript transcribes that explicitly. The preprint expressly removes spectral smoothness in this part, and its Proposition 8.7 covers n=1,2; the final proof combines Propositions 8.6, 8.7, and 8.10. Thus the use in every n>=1 is supported, rather than inferred from a theorem restricted to n>=3.
- The regular-sublevel lemma is valid on a space with a countable base. Each exceptional value is the infimum on a nonempty basic open set containing a bad level point. The sign in applying this lemma to h=max_i(f_i/w_i), with level -epsilon, is correct. Avoiding a countable exceptional set and a finite dependence set still permits a decreasing positive sequence tending to zero.
- Checked the explicit R_i in dimension n+1=2 as well as higher dimensions. The off-diagonal entry and two diagonal entries prove independence. Their leading blocks are positive definite, including n=1. A suitable coordinate minor of Q_i+epsilon R_i has nonzero cubic leading coefficient, regardless of dependence of the original triple.
- Properness of the original hull excludes a common strict negative leading direction by the accepted lemma. Subtracting epsilon times each positive leading form converts a putative nonpositive perturbed direction to that excluded direction. Dividing an unbounded feasible sequence by its norm yields precisely a forbidden nonzero direction; hence each perturbed nonstrict set is compact. Signed PDLC persists by continuity, without an incorrect nonnegative-sign assumption on its certificate. Strict feasibility, regularity, and eventual inclusion of each fixed finite feasible subset are all established.
- Strictification correctly deletes globally nonpositive polynomials. A retained quadratic cannot have a zero in the interior of its nonpositive sublevel: the exact Taylor formula at a local maximum proves global nonpositivity. Interior commutes with the finite intersection. The hull of a nonempty open set is open, and an open convex set is the interior of its closure. Boundedness excludes deletion of every row.
- The four-tuple limit has fixed finite cardinality, nonzero simplex limits, and convergent matrices. Eigenvalue continuity supplies at most one negative eigenvalue; strict negativity at the common feasible lift supplies at least one. The negative eigenvalue is consequently simple and stays separated from zero in the limit. Subsequence convergence of oriented unit eigenvectors is legitimate.
- The goodness limit is not merely an interchange of strict inequalities with limits. For each fixed x in S, eventual membership in S_k makes the lifted segment to x^0 negative for every retained aggregate. This fixes its eigenvector sign. Original strict feasibility excludes zero limiting scalar product. The singular one-negative-eigenvalue decomposition then puts all of S in the same convex negative component. This establishes strict goodness on the entire ordinary hull. Conversely, finitely many strict limiting inequalities imply eventual satisfaction of the perturbed descriptions, proving the reverse inclusion without taking a closure.
- The dependent-triple remark uses a compact normalized cone slice: evaluating the generators negatively at one feasible lift makes the slice the convex hull of finitely many normalized generators. In dimension at most two it is a segment or point whose endpoints are generator rays. The reduction preserves strict inequalities, and Yildiran's nonempty two-constraint theorem gives the claimed good multipliers after sign conversion. Under properness its whole-space alternative is excluded.
- Recomputed the sharpness system, signed PDLC identity, feasible point, four ray slacks, and the cone decomposition. The repeated rho coefficient gives the necessary condition c<=a+b only when n>=3, which is exactly the stated sharpness scope. The proposed interval for t is nonempty under that condition. The four credited rays are good, and each witness excludes every nonzero good multiplier except its designated ray. The universal upper bound then makes the four-ray intersection exact. The minor zero-vector wording above is the only issue found here.
- The oriented SOC formula follows from the same negative components. Its weak version equals the closure because the common strict feasible point makes every segment from a weakly feasible point strictly feasible. The half-ball example satisfies PDLC after adding the redundant row and correctly distinguishes this closure from both naive weak quadratic replacement and the hull of the original weak system.
