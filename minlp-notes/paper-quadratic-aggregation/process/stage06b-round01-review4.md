## Mathematical audit

1. **Cone and directional bound.** Strict feasibility makes evaluation at
   one feasible lift strictly negative on every nonzero nonnegative
   multiplier. This rules out zero combinations, proves pointedness, and
   gives the compact polygon section used to equate the numbers of rays
   and facets. Retaining extreme generators preserves both the strict
   feasible set and the cone of aggregation matrices. The HHC proof has
   the needed dimension restriction: a homogeneous hyperplane has real
   dimension `n >= 3`, and restriction preserves the signed positive
   definite combination. The joint image of all rows is indeed a linear
   image of the three-basis-form image.

2. **Exit along a definite direction.** `J_-(D)` cannot be empty, because
   `D` cannot belong to the matrix cone. The displayed minimum is finite
   and nonnegative, keeps every facet inequality feasible, and activates
   a selected facet. A zero endpoint would force the initial good matrix
   to be negative definite, impossible for its one-negative-eigenvalue
   inertia. The manuscript explicitly verifies nonnegative endpoint
   coefficients and PSD coefficient difference, so the improvement lemma
   applies even when matrix representations are nonunique. Domination
   has the correct set-inclusion direction. Pair-support reduction on the
   selected facets then gives exactly the stated upper bound, allowing
   empty facets and duplicate endpoint cuts. The perturbation
   `D = E + delta D0` establishing the conditional `2k-2` bound has the
   correct strict sign and requires no closure assumption on the original
   weak set.

3. **Ellipsoid family.** The Vandermonde sign and witness identity are
   correct. Positive leading coefficients give boundedness, convexity,
   and strict feasibility. Each witness exposes exactly its original
   generator ray, which proves both the cone count and the necessity of
   every original ray in any exact strict family. This argument is valid
   even for an infinite strict family: membership in an intersection is
   pointwise, so excluding the witness requires one nonnegative value.
   The separate finite weak argument correctly uses a common open
   neighborhood and a radial perturbation; it makes no invalid claim
   about infinite weak families. Shrinking toward zero proves equality
   of the weak set with the closure.

4. **Augmented family.** The two negative square rows do change the strict
   set. The generic small midpoint perturbation proves `conv(V) = U`
   without assuming these rows are strictly redundant. Both extra rows
   are strictly negative at each ellipsoid witness, preserving the exact
   indispensability argument. The zero constant coordinate exposes the
   two-dimensional face generated by `-E1,-E2`, so those rays are extreme;
   the old exposed rays remain extreme and distinct. Thus `k = m+2`.
   The displayed nonnegative identity produces `-E3`. The PSD cone in
   this diagonal three-dimensional span is exactly the nonnegative
   orthant, including the repeated `rho` coefficient, so the claimed
   complementary cone inclusion follows.

5. **Closed-set hypotheses and scope.** The augmented weak set is the
   original compact intersection of positive definite ellipsoids. Its
   interior is exactly `U`, and radial shrinking proves regularity.
   Positive definite original leading matrices rule out all nonzero
   common directions at infinity. The finite weak witness argument
   continues to work with the added rows. Therefore the exact count
   `m=k-2` is valid for strict descriptions and finite weak descriptions.
   This disproves the proposed general-cone two-aggregation extension,
   while leaving an unconditional `2k-2` upper bound undecided. The last
   paragraph states that distinction expressly. Its warning about
   subsystem intersections and its separate inertia example are correct.
