## Detailed mathematical checks

- The Gram-fiber proof treats singular Gram matrices correctly: the positive-eigenvalue rows can be extended to an orthonormal row system when the replication count is at least the row count. The trace maximum and both signs are attained. The even-dimensional rotation path and the odd-dimensional path leaving the smallest singular axis fixed give the required full interval; the latter reaches a nonpositive value because the sum of singular values is at least three times the smallest one. The separate scalar exception is necessary and is stated correctly. For fewer columns, the rank obstruction on `t=0` proves failure. Zero hyperplane coefficients and zero Gram matrices do not create missing cases.
- The squared-fidelity variational proof has the correct order of matrix factors in Cauchy--Schwarz. The positive definite optimizer solves `ZGZ=H`. Regularizing both matrices proves the singular case without requiring a singular inverse. Taking an infimum of linear functions proves the separate concavity needed for the hyperplane image, without claiming joint concavity of the squared quantity.
- The good cone of the two-ball example follows from eigenvalue replication, and the negative scalar block is strictly negative for every nonzero cone member. The ray witnesses isolate precisely the stated positive ray; the finite weak-family argument preserves strict slack before producing a positive violation of an omitted valid inequality.
- The direct two-point hull construction is valid already for `r=2`. Perpendicularity to `b u-a v` gives the required equality of scaled inner products. The chosen positive `k<1` makes both norm constraints strict and the inner product greater than one half. The two roots have opposite signs, and the displayed unequal weights recover the original point. No direction perpendicular to both original vectors is assumed. The Schur-complement lift and the closure argument handle zero slacks and the scalar lift at exactly one half. Compactness of the original weak set justifies the ordinary weak-hull equality.
- The arbitrary-quadratic obstruction is sound. The rational radicand has simple zeros and poles, so it is not a rational square. Primitivity and Gauss's lemma transfer divisibility to the irreducible quartic. A nonzero quadratic therefore has finitely many zeros on the compact analytic arc. Strict inequalities cannot have identically zero planar restrictions because the planar origin lies in the open hull. The countable strict argument and the finite weak neighborhood argument use different, appropriate cardinality steps. Neither is overextended to arbitrary Boolean formulas or lifts.
- The angle approximation uses only nonnegative directions, not positive semidefiniteness of the displayed two-by-two matrix. Endpoint cuts provide both ball bounds; the operator norm and second derivative estimates give the stated defect. The radial correction has the correct scaling, and its norm estimate gives the claimed upper constant. Rational meshes have `2m+1` distinct multipliers, the correct angle gaps, exact cone membership, and the stated conservative bit bound.
- The lower-bound proof permits interior multipliers and arbitrary rescaling. Its perturbed Gram matrices remain positive definite. If a cut excludes two witnesses, the AM--GM estimate yields squared parameter distance less than `16(a^2-1)=4/(5N^2)+1/(100N^4)`, which is below the grid spacing squared even at `N=1`. The surviving witness violates its own valid multiplier by `2 eta`; the gradient bound on the convex product of balls yields exactly `sqrt(2)/(2000 N^2)`. Unbounded relaxations cause no exception.
- The support-function identity uses projection onto a compact convex set correctly. In the one-objective result, the enlarged value set is convex by cone-convexity of the quadratic map and has nonempty interior. The included boundary point can be supported via the closure without assuming the value set is closed. Recession directions put the multiplier in the correct dual cone; strict negativity at the origin forces a positive objective weight. Complementarity then supplies the common attained minimum. The manuscript correctly avoids presenting this as an algorithm for finding the multiplier.

In particular, the coefficient-cone normalization eliminates the constant before taking a limit; closedness of the finite coefficient cone recovers actual nonnegative weights.
