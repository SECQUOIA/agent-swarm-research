## Mathematical assessment

1. **Certificate theorem and small dimensions.** The proof correctly separates the nonclosed homogeneous image from the open negative orthant. The supporting-halfspace argument excludes homogeneous points with either sign of the final coordinate and uses the negative-direction lemma for its zero coordinate. Eliminating the constant before normalizing the quadratic/linear coefficient pair handles cancellation; finite generation really supplies closedness of the coefficient cone. The coefficient subsequence is fixed before testing arbitrary vectors. The proof does not introduce an accidental n >= 2 assumption. The additional emptiness characterization appropriately requires n >= 2, and the imported all-good description appropriately requires n >= 3.
2. **Closed systems and Shor projection.** The cone-image proof retains closure exactly where needed and then strengthens the whole-space conclusion using the strict feasible point and a reflected target. The explicit nonclosed projection calculations, including the compact variant, check out. The text does not conflate conv(T), closure(conv(S)), and a projection of a closed spectrahedron.
3. **Gram fibers and singular matrices.** The extension of normalized positive-eigenvalue rows to a rectangular orthonormal frame handles singular Gram matrices. The even/odd rotation argument attains the entire trace interval; its odd-dimensional endpoint estimate is sufficient. The exceptional k = r = 1 image formula is explicitly excluded while HHC is separately proved. Fidelity regularization supplies the singular case, and the rank obstruction proves necessity of r >= k.
4. **Exact hulls and cardinality.** The two-point construction works when r = 2 and when bu - av = 0; its roots have opposite signs, its weights are positive, and each displayed feasibility expansion has the required strict sign. The closure argument handles a lift with sigma = 1/2 by a small increase after obtaining positive definiteness. The arbitrary-quadratic obstruction correctly distinguishes countably many strict inequalities from finitely many weak inequalities. Its restricted polynomials cannot vanish identically in the strict case because the planar origin is feasible; analytic zeros on a compact arc are finite unless identically zero, which irreducibility excludes. No stronger countable weak obstruction is asserted.
5. **Approximation and duality.** The angular proof tests the positive quadrant rather than incorrectly imposing PSD on B(x). The mesh bound includes N = 2, and the lower proof permits arbitrary interior multipliers and scales. The grid separation constants and gradient-to-distance conversion agree with sqrt(2)/(2000 N^2). The one-objective proof supports the closure of a solid convex image at an included boundary point and does not silently assume that image is closed. Strict feasibility forces the objective multiplier to be positive, and common attainment follows from complementarity. The distinction between uniform representation and one-objective optimization is clear.
6. **PDLC strict transfer.** The chosen perturbation matrices are independent even when n = 1. Avoiding finitely many determinant zeros and countably many irregular levels is justified. Properness supplies the absence of common strictly negative leading directions, which becomes the required no-points-at-infinity condition after perturbation. The strictification lemma removes globally nonpositive quadratics before taking interiors. The limit controls the actual negative components and thereby strict validity; reversing the inclusion uses only finitely many strict limiting inequalities. The primary BD text explicitly covers the n = 1,2 branch and states the regular weak four-bound used here. Singular limiting matrices are covered by the component decomposition. The separate oriented SOC closure avoids the weak-sign counterexample.
7. **Appendices.** The local-certificate LP has the correct box-minimum inequality direction and normalized equality extension. Its rational example and tangent calculation are consistent. The directional facet bound uses the PSD-improvement lemma only for a nonredundant good cut and checks its nonnegative coefficient hypothesis. The pair-support reduction uses goodness relative to the full set, as the cited BDS Proposition 9.6 requires. The enlarged ellipsoid family proves precisely the claimed complementary-cone obstruction and does not claim to refute an unconditional 2k - 2 bound.
