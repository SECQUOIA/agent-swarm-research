## Mathematical assessment

1. **Certificate theorem and hypotheses.** The no-negative-leading-direction midpoint argument, strict supporting halfspace, and treatment of both signs of the homogenizing variable are correct. Separation only requires one set to be open, so no unproved closedness of the quadratic image is used. Strict feasibility bounds the constant coefficient from above with the right sign. Nonvanishing of the coefficient pair is established before division. Its normalization uses a closed, finitely generated coefficient cone; compactness gives one subsequence valid for every fixed test vector. Recovery of actual nonnegative weights from cone membership is justified. The quantitative alternative has the correct norm and eigenvalue directions. AHC is demonstrably weaker than HHC, and the result's low-dimensional quantifiers are consistent.

2. **Closed systems and Shor projection.** The distinction between properness and exactness is maintained. For the Shor result, the cone image need not be closed; its dual is exactly the PSD aggregation cone, and the bipolar step produces a closure. The quadratic mixing identity has the correct sign and puts mixtures with a strict point in the cone's interior. The midpoint argument for the actual whole-space projection avoids confusing dense projection with attained feasibility. I checked both nonclosed-projection descriptions directly using the PSD covariance matrix and its forced zero off-diagonal entry when the first diagonal entry vanishes. The compact variant meets the stated finite-generated-cone representation condition. The manuscript's qualification of Kojima–Tunçel is warranted: the inspected proof explicitly uses an unqualified sum-of-duals identity, whereas the presented examples show why a closure qualification matters. The manuscript correctly credits the earlier closure result rather than presenting it as new.

3. **Examples delimiting assumptions.** The ordinary hidden-convexity counterexample has the stated proper hull and only trivial PSD aggregates; the hyperplane midpoint obstruction survives in the three-row reduction. The empty strict system with a full-dimensional proper closed hull has HHC through its two-form reduction, and the asserted absence of closed-system good multipliers follows from two genuinely negative homogeneous eigenvalues. The strip example correctly separates existence of a convex certificate from a full hull description.

4. **Gram criterion and infinite example.** The Gram-factor extension handles singular matrices. The trace-range argument accounts for the disconnected real orthogonal group and the exceptional scalar case. Squared-fidelity concavity follows from the stated classical variational formula, including the singular limit. The rank obstruction proves sharpness. The repeated eigenvalues identify the good cone of the two-ball example exactly. The indispensable-ray witnesses satisfy the required positive-definite Gram constraints. The new two-point proof works for every replication count at least two: perpendicularity to `b u - a v`, equal normalized scalar products, and the two roots give the claimed norm and inner-product increments. The closure and compact weak-hull steps are valid. The analytic quartic-arc proof correctly distinguishes the countable strict obstruction from the finite nonstrict obstruction.

5. **Approximation and objective exactness.** Endpoint cuts bound the sampled relaxation; the second-derivative bound and radial correction have the correct constants. The rational mesh counts distinct rays correctly. In the lower bound, an arbitrary interior multiplier can exclude at most one grid witness, and the Lipschitz conversion gives the displayed Euclidean distance constant. Support-function equality is used only for bounded relaxations. The one-objective proof supplies a supporting hyperplane without assuming its auxiliary convex set is closed, uses strict feasibility to rule out a zero objective multiplier, and proves common attainment. It does not imply an algorithmic complexity claim.

6. **Four-bound.** The external theorem is stated with its necessary regularity and infinity conditions and is applicable in the positive dimensions asserted. The inward perturbations enforce no points at infinity, boundedness, strict feasibility, independence away from finitely many parameters, and generic-level regularity. Strictification explicitly removes globally nonpositive redundant rows. The limiting multipliers retain exactly one negative eigenvalue and a consistent negative component; strict validity is not inferred merely by continuity. Conversely, finitely many strict limit inequalities persist for a sufficiently late perturbation. The sharpness witnesses and oriented closed SOC representation are consistent. Prior four-bound and sharpness credit is clear.

7. **Appendices.** The local certificate and diagonal-dominance LP have correct inequality directions, normalization, and exact margins; the application carefully distinguishes strength over a termwise relaxation from strength over the Shor lift. The three-span facet argument uses goodness relative to the full system, and its cited pair-support reduction has the required hypotheses. The ellipsoid witnesses and added negative-square rows prove the claimed unbounded necessary family in the complementary-cone case without purporting to refute the weaker `2k-2` bound.

## Whole-paper coherence, scope, and readability

The opening distinction among an existence certificate, an exact description, and a finite approximation gives the broad manuscript a coherent structure. Strict and nonstrict inequalities, ordinary hulls and closed hulls, globally convex and good aggregations, and signed PDLC versus nonnegative weights are handled consistently across sections. The independent two-point proof prevents an accidental dependence on an inapplicable dimension threshold. The formal-verification section explicitly separates paper statements from formalized variants and constants, and it lists major results outside the formal scope. The appendices are relevant applications and limitations rather than unproved extensions of the main claims.

Novelty language is qualified and specific. The paper credits classical separation, two-form theory, fidelity identities, earlier infinite-aggregation examples, SDP convexification, approximation exponents, the prior regular weak four-bound, and the known four-necessary example. Its three conjecture-resolution claims match the inspected conjectures. My source checks support these distinctions; they are not a claim to have exhaustively searched all literature independently of the documented literature review.
