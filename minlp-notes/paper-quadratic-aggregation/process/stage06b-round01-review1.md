## Proof and quantifier audit

- **Cone geometry and retained rows.** Strict feasibility makes evaluation at a feasible lift negative on every nonzero nonnegative combination of the generators. Consequently no such combination is zero, the matrix cone is pointed, and its evaluation-normalized slice is the convex hull of normalized generators. Since the linear span has dimension three, that slice is a genuine two-dimensional compact polygon. It has equally many vertices and edges; this justifies k extreme rays, k facets, and exactly two extreme generators per facet. Deleting other rows preserves both the strict feasible set and the matrix cone.
- **HHC and the full hull theorem.** Restricting the three basis forms to a homogeneous hyperplane retains a positive definite combination, even if their restrictions become dependent. The hyperplane has dimension n>=3, as required by the stated three-form convexity implication. The original many-form image is a linear image of the resulting convex image. Thus all the hypotheses used for BDS's full-system good-aggregation description are supplied.
- **Directional exit.** A positive definite D cannot belong to the matrix cone, by evaluating at a feasible lift. Hence J_-(D) is nonempty. The minimum ratio for t_* is finite and nonnegative; all other facets remain satisfied, including those evaluating to zero on D. This remains correct when t_*=0. A zero endpoint would make the initial matrix negative definite when t_*>0, contradicting its one-negative-eigenvalue inertia. The nonnegative representations at both ends explicitly meet the coefficient hypothesis of the PSD improvement result. Discarding whole-space sublevel cuts loses no restriction. Domination of every remaining good cut proves equality of the selected-facet intersection with the hull.
- **Two cuts per selected facet.** The reduction uses BDS Proposition 9.6 with the retained original pair and goodness relative to the entire original S. It does not infer goodness for the two-row subsystem, nor interchange convex hull with intersection. Empty facet families cause no difficulty. The hypotheses of Proposition 9.6 include HHC, nonemptiness, and properness, all already proved or assumed here. The resulting count is at most 2|J_-(D)|.
- **Conditional count.** For E in the PSD cone outside the negative matrix cone, at least one facet functional is positive. Perturbing by a sufficiently small positive multiple of a fixed positive definite D_0 keeps that strict positivity and makes the matrix positive definite. Thus at least one facet is outside J_-(D), establishing the conditional 2k−2 count with the stated implication direction.
- **Original ellipsoids.** Recomputed the Vandermonde determinant sign and witness values. The span is exactly three-dimensional for distinct parameters in (0,1), and its diagonal basis contains a positive definite homogeneous matrix. Each ellipsoid is bounded, convex, strictly feasible, and has exactly one negative homogeneous eigenvalue. At p_i precisely its own row is zero and every other row is negative. This proves both exposure of each original matrix ray and its indispensability in any strict aggregation family, including an infinite one. For a finite weak family, omitted-ray strict slack persists locally and radial dilation leaves the true closed hull, giving the asserted necessity. Zero weak inequalities, if included, are globally redundant and may be deleted before that argument. Shrinking toward the origin proves the matching weak description.
