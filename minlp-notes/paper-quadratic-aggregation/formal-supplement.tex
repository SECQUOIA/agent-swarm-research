This supplement to the paper \emph{Quadratic Aggregation: Certificates,
Finite Descriptions, and Approximation} describes five Lean packages and
states how each formal theorem corresponds to a mathematical statement of
the paper. Sections~\ref{sec:setting} and~\ref{sec:certificate} repeat
the setting and the certificate proof, so the core account is
self-contained; each later section states its own system. The portable
source project and the audit partitions are in \path{supplement/lean/}.

The packages verify: the certificate theorem under asymptotic hyperplane
convexity and its HHC specialization, with the separation and cone lemmas
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-verification}); closed-system properness, the
whole-space Shor criterion and its consequences, exact
signed-coordinate SDP tests, Dines'
theorem, and two boundary examples (Section~\ref{sec:formal-consequences});
HHC of the two-ball example, its good-aggregation cone, the continuum of
indispensable strict rays, and the absence of a finite weak
good-aggregation description (Section~\ref{sec:formal-infinite-aggregation});
the exact strict and closed hulls of that example and their finite
semidefinite lifts (Section~\ref{sec:formal-exact-hull}); and its
$\Theta(N^{-2})$ approximation bounds with rational meshes
(Section~\ref{sec:formal-aggregation-accuracy}).

The packages do not verify every result of the paper. Not covered are,
among others, the general sharp Gram-map theorem, the obstruction for arbitrary quadratic
descriptions, sufficiency of a countable dense weak family, the paper's
$2n+1$ trace-and-vector SDP test (only the signed-coordinate test is
formal), the paper's lower approximation constant $\sqrt2/2000$ (the
formal constant is $1/2000$), the four-aggregation PDLC theorem, the
many-row appendix, the local-certificate application, the
single-objective duality result, the sufficient criteria based on
three-form convexity, the general closure equality for the Shor
projection, and the paper's other counterexamples (only the two
boundary examples and the two-ball example are formal). The spectral
quantitative alternative to the certificate proof is not fully
formalized. Literature priority and numerical solver correctness are
outside every package.
