\section{Formal verification and reproducible supplements}
\label{sec:formal-overview}

The accompanying Lean development verifies substantial, specified parts
of the paper. It consists of 64 modules with 909 owned declarations,
together with five imported local support modules. The portable sources,
pinned Lean~4.33.1 toolchain and Mathlib dependency manifest, declaration
coverage maps, and targeted verification runner are included in
\path{supplement/lean/}. A separate detailed formal supplement describes
the theorem interfaces and their relation to the mathematical statements.
The following table records the scope of the five packages; the sizes list modules and owned declarations. The package
numbers identify the supplied audit partitions, not mathematical assumptions.

criterion and its AHC consequences, exact signed-coordinate SDP tests,

Two differences in constants and formulations matter. Package~28 checks
$n(n+1)+2n$ signed coordinate objectives; the shorter $2n+1$ trace-and-vector
test in Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-sdp} is proved in the paper but is not
that formal theorem. Package~31 proves the same upper bound as
Theorem~\ref{thm:approximation-rate} and the lower bound
$1/(2000N^2)$, which implies its original stated lower bound
$\sqrt2(\log2)^2/(1600N^2)$. The improved lower constant
$\sqrt2/2000$ used in this paper is not formalized. The formal
approximation model includes an explicit transport to Euclidean norm in
all $2r$ coordinates; it does not silently use a product maximum norm.

The formal definitions use actual feasible sets, ordinary convex hulls,
nonnegative aggregation weights, and homogeneous eigenvalues. In
particular, HHC of the example is proved rather than assumed, and the
exact hull package proves its two-point construction without importing a
general HHC hull theorem as an additional premise. The main certificate
proof likewise derives its separation weights and its nonzero coefficient
limit. These are not assumptions hidden in the formal theorem interfaces.

For each package, warning-free targeted compilation, a transitive axiom
audit of all owned declarations, and per-module kernel replay have passed
with recorded source fingerprints. The audit permits only
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
The supplied runner reproduces these checks for the portable project and
records whether its inputs remain unchanged. Kernel replay uses Lean's
kernel and imported dependencies; the dependencies are not all rebuilt
and individually replayed, and the check is not an independent
proof-assistant implementation. Exact rational example checks and the
figure-generation program are also supplied. Finite computations check
their stated identities and witnesses, not universal theorems by sampling.

This verification does not cover the general sharp Gram-map theorem,
the arbitrary-quadratic obstruction, countable dense weak sufficiency,
the improved lower constant, the
four-aggregation PDLC theorem, the many-row appendix, the local-certificate
application, or the single-objective duality result. The spectral
quantitative alternative to the certificate proof is not fully formalized.
Literature priority and numerical solver correctness are outside every
formal package. The paper supplies mathematical proofs and explicit
source comparisons for the results in these categories.
