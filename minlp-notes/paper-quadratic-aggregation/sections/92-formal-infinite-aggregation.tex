\section{Formal verification of the infinite-aggregation example}
\label{sec:formal-infinite-aggregation}

The third Lean package concerns the explicit family
\[
 S_r=\{(u,v)\in\R^r\times\R^r:
 \|u\|^2<1,\ \|v\|^2<1,\ u\transpose v>1/2\},\qquad r\geq2.
\]
Squared lengths and inner products in the formalization are Euclidean
coordinate sums. The package treats the actual quadratic map and ordinary
convex hull, including the four-variable case $r=2$.

\paragraph{Geometry and good multipliers.}
The set $S_r$ is nonempty and bounded, and its ordinary convex hull is
proper. Its homogeneous map is
\[
 (u,v,t)\longmapsto
 (\|u\|^2-t^2,\ \|v\|^2-t^2,\ t^2/2-u\transpose v).
\]
The formal HHC assertion requires convexity of this image on the kernel of
every nonzero real linear functional. It is proved for all $r\geq2$;
HHC is not supplied as an additional premise for this example.

The proof uses an explicit orthonormal frame and an algebraic unit-circle
construction to obtain the full range of a linear functional on a fixed
two-column Gram fiber. For a hyperplane
$\tr(P\transpose U)+st=0$, put $G=U\transpose U$, $H=P\transpose P$,
and $T=t^2$. Its exact feasible Gram domain is
\[
 G\succeq0,\qquad T\geq0,\qquad
 s^2T\leq\tr(HG)+2\sqrt{\det H\det G}.
\]
Both necessity and realization are proved, including singular Gram
matrices and zero coefficients. Concavity of the square root of the
two-by-two determinant proves convexity of this domain. The homogeneous
quadratic image is its linear image, which gives HHC.

A good multiplier is defined by the original conditions: it is nonzero
and nonnegative, its homogeneous aggregate has at most one negative
eigenvalue counted with multiplicity, and its strict aggregation contains
$\conv S_r$. The proved classification is
\[
 \lambda\text{ is good}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \lambda\geq0,\quad\lambda\ne0,\quad
 \lambda_3^2\leq4\lambda_1\lambda_2.
\]
The homogeneous matrix consists of $r$ copies of
\[
 B_\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_1&-\lambda_3/2\\
                         -\lambda_3/2&\lambda_2\end{pmatrix}
\]
and the scalar $\lambda_3/2-\lambda_1-\lambda_2$, up to coordinate order.
For nonnegative multipliers violating the discriminant inequality, the
repeated block gives at least $r$ negative eigenvalues. For nonzero
nonnegative multipliers satisfying it, the block is PSD and the scalar is
strictly negative, so there is exactly one negative eigenvalue. Convexity of the
original aggregate and its strict negativity on $S_r$ establish strict
negativity throughout the ordinary convex hull. Thus the classification
does not assume a general aggregation hull-description theorem.

\paragraph{Indispensable strict rays.}
For every $\tau\in[1,2]$, the package constructs actual vectors whose Gram
matrix is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1-1/(10\tau)&2/5\\2/5&1-\tau/10
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their point $x_\tau$ has residual vector
$\frac1{10}(-1/\tau,-\tau,1)$. Every good aggregate is nonpositive there,
with equality exactly for positive multiples of
$\rho(\tau)=(\tau,1/\tau,2)$. The multiplier $\rho(\tau)$ is itself good,
so $x_\tau\notin\conv S_r$; every good multiplier on a different ray is
strictly satisfied at this point.

Consequently, if a family of strict good aggregations describes
$\conv S_r$ exactly, it contains a positive multiple of $\rho(\tau)$ for
every $\tau\in[1,2]$. The formal cardinality argument normalizes weights
to third coordinate two and proves that the set of normalized weights is
uncountable. This counts distinct rays, not repeated indices in a family.
In particular, no countable family of strict good aggregations describes
the ordinary hull. This statement is conditional on an exact description;
the package does not separately prove that the intersection of all good
strict aggregations equals the hull.

\paragraph{The closed hull requires a different witness.}
For any finite family of good multipliers, choose an omitted ray and
decrease the Gram off-diagonal $2/5$ by a sufficiently small positive
amount. The perturbed Gram matrix remains positive definite and is
realized by actual vectors. Every selected aggregate is strictly negative
there, while the omitted aggregate is strictly positive. Every good
aggregate is nonpositive on $\cl(\conv S_r)$ by continuity. Hence the
perturbed point is outside this closed hull, although it satisfies every
selected weak aggregation. No finite intersection of weak good
aggregations can therefore equal $\cl(\conv S_r)$.

\paragraph{Scope and records.}
The Lean sources are in \texttt{Formal.InfiniteAggregation}; the frozen
claims and declaration coverage are in \path{supplement/lean/audits/29-infinite-aggregation/}.
This package does not establish an exact hull formula, countable weak
aggregation sufficiency, equality with the hull of the original weak
system, a finite SDP lift, an obstruction to arbitrary quadratic
descriptions, approximation bounds, or the general sharp Gram-map theorem.
It also makes no literature-priority claim.
