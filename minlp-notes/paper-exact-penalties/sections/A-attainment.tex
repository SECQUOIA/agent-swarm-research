\section{Remark on Example~13 of Lefebvre and Schmidt}\label{app:attainment}
Under the supremum-based Definition~1 of \citet{LefebvreSchmidt2025},
their Example~13 is value-exact at every $\rho\ge0$, although no finite
multiplier attains the dual value. The example therefore shows
nonattainment, not failure of value-exactness. It is a mixed-integer
counterpart of Example~\ref{ex:onesided}.

Definition~1 of \citet{LefebvreSchmidt2025} says that a problem admits
an exact penalty representation if the duality gap $v-D_\rho$ vanishes
for some finite $\rho$, where $D_\rho$ is the supremum over multipliers
as in \eqref{eq:model}. In our terms, this means $\rho_*<\infty$. The
source states that Example~13 admits no finite exact penalty
representation. After renaming the variables $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ as
$(u,y,z)$, the example has native set, objective and relaxed equality
\[
 X=\{(u,y,z)\in[-1,1]^2\times\{0,1\}:u+2z\le1,\ u^2\le y\},
 \qquad f=u-z/2,\qquad r=y,
\]
with $m=1$ and the absolute value as norm. The equality $y=0$ and the
constraint $u^2\le y$ force $u=0$, and then $2z\le1$ forces $z=0$. So
$(0,0,0)$ is the only feasible point and $v=0$.

\paragraph{Value-exactness.}
Every native point has residual $y\ge u^2\ge0$. As for one-sided
residuals in Section~\ref{sec:geometry}, every balanced mixture is then
supported on $F$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:mixture} gives $D_\rho=0=v$
for every $\rho\ge0$. Thus $\rho_*=0$, and the example satisfies
Definition~1.

\paragraph{No attaining multiplier.}
Fix $\lambda$ and $\rho$, and put $t=\lambda+\rho$. Since $y\ge0$ on
$X$, the penalized objective is $u-z/2+ty$. For $-1<u<0$, the point
$(u,u^2,0)$ is native, and its penalized value $u+tu^2=u(1+tu)$ is
negative whenever also $tu>-1$. Such $u$ exist for every finite $t$.
Hence $L_\rho(\lambda)<0=v$ for every finite $\lambda$ and $\rho$: no
multiplier attains $D_\rho$, and $\rho_*(\lambda)=+\infty$ for every
$\lambda$. Multipliers whose penalized values approach $v$ must
therefore grow without bound; a bounded sequence of them would have a
limit point attaining $v$, by continuity of $L_\rho$.

As a check, for $t\ge1/2$ the value can be computed in closed form. The
slice $z=1$ contains only the point $(-1,1,1)$, with penalized value
$t-3/2$. On the slice $z=0$, minimizing first over $y$ gives $y=u^2$,
and then $u=-1/(2t)$, with value $-1/(4t)$. Hence
\[
 L_\rho(\lambda)=\min\{t-3/2,\,-1/(4t)\}\qquad(t=\lambda+\rho\ge1/2),
\]
which tends to $0=D_\rho$ as $\lambda\to+\infty$ and is negative for
every finite $\lambda$.

\paragraph{Relation to the source's argument.}
The source reduces Example~13 to the multiplier $\lambda=0$ by its
Lemmas~2 and~3, and then shows that $L_\rho(0)<0$ for every $\rho$. Its
Lemma~3 (and its symmetric converse) transfers exactness attained at one
fixed multiplier to another; it does not apply when the supremum is not
attained. The source's Theorems~10 and~12 and Corollary~5, however,
equate an exact penalty representation with attainment at a fixed
multiplier, and its Theorem~14 concludes this attained notion. Under that
notion the conclusion of Example~13 is correct, and the example still
shows that Assumption~5 of \citet{LefebvreSchmidt2025} cannot be dropped
from their Theorem~14: the equality-feasible slice $z=0$ has no point
with $y=0$ and $u^2<y$, and $L_\rho(\lambda)<v$ for all finite $\lambda$
and $\rho$. The point concerns only Definition~1. It does not affect
their Theorem~14, nor Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}, whose dual supremum is
attained.

For a genuine failure of value-exactness, see
Example~\ref{ex:atom}. Like Example~13, it violates the refined Slater
condition. Unlike Example~13, its residuals take both signs, so balanced
mixtures need not lie on $F$, and at right-hand side zero no finite
penalty is value-exact.
