\section{Hardness of calibrating the smallest penalty}\label{sec:calibration}
A short sufficient coefficient can be easy to obtain while a nearly smallest
one is hard to find. We prove this for a binary box, a linear objective and
one equality. The original problem has a supplied unique optimizer.
Throughout this section, the least penalty means $\rho_*$ from Section~\ref{sec:geometry},
with the multiplier optimized and exactness defined by dual values.

\subsection{A binary-box construction}
Take a \textsc{Subset Sum} instance with positive integer items
$a_1,\ldots,a_n$ and positive target $B$. Append $a_{n+1}=B+1$ and,
for an integer $K\ge2$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subsetmodel}
 X=\{0,1\}^{n+2},\qquad f(x,q)=-q,\qquad
 r(x,q)=K\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}a_i x_i-(KB+1)q.
\end{equation}
The equality $r=0$ has the unique solution $(x,q)=(0,0)$: at $q=1$
it contradicts divisibility by $K$, and at $q=0$ positivity forces all
items to be absent. Thus the primal value is zero without solving the
subset-sum instance. Linear optimization over $X$ is also immediate;
this does not make the augmented absolute-residual subproblem easy.

Let $S$ be the attainable subset sums after appending the item, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nearestsums}
 S_- =\max\{s\in S:s\le B\},\qquad
 d_-=K(B-S_-)+1,\qquad d_+=K-1.
\end{equation}
The empty subset ensures that $S_-$ exists. The smallest attainable sum
above $B$ is $B+1$, and appending that positive item does not change
whether $B$ is attainable.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:calibrationdual}
For \eqref{eq:subsetmodel} and every $\rho\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:calibrationdual}
 D_\rho=\min\left\{0,-1+\rho\frac{2d_-d_+}{d_-+d_+}\right\},\qquad
 \rho_*=\frac12\left(\frac1{d_-}+\frac1{d_+}\right).
\end{equation}
An attaining multiplier is
$\lambda_\rho=\rho(d_--d_+)/(d_-+d_+)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The nearest negative and positive residuals at $q=1$ are $-d_-$ and
$d_+$, both with cost $-1$. Their augmented values are
$-1+(\rho-\lambda)d_-$ and $-1+(\rho+\lambda)d_+$.
Weights $d_+/(d_-+d_+)$ and $d_-/(d_-+d_+)$ cancel the multiplier
and give the nonzero branch in \eqref{eq:calibrationdual} as an upper
bound. The feasible origin also bounds $D_\rho$ by zero.
At $\lambda_\rho$ the two values agree, and
$\rho\pm\lambda_\rho\ge0$. Every other $q=1$ residual has at least
the corresponding magnitude, while every $q=0$ residual is nonnegative
and has cost zero. Thus the upper bound is attained.
\end{proof}
At $\rho_*$ the two infeasible witnesses tie with the primal optimum.
Every strictly larger coefficient, with the multiplier fixed at
$\lambda_{\rho_*}$, is minimizer-set exact. A universally sufficient
value coefficient is already available: $\rho=1$ at multiplier zero.
All nonzero residuals are integers and the objective is $0$ or $-1$.
Infeasible points can tie at coefficient one; every coefficient greater
than one is minimizer-set exact at multiplier zero.

If the subset-sum instance is a YES instance, then $d_-=1$; if it is
NO, then $d_-\ge K+1$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thresholdgap}
 \rho_* =\frac{K}{2(K-1)}>\frac12\quad\text{in YES instances},
 \qquad
 0<\rho_*\le\frac{K}{K^2-1}\quad\text{in NO instances}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:coNP}
On the family \eqref{eq:subsetmodel} with $K=4$, deciding whether
$D_{1/3}=0$ is coNP-complete. More precisely, $D_{1/3}=-1/2$ in YES
subset-sum instances and $D_{1/3}=0$ in NO instances.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In YES instances, $d_-=1$, $d_+=3$, and
\eqref{eq:calibrationdual} gives $D_{1/3}=-1/2$.
In NO instances, $\rho_*\le4/15<1/3$, so $D_{1/3}=0$.
This is a polynomial reduction from the complement of \textsc{Subset Sum}.
On this specified family, a subset summing to $B$ is a polynomial
certificate of nonexactness, proving membership in coNP.
\end{proof}
Thus computing $\rho_*$ is NP-hard, and evaluating $D_{1/3}$ to
absolute error strictly below $1/4$ would decide \textsc{Subset Sum}.
By contrast, $D_0=-1$ for every instance of this family.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:polyfactor}
Fix any polynomial $p$ with $p(L)\ge1$. Unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$,
no polynomial-time algorithm returns, on every instance
\eqref{eq:subsetmodel} of binary length $L$, a rational $\widehat\rho$
satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polyfactor}
 \rho_*\le\widehat\rho\le p(L)\rho_*.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\ell$ be the input length of the original subset-sum instance and
choose $K=2^\ell$. There are $O(\ell)$ coefficients, each of
$O(\ell)$ bits, so the constructed length is $L=O(\ell^2)$ and the
construction takes polynomial time. Every admissible YES output exceeds
$1/2$. Every admissible NO output is at most
\[
 p(L)\frac{2^\ell}{2^{2\ell}-1}<\frac12
\]
for all sufficiently large $\ell$, since an exponential dominates the
fixed polynomial $p(O(\ell^2))$. The finitely many smaller inputs can
be handled by exhaustive enumeration. Comparing the output with $1/2$
would therefore decide \textsc{Subset Sum} in polynomial time.
\end{proof}
The same construction rules out a symmetric estimate
$\rho_*/p(L)\le\widehat\rho\le p(L)\rho_*$: YES outputs exceed
$1/(2p(L))$ and NO outputs are smaller for sufficiently large $\ell$.
For a fixed one-sided factor $C$, a constant $K$ with $K+1>2C$
suffices, because the ratio between the YES threshold and the NO upper
bound is $(K+1)/2$. Positive rational scaling of the equality rescales
both penalty thresholds by the reciprocal factor and preserves this
relative gap. It does not remove the small arithmetic spacing.
The binary-box result is a bit-complexity obstruction, not strong
hardness or an APX-completeness classification.

The same calibration obstruction holds with the multiplier fixed at zero.
The two nearest improving residuals give
\[
 \rho_*(0)=\max\{1/d_-,1/d_+\}.
\]
Indeed, all other improving points have larger residual magnitude, and
the cost-zero points impose no positive requirement. For $K\ge3$ this
threshold is one in YES instances and $1/(K-1)$ in NO instances.
Taking $K=2^\ell$ in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:polyfactor} therefore
also excludes polynomial-factor sufficient calibration at multiplier
zero; the finitely many small inputs can again be enumerated.

\subsection{Unit coefficients with a harder native set}
For a nonempty simple graph $G=(V,E)$, take binary variables
$x_v,p_+,p_-$ with native constraints
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stablemodel}
 p_++p_-\le1,\quad x_v\le p_++p_-\ (v\in V),\quad
 x_u+x_v\le1\ (uv\in E).
\end{equation}
Set $f=-\sum_vx_v$ and $r=p_+-p_-$. The equality again forces the
unique primal point zero. If $\alpha(G)$ is the maximum stable-set
size, the native branches have residual zero and cost zero, or residual
$\pm1$ and minimum cost $-\alpha(G)$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stabledual}
 L_\rho(\lambda)=\min\{0,\rho-\alpha(G)-|\lambda|\},\quad
 D_\rho=\min\{0,\rho-\alpha(G)\},\quad \rho_*=\alpha(G).
\end{equation}
Computing $\rho_*$ is strongly NP-hard with all model coefficients and
right-hand sides in $\{-1,0,1\}$. Exactness at an integer $k$ is the
coNP-complete condition $\alpha(G)\le k$; a stable set of size $k+1$
certifies failure. At equality, infeasible maximum stable sets tie.
At multiplier zero, minimizer-set exactness requires
$\rho>\alpha(G)$. Here the native optimization is already hard,
unlike linear optimization over the box in \eqref{eq:subsetmodel}.

\subsection{Relation to penalty-choice hardness}
The growing-binary-dimension results answer the smallest-penalty question
in the conclusion of \citet{LefebvreSchmidt2025}; they do not assert
hardness in fixed total dimension. Conservative sufficient bounds are
also established: for binary quadratic problems with integer linear
constraints, \citet[Sections~5--6 of the inspected preprint]{GusmeroliWiegele2022} obtain exact
squared penalties from objective bounds and residual separation.
Our statement concerns approximation of the instance's least
norm-penalty threshold, not the general idea of using a safe upper bound.
Generic penalty-choice hardness, even with a known constrained optimum,
is older.
\citet[Section~IV.A, Lemma~1 of the inspected preprint]{Alessandroni2025}
prove hardness for exact QUBO reformulations. Their reduction imposes
$x=0$ and uses a squared vector residual and a quadratic objective.
On binary variables, its penalty is $M\sum_i x_i$, which unrestricted
linear multipliers reproduce with augmentation coefficient zero.
Thus that reduction does not give a positive optimized-dual threshold.
Our restrictions and polynomial-factor conclusion are the distinction.

Other tuning results use different quantifiers.
\citet[Section~2.2]{Mirkarimi2024} note that a universally successful,
polynomially tuned linear Ising penalty for knapsack would imply
$\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, allowing the possibility that no suitable
linear penalty exists on some instances.
\citet[Definition~1 and Theorem~2]{Alessandroni2026} instead study
probabilistic feasibility and objective guarantees when exact Gibbs
sampling is used for approximate optimization. These conclusions differ from approximating an always-existing
positive norm-penalty threshold after unrestricted multiplier optimization.
