\section{A doubly exponential lower bound}\label{sec:lower}
This section shows that convex quadratic native constraints can force a
doubly exponentially large exact penalty. We construct instances with one
binary variable, $d+1$ continuous variables and $O(d\log(d+1))$ input bits
whose least penalty is $2^{2^d-1}$, so that every exact rational
coefficient needs at least $2^d$ numerator bits. For a convex quadratic objective over a rational mixed-integer
polyhedron, some exact norm penalty has polynomial encoding length
\citep[Theorem~11]{GuAhmedDey2020}; the construction, whose objective is
linear, shows that this guarantee does not extend to convex quadratic
native constraints. Numbers are encoded as described in
Section~\ref{sec:geometry}.

\subsection{Construction and intuition}
For an integer $d\ge1$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
 A_d=\{a\in[0,1]^d:\ a_1\ge1/4,\ a_{i+1}\ge a_i^2\ (1\le i<d)\},
 \qquad \delta_d=2^{-2^d}.
\end{equation}
Squaring is increasing on $[0,1]$, so induction gives $a_i\ge2^{-2^i}$
for every $a\in A_d$ and every $i$. The point $\bar a$ with
$\bar a_i=2^{-2^i}$ lies in $A_d$ and attains all these bounds. In
particular, the smallest value of $a_d$ on $A_d$ is $\delta_d$. This
squaring chain is classical (Section~\ref{sec:related}). Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onebinary}
 X_d=\{(a,y,q):\ a\in A_d,\ -1\le y\le1,\ q\in\{0,1\},\
                  y\ge a_d-2(1-q)\}.
\end{equation}
We call the last inequality the \emph{switching inequality}. The instance
minimizes $-q$ over $(a,y,q)\in X_d$ subject to the relaxed equality $y=0$.
In the notation of~\eqref{eq:model}, $X=X_d$, $f=-q$, $r=y$, $m=1$ and
$\norm r=|y|$. We write $v_d$, $L_{d,\rho}(\lambda)$, $D_{d,\rho}$,
$\rho_{*,d}$ and $\rho_{*,d}(\lambda)$ for the optimal value, the penalized
value, the dual value, the least penalty and the threshold at
multiplier~$\lambda$ of this instance.

\paragraph{Why the penalty must be large.}
Setting $q=1$ lowers the objective by one. At $q=1$ the switching
inequality reads $y\ge a_d$, and the chain forces $a_d\ge\delta_d$. So the
improving slice $q=1$ never meets the relaxed equality $y=0$, but it comes
within distance $\delta_d$ of it, and this distance is attained. At
multiplier zero, the penalty must charge the residual $\delta_d$ at least
the objective gain of one, which requires $\rho\ge1/\delta_d$. A positive
multiplier $\lambda$ helps at the improving point, because it adds
$\lambda\delta_d$ to its penalized value. At $q=0$, however, the switching
inequality is slack, and $y$ ranges over all of $[-1,1]$ at cost zero. The
point $(q,y)=(0,-1)$ has penalized value $\rho-\lambda$, so a multiplier
larger than $\rho$ creates a new violation there. Balancing the two points
gives the threshold $1/(2\delta_d)$, half the zero-multiplier value.
Residuals of both signs are therefore what make the bound hold for every
multiplier. In the terms of Lemma~\ref{lem:slices}, $\delta_d$ is the
separation of the equality-infeasible slice $q=1$, and the large
coefficient is caused by this separation alone.

Since $f$ and $r$ depend only on $(q,y)$, all quantities above are
determined by the projection of $X_d$ onto the $(q,y)$-plane. The proof
below computes this projection first.

\subsection{The least penalty}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lower}
Let $d\ge1$. The instance defined by~\eqref{eq:onebinary}, with objective
$-q$ and relaxed equality $y=0$, has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Its optimal value is $v_d=0$, and for every $\rho\ge0$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dualformula}
 D_{d,\rho}=\min\left\{0,\frac{2\rho\delta_d-1}{1+\delta_d}\right\}.
\end{equation}
For every $\rho\ge0$ the dual supremum is attained by some multiplier.
\item[(b)] Its least penalty is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lowerthreshold}
 \rho_{*,d}=\frac1{2\delta_d}=2^{2^d-1}.
\end{equation}
Each notion of exactness in Definition~\ref{def:exactness} implies
value-exactness, so every coefficient that is exact in any of these senses
is at least $\rho_{*,d}$. If such a coefficient is a rational number
$\rho_1/\rho_2$ with positive integers $\rho_1,\rho_2$, then the numerator
$\rho_1$ has at least $2^d$ bits.
\item[(c)] The instance has one binary variable, $d+1$ continuous
variables and a linear objective. Its native constraints are the $d-1$
convex quadratic inequalities $a_i^2\le a_{i+1}$ and affine constraints,
with all coefficients from a fixed finite set of rationals. Its sparse
encoding length is $O(d\log(d+1))$.
\item[(d)] Each of the two integer slices $q=0$ and $q=1$ contains a point
at which every native inequality in the continuous variables, the variable
bounds included, holds with slack at least $1/8$. On the
equality-feasible slice $q=0$, this point also satisfies $y=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
For $d=1$ the chain has no quadratic inequality, so the instance is an
MILP with $\rho_{*,1}=2$; the convex quadratic structure starts at $d=2$.

\begin{proof}
We compute the projection of $X_d$ onto $(q,y)$, bound $D_{d,\rho}$ from
above by a balanced mixture and from below by explicit multipliers, and
then check parts (b)--(d) directly.

\emph{Projection.} We show that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection}
 \{(q,y):(a,y,q)\in X_d\}
 =(\{0\}\times[-1,1])\ \cup\ (\{1\}\times[\delta_d,1]).
\end{equation}
(i) If $q=1$, the switching inequality and the chain give
$y\ge a_d\ge\delta_d$, so the part with $q=1$ lies in
$\{1\}\times[\delta_d,1]$. (ii) Conversely, take $a=\bar a$. At $q=1$ the
switching inequality reads $y\ge\delta_d$, so every $y\in[\delta_d,1]$
occurs. At $q=0$ it reads $y\ge a_d-2$, and $a_d-2\le-1$, so every
$y\in[-1,1]$ occurs. (iii) Since $\delta_d>0$, a native point with $y=0$
has $q=0$, and such points exist. Hence $v_d=0$.

\emph{Reduction to the projection.} Since $f=-q$ and $r=y$, the penalized
value is the minimum of $-q+\lambda y+\rho|y|$ over the set
\eqref{eq:projection}. For fixed $q$ this function is piecewise linear in
$y$ with a single break at $y=0$, so on each interval its minimum is
attained at an endpoint or at $y=0$.

\emph{Upper bound.} The points $(q,y)=(0,-1)$ and $(1,\delta_d)$ have
residuals $-1$ and $\delta_d$. With weights $\delta_d/(1+\delta_d)$ and
$1/(1+\delta_d)$ their average residual is zero, so they form a balanced
mixture (Proposition~\ref{prop:mixture}). Their penalized values are
$\rho-\lambda$ and $-1+(\rho+\lambda)\delta_d$, and the weighted average is
\[
 \frac{\delta_d(\rho-\lambda)-1+(\rho+\lambda)\delta_d}{1+\delta_d}
 =\frac{2\rho\delta_d-1}{1+\delta_d},
\]
independently of $\lambda$. The feasible point $(0,0)$ has penalized value
zero. Hence $L_{d,\rho}(\lambda)$ is at most the right-hand side
of~\eqref{eq:dualformula} for every $\lambda$, and so is $D_{d,\rho}$.

\emph{Lower bound and attainment.} First let $0\le\rho\le1/(2\delta_d)$
and take
\[
 \lambda_\rho=\frac{1+\rho(1-\delta_d)}{1+\delta_d}.
\]
Then $\lambda_\rho\ge\rho$, since this inequality is equivalent to
$2\rho\delta_d\le1$. At $q=0$, the function $\lambda_\rho y+\rho|y|$ is
nondecreasing on $[-1,1]$, so its minimum is at $y=-1$, with value
$\rho-\lambda_\rho=(2\rho\delta_d-1)/(1+\delta_d)$. At $q=1$, the
function $-1+(\lambda_\rho+\rho)y$ is nondecreasing, so its minimum is at
$y=\delta_d$, with value
$-1+(\lambda_\rho+\rho)\delta_d=(2\rho\delta_d-1)/(1+\delta_d)$. Both
values equal the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:dualformula}, which is
nonpositive here. Next let $\rho\ge1/(2\delta_d)$ and take $\lambda=\rho$.
At $q=0$ the penalized value is $2\rho y\ge0$ for $y\ge0$ and $0$ for
$y\le0$. At $q=1$ it is $-1+2\rho y\ge-1+2\rho\delta_d\ge0$. So
$L_{d,\rho}(\rho)=0$, again the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:dualformula}. In
both cases the upper bound is attained, which proves (a).

\emph{Part (b).} By~\eqref{eq:dualformula}, $D_{d,\rho}=0=v_d$ exactly
when $2\rho\delta_d\ge1$, which gives~\eqref{eq:lowerthreshold}. The
integer $2^{2^d-1}$ has exactly $2^d$ bits. If
$\rho_1/\rho_2\ge2^{2^d-1}$ with positive integers $\rho_1,\rho_2$, then
$\rho_1\ge\rho_1/\rho_2\ge2^{2^d-1}$, so $\rho_1$ has at least $2^d$ bits.

\emph{Part (c).} Each inequality $a_i^2-a_{i+1}\le0$ is convex quadratic.
All other native constraints are affine: the bounds, the seed
$a_1\ge1/4$ and the switching inequality $y-a_d-2q\ge-2$. All coefficients
and right-hand sides lie in $\{0,\pm1,\pm2,1/4\}$. There are $O(d)$
nonzero coefficients, each stored with variable indices of
$O(\log(d+1))$ bits.

\emph{Part (d).} Set $a_i=3/8$ for all $i$. The seed has slack $1/8$, each
chain inequality has slack $3/8-9/64=15/64$, and the bounds
$0\le a_i\le1$ have slack at least $3/8$. On the slice $q=0$ take $y=0$:
the bounds on $y$ have slack $1$, the switching inequality has slack
$0-(3/8-2)=13/8$, and the relaxed equality holds. On the slice $q=1$ take
$y=1/2$: the bounds on $y$ have slack $1/2$ and the switching inequality
has slack $1/2-3/8=1/8$. The smallest slack is $1/8$ for every $d$, even
though the slice $q=1$ comes within $\delta_d$ of $y=0$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Multipliers and the zero-multiplier threshold.}
The threshold~\eqref{eq:fixed} at a fixed multiplier $\lambda$ is
\[
 \rho_{*,d}(\lambda)=\max\{0,\ 1/\delta_d-\lambda,\ \lambda\}.
\]
Indeed, the native points with $q=1$ have ratios
$(1-\lambda y)/y=1/y-\lambda$, largest at $y=\delta_d$; those with $q=0$
and $y>0$ have ratio $-\lambda$, and those with $y<0$ have ratio
$\lambda$. Equivalently, Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar} applies with
$A_+=1/\delta_d$ and $A_-=0$. This formula gives four facts.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\lambda=\rho_{*,d}$ is the unique multiplier at which $\rho_{*,d}$
is exact.
\item At $\rho=\lambda=\rho_{*,d}$, both points $(q,y)=(0,-1)$ and
$(1,\delta_d)$ have penalized value $0=v_d$. So $\rho_{*,d}$ is exact at
this multiplier but not minimizer-exact.
\item For every $\rho>\rho_{*,d}$, the pair $(\rho,\rho_{*,d})$ is
minimizer-exact: every native point with $y\ne0$ has positive penalized
value, namely $(\rho+\rho_{*,d})y$ or $(\rho-\rho_{*,d})|y|$ at $q=0$, and
at least $-1+(\rho+\rho_{*,d})\delta_d>0$ at $q=1$.
\item The zero-multiplier threshold is $\rho_{*,d}(0)=1/\delta_d=2^{2^d}$,
twice the least penalty; it has $2^d+1$ bits.
\end{itemize}
Thus optimizing the multiplier saves exactly one bit here.
Appendix~\ref{app:symmetric} gives a variant with two binary variables in
which residuals of both signs occur at cost $-1$; there the optimal
multiplier is zero and both thresholds equal $2^{2^d}$.

\begin{remark}[Assumptions of Lefebvre and Schmidt]
The instance satisfies Assumptions~1--5 of \citet{LefebvreSchmidt2025}.
Write $q\in\{0,1\}$ as an integer variable with bounds $0\le q\le1$ and
take the absolute value as penalty function. Assumptions~1--4 are then
immediate: $X_d$ is nonempty and compact, the objective $-q$ is linear,
and the penalty function is a norm. Their Assumption~5, a Slater condition
for each fixed integer assignment, holds at $q=0$ through the strict point
with $a_i=3/8$ and $y=0$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(d), and at $q=1$
because no native point satisfies $y=0$ (since $y\ge\delta_d>0$). Their
Theorem~14 therefore gives, for every fixed multiplier, a finite penalty
that is exact at that multiplier; here this is the finite threshold
$\rho_{*,d}(\lambda)$ above. Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} shows that these
penalties need at least $2^d$ bits, even after the multiplier is optimized.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Encoding consequences}
The lower bound survives if only a fixed additive accuracy is required.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed additive accuracy]\label{cor:accuracy}
Let $0\le\epsilon<1$. For the instance of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower},
$v_d-D_{d,\rho}\le\epsilon$ holds if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:accuracy}
 \rho\ge\max\left\{0,\frac{1-\epsilon(1+\delta_d)}{2\delta_d}\right\}.
\end{equation}
For each fixed $\epsilon<1$ and all sufficiently large $d$, the numerator
of every rational $\rho$ satisfying~\eqref{eq:accuracy}, written with
positive integer numerator and denominator, has at least
$2^d-O_\epsilon(1)$ bits.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By~\eqref{eq:dualformula} and $v_d=0$, the gap is
$\max\{0,(1-2\rho\delta_d)/(1+\delta_d)\}$. Since $\epsilon\ge0$, it is at
most $\epsilon$ exactly when $1-2\rho\delta_d\le\epsilon(1+\delta_d)$,
which is~\eqref{eq:accuracy}. If $d$ is so large that
$\epsilon\delta_d\le(1-\epsilon)/2$, then
$1-\epsilon-\epsilon\delta_d\ge(1-\epsilon)/2$, and the right-hand side
of~\eqref{eq:accuracy} is at least
$(1-\epsilon)/(4\delta_d)=(1-\epsilon)2^{2^d-2}>0$. As in
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(b), the numerator is then at least
$(1-\epsilon)2^{2^d-2}$, so it has more than $2^d-2+\log_2(1-\epsilon)$
bits.
\end{proof}
For example, accuracy $\epsilon=1/2$ requires
$\rho\ge(1-\delta_d)/(4\delta_d)$. The objective takes only the values $0$
and $-1$, so its range is one, and $\epsilon$ is a fixed accuracy relative
to that range. For $\epsilon\ge1$ the condition holds for every
$\rho\ge0$, because the gap never exceeds $1/(1+\delta_d)$.

\begin{remark}[Input length]
The sparse encoding length $N$ of the instance is $\Theta(d\log(d+1))$,
because it records $\Theta(d)$ variable indices, most of which need
$\Theta(\log(d+1))$ bits. Hence $d=\Theta(N/\log N)$, and the lower
bound of $2^d$ bits is $2^{\Theta(N/\log N)}$ bits. This is
superpolynomial in $N$ but not $2^{\Omega(N)}$. In the continuous dimension $n=d+1$ the bound is
exponential. Remark~\ref{rem:seed} below adds a linear dependence on $N$.
The bound concerns the size of exact penalties only. It is not a lower
bound on the effort to solve the original problem, whose value $v_d=0$ is
immediate from~\eqref{eq:projection}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A longer seed]\label{rem:seed}
Fix an integer $t\ge2$ and replace the seed inequality $a_1\ge1/4$
in~\eqref{eq:chain} by $a_1\ge2^{-t}$; the case $t=2$ is the instance of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}. The same induction gives
$a_i\ge2^{-t2^{i-1}}$, with equality at $\bar a_i=2^{-t2^{i-1}}$, so the
separation of the slice $q=1$ becomes $\delta=2^{-t2^{d-1}}$. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} uses only that the chain forces $a_d\ge\delta$,
that $\bar a$ attains $a_d=\delta$, and that $0<\delta\le1$. It therefore
applies with $\delta$ in place of $\delta_d$. The least penalty is
$1/(2\delta)=2^{t2^{d-1}-1}$, which has $t2^{d-1}$ bits, and the
zero-multiplier threshold $1/\delta$ has $t2^{d-1}+1$ bits. The strict
points with $a_i=3/8$ remain valid: the seed slack is
$3/8-2^{-t}\ge1/8$, and the other slacks do not change. The sparse
encoding length becomes $N=O(d\log(d+1)+t)$, and $N>t$ because the seed
coefficient alone has $t+1$ bits.

Now take $t$ at least of order $d\log(d+1)$, so that $N=\Theta(t)$. The
instance has $n=d+1$ continuous variables, satisfies
Assumption~\ref{ass:model}, and every exact rational coefficient has a
numerator of at least $t2^{d-1}=\Omega(N2^n)$ bits. Since the chain
has $k=d-1$ native inequalities that are nonlinear in the continuous
variables, this is also $\Omega(N2^k)$ bits. Sections~\ref{sec:upper}
and~\ref{sec:fixedcount} compare these lower bounds with the upper
bounds.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What the bound depends on}\label{sec:lower-scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} concerns a fixed norm penalty, a fixed relaxed
equality and ordinary binary encoding of the coefficient. The penalty
function matters only through its linear growth at zero; changing the
growth, the scaling of the equality or the number format changes the
question.

\paragraph{Penalty functions with at most linear growth.}
Let $\psi:\R\to[0,\infty)$ satisfy $\psi(y)\le C|y|$ for $|y|\le1$, with
a constant $C>0$, and replace $\rho|y|$ by $\rho\psi(y)$ in the penalized
value. The balanced mixture from the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}, with
the same weights, gives for every multiplier $\lambda$ the upper bound
\[
 \frac{-1+\rho(\psi(\delta_d)+\delta_d\psi(-1))}{1+\delta_d}
 \le\frac{-1+2C\rho\delta_d}{1+\delta_d}.
\]
So the dual value is negative, and the penalty is not value-exact, whenever
$\rho<1/(2C\delta_d)$. This includes the squared penalty
$\psi(y)=y^2$ of the classical augmented Lagrangian, with $C=1$. The obstruction therefore comes from linear growth
at zero, not from the norm axioms.

\paragraph{Fractional powers.}
Without linear growth the threshold can be small. Let $p_d=2^{-d}$, a
rational exponent with $O(d)$ bits. Then $\delta_d^{p_d}=1/2$. With
multiplier zero and augmentation $2|y|^{p_d}$, the penalized objective
$-q+2|y|^{p_d}$ is nonnegative at $q=0$, with value zero at $y=0$, and at
$q=1$ it is at least $-1+2\delta_d^{p_d}=0$. So the coefficient two is
exact, and every larger coefficient is minimizer-exact at multiplier zero.
This augmenting function, however, depends on the instance, is not
Lipschitz at zero, is not a norm and is not convex. It changes both the
encoding question and the continuous subproblem.

\paragraph{Scaling and number format.}
Rescaling the relaxed equality to $2^{2^d}y=0$ divides the least penalty
and the zero-multiplier threshold by $2^{2^d}$, so the least penalty
becomes $1/2$. The doubly exponential
number then sits in the data: the coefficient $2^{2^d}$ has $2^d+1$ bits,
so the input length becomes exponential in $d$. Similarly, writing
coefficients in a succinct exponent notation changes the representation
whose length Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} measures. Both changes alter the
formulation or representation to which the theorem applies.
