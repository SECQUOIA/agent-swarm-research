\section{Right-hand-side perturbations}\label{sec:perturbation}
The worst-case bounds concern a fixed equality. We now perturb its
right-hand side and prove a finite-grid encoding guarantee. This changes
the optimization problem; exactness is asserted for the sampled instance.
The conditioning principle is classical: avoiding convex boundaries
controls distance to feasibility changes
\citep{DunaganSpielmanTeng2011}; spherical uniform-noise boundary estimates
are also known \citep[Theorem~3.3 and Corollary~3.4 of the inspected preprint]{BuergisserAmelunxen2012}.
The contribution in this section is the explicit finite-grid
exact-penalty guarantee, including its bit length and its feasibility
alternative. We prove the cube estimate and grid transfer used for this
application; the Gaussian result later uses
\citet[Lemma~2.3.4]{DunaganSpielmanTeng2011}.

There are at most $K\ge1$ nonempty compact convex native slices $X_z$.
On each slice, $r_z:X_z\to\R^m$ is affine and $f_z$ is continuous
and convex, with supplied common bounds
$L\le f_z\le U$ and range $M=U-L$. All slices, maps and objectives
are fixed before sampling. For a right-hand side $b$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rhsmodel}
 \begin{aligned}
 v(b)&=\min\{f_z(x):z\in Z,\ x\in X_z,\ r_z(x)=b\},\\
 p_\rho(b)&=\min_{z\in Z,\,x\in X_z}
             \{f_z(x)+\rho\norm{r_z(x)-b}_\infty\},
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $v(b)=+\infty$ if infeasible. At a feasible $b$, $\rho_*(b)$ denotes
the optimized threshold of Section~\ref{sec:geometry} for residual
$r_z(x)-b$ and the infinity norm. Each image $C_z=r_z(X_z)$ is
compact and convex. Boundaries are ambient topological boundaries;
a lower-dimensional closed convex set is its own boundary.
Distance to the empty set is $+\infty$.

\subsection{A deterministic boundary-margin bound}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rhsmargin}
Suppose $v(b)<\infty$, $d>0$, and
$\operatorname{dist}_\infty(b,\partial C_z)>d$ for every slice.
Then $p_{M/d}(b)=v(b)$. Every strictly larger coefficient is
minimizer-set exact at multiplier zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a feasible slice, the cube $b+[-d,d]^m$ is contained in $C_z$:
otherwise a segment from $b$ to a point outside the image would cross
its boundary within distance $d$. For a native point $x$ with
$t=\norm{r_z(x)-b}_\infty>0$, choose $x'\in X_z$ with
\[
 r_z(x')=b-\frac d t(r_z(x)-b).
\]
Then $x_b=(dx+tx')/(d+t)$ is feasible at $b$. Convexity gives
\[
 v_z(b)\le\frac d{d+t}f_z(x)+\frac t{d+t}U,
 \qquad f_z(x)\ge v_z(b)-\frac{U-v_z(b)}d t
                 \ge v(b)-\frac Md t.
\]
The last inequality also holds at $t=0$. On an infeasible slice, a
closest image point lies on its boundary, so $t>d$ everywhere and
$f_z(x)+(M/d)t\ge L+M\ge v(b)$.
A primal optimum attains the lower bound $v(b)$. Increasing the
coefficient strictly adds a positive amount to every nonzero-residual
point. If $M=0$, the zero coefficient gives equality of values and a
positive coefficient gives the asserted minimizer-set conclusion.
\end{proof}
Neither differentiability nor computed multipliers are required.
The relation between convex exact penalties and multiplier bounds is
classical; see \citet[Theorem~4.2]{FriedlanderTseng2007} for a polar-gauge
characterization under a nonempty compact solution set.
Convexity of the objective is essential: on $[-1,1]$ with $r(x)=x$,
$b=0$ and $f(x)=-\sqrt{|x|}$, the image-boundary distance and objective
range are both one, but the balanced pair $x=\pm t$ gives average
augmented value $-\sqrt t+\rho t<0$ for small $t>0$, at every multiplier.
No finite penalty closes that dual gap.

\subsection{Convex-boundary tubes in a cube}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cubetube}
Let $Q=b_0+[-\sigma,\sigma]^m$, $\sigma>0$, and let $B$ be uniform
on $Q$. For any closed convex $C\subseteq\R^m$ and $d\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubetube}
 \Pr\{\operatorname{dist}_\infty(B,\partial C)\le d\}
       \le\min\{1,2md/\sigma\}.
\end{equation}
The same upper bound holds with Euclidean distance in the event.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $r>0$ and $S_i=[-r,r]e_i$. Addition $A+S_i$ and erosion
$A\ominus S_i=\{x:x+S_i\subseteq A\}$ preserve convexity and
closedness. On a line parallel to $e_i$, a section of a closed convex
set is an interval, possibly empty, a point, a ray or the whole line.
Addition extends each finite endpoint by $r$. Erosion removes at most
$r$ at each finite endpoint, possibly deleting a short interval.
After clipping to the corresponding section of $Q$, either operation
changes length by at most $2r$. Thus Fubini's theorem gives
\begin{align*}
 \operatorname{vol}(((A+S_i)\setminus A)\cap Q)&\le2r(2\sigma)^{m-1},\\
 \operatorname{vol}((A\setminus(A\ominus S_i))\cap Q)&\le2r(2\sigma)^{m-1}.
\end{align*}
Only changed portions inside $Q$ are measured, so no infinite volumes
are subtracted. Successive additions give $A+[-r,r]^m$; successive
erosions give $A\ominus[-r,r]^m$, since
$(A\ominus S)\ominus T=A\ominus(S+T)$. Telescoping the nested sets
bounds each resulting shell inside $Q$ by $2mr(2\sigma)^{m-1}$.

Take $r>d$. A point within distance $d$ of $\partial C$ is in
$C+[-r,r]^m$ and is not in $C\ominus[-r,r]^m$: a nearby boundary
point has exterior points arbitrarily close to it, one of which lies
in that cube about the given point. Hence the closed $d$-tube lies
in the union of the two shells. Dividing their volume bound by
$(2\sigma)^m$ and letting $r\downarrow d$ proves \eqref{eq:cubetube},
including $d=0$. This argument covers unbounded and lower-dimensional
sets. For $C=\varnothing$ or $\R^m$, the boundary is empty and the
claim is immediate. Finally, distance in the infinity norm is no greater
than Euclidean distance, so the latter event is contained in the former.
\end{proof}
The coefficient $2m$ is sharp for bounds of the form $c_m d/\sigma$.
For $C=[-a,a]^m$, $0<a<\sigma$, and small $d>0$, the tube probability
is $((a+d)^m-(a-d)^m)/\sigma^m$. Its derivative at zero is
$(2m/\sigma)(a/\sigma)^{m-1}$; let $a\uparrow\sigma$.

\subsection{Rational-grid exactness and its encoding}
For an integer $q\ge1$, sample each coordinate of $G$ independently
and uniformly from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centergrid}
 b_{0i}-\sigma+(j+1/2)h,\qquad
 j=0,\ldots,q-1,\qquad h=2\sigma/q.
\end{equation}
Adding independent uniform jitter from $[-h/2,h/2]^m$ makes
$B=G+J$ uniform on $Q$, with $\norm{B-G}_\infty\le\sigma/q$.
The distance function is one-Lipschitz, so Lemma~\ref{lem:cubetube} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gridtube}
 \Pr\{\operatorname{dist}_\infty(G,\partial C)\le d\}
       \le\min\{1,2m(d/\sigma+1/q)\}.
\end{equation}
This rounding term is necessary: in dimension one, an interval whose
two endpoints are distinct grid points has boundary probability $2/q$
at $d=0$, exactly the displayed correction. A continuous null-set
argument alone cannot justify a grid guarantee.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:gridsmoothing}
Let $b_0,\sigma,\epsilon,L,U$ be rational, with $\sigma>0$ and
$0<\epsilon<1$. If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gridsmoothing}
 q\ge\left\lceil\frac{4mK}{\epsilon}\right\rceil,\qquad
 d=\frac{\sigma\epsilon}{4mK},\qquad
 \bar\rho=\frac{4mKM}{\sigma\epsilon},
\end{equation}
then with probability at least $1-\epsilon$ the sampled problem is
infeasible or $p_{\bar\rho}(G)=v(G)$. On the same event, whenever it
is feasible, every coefficient larger than $\bar\rho$ is minimizer-set
exact at multiplier zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A union bound over the fixed images gives failure probability at most
$2mK(d/\sigma+1/q)\le\epsilon$ for simultaneous strict boundary
distance greater than $d$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:rhsmargin}.
\end{proof}
Choose $q$ as the smallest power of two meeting the bound. Then
$\log_2 q=O(\log m+\log K+\log(1/\epsilon))$ and exact uniform
sampling uses $m\log_2q$ independent random bits. The grid points and
$\bar\rho$ have encoding length polynomial in the supplied rational
data, $m$ and $\log K$. An integer coefficient strictly larger than
$\bar\rho$ has the same bound. For boxed integer variables, the product
of their numbers of allowed integer values supplies $K$, with
polynomially bounded $\log K$. Neither computing this coefficient nor
sampling requires enumerating slices. No efficient recognition of the
structural promises or global optimization procedure is asserted.

An endpoint grid with $q+1$ points per coordinate,
$b_{0i}-\sigma+jh$ for $0\le j\le q$, has a similar bound.
Its jitter cells tile the larger cube of radius $\sigma+h/2$, giving
\[
 \Pr\{\operatorname{dist}_\infty(G,\partial C)\le d\}
 \le\min\left\{1,\frac{2m(d+h/2)}{\sigma+h/2}\right\}
 \le\min\{1,2m(d/\sigma+1/q)\}.
\]
Thus the theorem also holds for that convention, with the appropriate
uniform grid sampling. For either grid, the Euclidean boundary event
obeys the same estimate by inclusion and infinity-norm jitter; this
observation alone does not replace the cube margin used by the penalty.

\subsection{Feasibility, stability and the original instance}
The infeasibility alternative cannot be dropped. For example, a finite
union of lower-dimensional images is hit with probability zero under
continuous full-dimensional noise. If a known slice satisfies
$Q\subseteq C_{z_0}$, every centered-grid sample is feasible and the
theorem gives unconditional exactness probability $1-\epsilon$.
More generally, if feasibility has probability at least $p_0>0$, then
the conditional failure probability given feasibility is at most
$\epsilon/p_0$. To obtain conditional failure at most $\epsilon$, use
$\epsilon p_0$ in the construction.

At a feasible point $b$ with all image-boundary distances greater than $d$, the
set of feasible integer assignments is unchanged throughout
$b+[-d/2,d/2]^m$. Every point there retains boundary margin greater
than $d/2$. Applying the repair inequality in both directions shows
that each feasible slice value is $2M/d$-Lipschitz in the infinity
norm on that cube. Taking the minimum over the same finite collection
of slice values preserves this Lipschitz bound. The coefficient $2M/d$
is value-exact throughout the cube, and every larger one is minimizer-set
exact there.
Lipschitz value bounds across discrete choices under uniform Slater
conditions also appear in mixed-integer optimal control
\citep[Theorem~3]{GugatHante2017}.

This local conclusion does not recover the original optimum at $b_0$.
In the chain example, moving the right-hand side from zero to
$\delta_n$ makes the improving binary assignment feasible and changes the
optimum from zero to $-1$. Shorter penalty encodings after perturbation
can therefore accompany a change in the optimal integer assignment.

\subsection{Numerical size: unavoidable tails and slice dependence}
Let an anchor slice have residual image $[0,1]$ and cost zero, and let
$K-1$ singleton slices have images $j/K$, $1\le j\le K-1$, and cost
$-1$. At a non-atom $b\in(0,1)$, the primal value is zero. For a
singleton at distance $t>0$, choose an anchor point with opposite
residual of magnitude $s>0$. The balanced mixture with weights
$s/(s+t)$ and $t/(s+t)$ gives augmented value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tailmixture}
 \frac{s}{s+t}(-1+2\rho t),
\end{equation}
independently of the multiplier. Thus the optimized-dual threshold is
at least $1/(2t)$. If $B$ is uniform on $[0,1]$, the disjoint intervals
$|B-j/K|<1/(2T)$ have total length $(K-1)/T$ for $T\ge K$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penaltytail}
 \Pr\{\rho_*(B)>T\}\ge(K-1)/T\qquad(T\ge K).
\end{equation}
The order $K/\epsilon$ in a high-probability numerical bound is therefore
necessary in this slice model, even with robust feasibility and optimized
multipliers.
For the centered grid on $[0,1]$, if $K$ divides $q$ and $q\ge2K$,
each of the $K-1$ singleton images has two adjacent centers at distance
$1/(2q)$. These $2(K-1)$ centers are distinct, so
$\Pr\{\rho_*(G)\ge q\}\ge2(K-1)/q$.

For an almost-sure slice-count obstruction, put singleton images at
$j/J$, $j=0,\ldots,J$, with cost $-1$, and retain the anchor. There
are $K=J+2$ slices. Between neighboring grid points, the singleton
residual magnitudes $u,v>0$ satisfy $u+v=1/J$. Their balanced mixture
has cost $-1$ and mean absolute residual
$2uv/(u+v)\le1/(2J)$. Thus $\rho_*(b)\ge2J$ for almost every
$b\in[0,1]$. These examples concern a specified collection of slices;
no succinct encoding of every such collection is assumed.

Even two slices can give an infinite mean numerical penalty. Take an
anchor image $[-1,1]$ of cost zero and a singleton at zero of cost $-1$.
For $b\in(-1,1)\setminus\{0\}$, \eqref{eq:tailmixture} and the
multiplier $\lambda=-\rho\operatorname{sign}(b)$ prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infinitemean}
 \rho_*(b)=\frac1{2|b|}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, at that threshold the anchor's augmented objective is nonnegative
and the singleton's is zero. The zero-multiplier threshold is instead
$1/|b|$. Uniform continuous noise on $[-1,1]$ gives
$\mathbb E\rho_*(B)=+\infty$, while
$\mathbb E\log(1+\rho_*(B))<\infty$ by integration near zero.
The finite-grid theorem is a high-probability statement; its exceptional
atoms can even require an infinite threshold. Consider the convex quadratic
slice $0\le t\le1$, $t^2\le s\le1$, with cost $-t$ and image coordinate
$s$, and a second singleton slice with image $\{-1\}$ and cost zero.
At right-hand side zero the primal value is zero. Mixing $(t,s)=(u,u^2)$
with the singleton, using weights $1/(1+u^2)$ and $u^2/(1+u^2)$,
gives cost $-u/(1+u^2)$ and mean absolute residual $2u^2/(1+u^2)$.
Its threshold ratio is $1/(2u)$, unbounded as $u\downarrow0$.
Thus $\rho_*(0)=+\infty$. A rational grid containing zero gives this
event positive probability, so no finite expected threshold or
penalty-encoding requirement follows in general. The boundary-margin
condition properly excludes that atom; refined Slater also fails there.

\subsection{A Gaussian companion in the Euclidean norm}
The deterministic repair proof works with any norm when its ball and
its distance are used consistently. For Gaussian noise, use the Euclidean
norm for both. Let $B=b_0+\sigma\xi$, $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,I_m)$.
\citet[Lemma~2.3.4]{DunaganSpielmanTeng2011} bound each of the inner
and outer Euclidean tubes of a convex body by
$4m^{1/4}d/\sigma$. Their sum and a union bound give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussiantube}
 \Pr\{\min_z\operatorname{dist}_2(B,\partial C_z)\le d\}
       \le 8K m^{1/4}d/\sigma.
\end{equation}
This also covers lower-dimensional compact images. To see this, replace
$C$ by its parallel body $C+\eta\mathbb B_2$, $\eta>0$. For a
lower-dimensional $C$, the distances to these boundaries converge to
$\operatorname{dist}_2(\cdot,C)$ as $\eta\downarrow0$: outside $C$
use the distance to a parallel body, and at a point of $C$ use a normal
to its affine hull. Apply the convex-body bound at any radius greater
than $d$, then let $\eta\downarrow0$ and the radius decrease to $d$.
Thus, with probability at least $1-\epsilon$, the sampled instance is
infeasible or the Euclidean zero-multiplier coefficient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussianpenalty}
 \frac{8MKm^{1/4}}{\sigma\epsilon}
\end{equation}
is value-exact. A larger coefficient gives minimizer-set exactness.
This is a direct Gaussian conditioning corollary, with a different
noise model and norm from the rational-grid encoding result.
