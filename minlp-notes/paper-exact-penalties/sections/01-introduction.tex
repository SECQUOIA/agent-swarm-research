\section{Introduction}
An exact norm penalty replaces a linking equality system by a finite
charge for its violation without changing the optimal value. Existence alone does
not say whether that coefficient can be stored or produced efficiently.
Nor does an easily available sufficient coefficient make the smallest
one easy to find. We study these two questions using ordinary binary
encoding: \emph{representation} asks for the number of bits in a
sufficient coefficient, whereas \emph{calibration} asks how closely one
can approximate the least sufficient coefficient.

The main contribution is a quantitative account of what changes when
convex quadratic constraints are added to the native mixed-integer set.
Here \emph{native} refers to all constraints retained while a separate
affine equality system is penalized. Our lower bound applies even
after maximizing over all linking multipliers, so it cannot be removed
by a better multiplier choice. Our upper bounds give equality of the
full minimizer sets at multiplier zero, a stronger conclusion than
equality of optimized dual values. Section~\ref{sec:geometry} states
these distinctions precisely.

\paragraph{Main results and their significance.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} gives a compact convex-quadratic family with
chain length $d$, one binary variable, a linear objective and $d+1$
continuous variables whose
least optimized-dual penalty is $2^{2^d-1}$. The data use a fixed finite
set of rational coefficients and $O(d\log(d+1))$ bits, but every exact
rational penalty needs at least $2^d$ numerator bits. Feasible integer
slices have uniformly strict points, and fixed additive accuracy below
the unit objective range does not remove the obstruction
(Corollary~\ref{cor:accuracy}). Thus finite norm-penalty exactness for
convex quadratic constraints does not extend the polynomial encoding
guarantee known for linear native constraints. The obstruction concerns
the penalty representation, not the difficulty of finding the original
optimum, which is immediate in this family.

Theorems~\ref{thm:generalupper} and~\ref{thm:fixedcount} supply positive
bounds under explicit finite boxes and refined Slater on every
equality-feasible slice. For input length $N$ and $n$ continuous
variables, there exists a sufficient integer coefficient with
$(N+1)2^{O(n)}$ bits for both the infinity and one-norm penalties.
A second bound is $N^{O(k+1)}$ bits if at most $k$ native inequalities
are nonlinear in the continuous variables, with no restriction on the
number of affine rows or integer assignments. Within this model, these statements locate the
obstruction in the growing nonlinear continuous structure: fixing
$n$ or $k$ permits polynomial-time output of a conservative coefficient
(Corollary~\ref{cor:printing}). They do not provide a practical small
coefficient or an algorithm for solving the penalized subproblem.

The complementary results separate two further issues.
Theorem~\ref{thm:polyfactor} rules out polynomial-time calibration within
any fixed polynomial factor, unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, on a binary
box with one equality, a linear objective, a known unique primal
optimizer and a known sufficient coefficient of one. Both the
optimized-multiplier and zero-multiplier thresholds have this hardness.
Section~\ref{sec:perturbation} gives a different route to short
coefficients: a sampled rational right-hand side, with probability at
least $1-\epsilon$, is either infeasible or admits the explicit
zero-multiplier infinity-norm coefficient $4mKM/(\sigma\epsilon)$.
The slices are compact and convex, with continuous convex objectives and affine
residuals. Here $m$ is the residual dimension, $K$ bounds the number of
slices, $M$ is their common objective range, and $\sigma$ is the
noise-cube radius. The finite-grid guarantee includes the sampling
precision and coefficient encoding. It applies to the sampled problem;
it does not guarantee preservation of the original optimizer or bound
the expected numerical penalty.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
\citet{BolandEberhard2015} establish finite-penalty exactness for bounded
pure integer programs under their augmentation assumptions;
\citet{FeizollahiAhmedSun2017} establish norm-penalty exactness for
rational MILPs.
For a convex quadratic objective over a rational mixed-integer
polyhedron, \citet[Theorem~11]{GuAhmedDey2020} prove polynomial binary
encoding of an exact norm penalty.
\citet[Theorems~3.1--3.3 of the inspected preprint]{Bhardwaj2024}
give value-function proofs of existence and data-dependent penalty
parameters for MILPs and MIQPs. For nonlinear convex objectives over
polyhedral mixed-integer sets they also prove exactness, with an explicit
bound under strong convexity and smoothness.
For nonlinear native constraints,
\citet[Theorem~14]{LefebvreSchmidt2025} prove finite exactness at each
fixed multiplier under compactness, differentiable convex objectives,
convex continuous slices and refined Slater on every equality-feasible
slice. Their December 15, 2025 manuscript asks whether polynomial
penalty encoding extends to quadratically constrained models and
whether a smallest exact penalty can be computed in polynomial time.
Our lower bound answers the first question negatively in growing
continuous dimension; the binary-box reduction answers the second
negatively in growing binary dimension, subject to
$\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$.

The lower-bound mechanism has important precedents.
\citet[Section~6 of the inspected preprint]{Bienstock2022} give quadratic
large-coordinate and small-violation examples; their nonconvex
Example~6.2 already implies large zero-multiplier norm penalties.
\citet[Sections~2 and~4, Result~4]{Beck2023} use a bounded convex
squaring chain with Slater regularity to show sensitivity of bilevel
problems to feasibility errors. We do not claim the squaring chain or
its small residuals as new. To the best of our knowledge, the new
lower-bound statement is the exact optimized-dual threshold in
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} for a compact convex-quadratic model with one
binary variable and uniform strict points, together with its
fixed-accuracy consequence. Opposite-sign residuals are what make
the bound hold for every multiplier.

The upper bounds use established real-algebraic tools:
component-radius bounds \citep{BasuRoy2010} and quantitative elimination
\citep{BasuPollackRoy2006}. Few-quadratic methods already control
algebraic complexity in the number of quadratic constraints
\citep{GrigorievPasechnik2005,KammingaRudolph2025}.
The contribution here is the resulting penalty encoding bounds,
including the dependence on the number of \emph{nonlinear native}
inequalities while allowing arbitrarily many affine rows.
To the best of our knowledge, these bounds for the boxed convex-slice
model of Section~\ref{sec:upper} are not established in the cited
exact-penalty or few-quadratic results. The linear-constraint case and
polynomial-time conservative output in that case are prior results,
not new consequences claimed here; Section~\ref{sec:fixedcount} gives
the detailed comparison.

Generic penalty-choice hardness, even with a known constrained optimum,
also predates this paper \citep{Alessandroni2025}. The distinction in
Theorem~\ref{thm:polyfactor} is the polynomial-factor conclusion for an
always-positive norm-penalty threshold after unrestricted multiplier
optimization, under the binary-box restrictions above. We are not aware
of that restricted conclusion in the prior tuning results compared in
Section~\ref{sec:calibration}. Finally, distance to convex boundaries is
a classical conditioning device
\citep{DunaganSpielmanTeng2011,BuergisserAmelunxen2012}. Our perturbation
result is an explicit application to exact penalties on finitely many
convex slices, with rational-grid and feasibility qualifications; we
make no separate priority claim for the boundary-avoidance principle,
the Gaussian tube estimate or the convex repair argument.
