\section{Encoding upper bounds in the continuous dimension}\label{sec:upper}
This section proves that the doubly exponential growth of
Section~\ref{sec:lower} is the worst possible in the continuous
dimension, up to the constant in the exponent. For bounded
mixed-integer quadratic models that are convex in the continuous
variables for each integer assignment and satisfy the refined Slater
condition (Assumption~\ref{ass:model}), some integer coefficient with
$(N+1)2^{O(n)}$ bits is minimizer-exact at multiplier zero, where $N$ is
the input length and $n$ the number of continuous variables. The bound
does not depend on the number of integer variables, integer assignments
or affine rows, except through $N$.

\subsection{Model and assumptions}
Throughout this section the instance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadraticmodel}
 v=\min\{f(x,z):(x,z)\in X,\ Ax+Bz-b=0\},\qquad
 x\in\R^n,\ z\in\Z^p,
\end{equation}
with native set
$X=\{(x,z)\in\R^n\times\Z^p:g_i(x,z)\le0\ (1\le i\le s)\}$ and
relaxed equalities $r(x,z)=Ax+Bz-b=0$, where $A$, $B$ and $b$ are
rational, $n\ge1$ and $r$ has $m\ge1$ coordinates. Since $r$ links the
continuous and the integer variables, we also call these equalities the
linking equalities and their multipliers linking multipliers. The number
$s$ counts all native inequalities, including the variable bounds;
native affine equalities are written as pairs of inequalities. We call
each native inequality a \emph{row}. For an integer assignment $z$ we
identify the slice with $X_z=\{x\in\R^n:(x,z)\in X\}$; equality-feasible
and equality-infeasible slices are as in Section~\ref{sec:geometry}, and
empty slices are discarded.

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:model}
The instance \eqref{eq:quadraticmodel} satisfies the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(A1)] \emph{Quadratic data in explicit encoding.} The objective
 $f$ and the native constraint functions $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ are
 polynomials in $(x,z)$ of total degree at most two with rational
 coefficients. The input lists them in expanded form, with every
 coefficient and every variable index written in binary, together with
 $A$, $B$ and $b$. Its total binary length is $N\ge2$; in particular
 $s,m,n\le N$.
\item[(A2)] \emph{Finite bounds.} Every continuous and every integer
 variable has finite rational lower and upper bounds, listed among the
 native inequalities.
\item[(A3)] \emph{Convexity in the continuous variables.} For every
 integer assignment $z$, the functions $f(\cdot,z)$ and
 $g_i(\cdot,z)$, $1\le i\le s$, are convex on $\R^n$.
\item[(A4)] \emph{Feasibility and refined Slater condition.} The
 feasible set of \eqref{eq:quadraticmodel} is nonempty, and every
 equality-feasible slice $X_z$ contains a point $x$ with $Ax+Bz=b$ and
 $g_i(x,z)<0$ for every native inequality $g_i$ that is nonlinear in
 $x$.
\end{itemize}
\end{assumption}
By (A2), $X$ is compact and there are finitely many slices. The
$x$-Hessian of a polynomial of degree at most two is constant. Hence
convexity in (A3), and whether a row is nonlinear in $x$, do not depend
on $z$. In (A4), inequalities affine in $x$ may hold with equality; this
includes rows such as $x_1z_1+z_1^2\le3$ that contain integer variables.
The condition asks for no quantitative margin, and nothing is assumed on
equality-infeasible slices. This matters: in the chain of
Section~\ref{sec:lower}, the large penalty comes from an
equality-infeasible slice.

For a positive integer $a$, write
$\operatorname{bit}(a)=1+\lfloor\log_2 a\rfloor$ for its binary length.
All constants implicit in $O(\cdot)$ below are absolute. In particular,
they do not depend on the number $p$ of integer variables or on the
number of slices; the bounds depend on the instance only through $N$ and
$n$.

\subsection{Main result and proof strategy}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:generalupper}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:model}, there is an integer $\rho\ge1$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generalbits}
 \operatorname{bit}(\rho)\le (N+1)2^{O(n)}
\end{equation}
that is minimizer-exact at multiplier zero for both the infinity norm
and the one-norm; the same $\rho$ works for both.
\end{theorem}
Minimizer-exactness at multiplier zero implies exactness at multiplier
zero and value-exactness (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}). Hence both
thresholds $\rho_*(0)$ and $\rho_*$ are at most this $\rho$.

\paragraph{Proof strategy.}
We use the infinity norm, whose dual norm is the one-norm, and deduce
the one-norm case at the end. Lemma~\ref{lem:slices} reduces exactness
at multiplier zero to two uniform bounds:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] on each equality-feasible slice, one linking multiplier
 $\lambda_z$ with $f+\lambda_z^Tr\ge v_z$ on the slice and a bound on
 its dual norm $\norm{\lambda_z}_1$;
\item[(b)] on each equality-infeasible slice, a lower bound on
 $\min_{x\in X_z}\norm{r(x,z)}_\infty$.
\end{itemize}
After $z$ is substituted, both become bounds on the coordinates of
points of basic closed semialgebraic sets in $O(n)$ variables, defined
by polynomials of degree two with integer coefficients of bitsize $O(N)$.
One result from real algebraic geometry (Lemma~\ref{lem:radii}) bounds
such coordinates by a radius $R$ with $\log R=O(N)2^{O(n)}$. The radius
depends only on the number of variables, the number of polynomials and
the coefficient bitsize. It is therefore the same for every slice, and
no factor for the number of slices appears.

The number of variables is the critical parameter, because the radius
bound is exponential in it. For (a), the full KKT system has $n+s+m$
unknowns, which would give $2^{O(n+s+m)}$ in the exponent. A conic
Carath\'eodory argument keeps at most $n$ multipliers, so the system has
at most $2n$ variables. For (b), Lemma~\ref{lem:radii} bounds coordinates
of points, not distances from zero; we therefore encode the reciprocal
$1/\norm{r}_\infty$ as one extra variable.

\subsection{Effective radii}
A \emph{basic closed set} is the set of real solutions of a finite
conjunction of polynomial equations $P=0$ and weak inequalities $P\le0$
or $P\ge0$.

\begin{lemma}[Effective radii]\label{lem:radii}
Let $\mathcal P$ be a list of at most $S$ polynomials in $\ell$
variables, with integer coefficients of bitsize at most $\tau\ge1$ and
degree at most two. There is a radius $R\ge1$, depending only on
$(S,\ell,\tau)$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radius}
 \log_2 R\le(\tau+\log_2(S+1)+1)2^{O(\ell)},
\end{equation}
such that the closed ball of radius $R$ centred at the origin meets
every nonempty basic closed set defined by polynomials from $\mathcal P$
and contains every such set that is bounded. The same holds for finite
unions of such sets.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We combine two radius bounds of \citet{BasuRoy2010}, stated for weak
sign conditions. A weak sign condition on $\mathcal P$ prescribes, for
each $P\in\mathcal P$, one of $P=0$, $P\le0$ or $P\ge0$; its
realization is the set of points of $\R^\ell$ that satisfy all these
conditions. Apply the bounded-component radius of
\citet[Theorem~3]{BasuRoy2010} and the component-meeting radius of
\citet[Theorem~4]{BasuRoy2010} to weak sign conditions on the full list
of polynomials and take the larger radius, or $1$ if that is larger.
The first ball contains every bounded connected component of every
realization; the second meets every connected component of every
nonempty realization.

These theorems use auxiliary parameters called $N$ and $D$; to avoid a
clash with the input length, we write them $N_{\mathrm{BR}}$ and
$D_{\mathrm{BR}}$. For degree two, $N_{\mathrm{BR}}=5\cdot4^{\ell-1}$ and
$D_{\mathrm{BR}}=3\ell+2$ in Theorem~3, and
$N_{\mathrm{BR}}=6\cdot5^{\ell-1}$ and $D_{\mathrm{BR}}=4\ell+2$ in
Theorem~4. Theorem~4 rests on \citet[Theorem~2]{BasuRoy2010}, whose proof
contains a term $\operatorname{bit}(N_{\mathrm{BR}}!)$ in its bound on
$1/u_0$ that the statements omit; we retain it. Substituting these values
into the explicit formulas shows that the binary logarithm of each radius
is at most $2^{O(\ell)}(\tau+\operatorname{bit}(S)+1)$; since
$N_{\mathrm{BR}}=2^{O(\ell)}$, the term
$\operatorname{bit}(N_{\mathrm{BR}}!)$ is also absorbed by
$2^{O(\ell)}$. This gives \eqref{eq:radius}, because
$\operatorname{bit}(S)\le\log_2(S+1)+1$. The formulas depend only on the
number of polynomials, the number of variables, the degree and the
coefficient bitsize, and they do not decrease when the number of
polynomials grows. Hence $R$ depends only on $(S,\ell,\tau)$.

Let $Y$ be a basic closed set defined by polynomials from $\mathcal P$.
It is the union of the realizations of those weak sign conditions on the
full list that are compatible with its definition; a polynomial not used
in the definition contributes the two choices $P\le0$ and $P\ge0$. If
$Y$ is nonempty, one of these realizations is nonempty, and the ball
meets each of its connected components; hence it meets $Y$. If $Y$ is
bounded, each of these realizations is bounded, so all of its connected
components are bounded and lie in the ball; hence $Y$ lies in the ball.
For a finite union, apply the first argument to a nonempty member; if the
union is bounded, every member is bounded, and the second argument
applies to each member.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Uniform coefficient heights}
Lemma~\ref{lem:radii} needs integer coefficients of bounded bitsize. All
polynomial systems below are built from the input polynomials after $z$
is substituted, by differentiation in $x$ and by multiplying affine
polynomials by one new variable (a multiplier, or the variable $t$ of
Section~\ref{sec:upper-infeasible}). We check that each such system can
be written with integer coefficients of bitsize
\begin{equation}\label{eq:height}
 \tau=O(N),
\end{equation}
uniformly over all integer assignments.
\begin{itemize}
\item Multiply every equation and inequality of a system by the product
 of all denominators in the input. This positive integer is below $2^N$
 and turns every input coefficient into an integer of absolute value
 below $2^N$. Multiplying an equation or inequality by a positive integer
 does not change its solution set, so all variables, including the
 multipliers, keep their meaning. We do not rescale the residual $r$ in
 the penalty itself, since that would change the penalty.
\item By (A2), the bounds on all variables are input inequalities, so
 every coordinate of a point of $X$, continuous or integer, has absolute
 value below $2^N$; in particular $|z_j|<2^N$. Substituting $z$ into a monomial of degree at most two
 multiplies its coefficient by less than $2^{2N}$. Each polynomial has
 at most $N$ monomials, so collecting terms adds at most
 $\log_2N+1$ bits.
\item Differentiation multiplies coefficients by at most two, and
 multiplying a polynomial by a new variable leaves its coefficients
 unchanged.
\end{itemize}
We also use an objective bound $|f|\le F_0$ on $X$, with an integer
$F_0\ge1$ and $\operatorname{bit}(F_0)=O(N)$. For example, let $c_f$ be
the sum of the absolute values of the objective coefficients and take
$F_0=1+\lceil c_f2^{2N}\rceil$. Then $|f|\le F_0$ on $X$, because every
coordinate is below $2^N$ in absolute value; since $c_f<N2^N$, we get
$\operatorname{bit}(F_0)=O(N)$.

\subsection{Feasible slices: a sparse KKT certificate}
Fix an equality-feasible assignment $z$ and suppress it from the
notation: $g_i(x)=g_i(x,z)$, $r(x)=Ax+Bz-b$, and
\[
 v_z=\min\{f(x):x\in X_z,\ r(x)=0\}.
\]
We need a linking multiplier $\lambda_z\in\R^m$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slicemultiplier}
 f(x)+\lambda_z^Tr(x)\ge v_z\qquad\text{for all }x\in X_z,
\end{equation}
and a bound on $\norm{\lambda_z}_1$. A KKT multiplier satisfies
\eqref{eq:slicemultiplier}. The work is to find one that is a coordinate
of a point in a system with at most $2n$ variables.

\paragraph{Existence of a KKT certificate.}
The slice problem has an optimal solution $x^*$, because its feasible
set is nonempty and compact. By (A3) and (A4), it is a convex program
with finite convex functions on $\R^n$ and affine equality constraints
that has a feasible point satisfying strictly every inequality that is
not affine. Hence \citet[Theorem~28.2]{Rockafellar1970} gives a
Kuhn--Tucker vector, and for differentiable data
\citet[Theorem~28.3]{Rockafellar1970} gives the KKT conditions at $x^*$:
there are $\mu\in\R^s$ and $\lambda\in\R^m$ with
\[
 \mu\ge0,\qquad \mu_ig_i(x^*)=0\ (1\le i\le s),\qquad
 \nabla f(x^*)+\sum_{i=1}^s\mu_i\nabla g_i(x^*)+A^T\lambda=0.
\]
This holds even when the active affine rows are linearly dependent.

\paragraph{Sparsification.}
Let $e=\operatorname{rank}A$, and choose a set $J$ of $e$ linearly
independent rows of $A$ that span its row space $W$. Stationarity says
that $-\nabla f(x^*)$ is a nonnegative combination of active native
gradients plus a vector in $W$. Modulo $W$ we are in the space
$\R^n/W$ of dimension $n-e$. There, by the conic Carath\'eodory theorem,
the class of $-\nabla f(x^*)$ is a nonnegative combination of the
classes of at most $n-e$ active native gradients, indexed by a set $I$.
The remainder lies in $W$ and is therefore a combination of the rows in
$J$. This gives new multipliers $\mu_i\ge0$ ($i\in I$) and $\lambda_j$
($j\in J$) with $|I|+|J|\le n$. The sets $I$ and $J$ depend on $z$, and
$I$ may contain variable-bound rows. Consequently the following system
in $(x,\mu_I,\lambda_J)$ has a solution:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparsekkt}
 \begin{aligned}
 &g_i(x)\le0\quad(1\le i\le s),\qquad Ax+Bz-b=0,\\
 &\mu_i\ge0,\quad g_i(x)=0\quad(i\in I),\\
 &\nabla f(x)+\sum_{i\in I}\mu_i\nabla g_i(x)
                  +\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_j A_j^T=0.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
All $m$ linking rows remain as constraints, although only the
multipliers indexed by $J$ appear. The equations $g_i(x)=0$ for $i\in I$
replace the complementarity products $\mu_ig_i(x)=0$, which have degree
three, and reuse the native polynomials. Hence \eqref{eq:sparsekkt} is a
basic closed set in at most $2n$ variables, defined by at most $s+m+2n$
polynomials of degree at most two (each $\mu_i\nabla g_i$ is a multiplier
times an affine function).

\paragraph{Every solution is a certificate.}
Let $(x,\mu_I,\lambda_J)$ solve \eqref{eq:sparsekkt}, and extend
$\lambda_J$ by zeros to $\lambda_z\in\R^m$. The Lagrangian
\[
 \mathcal L(y)=f(y)+\sum_{i\in I}\mu_ig_i(y)+\lambda_z^Tr(y)
\]
is convex on $\R^n$ and stationary at $x$, so it is minimized at $x$,
where its value is $f(x)$. For every feasible $y$ of the slice problem,
$f(y)\ge\mathcal L(y)\ge\mathcal L(x)=f(x)$; since $x$ is itself feasible,
$f(x)=v_z$. For every $y\in X_z$, the terms $\mu_ig_i(y)$ are
nonpositive, so
$f(y)+\lambda_z^Tr(y)\ge\mathcal L(y)\ge v_z$. This is
\eqref{eq:slicemultiplier}.

\paragraph{Size of the certificate.}
By \eqref{eq:height}, the system \eqref{eq:sparsekkt} can be cleared to
integer coefficients of bitsize $\tau=O(N)$. Let $R_M$ be the largest of
the radii that Lemma~\ref{lem:radii} gives for at most $s+m+2n$
polynomials, at most $2n$ variables and bitsize $\tau$. By the meeting
part of the lemma, some solution of \eqref{eq:sparsekkt} has all
coordinates bounded by $R_M$ in absolute value, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RM}
 \log_2 R_M\le (\tau+\log_2(s+m+2n+1)+1)2^{O(n)}.
\end{equation}
The corresponding multiplier satisfies
$\norm{\lambda_z}_1\le|J|R_M\le nR_M$. The bound $R_M$ is the same for
every equality-feasible slice. It bounds one suitable certificate, not
all optimal multipliers, whose set can be unbounded.

\subsection{Infeasible slices: separation from the relaxed equalities}
\label{sec:upper-infeasible}
Fix a nonempty equality-infeasible slice $X_z$. Since $X_z$ is compact
and $r$ does not vanish on it,
\[
 \delta_z=\min_{x\in X_z}\norm{Ax+Bz-b}_\infty>0.
\]
We need a lower bound on $\delta_z$, but Lemma~\ref{lem:radii} bounds
coordinates of points. The idea is to encode $t=1/\norm{r(x,z)}_\infty$
as a coordinate: its graph over $X_z$ is bounded, so the lemma bounds
$t$, and hence $1/\delta_z$. The infinity norm keeps this description at
degree two. For the Euclidean norm one would need
$t^2\norm{r}_2^2=1$, of degree four.

Write $r_j=r_j(x,z)$ and consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reciprocal}
 \Gamma_z=\left\{(x,t):\begin{array}{l}
 x\in X_z,\quad t\ge0,\quad -1\le tr_j\le1\ (1\le j\le m),\\
 tr_j=1\ \text{or}\ tr_j=-1\ \text{for at least one }j
 \end{array}\right\}.
\end{equation}
For $x\in X_z$, these conditions say $t|r_j|\le1$ for all $j$, with
equality for some $j$; thus $t=1/\norm{r(x,z)}_\infty$. Hence $\Gamma_z$
is exactly the graph of $t=1/\norm{r(x,z)}_\infty$ over $X_z$. It is
nonempty and compact, since $\norm{r}_\infty\ge\delta_z>0$ on the
compact set $X_z$. Choosing a coordinate $j$ and a sign in the last line
writes $\Gamma_z$ as a union of $2m$ basic closed sets. Each equation
$tr_j=\pm1$ uses a polynomial already present in $-1\le tr_j\le1$. So
only $s+2m+1$ polynomials of degree two in $n+1$ variables are needed,
and by \eqref{eq:height} their coefficients can be cleared to bitsize
$\tau=O(N)$. The containment part of Lemma~\ref{lem:radii} bounds $t$ on
$\Gamma_z$. At a minimizer of $\norm{r}_\infty$ over $X_z$, $t=1/\delta_z$;
therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:RD}
 \delta_z^{-1}\le R_D,\qquad
 \log_2 R_D\le(\tau+\log_2(s+2m+2)+1)2^{O(n)},
\end{equation}
where $R_D$ is the radius of Lemma~\ref{lem:radii} for $s+2m+1$
polynomials, $n+1$ variables and bitsize $\tau$; it is the same for every
equality-infeasible slice. This step uses no KKT condition, which is why
no regularity is needed on equality-infeasible slices.

\subsection{Proof of the theorem and comparison with the lower bound}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:generalupper}]
We apply Lemma~\ref{lem:slices} with the bounds of the two preceding
subsections. Let $E\ge0$ be the least integer with
$2^E\ge\max\{R_M,R_D\}$, where $R_M$ and $R_D$ are the uniform radii in
\eqref{eq:RM} and \eqref{eq:RD}, and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sufficientrho}
 \rho=(n+2F_0+1)2^E.
\end{equation}
This integer is strictly larger than both $nR_M$ and $2F_0R_D$.

Consider first the infinity norm, whose dual norm is the one-norm. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:slices} with objective bounds
$\underline f=-F_0$ and $\overline f=F_0$, multiplier bound
$\Lambda=nR_M$ from the feasible slices, and separation
$\delta=1/R_D$ from \eqref{eq:RD}. Since $\rho$ is strictly larger than
$\max\{\Lambda,(\overline f-\underline f)/\delta\}=\max\{nR_M,2F_0R_D\}$,
it is minimizer-exact at multiplier zero. In detail, on an
equality-feasible slice a point with nonzero residual has penalized
value at least $v_z+(\rho-nR_M)\norm r_\infty>v$, by
\eqref{eq:slicemultiplier} and
$|\lambda_z^Tr|\le\norm{\lambda_z}_1\norm r_\infty$. On an
equality-infeasible slice every point has penalized value at least
$-F_0+\rho/R_D>F_0\ge v$.

The same coefficient works for the one-norm. Indeed,
$\norm r_1\ge\norm r_\infty$, and both vanish on the feasible set of
\eqref{eq:quadraticmodel}. So the one-norm penalized objective is at
least the infinity-norm one and equals $f$ on the feasible set; its
minimum value is $v$, and its minimizers are exactly the minimizers of
\eqref{eq:quadraticmodel}.

Finally, $\operatorname{bit}(\rho)\le\operatorname{bit}(n+2F_0+1)+E$.
Here $\operatorname{bit}(n+2F_0+1)=O(N)$ because $n\le N$ and
$\operatorname{bit}(F_0)=O(N)$. By \eqref{eq:height}, \eqref{eq:RM},
\eqref{eq:RD} and $s,m,n\le N$, we have $E\le1+\max\{\log_2R_M,\log_2R_D\}
=(N+1)2^{O(n)}$. This proves \eqref{eq:generalbits}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Comparison with the lower bound.}
The chain instance of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} satisfies
Assumption~\ref{ass:model}. It has $n=d+1$ continuous variables, and its
zero-multiplier threshold is $1/\delta_d=2^{2^d}$. So every coefficient
exact at multiplier zero needs at least $2^d+1$ bits, while
Theorem~\ref{thm:generalupper} gives $(N+1)2^{O(n)}$ bits. Thus the
exponential dependence on $n$ in \eqref{eq:generalbits} cannot be
removed. Remark~\ref{rem:seed} shows that the factor $N$ is also needed:
for each $n\ge2$ and each $N$ at least of order $n\log n$, it gives instances
with $n$ continuous variables and input length $O(N)$ whose least
penalty $\rho_*$ has $\Omega(N2^n)$ bits. Every coefficient that is
exact in any of the senses of Section~\ref{sec:geometry} is at least
$\rho_*$. Hence the worst-case bit length of a sufficient coefficient is
$N2^{\Theta(n)}$, for $N$ at least of order $n\log n$. The
constant in the exponent $O(n)$ is not optimized. Numerically, the
coefficient may be as large as $2^{(N+1)2^{O(n)}}$.
Section~\ref{sec:fixedcount} shows that a superpolynomial bit length
also requires many native inequalities that are nonlinear in the
continuous variables.
