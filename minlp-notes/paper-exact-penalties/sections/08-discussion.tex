\section{Discussion and open questions}\label{sec:discussion}
The results separate three issues about exact norm penalties: whether a
sufficient coefficient has a short encoding, whether a nearly least one
can be found efficiently, and how the position of the right-hand side
affects both. This section draws lessons for choosing penalties, states
the scope of the results and lists open questions.

\paragraph{Lessons for choosing penalties.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Large penalties come from nearly feasible improving
 assignments.} If an integer assignment that would improve the objective
 has a point that comes very close to satisfying the relaxed equalities,
 then by \eqref{eq:fixed} the zero-multiplier threshold is at least the
 objective gain divided by the residual norm of that point. The least
 penalty is at least half this ratio when, in addition, some native point
 of cost at most $v$ has a residual pointing in the opposite direction,
 because a balanced mixture of the two points cancels every multiplier;
 without such points the least penalty can be small (for one relaxed
 equality with one-sided residuals it is zero). Slater regularity on the
 equality-feasible slices prevents neither effect: in
 Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} every integer slice has points with slack $1/8$,
 yet the improving slice comes within $\delta_d=2^{-2^d}$ of the relaxed
 equality.
\item \emph{A safe coefficient follows from two separate bounds.} By
 Lemma~\ref{lem:slices}, a bound $\Lambda$ on multipliers on the
 equality-feasible slices and a lower bound $\delta$ on the residual of
 the equality-infeasible slices, together with objective bounds, give a
 coefficient that is exact at multiplier zero. Objective bounds are often
 available, and Slater points control $\Lambda$
 (Sections~\ref{sec:upper} and~\ref{sec:fixedcount}). The separation
 $\delta$ is the quantity that can be tiny.
\item \emph{Optimizing the multiplier does not remove the growth.} In
 Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} the least penalty is half the zero-multiplier
 threshold, and both are doubly exponential in $d$. With one relaxed
 equality, optimizing the multiplier reduces the required coefficient by
 at most a factor of two when both one-sided rates are nonnegative
 (Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar}). For several relaxed equalities we prove
 no such factor bound.
\item \emph{Safe is easy, nearly least is hard.} On the instances of
 Theorem~\ref{thm:polyfactor}, the coefficient $\rho=1$ is exact at
 multiplier zero and the optimal solution is known, yet no
 polynomial-time algorithm finds a sufficient coefficient within a
 polynomial factor of the least penalty unless $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.
 The difficulty lies in testing whether a given coefficient is exact
 (Remark~\ref{rem:hardnesslocation}), and rescaling the relaxed equality
 does not help (Remark~\ref{rem:rescaling}). The hardness is worst-case
 and weak, and it uses growing binary dimension.
\item \emph{Random right-hand sides avoid the bad case but change the
 problem.} A random right-hand side avoids the boundaries that cause large
 penalties with high probability (Theorem~\ref{thm:gridsmoothing}), but
 the guarantee is for the perturbed problem, whose optimal integer
 assignment can differ from the original one
 (Section~\ref{sec:perturbation-stability}). The coefficient grows like
 $K/\epsilon$, this order is necessary, and the least penalty has heavy
 tails (Section~\ref{sec:numerical-size}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Scope.}
All results except one lower-bound extension concern norm penalties with
a fixed norm and a fixed residual map, and coefficients in ordinary
binary encoding. The extension is that the lower bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} holds for every penalty function with at most
linear growth near zero, including the squared (proximal) augmentation
(Section~\ref{sec:lower-scope}). Apart from that lower bound, nothing is
claimed about the classical augmented Lagrangian with a squared penalty,
about solving the penalized subproblems or about solver performance.
Section~\ref{sec:lower-scope} also explains why rescaling the relaxed
equality, an instance-dependent fractional-power augmentation or
succinct exponent notation changes the question. The upper bounds of
Sections~\ref{sec:upper} and~\ref{sec:fixedcount} are existence bounds
whose constants are not evaluated, and the coefficients they give may be
numerically huge.

\subsection*{Open questions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The fixed-count gap.} Within the class of
 Assumption~\ref{ass:model} with at most $k$ native inequalities that are
 nonlinear in the continuous variables, the chain variant of
 Remark~\ref{rem:seed} needs $\Omega(N2^k)$ bits, while
 Theorem~\ref{thm:fixedcount} gives $N^{O(k+1)}$ bits. Is the worst case
 $N2^{\Theta(k)}$, $N^{\Theta(k)}$ or in between?
\item \emph{Without the refined Slater condition.} Suppose a boxed
 instance with convex quadratic slices has a finite least penalty, but
 the refined Slater condition fails on some equality-feasible slice. Does
 a sufficient coefficient with $(N+1)2^{O(n)}$ or $N^{O(k+1)}$ bits still
 exist? If not, what is the worst-case encoding length of $\rho_*$?
\item \emph{Nonconvex quadratic native constraints.} Under assumptions
 that guarantee finite exactness for nonconvex slices, such as those of
 \citet[Theorem~17]{LefebvreSchmidt2025}, do encoding bounds of the form
 $(N+1)2^{O(n)}$ still hold for degree-two data, or can nonconvexity force
 longer coefficients at fixed $n$?
\item \emph{Calibration in fixed dimension.} Our hardness results use a
 growing number of binary variables. For a fixed number of variables, or
 for a fixed number of integer variables with linear native constraints,
 can $\rho_*$ or $\rho_*(0)$ be computed, or approximated within a
 constant factor, in polynomial time?
\item \emph{Sharper perturbation bounds.} Can $K$ in
 Theorem~\ref{thm:gridsmoothing} be replaced by the number of slices
 whose residual images come near the right-hand side? For which noise
 radii does the perturbed problem keep the original optimal integer
 assignment with high probability while still admitting an explicit
 short coefficient?
\item \emph{Usable conservative coefficients.} Can the constants in
 Theorems~\ref{thm:generalupper} and~\ref{thm:fixedcount} be evaluated,
 or replaced by instance-dependent certificates, for example computable
 lower bounds on the separations of equality-infeasible slices and on
 Slater margins, so that the conservative coefficients become usable in
 computation?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Supplementary checks.}
Supplementary scripts (folder \texttt{verification}) check, with exact rational or symbolic arithmetic, the finite envelopes
and thresholds of the lower-bound instances (including the seed variant
of Remark~\ref{rem:seed}), the determinant identities of Section~\ref{sec:fixedcount} and a
degenerate example (no Slater point, singular objective Hessian,
divergent regularized multipliers) for the regularization in the proof
of Lemma~\ref{lem:nonzerovalue},
the calibration formulas and thresholds, and selected grid, tail and
feasibility-transfer identities from Section~\ref{sec:perturbation}. These checks support the
algebra; the proofs establish the general claims.
