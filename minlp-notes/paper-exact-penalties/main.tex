\begin{abstract}
Finite exactness does not ensure that a norm penalty has a short binary
representation or that its smallest sufficient coefficient can be
approximated efficiently. Our main contribution is to quantify this gap
for mixed-integer convex quadratic models. A compact family with one
binary variable, $d+1$ continuous variables and fixed rational
coefficients has least penalty $2^{2^d-1}$, even after optimizing an
unrestricted scalar linking multiplier. Thus every sufficient rational coefficient needs
at least $2^d$ numerator bits. This obstruction persists at fixed additive
accuracy and with uniform strict points on feasible integer slices.
Conversely, for quadratic input of length $N$ with $n$ continuous
variables, explicit finite boxes and refined Slater on every
equality-feasible slice, there is a sufficient integer zero-multiplier
coefficient with $(N+1)2^{O(n)}$ bits for the infinity and one-norm
penalties, preserving the full minimizer set. A second bound is
$N^{O(k+1)}$ bits when only $k$ native inequalities are nonlinear in
the continuous variables, allowing arbitrarily many affine rows.
Two complementary results distinguish representation from calibration
and conditioning. Calibration within any polynomial factor of the least
optimized-dual penalty is NP-hard even on a binary box with a known
unique primal optimizer and an obvious sufficient coefficient. Finite
rational right-hand-side perturbations instead give short sufficient
infinity-norm coefficients with high probability for finite unions of
compact convex slices with continuous convex objectives and affine residuals. This guarantee concerns the perturbed problem and allows
infeasibility; examples delimit its dependence on the slice count and
its implications for numerical size and expectations.
\end{abstract}
