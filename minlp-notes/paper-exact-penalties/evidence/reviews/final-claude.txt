## Findings, ranked

1. **Refined Slater hypothesis is ambiguous about the linking equalities** (`sections/04-general-upper.tex:17-21`, PDF p. 6). The text asks for "a point satisfying all its affine constraints and strictly satisfying every native inequality nonlinear in x" and never says the point must satisfy Ax+Bz=b. The KKT step in Section 4.2 and the margin problem σ_z in `05-fixed-count.tex:187-194` both need it. Under the native-only reading Theorem 4.1 is false: Lefebvre–Schmidt Example 13 (Appendix A) has a strict native point with s=1/2, yet L_ρ(0)<0=v for every finite ρ. "Equality-feasible slice" is also never defined. Fix: state that the point satisfies the linking equalities and native affine constraints and is strict on every native inequality nonlinear in x, and define equality-feasible slice as X_z∩{r=0}≠∅.

2. **The printing corollary omits its k=0 antecedent** (`05-fixed-count.tex:247-278`, `01-introduction.tex:43-54`, `evidence/coverage.md:21,97-103`, `evidence/stage2-sources.md:42`). Lefebvre–Schmidt Theorem 15 (retained text, lines 1134-1140) already computes a sufficient penalty in polynomial time for MILPs, and their text says it extends easily to convex MIQPs; Gu–Ahmed–Dey Theorem 11 gives the polynomial encoding. The evidence files call the corollary "a new deduction". Fix: one sentence in Section 5.3 crediting the linear-constraint case and stating that the new content is fixed k≥1 and fixed n.

3. **Zero-multiplier calibration hardness is proved in the notes but omitted** (`06-calibration.tex:106-115`). `research-20260925/minimum-penalty-independent-review.md:100` records that the same gadget has zero-multiplier threshold 1 on YES and 1/(K−1) on NO instances, so the polynomial-factor argument works at multiplier zero too. Sections 4 and 5 frame their conservative outputs as zero-multiplier coefficients, so the omission weakens the paper's own contrast. Also, ρ=1 at multiplier zero is only value-exact: on YES instances the residual −1 point ties. The text says "value coefficient", which is correct, but a word on minimizer-set exactness would help. Fix: two sentences.

4. **Section 7 credits only Dunagan–Spielman–Teng** (`07-perturbations.tex:5-8, 56, 180-188`). The repository's own review (`smoothed-penalty-novelty.md`, `smoothed-penalty-geometry.md:277-287`) names Friedlander–Tseng Theorem 4.2, Boț–Csetnek and Robinson's range/radius bound, Gugat–Hante Theorem 3 (Lipschitz value functions across discrete choices under uniform Slater), and Bürgisser–Amelunxen's linear-in-dimension uniform-noise tube bound. The paper calls the tools elementary and classical, which is right, but cites none of these. Bürgisser–Amelunxen is not retained locally, so retain it before citing. Fix: two or three sentences.

5. **The discussion overstates the scripts** (`08-discussion.tex:19-21`). "Supplementary scripts check ... calibration reductions" while the script prints "Not checked: general complexity reductions". Fix: "calibration dual formulas and thresholds".

6. **Several script checks are weaker than their descriptions.** In `check_calibration_perturbation.py`, lines 121-123 count the two named endpoints of the grid list and divide by q, so the "exact atomic correction" check is a tautology; the "infinite-threshold grid atom" check (lines 176-185) involves no grid; with q≤8 and d≥1/8 the grid bound tested is at least 1 in most of the 240 cases. In `check_upper_bound.py:83-89` the reciprocal-graph check asserts (1/r)·r=1 for m=1 only. README wording is not false, but these add little confidence. Fix: compute the 2/q correction from `box_boundary_distance`, add q∈{16,32,64} with smaller d, and replace the reciprocal check with an m=2 characterization of T_z, or soften the descriptions.

7. **Broken table in the coverage map** (`evidence/coverage.md:26`). A blank line splits the table, so the Gaussian-companion and infinite-threshold-atom rows on lines 27-28 will not render as table rows. Fix: delete the blank line.

8. **Wording and notation nits.**
   - `01-introduction.tex:47-48`: "complements this with N^{O(k+1)}" should say "bits".
   - `07-perturbations.tex:192`: q is the grid count in Section 7 but names the chain's binary variable here.
   - `07-perturbations.tex:206-249`: ρ_*(b) is used without saying it is the Section 2 threshold for residual r_z(x)−b in the infinity norm; "singleton slice of residual −1" should say image {−1}.
   - `05-fixed-count.tex:272-273`: "The absolute constants follow from the effective bounds" holds for fixed n (Basu–Roy is explicit) but not for fixed k, where Theorem 2.27 gives only O(·) exponents. The existence claim stands; say the fixed-k constant is not made explicit by the cited theorem.
   - `07-perturbations.tex:170-172, 207-215`: the K/ε necessity tail and the lower-dimensional infeasibility remark use continuous uniform noise, while the theorem is a grid statement. A grid version is immediate: with b_0=σ=1/2 and K dividing q, the grid points adjacent to the atoms j/K are at distance 1/(2q), so Pr{ρ_*(G)≥q}=2(K−1)/q≥ε/8. I checked this arithmetic.

9. **Optional.** Appendix A could point to Section 7.5 as the two-sided companion where no finite dual-value exact penalty exists and refined Slater fails, which restores the necessity role Lefebvre–Schmidt intended for Example 13. The stable-set model in Section 6.2 would also give strong polynomial-factor hardness via known inapproximability of α(G), and a citation for the "least exact penalty parameter" terminology (Dolgopolik, noted in `minimum-penalty-novelty.md:57`) would be conventional. These are enhancements, not defects.
