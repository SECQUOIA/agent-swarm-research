## Review result: no blocking errors, seven should-fix items, several minor ones

All four reviewers (one Fable on the whole target, three Opus on Section 2 and Appendix A, Section 3, and citations and packaging) independently recomputed the mathematics and found every stated result and proof correct. I confirmed this myself for the dual formulas, thresholds, multiplier interval, strict-point slacks, bit counts, symmetric variant, fractional-power remark, and the Example 13 analysis. The verification script passes, its output matches `lower-bound-results.txt`, and the PDF text matches the sources with consistent numbering. The surviving findings concern positioning, citations, and packaging accuracy. I verified each against the source texts in `research-20260925/parametric-sources/` and the repository notes before listing it.

### Should fix

1. **Appendix A never states the source claim it corrects.** `sections/A-attainment.tex:2` says the distinction "clarifies" Example 13, and line 25 says the example "demonstrates nonattainment, rather than failure of dual-value exactness". The source (text lines 937 to 939, 996) says the example "admits no finite exact penalty representation" and that Assumption 5 is "necessary", while its Definition 1 (lines 77 to 80) is supremum-based. Under that definition the example does have an exact penalty representation for every ρ, so the source's claim is false as stated. The source reaches it by reducing to λ = 0 via its Lemma 3, which transports only attained exactness. The repository review (`penalty-geometry-review.md`, section 4) calls this a material error. The appendix should quote the failing claim and name the step that fails, then keep the statement that Theorem 14 is unaffected.

2. **Introduction mischaracterizes Theorem 14 and over-scopes the negative answer.** `sections/01-introduction.tex:13-19`. Theorem 14 requires Assumptions 1 to 5, which also include a convex differentiable objective and a norm penalty, and its conclusion is exactness at every fixed multiplier. The paper never says explicitly that its instance satisfies those assumptions, which is the point of the "encoding, not existence" framing. Two repository audits (`publication-penalty-priority-audit.md`, `penalty-significance-review.md` section 1) require the qualifier that the answer covers growing continuous dimension and does not settle fixed total dimension, and `parametric-penalty-review.md` (section "Prior overlap") notes that Bienstock, Del Pia and Hildebrand's Example 6.2 already implies a large zero-multiplier penalty for nonconvex QCQPs. Add one sentence after Theorem 3.1 that Assumptions 1 to 5 hold, and one clause with the dimension qualifier and the zero-multiplier overlap.

3. **Beck et al. is cited for the wrong section.** `sections/01-introduction.tex:31` cites Section 2 for the sensitivity result. In the source, Section 2 is the problem statement with the chain and Slater regularity; the ε-feasibility sensitivity is Section 4 (source lines 99 to 101 and 279). Repository notes cite "Section 2 and Result 4". Cite Sections 2 and 4.

4. **Bibliography cites preprints where refereed versions exist, and lowercases "Lagrangian".** `references.bib` entries `Bienstock2022` and `Beck2023`. The first appeared in Mathematical Programming 197, 661 to 692 (2023), doi 10.1007/s10107-022-01818-3, already recorded in `literature/references.bib:1644-1650`. The second appeared in Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications 198, 428 to 447 (2023), doi 10.1007/s10957-023-02238-9, recorded in `parametric-penalty-review.md`. Keep the inspected-version notes, since theorem and section numbering in the journal versions was not verified. Also, `main.bbl:18,33,40` render "augmented lagrangian" because the title word is unbraced under plainnat.

5. **Title and opening paragraph promise calibration that this stage does not deliver.** `main.tex:19` and `sections/01-introduction.tex:5` ask "how difficult is it to find a nearly smallest one?" and nothing answers or defers it. Since hardness is intentionally deferred, add one sentence saying computational calibration is treated separately, or retitle the stage.

6. **README and script labels overstate what is checked.** `README.md:14` says the check "covers the projected native intervals", but `check_lower_bound.py:32-34` hard-codes the projected endpoints rather than deriving them. Line 77 to 79 labels a check "Fractional power identity" that asserts only 2^n · 2^{-n} = 1. Line 98 hard-codes "490" (correct today, 10 × 7 × 7, but not counted). The printed "source nonattainment branch formula" covers only the completed square on the z = 0 branch. `evidence/stage1-validation.md` describes the script accurately, so align README and print labels with it.

7. **Errors in `evidence/coverage.md`.** Line 3 says paths are relative to the repository root, but lines 12 to 18 and 69 to 71 use bare names that exist only under `research-20260925/`, and line 77 points at `formal/`, which is a different Lean project at the root. Line 22 cites "§5" of the hardness note for unit-data hardness; its headings put that in section 4, and section 5 is the literature comparison, so line 21's "§§1–4" already contains it.
