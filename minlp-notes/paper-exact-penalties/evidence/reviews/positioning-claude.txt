## Findings worth fixing

1. **Upper bounds stated without their norm restriction.** `main.tex:34-38` ("a zero-multiplier penalty with $(N+1)2^{O(n)}$ bits preserves the full minimizer set") and `sections/01-introduction.tex:37-40` never say which norm. Theorems 4.1 and 5.1 are proved only for the infinity and one norms, and the perturbation coefficient at `01-introduction.tex:56` is infinity-norm only. Since the abstract opens with norm penalties in general, readers will assume any norm. Line 38 ("a sufficient integer coefficient has ... bits") also reads as a statement about every sufficient coefficient, while the theorem only asserts one exists. Suggested: "some sufficient integer coefficient for the infinity or one norm has ...".

2. **"The bound improves to $N^{O(k+1)}$ bits"** at `main.tex:37-38` is not true in general. The two bounds are incomparable: with fixed n and large k, $(N+1)2^{O(n)}$ is the smaller one. The baseline wording "and a bound of ... when" was accurate.

3. **Bhardwaj attribution is generous.** `01-introduction.tex:70-73` says Theorems 3.1–3.3 "give analytic penalty bounds for MILPs and MIQPs". In the source, Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 state only existence with a parameter that "depends on" the data; explicit constants appear in the proofs (Propositions 4.6 and 4.8), and the explicit formula for the penalty is only Theorem 3.3 under strong convexity and smoothness. The source abstract does advertise "quantification", so this is imprecise rather than false. The same overstatement is in `evidence/positioning-sources.md:25-27`. Suggested: "existence results with data-dependent parameters for MILPs and MIQPs, and an explicit bound under strong convexity and smoothness".

4. **Section 2 comparison misdescribes Proposition 2.1.** `sections/02-geometry.tex:35-37` calls it "a self-contained specialization of this principle; we use it without assuming multiplier attainment." It is not a special case of Feizollahi–Ahmed–Sun Theorem 1: it covers arbitrary compact X and continuous f, with compactness replacing the relative-interior condition. And their Theorem 1 does not assume attainment either, so the contrast suggests a difference that is not there. Three reviewers flagged this independently. Suggested: "a residual-space analogue for compact X that needs no relative-interior condition".

5. **"At least a $1-\epsilon$ fraction of samples"** at `01-introduction.tex:54-56` paraphrases a probability statement (Theorem 7.4) as an empirical frequency. Because the draw is uniform on a finite grid, "a $1-\epsilon$ fraction of grid points" or "with probability at least $1-\epsilon$" would be exact.

6. **Notation switch d versus n.** The abstract (`main.tex:29-32`) and intro (`01-introduction.tex:22-26`) introduce Theorem 3.1 with d, but the theorem uses n throughout, and the intro uses n for continuous dimension a few lines later. The switch is unannounced. One clause identifying d with the theorem's n would fix it.

7. **Boland–Eberhard scope.** `01-introduction.tex:65-67` credits "finite-penalty exactness for bounded pure integer programs" to Boland–Eberhard. I confirmed the inspected manuscript's assumption (3) requires superlinear growth of the penalty, which excludes plain norms. The sentence avoids the word "norm" and the evidence file says this was deliberate, but in a paper about norm penalties the reader will assume norms. Consider "for superlinear penalty functions" or attributing the norm case on rational MILPs to Feizollahi–Ahmed–Sun Theorem 4 alone.

8. **Discussion caveat moved to the wrong results.** `sections/08-discussion.tex:16-19`: "Neither result guarantees favorable numerical conditioning" now refers to the calibration and perturbation results. The caveat that matters, that a polynomial bit bound still allows an exponentially large number, belongs to the upper bounds, where the baseline attached it. The following "also changes the representation question" has no antecedent for "also".

9. **Minor referents.** `07-perturbations.tex:12` "the cited tube bound" points readers to Bürgisser–Amelunxen, but Section 7.6 uses Dunagan–Spielman–Teng Lemma 2.3.4. `01-introduction.tex:13-14` defines "native" via "these constraints" (the quadratic ones only) and "a separate affine equality" (singular), while Sections 4–5 relax a system with m rows. `main.tex:30-31` "all linking multipliers" is plural for a single multiplier.

10. **Optional citation.** Kleinert, Labbé, Plein and Schmidt, "There's No Free Lunch" (Operations Research 68(6), 2020), proves that verifying a big-M is not polynomial unless P=NP. I confirmed the abstract online. It is a different quantity and does not weaken Theorem 6.3, but it is an earlier primary hardness result that fits the "Generic penalty-choice hardness ... predates this paper" sentence at `01-introduction.tex:115-116`. Judgment call.
