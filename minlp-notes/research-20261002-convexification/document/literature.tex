\section{Relationship to established results}\label{sec:literature}

The local source audit is
\pathref{../literature/README.md}{literature/README.md}, with retained
primary documents, a source manifest, and focused comparisons for
composition and stars. It is a targeted audit, not a proof of novelty.
In particular, the following ingredients should not be presented as
discoveries of this continuation.

Simultaneous support separation is established. Liers et
al.\ \cite{LiersEtAl2021} attribute the linear-combination hull
characterization to Ballerstein's dissertation
\cite{Ballerstein2013} and develop simultaneous separation for gas
networks. Tawarmalani's inclusion certificates
\cite{Tawarmalani2010} already explain why compatible representing
measures matter when combining hulls. The term ``inclusion certificate''
there refers to a mathematical measure representation, not this
implementation's serialized cut artifact. The dissertation download
was unavailable during this continuation; detailed historical claims
about its Chapter 5 remain attributed to the earlier repository audit,
while the hull characterization is corroborated by Liers et al.'s
inspected primary text.

Vector composite relaxations are also established. He and Tawarmalani
\cite{HeTawarmalani2021} transfer simultaneous separation through an
oracle-based construction; \cite{HeTawarmalani2022} gives common
triangulation conditions for combining simultaneous hypographs;
\cite{HeTawarmalani2024} treats vector composite graphs and shared
discretization. The checked 2024 preprint is its third version, whose
Proposition 28 and Corollary 29 explicitly address vector graphs.
Zhu, He, and Tawarmalani \cite{ZhuHeTawarmalani2026} develop
axis-aligned simultaneous relaxations and convergence on coupled
domains, with a large MINLPLib root-bound study. Li et al.
\cite{LiEtAl2026} construct univariate polynomial envelopes through
continuous Graham-scan ideas under their stated numerical model.
The present cut contract therefore concerns a particular supported
representation, exact final coefficients, and measured implementation
behavior, rather than the introduction of shared-variable convexification.

Bernstein enclosure and rigorous final affine shifts predate this
implementation. Garloff and Smith \cite{GarloffSmith2008}, Section 5,
explicitly choose an affine candidate numerically and validate a final
shift with interval Bernstein coefficients. Garloff, Jansson, and Smith
\cite{GarloffJanssonSmith2003} study inclusion under domain shrinkage.
Polynomial dependencies and selective products also appear in
reformulation-linearization methods \cite{SheraliTuncbilek1997}.
The new record binds an explicit expression graph, complete domain,
and final row so that the concrete claim can be replayed. This is an
implementation contract to evaluate, not a new general principle of
validated numerics.

Exact low-dimensional quadratic hull descriptions are known from
Anstreicher and Burer \cite{AnstreicherBurer2010}. Their Theorem 7
uses triangulation for polytopes of dimension at most three, and
their low-order completely positive representation explains why these
classes have exact semidefinite descriptions. The stationary-candidate
oracle of Theorem~\ref{thm:polygon} is a direct, exact arithmetic way
to compute support in two dimensions. It does not supersede or
introduce that hull theory. Locatelli \cite{Locatelli2018} and
M\"uller, Serrano, and Gleixner \cite{MullerSerranoGleixner2020}
already use constrained two-dimensional geometry for stronger
bilinear treatment. Our vector of quadratic features is broader than
one product, but improvement over independent McCormick inequalities
can duplicate strength already available in native solver cuts.

For stars, Del Pia and Khajavirad
\cite{DelPiaKhajavirad2026} directly cover box-only support through
their forest algorithm. Classical parametric QP
\cite{BemporadEtAl2002} explains positive-curvature affine responses.
Khajavirad \cite{Khajavirad2026} provides tight sparse SDP
relaxations under additional sign and graph hypotheses. Locatelli's
simplex-envelope stars \cite{Locatelli2015} refer to a different
convexity graph and domain. The explicit all-sign center--leaf row
extension here must be judged in this context; an unmatched exact
theorem in a short search does not establish priority.

The overlap example illustrates a limitation of a chosen finite
interface. It does not refute sparse moment convergence or known
finite-extraction results \cite{Lasserre2001,Lasserre2006}. Full
separator distributions, or enough moments to identify them under
additional structure, satisfy a stronger gluing condition than equal
means and squares. Neither the bounded cut loop nor the moment
hierarchy is shown to dominate the other generally.

Finally, automatic recognition is already a solver capability.
SCIP's nonlinear handlers, projected bilinear envelopes, RLT cuts,
and selected moment minors are described in
\cite{BestuzhevaEtAl2025}. The native baseline therefore remains the
appropriate primary comparator. The contribution supported by this
report is the integrated checked-row implementation, explicit support
classes and counterexamples, and its transparent positive and negative
evidence. Broad performance or first-method claims require evidence
beyond this bounded continuation.
