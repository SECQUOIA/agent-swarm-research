\section{Computational evidence and verification}\label{sec:evidence}

\subsection{The frozen comparison}

The experimental question is whether the checked block component is
useful after accounting for discovery, reformulation, sampling,
certification, and repeated work. The four modes are the original
parsed model (\texttt{baseline}), the common auxiliary formulation
without new cuts (\texttt{control}), every supported block within the
same work caps (\texttt{all}), and the automatic policy
(\texttt{auto}). Native SCIP separation remains enabled.

The saved protocol includes 13 predefined mechanism and control
models and a held-out slice of 24 MINLPLib models. The latter excludes
the 123 names occurring in two specified earlier repository campaigns,
filters by model metadata and parser support, and selects by a fixed
SHA256 ordering from 279 eligible models. It does not filter by new
handler applicability or observed outcomes. This is held out from the
two named campaigns, not from every possible past use of public
MINLPLib instances. The initial six-model proposal was expanded to
24 before new optimization outcomes, keeping the same order.
The archived metadata includes 11 held-out models with integer
variables and seven classified as convex. Four previously studied
instances---\texttt{btest14}, \texttt{waterno2\_06},
\texttt{ghg\_2veh}, and \texttt{chp\_partload}---form a separate
diagnostic suite and are excluded from the held-out summary.

Each main run has a six-second soft in-process budget, one SCIP thread,
BLAS/OpenMP thread environment variables set to one, seed zero,
and relative gap tolerance
$10^{-4}$. Each independent worker has a hard 20-second process
timeout. A separate one-node comparison uses two-second budgets on
the 24 held-out models and five predefined mechanism cases. Imports,
process startup, and independent post-run checking are measured
separately. No full run is warm-started from the one-node comparison.
The host also runs unrelated research tasks, so subsecond timing
differences are descriptive.

Discovery, model construction, and individual support calls are
nonpreemptive, so the soft budget can be exceeded; observed overshoots
are retained. If the hard timeout kills a process before its result is
written, its cut count is unknown. An absent record is not evidence of
zero cuts or successful replay. Strict source/native import refusals
are reported separately from optimization failures. These are refusals
by this research importer, shared by all modes, rather than native
SCIP failures. Selected-population totals retain their denominator,
but admitted counts and refusal counts must be shown separately;
only admitted models measure solver behavior.

Caching, native sampling, and selected star merging have separate
ablations when applicable. Repeats use a fixed second-seed subset.
The \texttt{no\_cache} switch is a combined reuse-policy ablation:
it disables exact-support sample exchange, previous-query skipping,
and certified mixture screening as well as sampled-value reuse.
Its comparison cannot isolate the causal effect of caching alone.
The aggregate campaign budget is bounded. Results preserve errors,
timeouts, domains rejected by the new component, and unfavorable
outcomes. Any rerun after an identified defect retains the original
record and explains the implementation change.

An independent evaluator checks every returned original variable
vector against original bounds, integrality, expression domains,
rows, and objective using scaled tolerance $10^{-5}$. This is a
numerical residual check. The checker also compares dual bounds to
known synthetic optima or archived feasible objective values with
explicit numerical tolerances. Neither check certifies a global dual
bound. Fresh-process support replay instead checks each saved cut
against the reconstructed model binding; a missing or failed replay
is not counted as a certified row.

outcome. The primary comparison is Table~\ref{tab:primary}.
``Solved'' means SCIP reported \texttt{optimal} or \texttt{gaplimit},
returned a finite primal objective, and passed the independent
original-model numerical check without a reference-bound conflict.

All four modes refused the same three held-out models because their
logarithm domains were not proved on the declared boxes:
\texttt{syn15m}, \texttt{cvxnonsep\_psig30r}, and
\texttt{syn10hfsg}. The inherited builder also could not handle the
genuine variable-exponent terms of \texttt{cvxnonsep\_pcon40r}.
These four cases were never comparable solver attempts. They remain
in the selected count of 24; the solver comparison concerns 20
admitted cases. This reports the limitations of the research importer,
not the capabilities of native SCIP on the refused models.

The additional held-out timeout in both cut modes was
\texttt{genpooling\_lee2}: baseline and control solved it in
approximately 5.61 and 4.89 seconds, while both cut modes reached
the six-second limit. \texttt{kall\_circles\_c6b} remained
time-limited in every mode. Thus the observed primary outcome is
adverse for enabling the new separator by default. Automatic selection
reduces work relative to unconditional activation but does not recover
the lost solve.

The synthetic improvement in solved count also needs its control:
\texttt{quartic\_balance\_8} is unsolved by baseline within six
seconds, but control already solves it without new cuts. Both cut
modes solve it too. The count change from 12 to 13 therefore cannot
be attributed to the new rows. On the exact overlap instance, native
baseline already reaches the optimum numerically in about 0.10
seconds; the merged cut modes take about 0.50 and 0.48 seconds.
The strict mathematical pair-hull gap remains useful, but this full
solver already has other ways to address that instance.

In the separate historical suite, only \texttt{waterno2\_06}
passes the common importer, and every mode is time-limited. Its
numerical lower bounds are approximately 26.59, 21.17, 7.05, and
2.41 for baseline, control, all, and auto. The other three historical
instances are refused before solving. This diagnostic does not extend
the earlier historical gains to the new verified component.

\subsection{Root strength, work selection, and ablations}

Held-out one-node comparisons are mixed. Relative to baseline,
\texttt{all} has three better bounds, 15 ties, and two worse bounds;
\texttt{auto} has three better, 14 ties, and three worse. Four
comparisons are unavailable because of the common import limitations.
The comparison tolerance is $10^{-6}$ times the larger bound scale.
These are the numerical bounds at the end of the separate two-second,
one-node runs, including native separation, rather than exact values
of the new block relaxations.

On the primary held-out runs, automatic selection reduces candidate
LP calls from 1073 to 323, recorded cuts from 230 to 106, and total
callback time from 5.59 to 3.05 seconds relative to \texttt{all}.
Support certification accounts for 0.70 and 0.34 seconds respectively.
There are 95 and 14 unsuccessful support attempts; these include
insufficient violation, unsupported structure, and exhausted budgets,
not invalid accepted cuts. Across all phases, the automatic
convex-combination screen is queried 506 times and permits two
skips. Its mathematical guarantee is tested, but this campaign does
not demonstrate large savings from the screen itself.

The recorded full held-out in-process totals are 17.40, 20.66, 25.41,
and 23.01 seconds. The corresponding sums of outer worker time,
including startup and checking, are 36.66, 40.12, 45.44, and 42.49
seconds. These totals retain available refusal costs; an unsupported
worker without an in-process result has only its outer time.
The shorter automatic callback total does not establish a broad
complete-solve gain. One-seed, shared-host timing and short budgets
also preclude precise estimates of small relative differences.

Disabling star merging on the overlap instance changes the all/auto
times to about 0.19/0.10 seconds, with numerical solution status
unchanged. The larger exact support class therefore has a strict
mathematical diagnostic but no demonstrated runtime advantage in
this native-solver case. The combined reuse-policy ablation also
keeps the selected mechanism cases numerically solved and shows
small mixed time differences. Because it removes exchange,
screening, and repeated-query checks along with caches, it cannot
isolate a cache-only effect. The fixed second-seed runs are retained
in the summary and do not constitute a population-level statistical
study. No algorithm tuning was performed in response to these
campaign outcomes.

\subsection{Replay coverage and numerical residual checks}

Fresh-process replay in
\pathref{../experiments/campaign-v1/replay.json}{campaign-v1/replay.json}
checks all 1082 recorded cuts against original-model feature bindings,
complete proof domains, final coefficients, and saved actual native
rows. All 1082 pass. The replay binds 308 saved original-model
outputs, verifies 101 frozen source/input hashes, and rejects all
12 coefficient, right-hand-side, domain, variable, scope, and native-row
tampering controls.

The eight remaining jobs are full and one-node attempts on
\texttt{cvxnonsep\_pcon40r} in four modes. They fail on the unsupported
variable exponent before optimization according to the worker trace,
but lack original-model and cut logs. Their logged cut count stays
unknown; they are not represented as successfully replayed runs with
zero cuts. There are 36 structured importer refusals across all
phases, retained with their model records. No hard process timeout or
aggregate-budget exhaustion occurs. Soft-budget overshoots remain
in the raw summary; they are not converted into extra allotted time.

The independent evaluator checks 270 returned original-variable
incumbents; all pass its scaled $10^{-5}$ test. No checked root or
final dual bound conflicts with its analytic optimum or archived
feasible reference under the stated tolerance. These results are
numerical sanity checks and do not convert a SCIP bound, its
incumbent, or its search into an exact proof. The independently
derived metrics audit and full integration review are retained
alongside the replay output.

\subsection{The exact overlap mechanism}

The standalone
\pathref{../experiments/star-mechanism.json}{experiments/star-mechanism.json}
stores the two rational pair measures, their shared moments, the
three-piece exact center partition, and the attained star minimizer
for Proposition~\ref{prop:overlap}. The pair-hull objective is exactly
zero and the assembled support value is exactly $1/128$, attained
at $(1,11/16,1)$. The replay accepted this proof and rejected a changed
objective constant. This is an exact algebraic and implementation
diagnostic, independent of SCIP and of its root-bound heuristics.
Its source hashes are retained in the same record. It establishes the
specific missing strength and the oracle's recovery of it; it does
not establish a runtime advantage over native dense SDP/RLT treatment.

The 24 quartics are $(x+iy/8)^4+ixy/13$, $i=1,\ldots,24$.
At 32 points the single-quartic C call was slower than NumPy, and
the low-degree cases remain slower in C even at 4096 points. The

soft budget; a wrapper must count it in outer process time when
assessing a compiled-sampler run.

\subsection{Targeted checks and actual-file review}

The combined targeted command recorded in
\pathref{../VERIFICATION.md}{VERIFICATION.md} passed 186 tests and
53 subtests across the new solver, theory, independent-review, and
experiment-harness files. A later pre-freeze nonfinite-objective
regression was checked by a focused four-test harness rerun; three
of those tests were already in the combined run. These numbers are
separate executed checks, not a claim that one final command ran 187
distinct tests. The archived command avoids the frozen source copies
and collects no unrelated repository tests.

The kernel tests exercise interior minima, exact final coefficient
rounding, complete subdivision coverage, strict row exclusions,
domain singularities, empty domains, and tampered model or proof
records. Screening tests exercise exact mixture normalization,
normalization-specific violation bounds, interval graph values,
infeasible samples, and replay. The polygon and star tests compare
independently formulated special cases, degeneracies, and the exact
overlap example. An additional star audit enumerates stationary
points on full-space faces rather than repeating the leaf algorithm.
Native sampling tests cover reference values, alignment, empty arrays,
unsupported data, and compiler or storage failure.

The full-space star audit compared 155 cases (76 feasible and 79
empty), checked 298 returned pieces, and rejected seven mutations.
A second audit used independent projection and verified leaf rules
over 786 complete center intervals in 88 cases, making 7063 exact
alternative-rule comparisons and rejecting three mutations. The
two checks exercise different contracts: attained global optima and
validity of every reported conditional piece.

The numerical and mathematical reviews read actual source and
certificate files. Their independence means a different research
agent checked the saved artifacts; it does not mean a separately
implemented arithmetic library, a formal proof assistant, or journal
peer review. The companion claim map records the precise test and
review paths. Verification is restricted to this topic; no
project-wide test run or CI inspection is claimed.
