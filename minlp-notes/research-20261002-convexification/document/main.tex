\begin{abstract}
Several nonlinear expressions can share variables and affine constraints
that separate scalar relaxations do not fully use. This report develops
an implemented support-cut interface for these dependencies. Candidate
directions may be chosen numerically; a separate calculation checks the
final coefficients on their complete domain before a cut is submitted.
Exact rational Bernstein bounds cover supported polynomial blocks, and
outward interval evaluation covers a stated elementary-function grammar.
We give exact rational support algorithms for quadratic vector graphs over
bounded two-dimensional polyhedra and for quadratic stars with affine
center--leaf constraints. We also characterize a limitation of intersecting
overlapping pair hulls and give a quantitative separating example.
Automatic discovery, bounded separation, cached work, and an optional native sampling
kernel connect the construction to SCIP while retaining native nonlinear
feasibility enforcement. A fixed short-run experiment and certificate
replay evaluate supported mechanisms and their costs. The report separates
cut validity from numerical solver bounds and feasible residual checks.
Of 24 held-out models, 20 pass the common research importer. Native
baseline and the reformulation control each solve 19 of those 20 within
the short budget; both cut modes solve 18. Automatic selection reduces
separation work, but these results do not support default activation.
The hull, Bernstein, and sparse quadratic ingredients have substantial
prior literature; this is an integrated research implementation with
explicit mathematical and computational limits, not a claim of general
solver acceleration or publication priority.
\end{abstract}

\section{The capability and the evidence standard}\label{sec:scope}

The task is to strengthen relaxations automatically when several nonlinear
expressions move together. The existing repository already contains scalar
composite envelopes, simultaneous univariate-curve cuts, and concave row
hulls. Their recorded results motivate this continuation, but they do not
establish a generally beneficial solver component. In particular, the
historical composite-univariate campaign solved 51 of 107 instances within
120 seconds with its Python handler, versus 54 with native SCIP. Its
curvature classification was validated with ball arithmetic, while function
values in final cuts used a floating-point safety shift. The historical
curve separator also documented a numerical-library accuracy assumption.
These are historical results, not reruns or certified outcomes of this
continuation. The original records are
\pathref{../../results/composite-univariate-envelopes.md}{results/composite-univariate-envelopes.md}
and
\pathref{../../research-20260922/curve-hulls/report.md}{research-20260922/curve-hulls/report.md}.

This report closes a narrower and testable development program: a checked
cut interface on explicit supported expression classes; automatic block
discovery and bounded repeated work; exact support for coupled quadratic
blocks and a larger structured extension; comparative solver evidence;
and a traceable comparison with primary literature. A supported cut is
checked against a supplied expression graph and domain. A completed SCIP
solve is not thereby converted into a replayable global optimality proof.

The distinction matters at three levels. First, a mathematical support
theorem proves validity for every feasible graph point. Second, a replay
record checks a concrete cut for a concrete model representation. Third,
SCIP reports numerical primal and dual information after its own presolve,
LP computations, transformations, and search. Only the first two levels
are covered by the new cut-certificate claim. Numerical primal residuals
and comparisons with known objectives are useful independent checks, but
are not exact feasibility or dual-bound certificates.

The companion \pathref{COVERAGE.md}{COVERAGE.md} maps claims to proofs,
code, saved outputs, and limitations. The frozen experiment protocol is
\pathref{../experiments/protocol.md}{experiments/protocol.md}. Internal
research-agent reviews are recorded separately from the construction;
they are not journal peer review or formal verification.

\section{Joint support cuts}\label{sec:support}

Let $D\subset\R^n$ be nonempty and compact, and let
$F=(f_1,\ldots,f_k):D\to\R^k$ be continuous. Write
\[
G_D=\{(x,F(x)):x\in D\},\qquad H_D=\conv G_D.
\]
The lifted variables $w_j$ represent the \emph{same} functions $f_j$ of
the \emph{same} variables $x$. For a direction $d=(a,b)\in\R^{n+k}$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support}
h_D(a,b)=\min_{x\in D}\{a^\top x+b^\top F(x)\}.
\end{equation}
We use lower support values, so every cut is written with a lower bound.
If integer variables occur in $D$, support over a continuous superset
remains valid for the mixed-integer graph, but need not describe its hull.

\begin{proposition}[Support and complete hull description]\label{prop:support}
If $\underline h\le h_D(a,b)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cut}
a^\top x+b^\top w\ge\underline h
\end{equation}
is valid for $H_D$. Moreover,
\[
H_D=\bigcap_{(a,b)\in\R^{n+k}}
\{(x,w):a^\top x+b^\top w\ge h_D(a,b)\}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The defining minimum makes the inequality valid at each point of $G_D$;
linearity preserves it under convex combinations. The graph is compact
by continuity, and its convex hull is compact in finite dimension. A point
outside that hull is strictly separated by a linear functional. Orient
that functional as a lower support inequality to obtain a member of the
displayed intersection that excludes the point.
\end{proof}

This is classical convex hull duality; simultaneous graph separation
from linear combinations is developed explicitly in
\cite{LiersEtAl2021,Tawarmalani2010}. The computational problem is to
evaluate or bound~\eqref{eq:support}, and to find a useful direction
without spending more than its benefit. A numerical direction generator
is permitted to be inaccurate: its output only chooses $(a,b)$.
Soundness depends on a bound for that \emph{final} direction, not on the
correctness or optimality of the direction search. The same observation
allows random samples, LP direction search, and a native sampling kernel
outside the cut-validity calculation.

For $n=1$ and $k=1$, optimizing over the coefficient of $x$ recovers the
biconjugate description of a scalar convex envelope. For $k>1$, joint
directions retain dependencies between outputs that intersected scalar
envelopes can omit. A finite collection of valid joint cuts generally
gives an outer approximation; absence of a violated generated cut does
not establish membership in $H_D$.

\begin{proposition}[Final floating-point row]\label{prop:round}
Suppose the exact values of the coefficients submitted to a numerical
solver are $\widehat a\in\Q^n$ and $\widehat b\in\Q^k$ (in particular,
finite binary floating-point numbers interpreted exactly). If a checker
establishes
\[
L\le\min_{x\in D}
\{\widehat a^\top x+\widehat b^\top F(x)\}
\]
and the submitted right-hand side $\widehat L$ satisfies
$\widehat L\le L$ exactly, the submitted mathematical row is valid on
$H_D$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:support} to the submitted coefficients and
then weaken the right-hand side from $L$ to $\widehat L$.
\end{proof}

Rounding an already proved rational row without checking its resulting
coefficients would not satisfy this contract. One alternative, when a
trusted box $\ell\le z\le u$ is available for all lifted coordinates,
is to replace the exact row $c^\top z\ge L$ by
\[
\widehat c^\top z\ge
L+\sum_i\min\{(\widehat c_i-c_i)\ell_i,
                 (\widehat c_i-c_i)u_i\},
\]
and round the right-hand side downward. The formula follows by bounding
each coordinate of $(\widehat c-c)^\top z$. Our principal contract is
the direct check for the final direction in
Proposition~\ref{prop:round}; it avoids needing auxiliary-variable bounds
for this correction.

\section{Finite certificates on the whole domain}\label{sec:numerics}

\subsection{Exact Bernstein bounds for rational polynomials}

Consider a polynomial $p$ on a rational box
$B=\prod_{i=1}^n[\ell_i,u_i]$. Substitute
$x_i=\ell_i+(u_i-\ell_i)t_i$ and eliminate fixed coordinates. In the
remaining coordinates, write
\[
p(x(t))=\sum_{\beta\le d}a_\beta t^\beta
       =\sum_{\alpha\le d}b_\alpha B_\alpha^d(t),\qquad
B_\alpha^d(t)=\prod_i
\binom{d_i}{\alpha_i}t_i^{\alpha_i}(1-t_i)^{d_i-\alpha_i}.
\]
Here $d$ is a componentwise degree bound and all coefficients are exact
rationals. The conversion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bernstein-conversion}
b_\alpha=\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}a_\beta
\prod_i\frac{\binom{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}}{\binom{d_i}{\beta_i}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Bernstein enclosure]\label{lem:bernstein}
On $B$, $\min_\alpha b_\alpha\le p\le\max_\alpha b_\alpha$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $0\le t_i\le1$, every $B_\alpha^d(t)$ is nonnegative and
$\sum_\alpha B_\alpha^d(t)=1$, by the binomial theorem in each
coordinate. Thus $p(x(t))$ is a convex combination of the coefficients.
Identity~\eqref{eq:bernstein-conversion} follows by expressing each
monomial $t^\beta$ in the tensor-product Bernstein basis; the univariate
identity has coefficients $\binom{\alpha}{\beta}/\binom d\beta$.
\end{proof}

The enclosure is classical
\cite{GarloffSmith2008,GarloffJanssonSmith2003}. The implementation contribution is to bind
the exact polynomial, its original box, and the checked final row to
the recorded partition and lower bounds. Adaptive subdivision can
improve a weak enclosure without changing the validity argument.

\begin{proposition}[Partition certificate]\label{prop:partition}
Let $B_1,\ldots,B_N$ be finitely many closed boxes whose union is $B$,
and suppose $L_j\le\inf_{B_j}p$ is verified for each $j$. Then
$L=\min_jL_j\le\inf_Bp$. The same conclusion holds for a finite interval
partition and any expression with a verified interval enclosure on every
cell.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every $x\in B$ belongs to a checked cell $B_j$, where
$p(x)\ge L_j\ge\min_iL_i$.
\end{proof}

The coverage premise is essential. Checking all supplied cells while
accepting an omitted end interval would not prove the claimed bound.
The certificate checker must start from the trusted original domain and
verify the entire subdivision, including endpoints. A cache key also
needs the expression, direction, and domain: reusing a valid bound from a
smaller domain on a larger domain is unsound.

\subsection{Supported elementary expressions and explicit failure}

For a supported elementary expression, outward ball or interval
arithmetic supplies an enclosure for the support objective on each
interval. Rational constants and final binary coefficients are embedded
exactly. Expression evaluation must retain domain requirements: a
logarithm needs a positive argument; division needs a denominator
excluding zero; powers require their stated real domain. Algebraic
simplification is not permission to remove the original expression's
domain restrictions.

If a pole, invalid domain, nonfinite enclosure, unsupported operation, or
resource limit prevents a complete bound, the procedure returns that
status. An exhausted direction search means only that no checked useful
cut was obtained within its budget. It does not prove that a point lies
in a hull, that the original constraints hold, or that the domain is
empty. Even a successfully checked cut need not be violated enough to
justify insertion into the LP.

The elementary support calculation can also use a curvature correction.
If the scalar direction $p$ is twice continuously differentiable on
$[a,b]$ and a verified bound gives $p''\le M$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chord-correction}
\min_{[a,b]}p\ge
\min\{p(a),p(b)\}-\max\{0,M\}\,(b-a)^2/8.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $p(t)-Mt^2/2$ is concave, so its chord inequality rearranges to
$p(t)\ge\operatorname{chord}_p(t)-M(t-a)(b-t)/2$.
Use $(t-a)(b-t)\le(b-a)^2/4$ when $M\ge0$; for $M<0$, the
chord itself is a lower bound. Verified lower endpoint values can
replace $p(a)$ and $p(b)$. The implementation first checks all feature
domains, including zero-weight features, then uses the maximum of this
bound and available natural enclosures. An unavailable derivative bound
or singular derivative disables this optional strengthening; it does
not license removing a domain endpoint.

The supported grammar, actual certificate schema, and solver binding
are stated with their implementation in Section~\ref{sec:integration}.
Neither Python's rational arithmetic nor the interval library is
formally verified here. Replay reduces the trusted computational path
and catches mismatched artifacts; it is not a proof of the underlying
software stack.

\section{Exact support for coupled quadratic pairs}\label{sec:polygon}

Let $P\subset\R^2$ be a bounded polyhedron described by rational bounds
and rational affine inequalities. The full quadratic feature graph uses
\[
v(x,y)=(1,x,y,x^2,xy,y^2).
\]
Any direction for a vector of quadratic expressions gives a quadratic
objective
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic}
q(x,y)=c_0+c_x\,x+c_y\,y+c_{xx}x^2+c_{xy}xy+c_{yy}y^2.
\end{equation}
All coefficients are rational, including exactly interpreted finite
binary floating-point inputs.

\begin{theorem}[Finite rational support candidates]\label{thm:polygon}
If $P$ is nonempty, the minimum of $q$ is attained at one of the following
rational points:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a vertex of $P$;
\item a stationary point in the relative interior of a polygon edge,
      or of $P$ itself when $P$ is a segment, on which the restriction
      of $q$ has strictly positive quadratic coefficient;
\item the stationary point of $q$ in $P$, if its Hessian is positive
      definite and $P$ is two-dimensional.
\end{enumerate}
Enumerating this list and evaluating it exactly gives an attained exact
support value. Empty, segment, and singleton domains are included.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The compact nonempty domain has a minimizer. If it lies on the boundary
of a two-dimensional polygon, or if the domain has dimension at most
one, restrict $q$ to the relevant closed segment. Its minimum is at an
endpoint unless its quadratic coefficient is positive and its stationary
point is interior; those are the listed candidates.

If a minimizer is interior in two dimensions, its gradient vanishes and
its constant Hessian $H$ is positive semidefinite. If $H$ is positive
definite, the unique stationary point is the solution of a nonsingular
rational linear system. If $H$ is singular, choose a nonzero vector
$d\in\ker H$. Along the line through the minimizer $z$, the quadratic
identity and $\nabla q(z)=0$ give $q(z+td)=q(z)$. The line reaches the
boundary of the bounded polygon, so a boundary minimizer also exists.
The zero Hessian case is included. Thus no extra singular interior
candidate is required. Every vertex is obtained from rational active
rows, and edge stationary parameters are rational ratios. Exact
evaluation therefore gives a rational minimum and rational minimizer.
\end{proof}

With $m$ affine rows including bounds, the implementation intersects all
pairs of nonparallel boundary lines and checks each intersection against
every row. A planar hull orders the retained vertices. This costs
$O(m^3)$ exact rational operations for the straightforward enumeration;
the edge and interior candidates add lower-order work. This is an
arithmetic-operation bound, not a floating-point runtime guarantee.
Rational arithmetic must account for numerator and denominator growth.

The certificate stores the original box, every affine row, every
coefficient, polygon vertices, all candidates, their exact values, and
the minimizer. Replay reconstructs the list from trusted inputs. The
producer and checker share geometric routines; this is reproducible
exact arithmetic with independent direction search, not an
independently implemented formal checker.

Using the row domain before convexifying can strengthen even an exact
box graph hull. On $x,y\ge0$, $x+y\le1$,
\[
x^2+2xy+y^2\le x+y,\qquad xy\le1/4.
\]
The first inequality is $s^2\le s$ for $s=x+y\in[0,1]$, and the
second follows from $4xy\le(x+y)^2$. In contrast, half mass at each
of $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$ is a valid box graph-hull point whose means
satisfy the row, but whose second-moment coordinates give
$x^2+2xy+y^2=2>x+y=1$ and $xy=1/2$. Thus intersecting the box hull
with the row is strictly weaker than convexifying the row domain.

This oracle solves support minimization for every vector of quadratic
expressions over the same pair domain. It does not promise to discover
every violated direction in bounded time. More general bivariate
polynomials use the Bernstein route when supported, with a potentially
large subdivision cost.

\section{What is complete and what remains outside the result}\label{sec:limits}

The supported program joins a complete domain-bound cut contract to
automatic generation, exact coupled quadratic support, a larger
structured support class, and saved comparative evidence. None of the
proofs asserts tractable exact separation for arbitrary overlapping
nonlinear blocks. Acyclic overlap alone does not make first-moment
agreement sufficient. General-function domain certification can reject
expressions near singularities, and bounded polynomial subdivision can
return an inconclusive result. These behaviors preserve soundness at
the cost of applicability or strength.

The computational standard is correspondingly limited. Fewer nodes or
a better root relaxation on a mechanism case is useful evidence for the
mechanism. It does not establish a population-wide runtime improvement.
The native sampling kernel speeds only candidate generation; validated
support and model checking retain their costs. The experiments retain
reformulation controls, unsuccessful activations, rejected domains,
timeouts, and numerical feasibility failures so that these costs are
visible.

The held-out solved count decreases: both cut modes solve 18 of the 20
admitted cases, versus 19 for baseline and control. The 24-case
selection also exposes three strict domain refusals and one
unsupported variable-exponent model, all common to the four modes.
The synthetic solved-count gain is already obtained by the
reformulation control. The recommended operational status is
therefore an optional research component for supported structures,
with default activation unsupported by this evidence. The exact
support classes, model-bound certificates, and obstruction examples
are completed results despite this adverse performance finding.

The full solver remains a numerical optimizer with native model
enforcement. Extending the cut records to certify all original-model
transformations, presolve deductions, LP bounds, spatial branching, and
primal feasibility is a different end-to-end certification task. The
present result supplies checked rows that could enter such a system.
