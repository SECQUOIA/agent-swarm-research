\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
An exact mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) proof establishes a bound for its input model. Certifying a convex mixed-integer nonlinear program (MINLP) requires two further links: the nonlinear cuts must be valid for the stated nonlinear model, and the proved MILP must be the master justified by those cuts and the original constraints. Without these links, exact discrete arithmetic can certify the wrong relaxation. The research problem addressed here is to make this complete connection explicit and replayable.

For minimization, the output of interest is a rational number $\beta$ and evidence establishing
\begin{equation}
  \beta\leq f(x)\qquad\text{for every feasible mixed-integer point }x
  \label{eq:intro-bound}
\end{equation}
for a precisely interpreted nonlinear model. Outer approximation (OA) supplies the classical optimization principle: affine underestimators of convex functions define a MILP relaxation whose lower bounds transfer to the original problem \citep{duran1986-an-outer-approximation-algorithm-for,kronqvist2019-a-review-and-comparison-of}. The certification difficulty lies in preserving that principle through expression translation, derivative evaluation, coefficient rounding, master construction, and proof checking. For example, rounding a tangent's slope without correcting its intercept can exclude a nonlinear feasible point; a proof for a different master cannot repair that error.

We develop and implement a certificate interface that joins these obligations. A common exact interpretation of the loaded expression tree governs nonlinear checks and master construction. Rigorous enclosures justify rational affine underestimators, an explicit correspondence check binds the discrete proof to the reconstructed master, and complete rational replay establishes its bound. The soundness argument constructs an objective-preserving image of every original feasible point in that master. Numerical search supplies candidate points, slopes, and proofs; acceptance depends on their checked properties rather than on numerical subproblem accuracy. The resulting artifact can be replayed without repeating the search.

\subsection{Contribution and scope}
\label{sec:contribution}
The contribution is the concrete integration of established convex-support and discrete-proof principles into a checked nonlinear-to-linear certificate interface, together with evidence about its reliability. We distinguish four parts of the work:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Method.} The interface uses one exact loaded-model interpretation, a rational cut contract for bounded, one-sided, and free coordinates, explicit master identity, and complete discrete-proof replay. We prove the composition sound: accepted evidence satisfying the stated mathematical contracts implies \eqref{eq:intro-bound}.
\item \emph{Empirical findings.} A frozen uniform experiment yields accepted artifacts for 203 of 289 attempted models in separate replay. Exact audits identify invalid supplied proof inferences that received external acceptance and infeasible saved solver points. These local witnesses distinguish failures of particular justifications from conservative rejection or mere disagreement with a reference objective.
\item \emph{Validation.} A suite of 161 targeted tests exercises executable contracts and regression cases. Lean proofs cover the mathematical contract, including propagation, curvature and cut rules, typed discrete checking, master identity, bound transfer, and primal completion. The nonlinear results require genuine derivative and enclosure data. The Python expression, interval, master-matching, and proof-checking software remains part of the trusted base.
\item \emph{Reusable artifacts.} Portable checker code, frozen inputs and outcomes, complete proof bundles, exact failure extracts, and original-model witness audits support reproduction. A descriptive catalogue collects checked bounds for 222 distinct loaded models across the recorded protocols; it is a reusable collection, not a uniform-run success rate.
\end{enumerate}
The method's significance is that a nonlinear bound is tied to its stated model by explicit checking obligations. The empirical contribution shows where those obligations change what can be justified, and the validation and artifacts make the reasoning inspectable. We establish neither a new general support-minimization principle nor a certificate-size or solver-performance advantage over prior work.

The implementation covers supported smooth convex expressions and exactly recognized quadratic and structured functions. General nonconvex MINLP, nonlinear equalities, and unsupported expression domains lie outside its contract. It certifies the exact interpretation of the loaded expression model, whose relation to the source modeling file is specified in Section~\ref{sec:model}. A lower bound becomes a certified optimality gap only when combined with an independently checked feasible upper-bound witness for that same model. We obtain matching witnesses for a self-contained quadratic example and \texttt{clay0204m}; proximity to a library reference value alone supplies no such witness.

\subsection{Relationship to prior work}
\label{sec:prior-work}
\paragraph{Mixed-integer convex optimality certificates.}
Certificates for convex MINLP are established prior work. \citet{baes2016-duality-for-mixed-integer-convex} derive finite optimality certificates and mixed-integer duality using subgradients and mixed-integer-free polyhedra. Under their respective assumptions, Theorems 1 and 3 bound the number of certificate points by $2^{|J|}$, where $|J|$ is the number of integer variables. This is a bound on the number of points, not on the bit length of their real-valued data. \citet{basu2017-optimality-certificates-for-convex-minimization} relate finite convex optimality certificates to Helly numbers for more general feasible sets.

The closest computational comparison is \citet{halbig2024-computing-optimality-certificates-for-convex}. Their generalized certificates consist of suspension points (points used to anchor the certificate planes) and hyperplanes. They give construction and reduction algorithms, structural bounds on certificate size, and experiments on MINLPLib and MIPLIB. Their verification procedure removes integrality and solves a continuous convex problem to test each certificate plane (Algorithm~3); a linear mixed-integer optimization problem tests integer-freeness of the certificate polyhedron.

Our interface checks nonlinear cut inequalities by recomputing rigorous function and derivative enclosures at supplied support points, then replays saved rational inferences for the discrete bound. It gives an outer-approximation certificate with a different checking procedure from the geometric certificates above. The certified bound may come from an interrupted master solve and need not equal a known optimum. Its soundness implication assumes valid underestimators and a valid master-bound proof, without a Slater condition or an attainment premise. This is a conditional validity result, not a completeness theorem guaranteeing such evidence for every optimum.

\paragraph{Outer approximation and numerical safeguards.}
\citet{coey2020-outer-approximation-with-conic-certificates} derive outer-approximation cuts from conic dual certificates and analyze scaling in the presence of MILP feasibility tolerances. Our interface checks affine underestimation for explicitly interpreted nonlinear expressions and exports an exact rational master proof. The approaches differ in their nonlinear representations and checking obligations.

Directed rounding and rational repair provide established safeguards for approximate numerical information. \citet{neumaier2004-safe-bounds-in-linear-and} develop safe bounds and cuts for linear and mixed-integer linear programming. \citet{eifler2024-safe-and-verified-gomory-mixed} combine safe Gomory mixed-integer cuts with rational proof verification. \citet{borst2024-certified-constraint-propagation-and-dual} treat certified propagation and dual proof analysis.

\citet{jansson2004-rigorous-convex} develops rigorous lower bounds for convex optimization, and \citet{jansson2009-verified-convex} surveys verified convex and conic computations and their use in reliable global and combinatorial optimization. \citet{messine2019-reliable-relaxation} minimize supporting affine functions over finite boxes to bound convex relaxations. Our finite-box correction applies this established calculation to the residual obtained after subtracting the proposed affine row. \citet{elloumi2025-qibex} describe QIBEX-R, which combines interval branch-and-bound for quadratic mixed-integer problems with curvature correction and rigorous relaxation bounds, drawing on this verified-bounding literature. These rigorous-bounding methods provide precedents for the nonlinear layer; our contribution joins that layer to explicit model/master binding and saved discrete-proof replay.

\paragraph{MILP proofs and checker correctness.}
\citet{cheung2017-vipr} introduce the VIPR format, whose inference steps can be checked independently of the producing MILP solver. Exact MILP solving and certificate production are available in the SCIP software family \citep{hojny2025-the-scip-optimization-suite-10}. Recent work also constructs VIPR proofs using a black-box ILP solver and rational reconstruction \citep{szeider2026-blackbox-vipr}. These methods supply discrete evidence; they do not establish the validity of nonlinear cuts supplied as input.

Proof-format semantics and correctness of a particular executable checker are separate issues. \citet{wood2026-vipr-smt} prove a logical encoding of VIPR validity correct in Why3 and implement an SMT-based checker. Their theorem verifies the logical transformation; it does not automatically verify the C++ translation program. They also identify trust-sensitive aspects of the existing checker. The CakeML project also contains a VIPR formalization, pure checker, and verified program with file and standard-input semantics theorems \citep{cakeml2026-vipr}. Our Python replay kernel is a separate, unmechanized implementation that checks complete proofs in a restricted subset of VIPR. Its acceptance contract includes binding to the input master and rejection of unsupported or malformed cases.

\paragraph{Formal nonlinear optimization and verified reductions.}
\citet{solovyev2013-formal-verification-of-nonlinear-inequalities} verify nonlinear inequalities using Taylor interval approximations in HOL Light. \citet{narkawicz2014-a-formally-verified-generic-branching} verify a generic global-optimization branching algorithm in PVS. \citet{magron2015-formal} combine semialgebraic approximations and sums-of-squares witnesses with Coq to establish nonlinear lower bounds. These methods provide important precedents for proof-producing numerical computation, with a different representation of the nonlinear and discrete reasoning.

CvxLean verifies optimization reductions in Lean \citep{bentkamp2023-verified-reductions-for-optimization}. Its reduction proofs explain why a transformed problem represents the original one; the external numerical solve is a distinct obligation. This distinction also governs our formalization: Section~\ref{sec:formalization} states the proved implications and the separate executable obligations needed to apply them to an artifact.
