## Baes and Halbig comparison

I reread Baes et al.'s local full text around Theorems 1 and 3 and rendered/visually inspected both original theorem pages. They state a certificate with `k <= 2^n` points, with `n` the number of integer variables. The constrained theorem imposes the mixed-integer Slater hypothesis and other stated setup assumptions. The introduction's “under their respective assumptions” and its explicit distinction between number of points and bit length are correct. It does not transfer their existence/completeness claim to the present interface.

Halbig et al.'s local Section 4.3 and Algorithm 3 support the manuscript's description: plane checking removes integrality and invokes a continuous convex solve, while the certificate polyhedron's integer-freeness is tested through a linear mixed-integer problem. The present paper's distinction is the replay of explicit rigorous nonlinear evidence and saved discrete derivations. It does not assert that Halbig's certificates lack mathematical validity, that independent convex-MINLP verification is new, or that its large rational proofs improve their geometric certificate-size results. The comparison is prominent and sufficiently precise for the supported contribution claim.

The sentence about soundness without Slater remains valid because it assumes valid supports/cuts and a valid master proof, while expressly refusing a general assertion that such evidence exists or certifies the exact optimum. That is a soundness-versus-completeness distinction, not an unproved improvement of Baes or Halbig.

## VIPR and formal checking

The introduction correctly credits Cheung–Gleixner–Steffy for VIPR and treats the discrete proof as inherited evidence whose nonlinear inputs still need justification. No new novelty claim has slipped into the local rule proofs or repaired checker discussion.

I refreshed the [Wood et al. v4 primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10420v4). Its stated contribution is a logically verified predicate transformation using Why3 plus an SMT checker implementation. Its discussion identifies assumptions and trust concerns in the older executable checker. The integrated comparison correctly distinguishes those logical proofs from verification of the C++ translation implementation and avoids claiming priority for the general checker risks.

The [pinned CakeML VIPR project source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CakeML/cakeml/8a32c2e8a6a3cfc7495ed13c8de9865df0c15510/examples/vipr/README.md) supports recognizing a formal checker/program precedent. The present work does not claim executable proof-checker formalization as its own achievement or infer that a benchmark passed CakeML merely because CakeML has a VIPR development.

CvxLean, HOL Light nonlinear inequalities, PVS optimization, and Coq nonlinear bounds are credited as distinct precedents. The actual present Lean contribution is consistently described as coordinate inequalities, rational finite-sum correction, selected semantic transfer/cutoff implications, and primal completion. Support/enclosure truth and concrete artifact extraction remain premises or excluded executable work. Abstract, introduction, Section 5, and discussion agree on that scope; none says the Python checker or an individual MINLP benchmark was mechanized.

## Originality and significance across the integrated text

The abstract explicitly says the contribution builds on established certificates, numerical bounds, and linear proof formats. The introduction identifies exact model interpretation, rational-cut/master/proof correspondence, selected mechanized implications, and versioned empirical evidence as the deliverables. The discussion explains their practical value without implying that elementary support inequalities or relaxation inclusion were discovered here.

The computational claims used to establish significance retain their qualification: the two exact optima use original-model witnesses, reference proximity is not optimality, local invalid inferences do not imply false final bounds, the merged 222-model catalogue is not a uniform-budget success rate, and shared-machine timings do not establish superiority. The historical repair evidence is presented as evidence about this implemented chain rather than a theorem proving universal executable correctness.

This review supports the scoped methods/software contribution, not an assurance of journal acceptance or a proof that no other project has implemented a similar combination. The mandatory Stage 6 whole-paper review remains separate from this clean Stage 5 literature verdict.
