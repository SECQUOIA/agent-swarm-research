## Mathematical and implementation correspondence

1. **Coordinate contract.** `Coordinate.contains`, `accepts`, and `correction` match the rational sufficient tests in the manuscript enclosure table. The bounded correction is exactly `max (hi*(z-L)) (lo*(z-U))`; the lower half-line uses `hi*(z-L)` with `lo >= 0`; the upper half-line uses `lo*(z-U)` with `hi <= 0`; the free case requires both enclosure endpoints zero. The theorem applies to a real residual enclosed between the rational endpoints. The signs in the upper-half-line multiplication argument are correct.

2. **Fixed, empty, and infinite cases.** `fixed_correction` proves zero when the lower, upper, and support coordinates coincide, independently of the gradient enclosure. A bounded interval with inconsistent endpoints cannot satisfy support membership. The unbounded cases use constructors and inequalities, never a real-valued infinity sentinel. The finite coordinate type in `SafeCuts.lean` has no nonemptiness requirement, so the empty-dimensional case is included without a division or a selected coordinate. The manuscript accurately excludes formalization of the supremum equality and necessity of the sign conditions.

3. **Safe-cut theorem is substantive and conditional at the intended interfaces.** `safe_affine_of_shift_bounds` assumes the support inequality, individual shift inequalities, and intercept test, then derives the desired affine underestimator by finite-sum algebra. `rational_enclosure_cut` derives the shift inequalities from the coordinate theorem and genuine real residual enclosures; it does not take the final underestimator as a premise. Its lower value enclosure bounds exactly `g(z)+q·z+c-a·z`, consistent with Section 3. All certificate coordinates and coefficients are rational while true function/support values remain real. The theorem assumes neither rational gradients nor an implemented subgradient oracle.

4. **Relation to actual interval code.** The formal result corresponds to Corollary 3.3's rational-enclosure sufficient test. The Python implementation uses direct outward interval evaluation of the support correction, as Section 3 and Section 4 explain. Section 5 correctly leaves actual interval enclosure computation, precision, rational endpoint extraction, symbolic differentiation, and domain/curvature checking outside the mechanized result. It makes no claim that compiling this Lean module proves that a particular mpmath result supplies these premises.

5. **Transfer assumptions remain visible.** `objective_preserving_transfer` takes feasible-point mapping and exact objective equality as premises. The manuscript explicitly ties these to the unmechanized extraction, propagation, serialization, and master-identity obligations. The epigraph specialization defines a master and proves graph-point membership from base inclusion and validity of all cuts over the original epigraph; it does not merely assume the final graph-membership conclusion. `epigraph_cut_of_underestimator` supplies the elementary connection from row underestimation to epigraph-cut validity. The abstraction permits arbitrary cut index sets, which only strengthens the mathematical implication and does not claim broader executable syntax support.

6. **Weak incumbent cutoff.** `incumbent_cutoff_lifting` takes an actual feasible incumbent and a bound on the no-worse-than-incumbent subset. Applying that bound to the incumbent establishes the threshold needed outside the cutoff. No original or master minimizer is assumed. This is precisely the lifting portion of Theorem 3.6; the text and coverage expressly exclude the preceding VIPR inference invariant and executable proof rules.

7. **Primal witnesses and infima.** The pointwise lower-bound definition works on empty sets. `primal_infimum_bounds` separately constructs nonemptiness of the objective image from an actual feasible witness and boundedness below from the finite global lower bound before applying conditional real-infimum results. It does not use the real infimum of an empty set as positive infinity. `matching_primal_attains` derives equality and pointwise optimality from a feasible witness whose objective is at most the lower bound. The signed-objective theorems give the correct maximization inequality.
