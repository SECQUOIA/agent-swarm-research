## Minor corrections

1. **Give the monomial recognizer its specific prior-work citation.** In `sections/04-implementation.tex`, the “Monomials and geometric means” paragraph calls these “familiar sign tests,” then states and proves them. This correctly avoids novelty, but the implementation itself identifies the Lundell–Westerlund conditions and the paper should preserve that attribution. The primary author-hosted article [Lundell and Westerlund, *Convex underestimation strategies for signomial functions*](https://users.abo.fi/twesterl/some-selected-papers/26.%20OMS-AL-TW-2009.pdf), Optimization Methods & Software 24(4–5), 505–522 (2009), DOI `10.1080/10556780802702278`, gives the same conditions in Theorems 2.1 and 2.2 on printed page 507 (PDF page 3). Add that citation at the recognition paragraph or lemma and retain the self-contained proof. The cited article itself attributes earlier results, so do not describe Lundell–Westerlund as necessarily the first discoverers. This is a citation omission, not an unsupported claim of novelty or a mathematical defect. I inspected the primary extracted PDF text; the web screenshot request subsequently timed out, so no claim of visual original-page verification is made for this new source.

2. **Make the no-forward-reference premise explicit in the inference proposition.** `prop:vipr-invariant` proves its conclusion by induction in derivation order. Its rule list should say explicitly that all inference references point to strictly earlier rows. Otherwise a reader using only the proposition can miss the well-foundedness premise; self-referential linear combinations are not justified by induction. Section 4 and `_scan`/`validate_vipr` already impose this restriction, including zero multipliers, so the implemented contract and integrated argument are correct. Add one sentence before the proposition's rules stating this requirement. No behavioral change is needed.

## Comparison with primary VIPR literature

I reopened [Wood et al., open v4, Definitions 10–12 and Section 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.10420v4). Its solution cutoff, compatible linear combinations, assumption union, rounding, and unsplit dependency formula support the attribution in Section 3. The manuscript supplies a standalone proof for its actual rules and accurately keeps the C++ implementation outside the Why3 logical theorem.

There are intentional differences: Wood's Definition 12 uses the last row for the requested dual endpoint, while this kernel can record an earlier proving row and still check every later row; the kernel also requires actual assumption rows as split references and excludes stronger integer-objective cutoffs. Sections 3–4 describe these restrictions and the earlier-row policy explicitly. The soundness theorem supports that policy; the paper should continue calling it the kernel's checking contract rather than asserting exact equivalence to Wood's validity predicate. No comparison benchmark or equivalence proof is needed for the current claim.

The work's original content remains the concrete nonlinear-to-master-to-proof integration and reliability evidence. The support inequality, monomial criteria, PSD criteria, rational arithmetic, branch inference principles, and ordinary VIPR checking remain established ingredients. The current introduction already makes that boundary clear.
