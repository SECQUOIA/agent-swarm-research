\begin{abstract}
An exact mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) proof certifies a bound only for its own input. To certify a lower bound for a convex mixed-integer nonlinear program (MINLP) by outer approximation, two further links must be checked: every nonlinear cut must be valid for the stated model, and the proved MILP must be exactly the master built from those cuts and the original constraints. Building on established convex certificates, rigorous-bounding principles, and exact MILP proofs in the solver-independent VIPR format, we develop and implement a certificate interface that checks both links. One exact interpretation of the loaded expression tree governs curvature checks, cut checks, and master construction. Rational affine cuts are justified by rigorous function and derivative enclosures, with a rational intercept correction for bounded, half-line, and free coordinates. The regenerated master is matched to the proof input, and every discrete inference is replayed in rational arithmetic. We prove that accepted evidence bounds the objective at every feasible point of the interpreted model, independently of numerical subproblem accuracy and without attainment or Slater assumptions. Lean proofs cover the mathematical contract and include reference checkers for typed discrete proofs and rational quadratic matrices; the Python implementation and the benchmark certificates are not formally verified. On 289 convex MINLPLib models, a frozen uniform protocol yields certificates that pass separate replay for 203. Exact audits find three generated proofs that an external VIPR checker accepted although they contain invalid linear-combination or integer-disjunction steps, and show two saved solver points to be infeasible.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} convex MINLP; outer approximation; lower-bound certificates; rational arithmetic; verification.
