## Minor findings

1. **Complete the LinA comparison for the relevant convex case.** At `sections/00-introduction.tex:241–246`, the statement that the general corridor model permits discontinuity is true, but it omits a directly relevant fact: Codsi et al. explicitly state that their convex-corridor algorithm produces a continuous PWL function and also solves the continuous problem (Remark 3). See [[codsi2025-lina-a-faster-approach-to]] p.10, printed p.274; the general discontinuity convention is p.5 and the logarithmic oracle count for one maximal segment is p.12. Leaving the current contrast alone can suggest that continuity separates the present scalar result from that prior algorithm, even though the closest convex case already preserves continuity. State that the general model permits discontinuity while its convex case yields continuous PWL approximants. Then make the actual distinction: comparison with arbitrary convex integer lifts and total rational encoding of an implicit partition. Specify that the logarithmic oracle count is **per maximal-segment computation**, so it cannot be read as the total complexity for a many-segment partition. [Published source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12532-024-00274-8).

2. **State the rational algorithm's interval and tolerance hypotheses in its introductory summary.** At `sections/00-introduction.tex:143–145`, the paragraph follows an existence statement for every compact interval and every positive tolerance, but the cited compiler theorem assumes a rational polynomial on `[0,1]` and a rational positive tolerance (`sections/03-scalar-nonlinear.tex:471–478`). The current wording leaves those preceding unrestricted parameters in scope. Add “on `[0,1]`, for rational positive tolerance” (or mention normalization of rational interval endpoints), and say that this data is included in the input length. This is a summary-scope correction, not a defect in the theorem as stated.

3. **Add a short comparison to the established integer-count/formulation-size tradeoff literature.** `sections/00-introduction.tex:38–47` and `201–211` explain a distinction that also motivates mixed-integer extension complexity, but currently cite only representability and binary embeddings. The directly relevant adjacent line is Cevallos, Weltge, and Zenklusen, *Lifting Linear Extension Complexity Bounds to the Mixed-Integer Setting* (SODA 2018), with a recent continuation by Schade, Sinha, and Weltge, *Lower bounds on the complexity of mixed-integer programs for stable set and knapsack* (Mathematical Programming 216, 135–176, 2026; online 2025). One or two sentences should credit that work for lower bounds trading integer count against linear formulation size for combinatorial sets, and distinguish the present accuracy-dependent nonlinear graph model and unrestricted convex lifts. This omission does not undermine the nc-rank priority claim, but the comparison would close a conspicuous adjacent literature gap in a paper emphasizing both integer dimension and description cost. Primary sources: [Cevallos et al., arXiv:1712.02176](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.02176); [Schade et al., Section 1 and Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-025-02234-z). I read the former's primary abstract and the latter's introduction/model/theorem statements; no claim here depends on their proof details.

4. **Use the exact published title for the IQS reference.** The newly converted journal entry `references.bib`, key `iqs2018`, drops the hyphen in “non-commutative.” The published title is *Constructive non-commutative rank computation is in deterministic polynomial time*. Preserve that title. The supplied authors, volume 27, pages 561–593, year 2018, and DOI agree with the [publisher record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00037-018-0165-7).
