# Stage 3 primary-source audit

This records passages read for the scalar author pass, not a claim to have audited every argument in every cited source. Existing local originals and their extracted text supplied the main evidence. Online searches checked current publication metadata and screened the closest approximation/representation terminology; absence from those searches is not proof of priority.

## Approximation and compilation

- **Codsi, Ngueveu and Gendron, LinA (2025).** Local published original and `literature/papers/codsi2025-lina-a-faster-approach-to/fulltext.md`: p.7–8 (printed 271–272), Proposition 1 and Corollary 1, establish greedy maximal segmentation in the corridor model; p.10 (274), Remark 3, states continuity for convex corridors; p.11–12 (275–276), Section 4.1, supplies dichotomy and the logarithmic number of function/derivative oracle calls for one maximal segment; p.17–18 (281–282), Lemma 4, bounds splitting overhead by the number of split points. Their assumptions in Section 4.1 include continuously differentiable convex corridor boundaries. These passages support the revised local comparison; continuity is explicitly not claimed as new. The old 2021 technical-report reference is no longer needed. [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12532-024-00274-8).
- **Simchowitz et al. (2018), arXiv:1808.04523v3.** Cached full text `build/source-cache/simchowitz2018.txt`, manuscript pp.3–4, Lemma 2.1 and Definition 1/equations (3)–(5): maximum chord error is within a factor of two of the midpoint gap; local approximation moduli have explicit endpoint corrections. The source concerns adaptive sampling and regression. It supports the credited chord estimate and adjacent accuracy-dependent measure, not the arbitrary-convex-lift count comparison. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.04523v3).
- **Avis et al. (2019).** Local original/extracted text under `literature/papers/avis2019-polynomial-size-linear-programs-for/`, Section 3, Lemma 1: an acyclic Boolean circuit has a linear description with a unique Boolean extension for fixed Boolean inputs. Section 4 explains bounded pseudocode conversion. This is the established principle behind continuous internal wires; it is not a claim that arbitrary nonlinear computation is linear without discrete inputs. [Source version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.0807).
- **Adams and Henry (2012).** Cached original `build/source-cache/adams2012.pdf` and text, author manuscript Section 2, pp.3–7: logarithmic discrete-function encoding and scaling with a nonnegative continuous weight. The publication metadata were independently checked at the publisher: *Operations Research* 60(6), 1477–1490, DOI 10.1287/opre.1120.1106. The bibliography now cites the journal but explicitly identifies the linked SAND2012-0505P manuscript as the Section 2 locator basis. [Publisher](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.1120.1106), [inspected manuscript](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1648449).
- **Sagraloff and Mehlhorn (2016).** Cached full original `build/source-cache/sagraloff2015.pdf`, arXiv:1308.4088v2; Theorem 36, manuscript p.41, states polynomial bit complexity for isolating/refining square-free integer-polynomial roots to a requested number of bits. Square-free preprocessing is necessary for repeated roots and is stated in our text. Publisher metadata identify *Journal of Symbolic Computation* 73, 46–86 (2016), DOI 10.1016/j.jsc.2015.03.004. The bibliography and in-text locator explicitly preserve Theorem 36 of the linked preprint; no claim of identical journal numbering is made. [Publisher metadata](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747717115000292), [inspected version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4088v2).
- **DLMF Section 3.5(v).** The current NIST page was opened; equations (3.5.18)–(3.5.20) and the accompanying text give positive Gaussian weights and polynomial exactness. Our proof additionally derives the conservative Legendre weight conditioning and rational evaluation bounds it needs. [Primary reference](https://dlmf.nist.gov/3.5#v).

## Positive powers and encoding boundaries

- **Teles, Castro and Matos (2013).** Local original/extracted text, `literature/papers/teles2013-multi-parametric-disaggregation-technique-for/`, p.6–8 (printed 232–234), Section 3, Remarks 1–4: decimal power disaggregation, discrete/continuous product linearization, recursive monomial construction, and truncation. This supports the explicit credit for radix and polynomial MILP mechanisms. No claim is made that this earlier numerical approximation already compares against unrestricted convex integer lifts.
- **Bonito and Pasciak (2015).** Local author manuscript under `literature/papers/bonito2015-numerical-approximation-of-fractional-powers/`, p.14–15, Section 3.3, equation (37) and Lemma 3.4; cached `build/source-cache/bonito2015.txt` supplies legible equations. The positive resolvent quadrature approximates inverse powers uniformly for the positive spectral parameter bounded away from zero. Substituting its parameter as 1/t yields the cited positive t/(t+b) terms. Our rational coefficients, endpoint normalization and uniform D-dependent bit bounds are proved locally; positivity/resolvent approximation itself is established. [Linked manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.0888).
- **Boyd and Vandenberghe (2004).** Original author-hosted book, Section 5.9, printed pp.264–267, gives dual-cone weak duality, Slater strong duality and attained-optimum optimality conditions. The manuscript's homogeneous value gadget is directly proved, including zero weight, and its repeated-square instance has an explicit certificate. [Book](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/bv_cvxbook.pdf).
- **O'Donnell (2017).** Local original/extracted text, Section 2, Theorem 1, p.6 (59:6): an explicit degree-two SOS example requires exponentially long approximate certificates; the following discussion distinguishes exact feasibility from very small constraint violations. This supports the surrounding attribution of long conic/SOS solution encodings, not the specific root-graph MILP–MISOCP separation. [Published source](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2017.59).
- **Wang (2024).** Local original/extracted text, Section 4, Theorem 18 and Corollary 21, gives short simple second-order-cone descriptions of rational weighted geometric means through mediated sequences. This is credited as established conic power-representation theory. Our fixed-error graph containment and comparison with ordinary explicit rational MILP encoding are separately proved. [Source version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05924).

The scalar section adds no global priority assertion. Its contribution statements identify the proved count comparisons and rational compilation/encoding results, while crediting chord estimates, greedy segmentation, circuit linearization, radix methods and conic power lifts. The manuscript's qualified global nc-rank claim is outside this stage and was not strengthened.
