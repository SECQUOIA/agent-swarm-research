This audit is independent of the stage author and predates the fifteen-reviewer gate. It records checks, not a general certification of the manuscript. No existing research or literature file was changed.

## Primary-source imports checked

PDFs and extracted text are in `build/source-cache/`; retrieval URLs and SHA-256 hashes are recorded in `stage2-primary-retrievals.json`.

- Zhang and Sra (2016), Corollary 8 and the proof of Theorem 9, printed pp.8–9: the projected one-step comparison uses the distance to the comparison point in its curvature factor. Its summed inequality bounds the mean gap and therefore the best iterate, independently of the theorem's final averaged-iterate statement. Combining with metric projection and local rounding gives the manuscript's intended inexact recurrence; it does not require tracking one exact numerical trajectory.
- Criscitiello and Boumal, Appendix I, printed pp.70–71: the metric is exactly `tr(P^-1 X P^-1 Y)` and the manifold is Hadamard; Proposition I.1 supplies the sectional curvature lower bound -1/2. The weaker -1 bound used in the construction is valid. Dimension one is flat and satisfies the same weaker bound.
- Ivanyos, Qiao and Subrahmanyam (2018), Theorem 1.5, printed p.6 of the cached version: output includes a maximal shrunk base-field subspace. The rational case explicitly controls intermediate and final bit sizes; an arithmetic-operation bound alone would be insufficient.
- Grötschel, Lovász and Schrijver (1981), Definition (5), printed p.172, and Theorem (3.1), p.177: the weak-optimization guarantee gives both Euclidean distance at most the requested tolerance and objective loss at most that tolerance against all points of the original body. It is not merely a comparison with an eroded body. The definition of weak separation has normal norm **at least** one; root visually inspected PDF p.172 to resolve the ambiguous OCR inequality. Exact strong normals normalized by their nonzero infinity norm satisfy it. Known inner and outer balls and polynomial query/output encodings are required.
- Dadush, Peikert and Vempala, Definition B.2 and Theorem B.5, printed pp.37–38: the strong separator is rational with polynomial output height. GLS-Round is deterministic and returns a rational positive definite ellipsoid matrix and a potentially real center, with outer containment and either small volume or an inner copy scaled by `(d+1)sqrt(d)`. The given lower-volume threshold rules out the small-volume branch. Symmetry removes the center from both inclusions; no rational computation of the center is needed.
- Del Pia (2026) was retrieved as an adjacent explicit rational spectral reference. Its near-diagonalization statement is an established ingredient; it is not the whole-graph formulation theorem. Its relevance should be described narrowly, with complete paper metadata checked by the author.

- Checked the effective nonlinear output image equality by affine intersection in the reverse direction, including formulations whose errors initially leave that image. Checked pullback inner/outer radii and nonzero pulled-back separating normals.

- Checked PSD block traces, block determinant inequality, and unconditional downward closure of the expected Jensen vector. Domain independence is essential for the blockwise quotient.
