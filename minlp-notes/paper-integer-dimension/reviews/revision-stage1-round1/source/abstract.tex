\begin{abstract}
We study the minimum number of integer variables in a convex lift that
contains a nonlinear graph and admits only a prescribed vertical error.
The competing lifts may have unrestricted continuous dimension, real
coefficients and unbounded integer variables. For a fixed quadratic
system on a full-dimensional box, we prove that both this minimum and
the minimum binary linear dimension equal one half of the noncommutative
rank of the Hessian space times $\log_2(1/\varepsilon)$, up to an additive
constant. At finite accuracy, a covariance determinant characterizes the
count within $O(n\log(n+1))$, with rational polynomial-time constructions,
certificates and sharper bounds for positive structure. For continuous
convex scalar graphs, binary linear dimension is within two of the general
convex integer minimum. Certified curvature integration and implicit knot
compilation give rational polynomial-size analogues, including dense convex
polynomials, positive sparse polynomials and binary-encoded powers.
For coupled convex outputs, shared curvature bases yield logarithmic-rank
overheads, including separable inputs and unconditional oracle error bodies.
Explicit examples show limits of scalarization, exact binary/general-integer
separations, and a fixed-error exponential separation between rational
MILP and second-order-cone encoding lengths. Throughout, integer count,
continuous size, coefficient encoding and construction time are distinguished.
\end{abstract}
