# Independent review protocol

Each stage is reviewed by fifteen agents after its author finishes. Reviewers read the entire completed stage and use different primary lenses to reduce correlated blind spots. They may inspect original results, audits, verification scripts, and primary literature. They must independently assess the reasoning rather than accept an existing audit label.

Review reports name the reviewed files and SHA-256 hashes, state their coverage and limitations, and list concrete findings with locations, reasoning, severity, and a proposed correction where possible. An unproved nontrivial step supporting a main result, a false claim, a missing material hypothesis, or a materially misleading scope/priority claim is major. Minor findings affect clarity, local justification, terminology, or presentation without overturning the stated contribution. Speculative concerns without an identified failure are questions, not automatically major findings.

Reviewers do not edit the manuscript. The root agent adjudicates each finding and records reasons for accepting, modifying, or rejecting it. A separate correction agent handles accepted findings. Any accepted major issue triggers another fifteen-reviewer round on the corrected stage. No next mathematical stage begins before the gate passes. Whole-paper review uses the same process and also examines consistency, completeness, dependencies, bibliography, and exposition across stages.

A passed gate means no unresolved accepted major issue remains in the material reviewed. It does not imply external peer review, formal verification, or guaranteed correctness. The final whole-paper gate also resolves accepted minor issues and records the disposition of every report.
