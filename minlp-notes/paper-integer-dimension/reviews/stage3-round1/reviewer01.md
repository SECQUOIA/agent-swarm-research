## Independent reconstruction

- **Scalar geometry, lines 1–72.** The three-piece refinement is valid for continuous convex functions, including nonsmooth ones. The superlevel interval is strictly interior when the gap exceeds the threshold. On each exterior piece the replacement gap is bounded by the old gap; on the central piece it is exactly the old gap minus the threshold. Compact parity contacts have attained span endpoints, so their midpoint inequalities pass to the span endpoints. Concavity bounds the maximum span gap by twice its midpoint gap. A finite closed interval cover can be trimmed greedily: at every unfinished right endpoint, finiteness and coverage guarantee a member that extends beyond it, so the procedure advances and uses each chosen member at most once. Singleton members do not invalidate this argument.
- **Maximal packings and separable transfer, lines 52–63 and 557–643.** Uniform continuity gives a uniform positive distance below which every pair is compatible and hence bounds every incompatible set's cardinality. Greedy addition therefore terminates in a finite maximal set. Interval enlargement increases the midpoint Jensen gap; this follows directly for nonnegative hinge sums and passes through uniform convex polygonal approximation. Thus each compatibility set is a closed interval. Splitting those intervals at the packing points, trimming, and refining gives the factors `2P` and `6P`. Tent kernels give Jensen superadditivity even for continuous convex summands. The product code deletes at most `6^r` index points per selected point; its construction is used only in the lower-bound existence argument. I recomputed the capacities `12P`, `481P`, and `5832P` and the stated independent-output and scalar-sum overheads.
- **Curvature and counterexamples, lines 74–181.** I checked the split at `(b-t)A(t)=1`, the estimate `E <= m^2 + 3m/2`, both cases of the potential inequality, its terminal sign, and the factors 24, 48, and 49. The degree-gap curvature estimate and geometric point separation give the claimed incompatible points. The fixed-tolerance raw-curvature example changes the function with the degree and makes no false fixed-function asymptotic claim.
- **Certified integration and compilation, lines 183–466.** I checked conditional integrality of all circuit wires, the common-denominator requirement, signed decoding, and exact interpolation using existing index bits. The positive-coefficient sector argument and the signed Taylor certificate both give the required complex disk. The failed-panel/root-proximity argument controls active branches per depth; the discriminant bound controls depth in bit length, not the reciprocal root separation. Cutting accepted panels preserves their disks. Gaussian positivity and exactness yield the explicit Taylor-tail bound; individual node/weight refinements avoid a joint algebraic field. The normalized inverse modulus handles small density. The final compiler only needs mass accuracy, and its uncertain comparisons, targets above total mass, forced endpoints, reversed knots, duplicate knots, and zero-bit case all retain the stated error and coverage. I recomputed `81529/102400 < 13/16` and the final `15/16` band error.
- **Dense convex hybrid, lines 468–555.** Rounding a fine partition down to the grid remains valid after deleting repeats: at each change of rounded value, the last knot in the preceding group and first knot in the next group are adjacent original knots. Their expanded interval contains the new cell. Restriction gives greedy optimality. Failure after `9D` cells proves `N_eta > D`, absorbing the curvature-piece count. Splitting the optimal partition gives the needed sum of local counts, and one global index encodes their sum. Rational affine remapping and a polynomial-length common denominator preserve exact endpoints. The resulting count is below `1458 * 2^p`, giving the eleven-bit overhead.
- **Positive systems, lines 645–939.** I checked the baseline power transform and expected Jensen domination under unconditionality. Supporting scalarization preserves the allocation optimum, including zero multipliers and coordinates with zero scalarized weight. The coordinate homeomorphism is used to cover the transformed unit cube; volume preservation is never assumed. The dyadic endpoint layers uniformly bound normalized monomial curvature. Both the dense exact shared-power recurrences and sparse rounded-power evaluator use only the declared layer/prefix bits. The output bands contain the graph and keep every admitted error inside a coordinatewise dominated feasible budget.
- **Pure and rational powers, lines 941–1328.** I checked the degree-independent chord estimate, the scale `min(1, alpha-1)` for exponents between one and two, and the transformed covariance constants. The Stieltjes proof accounts for both tails, uniformly bounded complex panels, positive coefficient rounding, exact normalization, and denominator bit lengths. The general `P/Q` recurrence has the supplied positive denominator bound needed to force its variables; clipping the input retains coverage of a nonmonotone path. Integer inverse-power bisection terminates safely at uncertain comparisons. Rational log/exp series use polynomially many bits, including when the exponent is close to one. Dense numerical-degree complexity and binary exponent complexity are kept separate throughout.
- **Relative error and encoding, lines 1330–1564.** Residues modulo an arbitrary integer establish the convex-power obstruction, the concave threshold, and the truncated-domain lower estimate. The rectangle upper bound gives two-sided relative error. The rational MILP lower bound freezes an unrestricted integer witness only in the right-hand side; the basis determinant depends on original row coefficients, whose total bit budget yields the linear-in-`D` lower bound. Free-variable splitting supplies an optimal basic feasible solution even if the original fiber has lineality. I checked the matching four-bit upper. In the conic value gadget, original feasible primal and dual witnesses force zero value at zero weight. The explicit squaring-chain dual vectors satisfy rotated-cone membership and telescope to the asserted optimum. Exact conic encoding is not presented as a polynomial-time or tolerance-stable solution algorithm.
