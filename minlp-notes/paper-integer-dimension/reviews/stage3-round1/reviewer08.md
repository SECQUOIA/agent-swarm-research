The allocation proof received additional scrutiny. In `eq:allocation-supporting-normal`, a sufficiently small positive allocation is strictly feasible for the body and coordinate caps, so the stated normal-cone decomposition applies. At a positive coordinate of `Cp*`, decreasing that coordinate stays in the unconditional body and forces the corresponding normal component nonnegative. At a zero coordinate, positivity of every `p_i*` and nonnegativity of `C` imply that the whole row is zero. Deleting that normal component preserves support equality: the unconditional support function decreases under decreasing absolute coordinates, while its value at `Cp*` stays fixed. This also leaves `C^T lambda` unchanged. Thus the use of nonnegative scalarization coefficients is justified, including zero rows.

The capped scalar allocation has the same optimum as the original allocation: the tangent inequality for the log product and the cap complementarity terms give the displayed comparison for every positive scalar-feasible allocation. Zero products cannot improve it. If `lambda=0`, every coordinate is capped at one and the lower bound is trivial; if only some `a_i` vanish, those coordinates likewise have optimal allocation one and can use the identity input map. Nonzero `lambda` has positive support value because the body contains a ball about zero. The square root of each normalized positive mixture is a convex increasing homeomorphism by convexity and coordinatewise monotonicity of the Euclidean norm on the nonnegative orthant. The transformed supports cover a unit cube, and the proof takes uniform covariance in that cube; it does not silently assume preservation of original-coordinate volume. The factors `p_i=Sigma_ii/2`, `2^r D_K` and the final dimension constants are consistent.

The baseline construction also retains its distinct content. Its power transform gives `p_i=2 Sigma_ii/D_i^2`; the direct original-axis prefix recurrences compute exact powers with no new integers and produce the stated Taylor rectangle. In the stronger construction, the normalized layer curvature is uniformly at most two, the final layer has width at most `1/D_i`, and the zero-prefix-bit case is explicitly handled. Matching layer selectors are forced Boolean at integral external codes. Their products therefore use exact binary-product hulls legitimately. The dense recurrence count and sparse rounded-endpoint count use the same declared layer/cell bits but different arithmetic; the sparse claim is polynomial in exponent bit lengths. Both graph containment and every admitted output error follow from explicit rational rectangles and unconditional domination, without representing a curved body by finitely many exact linear inequalities.

For the oracle import, I reread accepted `lem:block-logdet-oracle` and the scalar source `notes/rational-log-product-convex-body-oracle.md`. The accepted proof gives the explicit hypograph inner and outer balls, weak logarithmic separators, positive coordinate caps, and exact rational central-ball repair. Its corrected estimate controls objective change, not gradient variation. The repair is a convex combination of a point of the hypograph and a point of its known interior ball, so it genuinely restores exact feasibility. Its objective loss is at most `nu`; taking `nu=1` costs only `1/(2 ln 2)` in the allocation count. Nonnegative `C` and unconditionality are required for stage3 graph geometry, not for this oracle. The inherited distinction is stated accurately.
