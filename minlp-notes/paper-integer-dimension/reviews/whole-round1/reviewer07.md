## Extra scalar-curvature checks

1. **Chord refinement and maximal packing, lines 11–72.** On an error-`2 eta` chord, the two endpoints of the `eta` superlevel interval give at most three valid restrictions, including plateau cases. A contact span has chord error at most twice its midpoint gap. The finite cover can be trimmed to a partition. Compatibility intervals around a finite maximal packing split at the selected points, giving `N_(2 eta) <= 2P` and `N_eta <= 6P`. These arguments need continuity and convexity, not differentiability or a computed packing.

2. **Truncated mass, lines 76–145.** I independently reproduced `E <= m^2 + 3m/2` by splitting where `(b-t)A(t)` is below one. On the other set monotonicity gives `m >= (b-t)A(t)-1/2`. For the reverse direction the midpoint tent gives `E <= 8` on an admissible interval. The potential inequality is valid in both `b-a <= 1-b` and `b-a > 1-b` cases and includes `b=1`; summing cancels the potential with a favorable endpoint sign. Scaling the density by two and using the chord bound gives the constant 49 in the dimension lower bound. Zero mass is treated as affine.

3. **Different curvature obstructions, lines 147–181.** The disjoint monomial layers establish the raw-curvature lower bound at fixed tolerance. The different tolerance `1/(512M)` supplies the allocation gap. The manuscript does not exchange these quantifiers or claim that a degree penalty is necessary relative to the true optimum.

4. **Positive and signed integration, lines 235–390.** The branch polynomial is nonzero at one. Cutoff and root-neighborhood omissions have explicitly bounded mass. Positive coefficients give analytic sectors; signed coefficients instead use the exact Taylor test. Every failed panel is close to a complex root, so there are only `O(d^2)` failures per depth. Square-free height and discriminant separation bound the depth polynomially; they are not used to enumerate an exponentially fine uniform mesh. Intersected panels keep their certified disks. Gaussian positivity and polynomial exactness bound the analytic error, and the individual node/weight bounds permit rational approximation without a joint algebraic field. Absolute square-root accuracy avoids an unjustified global positive lower bound. The separate inverse modulus uses the positive rational value `H(delta/4)` and therefore remains polynomial even with signed-coefficient cancellation.

5. **Mass knots and graph bands, lines 394–458.** Targets can lie above the true total mass, but by at most `2 zeta`; the endpoint-adjacent bisection case accounts for this. The common mass certificate implies consecutive-cell mass at most `133/320`, including reversed knots. Its Taylor bound is exactly `81529/102400 < 13/16`. Downward endpoint error `eta/8` and the displayed asymmetric band yield exact graph containment and admitted error `15 eta/16`. The count is computed from the input integral estimate and does not require access to the comparator lift.

6. **Hybrid compiler, lines 470–548.** Rounded optimal knots remain inside expanded original intervals after duplicates are discarded. Greedy maximal extension minimizes the count on the implicit grid because feasibility is hereditary under restriction. Failure to finish in `9D` cells proves `N_eta > D`, which pays for curvature splitting. Rational root brackets and monotone complements give `b <= 3D`; splitting a global optimal partition gives `sum n_j <= N_eta+b-1`. Consequently the local capacities sum to less than `486 N_eta`, giving the eleven-bit comparison. Piece normalization, reflection, common denominators, and one global index preserve polynomial encoding and exact coverage.
