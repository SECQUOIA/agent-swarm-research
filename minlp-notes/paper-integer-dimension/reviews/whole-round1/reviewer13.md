## Full-paper proof assessment

**Foundations and precision laws — `sections/01-foundations.tex`.** I checked that the model allows nonclosed convex lifts, unrestricted finite continuous dimension, real coefficients, and unbounded general integer coordinates. The lower-bound arguments use compact closures of projected contact sets and continuity of the graph/error condition rather than assuming measurable witness selections or closed lifts. The finite-union constructions account for zero-weight recession behavior. Square, product, fractional-cover and scalar-quadratic arguments have the needed separate endpoint, rank-zero, and one-sided interpretations. The shared discretization does not charge auxiliary continuous Boolean wires as integers.

For the noncommutative-rank law I reconstructed the principal compression, symmetric shrunk-subspace dimension identities, covariance fourth-moment estimate, and capacity/determinant lower bound. The real-field descent and the distinction between joint noncommutative rank and maximum scalar rank are maintained. The cross-product example gives coefficient three, although the scalar half-rank is at most two. The smooth-map argument uses a local nonsingular Hessian block for the oscillatory lower bound and fixed-coordinate Taylor bands for its upper bound. Constant-rank scalar charts and positive perspectives have the additional structure their exact rates require. The text does not extend these exact statements to rank-changing smooth boundaries or unrestricted mixed-coordinate situations.

**Finite quadratic theory — `sections/02-quadratic-finite.tex`.** I checked the covariance lower bound and common-grid upper bound with their distinct dimension losses, including the quadratic-system normalization. The finite covariance benchmark is not asserted to equal the minimum. The residual certificate gives a sufficient, quantitatively controlled certificate, with rational Hessians and tolerances explicitly supplied. The rational algorithm separately handles positive-definite optimization, inexact matrix operations, branch selection, feasibility repair, diagonalization, and grid encoding; this avoids identifying a real-arithmetic optimizer with a short rational formulation. The determinant and input-box denominator bounds have their required encoding dependence.

I also checked the PSD and correlation-body arguments, effective input and output images, quotient-domain geometry, block/feature/forest refinements, and the correlated-domain counterexample. Diagonalization does not authorize replacing a correlated domain by independent coordinate ranges. The hardness reduction distinguishes zero recognition from positive-optimum additive approximation, and establishes the stated fixed sublinear-power barrier without claiming a sharper near-linear barrier.

**Scalar and separable nonlinear theory — `sections/03-scalar-nonlinear.tex`.** I reconstructed the three-piece chord comparison, truncated-curvature estimates, certified integration and knot placement, hybrid greedy/integration switch, and indexed rational compiler. Exact containment and admitted-band errors are checked separately. The common denominator and circuit claims account for bit lengths, not only the number of interpolation knots. The separable packing/allocation estimates require the positivity and domain-product hypotheses actually stated.

The positive polynomial, sparse exponent, pure-power and rational approximation results retain the distinction between numerical degree and binary-encoded exponent. The inverse-power construction is for exponents greater than one; it is not silently substituted for the small-exponent encoding example. Relative-error behavior at zero and the threshold for subunit powers are treated separately. For the MILP/MISOCP separation I checked the fixed-binary-section LP denominator argument, the four-bit knot construction, and the conic primal/dual equality certificate, including its zero-weight case. ## Separation and open-boundary checks

1. **Positive separated scales, cap sets, and three witnesses — the portion preceding `thm:exact-box-gap` in section 4.** I checked the scale separation and the strict thirds-contact violation for the positive power witnesses. Each scalarization can have its own one-bit partition without giving a common one-bit partition for the vector. The cap-set reduction requires distinct residues and a forbidden zero-sum triple; it does not infer that pairwise midpoint incompatibility alone is a cap-set obstruction. The minimizer of `(1+t+t^2)/t^(2/3)` satisfies `4t^2+t-2=0`, giving the stated constant. The stronger one-integer three-witness exclusion uses convexification inside an integer section and then another convex combination. It is not merely a drawing of segments between three chosen labels. Neither argument alone establishes a growing additive binary/general-integer gap for the open one-input convex box class.

2. **Exact convex product counts — `sections/04-vector.tex:885`, `thm:exact-box-gap`.** I checked both lower bounds and both constructions. The three contacts in each coordinate force distinct binary codes, while the modulus-three product argument forces at least `n` general integers. The middle integer section of the proposed lift contains all mixtures with weights `(t,1-2t,t)`; checking only its generating midpoint would be insufficient. The manuscript bounds the entire section. For the stated constants, the middle interval and output bounds fit the unit error tube and include the exact graph. Cartesian products preserve these properties. Thus the counts are `n` and `ceil(n log_2 3)`. This family increases input dimension and does not settle the fixed one-input question. The finite upper construction is not claimed to have polynomial continuous size.

3. **Hinge/Bernstein supporting development — following the exact product construction.** The separate hinge construction and its Bernstein perturbation retain a strict error margin. Convexity is preserved by the second-difference condition, and perturbing the graph and thickening its lift consumes less than the available slack. This development is retained rather than conflated with the different fixed-degree polynomial construction.

4. **Nonconvex scalar polynomial gap — `sections/04-vector.tex:1000`, `thm:nonconvex-gap`.** I checked the repeated triangular graph, its period/orientation integer encoding, the uniform Bernstein error, and the peak/trough binary obstruction. The construction proves an upper bound of two general integers; the theorem does not require an unproved equality. Degree is bounded below through alternating roots and above through the Bernstein degree. These estimates justify logarithmic degree dependence as a worst-case phenomenon. The later arbitrary-polynomial overlay explicitly brackets curvature zeros, uses convex or concave pieces with the appropriate band direction, and pays for numerical degree. It does not inherit convexity from the nonconvex example.

5. **Tilted bodies — `sections/04-vector.tex:1142`, `prop:tilted-gap`.** Adding the common sufficiently curved quadratic makes both outputs convex. The tilted body's difference constraint still sees the original scalar oscillation. Its second-coordinate width permits exact graph containment in the proposed lift. The peak second-difference estimate forces the convexifying coefficient, and hence the relevant conditioning ratio, to grow with the number of oscillations. This is neither a fixed-condition example nor an unconditional-body example. The introduction and conclusion preserve those limits.

6. **Conditioning — `sections/04-vector.tex:1194`, `prop:conditioning`.** The outer/inner-radius comparisons are used at the correct tolerances. The simplex comparison uses the nonnegativity of both vectors to improve the Euclidean bound. For unconditional bodies, coordinate normalization gives the stated dimension-dependent inclusion between the cross-polytope and cube. These positive comparisons are compatible with the preceding tilted counterexample.

7. **Remaining one-input box question — `sections/04-vector.tex:1258`.** The individual strict component upper-violation sets are convex and have integer-free projections, but their union need not retain that property under convexification. An independent model check illustrates the issue: take the constant graph `F=(0,0)` on `[0,1]`, unit box error, and the convex lift `-1/2 <= z <= 1/2`, `w=(-4z,4z)`, with unrestricted `x` in the interval. Its only integer section is `z=0`, exactly the graph. The two upper-violation projections are `[-1/2,-1/4)` and `(1/4,1/2]`; each is integer-free but their joint convex hull contains zero. Thus the manuscript's warning concerns a real obstruction in the allowed model. The cited mixed-integer Helly number still depends on unrestricted continuous dimension and concerns infeasibility certificates, not a direct graph-cover bound. An integral Radon argument needs an integer intersection of disjoint hulls and compatible graph violation; parity alone supplies neither. The manuscript identifies these limitations without declaring the methods impossible or the question resolved.
