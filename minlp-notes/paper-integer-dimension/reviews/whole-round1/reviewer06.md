The additional smooth/perspective/hardness scrutiny gave the following conclusions. References here identify checked arguments, not findings.

In `01-foundations.tex`, `lem:oscillatory` and `lem:smooth-volume`, the small product neighborhood makes the mixed Hessian uniformly close to one fixed invertible matrix. Integrating that matrix along the segment gives a lower bound proportional to `|X-Z|` for the phase-difference gradient. Higher derivatives retain that same factor. Repeated integration by parts therefore gives the required integrable kernel bound, and Schur's test gives the half-dimension operator exponent. The proof explicitly uses smooth cutoffs and permits indicator functions by continuity in `L^2`; the contact sets need no regular boundary, convexity or bounded aspect ratio.

The matrix-evaluation witness in `lem:smooth-volume` can be chosen real because its determinant is a nonzero polynomial with real coefficients. The repeated-point mixed Hessian is exactly minus one half of the evaluated Hessian pencil. On the Cartesian contact powers the phase is uniformly small, so its real part cannot cancel the volume lower bound. Applying the operator norm to the two indicators gives a factor `v^d`, while integration over both contact powers gives `v^(2d)`. Taking the `d`th root cancels the evaluation dimension from the accuracy exponent. Principal compression then supplies the partial-rank affine slice in `thm:smooth-ranks`. Constants depend on the fixed map and neighborhood, not on the chosen contacts or accuracy.

The global smooth upper uses the symmetric shrinking decomposition of the entire Hessian span, not a separate basis at each input. All nonzero Hessian entries have endpoint precision weights summing to at least one. Taylor segments remain in the transformed original box, including coordinates with zero precision weight. For polynomial outputs, forbidden entries vanish identically, which justifies Taylor expansion from a prefix point in the enclosing box. Products contain only existing bits and at most one continuous residual. The fixed-degree expansion count is valid and is not a polynomial-in-binary-degree claim.

In `thm:constant-rank`, constant rank holds on a neighborhood of the whole box. The invertible principal Hessian block supplies the partial Legendre chart, and its zero Schur complement gives `g(p,v)=b(p)^T v+c(p)`. This is a differential coordinate calculation and does not require convexity of the scalar function. Each fixed-gradient fiber is affine in the original coordinates. Its thickening is therefore a bounded polytope. The affine approximation agrees with both function and gradient along the central fiber, so the quadratic remainder bound holds on the entire tube, including points whose gradient parameter lies outside the selected parameter cell. Compactness supplies finitely many charts covering boundary points as well. This proves the real finite-union upper count; it does not supply a short rational formulation for arbitrary smooth data. The varying null line of the norm and the rational quadratic-over-linear example correctly demonstrate that the global-span bound can be strict.

For `prop:perspective`, dividing every homogenized row by positive `t` recovers the original binary formulation exactly. The four product inequalities force `v'_i=t z_i` for binary `z_i`, including `ell=u`. Continuous auxiliaries need no numerical bounds: their scaling is absorbed in the rows. The exact graph is retained, all admitted errors scale by at most `u`, and a fixed positive-`t` slice gives the lower transfer for both models. The transformed perspective domain is convex and compact under the standing assumptions; quadratic box domains give the explicit bounded polytope stated. The ordinary positive-denominator box version retains its requested input constraints. No upper transfer for arbitrary unbounded integer products or the cone apex is claimed.

The arithmetic-circuit identity-testing observation in `01-foundations.tex` has a gap of exactly one between its two leading coefficients. At a nonzero interior value of the input polynomial, the indicated scalar Hessian is nonsingular. Thus an approximation error strictly below one half separates the cases. The proof only reduces identity testing to coefficient computation; it neither assumes deterministic hardness of identity testing nor applies that obstacle to dense coefficient input.

In `02-quadratic-finite.tex`, `lem:zero-count-maxcut` and `thm:count-hardness`, the graph quadratic reaches its maximum at a Boolean cut and vanishes at the cube center. The complementary maximizing vertices force the exact zero-count equivalence. Replication produces `2^t` pairwise midpoint-incompatible graph points, so arbitrary integer witnesses require distinct parity classes. The appended `8z^2` coordinate gives low-case optimum exactly one and high-case lower bound `t+1`; its one-binary upper band contains the whole graph. Dividing the graph outputs by `k-1/2` fixes every tolerance at one with polynomial encoding. For fixed `delta>0`, choosing a fixed integer `q>1/delta` and `t=n^q` makes the approximation allowance smaller than the packing gap for sufficiently large input size. The reduction remains polynomial in the original graph encoding. The coNP-completeness statement is restricted to the graph-quadratic family, and the theorem excludes fixed sublinear powers without excluding `N/log N`. Construction complexity is correctly separated from solving the resulting formulation.
