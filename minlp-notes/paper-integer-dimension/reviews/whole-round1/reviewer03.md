## Extra-depth algebraic and rational audit

The principal compression and symmetric shrinking arguments occur at `sections/01-foundations.tex:597` and `:621`; the covariance lemma and nc-rank theorem begin at `:672` and `:744`. The uniform rational theorem begins at `sections/02-quadratic-finite.tex:245`.

1. **Free-field and real-field issues.** Hermitian elimination uses either a nonzero diagonal pivot or an invertible off-diagonal two-by-two principal block. The Schur complement remains Hermitian over the free skew field, so the induction proves the required principal compression. The involution fixing the free variables and conjugating scalar coefficients is an actual imported structure, not an assumption that noncommutative matrix entries commute. I checked the corresponding setup in [Volčič, *Hilbert's 17th problem in free skew fields*](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.02314). The real descent argument uses supermodularity of the deficiency function and conjugation; it does not merely assume that a complex optimal shrinking subspace has a real basis.
2. **Symmetric shrinking and weighted depths.** For maximal-deficiency (U), setting (V=\sum_jH_jU), (Z=U\cap V^\perp), (W=V\cap U^\perp), and (Q=(Z+W)^\perp) yields the displayed zero blocks and (2\dim W+\dim Q=r). The dimension identity follows from the two adjoint projection maps having equal rank. Symmetry sends (H_jZ) into (W). Thus depths (0,K,\lceil K/2\rceil) make every nonzero monomial's residual-width product at most (2^{-K}). This includes zero-depth coordinates and odd (K).
3. **Rational nc-rank import.** I read Theorem 1.5 and Lemma 5.3 in the cached primary IQS manuscript, `build/source-cache/iqs2018.txt`. The former explicitly returns a maximal-deficiency shrinking subspace and gives polynomial intermediate/final size over \(\mathbb Q\); the latter preserves nc-rank under field extension. These are the precise facts needed to match the manuscript's complex rank with a rational construction. Subsequent nullspaces, intersections, and inverses are only a fixed number of exact rational linear-algebra operations. No algebraic orthonormal basis is silently required.
4. **Capacity hypotheses and scaling.** I checked GGOW's free-rank/shrinking correspondence, capacity definition and scaling, and Theorem 2.18 in `build/source-cache/ggow2020.txt`. Its lower bound for integral Kraus matrices is (r^{-2r}) under rank nondecrease; no omitted entry-height normalization is needed. For the principal compression (G_j), denominator clearing gives \(\operatorname{cap}(T_G)\ge D_H^{-2r}r^{-2r}\). The exponent (2r), the box-volume lower bound (V_I\ge E_B^{-1}), and the subsequent logarithmic lower bound in `eq:rational-ncrank-lower-explicit` are consistent. Restricting to a principal coordinate slice preserves a genuine box, which is material to this denominator argument.
5. **Upper construction and encoding.** Rational enclosing intervals are computed from rowwise extrema of (T^{-1}x), but all original bounds on (x=c+Ay) remain in the formulation. Translation and diagonal normalization preserve the necessary quadratic zero blocks. The coefficient sum (C) and integer (b=\max(0,\lceil\log_2(C/4)\rceil)) have polynomial input height. The common-prefix error is (C2^{-K}/4\le2^{-k}), and the count is at most (rk/2+rb/2+n/2). Numerical width or witness magnitude is never substituted for binary encoding. The statement following the proof accounts for the full encoding of an arbitrary rational tolerance.
6. **Accuracy of the finite optimizer.** I checked the rational Jacobi lemma (`sections/02-quadratic-finite.tex:353`) and finite algorithm (`:432`), including the inexact recurrence (`:512`). Rational rotations do not require separated eigenvalues. Matrix-function and metric-error bounds are based on explicit spectral ranges; the fixed dyadic rounding prevents denominator growth across iterations. I checked the imported projection/descent inequality in Zhang–Sra, Lemma 7/Corollary 8, and the SPD curvature bound in Criscitiello–Boumal, Proposition I.1, in the cached primary texts. The manuscript budgets numerical error, determinant loss, and the final rational grid separately. I found no gap converting a real-arithmetic optimizer into the claimed polynomial-bit construction.

These source checks concern the passages actually used. They do not amount to independent proofs of the imported nc-rank algorithms or a survey establishing priority against every external formulation paper.
