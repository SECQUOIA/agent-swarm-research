## Additional scrutiny of the relative-error and encoding chain

1. **`prop:relative-power`, section 03, lines 1340–1392.** For convex powers the residue combination has integral index and ratio at least `M^(q-1)/2^q`; for concave powers the different sequence yields ratio at most `M^(q-1)+M^(-q)`. These bounds give exactly the stated tolerance thresholds. The proof works on the punctured domain without closure. Adding zero changes neither obstruction, while the concave zero-integer hypograph is convex and valid at relative tolerance one. On `[a,1]`, the geometric packing remains inside the truncated interval, and the rectangle ratio lower bound implies the required lower relative-error bound. The resulting statement is an order law, not matching leading constants. Zero error is handled separately.

2. **`thm:root-encoding`, section 03, lines 1403–1473.** Freezing a witness for `(2^(-D),1/2)` leaves a feasible rational LP after imposing `w >= 1/4`. Its finite optimum is attained and positive because the entire projected set obeys the graph tube at zero. Clearing denominators before freezing makes the continuous matrix independent of the magnitude of the integer witness. After splitting free variables and introducing slacks, an optimal basis exists even if the original polyhedron has lineality. The objective, including a difference of split variables, has denominator dividing the common basis determinant. Row-wise total encoding bounds the sum of logarithmic row norms by `O(s)`, giving the needed `Omega(D)` rather than a weaker square-root encoding bound. The matching sixteen-segment construction contains the exact graph, with both its graph value and admitted output in a band of width `1/16`.

3. **`lem:conic-value`, section 03, lines 1482–1505.** For positive weight, scaling and weak duality give exact multiplication by the fixed optimum. For zero weight, an original feasible primal point and feasible dual point respectively force the common objective to be nonpositive and nonnegative. Equality therefore leaves only zero. This deals with inactive selectors and rules out unwanted recession output; attainment is separately used to construct every positive-weight point. No variable product appears in the encoded constraints.

4. **`thm:root-conic-separation`, section 03, lines 1507–1567.** The rotated cone normalization is self-dual under the ordinary pairing. The explicit dual vectors lie on its boundary; intermediate coefficients cancel and the constant terms telescope to the claimed optimum. Their potentially exponential rational length is witness length, not formulation data length. Zero and one knots use direct equations. With four bits, every inactive segment has zero weight, so the preceding zero-weight lemma is necessary and sufficient to make it contribute zero. There are only constantly many chains of length `B`. The structured numerical count and ordinary sparse index cost are distinguished. Exact primal–dual equality is part of the representation, and no claim of tolerance-stable numerical solution or short exact optimal output is made.
