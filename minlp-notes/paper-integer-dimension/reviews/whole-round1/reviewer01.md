## Foundational reconstruction

1. **Representation and contacts — `01-foundations.tex`, lines 13–125.** The graph sandwich quantifies over every admitted point, while graph containment supplies actual witnesses. Projection of a convex real-index section is convex even when neither it nor the lift is closed. Convex combinations of full witnesses establish the section and integral-mixture rules. Closing each parity contact inside the compact domain preserves the midpoint inequality by continuity and closedness of the permitted error set. The resulting finite compact cover is sufficient for every subsequent volume argument; neither a measurable witness selection nor measurable original projections are needed. Affine output subtraction, transformed domains, and affine input slices preserve the required models. The finite-disjunction proof handles zero-weight components through boundedness or the common recession cone, including a one-member, zero-bit disjunction.
2. **Elementary lower bounds and constructions — lines 135–436.** I checked the strong-curvature diameter/volume constants, exact square threshold, product area estimate, four-contact width obstruction, unequal-edge LP, and shared residual-product encoding. In the square and monomial constructions, the residual endpoint includes input one and empty prefixes are valid. Exact residual monomial values establish graph containment, while envelopes control every feasible residual value. The indefinite-volume proof uses an attained maximum simplex and polarization; its determinant estimate gives the stated principal-slice constant. Zero rank gives an exact affine LP.
3. **One-sided claims — lines 440–561.** Negative curvature supplies the epigraph lower obstruction and positive curvature the hypograph obstruction. The continuous folding lift projects to the intended piecewise-linear underestimator because the current weighted fold loss dominates the optimized future gain. Signed-square assembly preserves unbounded epigraph/hypograph directions. The exact product threshold is correctly asserted for convex lifts; exact finite-LP attainment there is not asserted. The projected-facet argument counts distinct exposed faces and survives equalities and lineality.
4. **Quadratic-system lower chain — lines 566–853.** I reconstructed Hermitian principal compression with the correct multiplication order, descent of a maximizing complex shrunk subspace to a real one, and the orthogonal `Z,W,Q` construction. Its exponent sum is `r/2`. The centered fourth-moment identity has the stated nonnegative discarded terms even for indefinite Hessians. Positive-volume contacts have positive definite covariance; whitening and the centered-ball moment comparison give the volume factor. Both the capacity route and the separate Hall/permanent route bound determinant by energy. The sum-of-squares certificate and cross-product/direct-sum example retain their distinct proofs. The principal slice and parity cover give the explicit lower constant; the shared monomial construction gives the matching upper exponent. This is equality of asymptotic expansions, not equality of the two minima at every tolerance.
5. **Other contact mechanisms throughout the paper.** Transformed supports in positive-polynomial and power lower bounds are compact homeomorphic images covering the transformed cube; the arguments use volume there and do not assume preservation of original volume. Product packings rely on ordered Jensen superadditivity and bounded deletion neighborhoods. Relative-error obstructions use finite residue classes modulo a chosen integer and require no closure at the excluded endpoint. Cap-set and thirds obstructions average full witnesses, including the further convexification within the middle integer section. Binary lower bounds that use arbitrary convex fixed-code sections are explicitly stronger than the paper's binary-linear minimum.
