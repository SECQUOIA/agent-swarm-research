## Full proof-chain assessment

1. **Representation and contact geometry.** The graph sandwich retains all exact contacts and controls every admitted error. The default error body is explicitly centered at zero. The definition keeps arbitrary convex integer lifts separate from binary linear lifts, and the later lower bounds for the larger convex-binary class are identified as such. Parity classes are not called convex sections. Taking closures only in the visible input space gives compact contact sets and valid limiting midpoint inequalities without closedness of the lift, bounded integer witnesses or measurability of the initial witness supports. The finite-mixture statement supports the later modulus-three and cap-set arguments. Finite disjunction supplies the needed linear upper constructions, including singleton and zero-bit cases.

2. **Quadratic asymptotics.** The exact square construction includes the upper endpoint and empty prefix, while the product construction shares input bits and residual monomials. Product widths and volumes imply the graph-cover LP lower bound; rounded fractional allocation supplies its upper bound. The indefinite determinant estimate gives the scalar rank lower rate without a false strong-convexity assumption. The one-sided inertia result distinguishes exact convex epigraphs from finite linear threshold attainment. For systems, principal compression over the Hermitian free field provides an original-coordinate slice. Real descent and symmetric shrinking provide orthogonal subspaces with the required zero blocks. Their precision exponents sum to half the noncommutative rank. Covariance plus capacity, or the independently supplied Hall/permanent argument, bounds contact volume. The cross-product example correctly separates joint rank from maximum scalar-combination rank.

3. **Smooth maps and perspectives.** The local smooth lower estimate uses a matrix-evaluation phase and the mixed-Hessian oscillatory bound. Its tensor dimension cancels from the final precision exponent; it does not impose an aspect-ratio condition on contacts. The global-span upper keeps the original transformed domain and uses actual affine Taylor bands. For fixed-degree polynomials, forbidden Hessian entries vanish identically, so prefix expansion on the enclosing box is justified and has the stated polynomial logarithmic size. The constant-rank scalar proof uses partial Legendre coordinates to construct affine fibers and actual polyhedral tubes in the original space. It controls the entire tube and covers boundary points using the neighborhood hypothesis. The perspective proof homogenizes every original row, represents only binary-times-scale products and divides by a strictly positive scale. It does not claim an analogous unrestricted-integer upper transfer.

4. **Finite covariance and rational construction.** Scaling contact covariance simultaneously respects the spectral cap and all unequal energy budgets. The common monomial error matrix controls simultaneous outputs without an output-count penalty. The determinant certificate has the correct Lagrangian signs and relative spectral-distance estimate. Commuting reduction uses geometric means rather than ordinary convexity of the energy-feasible set. The rational nc-rank construction uses rational bases of orthogonal spaces, and its lower estimate retains capacity scaling by the Hessian denominator to power twice the rank. The finite algorithm's exact penalty, bounded normalized gradients and polynomial metric radius support a recurrence for the actual stored rational iterates. Rational Jacobi updates retain exact orthogonality and do not need eigenvalue separation. Near-active branches, matrix functions, local rounding and final exact feasibility repair are accounted for separately; the rational grid loses only the stated additional linear count.

5. **Error bodies, active inputs and positive structure.** Grouped PSD energies control the complete correlated error vector and explicitly allow unmeasured directions only in the announced extension. The correlation-SDP construction provides an exactly feasible branch and a certified upper objective value for different uses. General-body reduction intersects with the effective nonlinear output image while retaining the entire graph. Symmetry removes the possibly real center from the imported ellipsoid rounding. Input quotienting subtracts affine terms before projection and keeps an exact linear lift of the zonotope. The block-logdet oracle supplies a known interior ball, valid rational weak separators and a central-ball exact-feasibility repair. PSD blocks, diagonal coordinates, integer features and forest differences retain the domain-volume and activity hypotheses needed for their sharper losses. The thin-domain example explains why these hypotheses cannot be dropped.

6. **Hardness and asymptotic quantifiers.** The Max-Cut threshold is exact for the restricted zero-count family. Replicated contacts give a parity packing; appending the square changes the low optimum to one while enlarging the high-case packing. Fixed polynomial replication excludes every stated fixed power-sublinear additive or multiplicative guarantee. This reasoning reads the declared count of a returned valid formulation and does not assume that the formulation can be optimized efficiently. The paper also distinguishes the unconditional covariance-benchmark gap from a universal computational lower bound, and expressly leaves losses such as N/log N outside the proved hardness.

7. **Scalar shape and certified integration.** Chord refinement, finite maximal incompatibility packing and Jensen superadditivity supply the scalar count comparisons independently of a polynomial representation. The truncated mass upper and reverse potential estimates have compatible constants and endpoint cases. The two degree-gap examples use different tolerances and refute different benchmarks. Certified curvature integration separates exact polynomial branches from analytic square-root panels. For signed coefficients, failed panels lie near complex roots, and separation bounds limit depth; the total adaptive tree is polynomial. Gaussian positivity, exactness, weight conditioning and rational evaluation control numerical error. The inverse modulus handles cancellation, and mass-accurate bisection needs no unjustified density lower bound. Repeated/reversed knots still give a covering polygonal path and valid directed bands.

8. **Compilation and separability.** Only the external index bits are declared integral; exact gate constraints force the internal wires to Boolean values. A known common denominator, signed offsets, shared interpolation and explicit invalid-code exclusion make the arithmetic-to-MILP interface precise. The dense convex hybrid uses the greedy stopping certificate to absorb curvature splitting when enumeration would be large. Separable lower comparisons use Jensen superadditivity and product codes, rather than comparing unrelated local optima. The later vector overlay adds ordered canonical quantiles, a common rational grid, exact multiset order statistics and source-cell metadata. This maintains common-input containment, including duplicates and mixed curvature signs.

9. **Positive allocation, powers and encoding.** Supporting nonnegative multipliers remove degree from the allocation lower bound without being required computational inputs. Dyadic layers control normalized curvature, and sparse rounded powers depend on exponent bit length instead of exact huge numerators. The finite pure-power theorem, numerical-degree Stieltjes construction and binary rational-exponent compiler have distinct complexity statements. The rational P/Q gadget requires a supplied positive denominator bound and works at zero bits. The inverse-power enclosures and directed bands contain the graph as well as bounding admitted errors. Relative-error obstructions use an arbitrary modulus and the appropriate convex/concave thresholds. The root MILP lower bound controls basis denominators independently of integer-witness magnitude. The conic-value lemma treats zero weight explicitly, and the repeated-squaring dual certifies the short conic encoding. Large witness values are not confused with long coefficients.

10. **Vector geometry and separations.** Selecting original convex outputs preserves nonnegative chord gaps despite signed representation coefficients. Facet and oracle constructions use a half-body or explicit inner-image band, not an unsupported independent downward shift. Rational spanners have a fixed global denominator and exact-feasibility repair; positive-polar access is weak and is derived from the original strong oracle. Effective-coordinate rounding keeps all relevant errors inside the nonlinear image. One concatenated basis works across separable inputs, and the six local capacity constants match their product-packing bounds. The positive-power, cap-set and repeated-convexification arguments do not claim a growing binary/general-integer difference. The exact convex product family verifies every mixed middle integer section and yields both exact counts. Bernstein triangular waves give a nonconvex scalar separation; the tilted convexification changes the body and has growing conditioning. Neither construction is used to settle the remaining one-input convex box question.

## Standalone exposition and publication assessment

The paper gives enough local explanation to reconstruct why the techniques change: determinant volume replaces strong convexity for indefinite forms; oscillatory estimates replace an inadequate thin-contact perturbation argument; certified random access replaces explicit enumeration; and common positive curvature bases replace unrelated scalar formulations. These transitions are material to comprehension and are present.

The result map and local normalization conventions make the repeated rank and determinant symbols manageable. Noncommutative rank, common nonlinear input rank, curvature-span rank and output-image dimension are not presented as interchangeable. Every major computational theorem identifies a rational representation or oracle interface; finite real-coefficient comparisons are not silently advertised as short rational formulations. The introduction and conclusion retain this distinction and do not imply easy MILP solution or ideality.

Some stronger results supersede earlier numerical bounds, but the retained proofs have distinct uses: the direct Taylor/prefix construction, feature-curve geometry, maximum-product allocation, elementary normalization/simplex bounds, hinge stability and the permanent alternative explain mechanisms not supplied by the sharper count alone. I found no redundant block whose presence creates a material obstacle or false dependency.

The paper is long and technically dense, so its eventual venue and editorial organization remain judgment calls. That is not a mathematical defect. The present source is suitable for substantive external mathematical assessment, subject to the ordinary limits of a bounded internal review. Its predecessor credit is explicit, and it does not assert exhaustive novelty.
