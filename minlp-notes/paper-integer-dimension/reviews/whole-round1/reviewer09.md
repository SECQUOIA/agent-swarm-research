## Additional depth on positive mixtures and powers

- **Allocation and lower geometry.** The positive allocation oracle correctly specializes the block logdet oracle. The optimization interface does not need positivity or unconditionality, while the later error-domination proof does. At an optimal allocation, the supporting normal and coordinate cap multipliers give a scalarization with the same optimum product. Zero output rows can be assigned zero normal weight. The normalized square-root mixture is convex, so its Jensen lower bound leads to feasible covariance diagonals and the claimed degree-free lower constant. The earlier feature-curve proof is also retained; it is not silently replaced by the stronger supporting-scalarization proof.
- **Dyadic layers and sparse exponents (`thm:positive-loglog`).** I checked both the final endpoint layer and the nonfinal layers, including the normalized second-derivative estimate using `(v+1)^2 exp(-v)`. Layer codes, within-layer cell codes, implied selectors and unused codes account for the stated count. In the dense construction, exact prefix powers and first-order residual terms give the required Taylor remainder. In the sparse construction, only listed powers are evaluated; downward repeated-squaring error is controlled at a precision polynomial in exponent bit length. Shared interpolation and the enlarged band supply both graph containment and validity, with no extra declared integers. The resulting double-logarithmic degree overhead is a count comparison, not a numerical-degree algorithm disguised as a sparse one.
- **Pure powers and the exponent-one limit.** The power-coordinate Jensen and chord inequalities have the right normalization for both exponents at least two and exponents between one and two. Scaling by `s=min(1,alpha-1)` is necessary near one and appears in both allocation and error budgets. For rational exponents, the cost of `log(1/s)` is polynomial in rational input length. An inverse-knot approximation need not give monotone interior knots: the continuous polygonal path between exact endpoint inputs zero and one still covers the full interval. The bands are checked at its actual interpolated input, using the Lipschitz correction. This proves containment without assuming exact algebraic inverse coefficients.
- **Stieltjes construction (`lem:stieltjes-power`).** I recomputed the lower and upper tails, the complex-panel Taylor bound, positive Gauss quadrature error, relative rational coefficient perturbation and normalization at one. The estimate `E<delta/8`, together with `I_gamma(1)>=2`, controls the normalized error by `2E/(2-E)`. Small nodes and weights require only polynomial precision in the numerical degree and requested accuracy. The term count is polynomial in numerical `D`; the manuscript expressly uses a different algorithm for binary-encoded large exponents. The signed `P/Q` endpoint-product gadget needs the supplied positive denominator certificate and keeps all reciprocal wires continuous.
- **Inverse arithmetic and final constants.** Integer inverse knots use rounded binary powering with an uncertainty branch controlled by a root derivative bound. Rational inverse knots use range reduction, the arctanh logarithm series, a small negative argument for an odd exponential truncation, a power-of-two rescaling of magnitude `O(L)`, and final dyadic rounding. Intermediate exact rational lengths remain polynomial. Allocation loss, depth ceilings and the lower constants give the stated `6.5r+1`/`7r+1` rational-power comparisons; the finite real-knot and dense reciprocal statements retain their own constants and complexity qualifications.
