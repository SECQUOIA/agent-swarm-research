The retained mathematical results are sound after the qualifications and proof completions below. The two important limits are that a finite trajectory cannot establish or refute an infinite-sequence uniform-pointedness hypothesis, and that a ratio of local objective bounds supplies no objective-independent cut-depth guarantee. ## Minor geometry: complete statements and proof corrections

Write `M=[[a,b],[c,d]]`. The canonical coordinates are `x=((a+d)/2,(c-b)/2)` and `y=((d-a)/2,(b+c)/2)`, so `det M=||x||²-||y||²` has signature `(2,2)`. All main minor statements concern the full singular locus and an invertible positive-determinant apex. A column interchange handles negative determinant; it does not justify replacing the full singular locus by a rank-one PSD projection.

For a full-dimensional closed convex set containing that apex, singular-locus freeness is equivalent to containment in `{det≥0}`. The determinant has constant positive sign on its connected interior; conversely no singular matrix is interior to `{det≥0}`. Homogeneity then gives `cl cone C⊆{det≥0}` directly, proving every maximal such set is a cone. Thus the previous appeal to a conification lemma is unnecessary. A segment from a positive determinant apex to a negative determinant point gives an earlier zero, so the equation and sublevel corner bounds coincide for positive weights.

The orbit is `C_F={M:sym(F^T M)⪰0}`, `det F>0`, with parameters modulo positive scalar. Under `M↦AMB^T`, `F'^T=BF^T A^{-1}`; transposition gives `F^{-1}`. A direct proof of maximality of `C_I` avoids any classification dependency: an outside point has a symmetric part with a negative eigenvalue; combine it with an interior member of `C_I` whose skew part cancels and whose symmetric part makes the midpoint indefinite. That midpoint is interior to the convex enlargement. Connecting it to the positive-determinant interior point `I` gives an interior singular matrix. Thus no convex free enlargement exists. Linear maps transfer maximality to all `C_F`.

Equality `C_F=C_F'` gives `F PSD=F' PSD`. Left multiplication by `F^{-1}F'` must preserve the symmetric matrix space. Testing a symmetric basis makes it a scalar identity, and preserving PSD forces the scalar positive. The orbit consequently has projective dimension three.

The transformed polar rule is precisely `F^T Mbar=H≻0`, `H` symmetric, modulo scaling, hence dimension two and equivariant. Its intersection with the rotation family is one cone: uniqueness of the positive polar decomposition forces `R=U`. The original proof's phrase “both signs give C_U” needs its scalar-parameter interpretation made explicit: `C_{-U}` is the opposite cone; it is not a second member containing the apex in its interior. The rewritten proof uses only the positive polar choice.

For a parameter subspace `L`, family containment of `T_z` is exactly the LMIs `sym(G Mbar)≻0` and `sym(GV)⪰0` at all level-simplex vertices, with `G=F^T∈L`. Strict positive definiteness at the apex automatically gives `det F>0`. The spaces have dimensions four (orbit), three (point rule), two (rotation span), and one (fixed polar ray). The strict apex condition excludes the zero parameter. Monotonicity gives a numerical bisection method, but numerical infeasibility decisions are not upper bounds. A rational tuple of PD matrices with `Σ_V tr(GVY_V)=0` for a basis of `L` is an exact upper certificate. For the full orbit it is simply `Σ_V VY_V=0`.

Independent supported rays can always be selected among minimizers: a supported kernel direction preserves the projected point, and optimality forces its objective cost zero; move until a coordinate vanishes. For an independent support of size `k`, the objective-level directions form a nonnegative subspace of dimension `k−1`, giving `k≤3`. If the determinant gradient vanishes on the whole supported span, nonnegativity extends to that span, giving `k≤2`. At a nonzero null point, the tangent hyperplane has one-dimensional radical and quotient signature `(1,1)`. A two-dimensional nonnegative subspace in it must contain the null point; support three therefore implies the apex belongs to the supported three-ray span. Support two gives tangency and nonnegative edge determinant. The smallest-support existence argument does not bound every minimizer: duplicated rays can create larger supports.

For a nonzero rank-one contact `M_*=ab^T`, a tangent direction `D` with `det D>0`, and `J=[[0,1],[-1,0]]`, put `G0=J adj D`, `G1=J adj M_*`. Then `G1 a=0`, `G0 a=c0 b≠0`, and `det(G0+tG1)=det D`. Segment containment first forces `F^T a=cb` with `c>0`. Positive semidefiniteness on both sides of the contact forces `sym(F^T D)` to be a scalar `bb^T`; subtract that scalar multiple of the contact to obtain a skew matrix. Inverting `D+tM_*` gives `F^T=θ(G0+tG1)`, `θc0>0`. This proof is independent of all prior notes. An empty intersection of vertex parameter intervals proves nonattainment only; a rational dual certificate at a level below one supplies the strict supremum gap.

The contact cone is exactly `{F^{-T}bb^T:b∈R²}`, dimension two. Exact attainment forces every minimizer into this cone because the entire optimal simplex belongs to the set. For a unique support-one contact of the polar cone, the projected ray's skew polar coordinate must vanish, a single hyperplane condition. No measure-zero theorem for arbitrary support-two data is asserted.

For `Mbar=diag(1,δ)` and rays `E12,-E11,-δE22,-δE21`, positive weights all one give `zK=1`. The polar ray steps are `(2√δ,1,1,2/√δ)` and its bound is `2√δ`; `F^T=diag(1,1/δ)` belongs to the point-rule family and has steps `(2,1,1,2)`. Every rotation member has bound at most `((16+2√73)/3)√δ`. The same corner is a one-variable scaling of an exact unscaled polar choice. Thus polar and rotation choices vanish, while the equivariant point-rule and orbit families stay exact. The stronger numerical asymptotic `≈4√δ` remains numerical.

For symmetric principal minors with positive determinant, the trace determines the component: positive trace gives the PD component and negative trace the ND component. A connected free interior stays in that component, so its closure lies in PSD or NSD, respectively. These convex cones are therefore the unique maximal full-singular-locus-free sets. All level simplices below the corner bound stay in the same component, proving the polar cone's cut exact. This proof is complete without the general supremum theorem. The NSD case uses polar factor `−I`; negative determinant is a distinct signature `(2,1)` problem.

The one-diagonal outer-product projection has closure `{det M=0,a≥0}`. The explicit square-root realization for `a>0` and the three boundary cases `b≠0`, `c≠0`, `b=c=0` prove this exactly. If diagonal bounds instead restrict a principal forbidden projection to rank-one PSD matrices, the quoted NSD-halfspace classification concerns an indefinite apex, with `<A,Mbar>>0` and `A⪯0`. It does not apply at a PD apex. The corresponding valid inequality is a nonnegative combination of at most two PSD test inequalities, but a particular eigenvector separator need not choose those vectors. This is prior-work context, not an extension of the full-singular-locus trace theorem.

## Convergence: complete hypotheses and repairs

The exact loop starts from a bounded polytope with nonempty nonlinear feasible set, solves each LP at an optimal vertex with an optimal nonsingular basis, retains all valid cuts, and adds a cut for every violated term. The sets exclude the violated side, so the cuts remain valid for the equations. The theorem itself uses only compactness, closed constraints and persistent cuts, not bilinearity, positive reduced costs, or maximality.

Its depth is `dist(v^r,K_r∩H_{r,e})`, not necessarily distance to the cut hyperplane. For every `t>0`, a uniform positive function `η(t)` must lower-bound that depth whenever the current point is at distance at least `t` from the term equation. If an accumulation point violated a term, a convergent subsequence would eventually be at least distance `t` from it. Each selected earlier cut excludes a ball of radius `η(t)` around that subsequence point from every later vertex, contradicting convergence. All accumulation points are feasible; monotonicity and continuity give optimal objective-value convergence. There is no finite termination or rate claim.

Normalize all full-space basis rays `u_j=r_j/||r_j||`, and put `γ=dist(0,conv{u_j})`, `δ=min_j α_j||r_j||` over finite steps. For a cut-feasible conic combination, the cut implies its total normalized-ray mass is at least `δ`; its displacement norm is at least `γ` times that mass. Therefore `d≥γδ`. Every individual nonsingular basis cone is pointed and has positive γ. Only a uniform lower bound over all rounds supplies the corollary. Uniform pointedness is sufficient when paired with uniform positive steps, and is not needed by the depth theorem itself.

A one-term loop transfers this proof if it selects maximum constraint distance and supplies the same depth function, or if it selects maximum raw residual and has `d≥const·q`. For pairwise distinct indices, changing only the product coordinate proves `q=|w−xy|≥dist(v,S_e)`. An arbitrary violated-term selection can indefinitely postpone the needed term; no such transfer is claimed.

On a fixed coordinate box, the analytical Case-4 set contains its underlying norm set, which contains a ball of radius `(a−b)/√2=q/[√2(a+b)]`. The two affine canonical maps have operator norm at most `1/√2`; `a,b≤L_B` gives steps at least `q/(2√2 L_B)`. The appendix proves the closed formula and violated-side freeness directly, including exceptional endpoint direction cases and the opposite side.

The reference orbit parameter `F^T=M_sigma(sbar)^{-1}` contains a ball of radius `σ_min(M_sigma(sbar))≥q/√(3B²+1)`. The proof uses the exact Frobenius isometry of the linear matrix map and the singular-value identity, for either determinant sign. The geometric optimized objective gives a uniform step bound if its returned value is at least a fixed fraction θ of the supremum. The Euclidean perturbed objective does so if θ and a positive perturbation τ are both fixed, with normalized costs in `[0,1]`. These are idealized algorithms. Fixed absolute bisection error supplies no uniform multiplicative θ as the optimal value tends to zero, and no convergence result for the finite numerical implementation follows without such a guarantee.

Proposition 5 varies objectives, even with a fixed apex `(0,0,1)`, fixed orthant cone, fixed violation one, distance one to the forbidden set, γ=`1/√3`, and fixed-box embedding. With ray costs `(1,ε,1)`, the corner bound is `2√ε`. Every admissible free set satisfies `α_y α_x≤4`; a ratio at least θ therefore forces depth at most `2√ε/θ`. The explicit maximal orbit set `w≥(√ε x+y/√ε)²/4` attains ratio one and has exact depth `2√ε/√(1+ε²)→0`. This proves no objective-independent implication from the raw ratio to uniform depth. Since the smallest positive cost tends to zero across instances, it is not a nonconvergent bound-optimal loop on one fixed problem.

The stalling theorem fixes one objective, one bounded box, and one bilinear equation, but chooses an adversarial sequence of maximal sets. For increasing `ξ_r→ξ_inf<2/√ε`, using `C_{2/ξ_{r+1}}` adds exactly `x/ξ_{r+1}+ξ_{r+1}y/4≥1`. Induction gives unique optimal vertices `(ξ_r,0,1)`, all with violation one, values `1+εξ_r`, and limit strictly below the nonlinear optimum `1+2√ε`. The complete basis and step calculations are printed in E. Maximality of the parabolic sets is proved directly by showing every convex enlargement contains a strict forbidden interior point.

Pairwise angles away from π do not in general prove uniform pointedness. The repaired construction uses `T=ξ_inf²/4` and separating vector `h=(1,T+1,1)`. Its inner products with all normalized rays are at least `1/√(1+T²)`, so `γ_r≥1/[√(1+T²)√(2+(T+1)²)]>0` in every round. The depth is at most `ξ_{r+1}−ξ_r→0`. This gives a genuine fixed-violation suboptimal-limit counterexample despite uniform pointedness. Its sets are images of the INITIAL default Case-4 set, not the successive default choices, and the starting relaxation is a box, not a McCormick relaxation.

Finally exact local bound attainment forces `ω_jα_j=zK` on the support of a feasible corner minimizer. Validity and the objective inequality sandwich the cut sum at that minimizer between one and one, proving the equality. The feasible point lies in the relative interior of the corresponding intercept simplex, which belongs to the optimal corner-LP face. Support at least two gives an additional zero reduced cost at each intercept basis; support one does not. The intercepts may lie outside the current full relaxation. Neither this proposition nor the finite tie counts establish a causal explanation of slow reoptimization.
