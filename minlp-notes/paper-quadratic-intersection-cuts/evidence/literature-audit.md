# Literature and attribution audit

Reviewed 6 October 2026 against the integrated manuscript source then present in `sections/00`, `01`, `03`–`09`, `appendices/A`–`E`, the evidence brief, local literature notes and extracted full text, and the cited bibliography. The audit is limited to attribution, source versions, hypotheses, and novelty scope; mathematical proof reviews and computational audits are recorded separately. No experiments were rerun.

## Novelty disposition

The manuscript is strongest when it presents its contribution as a collection of precise results about objective-specific cut quality, restricted quadratic-free families, fixed-corner closures, and a repeated-cut loop. It should not suggest that choosing a free set, optimizing cut depth, characterizing maximal quadratic-free sets, or proving convergence of cutting-plane methods is itself new. The current introduction makes those distinctions explicitly. Its closing novelty sentence is suitably narrow if “the particular” is read literally: it covers the stated bounds, contact criteria, rational counterexamples, closure gaps, and adversarial sequence, not the established frameworks around them. The novelty claim remains qualified as “to the best of our knowledge”; this bounded search cannot certify priority.

## Current text and source-version qualifications

- The abstract and discussion make no external priority claim beyond the qualified introduction statement. The introduction explicitly calls the unrestricted CGF results background and separates single-cut bounds, reoptimized LP bounds, closures, and repeated rounds.
- The contact/attainment claim is limited to the manuscript’s support-one bilinear setting with the stated unique minimizer and positive noncontact heights: the closed interval intersection characterizes supremum equality; strict interval membership at the apex is needed for attainment. This is a specialized criterion, not a replacement for general CGF sufficiency results.
- The closure statement is about fixed corners and the named families. In particular, upward completion can restore exactness at a particular corner without giving a uniform positive approximation factor. The exact BP infimum `a₁=7/2` is attained; the three-cut value is a certified lower bound `24884029/25000000 = 0.99536116`, not a generic closure theorem.
- `BCM2020` Theorem 23(iv) concerns the cited rotation family. The manuscript’s extra orbit parameters do not classify all maximal determinant-free sets. The §6 polar-factor formula references the BCM source and the Chmiela–Muñoz–Serrano 2020 report after the final correction.
- `CMS2023` is retained only for the generic published implementation attribution. The inspected ZIB report is cited for report-specific Case-4 formulas and its root experiments. The precise 2023 journal content remains unavailable, so no journal-only difference is asserted.
- Fischetti–Monaci is an accepted manuscript/postprint source matching its 2020 EJOR publication; it is not the SCIP procedure. Results are stated with their heterogeneous sample and solver qualifications.
- The bibliography key `CCDLM2015` has the journal range 276–301. Crossref and the inspected cover/title-page metadata conflict with the author/publication records; the latter support 276–301, which is used in the bibliography. The local HAL author PDF supports Theorem 6.3 on PDF pp. 25–26.
- `Renegar1992PartIII` is the inspected theorem source used in Appendix A. Part I is metadata-only, is not cited, and is not needed for the present claim. The current bibliography has no Part I Renegar key, no GLS1981 entry, and no obsolete `Chmiela2023` alias.
- Bibliography review: all citation keys used by the current manuscript are defined in `references.bib`; entries use conventional publication metadata, with notes retained only to identify unpublished or versioned preprint/report records. A final compile/link check belongs to the root build; this audit did not rebuild the project.
