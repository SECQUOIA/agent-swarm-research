## 1. Setting and standing facts

Throughout, a *pair hull* is the convex hull of a continuous feature map on a
compact domain. For the path x–y–z with y ∈ [ℓ,u] and x, z ∈ [0,1],
```
H_xy = conv{(x, y, x², xy, y²) : (x,y) ∈ [0,1]×[ℓ,u]},
H_yz = conv{(y, z, y², yz, z²) : (y,z) ∈ [ℓ,u]×[0,1]},
R    = {(h_L, h_R) ∈ H_xy × H_yz : the y and y² coordinates of h_L and h_R agree}.
```
For a polynomial f that is affine in a block's features, L(f) denotes its
linearization, which is affine in the lifted coordinates.

**Fact 0 (representing measures).** If K is compact and φ continuous, then
conv φ(K) is compact (Carathéodory). Its points are exactly the vectors
∫φ dμ, where μ ranges over finitely supported probability measures on K. The
barycenter of any Borel probability measure on K also lies in this set. If f
is affine in φ and f ≥ 0 on K, then L(f) ≥ 0 on conv φ(K). Moreover, for
h = Σλ_iφ(p_i) with λ_i > 0, L(f)(h) = 0 holds iff every p_i lies in {f = 0}.

## 3. The family: exact value of the glued relaxation

Let ℓ ≤ u and a, b, c, d ∈ ℝ with A = {a, a+b} ⊂ [ℓ,u] and C = {c, c+d} ⊂ [ℓ,u].
Let δ = dist(A,C), and let β ≥ b² and β' ≥ d² be penalty weights. Define
```
D_L(x,y) = (y−a−bx)² + β x(1−x),   D_R(y,z) = (y−c−dz)² + β' z(1−z),   D = D_L + D_R,
```
on B = [0,1]×[ℓ,u]×[0,1]. The report's family is the case β = b², β' = d². The
1/128 example has β = β' = 1 ≥ b² = 1/4 and 1 ≥ d² = 25/64.

Say that A and C *strictly interleave* if they are disjoint, both have two
points, and the sorted union alternates between A and C. With a < a+b and
c < c+d this means a < c < a+b < c+d or c < a < c+d < a+b.

**Lemma 1 (leaf elimination).** For fixed y, D_L is concave or affine in x:
its x² coefficient is b² − β ≤ 0. When β = b², D_L = (y−a)² + bx(b − 2(y−a)).
Hence min_{x∈[0,1]} D_L(x,y) = min{(y−a)², (y−a−b)²} = dist(y,A)². Moreover
D_L ≥ 0 on [0,1]×ℝ. Its zero set is Z_L = {(x,y) : y = a + bx, βx(1−x) = 0}.
If β > 0 this forces x ∈ {0,1}; if β = 0 then b = 0. In both cases
π_y(Z_L) = A.

*Proof.* Both summands of D_L are nonnegative on [0,1]×ℝ. A function that is
concave or affine on [0,1] attains its minimum at 0 or 1, where the penalty
vanishes. □

**Theorem A.**
1. min_B D = δ²/2. It is attained at (x*, (s+t)/2, z*), where s = a + bx* ∈ A
   and t = c + dz* ∈ C form a closest pair.
2. The minimum of L(D) over R equals
   ```
   inf { ∫dist(·,A)² dν_L + ∫dist(·,C)² dν_R :  ν_L, ν_R probability measures on [ℓ,u]
         with ∫y dν_L = ∫y dν_R and ∫y² dν_L = ∫y² dν_R }
     = inf_{(m,s)} [Φ_A(m,s) + Φ_C(m,s)],
   ```
   where Φ_A(m,s) = inf{∫dist(·,A)² dν : ν on [ℓ,u], ∫y dν = m, ∫y² dν = s}.
3. min_R L(D) = 0 if A ∩ C ≠ ∅ or A and C strictly interleave.
   Otherwise min_R L(D) = δ²/2.
4. Consequently the pair-hull gap min_B D − min_R L(D) equals δ²/2 when A and C
   strictly interleave, and 0 in every other configuration.
5. Assume strict interleaving. Then the zero witness in R is unique. Its local
   measures are (1−p)δ_(0,a) + pδ_(1,a+b) and (1−q)δ_(c,0) + qδ_(c+d,1), where
   (p,q) ∈ (0,1)² is the unique solution of pb − qd = c − a and
   p((a+b)² − a²) − q((c+d)² − c²) = c² − a².

*Proof.*
(1) By Lemma 1, min_B D = min_{y∈[ℓ,u]} min_{s∈A,t∈C} [(y−s)² + (y−t)²]. Also
(y−s)² + (y−t)² = 2(y − (s+t)/2)² + (s−t)²/2, and the midpoint (s+t)/2 lies
in [ℓ,u]. So the minimum is min_{s,t}(s−t)²/2 = δ²/2.

(2) Let (h_L,h_R) ∈ R. By Fact 0, choose finitely supported μ_L on
[0,1]×[ℓ,u] with ∫φ_L dμ_L = h_L, and similarly μ_R. Their y-marginals ν_L, ν_R
have equal first and second moments. By Lemma 1,
L(D_L)(h_L) = ∫D_L dμ_L ≥ ∫dist(y,A)² dν_L, and likewise for the right block.
Conversely, let ν_L, ν_R be finitely supported with matched moments. Map each
atom y of ν_L to (x*(y), y), where x*(y) ∈ {0,1} attains dist(y,A)². This
gives μ_L with ∫D_L dμ_L = ∫dist² dν_L, whose moment vector lies in H_xy;
treat ν_R the same way. It is enough to consider finitely supported ν, since
general measures can be replaced by finitely supported ones with the same
moments (Fact 0). The infimum over pairs splits over the common value (m,s)
of the moments, which gives the Φ form. The minimum over R is attained
because R is compact and nonempty.

(3) *Zero cases.* If t ∈ A ∩ C, take ν_L = ν_R = δ_t. If A and C strictly
interleave, Theorem C2 with k = 2 and |A|+|C| = 4 gives probability measures
on A and on C with equal moments of orders 0, 1 and 2. Lift them atom by atom
as in (2).

*Positive cases.* Assume A ∩ C = ∅ without strict interleaving. The union
A ∪ C has at most 4 points, and three label changes would require four
alternating points, which is strict interleaving. So the sorted labels change
at most twice. Either A and C are
*separated* (one lies entirely below the other), or they are *nested* (one
lies in the open gap between the two points of the other). We use the
following bound.

> **Lemma 2 (quadratic multiplier).** Let p be a polynomial of degree at most
> 2. Suppose κ₁ ≤ dist(y,A)² − p(y) and κ₂ ≤ dist(y,C)² + p(y) for all real y.
> Then min_R L(D) ≥ κ₁ + κ₂.
> *Proof.* For ν_L, ν_R as in (2), ∫dist_A² dν_L + ∫dist_C² dν_R equals
> ∫(dist_A² − p)dν_L + ∫(dist_C² + p)dν_R + (∫p dν_L − ∫p dν_R). The last
> bracket vanishes because the moments of orders 0, 1 and 2 match. □

We also use the elementary formula inf_y [α(y−s)² + β(y−m)²] = αβ(s−m)²/(α+β)
for α + β > 0, and the identity dist(y,S)² = min_{s∈S}(y−s)², which lets us
take the infimum one point s at a time.

*Separated* (by the symmetry of D between the two blocks, assume
max A < min C). Let α₀ = max A and β₀ = min C. Then δ = β₀ − α₀ > 0. Let
m₀ = (α₀+β₀)/2 and p(y) = δ(y − m₀). For s ∈ A,
inf_y[(y−s)² − δ(y−m₀)] = −δ²/4 − δ(s−m₀) ≥ −δ²/4 + δ²/2 = δ²/4.
For t ∈ C, inf_y[(y−t)² + δ(y−m₀)] = −δ²/4 + δ(t−m₀) ≥ δ²/4.
Lemma 2 gives min_R L(D) ≥ δ²/2. (Here p is linear, so matching E y alone
would already suffice.)

*Nested* (by symmetry, assume C lies in the gap of A = {a₋ < a₊}). Let
m = (min C + max C)/2 and R₀ = (max C − min C)/2. Then δ = min(min C − a₋,
a₊ − max C), and every s ∈ A satisfies |s − m| ≥ δ + R₀ =: r: indeed
m − a₋ = (min C − a₋) + R₀ and a₊ − m = (a₊ − max C) + R₀. Every t ∈ C
satisfies |t − m| ≤ R₀.
If R₀ > 0, let γ = (r − R₀)/(r + R₀) ∈ (0,1) and p(y) = −γ(y−m)². Then
inf_y[(y−s)² + γ(y−m)²] = γ(s−m)²/(1+γ) ≥ γr²/(1+γ), and
inf_y[(y−t)² − γ(y−m)²] = −γ(t−m)²/(1−γ) ≥ −γR₀²/(1−γ).
Using 1+γ = 2r/(r+R₀) and 1−γ = 2R₀/(r+R₀), the sum of the two bounds is
γ(r+R₀)(r−R₀)/2 = (r−R₀)²/2 = δ²/2.
If R₀ = 0 (C = {m}), take γ = 1. Then dist_C² + p ≡ 0 and
dist_A² − p ≥ min_s (s−m)²/2 = δ²/2.
In both cases Lemma 2 gives min_R L(D) ≥ δ²/2. Graph points belong to R, so
min_R L(D) ≤ min_B D = δ²/2.

(4) This follows from (1) and (3).

(5) Let (h_L,h_R) be a zero witness. By Fact 0, its representing measures are
supported on Z_L and Z_R. With b ≠ 0, Z_L = {(0,a),(1,a+b)} and x = (y−a)/b on
Z_L, so μ_L is determined by its y-marginal; likewise on the right. The
y-marginals are probability measures on the four alternating points with
matched moments of orders 0, 1 and 2. Theorem C2 shows that these measures
exist, are unique and have full support. This is the stated linear system,
whose unique solution satisfies 0 < p, q < 1. □

*Remarks.*
(i) Theorem A(2) is the task's φ_A formula. By Theorem C1 below, the value is
0 iff the chords conv{(t,t²): t∈A} and conv{(t,t²): t∈C} meet. For two-point
sets this happens iff they interleave or share a point.
(ii) The gap is all-or-nothing in this family. A configuration that is not
interleaving is already handled exactly by R, and the multiplier p gives an
explicit dual certificate. In the 1/128 example the order is
0 < 1/4 < 5/8 < 3/4, labels C A C A, so the sets strictly interleave and the
gap is δ²/2 = 1/128.
(iii) For k ≥ 3 shared moments, a configuration without a zero witness can
still have a gap strictly between 0 and δ²/2, and this gap depends on [ℓ,u].
Numerically (`M4_numeric_explore.out`), A = {0,2}, C = {1,3} and k = 3 give
lifted value ≈ 0.378 on [0,3] and ≈ 0.238 on [−2,5], against δ²/2 = 0.5.
The all-or-nothing behavior is therefore specific to k ≤ 2.

## 4. Dense PSD and McCormick for the missing product xz

Let M(w) be the 4×4 moment matrix of (1,x,y,z). Its entries are the
coordinates of a point of R, with an unknown w in the (x,z) position.

**Theorem B (zero witnesses).** Assume A and C strictly interleave (so b ≠ 0 ≠ d).
Take β = b² and β' = d² (for β > b² the witness has the same form). Let v* be
the unique zero witness of Theorem A(5). Then:
1. M(w) ⪰ 0 iff w = w*, where
   w* = (E[yz] − a m_z)/b = (E[xy] − c m_x)/d = E_ν[(y−a)(y−c)]/(bd);
   ν is the common separator distribution, and the three expressions agree.
2. w* violates exactly one of the four McCormick inequalities
   w ≥ 0, w ≥ m_x + m_z − 1, w ≤ m_x, w ≤ m_z.
   With b, d > 0, it violates w ≤ m_x if max(A ∪ C) ∈ A and w ≤ m_z if
   max(A ∪ C) ∈ C. Other orientations follow by complementing leaves (see the
   proof). When bd < 0, a lower inequality fails; when bd > 0, an upper one fails.
3. Each ingredient alone is insufficient. PSD alone admits w*. McCormick alone
   admits every w in [max(0, m_x+m_z−1), min(m_x, m_z)], which is nonempty.

*Proof.*
(1) Let u_L = (−a, −b, 1, 0)ᵀ. Then u_Lᵀ M(w) u_L = L((y−a−bx)²)(v*), which
does not involve w. Since L(D_L)(v*) = 0 and both summands of D_L have
nonnegative lifts on H_xy, this value is 0. If M(w) ⪰ 0, a zero quadratic form
forces M(w)u_L = 0. The z-row of this equation reads −a m_z − bw + E[yz] = 0.
Similarly u_R = (−c, 0, 1, −d)ᵀ gives −c m_x + E[xy] − dw = 0 from the x-row.
The two values of w coincide because E_ν[(y−a)(y−c)] is the same under both
local y-marginals (equal moments up to order 2). Under the left marginal the
atom y = a contributes 0, so it equals pb(a+b−c) = b(E[xy] − c m_x). Under the
right marginal it equals qd(c+d−a) = d(E[yz] − a m_z).
Conversely, set ψ(y) = (1, (y−a)/b, y, (y−c)/d). Every entry of E_ν[ψψᵀ] is a
quadratic moment of ν, so it can be evaluated under either local marginal. On
Z_L, x = (y−a)/b, and on Z_R, z = (y−c)/d. Hence E_ν[ψψᵀ] = M(w*), which is
PSD as a second-moment matrix.

(2) First assume b, d > 0. Then p, q ∈ (0,1), w* = q(c+d−a)/b and
w* = p(a+b−c)/d.
- Order a < c < a+b < c+d: since c+d−a > b, w* > q = m_z. Since
  0 < a+b−c < d, 0 < w* < p = m_x. Also w* > m_z ≥ m_x + m_z − 1. Only
  w ≤ m_z fails.
- Order c < a < c+d < a+b: symmetrically, w* > p = m_x, 0 < w* < q, and only
  w ≤ m_x fails.

For other signs, replace x by x' = 1 − x. This maps (a,b) to (a+b, −b) and
leaves D_L, A and Z_L unchanged. It maps the moment point affinely, with
w' = m_z − w and m_x' = 1 − m_x. It maps M to PᵀMP with P invertible, so PSD
is preserved. It permutes the McCormick inequalities: {w' ≥ 0} = {w ≤ m_z},
{w' ≥ m_x' + m_z − 1} = {w ≤ m_x}, {w' ≤ m_x'} = {w ≥ m_x + m_z − 1}, and
{w' ≤ m_z} = {w ≥ 0}. The case d < 0 is the same with z. Exactly one
inequality fails in every orientation, because the property is invariant
under this permutation.

(3) PSD alone admits w* by (1). Since m_x, m_z ∈ [0,1], the McCormick interval
is nonempty. □

In the 1/128 example, c < a < c+d < a+b, so w* = (1/2)(3/4)/(5/8) = 3/5 > m_x = 1/2,
as Report B states. The completion is unique: for w ≠ 3/5 the vector
u_L − t e₄ with t = (M u_L)₄ / M₄₄ gives the value −(M u_L)₄²/M₄₄ < 0.

**Theorem B' (dense PSD + McCormick is exact on the whole family).** Let
u = y − a − bx and v = y − c − dz. Write A₀ = a, A₁ = a+b, C₀ = c, C₁ = c+d,
and F_ij = ((C_j − A_i)² − δ²)/2 ≥ 0. Let ℓ₀₀ = (1−x)(1−z), ℓ₁₀ = x(1−z),
ℓ₀₁ = (1−x)z and ℓ₁₁ = xz. Then, as a polynomial identity,
```
D − δ²/2 = (u+v)²/2 + Σ_{i,j∈{0,1}} F_ij ℓ_ij(x,z) + (β − b²/2) x(1−x) + (β' − d²/2) z(1−z).
```
Consequently L(D) ≥ δ²/2 at every point that satisfies three conditions: the
xy and yz pair coordinates lie in their pair hulls, the dense moment matrix
M(w) is PSD, and w satisfies the four McCormick inequalities for xz. This holds
for all a, b, c, d and every configuration of A and C.

*Proof.* Write u² + v² = (u+v)²/2 + (u−v)²/2, where u − v = (c−a) + dz − bx.
Then
D = (u+v)²/2 + g + (β − b²/2)x(1−x) + (β' − d²/2)z(1−z), with
g = (u−v)²/2 + (b²/2)x(1−x) + (d²/2)z(1−z).
The x² and z² coefficients of g are b²/2 − b²/2 = 0 and d²/2 − d²/2 = 0, so g
is bilinear in (x,z). A bilinear function equals its interpolant from the
four vertices, so g = Σ_ij g(i,j) ℓ_ij with g(i,j) = (C_j − A_i)²/2. Since
Σ_ij ℓ_ij = 1, g − δ²/2 = Σ_ij F_ij ℓ_ij. The identity was also checked
symbolically (`M4_family.py`).
Each lifted ℓ_ij is a McCormick expression: L(ℓ₁₁) = w,
L(ℓ₁₀) = m_x − w, L(ℓ₀₁) = m_z − w, L(ℓ₀₀) = 1 − m_x − m_z + w. The lifts of
x(1−x) and z(1−z) are nonnegative on the pair hulls. Finally,
L((u+v)²) = eᵀ M(w) e ≥ 0, where e = (−a−c, −b, 2, −d) is the coefficient
vector of u + v in the basis (1, x, y, z). The
multipliers β − b²/2 and β' − d²/2 are nonnegative because β ≥ b² and β' ≥ d². □

*Consequence for the paper.* Report B's sentence "general dominance over SDP
or RLT is not asserted" is too weak a caveat. On this family, a relaxation
with the dense moment matrix and McCormick inequalities for the nonedge
product xz proves the exact cut D ≥ δ²/2. It uses a single PSD direction
(u+v) and no triangle inequalities. The star cut's advantage here is only
that it needs neither the nonedge coordinate nor an SDP. It is not a gain in
strength. A numerical SDP run (`M4_numeric_explore.out`) agrees. On the
1/128 example D, its reduced form D̃, and three further members (including
b < 0 and d < 0), the bound is 0 with pair hulls only, 0 with dense PSD but
without McCormick(xz), and 0 with McCormick(xz) but without dense PSD. With
both, it equals the global value.

## 5. k-moment gluing on a scalar separator

**Setting.** Let K_L ⊂ ℝ^p × ℝ and K_R ⊂ ℝ × ℝ^q be nonempty compact sets
with points (x,y) and (y,z). The feature maps φ_L, φ_R are continuous. Their
first k coordinates are y, y², ..., y^k (the shared separator moments), and
they may contain any further local features ψ_L(x,y), ψ_R(y,z). Let
H_L = conv φ_L(K_L), H_R = conv φ_R(K_R), and
R_k = {(h_L,h_R) ∈ H_L × H_R : (h_L)_j = (h_R)_j for j = 1, ..., k}.
Let D_L be affine in φ_L with D_L ≥ 0 on K_L, and likewise D_R. Let
Z_L = {D_L = 0}, A = π_y(Z_L), Z_R = {D_R = 0} and C = π_y(Z_R). These sets
are compact. Let K = {(x,y,z): (x,y) ∈ K_L, (y,z) ∈ K_R}, and set the minimum
over an empty set to +∞. Write M_k(t) = (t, t², ..., t^k).

**Theorem C1 (zero witnesses).**
1. min_K (D_L + D_R) = 0 iff A ∩ C ≠ ∅. If A ∩ C = ∅ and K ≠ ∅, this minimum
   is positive.
2. R_k contains a point with L(D) = 0 iff there exist probability measures α
   on A and γ on C with ∫t^j dα = ∫t^j dγ for j = 1, ..., k. Such measures
   may be taken finitely supported. Equivalently,
   conv M_k(A) ∩ conv M_k(C) ≠ ∅. Since L(D) ≥ 0 on R_k, this is the
   condition for the R_k bound to be 0.
3. Hence R_k reports bound 0 while the true minimum is positive iff A ∩ C = ∅
   and the condition in (2) holds. This condition depends only on A, C and k.
   Enlarging the local features, even to all continuous functions on each
   block (the full local measure space), cannot repair the failure. Only the
   shared separator statistics matter.

*Proof.* (1) If t ∈ A ∩ C, pick (x,t) ∈ Z_L and (t,z) ∈ Z_R; then
(x,t,z) ∈ K and D = 0. Conversely, D = 0 forces both nonnegative terms to
vanish, so y ∈ A ∩ C. Positivity follows from compactness of K and continuity
of D.
(2) (⇐) Lift each atom t of α to some (x_t,t) ∈ Z_L, and the atoms of γ in the
same way. The resulting moment vectors lie in H_L and H_R (Fact 0), agree in
the first k coordinates, and give L(D) = 0.
(⇒) Write h_L = Σλ_iφ_L(p_i) with λ_i > 0. Then L(D) = 0 forces p_i ∈ Z_L
(Fact 0). Take α = Σλ_iδ_{y(p_i)} and define γ in the same way.
(3) This follows from (1) and (2). □

The *alternation number* alt(A,C) of disjoint nonempty sets is the largest r
for which there are points t₀ < ... < t_r in A ∪ C such that consecutive
points lie in different sets.

**Theorem C2 (alternation criterion).** Let A, C ⊂ ℝ be disjoint, nonempty
and compact. Then alt(A,C) is finite, and probability measures on A and C
with equal moments of orders 1..k exist iff alt(A,C) ≥ k+1. In particular:
- if |A| + |C| ≤ k+1, such measures never exist;
- if A and C are finite with |A| + |C| = k+2, they exist iff A and C alternate
  in sorted order.

When alt(A,C) ≥ k+1, take any alternating chain t₀ < ... < t_{k+1} and set
ω(t) = Π_j (t − t_j). Put mass proportional to 1/|ω'(t_i)| on t_i, and
normalize separately on the A-points and on the C-points. This gives such
measures, and on these k+2 nodes they are the only ones.

*Proof.* Let T = A ∪ C, with label λ(t) = +1 on A and −1 on C, and let
ε = dist(A,C) > 0.

*Greedy runs.* Let s₀ = min T. If the set
O_i = {t ∈ T : t > s_i, λ(t) ≠ λ(s_i)} is nonempty, let s_{i+1} = min O_i.
This minimum exists because O_i = T_{−λ(s_i)} ∩ [s_i+ε, ∞) is compact.
Moreover s_{i+1} ≥ s_i + ε, so the process stops at some s_r. By minimality,
every point of T in [s_i, s_{i+1}) has label λ(s_i), and every point of T in
[s_r, ∞) has label λ(s_r). Every alternating chain visits these r+1 runs in
strictly increasing order, so alt(A,C) = r < ∞.

*Separating polynomial.* For i = 1, ..., r, the set
T ∩ [s_{i−1}, s_i) = T_{λ(s_{i−1})} ∩ [s_{i−1}, s_i − ε] is compact. Let e_i be
its maximum and choose ρ_i ∈ (e_i, s_i). Then T ∩ (e_i, s_i) = ∅, and
ρ₁ < s₁ ≤ e₂ < ρ₂ < .... Let p(t) = λ(s₀) Π_{i=1}^r (ρ_i − t). A point t ∈ T in
run i satisfies ρ₁, ..., ρ_i < t < ρ_{i+1}, ..., ρ_r. Hence
sign p(t) = λ(s₀)(−1)^i = λ(t). So p, of degree r, is strictly positive on A
and strictly negative on C.

(⇒) If alt(A,C) = r ≤ k, then deg p ≤ k, so matched moments give
∫p dα = ∫p dγ. But ∫p dα > 0 > ∫p dγ, a contradiction.

(⇐) Take an alternating chain t₀ < ... < t_{k+1} and w_i = 1/ω'(t_i). The
coefficient of t^{k+1} in the Lagrange interpolant of f is Σ_i f(t_i)w_i. For
f = t^j with j ≤ k the interpolant is f itself, so Σ_i w_i t_i^j = 0 for
j = 0, ..., k. Also sign w_i = (−1)^{k+1−i}, so the signs strictly alternate.
Choose the sign of w so that it is positive exactly on the A-points; the
labels alternate along the chain. Let S = Σ_{w_i>0} w_i, which also equals
Σ_{w_i<0} |w_i| (the j = 0 equation). Define α = Σ_{t_i∈A}(w_i/S)δ_{t_i} and
γ = Σ_{t_i∈C}(|w_i|/S)δ_{t_i}. Uniqueness on these nodes: the (k+1)×(k+2)
Vandermonde matrix has full row rank, so its kernel is the line through w.

The special cases follow because alt ≤ |A| + |C| − 1, with equality iff the
labels alternate. □

**Theorem C3 (explicit construction for every k).** Let r ≥ 1 and
k ≤ 2^{r+1} − 2. For instance, r = ⌈log₂(k+2)⌉ − 1 when k ≥ 1; r = 1 covers
k ≤ 2. Use r leaves per side,
x, z ∈ [0,1]^r, y ∈ [0, 2^{r+1} − 1], and
```
D_L(x,y) = (y − Σ_{j=1}^r 2^j x_j)² + Σ_j 4^j x_j(1−x_j),
D_R(y,z) = (y − 1 − Σ_j 2^j z_j)² + Σ_j 4^j z_j(1−z_j).
```
The required local features are y, y², ..., y^{max(k,2)}, x_j, y x_j and
x_i x_j (i < j), with the analogous features on the right; the first k powers
of y are shared. Then:
1. D_L is multilinear in x, and min_x D_L(x,y) = dist(y,A)², where
   A = {0, 2, ..., 2^{r+1}−2}. Likewise C = {1, 3, ..., 2^{r+1}−1}.
2. min_K D = 1/2.
3. R_k contains a point with L(D) = 0. An explicit one: on t_i = i for
   i = 0, ..., k+1, take α_i = C(k+1,i)/2^k for even i and γ_i = C(k+1,i)/2^k
   for odd i. Lift t to the leaves by the binary digits of t/2 on the left and
   of (t−1)/2 on the right.

So for every k, gluing exact local hulls on k separator moments has bound 0
while the true value is 1/2: the relative gap is 100%.

*Proof.* (1) The x_j² coefficient is 4^j − 4^j = 0, so D_L is affine in each
leaf. A multilinear function on a box attains its minimum at a vertex. At the
vertex 1_S, D_L = (y − Σ_{j∈S}2^j)². These subset sums are exactly the even
numbers up to 2^{r+1} − 2. Since each b_j ≠ 0, Z_L consists of binary points.
(2) By (1), min_K D = min_y [dist(y,A)² + dist(y,C)²]. Here δ = 1 and every
midpoint lies in the interval, so the value is 1/2 (as in Theorem A(1)).
(3) The k-th finite-difference identity Σ_i (−1)^i C(k+1,i) P(i) = 0 for
deg P ≤ k shows that α and γ have equal moments of orders 0, ..., k. The total
mass of each is 2^k/2^k = 1. Equivalently, these are the weights of Theorem C2
on integer nodes. Then apply Theorem C1(2). □

For r = 1, 2, 3 this was verified exactly (`M4_alternation.py`): multilinearity,
min over leaves equal to dist², lifted D = 0 and matched moments up to
k = 2, 6, 14. Theorem C2 was checked on 8002 labelled random instances in
exact arithmetic, with an explicit measure in each feasible case and an
explicit separating polynomial in each infeasible case.

**Proposition C4 (asymptotic exactness).** Assume K ≠ ∅. Let m_k be the
minimum of L(D) over R_k, computed with any fixed local features plus
y, ..., y^k. Then m_k is nondecreasing and m_k → min_K D as k → ∞.

*Proof.* By Fact 0, m_k equals the minimum of ∫D_L dμ_L + ∫D_R dμ_R over
Borel probability measures μ_L on K_L and μ_R on K_R whose y-moments of
orders 1..k agree. The constraint sets decrease in k, and m_k ≤ min_K D
(Dirac measures). The space P(K_L) × P(K_R) is weak-* compact, the
constraints are closed and the objective is continuous, so minimizers
(μ_L^k, μ_R^k) exist. Take a weak-* convergent subsequence. In the limit
(μ_L, μ_R) all y-moments agree, so the two y-marginals coincide: compactly
supported measures on ℝ are determined by their moments (Weierstrass). By
Lasserre 2006, Lemma 6.3, they glue to a probability measure μ on K, and
∫D dμ = lim m_k. Hence lim m_k ≥ min_K D. □

The convergence is not uniform over instances (Theorem C3). For fixed A, C,
the absence of a zero witness (k ≥ alt(A,C)) does not by itself give
exactness (Section 3, Remark (iii)).

*Remarks.*
(a) The same proof of C1 works for any finite list g = (g₁, ..., g_k) of
continuous shared separator statistics: a zero witness exists iff
conv g(A) ∩ conv g(C) ≠ ∅. This covers the extra statistic q² in
overlap-review.md. Suppose (1, g₁, ..., g_k) is a Chebyshev (Haar) system on
an interval. The (⇐) half of C2 still holds: the kernel vector of the
collocation matrix strictly alternates, by Cramer's rule and the constant
sign of ordered Haar determinants. The (⇒) half needs generalized polynomials
with prescribed sign changes, which is classical for continuous T-systems
(Karlin–Studden 1966). I did not re-derive or source-check that half here, so
it should not be claimed without a source check.
(b) The fact that a signed measure annihilating polynomials of degree ≤ k
changes sign at least k+1 times is classical. Theorem C2 is an elementary
consequence. Its role here, as the exact criterion for when gluing on
separator moments fails, is the point of the development.

## 6. Full-marginal gluing and pure bilinear forests (Report B subsection)

**Proposition D (Lasserre 2006, Lemmas 6.3–6.4; tree form).** Let T be a tree
whose nodes are index sets I₁, ..., I_p ⊂ [n] with the running-intersection
property: for each variable, the blocks containing it form a subtree. Let
K_t ⊂ ℝ^{I_t} be compact and μ_t a Borel probability measure on K_t. Assume
that for every tree edge {s,t}, μ_s and μ_t have the same marginal on
I_s ∩ I_t. Then there is a Borel probability measure μ on
K = {x : x_{I_t} ∈ K_t for all t} whose marginal on each I_t is μ_t.

*Proof.* (i) *All overlaps agree.* If I_s ∩ I_t = J ≠ ∅, running intersection
puts J in every block on the tree path from s to t. Consecutive blocks on the
path agree on their common index set, which contains J, so μ_s and μ_t agree
on J.
(ii) *Order.* List the blocks in breadth-first order from a root, and let
W_{m−1} be the union of the first m−1 blocks. For the m-th block t with parent
π, Δ := I_t ∩ W_{m−1} = I_t ∩ I_π. Indeed, if j ∈ I_t ∩ I_s for an earlier s,
then the path from s to t passes through π, so j ∈ I_π.
(iii) *Glue.* By induction, μ^{(m−1)} on ℝ^{W_{m−1}} has the right marginals,
and its marginal on Δ equals that of μ_π, which equals that of μ_t. Euclidean
spaces are standard Borel, so disintegrate μ_t = ν_Δ(dx_Δ) q(dx_{I_t∖Δ} | x_Δ)
(Lasserre, Prop. 6.1). Set
μ^{(m)} = μ^{(m−1)}(dx_{W_{m−1}}) q(dx_{I_t∖Δ} | x_Δ). Its marginals are
μ^{(m−1)} on W_{m−1} and μ_t on I_t; this is Lasserre's Lemma 6.3.
(iv) *Support.* For each t, μ({x_{I_t} ∉ K_t}) = 0. A finite union of null
sets is null, so μ(K) = 1. □

Report B's paragraph matches this argument. Lasserre's Lemma 6.4 assumes
consistency on all intersecting pairs together with ordering (1.3); step (i)
shows that adjacent-edge agreement implies it. For compact supports, "same
complete marginal" is equivalent to "all moments agree" (Lasserre p. 18).
Matching finitely many moments is not enough in general (Section 5). It is
enough when both local separator projections lie in a common finite set
T ⊂ ℝ and moments of orders 0..|T|−1 are shared (Vandermonde), as Report B
states. Theorem C2 gives a different refinement that depends on the
objective: if the local zero sets project to A and C, then sharing moments of
orders 1..k with k ≥ alt(A,C) already excludes zero witnesses. It does not in
general force equal marginals or an exact bound.

**Proposition E (pure bilinear forest).** Let G = (V,E) be a forest and
x ∈ [0,1]^V. The McCormick relaxation of
{(x, (x_ix_j)_{ij∈E}) : x ∈ [0,1]^V} is its convex hull.

*Proof.* Let (m,w) satisfy McCormick on every edge. For an edge ij, the four
numbers p₁₁ = w_ij, p₁₀ = m_i − w_ij, p₀₁ = m_j − w_ij and
p₀₀ = 1 − m_i − m_j + w_ij are nonnegative exactly by McCormick and sum to 1.
They define a measure on {0,1}² whose marginal at i is Bernoulli(m_i). Induct
on |V|. If some vertex j is isolated, multiply the measure for G − j by
Bernoulli(m_j). Otherwise remove a leaf j with neighbor i, and glue the
measure for G − j (by induction) with the edge measure along x_i; both have
marginal Bernoulli(m_i) at i (Proposition D). The result is a measure on {0,1}^V with the
required means and edge products, so (m,w) lies in the hull of vertex graph
points. The converse inclusion holds because McCormick is valid. □

This result is classical: Padberg 1989, Prop. 8, gives
QP^G = QP^G_LP iff G is acyclic, for the Boolean quadric polytope. The hull
over the box equals the hull over the vertices because the map is multilinear.
The paper should cite Padberg and not present Proposition E as new. Report B's
closing remark is correct. In v̄ the separator has s_y = 5/16 ≠ m_y = 1/2, so
its y-marginal cannot be binary, and the forest argument does not apply.

## 7. Corollary `cor:gain` (Report B star.tex)

*Statement check.* P_i are compact polygons in (y, x_i), and
S = {(y,x) : (y,x_i) ∈ P_i ∀i} ≠ ∅. Let β_i = min_{P_i} q_i,
β_* = min_S Σ q_i and Δ = β_* − Σβ_i, and let M_i = argmin_{P_i} q_i.
- Δ ≥ 0 because each q_i ≥ β_i on P_i.
- The best constant c in Σq_i ≥ c on S is β_*. So Δ is the largest constant
  improvement over the sum of the separate pair inequalities.
- Δ = 0 iff ∩_i π_y(M_i) ≠ ∅. (⇐) Take a common y* and (y*, x_i) ∈ M_i for
  each i; the point lies in S and has value Σβ_i. (⇒) A minimizer of the star
  problem exists (S is compact). If its value is Σβ_i, every term equals its
  minimum, so its y lies in every π_y(M_i).
- Exact computability: each β_i is a pair support problem (polygon oracle, or
  the star oracle with one leaf). β_* is within the star class: rows involve
  y and one leaf, and the q_i are general quadratics in (y, x_i).

The corollary is **correct**. The proof in star.tex is complete, if terse.

*Needed clarification.* Δ compares with separately added pair cuts, not with
the glued pair-hull relaxation R of Section 1. Since L(q_i) ≥ β_i on each
pair hull, the R-gap β_* − min_R L(Σq_i) lies in [0, Δ]. Both extremes occur:
- the 1/128 example gives Δ = R-gap = 1/128 (strict interleaving);
- the nested member A = {0,3}, C = {1,2} (a = 0, b = 3, c = 1, d = 1,
  y ∈ [0,3]) gives Δ = 1/2 but R-gap = 0. The quadratic multiplier
  p(y) = −(1/2)(y − 3/2)² certifies min_R = 1/2 (`M4_star_oracle.py`).

The sentence after the corollary ("In the example below, both pair minima
are zero and the exact gain is 1/128") is correct. A positive Δ does not
imply that the star cut separates a point of R.

## 8. Literature positioning for N2

Sources checked in the local knowledge base:

- **Dey and Khajavirad 2025** (arXiv:2508.18435), Corollary 1 (p. 9): PP(G)
  decomposes over sections G₁, G₂ when G₁ ∩ G₂ is complete *and has no plus
  loops*. Theorem 2 (p. 20): PP(G) is SOC-representable when the plus-loop
  nodes form a stable set. The proof uses the neighborhood block N(i) of each
  plus-loop node (Prop. 5, p. 11), so the center and all its neighbors form a
  single block.
- **Khajavirad 2026** (arXiv:2601.18545), Lemma 4 (p. 6) restates this
  corollary.
- **Del Pia and Khajavirad 2018** (Math. Program. 170), Theorem 1 (p. 3):
  multilinear sets decompose over complete separators. The proof uses the
  unique convex representation on the separator's simplex.

The reduced example (3) uses only the plus loop y² and the two edges. In
their notation it shows that **the no-plus-loop hypothesis of Corollary 1
cannot be dropped**, even for a three-node path with one plus loop at the
center and no other loops. To see this, let G be the path x–y–z with
L = L⁺ = {yy}. Then v̄' = (1/2, 1/2, 4/5, 3/8, 1/2, 5/16) lies in
PP(G₁) ∩ PP(G₂), because the exact pair-hull points satisfy z_yy = y² ≥ y².
Yet L(D̃)(v̄') = 0, while min_{PP(G)} L(D̃) = 1/128 > 0. The minimum over PP(G)
is attained at z_yy = y² because the coefficient of z_yy is +2. I found no
such explicit witness in these papers; their necessity remark concerns
another hypothesis (Observation 6, p. 19, on Theorem 1's single plus loop).

The mechanism is the one Del Pia and Khajavirad identify: with a plus loop,
the separator's local hull conv{(y, y²)} is not a simplex, so convex
representations on it are not unique. Box-only star hulls whose plus loops
form a stable set are SOC-representable by Dey–Khajavirad Theorem 2. This
covers a plus loop at the center only, or plus loops at leaves only, but not
both. Box-only star support is also a special case of the Del Pia–Khajavirad
forest QP algorithm (already cited in Report B).

Recommended claim wording: an explicit, exactly quantified obstruction for
gluing exact pair hulls on separator moments (Theorems A and C1–C3), with the
criterion "zero witness iff alt(A,C) ≥ k+1". Do not claim that the
phenomenon of non-decomposability on non-simplex separators is new. Do not
claim an advantage over dense SDP with the nonedge product and its
McCormick inequalities (Theorem B').
