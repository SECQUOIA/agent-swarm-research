\section{Complete separation to a positive tolerance}\label{sec:separation}

An exact support oracle evaluates one direction. A separation routine
must also find a direction, and the bounded searches used inside a solver
can miss one. This section shows that, given an exact or approximate
support oracle, the search can be completed in the following weak sense:
for every query and every positive tolerance, a finite exact procedure
either returns a valid cut that the query violates or certifies that the
query is within the tolerance of the hull. The tools are classical, and
we do not claim the principle as new. We state the result because the
answer contract matters for a certified implementation, and because it
shows that the remaining difficulty of separation is cost, not a missing
case.

\subsection{Distance and the support function}

Let $P\subset\R^d$ be a nonempty bounded rational polytope, let
$F:\R^d\to\R^k$ be continuous, let $\bar z\in\Q^k$ be the query and let
$\epsilon>0$ be rational. To cover the aggregated cuts of
Section~\ref{sec:original}, a set $J\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}$ of coordinates
may be one-sided: we separate from
\[
U_J=\conv F(P)+\cone\{e_j:j\in J\}.
\]
For $F=(x,g(x))$, the query $(\bar x,b-A\bar v)$ and $J$ equal to the
coordinates of $g$, this is the set $K+Q$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}.
Directions are taken from $C_J=\{c\in[-1,1]^k:c_j\ge0\ (j\in J)\}$; we write
$h(c)=\min_{x\in P}c\T F(x)$, $\psi(c)=h(c)-c\T\bar z$ and
$\phi_J(w)=\sum_{j\notin J}\abs{w_j}+\sum_{j\in J}\max\{w_j,0\}$, and we let
$R\ge\max_{x\in P}\norm{F(x)-\bar z}_1$ be a rational bound, obtained for
example from interval enclosures of the $F_j$.

\begin{lemma}[Distance duality]\label{lem:distance}
$\dist_1(\bar z,U_J)=\min_{w\in\conv F(P)}\phi_J(w-\bar z)=\max_{c\in C_J}\psi(c)$,
and both extrema are attained. Moreover
$\abs{\psi(c)-\psi(c')}\le R\norm{c-c'}_\infty$ for all $c,c'\in C_J$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The set $\conv F(P)$ is compact, so $U_J$ is closed and convex (as in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}). Adding a cone vector reduces the
distance in a coordinate $j\in J$ to $\max\{w_j-\bar z_j,0\}$, which gives
the first expression. For the second, $c\T(z-\bar z)\ge\psi(c)$ for every
$z\in U_J$ and $c\in C_J$, while $c\T(z-\bar z)\le\norm{z-\bar z}_1$; hence
$\psi(c)\le\dist_1(\bar z,U_J)$. If the distance $\rho$ is positive, a
linear functional separates $U_J$ from the open $\ell_1$ ball of radius
$\rho$ around $\bar z$; scaled to $\ell_\infty$ norm one, it lies in $C_J$
because its infimum on $U_J$ is finite, and it has $\psi(c)\ge\rho$. The
Lipschitz bound holds pointwise for every $x\in P$ and survives taking
minima.
\end{proof}

An \emph{oracle of accuracy} $\delta\in[0,\epsilon)$ returns, for a rational
$c\in C_J$, a rational lower bound $L_c\le h(c)$ and a point $p_c\in P$ with
$c\T F(p_c)\le L_c+\delta$. For quadratic features, Theorem~\ref{thm:polytope}
is such an oracle with $\delta=0$; for polynomials of higher degree,
Lemma~\ref{lem:net} below gives one with any prescribed $\delta>0$.

\subsection{Column generation}

The procedure we analyze is the one used in practice, with exact
arithmetic: maintain a finite set $W$ of points $w=F(p)-\bar z$ with
$p\in P$; solve the \emph{master LP}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:master}
\mathrm{UB}=\max_{c\in C_J}\min_{w\in W}c\T w
=\min\Bigl\{\phi_J\Bigl(\sum_{w\in W}\lambda_ww\Bigr):\lambda\ge0,\ \textstyle\sum_w\lambda_w=1\Bigr\}
\end{equation}
in exact rational arithmetic, obtaining an optimal direction $\bar c$ and
weights $\lambda$; if $\mathrm{UB}\le\epsilon$, stop with the convex
combination as a \emph{within-tolerance} certificate; otherwise call the
oracle at $\bar c$; if $L_{\bar c}>\bar c\T\bar z$, return the cut
$\bar c\T z\ge L_{\bar c}$; otherwise add $F(p_{\bar c})-\bar z$ to $W$ and
repeat. The two problems in~\eqref{eq:master} are an LP and its dual. This
is the separation scheme of Fenchel cuts and local cuts
\citep{Boyd1994,ChvatalCookEspinoza2013}, in our norm and with cone
coordinates.

\begin{theorem}[Complete weak separation]\label{thm:weak-separation}
The procedure terminates after at most
$2k\lceil2R/(\epsilon-\delta)\rceil^{k-1}$ oracle calls. A returned cut is
valid for $U_J$ and violated at $\bar z$; it is returned whenever
$\dist_1(\bar z,U_J)>\epsilon$. A within-tolerance answer consists of at
most $k+1$ rational points of $P$ and rational weights whose mixture $w$
satisfies $\phi_J(w-\bar z)\le\epsilon$, so $\dist_1(\bar z,U_J)\le\epsilon$;
it is returned whenever $\bar z\in U_J$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Validity of a returned cut follows from $L_{\bar c}\le h(\bar c)$ and
$\bar c_j\ge0$ on the cone coordinates. The master value $\mathrm{UB}$ is
the value $\phi_J$ of a mixture of graph points, hence at least the
distance; a basic optimal dual solution has at most $k+1$ positive weights.
If the distance exceeds $\epsilon$, the procedure can therefore stop only
with a cut.

For termination, let $\bar c_1,\bar c_2,\dots$ be the directions at which the
oracle is called and $w_s$ the point added after call $s$. Since no cut was
returned at call $s$, $\bar c_s\T w_s\le L_{\bar c_s}+\delta-\bar c_s\T\bar z\le\delta$.
At a later call $t$, $w_s\in W$ and $\mathrm{UB}>\epsilon$, so
$\bar c_t\T w_s>\epsilon$. Hence $(\bar c_t-\bar c_s)\T w_s>\epsilon-\delta$, and
since $\norm{w_s}_1\le R$, $\norm{\bar c_t-\bar c_s}_\infty>(\epsilon-\delta)/R$.
Because $\mathrm{UB}>0$ and the master objective is positively homogeneous,
every $\bar c_t$ has a coordinate equal to $\pm1$ (equal to $1$ on $J$), so
it lies on one of at most $2k$ faces of $C_J$, each a product of $k-1$
intervals of length at most two. Covering each face by
$\lceil2R/(\epsilon-\delta)\rceil^{k-1}$ boxes of side at most
$(\epsilon-\delta)/R$ bounds the number of calls.
\end{proof}

The bound is exponential in $k$, but this is a property of the analysis,
not of the problem. The set of directions $C_J$ is a full-dimensional box
even when $\conv F(P)$ is lower-dimensional, so the central-cut ellipsoid
method can localize a good direction in it. Using the oracle answers
$-w/\norm w_\infty$ as cuts in direction space and finishing with the master
LP~\eqref{eq:master} over all recorded points gives the same two outcomes
after $O(k^2\log(kR/(\epsilon-\delta)))$ oracle calls
(Appendix~\ref{app:ellipsoid}). For quadratic features on a
polytope of fixed dimension, the oracle of Theorem~\ref{thm:polytope} is
polynomial, so weak separation with an exactly valid cut is then
polynomial in the input length and $\log(1/\epsilon)$. For the Euclidean
distance, Gilbert's nearest-point method gives yet another variant, with a
number of oracle calls that is polynomial in $1/\epsilon$ and does not depend
on $k$ \citep{Gilbert1966,BrierleyNavascuesVertesi2016}.

\paragraph{Relation to the ellipsoid method.}
In the oracle framework of \citet{GrotschelLovaszSchrijver1988}, weak
separation is obtained from weak optimization for every convex body,
without radius information. Convex bodies are full-dimensional by
definition there, however, and the weak separation problem asks only for a
hyperplane that is almost valid on the points deep inside the body. For a
lower-dimensional hull this requirement is vacuous, and an almost valid
hyperplane is not a cut. The contract above is different: the cut side is
exact, only the membership side is approximate, and lower-dimensional
hulls are allowed.

\subsection{Polynomials of higher degree}

For polynomial features, an exactly feasible sample net turns a support
problem into a finite one.

\begin{lemma}[Rational domain net]\label{lem:net}
Let $v_1,\dots,v_s$ be the vertices of $P$, let
$D_\infty=\max_i(\max_Px_i-\min_Px_i)$, let $F$ be continuously
differentiable on a box $B\supseteq P$, and let
$\Lambda\ge\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{i=1}^d\max_{x\in B}\abs{\partial_iF_j(x)}$. For an
integer $M\ge1$ let $S_M=\{\sum_i(n_i/M)v_i:n_i\in\Z_{\ge0},\ \sum_in_i=M\}\subset P$.
Then for every $c\in[-1,1]^k$,
\[
\min_{x\in S_M}c\T F(x)-\frac{\Lambda\,t\,D_\infty}M\ \le\ \min_{x\in P}c\T F(x)\ \le\ \min_{x\in S_M}c\T F(x),
\qquad t=\min\{s-1,\dim P\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Carath\'eodory's theorem in the affine hull of $P$, a point $x\in P$ is
a convex combination $\sum_{i\le t+1}\alpha_iv_{a_i}$ of at most $t+1$
vertices. Put $n_{a_i}=\lfloor M\alpha_i\rfloor$ for $i\le t$ and give the
remaining mass to $v_{a_{t+1}}$. The resulting $\hat x\in S_M$ satisfies
$x-\hat x=\sum_{i\le t}(\alpha_i-n_{a_i}/M)(v_{a_i}-v_{a_{t+1}})$, so
$\norm{x-\hat x}_\infty\le tD_\infty/M$. By the mean value theorem, $c\T F$
is $\Lambda$-Lipschitz for the $\ell_\infty$ norm on $B$. Apply this at a
minimizer over $P$; the upper bound holds because $S_M\subset P$.
\end{proof}

All points of $S_M$ are exactly feasible, including on lower-dimensional
polytopes, where a rectangular floating-point grid would almost never meet
an equality exactly. With $M\ge2\Lambda tD_\infty/\epsilon$ the net is an
oracle of accuracy $\epsilon/2$; the lower bound is
$\min_{S_M}c\T F-\Lambda tD_\infty/M$, and $\Lambda$ and $R$ come from rational
interval evaluation. The net has at most $\binom{M+s-1}{s-1}$ points, which
is far too many for routine use; this is consistent with the strong
NP-hardness of box-constrained quartic minimization on paths
\citep{DelPiaKhajavirad2026}.

\subsection{Outcomes and implementation}

The procedure returns \emph{cut} with an exact rational row, \emph{within
tolerance} with a mixture certificate, or \emph{empty domain} when exact
vertex enumeration of $P$ finds no vertex. A bounded variant, which caps
the number of oracle calls and net points, may in addition return
\emph{unresolved}, which carries no geometric information. The evidence
for the outcomes differs: a cut needs a lower bound over the whole domain,
whereas a within-tolerance answer needs only feasible points and exact
arithmetic, and each is checked accordingly. A rational cut can be
exported in binary64 with the correction~\eqref{eq:bound-correction},
using enclosures of the features; rounding to nearest preserves the signs
of the cone coefficients, and the export records whether the rounded row
still separates, because a very small violation can be lost.

Our standalone separation routine solves the master LP numerically,
makes the returned weights nonnegative and renormalizes them in exact
arithmetic, checks the resulting mixture exactly, and certifies every
proposed direction with the exact oracle. An inaccurate LP can therefore
waste work but cannot produce a wrong answer. Completeness is guaranteed
by a grid fallback over $C_J$, with values $-1+2i/N$ in the free coordinates,
$i/N$ in the cone coordinates, $i=0,\dots,N$, and
$N=\lceil2R/(\epsilon-\delta)\rceil$. Every direction is within $\ell_\infty$
distance $1/N$ of this grid, so if no grid direction yields a cut,
Lemma~\ref{lem:distance} bounds $\max_{C_J}\psi$ by $\delta+R/N\le\epsilon$; the
grid is twice as fine as necessary, and directions with $\norm c_\infty<1$
could be skipped. An exact
reference implementation of the column-generation procedure, with a
rational simplex method for~\eqref{eq:master}, is part of the
supplementary verification scripts; on the test instances it needed at
most six oracle calls, while the bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-separation}
ranged from $10^2$ to $10^{14}$. The SCIP separator of
Section~\ref{sec:implementation} does not run the complete procedure; it
uses a fixed budget and never interprets an unsuccessful search as
membership.

For aggregated cuts, a returned rational row maps to an original-variable
cut through Proposition~\ref{prop:aggregate}. A within-tolerance answer
bounds a distance in the lifted coordinates $(x,g)$, not a distance in the
original variables to the set $\mathcal C$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}.
