\begin{abstract}
Global solvers for nonconvex mixed-integer nonlinear programs relax
nonlinear expressions one at a time, which loses the dependence among
expressions that share variables and linear constraints. The convex hull of
the joint graph of such expressions is described by support cuts, whose
constants are minima of weighted sums of the expressions over their common
domain. We study how to compute, certify and use these cuts, with emphasis
on quadratic blocks. A cut with numerically chosen coefficients is valid if
a lower bound over the whole domain is certified for the exact binary64
coefficients that the solver stores. Eliminating the model rows exactly
gives cuts in the original variables, without auxiliary variables, whose
closure is the projected joint-graph relaxation, and bound-corrected
rounding makes the eliminated row safe. For quadratic blocks we give exact
rational support oracles for polytopes of fixed dimension and, with
$O((m+k)\log(m+k))$ operations, for stars with $k$ leaves and $m$ affine rows
that couple the center with one leaf. We show that exact pair hulls glued
on shared moments do not compose: on a path of three variables the gap is
either zero or $\delta^2/2$, depending only on whether two point sets
interleave, and an interface that shares $k$ moments misses the gap exactly
when the local zero sets alternate at least $k+1$ times. An exact finite
procedure completes the separation to any positive tolerance. A SCIP
implementation with replayable certificates was evaluated in three
campaigns on MINLPLib models. Every recorded cut passed replay, but the
cuts did not change which models SCIP solved and made the solves slower.
On a constructed family with the path structure, the cuts closed SCIP's
root gap and solved all instances, against 9 of 20 for native SCIP within
300 seconds, once the direction search tried each row's own direction.
\end{abstract}
