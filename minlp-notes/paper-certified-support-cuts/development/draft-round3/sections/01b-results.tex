Section~\ref{sec:computations} reports five campaigns, each run with solver
code, limits and protocol fixed in advance. All 274{,}489 cut records of the
last three campaigns passed a fresh-process replay, which re-executes the exact
support routines against the source model and the row stored by SCIP.
Constants taken from the separator's samples would have been wrong for 38\% of
the cuts on MINLPLib models and for almost all sampled path-family cuts, and
their rows would have cut off feasible solutions; local minimization reduced
the share of wrong constants to a few percent but still left rows that cut off
known optima, and even a numerical global solver once reported optimality for a
wrong constant. On MINLPLib models the cuts did not change which models SCIP
solved in campaigns 2 to~4 and left SCIP's own solving time unchanged; the
Python separator made the runs slower, and SCIP's disabled-by-default
separators improved the root bound more often. On 20 larger models that SCIP
does not solve within 60 seconds, the separator with default limits stopped
during block discovery on 12, and with raised limits its cuts improved one root
bound and worsened five. On constructed instances with the path structure of
Section~\ref{sec:composition}, SCIP's root bound is weak because of a presolve
step; with that step switched off, SCIP reaches the level of glued pair
relaxations and no further. A post hoc diagnostic and a later prospective
control showed that most of the remaining closure requires enough cuts per
block, and that trying the exact support of each whole row first supplies the
rest when the coupling row does not bind. On 20 fresh instances, this
configuration, fixed before the instances were generated, closed SCIP's root
gap and solved all 20 within 300 seconds, against at most 10 for SCIP in three
settings and 5 for Gurobi; with a binding coupling row the cuts solved all 20 as
well, but so did SCIP without its presolve step, faster. On a family of
constrained stars with up to 16 leaves, star blocks certified by the star
oracle made the root bound exact, which neither smaller blocks nor SCIP
achieved; Gurobi, which found good solutions faster, solved more of these
instances.
