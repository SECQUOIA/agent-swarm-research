\subsection{What the separator does and what it costs}\label{sec:funnel}

Table~\ref{tab:funnel} in Appendix~\ref{app:instances} follows the certified
rows from the support calls to the cuts that reach SCIP. Three observations explain most of the results of
Section~\ref{sec:minlplib}.



First, most certified supports are not violated at the LP point: on the
MINLPLib models, 65 to 86\% of the certified rows fell below the violation
threshold, because SCIP's relaxation already implied them up to the
threshold. Certification failed in few support calls. In the campaign-3 reruns
that recorded the outcome, every failure was \emph{incomplete} (a Bernstein
or ball-arithmetic bound reached its depth budget, or did not certify the
required value) or \emph{unsupported} (a quadratic block whose number of row
subsets exceeded the enumeration budget and which the fallback bounds do not
cover); none was an empty domain. In Part~4D, 307 of the 313 failures came
from one model.

Second, the stored-row check lost a substantial share of the violated rows
on MINLPLib models, mainly to presolve aggregation (Section~\ref{sec:minlplib}),
and a small share on the constructed families, where SCIP dropped coefficients
below its epsilon of $10^{-9}$ in at most 3\% of the violated rows. Each such loss would
be a silent change of a certified row if the check were absent. A separator
that declares its variables non-aggregatable before presolve avoids most of the
presolve losses; we did this only as an experiment (Part~4B2), because it
changes SCIP's own relaxation. A separator that works in the presolved space
would instead have to treat SCIP's reductions, which are made in floating
point, as part of the model that the certificate refers to. The losses to dropped and snapped coefficients could be avoided by
applying SCIP's coefficient handling in the exporter, followed by the
correction of Proposition~\ref{prop:export}.

Third, on the MINLPLib models the whole slowdown is the separator's own time.
SCIP's solving time without the callback was unchanged in every part: its
shifted geometric mean differed from SCIP's by at most 4\% in Parts~3A
and~3B, and the median per-run ratio was between 0.96 and 1.01 in Parts~4B2
and~4D. The callback cost about half a second per run in Part~3B, more than
SCIP's whole solving time on most of these models (median 0.16\,s). In
campaign~3, exact certification took 70\% of the callback time on Part~3B, the
direction search and discovery the rest; on the larger models of Part~4D,
discovery took 85\% of the callback time in mode \code{all}. All of this is
Python code; a compiled implementation would reduce it, but on these models it
would at best break even, because the cuts did not reduce SCIP's work. On the
constructed families, in contrast, the cuts reduced SCIP's own time sharply
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}), and the callback was the dominant remaining cost.
