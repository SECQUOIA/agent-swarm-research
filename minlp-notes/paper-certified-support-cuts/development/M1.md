Development results, by task:

1. **Clean closure theorem (Theorem 4.1).** It covers inequality rows,
   equality rows, affine remainders acting on all model variables
   including block variables, and the objective epigraph, all at once,
   through one affine map. Extensions:
   - Proposition 4.6: lower semicontinuity suffices for inequality
     features. For an arbitrary domain, the closure is the preimage of a
     closed convex hull.
   - Corollary 4.7: a finite-mixture ("rows in expectation") form.
   - Corollary 4.8: an aggregated-envelope form, which generalizes
     Ballerstein's description as cited by Liers et al.
   - Corollary 4.9: original-row cuts have the same closure as lifted
     atom-level support cuts.
2. **Single row (Theorem 5.2): yes, exactly.** For one inequality row the
   closure equals `{Sw ∈ conv D, vex_D g(Sw) + Aw ≤ b}`. For one equality
   row it is the set between the convex and concave envelopes.
3. **Joint gain (Example 7.3).** On `D = [0,1]`, take the rows
   `x^2 - s ≤ 0` and `-x^3 + t ≤ 0`. The point `(1/2, 1/4, 1/2)` lies in
   both single-row envelope relaxations. The joint cut
   `-5x/4 + 2s - t ≥ -1/4` excludes it by `3/8`.
   - Proposition 7.5 gives the exact joint closure: `s ≥ x^2` and
     `t ≤ τ(x, min{s,x})`. It equals the convex hull of the two-row
     feasible set.
   - Proposition 7.1: no gain is possible when the rows split over a
     product domain.
4. **Validity scope (Theorem 8.1, Corollary 8.2, Proposition 8.3).**
   - Validity needs only two things: block projections lie in `D`, and
     the selected rows hold. Integrality and all other constraints are
     irrelevant.
   - With a larger `D`, the cuts stay valid but the closure becomes the
     relaxation for that larger `D`.
   - Integrality is seen only through `D`; a discrete compact `D` is
     allowed.
   - The closure can be strictly weaker than the feasible hull even when
     `D` equals the block projection (Example 6.4).
   - Proposition 6.1 gives a rank condition on free remainder variables
     under which the closure equals the convex hull of the selected-row
     set.
5. **Final-row contract (Theorem 9.5).** This is the complete chain from
   source rows to the exported binary64 row. Proposition 9.4 shows that
   rounding of block-coordinate coefficients can be absorbed into the
   certified direction, so variable bounds are needed only for remainder
   coordinates.

## 1. Notation and standing hypotheses

- `w ∈ R^N`: all model variables, including an objective epigraph
  variable `t` when present.
- `S ∈ R^{d×N}`: the block map, `x = Sw`. In applications `S` selects
  `d` coordinates. Only linearity is used, except in Proposition 6.1.
- `D ⊂ R^d`: the block support domain.
- `g = (g_1,…,g_m): D → R^m`: inequality features. `h = (h_1,…,h_r): D → R^r`:
  equality features. Either `m = 0` or `r = 0` is allowed.
- Selected rows:
  `g(Sw) + Aw ≤ b` and `h(Sw) + Cw = e`, with
  `A ∈ R^{m×N}`, `b ∈ R^m`, `C ∈ R^{r×N}`, `e ∈ R^r`.
  The affine remainders `Aw` and `Cw` may involve any coordinates,
  including block coordinates. In the source notation,
  `g_i(x) + ℓ_i(v) ≤ b_i` with `ℓ_i(v) = A_i w`.
- Graph, hull, and cone:
  `G_D = {(u, g(u), h(u)) : u ∈ D} ⊂ R^{d+m+r}`, `K_D = conv G_D`,
  `Q = {0_d} × R^m_+ × {0_r}`.
- Affine map `T(w) = (Sw, b − Aw, e − Cw)`. Joint graph relaxation
  `R_D = T^{-1}(K_D + Q)`, that is,
  `R_D = {w : ∃ (u, y, η) ∈ K_D with u = Sw, y ≤ b − Aw, η = e − Cw}`.
- Directions `Λ = R^d × R^m_+ × R^r`. For `c = (a, λ, μ) ∈ R^{d+m+r}` put
  `φ_D(c) = min_{u∈D} [aᵀu + λᵀg(u) + μᵀh(u)]` (or `inf` when the minimum
  need not exist).
- The **aggregated cut** of `c ∈ Λ` is
  `π(c)ᵀ w ≥ ρ(c)`, with
  `π(c) = Sᵀa − Aᵀλ − Cᵀμ` and `ρ(c) = φ_D(c) − λᵀb − μᵀe`.
- Selected-row set: `Σ_D = {w : Sw ∈ D, g(Sw) + Aw ≤ b, h(Sw) + Cw = e}`.
- Envelopes: `vex_D f` and `cav_D f = −vex_D(−f)` (Lemma 5.1).

Throughout, `conv` is the convex hull, `cl` the closure, and `Δ_k` the
probability simplex in `R^k`. A cut is **valid on** a set `Ω` if every
point of `Ω` satisfies it.

Section 2 keeps the local notation of foundations.tex: there `h_D(a,b)`
is the lifted support value, and `ℓ_i, u_i` in Proposition 2.6 are
bounds. Elsewhere, `h` denotes the equality features and `u` a point
of `D`.

## 2. Lifted joint support cuts and the final row (C-SUP)

### 2.1 Support and hull description

Let `D ⊂ R^n` be nonempty and compact. Let `F = (f_1,…,f_k): D → R^k` be
continuous. Put `G = {(x, F(x)) : x ∈ D}`, `H_D = conv G`, and
`h_D(a, b) = min_{x∈D} [aᵀx + bᵀF(x)]`.

**Proposition 2.1 (foundations `prop:support`).**
(i) If `L ≤ h_D(a,b)`, then `aᵀx + bᵀw ≥ L` holds on `H_D`.
(ii) `H_D = ⋂_{(a,b) ∈ R^{n+k}} {(x,w) : aᵀx + bᵀw ≥ h_D(a,b)}`.

*Proof.*
- The minimum exists because `x ↦ aᵀx + bᵀF(x)` is continuous on a
  compact set.
- (i) Every point of `G` satisfies the inequality by definition of
  `h_D`. The set of points satisfying a linear inequality is convex, so
  it contains `conv G`.
- (ii) `G` is compact, being the continuous image of `D`. Hence
  `H_D` is compact (Carathéodory: `H_D` is the image of the compact set
  `Δ_{n+k+1} × G^{n+k+1}` under a continuous map). By (i), `H_D` lies in
  the intersection.
- Conversely, let `p ∉ H_D`. Strict separation of a point from a
  nonempty closed convex set gives `c = (a,b)` with
  `cᵀp < min_{q∈H_D} cᵀq`.
- A linear function attains its minimum over `conv G` at a point of `G`,
  so this minimum equals `h_D(a,b)`. Hence `p` violates the inequality
  for `(a,b)`. ∎

**Remark 2.2 (what "valid for the model" requires).**
- A model point `(x, w)` satisfies a lifted cut when `x ∈ D` and
  `w = F(x)`. The lifted variables must therefore be *equal* to the
  functions at every target point.
- Report A enforces `w = f(x)` through SCIP's native nonlinear
  constraints (`research-20261002-convexification/document/integration.tex`,
  lines 110–112), so the hypothesis holds there.
- Suppose only `w_j ≥ f_j(x)` is enforced. Then the cut is valid only if
  `b_j ≥ 0`, because `b_j w_j ≥ b_j f_j(x)` then holds. If only
  `w_j ≤ f_j(x)` is enforced, it needs `b_j ≤ 0`.
- This sign restriction is exactly the cone `Q` of Theorem 4.1.

**Remark 2.3 (integer coordinates).** The sentence "If integer variables
occur in `D`" should read "if some block coordinates are
integer-constrained in the model". Two choices are possible:
- Support over any compact `D` that contains the block coordinates of
  all mixed-integer target points is valid.
- Taking `D` itself discrete (Proposition 8.3) is also allowed. It gives
  the hull of the mixed-integer graph.

### 2.2 The exported row

**Proposition 2.4 (foundations `prop:round`).** Let `â ∈ Q^n` and
`b̂ ∈ Q^k` be the exact rational values of the coefficients actually
submitted (finite binary64 numbers are dyadic rationals). Suppose
`L ≤ min_{x∈D} [âᵀx + b̂ᵀF(x)]` is certified and `L̂ ≤ L` is checked by
exact comparison. Then `âᵀx + b̂ᵀw ≥ L̂` holds on `H_D`.

*Proof.* Proposition 2.1(i) with `(a,b) = (â, b̂)` gives the row with
right-hand side `L`. Lowering the right-hand side keeps it valid. ∎

**Remark 2.5.** The certified object is the row with exactly these
coefficients and this right-hand side. Any later change produces a
different row, which needs its own certificate or an exact replay
against the stored row. Examples of such changes:
- dropping tiny coefficients;
- merging duplicate entries in floating point;
- scaling;
- solver-side presolve substitution.

This is a hypothesis of the statement, not a defect in it. Report B
handles it by replaying the stored SCIP row (C-IMPL). Proposition 2.4 is
the case `Δ = 0` of Proposition 9.1.

### 2.3 Bound-assisted alternative

**Proposition 2.6 (foundations, the paragraph after `prop:round`).**
Assume:
- the exact rational row `cᵀz ≥ L` holds on a set `Ω`;
- the bounds `ℓ_i ≤ z_i ≤ u_i` hold on `Ω`, with entries in `R ∪ {±∞}`;
- `Δ = ĉ − c` and
  `E = Σ_i inf_{s ∈ [ℓ_i, u_i]} Δ_i s`, where the term is `0` when
  `Δ_i = 0` (even if a bound is infinite), `Δ_i ℓ_i` when `Δ_i > 0`, and
  `Δ_i u_i` when `Δ_i < 0`;
- `E` is finite.

Then every `L̂ ≤ L + E` gives a row `ĉᵀz ≥ L̂` that is valid on `Ω`.

*Proof.* For `z ∈ Ω`, `Δ_i z_i ≥ inf_{s∈[ℓ_i,u_i]} Δ_i s`, because
`z_i ∈ [ℓ_i, u_i]`. Summing gives `Δᵀz ≥ E`. Hence
`ĉᵀz = cᵀz + Δᵀz ≥ L + E ≥ L̂`. ∎

Two remarks on applying it:
- In foundations the box is "trusted" for all lifted coordinates. For a
  lifted coordinate `w_j`, the box must contain `f_j(x)` for every
  target `x`; an outward enclosure of `f_j(D)` suffices.
- If the box contains `G` it contains `H_D`, because a box is convex. The
  corrected row is then valid on all of `H_D`.

## 3. The original-variable cut

**Proposition 3.1 (aggregation `prop:aggregate`, general form).**
Assume:
- (V1) `Ω ⊆ R^N` and `D' ⊆ R^d` is any set on which `g` and `h` are
  real-valued (with the source semantics) and `S(Ω) ⊆ D'`;
- (V2) every `w ∈ Ω` satisfies the selected rows `g(Sw) + Aw ≤ b` and
  `h(Sw) + Cw = e`.

Let `c = (a, λ, μ) ∈ Λ`, so `λ ≥ 0` exactly, and let `β ∈ R` satisfy
`β ≤ inf_{u∈D'} [aᵀu + λᵀg(u) + μᵀh(u)]`. Then
`π(c)ᵀw ≥ β − λᵀb − μᵀe` holds on `Ω`, and therefore on `cl conv Ω`.

*Proof.*
1. Let `w ∈ Ω` and `u = Sw ∈ D'`. By the choice of `β`,
   `aᵀSw + λᵀg(Sw) + μᵀh(Sw) ≥ β`.
2. Since `λ ≥ 0` and `g(Sw) ≤ b − Aw` componentwise,
   `λᵀg(Sw) ≤ λᵀ(b − Aw)`. The equality rows give
   `μᵀh(Sw) = μᵀ(e − Cw)` for every sign of `μ`.
3. Hence `aᵀSw + λᵀ(b − Aw) + μᵀ(e − Cw) ≥ β`.
4. Rearranging gives `(Sᵀa − Aᵀλ − Cᵀμ)ᵀw ≥ β − λᵀb − μᵀe`.
5. The set satisfying a linear inequality is closed and convex, so the
   inequality extends to `cl conv Ω`. ∎

No convexity, continuity, compactness, constraint qualification, or
optimality of the multipliers is used. This is weak Lagrangian duality,
as the report already states. An equality row may be used as two opposite
inequality sides with nonnegative multipliers; their difference is a free
`μ_k`. A negative multiplier on a single inequality side is invalid.

## 4. The closure theorem

**Theorem 4.1 (closure of the aggregated cuts).** Assume:
- (H1) `D ⊂ R^d` is nonempty and compact;
- (H2) `g: D → R^m` and `h: D → R^r` are continuous;
- (H3) `S, A, b, C, e` are real matrices and vectors of compatible
  sizes, and no bounds are imposed on `w`.

Then:
- (a) `R_D` is closed and convex.
- (b) `R_D = ⋂_{c∈Λ} {w : π(c)ᵀw ≥ ρ(c)}`.
- (c) In (b), `Λ` may be replaced by any `Λ' ⊆ Λ∖{0}` that contains a
  positive multiple of every nonzero `c ∈ Λ`. Example:
  `Λ' = {c ∈ Λ : ‖c‖_1 = 1}`.

**Lemma 4.2.** `K_D` is compact and convex. `U := K_D + Q` is closed and
convex.

*Proof.*
- `G_D` is compact as the continuous image of `D`. Its convex hull is
  compact, as in Proposition 2.1.
- The sum of convex sets is convex.
- Closedness: let `k_n + q_n → p` with `k_n ∈ K_D` and `q_n ∈ Q`. By
  compactness a subsequence has `k_n → k ∈ K_D`. Then `q_n → p − k`,
  which lies in `Q` because `Q` is closed. So `p ∈ U`. ∎

**Lemma 4.3.** For `c = (a, λ, μ)`:
`inf_{p∈U} cᵀp = φ_D(c)` if `λ ≥ 0`, and `= −∞` otherwise.

*Proof.*
- Write `p = k + q` with `k ∈ K_D` and `q = (0, s, 0)`, `s ≥ 0`.
- If `λ ≥ 0`: `cᵀp = cᵀk + λᵀs ≥ cᵀk`. A linear function on the convex
  hull of the compact set `G_D` attains its minimum at a point of `G_D`.
  That minimum is `φ_D(c)`, and it is attained at a point of `G_D ⊆ U`.
- If `λ_i < 0`: fix `k ∈ K_D`. Then `cᵀ(k + τ e_{d+i}) → −∞` as `τ → ∞`. ∎

**Lemma 4.4.** `cᵀT(w) − φ_D(c) = π(c)ᵀw − ρ(c)` for every `w` and `c`.

*Proof.*
`cᵀT(w) = aᵀSw + λᵀ(b − Aw) + μᵀ(e − Cw) = (Sᵀa − Aᵀλ − Cᵀμ)ᵀw + λᵀb + μᵀe`. ∎

*Proof of Theorem 4.1.*
- Let `H_c = {p : cᵀp ≥ φ_D(c)}`. First, `U = ⋂_{c∈Λ} H_c`.
  - The inclusion `⊆` is Lemma 4.3.
  - For `⊇`, let `p ∉ U`. By Lemma 4.2 and strict separation there are
    `c ≠ 0` and `α` with `cᵀp < α ≤ cᵀq` for all `q ∈ U`. Then
    `inf_U cᵀq > −∞`, so Lemma 4.3 gives `λ ≥ 0`, that is, `c ∈ Λ`, and
    `inf_U cᵀq = φ_D(c)`. Hence `cᵀp < φ_D(c)` and `p ∉ H_c`.
- Taking preimages under the affine map `T`:
  `R_D = T^{-1}(U) = ⋂_{c∈Λ} T^{-1}(H_c)`.
- By Lemma 4.4, `T^{-1}(H_c) = {w : π(c)ᵀw ≥ ρ(c)}`. This proves (b).
- (a) follows because preimages of closed convex sets under affine maps
  are closed and convex.
- (c): for `τ > 0`, `φ_D(τc) = τ φ_D(c)`, `π(τc) = τ π(c)` and
  `ρ(τc) = τ ρ(c)`. So `τc` and `c` define the same halfspace. For
  `c = 0` the inequality is `0 ≥ 0`. ∎

The proof needs neither nonempty interior nor full dimension of `D`,
`K_D` or `R_D`.

**Remark 4.5 (how Theorem 4.1 covers the reported variants).**

1. *Remainders that use block variables.* `A` and `C` act on all of `w`,
   including the coordinates selected by `S`. No duplication is needed.
   The duplicated statement in `row-aggregation.md` §3 is equivalent:
   - Duplicate the block coordinates as `x'`. This gives a closure in
     `(x, x', z)`.
   - Restricting it to the subspace `x' = x` restricts each halfspace.
   - The restriction of an intersection is the intersection of the
     restrictions.

   The cut's coefficient on a block coordinate `j` is
   `a_k − (Aᵀλ + Cᵀμ)_j`, where `j` is the `k`-th selected coordinate.
2. *Objective.* For `min g_0(Sw) + α_0ᵀw + c_0`, add the variable `t` to
   `w` and the inequality row `g_0(Sw) + α_0ᵀw − t ≤ −c_0`, with `g_0`
   included among the features.
   - For maximization, add the row `−g_0(Sw) − α_0ᵀw + t ≤ c_0`.
   - The theorem applies unchanged. `t` is an unrestricted coordinate of
     `w`, and (H3) imposes no bounds.
   - Let `P` be any set of further constraints that does not involve
     `t`. Then
     `inf{t : w ∈ R_D ∩ P} = inf{c_0 + α_0ᵀw + y_0 : w ∈ P, (Sw, y, η) ∈ K_D, y_i ≤ b_i − A_i w (i ≥ 1), η = e − Cw}`,
     where `y_0` is the coordinate of `g_0`. This is the bound of the
     convexified problem. It is the cut-level form of the primal
     characterization of the Lagrangian dual (Section 10). Whether
     finitely many cuts reach this bound is a separate question, not
     addressed here.
3. *Equality row versus two opposite sides.* Replace `h(Sw) + Cw = e` by
   the two inequality rows with features `h` and `−h`. The new relaxation
   requires `(Sw, y, η, η') ∈ K' + Q'`, where
   `K' = {(u, y, η, −η) : (u, y, η) ∈ K_D}`. This forces both
   `η ≤ e − Cw` and `−η ≤ −e + Cw`, that is, `η = e − Cw`. The closure
   is the same.
4. *Linear rows.* A row with `g_i ≡ 0` (a purely linear row `A_iw ≤ b_i`)
   adds nothing to the aggregation:
   - `R_D` becomes `R_D ∩ {A_i w ≤ b_i}`.
   - Every cut with `λ_i > 0` equals the cut without row `i` plus
     `λ_i` times row `i`, because `φ_D` does not depend on `λ_i`.

   A linear row that involves only block coordinates can instead be
   placed into `D` (`D ← D ∩ {u : …}`). This can be strictly stronger.
   *Example:*
   - Take `D = [0,1]^2`, the row `u_1 + u_2 ≤ 1`, and the row
     `t ≤ x_1 x_2`.
   - With `D` the box, the point `(1/2, 1/2, t = 1/2)` satisfies the
     closure (`cav_{box}(x_1x_2) = min{x_1, x_2}`) and the linear row.
   - With `D` the triangle, `(1/2, 1/2)` lies on the face
     `u_1 + u_2 = 1`. Any representing mixture is supported on that edge,
     where `u_1u_2 = s(1−s)` is concave. Jensen's inequality gives
     `t ≤ 1/4`.

   This is the mechanism of Report A's row-constrained-domain example
   (lane C-POLY).
5. *Several blocks.* The union of the cut families of blocks
   `D_1, …, D_q` has closure `⋂_k R_{D_k}`. This is generally weaker than
   a closure over a joint domain, which the implementation does not
   claim.
6. *Empty domain.* If `D = ∅`, then `Σ_D = ∅`. When `S(Ω) ⊆ D` has been
   certified, this proves `Ω = ∅`. Without that certificate it proves
   nothing. The reported status `empty_domain` matches this.

**Proposition 4.6 (weaker regularity).**
(a) Theorem 4.1 holds verbatim (with attained minima) if each `g_i` is
real-valued and lower semicontinuous on the compact `D` and `h` is
continuous.
(b) For any nonempty `D ⊆ R^d` and any real-valued `g, h`, let
`φ_D = inf`. Then the intersection of all cuts with `c ∈ Λ` and
`φ_D(c) > −∞` equals `T^{-1}(cl(K_D + Q))`.
(c) Without lower semicontinuity of an inequality feature, the
intersection of all cuts can be strictly larger than `R_D`.

*Proof.*

(a) The minimum is attained: `aᵀu + λᵀg(u) + μᵀh(u)` is lower
semicontinuous for `λ ≥ 0`, and `D` is compact. It remains to prove
that `U = conv(G_D + Q) = K_D + Q` is closed. The rest of the proof of
Theorem 4.1 is unchanged.
1. Let `p_n ∈ U` with `p_n → p`, and put `M = d + m + r`.
2. By Carathéodory, `p_n = Σ_{j=0}^{M} θ_{nj} (u_{nj}, y_{nj}, h(u_{nj}))`
   with `θ_{n·} ∈ Δ_{M+1}`, `u_{nj} ∈ D` and `y_{nj} ≥ g(u_{nj})`.
3. Pass to a subsequence with `θ_{nj} → θ_j` and `u_{nj} → u_j ∈ D`.
   Continuity gives `h(u_{nj}) → h(u_j)`.
4. Let `γ` be a componentwise lower bound of `g` on `D`; it exists by
   lower semicontinuity and compactness. Each vector `θ_{nj} y_{nj}`
   is bounded:
   - below, by `θ_{nj} γ`;
   - above, by (y-part of `p_n`) − `Σ_{k≠j} θ_{nk} γ`.
5. Pass to a further subsequence with `θ_{nj} y_{nj} → v_j`.
6. If `θ_j > 0`: then `y_{nj} → y_j := v_j/θ_j` and
   `y_j ≥ liminf g(u_{nj}) ≥ g(u_j)`, so
   `q_j := (u_j, y_j, h(u_j)) ∈ G_D + Q`.
7. If `θ_j = 0`: then `θ_{nj}u_{nj} → 0`, `θ_{nj}h(u_{nj}) → 0`, and
   `v_j ≥ lim θ_{nj}γ = 0`.
8. Therefore `p = Σ_{θ_j>0} θ_j q_j + (0, Σ_{θ_j=0} v_j, 0)`, which lies
   in `conv(G_D + Q) + Q = U`.

(b) The closed convex hull of a nonempty set `X` is the intersection of
the closed halfspaces containing `X`. A halfspace `{cᵀp ≥ α}` with
`c ≠ 0` contains `X = G_D + Q` iff `λ ≥ 0` and `α ≤ φ_D(c)`; the
argument is the same as Lemma 4.3. Moreover,
`cl conv(G_D + Q) = cl(K_D + Q)`. Taking preimages under `T` gives the
claim.

(c) Take `D = [0,1]`, `g(0) = 1`, `g(u) = 0` for `u > 0` (upper but not
lower semicontinuous), and the row `g(x) − s ≤ 0`.
- In `K_D + Q`, the only points above `x = 0` are `(0, y)` with `y ≥ 1`,
  because `u ≥ 0` on `D`. So `(x, s) = (0, 1/2) ∉ R_D`.
- Every cut reads `ax + λs ≥ inf_{u∈[0,1]} (au + λg(u))`, with `λ ≥ 0`.
  Letting `u → 0⁺` shows `inf ≤ 0`, so `(0, 1/2)` satisfies all cuts. ∎

**Corollary 4.7 (finite mixtures; "rows in expectation").** Under (H1)–(H3),
`w ∈ R_D` iff there exist `k ≤ d + m + r + 1`, `θ ∈ Δ_k` and
`u_1, …, u_k ∈ D` with
- `Sw = Σ θ_j u_j`,
- `Σ θ_j g(u_j) ≤ b − Aw`,
- `Σ θ_j h(u_j) = e − Cw`.

*Proof.* By Carathéodory in `R^{d+m+r}`, every point of `K_D` is a
convex combination of at most `d + m + r + 1` points of `G_D`. ∎

This is the precise sense in which convexifying first and then imposing
the rows differs from imposing the rows and then convexifying. In
`R_D`, the rows need to hold only on average over the mixture, not at
each atom.

**Corollary 4.8 (aggregated-envelope form).** Under (H1)–(H3), for
`(λ, μ) ∈ R^m_+ × R^r` put `ψ_{λ,μ} = λᵀg + μᵀh`. Then

`R_D = {w : Sw ∈ conv D} ∩ ⋂_{(λ,μ)} {w : vex_D ψ_{λ,μ}(Sw) ≤ λᵀ(b − Aw) + μᵀ(e − Cw)}`.

*Proof.* The proof is given after Theorem 5.2.

With `m = 0` and the rows `h(x) − z = 0` (no other remainder), this is
the description of the graph hull of a continuous vector function over a
compact convex domain. Liers et al. (2021, Prop. 1, p. 6) attribute that
description to Ballerstein (2013).

**Corollary 4.9 (lifted atoms and original rows have the same closure).**
Suppose `g = Γ_g F` and `h = Γ_h F` for continuous atoms `F: D → R^k`
and matrices `Γ_g ∈ R^{m×k}` and `Γ_h ∈ R^{r×k}`. Put
`H_D(F) = conv{(u, F(u)) : u ∈ D}`. Then

`proj_w {(w, ω) : (Sw, ω) ∈ H_D(F), Γ_g ω + Aw ≤ b, Γ_h ω + Cw = e} = R_D`.

*Proof.*
- The linear map `Π(u, ω) = (u, Γ_g ω, Γ_h ω)` maps the graph of `F`
  onto `G_D`. Convex hulls commute with linear maps, so
  `Π(H_D(F)) = K_D`.
- Hence `∃ω` with `(Sw, ω) ∈ H_D(F)`, `Γ_gω ≤ b − Aw` and
  `Γ_hω = e − Cw` iff `∃ (Sw, y, η) ∈ K_D` with `y ≤ b − Aw` and
  `η = e − Cw`. ∎

Combined with Proposition 2.1(ii), this has a practical consequence. The
auxiliary-variable reformulation of Report A has all lifted joint support
cuts plus the linearized rows. Its projection is exactly the closure of
Report B's original-variable cuts, provided the same `D` and the same
rows are used. Measured differences between the two campaigns therefore
come from cut selection, budgets, and solver interaction, not from the
two closures.

## 5. A single row: the closure is the convex-envelope relaxation (task 2)

**Lemma 5.1 (convex envelope).** Let `D ⊂ R^d` be nonempty and compact,
and let `f: D → R` be continuous (lower semicontinuous suffices). Let
`K_f = conv{(u, f(u)) : u ∈ D}`. For `x ∈ conv D` define
`vex_D f(x) = min{y : (x, y) ∈ K_f + ({0} × R_+)}`. Then:
- (i) the minimum exists and equals `min{y : (x,y) ∈ K_f}`;
- (ii) `vex_D f` is convex on `conv D` and `vex_D f ≤ f` on `D`;
- (iii) every convex `ϑ: conv D → R` with `ϑ ≤ f` on `D` satisfies
  `ϑ ≤ vex_D f`;
- (iv) `vex_D f(x) = sup_{a∈R^d} [min_{u∈D}(aᵀu + f(u)) − aᵀx]`.

So `vex_D f` is the convex envelope of `f` over `D`, in both the
largest-convex-minorant sense and the biconjugate sense.

*Proof.*
- (i) The projection of `K_f` onto the `u`-coordinates is
  `conv D`, because convex hulls commute with linear maps. So the vertical
  section `{y : (x,y) ∈ K_f}` is nonempty for `x ∈ conv D`. It is compact
  and convex, since `K_f + {0}×R_+` is closed (Lemma 4.2 or
  Proposition 4.6(a)). Adding `R_+` does not change its minimum.
- (ii) The epigraph of `vex_D f` over `conv D` is `K_f + ({0} × R_+)`,
  which is convex. For `u ∈ D`, `(u, f(u)) ∈ K_f`.
- (iii) If `(x, y) = Σθ_j (u_j, f(u_j))`, then
  `y ≥ Σθ_j ϑ(u_j) ≥ ϑ(x)` by Jensen's inequality. Minimize over `y`.
- (iv) "≥": each function `x ↦ min_u(aᵀu + f(u)) − aᵀx` is affine and
  `≤ f` on `D`, so (iii) applies.
- (iv) "≤": let `y < vex_D f(x)`. Then `(x, y)` is outside the closed
  convex set `K_f + ({0}×R_+)`. Strict separation gives `(a', κ)` with
  `a'ᵀx + κy < inf(a'ᵀu + κy')` over that set.
  - `κ ≥ 0`, since otherwise the infimum is `−∞`.
  - `κ = 0` would give `a'ᵀx < min_D a'ᵀu`, contradicting `x ∈ conv D`.
  - So `κ > 0`. Scale to `κ = 1` and set `a = a'`. Then
    `y < min_u(aᵀu + f(u)) − aᵀx`, so the supremum exceeds `y`. ∎

**Theorem 5.2 (single row).** Assume (H1)–(H3).
(a) If `m = 1` and `r = 0` (one inequality row `g(Sw) + A w ≤ b`), then
`R_D = {w : Sw ∈ conv D, vex_D g(Sw) + A w ≤ b}`.
(b) If `m = 0` and `r = 1` (one equality row `h(Sw) + C w = e`), then
`R_D = {w : Sw ∈ conv D, vex_D h(Sw) ≤ e − C w ≤ cav_D h(Sw)}`.
(c) For affine `ℓ(u) = αᵀu + α_0`, `vex_D(g + ℓ) = vex_D g + ℓ` on
`conv D`. So moving block-coordinate terms between `g` and the
remainder does not change the relaxation.

*Proof.*
- (a) `w ∈ R_D` iff some `y` in the vertical section of `K_D` at `Sw`
  satisfies `y ≤ b − Aw`.
  - The section is nonempty iff `Sw ∈ conv D` (Lemma 5.1(i)).
  - It is a compact interval with smallest element `vex_D g(Sw)`.
- (b) The section is the compact interval `[vex_D h(x), cav_D h(x)]`.
  Its largest element is `−vex_D(−h)(x)`, because the hull of the graph
  of `−h` is the reflection of the hull of the graph of `h`. The equality
  row requires `e − Cw` to lie in this interval.
- (c) The affine bijection `(u, y) ↦ (u, y + ℓ(u))` maps the graph of
  `g` onto the graph of `g + ℓ`. It therefore maps hull onto hull and
  shifts the vertical minimum at `x` by `ℓ(x)`. ∎

**Answer to task 2.** Yes. For a single inequality row the closure of
all aggregated cuts is exactly the convex-envelope relaxation:

`{x ∈ conv D} ∩ {vex_D g(x) + ℓ(v) ≤ b}`.

Two conditions apply:
- The envelope is the one over the same compact `D` used for the
  support. It is defined on `conv D`, or set to `+∞` outside it.
- The condition `x ∈ conv D` comes from the cuts with `λ = 0`.

This holds even if `ℓ` uses block variables, by part (c). Using a
convex superset `B ⊇ D` (for example a box) on which `g` is defined
gives `vex_B g ≤ vex_D g`, a weaker relaxation.

*Proof of Corollary 4.8.*
1. Fix `(λ, μ)`. The single inequality row
   `ψ_{λ,μ}(Sw) + (λᵀA + μᵀC)w ≤ λᵀb + μᵀe` has a continuous feature.
2. Its cuts, with single-row multiplier `κ ≥ 0` and direction `a`, are
   exactly the cuts of `(a, κλ, κμ)` in the original family. Check:
   `π = Sᵀa − κ(Aᵀλ + Cᵀμ)`, `φ = φ_D(a, κλ, κμ)`, and
   `ρ = φ − κ(λᵀb + μᵀe)`.
3. By Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 5.2(a), the intersection of these cuts is
   the displayed set for `(λ, μ)`, intersected with `{Sw ∈ conv D}`.
4. As `(λ, μ)` ranges over `R^m_+ × R^r` and `κ ≥ 0`, the triples
   `(a, κλ, κμ)` exhaust `Λ`.
5. Hence the intersection over `(λ, μ)` is the intersection of all cuts,
   which is `R_D` by Theorem 4.1. ∎

## 6. Relation to the hull of the feasible set

**Proposition 6.1 (free remainders).** Split `w = (x, z)` with `x ∈ R^d`
the selected block coordinates (`S = [I_d 0]`) and `z ∈ R^p`. Write
`A = [A_x A_z]`, `C = [C_x C_z]` and `M = [A_z; C_z] ∈ R^{(m+r)×p}`.
Assume (H1)–(H3).
(a) `conv Σ_D ⊆ R_D`.
(b) If `rank M = m + r`, then `R_D = conv Σ_D`. In particular,
`conv Σ_D` is closed.

*Proof.*

(a) `R_D` is convex and contains `Σ_D`. For a point of `Σ_D`, take the
one-atom mixture in Corollary 4.7.

(b) Let `M⁺ ∈ R^{p×(m+r)}` satisfy `M M⁺ = I`; it exists by full row
rank.
1. Let `w = (x, z) ∈ R_D`. Corollary 4.7 gives `θ_j > 0` and `u_j ∈ D`
   with `Σθ_j u_j = x`, `ȳ := Σθ_j g(u_j) ≤ b − Aw` and
   `η̄ := Σθ_j h(u_j) = e − Cw`.
2. Define `ρ_j = (ȳ − g(u_j) + A_x(x − u_j), η̄ − h(u_j) + C_x(x − u_j))`
   and `z_j = z + M⁺ρ_j`.
3. The inequality rows hold at `(u_j, z_j)`:
   `g(u_j) + A_x u_j + A_z z_j = g(u_j) + A_x u_j + A_z z + ȳ − g(u_j) + A_x x − A_x u_j = ȳ + A_x x + A_z z ≤ b`.
4. The equality rows hold in the same way:
   `h(u_j) + C_x u_j + C_z z_j = η̄ + C_x x + C_z z = e`.
5. So `(u_j, z_j) ∈ Σ_D`.
6. Since `Σθ_jρ_j = 0`, `Σθ_j z_j = z`, and `Σθ_j u_j = x`. Hence
   `w ∈ conv Σ_D`. ∎

**Remark 6.2 (where the gap to the feasible hull comes from).** Let `P`
be the remaining constraints: variable bounds, other rows, and
integrality. Then
`R_D ∩ P ⊇ conv(Σ_D) ∩ P ⊇ conv(Σ_D ∩ P)`, and both inclusions can be
strict. Any gap between the cut closure and the feasible hull has one of
two sources:
1. The slack of some selected row cannot be absorbed by free remainder
   directions. Rows without a remainder, as in `x^2 = 1/4`, and rows
   sharing a single remainder variable (Example 6.4) are cases of this.
   This is the first inclusion.
2. Constraints outside the aggregation are intersected only after
   convexification. This is the second inclusion.

The objective row always contains `−t` and therefore always has a free
remainder direction. Liers et al. (2021) use the graph setting
`z = g(x)` with `z` otherwise free, where `M = −I`. In that setting
Proposition 6.1 says that simultaneous convexification reaches
`conv(X)`, consistent with their framework.

**Example 6.3 (report's `x^2 = 1/4`; exact closure).** Take `D = [0,1]`,
the single equality row `x^2 = 1/4`, and no remainder.
- By Theorem 5.2(b), `vex_{[0,1]} u^2 = u^2` because `u^2` is convex.
  `cav_{[0,1]} u^2 = u`, the chord.
- So `R_D = {x : x^2 ≤ 1/4 ≤ x} = [1/4, 1/2]`. The feasible set is
  `{1/2}`.
- Two cuts suffice to describe `R_D`:
  - `(a, μ) = (1, −1)`: `min_{[0,1]}(u − u^2) = 0` gives `x ≥ 1/4`.
  - `(a, μ) = (−1, 1)`: `min(u^2 − u) = −1/4` gives `x ≤ 1/2`.
- The report's argument also holds: with mass `3/4` at 0 and `1/4` at 1,
  `E u = E u^2 = 1/4`, so `x = 1/4` passes every cut.
- With a free remainder, `x^2 + z = 1/4` with `z ∈ R`, Proposition 6.1
  gives `R_D = conv Σ_D = {(x,z) : x ∈ [0,1], 1/4 − x ≤ z ≤ 1/4 − x^2}`,
  with no gap. The gap reappears exactly when `z` is fixed to `0` by a
  constraint outside the aggregation (source 2 in Remark 6.2).
- With `D = {1/2}` (the row placed inside the domain), `R_D = {1/2}`.

**Example 6.4 (a gap even when `D` equals the block projection).** Take
`D = [0,2]`, a single free remainder `z`, and the rows
`−(x−2)^2/2 + z ≤ 0` and `−x^2/2 + z ≤ 0`.
- `Σ_D = {(x,z) : x ∈ [0,2], z ≤ min{(x−2)^2, x^2}/2}`, and `S(Σ_D) = D`.
- `(1, 1) ∈ R_D`: the mixture `½δ_0 + ½δ_2` has `E u = 1` and
  `E[(u−2)^2/2] = E[u^2/2] = 1`.
- On `Σ_D`, `z ≤ 1/2`, because `min{(u−2)^2, u^2} ≤ 1` on `[0,2]`. So
  `(1,1) ∉ conv Σ_D`.
- Here `M = [1; 1]` has rank `1 < 2`. Shrinking `D` cannot help, since
  `D` is already exactly the block projection.

## 7. Joint aggregation versus separate single-row envelopes (task 3)

Always `R_D ⊆ ⋂_i R_D^{(i)}`, where `R_D^{(i)}` is the closure of row `i`
alone, because single-row cuts are cuts with `λ` (or `μ`) supported on
one row.

**Proposition 7.1 (no gain under product structure).** Split the block
coordinates `x = (x^1, x^2)` and assume `D = D^1 × D^2` with each `D^i`
compact. Split the rows into two groups, with the features of group `i`
depending only on `x^i`. Then `R_D = R^1 ∩ R^2`, where `R^i` is the
closure of group `i` alone over `D`.

*Proof.*
- After permuting coordinates, `G_D = G^1 × G^2`, where `G^i` is the
  graph of the group-`i` features over `D^i`. Then
  `conv(G^1 × G^2) = conv G^1 × conv G^2`:
  - `⊆` holds because the right side is convex and contains the product.
  - `⊇`: if `p_1 = Σα_i a_i` and `p_2 = Σβ_j b_j`, then
    `(p_1, p_2) = Σ_{i,j} α_iβ_j (a_i, b_j)`.
- `Q` splits the same way, so `U = U^1 × U^2` with
  `U^i = conv G^i + Q^i`.
- Let `T_i(w)` be the components of `T(w)` belonging to group `i`
  (block part `x^i`, slacks and equality residuals of group `i`). Then
  `R_D = T_1^{-1}(U^1) ∩ T_2^{-1}(U^2)`, and each `T_i^{-1}(U^i)`
  already forces `x^i ∈ conv D^i`.
- Now take group `i` alone over the full `D`. By the same product
  argument, its hull is `conv G^i × conv D^{3−i}`, after permutation. So
  its closure is
  `R^i = T_i^{-1}(U^i) ∩ {x^{3−i} ∈ conv D^{3−i}}`.
- Hence
  `R^1 ∩ R^2 = T_1^{-1}(U^1) ∩ T_2^{-1}(U^2) ∩ {x^1 ∈ conv D^1} ∩ {x^2 ∈ conv D^2} = R_D`. ∎

So joint aggregation can gain only when rows share block variables or
when `D` couples them, for example through a linear row placed in `D`.

**Example 7.2 (gain by cancellation; elementary).** On `D = [0,1]`, take
the rows `x^2 − s ≤ 0` and `−x^2 + t ≤ 0`.
- Separately: `s ≥ x^2` and `t ≤ x`.
- The joint cut `λ = (1,1)`, `a = 0` gives `t ≤ s`. It excludes
  `(1/2, 1/4, 1/2)`.
- This gain needs no support computation: the sum of the two source rows
  is linear. Example 7.3 is a genuinely nonlinear gain.

**Example 7.3 (main example).** Take `N = 3`, `w = (x, s, t)`, block
`x` (`d = 1`), and `D = [0,1]`, with the rows

- R1: `x^2 − s ≤ 0`, so `g_1(u) = u^2`, `A_1 = (0, −1, 0)`, `b_1 = 0`;
- R2: `−x^3 + t ≤ 0`, so `g_2(u) = −u^3`, `A_2 = (0, 0, 1)`, `b_2 = 0`;

and the point `w* = (1/2, 1/4, 1/2)`.

*Separate side.* By Theorem 5.2(a):
- `R^{(1)} = {x ∈ [0,1], s ≥ x^2}`, since `u^2` is convex.
- `R^{(2)} = {x ∈ [0,1], t ≤ x}`, because `vex_{[0,1]}(−u^3) = −u`:
  - `−u` is affine and `−u ≤ −u^3` on `[0,1]` (as `u^3 ≤ u`), so
    `−u ≤ vex` by Lemma 5.1(iii);
  - conversely, `vex(x) ≤ (1−x)g_2(0) + x g_2(1) = −x`.
- `w*` satisfies both: `1/4 ≤ 1/4` and `1/2 ≤ 1/2`.

*Joint side.* Take `c = (a, λ_1, λ_2) = (−5/4, 2, 1)`.
- The identity
  `−5u/4 + 2u^2 − u^3 + 1/4 = (u − 1/2)^2 (1 − u)` holds. Its right side
  is `≥ 0` on `[0,1]` and equals `0` at `u = 1/2`. So
  `φ_D(c) = −1/4`.
- `π(c) = (−5/4, 2, −1)` and `ρ(c) = −1/4`. The cut is
  `−5x/4 + 2s − t ≥ −1/4`.
- At `w*` the left side is `−5/8 + 1/2 − 1/2 = −5/8`, so the cut is
  violated by `3/8`.
- Direct validity check: on `Σ_D`,
  `2s − t ≥ 2x^2 − x^3 ≥ 5x/4 − 1/4`.
- In the language of Corollary 4.8:
  - The aggregated function `2u^2 − u^3` has
    `vex_{[0,1]}(2u^2 − u^3)(1/2) = 3/8`.
  - The tangent from `(1,1)` touches at `c` where
    `(c−1)^2(2c−1) = 0`, that is, `c = 1/2`.
  - The weighted sum of the separate envelopes at `1/2` is
    `2·(1/4) + (−1/2) = 0`.

The cut is the tangent line of this aggregated envelope at `x = 1/2`.

**Lemma 7.4 (cubic moment bound on `[0,1]`).** Let `0 ≤ x < 1` and
`x^2 ≤ m ≤ x`, and put `τ(x, m) = m − (x − m)^2/(1 − x)`.
(i) Every finitely supported probability measure `ν` on `[0,1]` with
`E u = x` and `E u^2 = m` has `E u^3 ≤ τ(x, m)`.
(ii) Let `c* = (x − m)/(1 − x)` and `θ = (x − c*)/(1 − c*)`. Then
`ν* = θδ_1 + (1 − θ)δ_{c*}` is a probability measure on `[0,1]` with
moments `(x, m, τ(x,m))`.

*Proof.*
(i)
1. For every real `c` and `u ∈ [0,1]`,
   `0 ≤ (u − c)^2(1 − u) = −u^3 + (1 + 2c)u^2 − (c^2 + 2c)u + c^2`.
2. Taking expectations: `E u^3 ≤ m + 2c(m − x) + c^2(1 − x)`.
3. At `c = c*` the right side is `m − (x − m)^2/(1 − x)`.

(ii)
- `c* ≥ 0` because `m ≤ x`.
- `c* ≤ x` iff `x − m ≤ x(1 − x)`, that is, iff `m ≥ x^2`.
- Since `x < 1`, we have `c* < 1` and `θ ∈ [0, 1)`.
- Moments:
  - `E u = θ(1 − c*) + c* = x`;
  - `E u^2 = θ(1 − c*^2) + c*^2 = (x − c*)(1 + c*) + c*^2 = x − c*(1 − x) = m`;
  - `E u^3 = (x − c*)(1 + c* + c*^2) + c*^3 = m − c*^2(1 − x) = τ(x, m)`. ∎

**Proposition 7.5 (exact joint closure of Example 7.3).**
`R_D = {(x, s, t) : 0 ≤ x ≤ 1, s ≥ x^2, t ≤ τ(x, min{s, x})}`, with
`τ(1, 1) := 1`. Two consequences:
- `R_D = conv{(x,s,t) : x ∈ [0,1], s ≥ x^2, t ≤ x^3}` (Proposition 6.1;
  `M = diag(−1, 1)` has rank 2).
- `R_D ⊊ R^{(1)} ∩ R^{(2)}`. The two sets differ exactly on
  `{x^2 ≤ s < x, τ(x,s) < t ≤ x}`, because `τ(x, m) < x` for `m < x`. At
  `(x,s) = (1/2, 1/4)`, the joint closure allows `t ≤ 1/8`; the separate
  envelopes allow `t ≤ 1/2`.

*Proof.* By Corollary 4.7, `(x,s,t) ∈ R_D` iff some finitely supported
`ν` on `[0,1]` has `E u = x`, `E u^2 ≤ s` and `E u^3 ≥ t`. For such `ν`,
`m_2 := E u^2 ∈ [x^2, x]`, by Jensen's inequality and `u^2 ≤ u`.
- "⊆" for `x < 1`:
  - `m_2 ≤ min{s, x}`.
  - `t ≤ E u^3 ≤ τ(x, m_2) ≤ τ(x, min{s,x})`. The last step holds because
    `∂τ/∂m = 1 + 2(x − m)/(1 − x) > 0` on `[x^2, x]`.
  - `s ≥ m_2 ≥ x^2`.
- "⊇" for `x < 1`: given `s ≥ x^2`, apply Lemma 7.4(ii) with
  `m = min{s,x} ∈ [x^2, x]`. The resulting `ν*` has `E u^2 = m ≤ s` and
  `E u^3 = τ(x, m) ≥ t`.
- `x = 1`: then `ν = δ_1`, so the conditions are `s ≥ 1` and `t ≤ 1`. ∎

Exact and floating-point checks of Example 7.3 are in
`M1_examples.py` (Section 11).

## 8. What the cuts are valid for (task 4)

**Theorem 8.1 (validity scope).** Let `Ω ⊆ R^N` be any set of points.
Assume (V1) and (V2) of Proposition 3.1 for some `D' ⊇ S(Ω)` on which
the source functions are real-valued. Then every cut certified over
`D'` (any `c ∈ Λ`, any `β ≤ inf_{D'}`) is valid on `Ω` and on
`cl conv Ω`.

No other property of `Ω` is used. In particular, the following are
irrelevant:
- integrality of any variable;
- constraints outside the selected rows;
- the objective (unless it is one of the selected rows);
- boundedness of the remainder variables.

If `D'` is compact and the features are continuous, all cuts from `D'`
together cut out exactly `R_{D'}` (Theorem 4.1), and

`R_{D'} ⊇ cl conv Σ_{D'} ⊇ cl conv Ω`.

*Proof.* Proposition 3.1. The chain of inclusions holds because `R_{D'}`
is closed and convex and contains `Σ_{D'}`, which contains `Ω`. ∎

**Corollary 8.2 (supersets of the block projection).** Let `D ⊆ D'` be
compact, with features continuous on `D'`, and `S(Ω) ⊆ D`.
- (i) `φ_D ≥ φ_{D'}` pointwise. Each `D'`-cut is implied by the `D`-cut
  with the same `c`, and `R_D ⊆ R_{D'}`.
- (ii) Both families are valid on `Ω`. Using a superset never
  invalidates a cut, but it makes the closure the relaxation for `D'`.
- (iii) Even `D = cl S(Ω)` does not give `cl conv Ω` in general
  (Example 6.4, where `D = S(Σ_D)`).
- (iv) A cut certified over `D` is not valid at points with
  `Sw ∉ D`.

*Proof.* (i) is monotonicity of the minimum under enlarging the domain,
together with Theorem 4.1. (ii) is Theorem 8.1. (iii) and (iv) are by
example. For (iv): with `D = [0, 1/2]` and feasible `x ∈ [0,1]`, the
`λ = 0` cut `−x ≥ −1/2` removes feasible points. ∎

This makes the reports' phrase "D contains the block coordinates of
every feasible point to which a proposed cut will apply" precise:

- *Global cuts:* `Ω = F`, the feasible set of the model. `D` must be
  built only from globally valid information: original bounds, and rows
  implied for every feasible point.
- *Node cuts:* if `D` (or a bound used in Section 9) comes from bounds
  valid only at a branch-and-bound node, then `Ω = F ∩ B_node`, and the
  cut is valid only in that subtree.
- *Cutoff cuts:* if `D` uses an objective cutoff `t ≤ U*`, then
  `Ω = F ∩ {objective ≤ U*}`. The cut is valid only for those
  solutions and may remove other feasible points.

**Proposition 8.3 (integer variables).** Let `I` index integer-
constrained block coordinates, `D` compact, and
`D_Z = {u ∈ D : u_I ∈ Z^I}`.
(i) `D_Z` is compact, so Theorems 4.1 and 5.2 apply to it, and
`R_{D_Z} ⊆ R_D`, often strictly.
(ii) If `S(Ω) ⊆ D_Z`, both cut families are valid on `Ω`. Integrality of
the remainder coordinates is never exploited: `R_D` and `R_{D_Z}` are
unchanged when integrality restrictions on remainder variables are added
or removed.
(iii) `R_{D_Z}` can still be strictly larger than the mixed-integer hull
`cl conv Ω`.

*Proof.*
- (i) `D_Z` is a closed subset of a compact set. Monotonicity is
  Corollary 8.2(i).
- (ii) Validity is Theorem 8.1. Invariance holds because `R_D` is
  defined by continuous constraints on `w`.
- (iii) Integer block: take `D_Z = {0, 1, 2}` and the equality row
  `x^2 = 1` with no remainder. Then `Ω = Σ_{D_Z} = {1}`. The mixture
  `(3/4)δ_0 + (1/4)δ_2` has `E u = 1/2` and `E u^2 = 1`, so
  `x = 1/2 ∈ R_{D_Z}` by Corollary 4.7.
- Integer remainder: take `D = [0,1]`, `z ∈ Z`, and the row
  `x^2 + z = 1/4`. Then `Ω = {(1/2, 0)}`, but `(1/4, 0) ∈ R_D`, because
  `1/4 − 1/4 ≤ 0 ≤ 1/4 − 1/16` (Example 6.3). ∎

**Example 8.4.** Take `x ∈ {0,1,2}` and the row `x^2 − s ≤ 0`.
- With `D = [0,2]`: `R_D = {x ∈ [0,2], s ≥ x^2}`.
- With `D_Z = {0,1,2}`: `K` is the triangle with vertices
  `(0,0), (1,1), (2,4)`, and `R_{D_Z} = {x ∈ [0,2], s ≥ max{x, 3x − 2}}`:
  - `max{x, 3x−2}` is convex and below the data at all three points, so
    it is `≤ vex` by Lemma 5.1(iii);
  - on each segment, the interpolation of adjacent data points equals
    `max{x, 3x−2}` and bounds `vex` from above.
- At `x = 1/2` the bounds are `s ≥ 1/4` versus `s ≥ 1/2`.

**Remark 8.5 (lifted cuts).** For the auxiliary-variable cuts of
Section 2, the analogue of (V1)–(V2) is `x ∈ D` and `w = F(x)` at every
target point. If only a one-sided relation `w_j ≥ f_j(x)` or
`w_j ≤ f_j(x)` is enforced, the coefficient sign of `w_j` must be
restricted (Remark 2.2).

## 9. Safe export after elimination

**Proposition 9.1 (aggregation `prop:elimination-round`).** Assume:
- the exact row `cᵀw ≥ r` is valid on `Ω`;
- the bounds `L_j ≤ w_j ≤ U_j` (entries in `R ∪ {±∞}`) hold on `Ω`;
- `ĉ` are the exact rational values of the exported binary64
  coefficients, and `Δ = ĉ − c`;
- `E = Σ_j e_j` is finite, where `e_j = 0` if `Δ_j = 0`,
  `e_j = Δ_j L_j` if `Δ_j > 0`, and `e_j = Δ_j U_j` if `Δ_j < 0`.

Then `ĉᵀw ≥ r̂` is valid on `Ω` for every `r̂ ≤ r + E`.

*Proof.* As in Proposition 2.6: `Δ_j w_j ≥ e_j` on `Ω` for each `j`. ∎

The one-sided condition in the report is exactly what this needs:
- a positive `Δ_j` needs a finite `L_j`;
- a negative `Δ_j` needs a finite `U_j`;
- a zero `Δ_j` needs no bound.

An exact epigraph coefficient on an unbounded `t` is therefore allowed.
`M1_rounding.py` verifies the statement on 230 random exports with an
exact LP, refuses 128 cases whose needed bound was infinite, and exhibits
uncorrected exports that are invalid. One deterministic case: rounding
`(1/3, 1/7)` to binary64 makes `ĉᵀw ≥ 1` invalid at `w = (3, 0)`.

**Remark 9.2 (provenance of the bounds).** The bounds must be
*mathematically* valid on `Ω`. Bounds produced by floating-point
propagation with feasibility tolerances do not automatically qualify.
Use original model bounds, the compact block domain `D`, or bounds
derived exactly (Report B's affine bound-propagation provenance,
C-IMPL). Bounds valid only at a node make the exported row local
(Section 8).

**Remark 9.3 (block coordinates never cause refusal).** By (V1), every
block coordinate has the finite valid bounds
`min_{u∈D} u_k ≤ (Sw)_k ≤ max_{u∈D} u_k`, since `D` is compact. Only
remainder coordinates can force a refusal.

**Proposition 9.4 (absorbing block rounding into the direction).** Let
`S` select the coordinates `B = {j_1, …, j_d}`. Let `λ ∈ Q^m_+` and
`μ ∈ Q^r` be exact (for example, binary64 values). Let `ĉ` be any
target vector of binary64 coefficients. Define `a ∈ Q^d` by
`a_k = ĉ_{j_k} + (Aᵀλ + Cᵀμ)_{j_k}`.
- Then `π(a, λ, μ)_j = ĉ_j` for `j ∈ B`, and
  `π(a, λ, μ)_j = −(Aᵀλ + Cᵀμ)_j` for `j ∉ B`.
- Suppose `β ≤ φ_D(a, λ, μ)` is certified for this exact `a`. Then
  `ĉᵀw ≥ r̂` is valid on `Ω` whenever
  `r̂ ≤ β − λᵀb − μᵀe + Σ_{j∉B} e_j`, with `e_j` computed from
  `Δ_j = ĉ_j − π_j` as in Proposition 9.1.
- In particular, no bounds are used at all if every remainder
  coefficient `π_j`, `j ∉ B`, is binary64-representable and exported
  unchanged. Example: a single row containing `t` with coefficient `−1`
  and binary64 `λ`.

*Proof.* Apply Proposition 3.1 to `c = (a, λ, μ)`. Then apply
Proposition 9.1 with `Δ_j = 0` for `j ∈ B`. ∎

This is a mild improvement over certifying a rounded direction and then
correcting all coordinates. Elimination creates new coefficients
`−Aᵀλ − Cᵀμ`, so certifying a binary64 direction does not by itself make
the eliminated row representable. The free direction `a` can absorb the
rounding of every block coordinate, including block coordinates that
also occur in remainders.

**Theorem 9.5 (final-row contract, mathematical core of N4).** Assume:
- (F1) `S(Ω) ⊆ D`, and `D` lies inside the real domain of every source
  expression, with the source semantics.
- (F2) Every `w ∈ Ω` satisfies the selected source rows exactly, with
  their recorded orientation.
- (F3) `a ∈ Q^d`, `λ ∈ Q^m_+`, `μ ∈ Q^r` are the exact values used.
- (F4) `β ≤ φ_D(a, λ, μ)` is certified over all of `D`. Compactness is
  not needed for this.
- (F5) `c = π(a, λ, μ)` and `r = β − λᵀb − μᵀe` are computed exactly from
  the exact source data, with duplicate occurrences of each variable
  merged.
- (F6) The exported binary64 coefficients `ĉ` and right-hand side `r̂`
  satisfy `r̂ ≤ r + E`, by exact comparison, where `E` is formed from
  bounds valid on `Ω` as in Proposition 9.1 and is finite.

Then `ĉᵀw ≥ r̂` on `Ω`, hence on `cl conv Ω`.

*Proof.* Proposition 3.1 gives `cᵀw ≥ r` on `Ω`. Proposition 9.1 gives
the exported row. ∎

The theorem does not cover rows changed by the solver after insertion
(Remark 2.5), the solver's feasibility tolerances, or any guarantee about
the solver's later search.

## 10. Prior work and novelty assessment (N3; mathematical part of N4)

- **Proposition 3.1 is weak Lagrangian duality.** Report B already
  attributes it this way (Boyd and Vandenberghe, §§5.1–5.2, as cited in
  Report B's audit `literature/aggregation-prior.md`).
- **Theorem 4.1 is the cut-level form of a classical principle:** the
  Lagrangian (or aggregation) bound equals the bound of the convexified
  problem. Precedents in the local literature base:
  - Chen and Luedtke (2022), *On Generating Lagrangian Cuts for Two-Stage
    Stochastic Integer Programs*, INFORMS J. Comput. 34(4), Prop. 2 and
    Thm. 3 (pp. 6–7). They show that the set cut out by all Lagrangian
    cuts `πᵀx + π_0θ ≥ Q*(π, π_0)` with `π_0 ≥ 0` equals the projection
    of the convex hull of the scenario set. That is the same separation
    argument, with the same cone sign, in a polyhedral setting.
  - Liers, Martin, Merkert, Mertens and Michaels (2021), J. Global
    Optim. 80, Prop. 1 (p. 6), attributed to Ballerstein (2013). The
    graph hull of a continuous vector function on a compact convex
    domain is the intersection, over all multipliers, of envelope
    relaxations of linear combinations. That is Corollary 4.8 in the
    equality/graph case. On p. 5 they also note that the intersection of
    separate envelope relaxations is in general strictly weaker than
    simultaneous convexification, which is the phenomenon of
    Section 7.
  - Dey and Xu (2026, arXiv) state the primal characterization of the
    Lagrangian dual and cite Geoffrion (1974) and Lemaréchal and Renaud
    (2001) for it. *Those two sources were not inspected here; they are
    not in the local literature base.*
- **Safe rounding is established technology:**
  - Neumaier and Shcherbina (2004), Math. Program. 99, pp. 4–6:
    rigorous LP lower bounds that bound residuals by variable bounds.
  - Eifler and Gleixner (2024), SIAM J. Optim. 34(1), Lemma 1 (p. 7) and
    Cor. 2 (p. 8): safe approximation and aggregation of rows using
    bound corrections.
  - Proposition 9.1 is the `≥`-row form of these.

**Assessment.**
- N3 should not be claimed as a new theorem. The closure statement, the
  aggregated-envelope form, and the joint-versus-separate gap are
  established principles.
- What the paper can claim is narrower: a precise and correct formulation
  that covers, together:
  - inequality rows with the cone sign;
  - equality rows;
  - affine remainders on all variables, including block variables;
  - the objective epigraph;
  - nonconvex compact `D`.

  It can also claim the small explicit examples (6.3, 6.4, 7.3, 8.4) and
  the free-remainder rank condition (Proposition 6.1). These are
  elementary. We searched for them only in the sources listed here and
  do not claim them as new.
- Report B's `literature.tex` cites Ballerstein (2013) and Liers et al.
  (2021) for simultaneous convexification in general, but not as
  precedents for the closure theorem. It does not cite Chen and Luedtke
  (2022), Geoffrion (1974) or Lemaréchal and Renaud (2001). The paper
  should cite Liers et al. Prop. 1 and Chen and Luedtke Thm. 3 next to
  the closure theorem, and add the two classical sources after
  inspecting them.
- For N4, the mathematics (Theorem 9.5) is elementary. Any novelty lies
  in the engineering contract and its implementation (source binding,
  replay), which lanes C-IMPL and C-EXP must support.
