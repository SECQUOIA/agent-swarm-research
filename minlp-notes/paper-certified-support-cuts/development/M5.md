## 2. C-SEP: distance duality, grids, and domain nets

### 2.0 Setting

Throughout this section:

- $P\subset\mathbb R^d$ is nonempty and compact. In the finite algorithms, $P=\{x:\ell\le x\le u,\ Ax\le b\}$ is a rational polytope with rational box $[\ell,u]\supseteq P$. Equalities appear as pairs of inequalities. Empty, singleton, and lower-dimensional $P$ are allowed.
- $F:P\to\mathbb R^k$ is continuous ($k\ge1$). For the exact algorithms, $F$ is a vector of rational polynomials, so $F(x)\in\mathbb Q^k$ for $x\in\mathbb Q^d$.
- $q\in\mathbb Q^k$ is the query, $\epsilon\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ the tolerance, and $J\subseteq\{1,\dots,k\}$ the set of cone coordinates.
- $H=\operatorname{conv}F(P)$ and $U_J=H+\operatorname{cone}\{e_j:j\in J\}$.
- $C_J=\{c\in[-1,1]^k: c_j\ge0\ (j\in J)\}$ and $S_J=\{c\in C_J:\|c\|_\infty=1\}$.
- $h(c)=\min_{x\in P}c^\top F(x)$ and $\psi(c)=h(c)-c^\top q$.
- $\phi_J(v)=\sum_{j\notin J}|v_j|+\sum_{j\in J}\max\{v_j,0\}$.
- $R\in\mathbb Q$ satisfies $R\ge\max_{x\in P}\|F(x)-q\|_1$. Report B's choice $R=\sum_j\max\{|\ell_j-q_j|,|u_j-q_j|\}$ from enclosures $\ell_j\le F_j\le u_j$ on $P$ qualifies, because $t\mapsto|t-q_j|$ is convex and so attains its maximum over $[\ell_j,u_j]$ at an endpoint.

A **support oracle of accuracy $\delta\in[0,\epsilon)$** takes a rational
$c\in C_J$ and returns a rational $L_c$ and a rational point $p_c\in P$ with
$$L_c\le h(c)\le c^\top F(p_c)\le L_c+\delta .\tag{O}$$
For quadratic $F$, Report B's exact polytope oracle gives $\delta=0$. For
polynomial $F$, the domain net of §2.4 gives $\delta=\delta_M$.

### 2.1 Distance duality

**Lemma 2.1 (distance duality).** Under §2.0 (only $P$ compact and nonempty and
$F$ continuous are needed):

1. $H$ is compact and $U_J$ is closed and convex.
2. $\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)=\min_{z\in H}\phi_J(z-q)$, and the minimum is attained.
3. $\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)=\max_{c\in C_J}\psi(c)$, and the maximum is attained.

*Proof.* (1) $F(P)$ is compact, being the continuous image of a compact set. In
$\mathbb R^k$ the convex hull of a compact set is compact: by Carathéodory it
is the image of the compact set $F(P)^{k+1}\times\Delta_{k+1}$ under a
continuous map. Let $Z=\operatorname{cone}\{e_j:j\in J\}$, a closed convex
cone. Take $y_n+s_n\to z$ with $y_n\in H$ and $s_n\in Z$. Pass to a subsequence
with $y_n\to y\in H$. Then $s_n=(y_n+s_n)-y_n\to z-y$, and $z-y\in Z$ because
$Z$ is closed. So $z\in U_J$, and $U_J$ is closed. It is convex as a sum of
convex sets.

(2) For $y\in H$ and $s\in Z$, the coordinates of $s$ outside $J$ are zero. In
coordinate $j\in J$, $\min_{s_j\ge0}|y_j+s_j-q_j|=\max\{y_j-q_j,0\}$, attained
at $s_j=\max\{q_j-y_j,0\}$. Hence
$\inf_{s\in Z}\|y+s-q\|_1=\phi_J(y-q)$, with attainment. Minimizing over the
compact set $H$ the continuous function $\phi_J(\cdot-q)$ gives an attained
minimum, which is $\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)$.

(3) *Weak duality.* For $c\in C_J$ and $v\in\mathbb R^k$, $c^\top v\le\phi_J(v)$:
if $j\notin J$, $c_jv_j\le|v_j|$; if $j\in J$, $c_j\in[0,1]$ gives
$c_jv_j\le\max\{v_j,0\}$. A linear function on $H=\operatorname{conv}F(P)$
attains its minimum on $F(P)$, so $\psi(c)=\min_{z\in H}c^\top(z-q)\le
c^\top(z-q)\le\phi_J(z-q)$ for every $z\in H$. Thus $\psi(c)\le\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)$.

*Strong duality.* Let $r=\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)$. If $r=0$, then $c=0$
gives $\psi(0)=0=r$. If $r>0$, the open ball $B=\{v:\|v-q\|_1<r\}$ is convex and
disjoint from the convex set $U_J$. By the finite-dimensional separation
theorem there is $c\neq0$ with $c^\top v\le c^\top z$ for all $v\in B$ and
$z\in U_J$. Scale $c$ so that $\|c\|_\infty=1$. Since
$\sup_{v\in B}c^\top v=c^\top q+r\|c\|_\infty$ (the dual norm of $\ell_1$ is
$\ell_\infty$), we get $\inf_{z\in U_J}c^\top z\ge c^\top q+r>-\infty$. For
$j\in J$ and $z\in U_J$, the ray $z+te_j$ ($t\ge0$) lies in $U_J$, so finiteness
of this infimum forces $c_j\ge0$. Hence $c\in C_J$, and
$\psi(c)=\min_{z\in H}c^\top z-c^\top q\ge\inf_{U_J}c^\top z-c^\top q\ge r$.
With weak duality, $\psi(c)=r$. $\square$

*Remark.* Part 3 also follows from the minimax theorem for the bilinear
function $(c,z)\mapsto c^\top(z-q)$ on the compact convex set $C_J\times H$,
using $\max_{c\in C_J}c^\top v=\phi_J(v)$. Report B's proof is the separation
argument above, written more briefly. The unbounded case $J\neq\varnothing$
needs only the closedness in part 1 and the sign argument; boundedness of
$U_J$ is never used.

**Lemma 2.2 (regularity of $\psi$).** On $C_J$:

1. $|\psi(c)-\psi(c')|\le R\|c-c'\|_\infty$.
2. $\psi$ is concave and positively homogeneous: $\psi(tc)=t\psi(c)$ for $t\ge0$.
3. If $\max_{C_J}\psi>0$, then every maximizer lies in $S_J$.

*Proof.* (1) For every $x\in P$,
$|(c-c')^\top(F(x)-q)|\le\|c-c'\|_\infty\|F(x)-q\|_1\le R\|c-c'\|_\infty$.
If $\psi(c)$ is attained at $x$, then
$\psi(c')\le c'^\top(F(x)-q)\le\psi(c)+R\|c-c'\|_\infty$. Swap the roles of $c$
and $c'$. (2) $\psi$ is a pointwise minimum of linear functions of $c$.
(3) Let $c^*$ be a maximizer with $\psi(c^*)>0$. Then $c^*\neq0$. If
$\|c^*\|_\infty<1$, then $c^*/\|c^*\|_\infty\in C_J$ (scaling preserves the sign
constraints) and has value $\psi(c^*)/\|c^*\|_\infty>\psi(c^*)$, a
contradiction. $\square$

**Lemma 2.3 (the two certificates).**

- (*cut*) If $c\in C_J$ and $\beta\le h(c)$, then $c^\top z\ge\beta$ holds on
  $U_J$, and $\beta-c^\top q\le\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)$.
- (*near-hull*) If $x_1,\dots,x_t\in P$, $\lambda\in\Delta_t$ and
  $z=\sum_i\lambda_iF(x_i)$, then $\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)\le\phi_J(z-q)$.

*Proof.* Cut: on $F(P)$ the inequality holds by definition of $h$. It extends to
$H$ by linearity and to $U_J$ because $c_j\ge0$ on $J$. The violation bound is
weak duality: $\beta-c^\top q\le\psi(c)\le\operatorname{dist}_1$. Near-hull:
$z\in H$, so apply Lemma 2.1(2). $\square$

### 2.2 The normal grid: verification and sharpening

Report B uses the grid
$G_N=\prod_jG_{N,j}$, with $G_{N,j}=\{-1+2i/N\}_{i=0}^N$ for $j\notin J$ and
$G_{N,j}=\{i/N\}_{i=0}^N$ for $j\in J$.

*Check of Report B's constants.* Every $c\in C_J$ is within $\infty$-distance
$1/N$ of $G_N$: round each coordinate to the nearest grid value. Free
coordinates have spacing $2/N$, so the error is at most $1/N$. Cone coordinates
have spacing $1/N$, so the error is at most $1/(2N)$. Report B's claim "within
$2/N$" is therefore true but loose by a factor of 2. With
$N\ge\lceil2R/(\epsilon-\delta)\rceil$ the conclusion $\delta+2R/N\le\epsilon$
holds, so Report B's theorem is correct as stated.

**Theorem 2.4 (sharpened grid).** Assume (O) and let
$N\ge\max\{1,\lceil R/(\epsilon-\delta)\rceil\}$. Query the oracle at every
$g\in G_N\cap S_J$.

- If some $L_g>g^\top q$, then $g^\top z\ge L_g$ is a valid rational cut for $U_J$
  that strictly excludes $q$.
- Otherwise $\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)\le\epsilon$.

The number of queries is
$|G_N\cap S_J|=(N+1)^k-(N-1)^{k-|J|}N^{|J|}\le 2k(N+1)^{k-1}$.

*Proof.* The cut case is Lemma 2.3. Otherwise, every $g\in G_N\cap S_J$
satisfies $\psi(g)\le g^\top(F(p_g)-q)\le L_g+\delta-g^\top q\le\delta$.

Suppose $r=\max_{C_J}\psi>0$. By Lemma 2.2(3) it is attained at some
$c^*\in S_J$. Then some coordinate $j$ has $c^*_j=\pm1$ ($j\notin J$) or
$c^*_j=1$ ($j\in J$). That coordinate value belongs to $G_{N,j}$. Round the
other coordinates to their nearest grid values. The resulting
$g\in G_N\cap S_J$ satisfies $\|g-c^*\|_\infty\le1/N$. By Lemma 2.2(1),
$r\le\psi(g)+R/N\le\delta+(\epsilon-\delta)=\epsilon$. If $r\le0$ there is
nothing to prove. In both cases Lemma 2.1 converts the bound on $r$ into the
distance bound.

Counting: the grid points outside $S_J$ have every free coordinate in the
$N-1$ interior values and every cone coordinate in the $N$ values
$\{0,\dots,(N-1)/N\}$. Finally,
$(N+1)^k-(N-1)^{k-|J|}N^{|J|}\le(N+1)^k-(N-1)^k\le 2k(N+1)^{k-1}$, using
$a^k-b^k\le k(a-b)a^{k-1}$ for $a\ge b\ge0$. $\square$

*Remark (anisotropic grids).* Lemma 2.2(1) holds coordinatewise:
$|\psi(c)-\psi(c')|\le\sum_jr_j|c_j-c'_j|$ with
$r_j\ge\max_P|F_j-q_j|$. Covering radii $\rho_j$ with
$\sum_jr_j\rho_j\le\epsilon-\delta$ suffice. With $\rho_j=(\epsilon-\delta)/(kr_j)$,
the grid size $\prod_j(kr_j/(\epsilon-\delta))$ is never larger than
$(R/(\epsilon-\delta))^k$ when $R=\sum_jr_j$, by the AM–GM inequality. The gain
is large when the feature ranges are unbalanced.

### 2.3 Tolerance arithmetic

From $N\ge 2R/(\epsilon-\delta)$ we get $2R/N\le\epsilon-\delta$, so
$\delta+2R/N\le\epsilon$ (Report B). Theorem 2.4 needs only
$R/N\le\epsilon-\delta$. The polynomial case requires $\delta=\delta_M<\epsilon$.
Report B's choice $\delta_M\le\epsilon/2$ satisfies this, and then
$N=\lceil 2R/(\epsilon-\delta_M)\rceil\le\lceil4R/\epsilon\rceil$. Division by
zero cannot occur: $\epsilon-\delta>0$, and $R=0$ or $L D_\infty=0$ gives
$N=1$ or $M=1$. If $R=0$, then $F\equiv q$ on $P$, and any vertex certifies
distance $0$.

### 2.4 The rational domain net (polynomial $F$)

Let $v_1,\dots,v_s$ be the vertices of $P$ and
$D_\infty=\max_i(\max_Px_i-\min_Px_i)=\max_{a,b}\|v_a-v_b\|_\infty$. The two
expressions agree because a linear function attains its extrema over $P$ at
vertices. Put
$S_M=\{\sum_a(n_a/M)v_a: n_a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},\ \sum_an_a=M\}\subseteq P$.

**Lemma 2.5 (Lipschitz in $x$).** Let $L\ge\sum_{j=1}^k\sum_{i=1}^d\sup_{y\in[\ell,u]}|\partial_iF_j(y)|$,
where $F$ is $C^1$ on the box $[\ell,u]\supseteq P$. Then
$|c^\top F(x)-c^\top F(y)|\le L\|x-y\|_\infty$ for all $x,y\in[\ell,u]$ and
$\|c\|_\infty\le1$.

*Proof.* The segment $[x,y]$ lies in the convex box. By the mean value theorem
there is $\xi_j\in[x,y]$ with $F_j(x)-F_j(y)=\nabla F_j(\xi_j)^\top(x-y)$, hence
$|F_j(x)-F_j(y)|\le\sum_i|\partial_iF_j(\xi_j)||x_i-y_i|\le(\sum_i\sup|\partial_iF_j|)\|x-y\|_\infty$.
Sum over $j$ and use $|c_j|\le1$. $\square$

Interval evaluation of the derivative polynomials over the box gives a valid
rational $L$. Report B's implementation does this monomial by monomial. That
is a valid outer enclosure: the exact range of a monomial over a box is the
product of the ranges of its univariate factors, and the enclosures of the
monomials are added.

**Lemma 2.6 (domain net, sharpened).** Every $x\in P$ has some $\hat x\in S_M$ with
$$\|x-\hat x\|_\infty\le\frac{\min\{s-1,\dim P\}\,D_\infty}{M}.$$
Consequently, with $\delta_M=L\min\{s-1,\dim P\}D_\infty/M$, for every $c$
with $\|c\|_\infty\le1$,
$$\min_{S_M}c^\top F-\delta_M\le\min_Pc^\top F\le\min_{S_M}c^\top F.$$

*Proof.* By Carathéodory's theorem in the affine hull of $P$, which has
dimension $\dim P$, $x=\sum_{a\in T}\alpha_av_a$ with $|T|=t\le\dim P+1$ and
$\alpha\in\Delta_T$. Using all vertices instead gives $t=s$ (Report B's
version). Order $T$ as $a_1,\dots,a_t$. Put $n_{a_i}=\lfloor M\alpha_{a_i}\rfloor$
for $i<t$, $n_{a_t}=M-\sum_{i<t}n_{a_i}$, and $n_a=0$ for $a\notin T$. Then
$n_{a_t}\ge M\alpha_{a_t}\ge0$, so $\hat x=\sum_an_av_a/M\in S_M$. Because
$\sum_{a\in T}(\alpha_a-n_a/M)=0$,
$$x-\hat x=\sum_{i<t}(\alpha_{a_i}-n_{a_i}/M)(v_{a_i}-v_{a_t}).$$
Each coefficient lies in $[0,1/M)$, and
$\|v_{a_i}-v_{a_t}\|_\infty\le D_\infty$. Hence the bound $(t-1)D_\infty/M$.

For the bracket, the right inequality holds because $S_M\subseteq P$. For the
left, let $x^*$ minimize $c^\top F$ over $P$ and take its $\hat x$. Lemma 2.5
gives $\min_{S_M}c^\top F\le c^\top F(\hat x)\le c^\top F(x^*)+\delta_M$. $\square$

The domain net therefore defines an oracle (O) with $\delta=\delta_M$: take
$L_c=\min_{S_M}c^\top F-\delta_M$ and $p_c$ a minimizing net point. Choosing
$M\ge\lceil 2L\min\{s-1,\dim P\}D_\infty/\epsilon\rceil$ gives
$\delta_M\le\epsilon/2$. The net still has at most $\binom{M+s-1}{s-1}$ points.
The sharpening reduces $M$, which matters because $s$ can be exponential in
$d$. A net built on a triangulation of $P$ by vertices of $P$ would also
reduce the cardinality, to at most (number of simplices)$\cdot\binom{M+d}{d}$.
This is a possible implementation change. The proofs above do not depend on
it.

*Exact feasibility.* Every point of $S_M$ is a rational convex combination of
exact vertices, so it lies in $P$ exactly, including on equality-defined
domains of zero volume. This is Report B's point and it is correct.

### 2.5 Other checks on C-SEP

- *Empty domain.* A bounded rational polytope is empty if and only if exact
  vertex enumeration finds no vertex: a nonempty polytope is the convex hull of
  its vertices.
- *Binary64 export* (Report B §"Implementation and rounding"). Let $c$ be the
  exact rational row with $c_J\ge0$ and let $\bar c$ be the actual binary64
  coefficients. The corrected row $\bar c^\top z\ge\beta+\sum_j\min\{(\bar c_j-c_j)\ell_j,(\bar c_j-c_j)u_j\}$,
  with its right side rounded down, is valid on $H$ by coordinatewise bounding.
  It is also valid on $U_J$ provided $\bar c_j\ge0$ for $j\in J$.
  Round-to-nearest preserves signs, so this holds. Here $[\ell_j,u_j]$ need to
  bound only $F_j$ on $P$, not the unbounded cone coordinates.
- *Implementation observation (not a soundness issue).* `solver/separation.py`
  already runs a Kelley-type loop: a numerical LP proposes a mixture or a
  normal, an exact support call is made, and the minimizer is added, for at
  most `proposal_rounds=8` rounds. Its numerical LP minimizes the plain $\ell_1$
  distance even when cone coordinates are requested; only the exact check uses
  $\phi_J$. Section 5 shows that this loop becomes complete when it uses an
  exact (or exactly verified) master LP with the $\phi_J$ objective and has no
  round cap. That removes the need for the grid fallback and its
  $(N+1)^k$-point near-hull certificate.

## 3. C-SCREEN: the screening theorem and Hölder pairing

**Setting.** Let $g(x)=(x,F(x))\in\mathbb R^r$ and $H_D=\operatorname{conv}g(D)$.
Let $\bar z\in\mathbb Q^r$ be the query and $s\in\mathbb Q^r_{>0}$ the scales.
Let $x^1,\dots,x^N\in D$ be exactly feasible samples, $\lambda\in\Delta_N$,
and $g_j(x^i)\in[\ell_{ij},u_{ij}]$ verified enclosures. Put
$L_j=\sum_i\lambda_i\ell_{ij}$, $U_j=\sum_i\lambda_iu_{ij}$,
$r_j=\max\{|\bar z_j-L_j|,|\bar z_j-U_j|\}/s_j$, $R_1=\sum_jr_j$ and
$R_\infty=\max_jr_j$.

**Theorem 3.1 (Report A, Thm `thm:screen`; verified).** Let $c^\top z\ge\beta$ be
valid on some set containing $z^*=\sum_i\lambda_ig(x^i)$, for instance $H_D$ or
$H_D+$cone. Then
$\beta-c^\top\bar z\le R_1$ if $\max_js_j|c_j|\le1$, and
$\beta-c^\top\bar z\le R_\infty$ if $\sum_js_j|c_j|\le1$.

*Proof.* $z^*_j\in[L_j,U_j]$ because $\lambda\ge0$ and $\sum\lambda_i=1$. The
function $t\mapsto|\bar z_j-t|$ is convex, so its maximum on $[L_j,U_j]$ is at
an endpoint: $|z^*_j-\bar z_j|/s_j\le r_j$. Validity gives
$\beta-c^\top\bar z\le c^\top(z^*-\bar z)\le\sum_j a_jb_j$ with
$a_j=s_j|c_j|\ge0$ and $b_j=|z^*_j-\bar z_j|/s_j\le r_j$. Hölder's inequality
gives $\sum_ja_jb_j\le\|a\|_\infty\|b\|_1\le R_1$ under the first
normalization and $\sum_ja_jb_j\le\|a\|_1\|b\|_\infty\le R_\infty$ under the
second. $\square$

The pairing is the $\ell_1$/$\ell_\infty$ dual-norm pairing in the scaled
coordinates $z_j/s_j$. Report A's implementation pairs them correctly:
`screening.py` uses `norm="1"` (sum of residuals) for the condition
$\max_js_j|c_j|\le1$. The integration's direction LP bounds each scaled
coefficient in $[-1,1]$ and calls `norm="1"`.

**Proposition 3.2 (tightness: the screen is the primal half of C-SEP duality).**
With exact graph values,
$$\sup\{\beta-c^\top\bar z:\ c^\top z\ge\beta\text{ valid on }H_D,\ \max_js_j|c_j|\le1\}
=\min_{z\in H_D}\sum_j|z_j-\bar z_j|/s_j,$$
and the analogous identity holds for $\sum_js_j|c_j|\le1$ and
$\max_j|z_j-\bar z_j|/s_j$. Therefore the best possible screening certificate
equals the largest normalized violation. The minimum is attained by a mixture
of at most $r+1$ graph points, which may have irrational parameters.

*Proof.* Substitute $z'_j=z_j/s_j$ and $c'_j=s_jc_j$, and apply Lemma 2.1 with
$J=\varnothing$ for the first identity. For the second, repeat the proof of
Lemma 2.1(3) with the $\ell_\infty$ ball and dual norm $\ell_1$. The best
$\beta$ for a given $c$ is $h(c)$. $\square$

**Example 3.3 (the swapped pairing is false).** Let $D=\{0\}$ and $g\equiv(0,0)$,
so $H_D=\{(0,0)\}$. Take $\bar z=(-1,-1)$ and $s=(1,1)$. The cut
$z_1+z_2\ge0$ is valid and max-normalized, with violation $2$. Yet
$R_\infty=1<2$. So $R_\infty$ does not bound max-normalized violations, while
$R_1=2$ does.

**Remark 3.4 (one-sided refinement).** If the cut has $c_j\ge0$ for $j\in J$
(aggregation cuts in original variables have this form), then for $j\in J$ one
may use $r_j=\max\{U_j-\bar z_j,0\}/s_j$, because
$c_j(z^*_j-\bar z_j)\le c_j\max\{z^*_j-\bar z_j,0\}$. This is the screening
form of $\phi_J$, and it matches C-SEP's cone distance.

*Novelty.* Theorem 3.1 is standard convex-combination geometry, as Report A
says. Its value lies in the exact replay contract.

## 4. C-BERN: Bernstein conversion, partitions, and the chord correction

### 4.1 Conversion formula

**Lemma 4.1 (univariate).** For $0\le\beta\le n$,
$t^\beta=\sum_{\alpha=\beta}^n\frac{\binom\alpha\beta}{\binom n\beta}B^n_\alpha(t)$,
where $B^n_\alpha(t)=\binom n\alpha t^\alpha(1-t)^{n-\alpha}$.

*Proof.* $t^\beta=t^\beta(t+(1-t))^{n-\beta}=\sum_{m=0}^{n-\beta}\binom{n-\beta}{m}t^{\beta+m}(1-t)^{n-\beta-m}$.
With $\alpha=\beta+m$ this equals
$\sum_\alpha\frac{\binom{n-\beta}{\alpha-\beta}}{\binom n\alpha}B^n_\alpha(t)$.
Finally, $\binom n\alpha\binom\alpha\beta=\binom n\beta\binom{n-\beta}{\alpha-\beta}$,
since both count the ways to choose nested sets $\beta$-subset $\subseteq$
$\alpha$-subset $\subseteq[n]$. $\square$

**Proposition 4.2 (tensor conversion, Report B eq. `bernstein-conversion`).**
Let $p(x(t))=\sum_{\beta\le d}a_\beta t^\beta$ in the free coordinates, after
the substitution $x_i=\ell_i+(u_i-\ell_i)t_i$ and elimination of fixed
coordinates ($\ell_i=u_i$). The degree bound $d$ may exceed the true degrees.
Then $p(x(t))=\sum_{\alpha\le d}b_\alpha B^d_\alpha(t)$ with
$b_\alpha=\sum_{\beta\le\alpha}a_\beta\prod_i\binom{\alpha_i}{\beta_i}/\binom{d_i}{\beta_i}$.

*Proof.* Take the product of Lemma 4.1 over the coordinates, with
$B^d_\alpha=\prod_iB^{d_i}_{\alpha_i}$, and collect terms. $\square$

**Lemma 4.3 (enclosure).** On the box, $\min_\alpha b_\alpha\le p\le\max_\alpha b_\alpha$.

*Proof.* For $t\in[0,1]^n$, $B^d_\alpha(t)\ge0$ and
$\sum_\alpha B^d_\alpha(t)=\prod_i(t_i+1-t_i)^{d_i}=1$. So $p(x(t))$ is a convex
combination of the $b_\alpha$. The substitution maps $[0,1]$ onto
$[\ell_i,u_i]$ for every free coordinate. $\square$

**Proposition 4.4 (partition certificate).** Let finitely many closed boxes
$B_1,\dots,B_N$ satisfy $\bigcup_jB_j\supseteq B$ (equality, as in Report B, is
not needed), and let $L_j\le\inf_{B_j}p$. Then $\min_jL_j\le\inf_Bp$. The same
holds for any function with a verified lower bound on each cell. *Proof.* Each
$x\in B$ lies in some $B_j$. $\square$

Report B's warning is correct and essential: coverage must be checked against
the trusted original domain, including endpoints and fixed coordinates.

### 4.2 Chord curvature correction

**Proposition 4.5.** Let $p\in C^2[a,b]$ with $a<b$, $h=b-a$, and $p''\le M$ on
$[a,b]$. Let $m_a\le p(a)$ and $m_b\le p(b)$ be verified lower bounds. Put
$$\chi(t)=m_a\frac{b-t}{h}+m_b\frac{t-a}{h}-\frac M2(t-a)(b-t).$$
Then:

1. $p\ge\chi$ on $[a,b]$.
2. (Report B's bound.) $\min_{[a,b]}p\ge\min\{m_a,m_b\}-\max\{0,M\}h^2/8$.
   For $M\le0$ the chord itself is a lower bound: $p(t)\ge m_a\frac{b-t}h+m_b\frac{t-a}h\ge\min\{m_a,m_b\}$.
3. (Sharp bound.) $\min_{[a,b]}p\ge\min_{[a,b]}\chi$, where
   $$\min_{[a,b]}\chi=\begin{cases}\frac{m_a+m_b}2-\frac{Mh^2}8-\frac{(m_b-m_a)^2}{2Mh^2}, & M>0\text{ and }|m_b-m_a|\le Mh^2/2,\\ \min\{m_a,m_b\}, & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
   This is never worse than item 2. It is best possible given only
   $(p(a),p(b),M)$: when $m_a=p(a)$ and $m_b=p(b)$, $\chi$ is itself admissible.
4. The constant $1/8$ in item 2 is tight, and $\max\{0,M\}$ cannot be replaced by $M$.

*Proof.* (1) $\varphi(t)=p(t)-Mt^2/2$ has $\varphi''=p''-M\le0$, so it is
concave and lies above its chord:
$\varphi(t)\ge\varphi(a)\frac{b-t}h+\varphi(b)\frac{t-a}h$. The chord of
$t^2$ satisfies $a^2\frac{b-t}h+b^2\frac{t-a}h-t^2=(t-a)(b-t)$. Adding $Mt^2/2$
back gives $p(t)\ge p(a)\frac{b-t}h+p(b)\frac{t-a}h-\frac M2(t-a)(b-t)$. The
weights $(b-t)/h$ and $(t-a)/h$ are nonnegative on $[a,b]$, so replacing
$p(a),p(b)$ by $m_a\le p(a)$ and $m_b\le p(b)$ preserves the inequality.
An equivalent route is the interpolation error formula
$p(t)-\text{chord}(t)=\tfrac12p''(\xi)(t-a)(t-b)$ together with $(t-a)(t-b)\le0$.

(2) The chord is at least $\min\{m_a,m_b\}$. If $M\ge0$, then
$(t-a)(b-t)\le h^2/4$ gives the correction $Mh^2/8$. If $M<0$, the correction
term $-\frac M2(t-a)(b-t)$ is nonnegative.

(3) Write $s=(t-a)/h\in[0,1]$, $K=Mh^2/2$ and $\Delta=m_b-m_a$. Then
$\chi=m_a+(\Delta-K)s+Ks^2$. For $M\le0$, $\chi$ is concave, so its minimum is
$\min\{m_a,m_b\}$. For $M>0$, the stationary point is $s^*=1/2-\Delta/(2K)$, which
lies in $[0,1]$ if and only if $|\Delta|\le K$. There
$\chi(s^*)=m_a-(\Delta-K)^2/(4K)=\frac{m_a+m_b}2-\frac K4-\frac{\Delta^2}{4K}$, which
is the displayed value. Otherwise the minimum is at an endpoint. Comparison
with item 2: $\frac{m_a+m_b}2-\min\{m_a,m_b\}=|\Delta|/2$, and
$|\Delta|/2-\Delta^2/(4K)=\frac{|\Delta|}{2}(1-\frac{|\Delta|}{2K})\ge0$ when
$|\Delta|\le K$. Optimality: $\chi''=M$, $\chi(a)=m_a$ and $\chi(b)=m_b$, so $\chi$
satisfies every hypothesis.

(4) Take $p=\frac M2(t-\frac{a+b}2)^2$ with $M>0$. Then
$p(a)=p(b)=Mh^2/8$ and $\min p=0$, which equals the bound. Now take
$p=-t^2$ on $[0,2]$ with $M=-2$. Using $M$ in place of $\max\{0,M\}$ would give
$-4+1=-3>\min p=-4$, which is false. $\square$

Report B's text ("$p(t)-Mt^2/2$ is concave, so its chord inequality rearranges
to $p(t)\ge\text{chord}_p(t)-M(t-a)(b-t)/2$ … for $M<0$, the chord itself is a
lower bound") is correct. The bound in item 2 is the classical
linear-interpolation error bound applied in one direction. Its value in the
paper lies in being one verified step of the certification chain, not in
novelty.

## 5. Development: complete separation by column generation

### 5.1 Algorithm CG

Data: as in §2.0, with an oracle (O) of accuracy $\delta<\epsilon$. The method
has two modes. *First-cut* mode returns the first separating cut. *Deep* mode
returns a cut whose violation is within $\epsilon$ of the distance.

0. If $P=\varnothing$, return `empty_domain`. Otherwise pick a rational
   $p_0\in P$ (for example a vertex) and set $W=\{w_0\}$ with $w_0=F(p_0)-q$,
   $\mathrm{LB}=0$, and no stored cut.
1. **Master LP (exact).** Compute
   $\mathrm{UB}=\max_{c\in C_J}\min_{w\in W}c^\top w$ together with an optimal
   $\bar c$ and an optimal solution $\lambda$ of the LP dual,
   $$\min\Big\{\phi_J\big(\textstyle\sum_{w\in W}\lambda_ww\big):\lambda\in\Delta_W\Big\}.$$
   Equivalently, solve $\min\sum_{j\notin J}(e^+_j+e^-_j)+\sum_{j\in J}e^+_j$
   subject to $\sum_w\lambda_ww-e^++e^-=0$, $\sum_w\lambda_w=1$, and
   $\lambda,e^\pm\ge0$.
2. **Stop test.** First-cut mode: stop if $\mathrm{UB}\le\epsilon$. Deep mode:
   stop if $\mathrm{UB}\le\mathrm{LB}+\epsilon$. On stopping, return the stored
   cut if there is one. Otherwise return `within_tolerance` with the points
   $p_w$ and weights $\lambda_w>0$.
3. **Exact support.** Call (O) at $\bar c$ to get $L,p$, and set
   $v=L-\bar c^\top q$. If $v>\mathrm{LB}$: set $\mathrm{LB}=v$ and store the
   cut $(\bar c,L)$. In first-cut mode, return it at once.
4. Add $w=F(p)-q$ to $W$ and go to step 1.

**Lemma 5.1 (LP facts).**

1. The two LPs in step 1 are a primal–dual pair with equal optimal values.
2. The dual has a basic optimal solution with at most $k+1$ positive weights $\lambda_w$.
3. If $\mathrm{UB}>0$, then every optimal $\bar c$ has $\|\bar c\|_\infty=1$.

*Proof.* (1) Write the $\phi_J$ LP in standard form $\min b^\top y$ subject to
$Ay=r$, $y=(\lambda,e^+,e^-)\ge0$. The rows are
"$\sum_w\lambda_ww_j-e^+_j+e^-_j=0$" ($j=1,\dots,k$) and
"$\sum_w\lambda_w=1$", so $r=(0,\dots,0,1)$. Costs are $0$ on $\lambda$ and
$1$ on every $e^+_j$. On $e^-_j$ the cost is $1$ for $j\notin J$ and $0$ for
$j\in J$; for $j\in J$, $e^-_j$ is the cone slack. For fixed $\lambda$ with
$v=\sum_w\lambda_ww$, the optimal $e^\pm$ give cost $|v_j|$ for $j\notin J$ and
$\max\{v_j,0\}$ for $j\in J$, so the LP value is
$\min_\lambda\phi_J(\sum_w\lambda_ww)$.

The LP dual is $\max\{r^\top u:A^\top u\le b\}$ with $u=(u_1,\dots,u_k,\tau)$.
Its constraints, column by column:

- column $\lambda_w$ gives $\sum_jw_ju_j+\tau\le0$;
- column $e^+_j$ gives $-u_j\le1$;
- column $e^-_j$ gives $u_j\le1$ for $j\notin J$ and $u_j\le0$ for $j\in J$.

Substitute $c=-u_{1:k}$. The bound constraints become exactly $c\in C_J$, and
the dual becomes $\max\{\tau:\tau\le c^\top w\ (w\in W),\ c\in C_J\}$, which is
the master LP. Both LPs are feasible, so by strong LP duality their optimal
values are equal.

(2) The standard form has $k+1$ equality rows, so a basic optimal solution,
which exists because the LP is feasible and bounded below by $0$, has at most
$k+1$ positive variables.

(3) The model $c\mapsto\min_{w\in W}c^\top w$ is positively homogeneous;
apply the argument of Lemma 2.2(3). $\square$

**Theorem 5.2 (correctness and finite termination of CG).** Assume §2.0 and (O).
Then:

1. *(Soundness.)* A returned cut $(\bar c,L)$ satisfies $\bar c\in C_J$, is
   valid on $U_J$, and has $L>\bar c^\top q$. A `within_tolerance` answer
   carries points $x_i\in P$ and rational weights with
   $\phi_J(\sum\lambda_iF(x_i)-q)\le\epsilon$, so
   $\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)\le\epsilon$. If the dual solution is basic, it
   uses at most $k+1$ points.
2. *(Deep mode.)* A returned cut also satisfies
   $L-\bar c^\top q\ge\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)-\epsilon$. The final
   $\mathrm{UB}$ and $\mathrm{LB}$ are certified bounds:
   $\mathrm{LB}\le\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)\le\mathrm{UB}\le\mathrm{LB}+\epsilon$.
3. *(Alternative.)* If $\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)>\epsilon$, a cut is
   returned. If $q\in U_J$, no cut is returned.
4. *(Termination.)* The number $T$ of oracle calls satisfies
   $$T\le 2k\Big\lceil\frac{2R}{\epsilon-\delta}\Big\rceil^{k-1}\qquad(T=0\text{ if }F(p_0)=q).$$

*Proof.* (1)–(3) follow from Lemma 2.3 and Lemma 5.1(1)–(2). The master value
$\mathrm{UB}$ equals the value $\phi_J(\sum\lambda_ww)$ of a near-hull
certificate, so $\mathrm{UB}\ge\operatorname{dist}_1$. Every stored violation
is a lower bound on $\operatorname{dist}_1$. In first-cut mode, stopping
without a cut means $\mathrm{UB}\le\epsilon$. If $\operatorname{dist}>\epsilon$,
the method cannot stop without a cut, and termination (item 4) then forces a
cut. In deep mode the returned violation is
$\mathrm{LB}\ge\mathrm{UB}-\epsilon\ge\operatorname{dist}-\epsilon$. A valid
cut cannot strictly separate a point of $U_J$.

(4) Let $\bar c_1,\dots,\bar c_T$ be the master solutions at which the oracle
is called. Let $W_t$ be the column set when $\bar c_t$ is computed and
$\mathrm{LB}_t$ the value of $\mathrm{LB}$ at that time. Let
$w_s=F(p_{\bar c_s})-q$ be the column added after call $s$, and $v_s$ the
violation computed at call $s$. Because call $t$ happens,
$\mathrm{UB}_t>\mathrm{LB}_t+\epsilon$. In first-cut mode
$\mathrm{LB}_t=0$, since a positive violation causes an immediate return.
Also $\mathrm{LB}_t\ge v_s$ for all $s<t$, as LB is a running maximum starting
at $0$.

For $s<t$, $w_s\in W_t$, so
$\bar c_t^\top w_s\ge\min_{w\in W_t}\bar c_t^\top w=\mathrm{UB}_t>v_s+\epsilon$.
By (O),
$\bar c_s^\top w_s=\bar c_s^\top F(p_{\bar c_s})-\bar c_s^\top q\le L_{\bar c_s}+\delta-\bar c_s^\top q=v_s+\delta$.
Subtracting, $(\bar c_t-\bar c_s)^\top w_s>\epsilon-\delta$. On the other hand,
$|(\bar c_t-\bar c_s)^\top w_s|\le\|\bar c_t-\bar c_s\|_\infty\|w_s\|_1\le R\|\bar c_t-\bar c_s\|_\infty$.
Hence $R>0$ and $\|\bar c_t-\bar c_s\|_\infty>\eta:=(\epsilon-\delta)/R$.

Since $\mathrm{UB}_t>0$, Lemma 5.1(3) gives $\bar c_t\in S_J$. The set $S_J$ is
the union of at most $2(k-|J|)+|J|\le2k$ faces $\{c\in C_J:c_j=\pm1\}$
($j\notin J$) and $\{c\in C_J:c_j=1\}$ ($j\in J$). Each face is a product of
$k-1$ intervals of length at most $2$. Split each interval into
$\lceil2/\eta\rceil$ closed subintervals of length at most $\eta$. This covers
each face by at most $\lceil2/\eta\rceil^{k-1}$ closed boxes, and any two points
of one box are within $\infty$-distance $\eta$. So each box contains at most one
$\bar c_t$, and $T\le2k\lceil2R/(\epsilon-\delta)\rceil^{k-1}$.

If $F(p_0)=q$, then $w_0=0$, so $\mathrm{UB}=0$ and the method stops before any
call. $\square$

*Corollary 5.3 (repeated columns).* Suppose that at call $t$ the oracle returns
a point whose column $w$ is already in $W_t$. Then $v_t>\mathrm{LB}_t+\epsilon-\delta>\mathrm{LB}_t$.

- In first-cut mode, $v_t>0$, so the call returns a cut and adds no column.
- In deep mode, the stop test succeeds at the next iteration.

*Proof.* $\bar c_t^\top w\ge\mathrm{UB}_t>\mathrm{LB}_t+\epsilon$ because
$w\in W_t$, and $\bar c_t^\top w\le v_t+\delta$ by (O). This gives the first
claim. In deep mode, $W$ is unchanged, so the next master LP has value
$\mathrm{UB}_t\le\bar c_t^\top w\le v_t+\delta\le\mathrm{LB}_{t+1}+\epsilon$. $\square$

The implementation checks for a cut before its exit
`if answer[1] in points: break`. With an exact master LP, that exit could
therefore never trigger. With the numerical LP it is a heuristic exit.

**Remarks 5.4.**

- *Comparison with the grid.* The bound in Theorem 5.2(4) has the same order
  as the sharpened grid (Theorem 2.4) and never exceeds Report B's
  $(N+1)^k$ with $N=\lceil2R/(\epsilon-\delta)\rceil$, because
  $2kN^{k-1}\le(N+1)^k$ for all $N,k\ge1$: with $x=1/N\le1$, one has
  $(1+x)^k\ge1+kx+\binom k2x^2\ge2kx$. In practice CG is far
  faster. On every checked instance it used at most 6 oracle calls, while the
  bounds for those instances reached about $10^{14}$ (§7). The worst case of Kelley's
  method is poor: Nesterov (*Introductory Lectures on Convex Optimization*,
  2004, §3.3.2) gives an example where its iteration count grows exponentially
  with dimension. I did not check that statement against the source here.
  Prop. 5.5 removes the exponential dependence.
- *Certificate size.* A `within_tolerance` certificate has at most $k+1$
  points (Lemma 5.1(2)). Report B's grid certificate stores one point per grid
  normal, $(N+1)^k$ points. Replay of the CG certificate checks feasibility of
  $\le k+1$ points, a convex combination, and $\phi_J\le\epsilon$, all exactly.
- *Inexact master LP.* The proof of item 4 uses only
  $\bar c_t\in C_J$ and $\min_{w\in W_t}\bar c_t^\top w>\mathrm{LB}_t+\epsilon$,
  checked exactly. Scaling to $\|\bar c_t\|_\infty=1$ preserves this. So a
  floating-point LP may propose $\bar c$ (clipped into $C_J$ and checked
  exactly) and $\lambda$ (clipped, renormalized, and checked exactly). An exact
  LP is needed only when both checks fail near the threshold. Soundness never
  depends on the LP.
- *$\epsilon=0$ (stop test "$\mathrm{UB}\le0$").* If
  $\Delta:=\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)>\delta$, first-cut CG returns a cut after
  at most $2k(\lceil2R/(\Delta-\delta)\rceil+1)^{k-1}$ oracle calls. The proof
  is that of Theorem 5.2(4) with $\mathrm{UB}_t\ge\Delta$ in place of
  $\mathrm{UB}_t>\epsilon$; it gives $\|\bar c_t-\bar c_s\|_\infty\ge(\Delta-\delta)/R$,
  and cells of side strictly smaller than this bound are used. If $q\in U_J$,
  the method stops only when a mixture of generated points has $\phi_J=0$,
  and it may run forever. With $\delta=0$ it is thus a semi-decision procedure
  for $q\notin U_J$. The positive tolerance is what gives an a priori bound.

**Example 5.8 (exact membership without a rational certificate).** Let
$P=[-3,3]$, $F(t)=(t,(t^2-2)^2)$, $J=\varnothing$ and $q=(0,0)\in\mathbb Q^2$.
Then $q=\tfrac12F(\sqrt2)+\tfrac12F(-\sqrt2)\in H$. The inequality $z_2\ge0$
is valid on $H$ and tight at $q$. Hence every representation of $q$ as a
mixture of graph points uses only graph points on the face $\{z_2=0\}$, that
is, only $t=\pm\sqrt2$. No mixture of rational points of $P$ equals $q$. So
`within_tolerance` with $\epsilon=0$ cannot be certified in rational
arithmetic, although $q\in H$. The positive tolerance in C-SEP is necessary
for this reason, not only for complexity.

### 5.2 Oracle-polynomial variant (ellipsoid proposals)

The exponential factor in $k$ is not intrinsic. Separation is an
optimization problem in the $k$-dimensional *direction* space, and the box
$C_J$ is full-dimensional even when $U_J$ is not.

**Proposition 5.5.** Assume §2.0 with $R>0$ and (O). Put
$\eta=(\epsilon-\delta)/2$, $\rho=\min\{\eta/R,1\}$ and $\nu=\rho^k/2$. Run the
GLS central-cut ellipsoid method (GLS Theorem 3.2.1,
[[grotschel1988-geometric-algorithms-and-combinatorial-optimization]] p.99) in
$\mathbb R^k$ with outer radius $k$ and volume parameter $\nu$. Use the
following answers at a query $y\in\mathbb Q^k$:

- if $y\notin C_J$, return the unit coordinate vector $\pm e_j$ of a violated bound;
- if $y\in C_J$, call (O) at $y$, record $w_y=F(p_y)-q$, and update LB and the
  stored cut as in CG step 3, without returning;
  - if $w_y=0$, stop: $q=F(p_y)$, so the distance is $0$;
  - otherwise return $-w_y/\|w_y\|_\infty$.

After at most $N_{\rm GLS}=\lceil5k|\log\nu|+5k^2|\log(2k)|\rceil=O\big(k^2\log(kR/(\epsilon-\delta))\big)$
oracle calls (GLS (3.2.2), p.99, with logarithms as in GLS), solve the exact
master LP of CG over all recorded columns. Its value satisfies
$\mathrm{UB}\le\mathrm{LB}+\epsilon$. So the output is either a deep cut
(if $\mathrm{LB}>0$) or a $\le k+1$-point `within_tolerance` certificate, as
in Theorem 5.2(1)–(3). Its correctness is certified independently of the
ellipsoid computation.

*Proof.* Let $\mathrm{LB}^\star$ be the final value of LB and $W$ the set of
recorded columns. Define the polytope
$K^\star=\{c\in C_J: w^\top c\ge\mathrm{LB}^\star+\delta+\eta\ \ \forall w\in W\}$.

Each answer given during the run is a valid GLS separation answer for
$K^\star$ with error $0$:

- a coordinate answer separates $y$ from $C_J\supseteq K^\star$;
- for a recorded $w_y$ and any $c\in K^\star$:
  $w_y^\top c\ge\mathrm{LB}^\star+\delta+\eta>v_y+\delta\ge w_y^\top y$, by (O)
  as in Theorem 5.2(4). Hence $(-w_y)^\top c<(-w_y)^\top y$.

Extend these answers to all of $\mathbb Q^k$ by any exact separation oracle
for the polytope $K^\star$. Because GLS's method is deterministic, it produces
the same run on this valid oracle. Our oracle never asserts membership, so
GLS's output (i) cannot occur. Output (ii) therefore gives an ellipsoid
$E\supseteq K^\star$ with $\operatorname{vol}E\le\nu$. Here
$K^\star\subseteq C_J\subseteq S(0,\sqrt k)\subseteq S(0,k)$, so $K^\star$ is
circumscribed.

Suppose now $\mathrm{UB}>\mathrm{LB}^\star+\epsilon=\mathrm{LB}^\star+\delta+2\eta$,
attained at $\bar c\in C_J$. For $c\in C_J$ with $\|c-\bar c\|_\infty\le\eta/R$
and every $w\in W$, $w^\top c\ge w^\top\bar c-R\eta/R\ge\mathrm{UB}-\eta>\mathrm{LB}^\star+\delta+\eta$.
So $C_J\cap\{c:\|c-\bar c\|_\infty\le\eta/R\}\subseteq K^\star$. Each factor
interval of $C_J$ has length at least $1$ and contains $\bar c_j$, so this set
contains a box of side $\rho$. Then
$\operatorname{vol}K^\star\ge\rho^k>\nu\ge\operatorname{vol}E$, a
contradiction. $\square$

*Remarks.*

- GLS's centers have $p=8N_{\rm GLS}$ binary digits, so every oracle query has
  polynomial encoding length. The final LP has at most $N_{\rm GLS}+1$ columns.
- **Corollary 5.6 (quadratic features, fixed $d$).** Suppose the C-POLY claim
  holds: exact rational quadratic support on a rational polytope in fixed
  dimension $d$ takes polynomial time, including bit complexity (another lane
  checks this). Then for quadratic $F$ and fixed $d$ (with $k$, the number of
  rows, and the encoding all unrestricted), the deep cut / `within_tolerance`
  contract is computable in time polynomial in the input size and
  $\log(1/\epsilon)$. When $k>d+d(d+1)/2$, the number of nonconstant monomials
  of degree at most $2$, the features are affinely dependent and
  $\operatorname{conv}F(P)$ lies in a proper affine subspace. GLS's weak
  separation is then vacuous (§6), but the result still holds.
- The CG master LP can be run alongside the ellipsoid as an early stop test.
  This loses nothing, because Prop. 5.5's argument applies to any superset of
  recorded columns.
- Related certificate concepts include Nemirovski, Onn and Rothblum,
  "Accuracy certificates for computational problems with convex structure",
  *Math. Oper. Res.* 35 (2010) 52–78 (not checked against the source here).
  Our certificate is simpler: a mixture of graph points, exactly checkable.

### 5.3 Sharpest correct form of C-SEP

**Theorem 5.7 (complete $\epsilon$-separation with two-sided certificates).**
Let $P$ be a nonempty bounded rational polytope (box plus rows), $F$ a vector of
rational polynomials, $q\in\mathbb Q^k$, $J\subseteq[k]$ and
$\epsilon\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$. There is a finite exact-arithmetic algorithm that
returns:

- (a) a rational $c\in C_J$ and $\beta$ with $c^\top z\ge\beta$ valid on
  $U_J=\operatorname{conv}F(P)+\operatorname{cone}\{e_j\}_{j\in J}$,
  $\beta-c^\top q>0$, and (deep mode) $\beta-c^\top q\ge\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)-\epsilon$; or
- (b) at most $k+1$ rational points of $P$ and rational convex weights whose
  mixture $z$ satisfies $\phi_J(z-q)\le\epsilon$, hence
  $\operatorname{dist}_1(q,U_J)\le\epsilon$.

(a) is returned whenever $\operatorname{dist}_1>\epsilon$; (b) whenever no
positive-violation cut was found, in particular whenever $q\in U_J$. The
oracle-call count is at most $2k\lceil2R/(\epsilon-\delta)\rceil^{k-1}$ (CG)
or $O(k^2\log(kR/(\epsilon-\delta)))$ (Prop. 5.5). Here $\delta=0$ for
quadratic $F$ (exact polytope support) and $\delta=\delta_M\le\epsilon/2$ for
general polynomial $F$ (Lemma 2.6). The empty domain is detected exactly by
vertex enumeration.

The theorem needs no full-dimensionality of $P$ or of $\operatorname{conv}F(P)$,
no inner ball, and no constraint qualification. The cut side is exact. Only
the membership side is approximate. The $\ell_1$/$\ell_\infty$ pairing is
chosen because the dual unit ball is a rational box, which keeps every step
an exact LP. Scaled coordinates (as in C-SCREEN) give a weighted $\ell_1$
distance by the same proof. Report B's grid theorem is the special case
"grid proposals instead of master-LP proposals", with a larger certificate.

## 6. Relation to Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver weak separation

Definitions (GLS 1988; locators refer to the local copy
[[grotschel1988-geometric-algorithms-and-combinatorial-optimization]]):

- $S(K,\epsilon)$ is the Euclidean $\epsilon$-neighborhood of $K$, and
  $S(K,-\epsilon)=\{x:S(x,\epsilon)\subseteq K\}$ is the set of points "deep
  in $K$".
- **WSEP** (2.1.13), p.63: given $y$ and $\delta>0$, either assert
  $y\in S(K,\delta)$, or find $c$ with $\|c\|_\infty=1$ and
  $c^\top x\le c^\top y+\delta$ for every $x\in S(K,-\delta)$.
- **WOPT**, WVIOL, WVAL, and WMEM are defined in (2.1.10)–(2.1.14), pp.62–63.
- *Circumscribed* means an outer radius $R$ with $K\subseteq S(0,R)$ is given.
  *Well-bounded* adds an inner radius $r$. *Centered* adds a center $a_0$ with
  $S(a_0,r)\subseteq K$ (Def. 2.1.16, p.65).
- The Chapter 4 equivalences are stated for convex **bodies**, i.e. compact
  full-dimensional convex sets (p.115).
- Main implications: WSEP ⇒ WVIOL for circumscribed bodies (Thm 4.2.2,
  p.117); WVAL ⇒ WSEP for circumscribed bodies (Thm 4.4.4, p.128); WOPT ⇒ WSEP
  for every convex body with no radius information (Thm 4.4.7, p.129); WMEM ⇒
  WVIOL needs a centered body (Thm 4.3.2, p.119).

Why GLS does not give the contract needed here:

1. *Full-dimensionality.* Our $K=\operatorname{conv}F(P)$ is often
   lower-dimensional. This happens with affinely dependent features, a
   lower-dimensional $P$, or fixed coordinates. For $J\neq\varnothing$, $U_J$
   is also unbounded. If $K$ is lower-dimensional, then $S(K,-\delta)=\varnothing$
   and WSEP's alternative (ii) holds for **every** normalized $c$; the answer
   carries no information.
2. *Almost-validity.* Even for full-dimensional $K$, WSEP's hyperplane is
   required to hold only on the deep part $S(K,-\delta)$, and only up to slack
   $\delta$. It need not be valid on $K$, and it need not exclude $y$. A cut
   for a MINLP relaxation must be valid on all of $K$. Recomputing its exact
   support value restores validity but can destroy separation.
3. *Strong problems.* Exact membership can lack a rational certificate, as
   Example 5.8 shows. GLS's strong equivalences (Ch. 6) are for polyhedra with
   given facet complexity, and $\operatorname{conv}F(P)$ is not a polyhedron.
4. *Norms.* GLS neighborhoods are Euclidean; ours are $\ell_1$. This is an
   inessential factor $\sqrt k$.

What is used instead is a *strong support oracle*: the exact value
$\min_Pc^\top F$ for rational $c$, which C-POLY supplies for quadratic $F$.
The contract mixes a strong cut side with a weak membership side. Under this
oracle, applying GLS's central-cut ellipsoid method in the direction space
(Prop. 5.5) gives the contract in oracle-polynomial time. It needs neither
full-dimensionality nor boundedness of $U_J$. The reason is that the region it
localizes, a subset of the box $C_J$, is always full-dimensional.

**Correction to Report B's wording.** The sentence "The familiar
polynomial-time weak-oracle equivalence requires a specified encoded
convex-body model, including its inner and outer radius information" should
be replaced. The key implication WOPT ⇒ WSEP needs no radius information
(GLS Thm 4.4.7). What GLS assume is a full-dimensional body. What they deliver
is only an almost-valid hyperplane. Suggested text is in §8.
