\begin{abstract}
Global solvers for nonconvex mixed-integer nonlinear programs relax nonlinear
expressions one at a time and lose their dependence through shared variables.
Support cuts of the convex hull of their joint graph retain this dependence.
Gluing exact pair hulls on shared moments is known to lose strength; we
quantify the loss. For a family of quadratic directions on a three-variable
path, the glued relaxation misses $\delta^2/2$ if two point sets at distance
$\delta$ interleave and nothing otherwise; an interface that shares $\kappa$
moments gives no positive bound exactly when the local zero sets alternate at
least $\kappa+1$ times; and from four variables on, joint blocks can be
strictly stronger than dense first-level semidefinite relaxations with all
McCormick inequalities. For quadratic stars with $k$ leaves, $m$ center--leaf
rows and $m_0$ center rows we give an exact support algorithm with
$O(m_0+(m+k)\log(m+k))$ operations. Eliminating model rows gives cuts in the
original variables, valid as stored if their bound is certified for the exact
binary64 direction, rounding is corrected and the stored row is checked. Every
cut recorded under our protocols passed replay, whereas sample-based constants
would have cut off feasible solutions. On MINLPLib models the cuts did not
help SCIP. On constructed families they closed nearly all of SCIP's root gap.
A configuration selected after a diagnostic solved all 20 fresh path
instances, against at most 10 for SCIP and 5 for Gurobi; with a binding
coupling row, SCIP without one presolve step did as well, and on stars Gurobi
solved the most.
\end{abstract}
