# Certified support cuts for shared nonlinear expressions and quadratic blocks

`main.tex` is the manuscript and `main.pdf` the compiled version. The author
field is intentionally blank. The manuscript rewrites and extends the two
internal reports in `research-20261002-convexification/` and
`research-20261003-convexification/`; those folders, their code and their
experiment records are unchanged.

Build from this directory:

```sh
latexmk -pdf -interaction=nonstopmode -halt-on-error main.tex
```

The Git checkout includes compact records for every saved campaign run, table
verification code, frozen inputs and source snapshots, and replay receipts.
See [compact verification](experiments/compact/README.md) for checks that need
no raw-data download. Complete raw run records and cut witnesses are preserved
in the [evidence archives](../artifacts/README.md); restore them before full
certificate replay. The raw files also remain at their original local paths.

## Main results

The manuscript quantifies when exact pair hulls glued on shared moments miss
the joint hull (interleaving theorem on a path, alternation criterion for kappa
shared moments, a full gap for every kappa) and shows that from four variables
on joint blocks can be strictly stronger than the dense first-level
semidefinite relaxation with all McCormick inequalities (exact rational
certificates in `verification/M7_dense_fullgap.py`). It gives an exact
`O(m0 + (m+k) log(m+k))` support algorithm for quadratic stars with
center-leaf rows and exact face enumeration for small polytopes, states the
closure of original-variable aggregation cuts, and specifies the checks under
which a numerically chosen cut is valid as SCIP stores it.

The certified SCIP separator was evaluated in five campaigns. All 274,489 cut
records of campaigns 3-5 passed replay (92,222 distinct rows). Constants from
samples or local minimization would have cut off feasible points; a numerical
global solve (Gurobi) was almost always right but once reported optimality for
a constant 1.8e-4 too high. On MINLPLib models the cuts were neutral for SCIP
and SCIP's disabled separators were stronger. On constructed path families the
cuts closed nearly all of SCIP's root gap; a configuration chosen after a post
hoc diagnostic solved all 20 fresh instances (SCIP at most 10, Gurobi 5); with
a binding coupling row SCIP without implicit discreteness did as well, faster.
On constrained stars with up to 16 leaves, star blocks made the root bound
optimal to within 1e-6, while Gurobi solved the most instances (22 of 30
against 19). Native SCIP remains the recommended default.
