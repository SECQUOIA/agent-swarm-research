The online search also combined “near-optimal”, “shattered”, “smoothed”,
“parametric”, “approximation”, “enumeration”, “Lawler”, and “sandwich”. It
found no closer matching primary theorem. Most returned results were unrelated
uses of analytical smoothing and were not used. Failure to find an identical
theorem is not evidence of absence. The manuscript therefore limits its novelty
statement to the stated combination and its exact assumptions, qualified by
“To the best of our knowledge.” The new statement is a complete parametric
dictionary theorem, not an assertion that the ingredients, smoothing strategy,
or optimization-to-enumeration principle are new.

The Röglin–Teng author manuscript was reopened for its generalized-gap and
moment context; its abstract and Sections 6.1–6.2 match the previously recorded
comparison. The affine-rank companion proof permits arbitrary deterministic
offsets and adds an explicit interval-mass box bound; it is presented as an
application of the classical higher-gap method. The scalar `2r-1` envelope
bound is the elementary order-two Davenport–Schinzel argument, proved in full
with the standard merge construction, so no uninspected textbook theorem is
needed. The discrepancy transfer is proved by half-line sections, product-law
replacement, and deterministic tie-breaking limits, rather than attributed to
an unverified general quadrature theorem.

The new region and subdivision corollaries combine the manuscript's existing
all-label moment/enumeration machinery with these classical algebraic facts.
They close the old notes' coarse atomic region/subdivision gaps; they do not
preserve the sharper continuous planar constants, construct maximal connected
regions, or establish a new general topology or CAD result. No further priority
claim is inferred from the source search.
