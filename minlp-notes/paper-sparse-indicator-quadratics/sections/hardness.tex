\section{Exact hardness at width two}\label{sec:hardness}
Unless $\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{ZPP}$, the numerical dependence in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} cannot in general be replaced by dependence on
encoding length. We first give the underlying
hardness result. Additive constants in the objectives below are included to
display nonnegative residuals; they can be subtracted from the decision threshold.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hardness}
Fix a rational $0<\theta\le1/10$, and put
\[
 \Delta=\frac{\theta^2(1-\theta^2)}{1+\theta^4}.
\]
The rational threshold decision problem for indicator quadratic optimization
is NP-complete under each of the following restrictions. In every case the
matrix has bandwidth two, maximum graph degree at most four, treewidth and pathwidth
two, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard-spectrum}
 (1-3\theta)I\preceq Q\preceq(1+3\theta+2\theta^2)I.
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item The matrix depends only on the dimension and $\theta$, all penalties
are positive, and the reduction has value at most $\Delta/4$ on YES
instances and greater than $\Delta$ on NO instances. Linear coefficients
and penalties may have exponentially large magnitudes.
\item The same matrix family has $|c_i|\le2$ and $0<\lambda_i\le1$.
The decision gap may be exponentially small.
\item All penalties equal one and $|c_i|\le9$. The matrix now depends on
the input, through possibly exponentially small nonzero entries.
\end{enumerate}
The data have polynomial binary encoding length. No strong NP-hardness
claim, or fixed positive gap in the last two cases, is made.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take a SUBSET SUM instance \cite{Karp} with positive integers $a_1,\ldots,a_n,B$,
where $n\ge2$. For the first claim define
$A_i=a_i\theta^{-(n-i+1)}$, $\eta=\Delta/(4n)$, and $s_0=0$.
Use indicators $z_i,w_i$ for the respective continuous variables $x_i,s_i$,
and minimize
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard-objective}
 F=\sum_i(x_i^2-2A_ix_i+A_i^2z_i)
   +\sum_i(s_i-\theta s_{i-1}-\theta x_i)^2
   +\theta^2(s_n-B)^2+\eta\sum_iw_i.
\end{equation}
On indicator-feasible points, the first summand is $(x_i-A_i z_i)^2$.
This identity is used only on that feasible set; the continuous quadratic
matrix in \eqref{eq:hard-objective} is independent of the indicators.
If $\sum_i a_i z_i=B$, take $x_i=A_i z_i$,
$s_i=\theta s_{i-1}+\theta x_i$, and every $w_i=1$.
All squares vanish, giving $F=\Delta/4$.

For the converse, write $e_i=x_i-A_i z_i$,
$r_i=s_i-\theta s_{i-1}-\theta x_i$, and $u=s_n-B$.
For every feasible point,
\[
 B-\sum_i a_i z_i
 =\sum_i\theta^{n-i+1}e_i+\sum_i\theta^{n-i}r_i-u.
\]
On a NO instance the left side has absolute value at least one.
Cauchy--Schwarz, applied to $(e,r,\theta u)$, gives
\[
 F\ge\left[\theta^{-2}+(1+\theta^2)
                    \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\theta^{2j}\right]^{-1}>\Delta.
\]
Thus arbitrary continuous adjustments cannot remove the gap.

In the order $(x_1,s_1,\ldots,x_n,s_n)$, every diagonal entry is
$1+\theta^2$. The only off-diagonal entries are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard-edges}
 Q_{x_i,s_i}=-\theta,\qquad
 Q_{s_{i-1},s_i}=-\theta,\qquad
 Q_{s_{i-1},x_i}=\theta^2\quad(i\ge2),
\end{equation}
with only the first type present at $i=1$. The bags
$\{x_1,s_1\},\{s_1,x_2,s_2\},\ldots,\{s_{n-1},x_n,s_n\}$
give a width-two path decomposition; a triangle makes the width exactly two.
The maximum degree is at most four. Absolute off-diagonal row sums are at
most $3\theta+\theta^2$, proving \eqref{eq:hard-spectrum} by Gershgorin.
In particular, the matrix is strictly diagonally dominant.
For fixed rational $\theta$, the powers defining $A_i$ and all remaining
data have polynomial encoding length. The rational threshold $\Delta/4$
therefore proves NP-hardness. It also proves NP-hardness of approximation
within absolute error $\Delta/4$, since the resulting YES and NO intervals
are disjoint.

For the second claim let $M=1+B+\sum_iA_i$, substitute
$(x,s)=M(\widehat x,\widehat s)$, and divide the objective by $M^2$.
The quadratic matrix is unchanged, $A_i,B$ become $A_i/M,B/M$, and the
state penalty becomes $\eta/M^2$. This gives the stated coefficient bounds
and scales both thresholds by $M^{-2}$. Even the optimizing coordinates are
bounded: the all-zero point has value at most $\theta^2$, so every
optimal control and dynamic residual has absolute value at most $\theta$. Hence
$|\widehat x_i|\le1+\theta$ and
$|\widehat s_i|\le\theta(2+\theta)/(1-\theta)<1$.

For the third claim, restrict to $0<B\le A:=\sum_i a_i$, since the other
instances are trivial. Set
\[
 b_i=\frac{a_i\theta^i}{A},\qquad
 T=\frac{B\theta^n}{A},\qquad D=4,
\]
so $0<b_i\le\theta$ and $0<T\le1$. With $t_0=D$, indicators $z_i,\zeta_i$,
and no constraints beyond their zero indicators, minimize
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-hard}
 H=\sum_i(x_i^2-2x_i+z_i)
 +\sum_i[t_i-\theta t_{i-1}-b_i x_i-D(1-\theta)]^2
 +\theta^2(t_n-D-T)^2+\sum_i\zeta_i.
\end{equation}
The first dynamic residual is $t_1-b_1x_1-D$.
For each $z$, let $s_0^z=0$ and
$s_i^z=\theta s_{i-1}^z+b_i z_i$.
Then $0\le s_i^z\le\theta^i$ and
$s_n^z=T$ exactly when $\sum_i a_i z_i=B$.
Put $e=x-z$, $d=t-D\mathbf1-s^z$, and let $L$ be the lower shift matrix.
The control and dynamic squares are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:activation-quadratic}
 \|e\|^2+\|(I-\theta L)d-\operatorname{Diag}(b)e\|^2
 \ge(1-3\theta)(\|e\|^2+\|d\|^2).
\end{equation}
Indeed, in this quadratic form a control row has diagonal $1+b_i^2$
and off-diagonal absolute sum at most $(1+\theta)b_i$.
An internal state row has diagonal $1+\theta^2$ and row sum at most
$2\theta+b_i+\theta b_{i+1}\le3\theta+\theta^2$;
the final row has diagonal one and row sum at most $2\theta$.
These bounds prove \eqref{eq:activation-quadratic}.

If $k$ state indicators vanish, the corresponding $d_i\le-D$, and thus
\[
 H\ge n-k+(1-3\theta)D^2k\ge n+10.2k.
\]
The feasible choice $x=z=0$, $t=D\mathbf1$, $\zeta=\mathbf1$ has
value $n+\theta^2T^2<n+1$. Consequently every optimum has all state
indicators on. Its value is $n$ plus a sum of squares, and equals $n$
exactly for a YES instance. Positive definiteness gives attainment, so
every NO value is strictly greater than $n$.
To quantify the gap, let
$r_i=t_i-\theta t_{i-1}-b_ix_i-D(1-\theta)$ and $u=t_n-D-T$.
Using $e=x-z$ and $t_0=D$, the recurrence gives
\[
 \frac{\theta^n}{A}\left(B-\sum_i a_i z_i\right)
 =\sum_i\frac{\theta^n a_i}{A}e_i
       +\sum_i\theta^{n-i}r_i-u.
\]
At a NO optimum, Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives
\[
 H-n\ge\frac{\theta^{2n}/A^2}
 {\theta^{2n}\sum_i a_i^2/A^2+
       \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\theta^{2j}+\theta^{-2}}>0.
\]
The residual vector is $(e,r,\theta u)$, and the numerator can be
exponentially small.

The homogeneous matrix has diagonals $1+b_i^2$ on controls and
$1+\theta^2$ on states. Its edges are $-b_i,-\theta,\theta b_i$
in the positions of \eqref{eq:hard-edges}. All are nonzero, and the same
row bounds prove the graph claims and \eqref{eq:hard-spectrum}.
Writing $h_1=D$, $h_i=D(1-\theta)$ for $i>1$, the linear coefficients are
$-2+2h_i b_i$ on controls, $-2h_i+2\theta h_{i+1}$ on nonterminal states,
and $-2h_n-2\theta^2(D+T)$ on the last state. Their magnitudes are below
nine. The constant is at most $16n+1/4$ and all data have polynomial
encoding length.

Finally, for any rational positive-definite instance, a selected support
is an NP certificate. Its unique continuous optimum is
$-Q_{JJ}^{-1}c_J/2$ and its value has polynomial rational encoding length,
by determinant bounds. Exact linear algebra verifies the threshold in
polynomial time. Supports containing zero optimizing coordinates remain
valid certificates. This proves membership in NP in all three cases.
\end{proof}

For the first family, dividing the objective by $1+\theta^2$ gives a
unit-diagonal matrix with
$\|Q-I\|_2\le(3\theta+\theta^2)/(1+\theta^2)\le4\theta$.
The third family directly has $\|Q-I\|_2\le3\theta+2\theta^2$.
Thus either hardness construction can approach the identity arbitrarily
closely. These proofs do not combine an input-independent matrix with unit
penalties. Also, sign changes cannot make
the triangle edges all nonpositive: their sign product is positive and
is unchanged by coordinate sign changes.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:magnitude}
For any fixed rational noise radius $\sigma>0$, an exact solver with
expected bit complexity polynomial only in the encoded input length,
uniformly over all linear coefficients and penalties under the finite-grid
noise model, would imply $\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{ZPP}$, even for the
fixed matrix family in Theorem~\ref{thm:hardness}(1).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
There are $n_{\mathrm{tot}}=2n$ indicators. Choose an integer $s\ge1$ with
$3s^2\Delta/4>2n_{\mathrm{tot}}\sigma$ and $s^2\Delta/(4n)>\sigma$; such an $s$
has polynomial encoding length and is computable by rational comparisons.
Let $c_0$ denote the additive constant in the expanded objective
\eqref{eq:hard-objective}. Replace $(c,\lambda,c_0)$ by
$(sc,s^2\lambda,s^2c_0)$, keeping $Q$.
The substitution $y=sx$ multiplies every old objective by $s^2$.
Any noise with $|\xi_i|\le\sigma$ changes the optimum by at most
$n_{\mathrm{tot}}\sigma$. The perturbed YES value is therefore at most
$s^2\Delta/4+n_{\mathrm{tot}}\sigma$, whereas the NO value exceeds
$s^2\Delta-n_{\mathrm{tot}}\sigma$.
These thresholds are disjoint for every draw, and all perturbed penalties
remain positive. Sample the solver's rational finite-grid noise and compare
its exact answer with their midpoint. The assumed expected bound would
give a zero-error expected-polynomial algorithm for SUBSET SUM.
\end{proof}
This scaling changes both $c$ and $\lambda$. It does not establish the
same obstruction under bounded indicator penalties.

Continuous chains encoding SUBSET SUM already occur in conditional linear
Gaussian inference \cite[Section~3]{LernerParr}; that mechanism is not new.
The contribution here is the simultaneous restriction to one positive-definite
quadratic, uncoupled zero indicators with positive costs, and a near-identity
bandwidth-two matrix, with the three distinct numerical regimes above.
Das and Kempe's bandwidth FPTAS \cite[Theorem~4.1]{DasKempe} constrains
the predictor-only covariance graph, the graph corresponding to our
quadratic matrix. Their exact tree result
\cite[Theorem~5.1]{DasKempe} instead includes the response vertex.
They impose a cardinality constraint, and the FPTAS gives a relative
approximation to explained variance; those guarantees differ from our
penalized exact problem. General sparse approximation hardness also
precedes this work: \c{C}ivril \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Civril} proves
inapproximability for a cardinality-constrained squared-projection
objective, without the graph, positive-definiteness, or spectral
restrictions used here.
The additive approximation guarantee for banded indicator quadratics in
\cite[Theorem~1]{GomezBanded} depends on numerical linear-coefficient and
accuracy bounds, consistently with the present reductions.
