\section{Exact messages at fixed treewidth}\label{sec:spectral}
We now provide the restricted additive oracle and its bit bound for
\eqref{eq:message}. This also fixes the numerical dependence of the main
result.

\subsection{Uniform conditional bounds}
For $k=|S|$ and any $A\subseteq I$, principal submatrices inherit
\eqref{eq:spectral}, and $\|Q_{AS}\|_2\le H$. Thus on $[-R,R]^k$,
\begin{align}
 \|x_A(t)\|_2&\le\frac{C\sqrt n+2H\sqrt{k}R}{2\mu}
                    \le M_k:=\frac{nC+2HkR}{2\mu},\label{eq:boundx}\\
 \|\nabla q_A(t)\|_2&=\|2Q_{SA}x_A(t)\|_2
                    \le L_k:=2HM_k.\label{eq:boundL}
\end{align}
The second formula follows by differentiating \eqref{eq:branch}.
The bounds are rational, uniform over all supports, and unchanged when
some indicator bits are fixed. The term involving $R$ is needed: a
coordinate bound for the global optimizer alone need not bound conditional
optimizers with a prescribed boundary.

\subsection{A restricted grid oracle}
Fix a rational boundary $t$ and an accuracy $\varepsilon>0$. First suppose
$M_k>0$. Let $B$ be the least positive even integer satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gridB}
 B^2\varepsilon\ge HnM_k^2.
\end{equation}
This choice gives $B\le2+M_k\sqrt{Hn/\varepsilon}$.
The square root is used only for this bound; $B$ is found by integer
comparisons of the rational inequality. Use the coordinate grid
\[
 G_B=\{-M_k+2M_kj/B:0\le j\le B\}.
\]
Each vertex $i\in I$ has an inactive state $(z_i,x_i)=(0,0)$ and active
states $(1,a)$ for $a\in G_B$. Since $B$ is even, active zero is included
and is distinct from inactive zero. Fixed bits delete incompatible states.

The grid objective is a sum of the unary costs
\[
 Q_{ii}x_i^2+(c_i+2(Q_{IS}t)_i)x_i+(\lambda_i+\xi_i)z_i
\]
and the edge costs $2Q_{ij}x_ix_j$ for $i<j$ in $I$ with $Q_{ij}\ne0$.
Restrict every bag
of the supplied decomposition to $I$; empty bags are harmless. Assign each
unary and edge cost to one bag containing its variables, exactly once.
For a bag $u$ and an assignment $s$ of its local states, let $a_u(s)$
be its assigned cost. Root this tree, write $B_u$ for its bag, and let
$D_u(s)$ be its optimal subtree cost conditional on $s$. The recurrence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DP}
 D_u(s)=a_u(s)+\sum_{v\text{ child of }u}
       \min_{r:\ r|_{B_u\cap B_v}=s|_{B_u\cap B_v}}D_v(r).
\end{equation}
The running-intersection property makes different child interiors disjoint
and ensures that the separator agreement is sufficient for compatibility.
Induction from the leaf bags shows that \eqref{eq:DP} computes the stated
conditional minimum of the factors assigned to each subtree. Minimizing at the root gives the
exact grid optimum. Each child table is first minimized over its states at
each separator assignment. Reading these projected tables at the parent
avoids a product of two bag tables. With at most $B+2$ states per vertex,
the arithmetic operation count is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DPcost}
 O\bigl((b+n^2)(B+2)^{w+1}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Back-pointers recover the minimizing support. No balance or degree bound
on the decomposition is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Certified additive oracle]\label{lem:gridoracle}
Solve this grid problem under any fixed-bit restrictions. For its support
$A$, return the exact noisy value $F_A(t)$ from \eqref{eq:noisybranch}.
This return satisfies \eqref{eq:oracle}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a truly optimal support $A^*$ for the restricted continuous problem
and its unique continuous minimizer. It lies in $[-M_k,M_k]^I$ by
\eqref{eq:boundx}. Round active coordinates to $G_B$ and keep inactive
coordinates zero. The error $e$ has
$\|e\|_2^2\le nM_k^2/B^2$. Stationarity on $A^*$ cancels the linear
term in this error, so the objective increase is
\[
 e^TQ_{II}e\le H\|e\|_2^2\le HnM_k^2/B^2\le\varepsilon.
\]
The optimal grid solution is no worse. Reoptimizing continuously on its
support can only decrease its cost, and preserves the fixed bits. Its exact
support value is therefore a feasible upper bound within $\varepsilon$ of
the restricted optimum.
\end{proof}

If $M_k=0$, every support minimizer is zero. Each free bit then minimizes
its noisy penalty $\lambda_i+\xi_i$ independently and fixed bits are retained; this gives an exact
oracle directly. Every restriction in this particular model is feasible
because an active bit permits zero. The empty-cell convention in
Lemma~\ref{lem:enumeration} is needed for the more general family $Z$.

\subsection{The algorithm and its expected bit complexity}
\begin{theorem}[Complete spectral messages]\label{thm:main}
Fix $w$. Given \eqref{eq:problem}--\eqref{eq:spectral}, a width-$w$
decomposition with $b$ bags, rational $R\ge0$, and rational $\sigma>0$,
let each $\xi_i$ independently be uniform on the grid \eqref{eq:noisegrid},
where $N$ is the smallest power of two at least
$2n$. There is an algorithm that, for every noise realization, constructs
a rational quadratic dictionary for every rooted subtree message on its
box $[-R,R]^{|S|}$ and returns a global optimizer of
\eqref{eq:problem}. Each dictionary includes every support optimal anywhere
on its box. The algorithm uses $n\log_2N=O(n\log(n+1))$ random bits.

More explicitly, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mainparameters}
 K=w+1,\quad M=\frac{nC+2HKR}{2\mu},\quad L=2HM,\quad
 \varepsilon=\frac{\sigma}{6n},\quad
 J_*=(1+12nKRL/\sigma)^K.
\end{equation}
Let $B$ be the least positive even integer satisfying \eqref{eq:gridB}
with $M$ in place of $M_k$ (take $B=2$
if $M=0$). If $\ell$ is the total binary input length, a polynomial $P_w$
independent of the numerical values bounds the expected number of bit
operations by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicitwork}
 P_w(\ell)\,bJ_*\,(B+2)^{w+1},\qquad
 B+2\le4+Mn\sqrt{6H/\sigma}.
\end{equation}
In particular, this is polynomial in the input length and the numerical
quantities $C,H,\mu^{-1},R,\sigma^{-1}$ at fixed $w$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Sample the noise once. For each requested message, use
Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary} with $\phi=1/(2\sigma)$, $\tau=1/N$,
and the common bounds $M,L$. They dominate \eqref{eq:boundx}--\eqref{eq:boundL}
for every separator. Use the midpoint net described in
\eqref{eq:netsize}, or one point for a singleton box or $L=0$. Its
cardinality is at most $J_*$. At each point the restricted oracle is
Lemma~\ref{lem:gridoracle}. Form the union of its outputs and construct
the noisy branch $F_A$ in \eqref{eq:noisybranch} for each retained support.
Equations
\eqref{eq:dictionaryexact} and \eqref{eq:envelope} prove correctness for
every sample. Minimizing the noisy constants $F_A$ in the empty-boundary
dictionary gives a global minimizing support;
\eqref{eq:optimizer} gives its rational optimizer. Affine minimizer maps may
also be stored with every branch.

We detail the bit bound. Every input rational has bit length at most $\ell$,
and $n,b\le O(\ell)$. The quantities $M,L,\varepsilon$ have polynomial
bit length. So do $\log B$, the coordinate subdivision count's logarithm,
the net coordinates, and the sampled penalties. This follows directly
from their formulas: products, reciprocals of positive rationals, and
integer ceilings increase encoding length polynomially. The choice of $B$
uses binary search with rational comparisons and polynomially many bit
operations.

Grid unary and edge costs are rational of polynomial bit length. A finite
entry in \eqref{eq:DP} is the value of an assignment to a subset of the
original factors, each occurring once. There are only $O(n^2)$ such factors.
A common denominator formed from their denominators has polynomial bit
length; summing any subset also has polynomial bit length. Taking minima
selects values and introduces no new denominators. Thus all additions and
comparisons in the grid dynamic program have uniformly polynomial bit
cost, including during rare runs with large enumerations.

For exact branch construction, clear denominators in a principal system
$Q_{AA}u=d$. The resulting integer matrix and right-hand sides have
polynomial bit length. Each determinant is a sum of at most $n!$ products
of $n$ such integers, so its bit length is polynomial in $\ell$. Since
$Q_{AA}$ is nonsingular, rational elimination (or determinant formulas)
gives polynomial-size coefficients in \eqref{eq:optimizer} and
\eqref{eq:branch}. Adding the rational noise penalties gives the same
bit bound for $F_A$. These systems can be solved in polynomial bit time.
No numerical conditioning estimate is being used as a substitute for exact
arithmetic.

The expected total number of oracle calls is at most
$bJ_*(1+en)$ by \eqref{eq:dictionarysize}. The calls have the deterministic
cost \eqref{eq:DPcost} times a polynomial bit factor. Creating partial
assignments, heap operations, deduplication, exact support evaluations,
and branch construction add a polynomial factor per output or call, as
established after Theorem~\ref{thm:dictionary}. All these factors are
absorbed by $P_w(\ell)$. Linearity of expectation sums the work of the
messages even though their interiors overlap and use the same noise.
Finally \eqref{eq:gridB} and \eqref{eq:mainparameters} give the displayed
bound on $B+2$ and hence \eqref{eq:explicitwork}.
\end{proof}

At a rational boundary point, evaluating the retained noisy branches $F_A$ and choosing
the smallest returns both the exact value and a conditional optimizer.
The theorem gives the message on the entire prescribed box, not only at
the net points. The net is used to find the dictionary, not to interpolate
its values. It gives no dictionary on an unbounded boundary domain.
Exceptional noise samples may require exponentially many outputs; both
termination and exactness still hold for them. The expectation concerns
running time, not correctness.

\subsection{An additive certificate for the original objective}
Let $v_0$ denote the optimum without noise. If $(\widehat x,\widehat z)$
is the exact perturbed optimizer, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certificate}
 \mathrm{LB}=v_\xi-\sum_i\max\{\xi_i,0\},\qquad
 \mathrm{UB}=v_\xi-\xi^T\widehat z.
\end{equation}
For every feasible $(x,z)$, its perturbed value is at least $v_\xi$ and
$\xi^Tz\le\sum_i\max\{\xi_i,0\}$. The unperturbed value is therefore at
least $\mathrm{LB}$. Evaluating at $(\widehat x,\widehat z)$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:certgap}
 \mathrm{LB}\le v_0\le\mathrm{UB},\qquad
 \mathrm{UB}-\mathrm{LB}
 =\sum_i\bigl(\max\{\xi_i,0\}-\xi_i\widehat z_i\bigr)
 \le\sum_i|\xi_i|\le n\sigma.
\end{equation}
Choosing $\sigma=\eta/n$ gives an always-valid additive gap at most $\eta$
with polynomial expected work in $1/\eta$ under fixed structural and
numerical bounds. This standard perturbation certificate is not a relative
approximation guarantee. Exact optimization of the original rational
instance does not follow by taking an unspecified infinitesimal noise.

The exact-rational reference \path{checks/spectral_reference.py} uses the valid
message-specific bounds $M_k,L_k$ and mesh in place of their common upper
bounds. Independent finite checks include support reoptimization and tie-only labels.
This is a constructive complexity result: repeated grid solves and the powers
in \eqref{eq:explicitwork} can be large. Neither a practical solver speedup
nor applicability to additional nonlinear constraints follows from the theorem.
