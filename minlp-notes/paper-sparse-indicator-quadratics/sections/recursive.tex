\section{Direct recursions under diagonal dominance}\label{sec:recursive}
The main spectral construction solves each requested message directly.
The alternative algorithms here compose child messages. Their stronger
numerical assumption gives an invariant box, which makes unrestricted
support elimination compatible with recursively retained formulas.
Neither algorithm needs bounded degree or bounded branching. Parametric
message recursion and support pruning on tree decompositions are established
\cite{BhathenaTrees,BhathenaStructured}. The particular expected bounds below
combine the invariant box with penalty-noise count and moment bounds.

\subsection{An invariant box}
Assume, in addition to symmetry,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SDD}
 0<d\le Q_{ii}\le D_0,\qquad
 \sum_{j\ne i}|Q_{ij}|\le\rho Q_{ii},\quad 0\le\rho<1,
 \qquad |c_i|\le C.
\end{equation}
For bit-complexity statements, the supplied bounds $d,D_0,\rho,C$ are
rational and their encodings are included in the input length $\ell$.
Strict diagonal dominance makes $Q$ positive definite. If $C>0$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:SDDbox}
 M=\frac{C}{2d(1-\rho)},\qquad I=[-M,M].
\end{equation}
If $C=0$, every unrestricted support optimizer is zero and selecting the
negative noisy penalties solves the global problem directly; the prescribed
invariant box is the singleton zero.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:invariant}
For every fixed support, every conditional continuous optimizer whose
boundary coordinates lie in $I$ has all its free coordinates in $I$.
A boundary-excluded branch with $k$ boundary coordinates satisfies, on $I^k$,
\[
 |\partial q/\partial t_s|\le2\rho D_0M,
 \qquad \|\nabla q\|\le2\sqrt{k}\rho D_0M.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If there is a free active coordinate, let $X$ be their maximum absolute
value. Its stationarity equation gives
$X\le C/(2d)+\rho\max\{X,M\}$. If $X>M$, it follows that
$(1-\rho)X\le C/(2d)=(1-\rho)M$, a contradiction. Inactive coordinates
are zero; deleting unused edges only decreases the off-diagonal row sums.
Differentiating the exact conditional optimum gives
$\partial q/\partial t_s=2\sum_{i\text{ internal}}Q_{si}x_i(t)$.
The invariant box and \eqref{eq:SDD} give the displayed bounds.
\end{proof}

The assertion holds for every support, even one that never wins. This is
stronger than a bound on the global optimizer alone. Penalties do not enter
the stationarity equations, so the box is uniform over all noise outcomes.

\subsection{Bounded-block scalar recursion}
Suppose every biconnected block of the support graph has at most $b_0$
vertices, counting a bridge as a two-vertex block. Blocks need not be cliques.
Root each component's block-cut tree at a vertex. Assign each edge cross
term to its unique block, and each diagonal, linear, and penalty term to
its vertex. A block message $H_B(t)$ excludes its parent vertex's own costs.
Represent a scalar envelope by distinct full quadratic formulas necessary
on open subintervals of $I$, with one support representative each and an
ordered list of its formula intervals. Deduplicate identical formulas;
isolated touches need no separate formula. Continuity preserves values on
the whole closed interval.

Write $p_u=\lambda_u+\xi_u$. The active vertex message and inactive atom are
\begin{align}
 F_u(t)&=Q_{uu}t^2+c_ut+p_u+\sum_{B\text{ child of }u}H_B(t),
                                                        \label{eq:vertexactive}\\
 A_u&=F_u(0)-p_u.\label{eq:vertexinactive}
\end{align}
For \eqref{eq:vertexactive}, merge child breakpoint lists while maintaining
the running sum of their current quadratic coefficients; update only the
children that change at a breakpoint. This avoids repeatedly summing all
children at a high-degree vertex. Each resulting polynomial is the full
conditional value of a genuine combination of child supports. For
\eqref{eq:vertexinactive}, store one best descendant representative at zero.
Switching the vertex bit to zero removes precisely $p_u$, and descendants
interact with the rest only through this fixed zero coordinate. Active zero
remains allowed, as required by the original model.

At a block $B$ with parent vertex $v$, choose for each
$u\in B\setminus\{v\}$ either a retained active quadratic or the inactive
atom. Add the block's cross terms, set inactive coordinates to zero, and
minimize unrestrictedly over the active block coordinates. The resulting
Schur complement is a full quadratic in $x_v$. Construct and prune the lower
envelope of these candidates using Lemma~\ref{lem:DS}.

\begin{theorem}[Block recursion]\label{thm:block}
Under \eqref{eq:SDD}, the preceding recursion constructs exact scalar
messages and an optimizer for every penalty vector. With independent noise
densities at most $\phi$, its expected arithmetic, comparison, and
quadratic-root operation count is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockcontinuous}
 O_{b_0}\!\left(n^2(2+8n\phi\rho D_0M^2)^{b_0-1}\right).
\end{equation}
For rational data and the endpoint grid with $N\ge4n2^n$, its expected
bit cost is a factor polynomial in the rational input length and $\log N$ times
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockgrid}
 O_{b_0}\!\left(n^2(3+8n\phi\rho D_0M^2)^{b_0-1}\right),
 \qquad \phi=1/(2\sigma).
\end{equation}
A smallest admissible power-of-two grid uses $O(n+\log(n+1))$ random bits
per coordinate. The continuous statement is an arithmetic-model result,
not a bit-complexity claim for arbitrary real samples.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every vertex polynomial and block candidate is the exact unrestricted
conditional value of a genuine complete support. Positive definiteness
makes each eliminated matrix, a Schur complement of a principal matrix,
positive definite. Thus no candidate can fall below the true conditional
value anywhere, even outside the intervals that led to its construction.
Conversely fix a true conditional optimum with parent value in $I$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant} puts its child boundary values in $I$. Replace
child descendants by retained representatives attaining their envelopes
there, using the inactive atom when appropriate. These replacements preserve
value and feasibility. The enumerated tuple then has the true value at those
block coordinates, and its unrestricted minimum is no larger. Candidate
validity gives the reverse inequality. This proves block coverage; the
vertex step follows directly from the sum in \eqref{eq:vertexactive}.
Open-interval coverage extends to breakpoints and endpoints by continuity.
Induction proves all messages. At a root, compare its inactive atom with the
minima of its active quadratics on $I$; the invariant box contains every
support optimizer. Stored choices and linear support solves recover an
optimizer.

Let $K_u$ count retained active formulas at $u$. The candidate count at
block $B$ is at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockcandidates}
 N_B=\prod_{u\in B\setminus\{v\}}(K_u+1).
\end{equation}
After subtracting the common vertex terms, its active support branches have
Lipschitz constant $L=2\rho D_0M$ on length $T=2M$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant}. Apply the scalar bounds to the full deterministic
support family, not to a random pruned family. Under continuous noise,
distinct descendant supports give different noisy polynomials almost surely:
at any fixed parameter, their difference contains $\xi_i$ with coefficient
$1$ or $-1$ for a bit on which the supports differ. Conditioning on the
remaining coordinates
makes equality a null event, and there are finitely many pairs. Thus the
necessary-formula count is bounded by the scalar unique-support interval
count. Theorem~\ref{thm:scalar} gives
$\E(K_u+1)\le2+8n\phi\rho D_0M^2$.
For grid noise, Theorem~\ref{thm:scalargrid} and
$4m2^m/N\le1$ for every subtree size $m\le n$ give
$\E(K_u+1)\le3+8n\phi\rho D_0M^2$.
Sibling vertex subtrees within one block have disjoint noise coordinates,
so their counts in \eqref{eq:blockcandidates} are independent. Fix all
representative-selection rules deterministically. This proves the corresponding
$(b_0-1)$st power bound on $\E N_B$. Ancestor and descendant counts
need not be independent.

There are $O(n)$ blocks. Each candidate requires bounded-dimensional Schur
elimination. Every formula has a support representative, hence each list
has at most $2^n$ entries and all logarithmic sorting factors are $O_{b_0}(n)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:DS} bounds each block envelope by $2N_B-1$ pieces.
All vertex merges process at most $2\sum_B N_B$ such child pieces.
Their sorting, envelope construction, and support bookkeeping therefore
fit the coarse deterministic bound
\[
 O_{b_0}\!\left(n\left(n+\sum_BN_B\right)\right).
\]
Taking expectations proves \eqref{eq:blockcontinuous} and the operation
part of \eqref{eq:blockgrid}; only first moments and sibling independence
were used.

For the bit statement, every stored full polynomial is an original
fixed-support Schur complement with rational coefficients of uniformly
polynomial bit length, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The same is true of
partial vertex sums, since they include original factors only once.
Breakpoints are roots of differences of two such quadratics; they have
algebraic degree at most two and polynomial-size defining coefficients.
Exact sorting, comparison, sign testing, and root operations thus have
polynomial bit cost. Algebraic breakpoints only locate intervals; stored
coefficients remain rational, so recursion creates no tower of algebraic
extensions. Rational support recovery has the same polynomial bit bound.
\end{proof}

The theorem gives explicit block estimates under stronger hypotheses than
the spectral theorem. For $b_0=2$, the graph is a forest; Bhathena et al.\
\cite{BhathenaTrees} already solve the unperturbed problem on arbitrary trees
in quadratic arithmetic time and memory. The present smoothed bound under
diagonal dominance is no improvement in that case. The perturbation certificate
\eqref{eq:certificate}--\eqref{eq:certgap} applies unchanged.

\subsection{Simultaneous arrangements at fixed treewidth}
Bounded block size can be dropped under the same diagonal-dominance
assumption. The price is a width-dependent algebraic exponent and a
higher-moment argument instead of the explicit scalar operation bound.

Root a width-$w$ decomposition after contracting adjacent bags when one
contains the other. There are at most $n$ nonempty bags: every nonroot bag
introduces a vertex absent from its parent, and running intersection assigns
each vertex to a unique highest bag. Every separator has size at most $w$.
Assign each objective term to its highest containing bag, as in
\eqref{eq:message}. The containing bags of an edge form a connected subtree,
so this owner is unique. Each subtree contains all terms involving its
internal vertices and no terms involving only its parent separator.

For each boundary indicator mode, fix inactive coordinates to zero. Its
$k\le w$ active coordinates range over $I^k$; active zero is allowed.
Retain only distinct full support quadratics that uniquely minimize on some
relative open subset of this box, after merging identical polynomials.
Store one support representative per formula. For $k=0$, retain one minimum
constant. This pruning preserves the envelope on the closed box: outside
pairwise equality sets the minimum formula is unique, and such points are
dense in the relative interior. A subsequence of their winners is constant
when approaching any box point, so continuity gives coverage there.

\begin{theorem}[Direct fixed-width recursion]\label{thm:recursive}
Under \eqref{eq:SDD}, there is an exact recursive algorithm with deterministic
bit work
\begin{equation}\label{eq:CADwork}
 P_w(\ell)(1+n+\mathcal K)^{a(w)},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal K$ is the sum of its final dictionary sizes over all bags
and boundary modes, $P_w$ is a polynomial, and $a(w)$ is a fixed computable
constant. Choose an integer $p\ge\max\{1,a(w)\}$ before sampling, and
let $N$ be the smallest power of two with $N\ge2n(n+1)^p$.
Under the grid \eqref{eq:noisegrid}, expected bit work is polynomial in
input length and the numerical parameters $d^{-1},D_0,C,(1-\rho)^{-1}$,
and $\sigma^{-1}$ at fixed $w$. The algorithm constructs every mode's
formula envelope on its closed box and a global optimizer, correctly for
every draw, with $O_w(\log(n+1))$ random bits per coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The case $w=0$ is separable, so assume $w\ge1$ and $C>0$.
Suppose child dictionaries have been constructed, and fix a bag indicator
mode. The active bag coordinates have dimension at most $w+1$. Lift each
child's appropriate dictionary to these coordinates, and form all pairwise
quadratic differences \emph{within each child}, together with box boundaries.
There are $O_w(1+\sum_j K_j^2)$ polynomials. Remove identically zero
differences and use a fixed rule for identical restricted polynomials.
A sign-invariant CAD in this fixed dimension has polynomial size and bit
cost \cite[Introduction]{ArnonCAD}. On each full-dimensional cell in the
relative open box, its sample point selects one minimum formula per child.
The selected tuple is minimizing throughout the cell. Thus a simultaneous
arrangement discovers the required tuples without a Cartesian product over
arbitrarily many children. In dimension zero, select a tuple at the one point.

For each discovered tuple, add its full child polynomials and the owned bag
terms. Set inactive variables to zero and unrestrictedly minimize over the
active bag coordinates forgotten from the parent separator. Do not impose
the cell as an optimization constraint. The resulting full quadratic is
the exact conditional value of a genuine internal support: child interiors
are disjoint with no cross edges, and elimination is a positive-definite
Schur complement. In particular this candidate is everywhere at least the
true message value, even if its optimizer leaves its discovery cell.

For the reverse inequality, fix a parent boundary point and an optimal
conditional support. Its bag coordinates $y$ lie in the invariant box.
Every child envelope is covered there, including at switching surfaces.
Approach $y$ by full-dimensional arrangement cells in the bag's relative
box. The parent coordinates may vary in this limit as well. There are
finitely many tuples, so some subsequence uses one fixed tuple. Continuity
makes its child polynomials attain all child envelope values at $y$.
At $y$ their sum with the owned terms equals the true conditional optimum.
Unrestricted elimination of forgotten coordinates yields a candidate no
larger than that optimum at the fixed parent boundary point. Candidate
validity proves equality. Dimension zero requires only direct evaluation.
This argument covers boundary points, lower-dimensional ties, and optima
that themselves win on no open cell.

Repeat over the $2^{w+1}$ bag modes and group candidates by parent boundary
mode. A second CAD on each candidate family retains just its uniquely
winning distinct polynomials on relative full-dimensional cells. The density
and continuity argument preceding the theorem proves that pruning preserves
all values on the closed box. At the root, one minimum constant gives the
optimum; its support linear system recovers a rational optimizer.

Every local CAD, candidate list, and pruning operation has work polynomial
in $1+\sum_jK_j$ for fixed $w$. Summing over at most $n$ bags and at most
$2^w$ outgoing modes proves \eqref{eq:CADwork} for a fixed computable
exponent. Uniform coefficient bit lengths follow from genuine-support Schur
complements of the original input. Algebraic CAD sample coordinates select
labels only: they are never substituted into the stored coefficients.
Thus the recursion retains rational coefficients and the uniform bit bound.
This also explains why no recursively growing algebraic extension is hidden
in \eqref{eq:CADwork}.

For the expectation, each final dictionary is bounded by the all-active-label
count of its full support family. Lemma~\ref{lem:invariant} supplies a
common Lipschitz bound $L_w=2w\rho D_0M$, an upper bound on
$2\sqrt{k}\rho D_0M$ for $k\le w$. Theorem~\ref{thm:parammoments}
gives each dictionary's $p$th moment at most $eJ^p$, where
\[
 J=[1+8wML_wn\phi(n+1)^p]^w,\qquad \phi=1/(2\sigma).
\]
There are at most $2^wn$ dictionaries. Applying convexity to these counts
and $1+n$ deterministic unit terms gives
\[
 \E(1+n+\mathcal K)^p\le e[(1+n+2^wn)J]^p.
\]
Since $p\ge a(w)$, this bounds the expectation of \eqref{eq:CADwork}.
No independence among message dictionaries is used. The bit count follows
from the chosen power-of-two grid.
\end{proof}

This algorithm returns formula envelopes and one representative support at
each value, rather than every tied label as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:subdivision}. Its polynomial degree depends on $w$;
no fixed-parameter bound with width-independent degree, or practical
performance advantage, is claimed. The standard CAD machinery and exact
support elimination are classical. The stated expectation follows from
their combination with the interval-mass moment bound, not from the planar
first moment alone.
