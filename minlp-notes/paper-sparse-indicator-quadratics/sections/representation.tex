\section{Two representation limits on stars}\label{sec:representation}
Exact tree optimization is polynomial \cite{BhathenaTrees}, but a
particular universal representation may still be large. We give two such
limits. Neither is a lower bound for arbitrary algorithms or for the
original quadratic-indicator epigraph hull.

\subsection{A face of the inverse-principal polytope}
For positive-definite $Q$, let
\[
 P_Q=\operatorname{conv}\{(\mathbf1_S,W^S):S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,n\}\},
 \qquad
 W^S_{SS}=Q_{SS}^{-1},\quad W^S_{ij}=0\text{ otherwise}.
\]
For $S=\varnothing$, set $W^S=0$.
This established polytope occurs in the convexification of Wei et al.
\cite[Section~3.1 and Theorem~1]{WeiHull}. Liu et al.
\cite[Section~4.3, Proposition~6]{LiuStieltjes} already prove computational
hardness for a related inverse-graph closure using a diagonal-minus-rank-one
matrix. The following result gives an unconditional representation bound
for sparse, uniformly conditioned matrices.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:star-face}
For $m\ge1$, let $b=1/(2m)$ and
\[
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\mathbf1^T\\b\mathbf1&I_{2m}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then $Q$ has a star support graph, rational entries of $O(\log(m+1))$ bits,
and condition number below six. The polytope $P_Q$ has an exposed face
affinely isomorphic to
$\operatorname{COR}(m)=\operatorname{conv}\{aa^T:a\in\{0,1\}^m\}$.
The same conclusion holds for the Stieltjes matrix obtained by changing
the sign of the root coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The eigenvalues are one and $1\pm1/\sqrt{2m}$, so the condition number
is at most $(1+1/\sqrt2)/(1-1/\sqrt2)<6$.
At an atom with root selected, write $z\in\{0,1\}^{2m}$ for the leaf
indicators, $k=\mathbf1^Tz$, and $t=(1-b^2k)^{-1}$. Block inversion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star-inverse}
 W_{00}=t,\qquad W_{0L}=-btz^T,\qquad
 W_{LL}=\operatorname{Diag}(z)+b^2tzz^T.
\end{equation}
At a root-inactive atom the leaf block is simply $\operatorname{Diag}(z)$.
Consider the affine functional
\[
 g(z_0,z,W)=(m+1)(1-z_0)+m-\mathbf1^Tz
                         +b^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^mW_{i,m+i}.
\]
If the root is inactive, $g=2m+1-k\ge1$.
Otherwise let $p$ be the number of fully selected pairs $(i,m+i)$.
Since $k\le m+p$, we obtain $g=m-k+tp\ge(t-1)p\ge0$.
It vanishes exactly when $p=0$ and $k=m$: the root is selected and
exactly one leaf in each pair is selected. Every other atom has
$g\ge b^2$. Indeed, when $p=0$ its positive value is integral, and when
$p>0$, $(t-1)p=b^2kp/(1-b^2k)\ge b^2$.
Thus $g=0$ exposes precisely the convex hull of atoms with leaf vector
$z=(a,\mathbf1-a)$, $a\in\{0,1\}^m$. This atom gap is not an
approximate extension-complexity bound.

On this face $t=\tau:=(1-mb^2)^{-1}$ is constant.
The affine map $X_{ii}=z_i$ and $X_{ij}=W_{ij}/(b^2\tau)$ for
$i\ne j$ maps onto $\operatorname{COR}(m)$. To prove injectivity as well,
put $a=\operatorname{diag}(X)$, $z=(a,\mathbf1-a)$, and
\[
 R(X)=\begin{pmatrix}
 X&a\mathbf1^T-X\\
 \mathbf1a^T-X&\mathbf1\mathbf1^T-a\mathbf1^T-\mathbf1a^T+X
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Recover $z_0=1$, $W_{00}=\tau$, $W_{0L}=-b\tau z^T$, and
$W_{LL}=\operatorname{Diag}(z)+b^2\tau R(X)$.
At binary atoms these are exactly \eqref{eq:star-inverse}; affinity
extends both inverse identities to their convex hulls.
Finally, if $D=\operatorname{Diag}(-1,1,\ldots,1)$, every padded
principal inverse for $DQD$ is $DW^SD$. The invertible linear map
$(z,W)\mapsto(z,DWD)$ identifies the two polytopes.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:conic}
For the matrices in Theorem~\ref{thm:star-face}, an exact lift of $P_Q$
requires exponentially many scalar nonnegative factors in an LP lift,
exponential total cone dimension in a second-order-cone lift, and
exponentially many positive-semidefinite blocks of any fixed order.
An unrestricted semidefinite lift requires total block order
$2^{\Omega(m^{2/13})}$. Scalar inequalities count as order-one factors.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Intersect the affine slice of any lift with the equation $g=0$ and compose
its projection with the face isomorphism. This gives a lift of
$\operatorname{COR}(m)$ over the same cone; no duality or strict-feasibility
argument is needed. Apply the fixed-order block bounds of
Fawzi and Parrilo \cite[Theorem~1]{FawziParrilo}. In particular, an
order-two lift needs at least $7^{-1/2}(9/7)^{m/2}$ blocks.
A Lorentz cone of dimension $k\ge3$ has a lift with $k-2$
three-dimensional Lorentz cones, each isomorphic to a positive-semidefinite
cone of order two. Dimensions one and two need at most their dimension
in scalar cones. Hence total second-order-cone dimension $D$ yields
at most $D$ order-two blocks, giving the stated exponential bound.
LP factors embed in order-two blocks as well.
For arbitrary semidefinite blocks, combine them into one block with order
equal to their total order and apply Lee, Raghavendra, and Steurer
\cite[Theorems~1.1 and 5.4]{LeeSDP}.
\end{proof}

The exponent $2/13$ is a valid established bound, not a claim of the
strongest currently known exponent. The SOCP conclusion concerns total
dimension, not the number of factors when their dimensions may grow.
Also, projecting out inverse entries can remove this obstruction. On the
exposed face, all indicators, inverse diagonals, and inverse entries on
the star edges depend only affinely on $a$; their projection is a cube.
The correlation information lies in leaf--leaf inverse entries. Thus this
argument gives no lower bound for epigraph formulations that eliminate $W$.

\subsection{Exact projected-row decision diagrams}
Choi et al.\ \cite[Definition~7 and Lemma~2]{ChoiDiagrams} store the
future rows of a factor $F$, where $Q=FF^T$, after orthogonal projection
away from selected past rows. Section~EC.2.2 of that work also permits
normalizing each stored row. Their Section~7.2 already observes exponential
size on stars. The following bound applies to every variable order and
every admissible choice of nonzero weights, including uniform weights
with bounded conditioning, and survives row normalization. It concerns
this specific state definition.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:dd-star}
Let $n\ge1$, $Q_{00}=Q_{ii}=1$, $Q_{0i}=Q_{i0}=b_i\ne0$ for $1\le i\le n$,
and all other off-diagonal entries be zero, where $\sum_i b_i^2<1$.
For unconstrained binary supports, every variable order in the exact
projected-row diagram has a layer with at least $2^{n-1}$ distinct states.
The conclusion persists under arbitrary nonzero rescaling of individual
stored rows. In particular, it holds for $b_i=1/(2n)$, for which
$\|Q-I\|_2=1/(2\sqrt n)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The factor rows $f_0,f_1,\ldots,f_n$ are linearly independent because
$Q$ is positive definite. Leaf rows are orthonormal and
$\langle f_0,f_i\rangle=b_i$.
Consider the layer immediately before the last leaf $j$ is processed.
All other $n-1$ leaves are in the past. Vary their selected subset $S$.
If the root is still future, its residual row is
\[
 r_0(S)=f_0-\sum_{i\in S}b_i f_i.
\]
Distinct subsets give nonproportional vectors: their coefficient on $f_0$
is always one, so proportionality would imply equality and then equality
of every membership bit.

If the root was processed, restrict its indicator to one. First project
away from the selected leaves and then away from $r_0(S)$.
Writing $d_S=\|r_0(S)\|^2=1-\sum_{i\in S}b_i^2>0$, the last leaf row is
\[
 r_j(S)=f_j-\frac{b_j}{d_S}
                      \left(f_0-\sum_{i\in S}b_i f_i\right).
\]
Its coefficient on $f_j$ is always one. If two such rows were
proportional they would be equal. Equality of the $f_0$ coefficients
gives equal $d_S$, and equality of every other coefficient then gives
the same subset, because all $b_i$ are nonzero.
Thus in either case the $2^{n-1}$ subsets yield distinct states even after
row rescaling. Normalizing intermediate rows does not change their spans,
so it produces scalar multiples of these same residuals. All prefixes are
feasible because there are no combinatorial support constraints.
The final norm formula follows from the two nonzero eigenvalues
$\pm\sqrt{\sum_i b_i^2}$ of $Q-I$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Unnormalized Gram states and a root-last order]
The dyadic choice $b_i=2^{-i}$ also distinguishes states even if
only their \emph{unnormalized residual Gram matrices} are stored. Indeed
$d_S=1-\sum_{i\in S}4^{-i}$ is different for every subset $S$, by uniqueness
of base-four expansions with digits zero and one. At the layer just before
the last leaf $j$, if the root is future, its residual Gram diagonal is
$d_S$. If the root has been selected, the displayed residual has squared
norm $1-b_j^2/d_S$, again different for every $S$. Thus every order has
$2^{n-1}$ distinct unnormalized Gram states for this choice. Its condition
number is less than $(1+1/\sqrt3)/(1-1/\sqrt3)$ because
$\sum_i4^{-i}<1/3$. This Gram conclusion is not asserted after row
normalization. Separately, for any nonzero weights in Theorem~\ref{thm:dd-star},
if the root is last, varying all $n$ leaves in the first residual formula
gives $2^n$ nonproportional row states immediately before the root.
\end{remark}

The ambient directions in these residual rows matter: equal Gram matrices
alone do not identify the normalized row states. The theorem does not
exclude other exact state definitions, objective-dependent pruning, or
approximate merging. It is consistent with the fixed-leaf-count tree
bounds of \cite[Section~7.2]{ChoiDiagrams} and with exact polynomial tree
optimization \cite{BhathenaTrees}.
