\section{Large exact messages from fixed local data}\label{sec:message-limits}
Hard optimization and large conditional messages are different obstructions.
The example below has an easy unconditioned optimum, but every one of its
support quadratics is needed in an exact scalar message.
This section is deterministic: $\xi=0$. Its worst-case dictionary lower
bound does not contradict the expected bound under independent penalty
perturbations in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
Fix rational $0<\theta\le1/10$ and define, for $t\in[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-message}
 V_n(t)=\min\left\{
 \sum_{i=1}^n(x_i^2-2x_i+z_i)
 +\sum_{i=1}^n(s_i-\theta s_{i-1}-\theta x_i)^2:
 \begin{array}{l}
 x_i(1-z_i)=0,\quad z_i\in\{0,1\},\\ s_0=0,\quad s_n=t
 \end{array}\right\}.
\end{equation}
The same stage objective applies at every step. A terminal equality defines
a conditional value function, rather than an extra constraint on the
unconditioned optimization model.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:residual-message}
Let $W_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\theta^{2j}$. The exact support values in
\eqref{eq:constant-message} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-branches}
 q_z(t)=\frac{(t-c_z)^2}{D_z},\qquad
 c_z=\sum_{i=1}^n\theta^{n-i+1}z_i,\qquad
 D_z=W_n+\sum_{i=1}^n\theta^{2(n-i+1)}z_i.
\end{equation}
Thus $V_n=\min_z q_z$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $z$, set $e_i=x_i-z_i$ and
$r_i=s_i-\theta s_{i-1}-\theta x_i$; inactive controls require $e_i=0$.
The residual objective is $\sum_i(e_i^2+r_i^2)$ and its only remaining
constraint is
\[
 t-c_z=\sum_i\theta^{n-i+1}e_i+\sum_i\theta^{n-i}r_i.
\]
Conversely, any such residuals reconstruct feasible controls and states.
Cauchy--Schwarz is attained at
$e_i=(t-c_z)\theta^{n-i+1}z_i/D_z$ and
$r_i=(t-c_z)\theta^{n-i}/D_z$, proving the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:fixed-data}
Every finite exact representation of $V_n$ on $[0,1]$ as a minimum of
polynomials, or as a piecewise polynomial function, uses at least $2^n$
distinct polynomial formulas. This holds for conditional messages of
pure indicator problems with unit penalties on every variable, a fixed
finite alphabet of rational local coefficients, and the graph and spectral
bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:hardness} (width at most two for all $n$,
and exactly two when $n\ge2$).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $C_\theta=(1+\theta^2)/(1-\theta^2)<2$. Every $D_z$ lies in
$[1,C_\theta]$, and all centers lie in $[0,\theta/(1-\theta)]$.
Their minimum separation is $\theta^n$. Indeed, if two strings first
differ at power $k<n$, their center difference has magnitude at least
$\theta^k(1-2\theta)/(1-\theta)\ge\theta^n$;
strings differing only at power $n$ attain $\theta^n$.
On $|t-c_z|\le\theta^n/3$, intersected with $[0,1]$, we have
\[
 q_z(t)\le\theta^{2n}/9,
 \qquad q_{z'}(t)\ge4\theta^{2n}/(9C_\theta)>q_z(t)
 \quad(z'\ne z).
\]
Each $q_z$ therefore agrees with $V_n$ on an ordinary open interval.
If finitely many representing polynomials cover that interval, one agrees
identically with $q_z$: finitely many nonzero polynomial differences have
only finitely many roots. This argument applies both to a minimum and to
a piecewise representation, even when the permitted polynomial degrees
exceed two. Distinct centers give distinct polynomials.

Before conditioning $s_n$, the quadratic matrix in
\eqref{eq:constant-message} has the edges \eqref{eq:hard-edges}, control
and nonterminal-state diagonals $1+\theta^2$, and final diagonal one.
The same row bounds prove \eqref{eq:hard-spectrum}. To add unit indicators
to all states, let $y_0=4$, attach $\zeta_i$ to $y_i$, and replace the
dynamic terms by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:message-lift}
 \sum_i[y_i-\theta y_{i-1}-\theta x_i-4(1-\theta)]^2
       +\sum_i\zeta_i,
 \qquad y_n=4+t.
\end{equation}
The separator is $S=\{y_n\}$; the lower bound already holds on
$y_n\in[4,5]\subseteq[-R,R]$ for $R\ge5$, corresponding to $t\in[0,1]$.
For a given $z$, its reference states satisfy
$s_0^z=0$, $s_i^z=\theta s_{i-1}^z+\theta z_i\ge0$.
With $e=x-z$ and $d=y-4\mathbf1-s^z$, the squares form the same
quadratic matrix just bounded. If $k$ state indicators are off, the
objective is at least $n-k+16(1-3\theta)k\ge n+10.2k$.
An all-on state choice with $x=z=0$, $y_i=4$ for $i<n$, and
$y_n=4+t$ costs $n+t^2\le n+1$. Hence every minimizing state indicator
is on. Before removing boundary-only terms, the conditional value is
$n+V_n(t)$; excluding the terminal indicator penalty gives
$n-1+V_n(t)$. Our message convention also subtracts the remaining
boundary-only polynomial. This common subtraction does not change the
number of indispensable polynomial formulas. Expanded linear coefficients
have magnitude at most eight; the quadratic and linear coefficients and
unit penalties belong to a finite alphabet depending only on $\theta$.
The removable additive constant is at most $16n$.
\end{proof}

This is a lower bound on explicit formulas, not on every representation or
optimization algorithm. Without conditioning the terminal state, every
control support has a zero-residual trajectory $x=z$, so the original
objective has value zero and the lifted objective has value $n$.

\subsection{Matching bounds for pruning original quadratics}
Let $N_n(\varepsilon)$ be the smallest number of the original quadratics
$q_z$ whose minimum $R$ satisfies $0\le R-V_n\le\varepsilon$ on $[0,1]$.
Set $N_\theta(\varepsilon)=\sup_{n\ge1}N_n(\varepsilon)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:pruning}
As $\varepsilon\downarrow0$,
\[
 N_\theta(\varepsilon)=\Theta_\theta(\varepsilon^{-\alpha_\theta}),
 \qquad \alpha_\theta=\frac{\log2}{2\log(1/\theta)}.
\]
The lower bound concerns pruning original support quadratics only.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Retain the last $m$ bits of a support, where $0\le m\le n$, and let
\[
 \delta=\sum_{j=m+1}^n\theta^j\le\frac{\theta^{m+1}}{1-\theta},
 \qquad
 \beta=\sum_{j=m+1}^n\theta^{2j}\le\frac{\theta^{2m+2}}{1-\theta^2}.
\]
For each suffix keep both the all-zero and all-one omitted prefixes.
These give at most $2^{m+1}$ original quadratics, retaining the original
$W_n$ in their denominators. For an optimal support, the two retained
centers $c_0,c_1$ bracket its center and have distance $\delta$.
The endpoint closest to $t$ has squared distance at most
$(t-c_z)^2+\delta^2/4$: outside $[c_0,c_1]$ it is no farther than $c_z$,
and inside the interval it is within $\delta/2$.
Its denominator differs from $D_z$ by at most $\beta$; all denominators
are at least one and $(t-c_z)^2\le1$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pruning-upper}
 0\le R-V_n\le\delta^2/4+\beta
 \le A_\theta\theta^{2m+2},\qquad
 A_\theta=\frac1{4(1-\theta)^2}+\frac1{1-\theta^2}.
\end{equation}
Keeping just the zero prefix also gives the simpler bound
$R-V_n\le2\delta+\beta$ with $2^m$ terms, by bounding the numerator
change by $2\delta$.
Taking $m=\lceil\log(A_\theta\theta^2/\varepsilon)/
(2\log(1/\theta))\rceil$, or retaining every support if $n\le m$,
proves the upper bound $4(A_\theta\theta^2/\varepsilon)^{\alpha_\theta}$
for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.

For the lower bound take $n\ge m\ge1$. The $2^m$ centers with zero
omitted prefix are separated by at least $\theta^m$, and $V_n$ vanishes
at each. A retained quadratic below $\varepsilon$ at one such center
must have its own center within $\sqrt{C_\theta\varepsilon}$.
If $\varepsilon<\theta^{2m}/(4C_\theta)$, it can serve at most one
of the $2^m$ centers. Choose
$m=\lfloor\log(1/(8C_\theta\varepsilon))/(2\log(1/\theta))\rfloor$.
Then $N_\theta(\varepsilon)\ge
\tfrac12(8C_\theta\varepsilon)^{-\alpha_\theta}$.
\end{proof}

A related example separates exact complexity from approximation even more
strongly. In \eqref{eq:constant-message} replace the control coefficient
$\theta$ in residual $i$ by
$b_i=2^{i-1}\theta^i/(2^n-1)$.
The same residual proof gives centers $c_z=h\sum_i2^{i-1}z_i$,
where $h=\theta^n/(2^n-1)$, and denominators
$D_z=W_n+\sum_i\theta^{2(n-i)}b_i^2z_i\ge1$.
Every quadratic is again indispensable, but the piecewise function equal
to zero on $[0,\theta^n]$ and to $q_{\mathbf1}$ on $[\theta^n,1]$
underestimates the message by at most $h^2/4$.
On the first interval use a nearest center; on the second the all-one
support has both the nearest center and the largest denominator.
This two-piece function is continuously differentiable at its breakpoint.
It is a piecewise approximation, not the pointwise minimum of its two
formulas over the entire interval.
The single quadratic $q_{\mathbf1}$ also overestimates the message by at
most $\theta^{2n}$: its value is at most that bound on $[0,\theta^n]$,
and it is exact thereafter.

Exponential growth of piecewise quadratic messages is known for broader
nonconvex models \cite[Section~5]{KuricTree}. Power-law approximation
rates for quadratic bases with a common Hessian are established
\cite[Section~III, Theorem~3.3]{GaubertPruning}; pruning under uniform
error also has a covering interpretation \cite[Section~VI]{GaubertPruning}.
Our contribution is the fixed-alphabet,
well-conditioned indicator realization and its matching pruning exponent,
including its support-dependent denominators. We claim neither a new
general phenomenon of message growth nor a lower bound for arbitrary
approximating functions.

Multi-period indicator models also admit polynomial algorithms when their
projected quadratic matrices are factorizable \cite{LeeMultiperiod}.
Eliminating the free internal states of \eqref{eq:constant-message}
gives the control matrix
\[
 \widetilde Q=I+\frac{uu^T}{W_n},\qquad u_i=\theta^{n-i+1},
 \qquad \widetilde Q^{-1}=I-\frac{uu^T}{W_n+u^Tu}.
\]
Every off-diagonal inverse entry is nonzero. For $n\ge3$, this natural
scalar projection is therefore not factorizable, since a nonsingular
factorizable matrix has a tridiagonal inverse
\cite[Proposition~1]{LeeMultiperiod}. This comparison does not exclude
every possible block reformulation.
