# Literature boundary for graph-constrained integral rounding

Date: 2026-09-28. This is a bounded literature investigation, not a novelty certification. The candidate theorem below was proposed by the applications researcher and is not proved in this note.

## Candidate distinction worth pursuing

Let a binary mode sequence occupy one mode on each interval of length `h`, and require successive modes to follow a directed graph `G` with self-loops. Compare its primitive with that of an arbitrary measurable simplex-valued target on a horizon of length `T`. The candidate question is the worst-case optimal maximum primitive discrepancy, uniformly over all targets.

The proposed classification is: complete directed graphs have discrepancy `O(h)`; strongly connected incomplete graphs have discrepancy `Theta(sqrt(T h))`, with graph-dependent constants; failure of strong connectivity can prevent convergence for some targets. This classification concerns a control-approximation metric. A state or objective approximation consequence still needs a separate continuity theorem and its assumptions.

The proposed lower-bound mechanism uses a constant target equally supported on two modes whose transition is forbidden in at least one direction. Tracking both occupancies closely requires repeated alternation. Traversing the missing orientation requires a relay outside the target support. Each relay consumes at least one grid interval, and relay occupancy has no negative target contribution with which to cancel. This can balance an inverse-switching tracking error against accumulated relay occupancy. It is important that the target class includes the boundary of the simplex.

The most useful potential contribution is a sharp, topology-dependent feasibility threshold for existing CIA formulations. The graph model itself and its incorporation into rounding solvers are established prior work. A theorem with matching upper/lower rates would explain when mesh refinement needs `O(epsilon^-2)` intervals instead of the unrestricted `O(epsilon^-1)` scale. Such a statement would concern worst-case reconstruction accuracy, not a general complexity lower bound for MINLP or a demonstrated speedup.

## Interior targets and the rotor-walk comparison

The applications researcher proposed a useful refinement: on any fixed strongly connected graph with self-loops, arbitrary time-varying targets satisfying `alpha_i(t) >= eta > 0` should admit primitive discrepancy `O_G(h/eta)`, independent of the horizon. This would distinguish a uniform interior-target theorem from the boundary-target obstruction and from fixed stationary-frequency results.

Here is an independently checked construction outline, offered for inclusion in the main theorem proof rather than as a fully reviewed theorem. Fix a closed directed visiting walk, and write `l_i` for the number of occurrences of mode `i` in its word. Use blocks of `q` grid cells, where `eta q >= max_i l_i + 2`. At each complete block endpoint, round the vector of cumulative relaxed occupancies in cell units to an integer vector with the correct total and coordinate errors less than one. This is possible by flooring each coordinate and adding one in as many coordinates as the integer total requires. Consecutive rounded endpoint vectors differ by nonnegative counts summing to `q`; each count is at least `eta q - 2`, hence at least `l_i`. Insert self-loops at occurrences of each mode in the fixed walk to realize those counts exactly. The repeated blocks join legally because the visiting walk is closed. Endpoint discrepancy is less than `h`; discrepancy within a block is bounded by `h + qh`. A final incomplete block can be filled by retaining the current mode, incurring at most another block-length bound. A prescribed initial mode requires choosing a closed visiting walk based at that mode.

The apportionment rule need not be monotone as time varies: the strictly positive block increment supplies the required monotonicity between the selected endpoints. Omitting that observation would leave a gap in the construction.

This argument appears elementary once the right distinction is made. Its relation to Holroyd–Propp is precise: their fixed Markov chain has a fixed stationary target, whereas this construction admits arbitrary measurable target variation and gives an explicit deterioration near the boundary. Neither observation by itself establishes novelty. A potentially stronger result would match the combined upper estimate `O_G(min(sqrt(T h), h/eta))` with a lower bound using a target concentrated on a forbidden pair and a small positive relay mass. That lower bound remains a candidate here.

The current significance assessment is **a potentially useful sharp classification, not yet a substantial general MINLP advance**. Transition-graph models, CIA, and legal-walk algorithms are old. The basic block construction and relay-count argument may support a concise theoretical contribution if their exact combination is absent from prior work. Importance would increase if the result yields tight, target-dependent reconstruction certificates for realistic mode graphs, materially better relaxations, or a solver mechanism that detects when unrestricted convexification is misleading. Those consequences have not been established or tested in this note.

## Closest verified primary sources

1. **Bürger, Zeile, Hahn, Altmann-Dieses, Sager, Diehl (2020), _pycombina: An Open-Source Tool for Solving Combinatorial Approximation Problems Arising in Mixed-Integer Optimal Control_.** [Open author PDF](https://cdn.syscop.de/publications/Buerger2020a.pdf), DOI `10.1016/j.ifacol.2020.12.1799`. Section 3.3, equation (7), models allowed direct successors `P_i` exactly through binary constraints on consecutive grid intervals. Section 3.2 supplies SUR, general MILP, and tailored branch-and-bound solution options; SUR is recommended when additional combinatorial constraints are absent. This is direct prior for graph-restricted CIA. The inspected seven-page paper does not establish the proposed topology-dependent minimax rate.

2. **Bestehorn, Hansknecht, Kirches, Manns, _Mixed-integer optimal control problems with switching costs: a shortest path approach_.** [Open manuscript](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/7630.pdf), [journal page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-020-01581-3). The paper optimizes switching costs while constraining integrated rounding discrepancy. It reduces the resulting problem to shortest paths on a DAG and gives complexity bounds in grid size, mode count, and discrepancy parameter. Remark 15 permits additional combinatorial constraints through graph modifications; the preceding paragraph explicitly notes that failure to find a path certifies infeasibility for the chosen constraints and discrepancy parameter. These algorithmic results must not be interpreted as proving that a mesh-independent discrepancy parameter remains feasible for every transition graph. No such universal feasibility claim was found in the inspected sections.

3. **Holroyd and Propp (2010), _Rotor Walks and Markov Chains_.** [Open manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.4507). Theorem 4 gives a time-independent bound on occupation-count discrepancy from the stationary distribution of a fixed irreducible finite Markov chain. This is relevant prior under different terminology: a deterministic legal graph walk can approximate positive stationary frequencies with bounded count error. Its constant depends on the transition mechanism and expected hitting times. It does not directly yield a bound uniform over all probability vectors, especially targets having zero mass on relay vertices. A regularization of a boundary target by a small positive relay mass can worsen those constants. Any claimed interior-versus-boundary extension should compare this theorem carefully.

4. **Zeile, Robuschi, Sager (2020/2021), _Mixed-integer optimal control under minimum dwell time constraints_.** [Open manuscript](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/7366.pdf), [journal page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-020-01533-x). The paper proves constructive integrated-control error bounds proportional to dwell duration plus mesh size using dwell-time next-forced rounding, and studies sharpness. These bounds are independent of horizon length at the level of control primitives. They concern dwell constraints without arbitrary forbidden successor arcs. Thus minimum dwell alone cannot explain the proposed square-root obstruction. The local repository contains its full text at `literature/papers/zeile2020-mixed-integer-optimal-control-under/fulltext.md`.

5. **Kirches, Lenders, Manns (2020), _Approximation Properties and Tight Bounds for Constrained Mixed-Integer Optimal Control_.** [Primary journal page](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/18M1182917), local full text `literature/papers/kirches2020-approximation-properties-and-tight-bounds/original.pdf`. Sections 2–5 and Theorem 8.3 were examined. Definition 5.4 restricts the rounded choice on interval `j` to indices having positive relaxed integral on that interval; its admissibility set is determined by the target rather than by the previously selected mode. Definition 5.2 uses continuity to obtain approximately feasible grids for mixed state-control constraints. Theorem 8.3 bounds the resulting support-restricted SUR discrepancy linearly in mesh size, with mode-dependent constant. This is not a legal-walk construction through relay modes of zero target mass. The introduction mentions switching constraints broadly, so a categorical claim that the paper has no relevance to switching constraints would be inaccurate; the narrow distinction is its actual rounding rule and approximate constraint-feasibility setting.

## Why the initial contraction direction was deprioritized

Existing dwell rounding already gives a small primitive error. Turning such an error into all-time state tracking under contraction resembles established averaging and robustness arguments. Two additional primary sources found by an independent subagent support that concern:

- **Bousquet and Slotine (2013), _Large Gain Stability of Extremum Seeking and Higher-Order Averaging Theory_.** [Open manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.4474), Section II and Lemma 1. Near-identity averaging converts smooth periodic forcing into a bounded residual, and contraction controls the resulting tracking error. It does not directly cover arbitrary measurable relaxed controls with dwell restrictions.
- **Taringoo, Nešić, Tan, Dower, _Averaging for nonlinear systems evolving on Riemannian manifolds_.** [Open manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.2357), Theorem 5. Under smooth periodic forcing, completeness, and a locally exponentially stable averaged equilibrium, the trajectory approximation is `O(epsilon)` uniformly on the infinite interval for initial conditions in a suitable neighborhood. This is not the same control class, but it establishes the principal stability-to-uniform-approximation mechanism.

The independent subagent read these averaging sources; this note records that report without independently reproducing their proofs. The local notes for Chen–Lazar's dwell-constrained MPC paper and Makarow–Kirches's fast-switching MPC paper were also consulted. They give further prior for rounding with dwell restrictions and practical closed-loop stability, respectively.
