# Prior-art audit for positive-kernel gluing

Research note, 2026-09-28. This is a source audit, not a proof or a novelty certificate.

This initial audit compared the construction with the published theorem of Korda, Magron, and Ríos-Zertuche (2024 online; 2025 volume). A subsequent [primary-source audit](sparse-putinar-prior.md) found that Magron's July 2025 and February 2026 author slides already publicly assert the inverse-square sparse preordering rate for two overlapping bags. The rate itself is therefore not claimed as new. The slides and published theorem differ, and that discrepancy remains unresolved. Sparse Jackson kernels, sparse polynomial convergence rates, and ordinary junction-tree measure gluing are established tools. The explicit transformation of finite sparse pseudomoments into consistent positive densities, quantitative sharpness, and ordinary-module extension require separate contribution assessments.

## Closest quantitative sparse result

[Korda, Magron, and Ríos-Zertuche, *Convergence rates for sums-of-squares hierarchies with correlative sparsity*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-024-02071-6), Theorems 6 and 8; Sections 2–4. The [open paper](https://d-nb.info/1330825241/34) was read at those locations.

Theorem 6 assumes a polynomial decomposition over bags satisfying running intersection and positivity on the full box. It gives a certificate in the sum of bag preorderings generated by (1-x_i^2), with componentwise degree bounds. Writing (J=max_j|J_j|), its sufficient degree is (O(\varepsilon^{-(J+3)/2})), hence error (O(r^{-2/(J+3)})) for fixed data. Constants also depend on the number of bags, ambient dimension, objective degrees, norms, and Lipschitz constants; only the exponent is governed by clique size.

Their proof first approximates separator minimum-value functions by polynomials, obtains positive bag polynomials, and then applies Jackson-kernel inversion. Section 2 already establishes tensor Jackson positivity, normalization, eigenvalues, and bag preordering membership. It does not present the proposed finite-moment density gluing construction.

Theorem 8 treats general constraints with bag running intersection, normalized constraints, explicit local ball certificates, and local Łojasiewicz inequalities. It is therefore broader in feasible sets than a box-only result.

**Independent transcription check.** The paper's rough Putinar summary gives exponent (1/(13+3J)) when the local Łojasiewicz exponents equal one. Direct substitution into displayed Theorem 8 equation (5) instead gives a sufficient degree exponent

\[
\frac12\left(2+\frac53(J+4)\frac{11}3\right)
=\frac{55J+238}{18},
\]

while equation (6) gives (13/3). Thus equation (5) yields the slightly weaker error exponent (18/(55J+238)). This arithmetic discrepancy was checked in both publisher HTML and PDF; it is not a checked erratum or a claim that the theorem is false. Use Theorem 6 for the proposed box comparison, where the stated exponent agrees with the displayed bound.

## Dense kernel results that must receive credit

[de Klerk, Hess, and Laurent (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1065264) prove (O(r^{-2})) error for a hierarchy of **upper** bounds over the box. They optimize expectations under polynomial densities relative to the product Chebyshev measure, allowing Schmüdgen-type densities. Their Jackson kernel is nonnegative and normalized; its first eigenvalue is \(\cos(\pi/(r+2))\). The [open author version](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/25758/M106526.pdf) and indexed full-text excerpts were examined. The excerpts explicitly call this the “well-established polynomial kernel method.” The kernel and quadratic concentration mechanism should not be presented as new.

[Laurent and Slot (2023; online 2022)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11590-022-01922-5), *An effective version of Schmüdgen's Positivstellensatz for the hypercube*, give **dense lower** bounds with error (O(r^{-2})), or certificate degree (O(\varepsilon^{-1/2})). They use a tensor Jackson operator, positivity in the box preordering, and inverse-operator estimates. Their Proposition 6 bounds Jackson eigenvalue defects by a constant times (k^2/(r+2)^2). The publisher text and indexed full-text excerpts were examined. This is the appropriate dense benchmark, but it does not itself provide a certificate restricted to the original bags.

[Gribling, de Klerk, and Vera (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1080/02331934.2026.2641624), *Revisiting the convergence rate of the Lasserre hierarchy for polynomial optimization over the hypercube*, concern the **quadratic-module** hierarchy. The [open preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00544) was inspected, including its introduction and concluding discussion. It develops a polynomial-kernel route to bounds close to the existing order-(1/r) guarantee and studies limitations of that route. It does not supply a sparse preordering quadratic-rate theorem. Keep quadratic-module and preordering claims separate: multiplying univariate positive kernels naturally introduces products of box generators.

**Current correction to the dense benchmark.** [Gribling, de Klerk, and Vera, *Squared polynomial approximation kernels for the hypercube: improved error bounds and implications for Lasserre hierarchies*](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2605.31496), submitted 2026-05-29, supersedes the order-\(1/r\) context above. Its Theorem 7 states

\[
f_{\min}-f_{(2r)}\le c_1n^3d^2\|f\|_{1,\mathrm{cheb}}
\frac{\log_2^3r}{r^2},
\]

for the dense quadratic module when \(n\mid r\) and \(r/\log_2r\ge c_2n^2d^2\), with explicit universal constants. The [full preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.31496v1) was inspected in its introduction, kernel construction, Theorem 7, and conclusions. No sparse hierarchy or marginal-gluing statement appears there; a full-text search for “spars” found none. Its squared-kernel construction uses an input-dependent normalization and polynomial approximation of its reciprocal. This is a preprint whose important proofs were not independently verified during this audit.

**Independent comparison.** Exact normalization matters here. Integrating an unnormalized tensor kernel over coordinates outside a separator leaves input-dependent factors inside the bag functional. Agreement of the separator moments alone does not identify those weighted marginals. Therefore the newer dense theorem does not immediately imply the proposed sparse rounding statement. This explains a difference in mechanisms, not a proof that the newer methods cannot be adapted.

## Product-domain result already in the repository

[Magron (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1137/25M1773416), *Convergence Rates for Polynomial Optimization on Set Products*, Theorem 11 and Corollary 12, tensorizes factor kernels over Cartesian products. The theorem assumes orthogonal polynomial decompositions, kernel-section membership in the factor preorderings, and quantitative eigenvalue control. Orders add across factors. Its corollary gives \(O(t^{-2})\) for products of spheres, balls, simplexes, and boxes. The local [full text](../../literature/papers/magron2026-convergence-rates-for-polynomial-optimization/fulltext.md) and [source record](../../literature/papers/magron2026-convergence-rates-for-polynomial-optimization/paper.md) were read. The factors use disjoint coordinate tuples, and the resulting certificate is a dense product-domain certificate. It is not a certificate restricted to overlapping bags. Any extension of the proposed theorem to product-valued variables should credit this kernel tensorization theorem and explain the separate gluing step.

## Continuous graphical-model and consistency precedents

[Fix and Agarwal (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-10578-9_18), *Duality and the Continuous Graphical Model*, formulate a continuous local marginal LP and approximate its dual with polynomial or piecewise-polynomial functions. Theorem 2, checked in the [open author paper](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~afix/Papers/ECCV14.pdf), gives error (O(ML/(dK))) for (L)-Lipschitz potentials, degree (d), and (K) pieces. The proof uses Lipschitz optimal dual variables and Jackson approximation. Its local consistency constraints concern factor-to-variable marginals. Exactness with respect to the original optimization problem requires additional graph structure; it is not automatic on loopy graphs. This is strong prior for polynomial message approximation and finite-moment consistency relaxations, but its stated rate is first order and its proof is dual approximation.

[Lasserre (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1137/05064504X), *Convergent SDP-Relaxations in Polynomial Optimization with Sparsity*, proves asymptotic sparse hierarchy convergence under running intersection and suitable local compactness certificates. The publisher abstract and [open manuscript](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2006/04/1367.pdf) were located. The source establishes that sparsity plus running intersection and a convergent moment hierarchy are not new. This audit did not recheck every measure-extension lemma in that manuscript.

[Vorob'ev (1962)](https://doi.org/10.1137/1107014), *Consistent Families of Measures and Their Extensions*, characterizes universal extendability for finite-domain families using an acyclicity condition. The [primary archive abstract](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=tvp&option_lang=eng&paperid=4710&wshow=paper) was examined. It is a foundational consistency reference, not a substitute for checking regular conditional probabilities and measurable gluing on continuous boxes.

## Current-survey check

[Laurent and Slot (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-95-1446-5_8), *An Overview of Convergence Rates for Sum of Squares Hierarchies in Polynomial Optimization*, Section 3.3.2, still identifies Korda et al. as the recent extension of convergence-rate analysis to sparse bounds. The [published open chapter](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/36052/36052.pdf) was read there and in its dense-rate tables. This corroborates the comparison target; a survey can lag developments and cannot establish that a stronger result is absent.

## Precise remaining research distinction

The following is an assessment of the proposed argument, not a conclusion borrowed from the sources.

1. For actual local probability measures, a common coordinatewise polynomial Markov kernel makes overlapping smoothed marginals identical when the requisite moments agree. Junction-tree gluing then applies. This combines established ingredients; significance must come from the quantitative theorem and its consequences.
2. Tensor degree matters. Degree (d) in every separator coordinate requires moments of total degree as large as (d|S|), not merely total degree (d). For bag pseudomoments, the positive-kernel certificate must fit within the local relaxation degree.
3. A useful SDP upgrade would prove positivity of the smoothed bag density from local **preordering** positivity, without assuming a representing measure. The expectation comparison must use legitimate finite-pseudomoment estimates; replacing them by a sup-norm inequality valid only for actual measures would be a gap.
4. The verified (O(r^{-2})) result improves the exponent displayed in the inspected published theorem, but earlier author slides already assert that sparse rate. This comparison establishes neither a new rate nor priority. An actual-measure relaxation is stronger than the SDP and cannot by itself justify a claim about that SDP.
5. A (C^2) objective extension is useful but does not alone settle originality: classical positive-operator approximation already exploits first and second moments. A constrained-MINLP extension requires an additional argument because box smoothing generally leaves nonlinear feasible sets. Integer coordinates likewise require an explicitly support-preserving kernel or separate exact treatment.

No claim of being first is justified by this search. A publishable novelty assessment still needs an independent search, ideally including citation descendants of the closest sparse result and related approximation theory under different terminology.
