# Independent review of adaptive multiplier coefficient growth

Date: 2026-09-28. The reviewer did not author
[the proposition](universal-multiplier.md). No substantive correction was
needed. This is a supporting scale obstruction, not a degree lower bound.

The proof correctly uses the real cone generated by the rational
vanishing space of the fixed quintic point. It must not use the full
real SOS cone, because the quartic already belongs to that larger cone.
I reread the inherited closedness and nonmembership arguments in the
[radial-order note](../../research-20260927/rational-radial-exponent-obstruction.md).
They supply the closed cone required by the new proof.

Normalization by a positive rational preserves rational SOS: every
positive rational is a finite sum of rational squares. Rational
substitution by `x/t` and multiplication by the rational square `t²`
also preserve it. At the fixed point `p`, every square factor vanishes
because their squared sum is zero. The stated degree ceiling is safe,
including odd degree bounds, because highest-degree real squares cannot
cancel. Thus the product belongs to the asserted vanishing cone.

Closedness gives a coefficient neighborhood of the constant-one
multiplier whose products remain outside the cone. The needed control
of linear coefficients is valid. If all coefficients of degrees two
through `d` of `H(x/t)` are bounded by `B`, nonnegativity at `±R e_i`
gives

\[
 |b_i|/t\le1/R+B\sum_{j=2}^d R^{j-1}.
\]

Mixed monomials vanish on these axes. The choice of `R` and `c` in the
note makes the right side at most the neighborhood radius. Since
`t≥1`, the hypothesized bound on the original degree-at-least-two
coefficients also bounds their scaled versions. All constants are
independent of `t`. The same argument applies to a denominator
normalized by its nonzero value at the origin, because a continuous
polynomial with no real zeros has constant sign. The squared-product
map is continuous, so no extra positivity assumption is hidden there.

The norm-equivalence consequence is valid at fixed degree. Since the
multiplier is nonnegative, its uniform norm is its maximum. For the
normalized denominator use its absolute value. The quadratic witness
`1+t²||x||²` attains the stated order of growth, establishing sharpness
of the exponent only for this coefficient ratio. The proof deliberately
does not control the constants as degree grows and does not cover
multipliers vanishing at the origin or denominators with real zeros.

I also read Theorem 3.3, Proposition 3.4, and Remarks 3.10 in
[Scheiderer's primary paper](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/32129).
The source comparison correctly distinguishes its degree-`e−2`
multiplier theorem for the specified degree-`e` norm forms from a
uniform adaptive-degree theorem for rational strongly SOS-convex
quartics. A multiplier need not itself be SOS in the general-degree
source theorem; the quartic case does permit an SOS quadratic
multiplier. The local note does not claim otherwise.

No computation is needed for this all-input continuity and coefficient
argument. The review is mathematical and source based; it does not
formally verify the inherited cone theorem in Lean or establish
publication priority. No project-wide verification or CI inspection
was performed.
