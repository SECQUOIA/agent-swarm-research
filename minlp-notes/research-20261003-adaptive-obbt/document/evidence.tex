\section{Prospective comparison and verification}\label{sec:evidence}

\subsection{Exact certificate demonstrations}

The exact checks and LP proposals are recorded in
\href{run:../theory/remaining-benefit-checks.json}{the certificate-check results}
and
\href{run:../theory/remaining-benefit-driver-checks.json}{the certified-driver results}.
Automatic discovery finds protected boxes on six of eight fixed
fixtures: three simple square-stall examples, a three-variable quadratic
with nonnegative square variables, and two linear models. On both
strictly contracting square fixtures the proposals do not certify a
fixed box. Those outcomes are inconclusive, not evidence that useful
tightening is absent. Every accepted certificate is replayed exactly.

The three-variable example minimizes
\[
 f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^3x_i^2+\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j
     =\tfrac12\left(\norm{x}_2^2+(x_1+x_2+x_3)^2\right)
\]
on $[-1,1]^3$, with unique optimum zero at the origin. The lifted LP
has square variables $s_i\ge0$, McCormick product variables $w_{ij}$,
and cutoff $\sum_i s_i+\sum_{i<j}w_{ij}\le0$.
At each face $x_i=\pm1$, set the other original coordinates to zero,
$s_i=1$, other squares to zero, the products involving $i$ to zero,
and the opposite product to $-1$. These feasible lifted points protect
all six faces. At the origin, squares zero and all three products $-1$
give objective $-3$, so every later OBBT relaxation has lower bound
at most $-3$. Nonnegative squares and $w_{ij}\ge-1$ prove that the
current LP bound is exactly $-3$. The witness also satisfies exact
convex square epigraphs: negative square variables do not cause this
stall. This instantiates the earlier study's row-stall mechanism.

Table~\ref{tab:driver} records representative standalone driver outcomes.
The linear coupled example reaches its fixed box after one exact round.
The zero-cutoff square contracts by one half each round and correctly
remains unfinished after five rounds. For the positive-cutoff square,
three rounds are certified; a later rational reconstruction fails an
exact primal row check. The last completed box is retained, with an
inconclusive status. This is an implementation precision limit, not a
mathematical stall.

Additional checks cover zero round budgets, invalid dual proofs,
rollback of incomplete rounds, preservation of earlier certified rounds,
invalid optional objective proposals, model and domain changes,
degenerate boxes, cutoff mixing, cached-pool frontiers, and the residual

\subsection{Frozen protocol}

The protocol in
\pathref{../experiments/PROTOCOL.md}{experiments/PROTOCOL.md}
selects 12 public models and 8 synthetic diagnostics before the current
comparative outcomes. Public admission uses the earlier September
study's metadata: between 5 and 300 variables, between 1 and 200
nonlinear variables, at most 500 quadratic terms, and at least five
seconds in every available historical native SCIP baseline run.
Eligible names are sorted by the SHA-256 digest of a fixed study prefix
and the name; the first twelve are retained, at most one per historical
family. The entire eligibility list and input hashes are frozen.
This is a selected cohort informed by separate historical results, not
a random sample of MINLP applications.

The synthetic structures are point packing, coupled square budgets,
bilinear cycles, and sparse indefinite quadratic programs, with two sizes
of each and generator seed 20261003. Distinct tiny pilot instances
exercise APIs and callback coverage and are excluded from the comparison.
Synthetic outcomes are reported separately from public-instance outcomes.

Each model is run with SCIP seed shifts 0 and 1 in native, fixed, and
adaptive arms, for 120 planned jobs. The native arm has no additional
propagator. The fixed and adaptive arms share the LP allowance and native
settings; the adaptive arm adds ordering, witness screening, and pilot
stopping. Native SCIP OBBT stays enabled throughout. No known optimum,
external feasible solution, or reference warm start enters the experiment.
The task order is shuffled with seed 20261003. Numerical libraries and
SCIP use one thread, with at most two worker processes at a time.

Each solver call has a ten-second budget including model construction and
sidecar work. Process elapsed time additionally includes startup, parsing,
validation, and output and is recorded separately. Every returned
incumbent is evaluated against the original bounds, integrality,
quadratic constraints, and objective. The declared numerical acceptance
threshold is $10^{-6}$ for the maximum scaled constraint residual and
integrality residual. An optimal status with an invalid incumbent counts
as unsuccessful. This is a floating-point diagnostic, not exact
feasibility certification.

The primary reported penalized time uses full process elapsed time for
successful jobs and 20 seconds for unsuccessful jobs (PAR-2). The
one-second-shift geometric mean reported below uses full process time.
Archived capped means instead use $\min\{t,10\}$ for process time $t$;
they are diagnostic, can hide overruns, and do not penalize a fast
unsuccessful termination. Raw total process time exposes startup
and overrun costs. The normalized final gap is
\[
 \min\left\{1,\frac{\max\{0,p-d\}}{\max\{1,|p|,|d|\}}\right\},
\]
where $p$ is a valid numerical incumbent objective and $d$ is a finite
dual bound; missing admissible values receive score one. Paired gap
comparisons report their eligible denominators. No timing statistic is
restricted silently to cases solved by every arm. The two seeds are
repeated runs on each model, not forty independent application families.

\subsection{Complete campaign outcomes}

All 120 scheduled jobs in \texttt{campaign-01} completed. Each arm solved
the same 14 of 40 model--seed pairs: 2 of 24 public pairs and 12 of 16
synthetic pairs. Neither extra policy added or lost a solve. Each arm
had one no-incumbent run: \texttt{genpooling\_meyer04}, seed zero.
No process failure, invalid incumbent, material objective mismatch, or
inconsistent primal--dual pair was reported under the declared numerical
checks.

Summed full process elapsed times were 308.050 seconds for native,
313.407 for fixed, and 313.618 for adaptive. The corresponding uncapped
one-second-shift geometric means were 5.957, 6.285, and 6.265 seconds.
These totals retain startup, validation, output, and deadline overrun.
The largest solver-call elapsed time was 10.093 seconds; the largest
complete process time was 10.606 seconds. The experiments shared a
36-logical-CPU machine with unrelated studies; starting load averages
were 17.76, 15.65, and 14.11. The small timing differences do not support
a claim of a stable speed advantage for either extra policy. The
comparison provides no evidence of a net solving benefit.

The adaptive policy used 1,719 directional LPs compared with 2,172 for
fixed, a 20.9\% reduction. This did not reduce measured sidecar time:
adaptive used 23.123 seconds versus fixed's 19.530 seconds, an 18.4\%
increase. Adaptive entered 741 calls, including 639 nonroot calls and
43 incumbent retriggers; fixed entered 404 calls, including 300 nonroot
calls and 39 incumbent retriggers. Accepted bound changes numbered
308 and 112, respectively. These counts show that the experiment
exercised in-tree tightening and retriggering. The lower LP count did
not translate into lower complete plugin cost; the additional calls
also required construction and checks.

The adaptive policy screened only two directions from cached feasible
witnesses. It stopped 574 calls after an unproductive pilot, 113 at the
per-call LP allowance, and six at a time budget. The corresponding fixed
counts were 349 LP-budget stops and seven time-budget stops. Both extra
arms recorded 48 unsupported-domain calls, all on \texttt{qp3}, where
the current box was unbounded for this sidecar. These calls remain in
the costs and outcomes. Four fixed and 26 adaptive directional attempts
did not supply a usable bound; individual LP status details for those
attempts were not retained, so their causes are not inferred.

Mean public gap scores improved relative to native, with much of the
change concentrated in \texttt{bayes2\_50} under both seeds. Among 23
public pairs with finite comparable gaps, adaptive improved four and
worsened ten by more than $10^{-4}$; fixed improved five and worsened
ten. Across all 39 comparable pairs, adaptive had four gap wins and
twelve losses. The averages reflect the sizes of changes as well as
their counts. Across all 40 pairs, adaptive had two PAR-2 wins and eleven
losses exceeding 5\% relative to native; fixed had one win and ten
losses. These are descriptive counts from a small, short-budget cohort,
not statistical evidence across independent problem families. The
practical conclusion is to retain native SCIP as the default and keep
the additional policy experimental.

\subsection{Provenance and targeted checks}

The campaign directory
\pathref{../experiments/runs/campaign-01}{experiments/runs/campaign-01}
contains immutable input and source hashes, the actual worker source,
every raw outcome and solver log, per-run analysis, and the complete
summary. The policy and analyzer were frozen before holdout outcomes.
During the uninterrupted campaign, review found that an interrupted
attempt could be overwritten by the live runner's resume path. The live
runner was fixed to archive incomplete attempts before retrying. This
recovery-only change did not alter the running campaign, its frozen
sources, or any measured attempt; a separate targeted recovery check
passed. The frozen campaign remains the authority for its reported data.

The final combined solver, reviewer, and experiment suite passed 31
targeted tests. Separate exact checks cover the certificate contracts,
constrained-theory examples, and optional-work accounting. A direct
OSiL evaluator checked all twelve normalized public models independently
of the experiment importer. The completed campaign audit checked all
120 expected jobs and numerically validated all 69 returned public
incumbents against the original OSiL data. The document build checks
references, citations, and PDF metadata. These are local checks for this
continuation; no project-wide verification or CI status/log inspection
is represented as evidence here.

The exact commands and outcomes are recorded in
\pathref{../VERIFICATION.md}{VERIFICATION.md} and the
\href{run:README.md}{document README}. The
\href{run:COVERAGE.md}{claim and evidence map} distinguishes proved
statements, checked proposals, numerical validation, and empirical
outcomes. Internal independent review is not formal verification or
external peer review.
