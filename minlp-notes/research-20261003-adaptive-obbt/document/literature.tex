\section{Related work and attribution}\label{sec:literature}

Selective OBBT, learned variable selection, reuse after incumbent
improvements, and adaptive scheduling of solver components all have
substantial prior work. The relevant gap is narrower than the phrase
``adaptive OBBT'' suggests.

Gleixner, Berthold, M\"uller, and Weltge develop primal filtering, LP
ordering, and Lagrangian variable bounds~\cite{gleixner2017enhancements}.
Their methods are direct antecedents of screening and cheap dual reuse.
The inspected SCIP implementation already includes feasibility filtering,
aggregate filtering, budgets, and generalized variable bounds. Its
separate generalized-bound propagator detects improved cutoffs and
reevaluates stored bounds~\cite{scip2026obbt}. Coramin implements
feasible-solution and aggregate-objective filtering and attributes these
techniques to the same OBBT literature~\cite{coramin2023filters}.
Therefore, incumbent awareness or skipping some coordinate LPs cannot
carry a broad novelty claim.

Cengil et al. dynamically select OBBT variables using learned rankings
for AC optimal power flow~\cite{cengil2025learning}. Their experiments
address relaxation strengthening for phase-angle differences; the result
is not a general MINLP runtime guarantee. G\'omez-Casares et al. study
conic OBBT, dual-information propagation, and instance-level learned
configuration selection in polynomial optimization~\cite{gomezcasares2025domain}.
Application-specific work also studies the balance between bound quality
and computation for ReLU networks and selective integrality in tightening
subproblems~\cite{badilla2024tradeoffs,pineda2025sweetspot}.
Explicitly retaining redundant bounds on lifted variables can improve
or worsen complete solver performance~\cite{gonzalezdiaz2025lifted}.
These findings support charging full cost and measuring complete solves.

The mathematical ingredients here are established. Nested fixed sets
and monotone iterations belong to order-theoretic fixed-point reasoning
and prior domain-reduction analysis~\cite{tarski1955fixpoint,caprara2010domain,caprara2016theoretical,belotti2012fbbt}.
The residual estimate is a matrix geometric-series argument of the
Banach--Perov type~\cite{jachymski2016perov}. Exact basis regions are
standard multiparametric LP objects~\cite{borrelli2003parametric}.
The report supplies their OBBT-specific premises, finite witnesses,
cutoff and domain reuse rules, and checked examples. It does not propose
a new general order theorem, contraction principle, or LP duality theorem.

The protected-box construction differs from ordinary current-round
filtering in one essential check: its witnesses must be feasible in the
relaxation rebuilt on their own coordinate hull. That establishes the
persistence needed for an all-future-round claim. Likewise, a residual
certificate needs a uniform sensitivity bound over a verified invariant
region, including every active-set change. A sequence of observed
contraction ratios is not such a bound. These precise contracts, and
their distinction from the implemented heuristic policy, are the
contribution assessed here. This targeted literature audit does not
establish publication priority for every formulation of those contracts.

Broader computation selection also has precedents. SCIP studies use
bandits for expensive components and schedule diving and large-neighborhood
heuristics jointly~\cite{hendel2018adaptive,chmiela2023scheduling}.
Hay et al. formalize the value of further computation and explicit
stopping in a probabilistic decision model~\cite{hay2012computations}.
Applying those ideas here requires assumptions on how strengthening
changes the later search. Anderson acceleration can propose fixed-point
candidates~\cite{walker2011anderson}; unconstrained mixing does not
make such candidates valid domain reductions. A proposed protected box
can be checked independently, but it is a lower limit on tightening,
not permission to delete the rest of the current search domain.
