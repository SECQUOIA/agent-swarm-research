\section{Finite certificates for remaining benefit}\label{sec:certificates}

\subsection{Primal witnesses and a frozen round}

Let $B=[\ell,u]$ and write $T_U(B)=[\ell',u']$. A point in the
projected set $K_U(B)$ is a primal witness. In a lifted implementation,
one must also supply and check its lifted coordinates.

\begin{proposition}[Current-round ceilings]\label{prop:current}
Let $W$ be a nonempty finite subset of $K_U(B)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-ceilings}
 0\le\ell'_i-\ell_i\le\min_{w\in W}w_i-\ell_i,
 \qquad
 0\le u_i-u'_i\le u_i-\max_{w\in W}w_i.
\end{equation}
In particular, a witness attaining $\ell_i$ or $u_i$ proves that the
corresponding directional bound cannot improve in this frozen round.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The minimum over all of $K_U(B)$ is no larger than the minimum over its
subset $W$; the maximum is no smaller than the maximum over $W$.
Containment in $B$ supplies nonnegativity.
\end{proof}

This is the mathematical basis of established feasibility filtering.
A relaxation optimum or a point returned for one coordinate may screen
several other directions. Screening from an already available point can
be cheap. Solving all $2n$ directional problems to obtain the witnesses
can cost as much as the round being screened; a certificate alone provides
no computational saving.

The quantifier ``this frozen round'' is essential. A witness may violate
the relaxation rebuilt on a smaller box, even if its original coordinates
remain in that box. Consequently, the current-round ceiling is not an
upper bound on total future tightening.
For example, relax $w=x^2$ on $[0,u]$, $u>0$, with square endpoint tangents and
the secant, and impose $w\le0$. The upper support is $u/2$, attained
at $(x,w)=(u/2,0)$. That point violates the new upper-endpoint tangent
after rebuilding on $[0,u/2]$. Repeated rounds halve the upper bound
and converge to zero, so the eventual reduction is $u$, even though
the first witness bounds the present reduction by $u/2$.

\subsection{An inner box protected under all future rounds}

\begin{definition}[Protected box]
A nonempty box $P=[a,b]\subseteq B$ is protected for the specified
relaxation family and cutoff $U$ if there is a finite witness pool
$W\subseteq K_U(P)$ whose coordinate hull is $P$.
\end{definition}

Feasibility is checked on the relaxation rebuilt on $P$, not merely on
the current outer box $B$. Up to $2n$ witnesses suffice: one attaining
each lower and upper face. Coincident faces can share witnesses.

\begin{theorem}[Finite protection certificate]\label{thm:protected}
Under~\eqref{eq:monotonicity}, a protected box $P$ satisfies
$T_U(P)=P$. Every exact OBBT iterate starting at a box $B_0\supseteq P$
contains $P$. More generally, this remains true for rounds with cutoffs
$U_k\ge U$ and for any sequence of exact directional OBBT updates using
the same monotone relaxation construction. Thus, for any such future
iterate $B_k=[\ell^k,u^k]$ starting at $B=[\ell,u]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:protected-ceilings}
 0\le\ell_i^k-\ell_i\le a_i-\ell_i,\qquad
 0\le u_i-u_i^k\le u_i-b_i.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $W\subseteq K_U(P)\subseteq P$ and $\hull W=P$, taking coordinate
hulls gives $T_U(P)=P$. If $P\subseteq B_k$ and $U_k\ge U$, then
Lemma~\ref{lem:order} gives
$P=T_U(P)\subseteq T_{U_k}(B_k)$, proving the round assertion by
induction. For a single directional update, the witness attaining the
corresponding face of $P$ remains feasible on the larger current box,
so the new bound cannot cross that face. Induction over individual
updates proves the sequential assertion. The endpoint inequalities are
exactly $P\subseteq B_k\subseteq B$.
\end{proof}

The same pool bounds the objective of the specified relaxation. Suppose
its lifted points $\widehat w$ use the same linear lifted objective
$c^T\widehat w+c_0$ and the lifted relaxation sets themselves are nested
under box restriction, as in the reference family below. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thresholds}
 U_W=\max_{\widehat w\in W}(c^T\widehat w+c_0),\qquad
 V_W=\min_{\widehat w\in W}(c^T\widehat w+c_0).
\end{equation}
The protection certificate remains reusable at any cutoff at least
$U_W$, provided the current box contains $P$ and the model and relaxation
rows are unchanged. The minimum relaxed objective on every such future
box is at most $V_W$, because the witnesses remain available.
If $L$ is a valid lower bound for that relaxed minimum, $V_W-L$ is an
upper bound on its possible increase from $L$. It does not bound the
improvement from cuts, integrality reasoning, or a different relaxation.

A smaller incumbent can cross $U_W$ and invalidate this certificate.
This gives an exact event at which reconsideration is justified, without
claiming that paying for another round will be profitable. Domain changes
can invalidate containment $P\subseteq B$, and additional cuts can
invalidate witness feasibility. Both require a new check. A protected
box for an LP relaxation may be removed by integer rounding; the theorem
does not include that stronger operator unless its witnesses satisfy the
corresponding premises.
One sufficient extension is integral protected endpoints on every integer
coordinate: if an exact lower bound is at most an integer face $a_i$,
its ceiling is still at most $a_i$, and the analogous floor argument
holds at $b_i$. This proves preservation under that rounding operation,
but not under arbitrary native integer propagation.

The hull of a finite nonempty set of original feasible sublevel points
is also protected whenever their exact lifts are available: the points
attain every hull face, and relaxation validity supplies feasible lifts
on the hull. Unlike generic relaxation witnesses, these original points
survive every additional cut valid for that sublevel set. Producing exact
feasible points for nonlinear equalities may itself be difficult.

\subsection{Adapting witnesses to a new cutoff}

In a fixed convex lifted relaxation, let $v$ be a feasible witness and
$z$ another feasible point with objective $c(z)<c(v)$, where $c$ includes
the objective constant. For $c(z)\le U'<c(v)$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixing}
 \theta=\frac{U'-c(z)}{c(v)-c(z)},\qquad
 v'=z+\theta(v-z).
\end{equation}
Convexity gives feasibility, and linearity gives $c(v')=U'$.
This is an inexpensive way to produce current-round witnesses at a
tighter cutoff. The point $z$ is a relaxation anchor; it need not be
feasible in the original nonlinear model. To certify a new protected box,
one must still take the hull of the new points and check them again in
the relaxation rebuilt on that hull. Convex mixing in an outer relaxation
does not perform that last step.

\begin{proposition}[Exact cutoff frontier of a finite witness pool]\label{prop:frontier}
Let $W=\{z_1,\ldots,z_k\}$ be feasible points of one convex lifted
relaxation with affine objective $c(z)$. Every coordinate minimum and
maximum over
$\operatorname{conv}(W)\cap\{z:c(z)\le U\}$ is attained either at
an original point with $c(z_j)\le U$ or at a mixture of a pair whose
objectives lie strictly on opposite sides of $U$, chosen to have
objective exactly $U$, provided that cutoff intersection is nonempty.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use convex coefficients $\alpha_j\ge0$ with
$\sum_j\alpha_j=1$ and $\sum_j\alpha_jc(z_j)\le U$.
Every extreme coefficient vector either has one positive component or
has two positive components and an active cutoff. Otherwise a nonzero
perturbation preserving the one or two active affine equations keeps
both signs feasible, contradicting extremality. Linear coordinate
objectives attain extrema at these coefficient vectors. With two
positive components the strict straddling gives the stated mixture;
if both objective values equal $U$, a single original point suffices.
\end{proof}

The \texttt{cutoff\_frontier} routine generates these points using at
most quadratically many pair mixtures and no new LP. It optimizes over
the cached convex hull, not the full relaxation. An empty result says
only that this pool cannot supply a witness. On a changed node box,
the cached points must first pass the rebuilt row checks.

\subsection{A residual certificate under a uniform contraction bound}

An alternative certificate bounds the accumulated future motion of the
OBBT endpoints. Represent a box by $p=(-\ell,u)\in\R^{2n}$, so shrinking
a box decreases $p$ componentwise, and let $F(p)$ represent one exact
round at a fixed cutoff. The next result requires a uniform bound on a
region, rather than a ratio observed on one trajectory.

\begin{theorem}[A posteriori remaining motion]\label{thm:residual}
Let $\mathcal D$ be a convex set of valid box-endpoint vectors that is
invariant under $F$, and assume $F(p)\le p$ on $\mathcal D$. Suppose
a nonnegative matrix $M$ satisfies, componentwise,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-lipschitz}
 |F(p)-F(q)|\le M|p-q|\qquad(p,q\in\mathcal D).
\end{equation}
For $p_0\in\mathcal D$, put $p_{k+1}=F(p_k)$ and
$d=p_0-p_1\ge0$. If a finite vector $e\ge0$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supersolution}
 d+Me\le e,
\end{equation}
then the limit $p_\infty$ exists and
\[
 0\le p_0-p_\infty\le e,\qquad
 0\le p_1-p_\infty\le e-d.
\]
If $\rho(M)<1$, one admissible choice is $e=(I-M)^{-1}d$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $\Delta_k=p_k-p_{k+1}\ge0$. The uniform estimate gives
$\Delta_{k+1}\le M\Delta_k$, so $\Delta_k\le M^kd$.
For each finite $r$, induction using~\eqref{eq:supersolution} yields
$\sum_{k=0}^{r}M^kd\le e$: the base follows from $d\le e$, and
the next partial sum is at most $d+Me\le e$. Therefore
$0\le p_0-p_{r+1}\le e$. The decreasing endpoint sequence is bounded
below, hence converges coordinatewise; taking limits proves the first
bound. Subtracting the first movement $d$ proves the second. If
$\rho(M)<1$, the nonnegative convergent Neumann series gives
$e=\sum_{k\ge0}M^kd$, which satisfies~\eqref{eq:supersolution} with
equality.
\end{proof}

For a scalar contraction constant $q<1$ in a compatible norm, the
corresponding estimate is the familiar geometric-series bound
$\norm{p_0-p_\infty}\le\norm{p_0-p_1}/(1-q)$.
An observed ratio of two successive endpoint changes is not a proof
of~\eqref{eq:matrix-lipschitz}. Active-set changes can make the next
round behave differently. A derivative bound at one point is likewise
insufficient; its bound must cover the invariant region and every
relevant active regime. The reference implementation checks the rational
supersolution inequality. That arithmetic check does not manufacture the
problem-specific uniform matrix bound.

The residual must be from an exact Jacobi round. A selective, interrupted,
or inaccurate round may show zero movement while unprocessed directions
still admit large changes. If a certified upper bound $\overline d\ge d$
is available, using $\overline d+Me\le e$ still bounds total motion
by $e$; the after-round bound is $e-d$ when the exact first movement
is known, not $e-\overline d$.

A finite rebuilt-McCormick example supplies its own uniform derivative
bound. With $x\in[0,u]$, square endpoint tangents and secant, and
cutoff $w\le r^2$ for $u\ge r>0$, the upper endpoint map is
\[
 F(u)=\frac{u^2+r^2}{2u},\qquad
 F'(u)=\frac{1-r^2/u^2}{2}.
\]
The upper-endpoint tangent proves the upper bound, and the lift
$(F(u),r^2)$ attains it. The interval $[r,u_0]$ is invariant, with
uniform constant $q=(1-r^2/u_0^2)/2<1/2$. For $r=1,u_0=2$, the
first residual is $3/4$ and $q=3/8$, giving total-motion ceiling
$6/5$; the actual total motion is $1$. This bound covers the changing
endpoint tangent coefficients, rather than assuming they are fixed.

If a common invariant region and cutoff interval admit the stronger
uniform estimate
\[
 |F_U(p)-F_V(q)|\le M|p-q|+h|U-V|,
 \qquad h\ge0,\quad\rho(M)<1,
\]
then any fixed points in that region satisfy
$|p_U-p_V|\le(I-M)^{-1}h|U-V|$. Substitute the fixed points, iterate
the nonnegative inequality, and use $M^k\to0$. Without this extra
uniform estimate, an old residual certificate cannot be transferred
to a tighter cutoff merely because the cutoff improved.

\subsection{An exact reference checker}

The reference family has real original variables $x$ with rational box
endpoints, auxiliary variables $w_{ij}$ for selected products, fixed rational linear rows in
$(x,w)$, and a rational linear objective. On $B=[\ell,u]$, each product
uses the four inequalities
\begin{align*}
 w_{ij}&\ge\ell_jx_i+\ell_ix_j-\ell_i\ell_j,&
 w_{ij}&\ge u_jx_i+u_ix_j-u_iu_j,\\
 w_{ij}&\le\ell_jx_i+u_ix_j-u_i\ell_j,&
 w_{ij}&\le u_jx_i+\ell_ix_j-\ell_iu_j.
\end{align*}
The case $i=j$ is allowed. It then gives a finite linear relaxation of
$x_i^2$, with endpoint tangents below and a secant above, rather than the
exact convex epigraph. Box restrictions and the linear objective cutoff
complete the rows.

These product relaxations are monotone under box restriction. For
distinct indices they describe the convex hull of the bilinear graph
on the rectangle, whose hull decreases when the rectangle shrinks.
For a square, each newly raised lower endpoint produces a tangent at
least as high as the old lower-endpoint tangent on the smaller interval;
the same holds at the upper endpoint, and the new secant is lower.
Keeping the fixed rows therefore preserves the required inclusion.

\pathref{../theory/certificates.py}{theory/certificates.py} represents
coefficients and witnesses as exact rational numbers and checks every
row. A floating-point LP may propose a witness, but only rational row
checks issue a certificate. The checked candidate hull must be contained
in the declared outer box, and its witnesses must attain every face.
Failure to produce or rationally validate a witness returns an unresolved
outcome; it is not evidence of absence of a protected box.

The certificate binds the full fixed model and relaxation construction.
Equality of stored row arrays does not prove where a local row is valid.
The caller must justify every fixed row on the original sublevel set
and domain concerned before interpreting the LP certificate as a valid
domain-reduction statement for that problem.
This small checker establishes the witness implications above. It does
not certify native SCIP rows, prove correctness of an entire nonlinear
search, or convert a numerical incumbent into an exact feasible point.
The operational policy evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:evidence} is
described separately in Section~\ref{sec:implementation}.

A ceiling on coordinate or relaxation-objective improvement is also
limited to those measures. An OBBT call with zero immediate bound gain
can still expose useful dual information or a generalized variable bound;
Gleixner et al. discuss this under degeneracy in their Remark~5
\cite{gleixner2017enhancements}. A stopping certificate for endpoint
movement therefore does not certify that every side effect of a new
auxiliary solve is worthless.
