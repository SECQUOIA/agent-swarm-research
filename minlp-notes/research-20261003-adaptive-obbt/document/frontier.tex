\section{What is established and what remains open}\label{sec:frontier}

The developed mathematical chain specifies the benefit being bounded,
the relaxation to which the statement applies, the event that invalidates
reuse, and the exact arithmetic needed to verify a proposed certificate.
It handles current-round screening, all-future-round protection,
conditional residual bounds, cutoff sensitivity, and active-set changes
under their respective assumptions. The implementations and exact
examples make those statements reviewable. The solver experiment provides
no demonstrated net solving benefit for the tested additional policy;
native SCIP remains the default. Fewer directional LPs did not mean
lower complete sidecar cost.

Several stronger interpretations do not follow. A protected inner box
does not authorize shrinking the search domain to that box: it limits
what the specified OBBT procedure can remove. An exact bound on endpoint
motion does not bound useful dual side effects, discrete propagation,
or total search time. A certificate for fixed LP rows need not survive
new cuts, integer rounding, or a different relaxation. A numerical solve
with validated sidecar bounds is not an independently certified complete
MINLP solve. A cap on optional work does not preserve the baseline's
search trajectory or runtime.

The remaining research questions are concrete. Cheap discovery of
protected boxes from points already generated by a native solver may
make future-round certificates operationally useful; the existence of
the checker alone does not demonstrate that saving. Uniform sensitivity
certificates for rebuilt nonlinear relaxations need bounds on changing
coefficients and coverage of all reachable regimes. Applying the nonlinear
repair theorem to broader model classes requires usable uniform residual,
repair, growth, and objective-error constants. A controller choosing among cuts, OBBT,
decomposition, branching, and incumbent search needs a validated model
connecting their different mathematical benefits to later search work.

These are genuine open extensions, not missing claims silently supplied
by the current software. The finite-history example and the enhanced-tree
runtime counterexample identify why stronger conclusions need additional
information. They do not prove that every useful restricted version is
impossible. Completion of this report means completion of its stated
proofs, implementations, checks, and experiment; it does not mean that
adaptive solver effort allocation is an exhausted research area.
