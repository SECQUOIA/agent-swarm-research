\section{Implementation and numerical scope}\label{sec:implementation}

The software has two separate roles. The exact reference modules check
the mathematical certificate formats on declared rational relaxation
families. The integrated solver prototype applies a limited scheduling
policy to a numerical search. Its outcomes test that policy, not the
automatic discovery of protected boxes or uniform contraction matrices.

\subsection{Exact reference modules}

\pathref{../theory/certificates.py}{theory/certificates.py} checks
current-round witness ceilings, protected boxes, cutoff mixing, objective
ceilings, and residual supersolutions. Exact-data constructors reject
floating-point inputs: intended coefficients must be integers, rational
strings, or rational numbers. The optional SciPy proposal routine can
solve up to $2n$ endpoint LPs, rationalize the proposed points, and check
their hull. It returns no certificate if any exact row check fails.
An optional additional objective LP proposes a stronger objective ceiling
after a protected box is found; only its primal feasibility must be
verified. Its cost is also charged.
This complete check of a proposed certificate is not a complete search
for every certificate that might exist.

\pathref{../theory/check_constrained_obbt.py}{theory/check_constrained_obbt.py}
verifies exact basis calculations and the explicit changing-cell and
cutoff examples. It does not enumerate basis regions of an arbitrary
solver model. The remaining-motion majorant routine checks
$d+Me\le e$; the caller is responsible for the uniform sensitivity
and invariance premises. The distinction prevents a successful arithmetic
test from being mistaken for an unsupported theorem about the native
solver's complete OBBT map.

\subsection{A standalone certified stopping workflow}

\pathref{../theory/certified_driver.py}{theory/certified_driver.py}
makes the protected-box check operational for the rational reference
family. A numerical solver proposes a primal point $z$ and multipliers
$\alpha$ for each endpoint LP $\min c^Tz$ subject to $Az\le b$.
The driver reconstructs rationals and checks exactly
\[
 Az\le b,\qquad \alpha\le0,\qquad
 A^T\alpha=c,\qquad c^Tz=\alpha^Tb.
\]
Weak duality proves the endpoint optimum. All variable bounds are
explicit rows. The driver commits a Jacobi round only after every
endpoint proof passes, then rechecks its witness pool on the rebuilt
coordinate hull. If that check succeeds, it stops with a protected
fixed box. An optional objective proposal needs only verified primal
feasibility to improve the objective ceiling.

The statuses distinguish a certified fixed box, an exhausted round
budget (\texttt{unfinished}), and a failed exact reconstruction
(\texttt{inconclusive}). An incomplete round is discarded while previous
certified rounds remain valid. Exact termination need not occur in
finite time, and rational reconstruction can fail even when the
underlying mathematical LP is well behaved. The driver retains proof
objects and reports all LP calls and elapsed time including exact checks.
This is a complete workflow for checking and using the stated stopping
certificate; it is not a complete exact LP solver or an all-future
stopping rule for native SCIP's changing relaxation.

\subsection{A limited policy inside SCIP}

\pathref{../solver/adaptive_obbt.py}{solver/adaptive_obbt.py} builds
the same original quadratic constraints in SCIP for all arms. It
normalizes maximization to minimization and uses an epigraph variable
for a quadratic objective. The additional propagator uses current local
bounds of the transformed original variables to construct a separate
linear relaxation. Native SCIP OBBT remains enabled in every arm.
The campaign uses SCIP 10.0.2 through PySCIPOpt 6.2.1, with Python
3.13.11 and SciPy 1.18.1. The newer pinned SCIP source inspected for
the literature audit is a separate artifact, not the benchmark binary.

The side relaxation contains the original linearized quadratic rows,
McCormick inequalities for bilinear products, square tangents and a
secant, auxiliary interval bounds, and an incumbent cutoff when available.
The square relaxation uses five current tangent locations and a bounded
pool of retained tangents. It is a finite LP approximation, not the
exact square epigraph used in Proposition~\ref{prop:equality}. It also
differs from the endpoint-only square relaxation of the smallest exact
reference checker. The theoretical statements apply to their declared
families; their quantitative rates are not transferred between them.

The fixed and adaptive arms share the same total directional LP allowance.
The adaptive arm orders nonlinear-variable directions using quadratic
coefficient and current-width scores, rechecks cached witnesses to screen
directions with small possible current-round gain, and stops a pilot
after unproductive probes. Scheduling state is indexed by node. A first
visit, a sufficiently improved incumbent, or a sufficiently reduced box
can admit another call, subject to depth, call, LP, and time limits.
Cutoff and box-change thresholds are policy parameters, not certified
predictions of runtime savings.

The frozen default allowance is 64 directional LPs in total, at most
12 per root call and 4 per nonroot call, 24 calls in total, 3 calls per
node, and depth at most 64. Each auxiliary LP has a nominal 0.25-second
limit, and sidecar work has a nominal allowance of 10\% of the run's
time budget. The adaptive pilot uses two LPs and stops if the accumulated
relative tightening is below $10^{-3}$. A 1\% scaled cutoff improvement
or a 10\% decrease in a coordinate width can retrigger a call. The
campaign source snapshot is the authority for every parameter actually
used in the experiments.

Cached primal points are rechecked against the current frozen side LP
before screening. A direction is screened when a checked point is within
$10^{-7}\max\{1,u_i-\ell_i\}$ of its endpoint. This is a tolerance
ceiling for the frozen LP, not proof that every future round is useless.
The protected-box and matrix-tail certificates are not used to control
the measured policy. No claim is made that a cheaply available
all-future-round certificate was demonstrated on the application cohort.

\subsection{Validated sidecar bounds}

The auxiliary numerical LP solver proposes dual information. The
propagator does not use a reported primal coordinate optimum directly as
a lower bound. The following residual formula explains its validation.

\begin{proposition}[A bound from any signed dual vector]\label{prop:dual-residual}
Consider minimizing $c^Tz$ over $Az\le b$ and a finite box
$z\in[\ell,u]$. For any $y\le0$, set $r=c-A^Ty$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-residual}
 \min c^Tz\ \ge\ y^Tb+
 \sum_j\min\{r_j\ell_j,r_ju_j\}.
\end{equation}
No exact dual stationarity is required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $y\le0$ and $Az\le b$, one has $y^TAz\ge y^Tb$.
Therefore $c^Tz=y^TAz+r^Tz\ge y^Tb+r^Tz$. Minimizing the latter
linear residual independently over the finite coordinate intervals
gives~\eqref{eq:dual-residual}.
\end{proof}

The implementation rounds the products, sums, and residual intervals
outward, rejecting nonfinite inputs or overflow. This validates a bound
for the stored binary floating-point LP rows. The row builder separately
forms exact dyadic expressions from the imported binary data and rounds
the coefficients outward. For an exact row $a^Tz\le\beta$, a rounded
coefficient vector $\widetilde a$ is made valid by choosing
\[
 \widetilde\beta\ge\beta+
 \sum_j\max\{(\widetilde a_j-a_j)\ell_j,
              (\widetilde a_j-a_j)u_j\}.
\]
Every point satisfying the exact row on the box then satisfies the
rounded row. The rounding arithmetic and residual-bound tests concern
this imported binary model, not an independent reconstruction of exact
decimal or symbolic data upstream of the importer.

The plugin adds a further outward margin before submitting a local bound
to SCIP. It rejects a proposal outside the current domain and checks
compatibility with integer rounding. It never turns a side-LP
infeasibility report into a node cutoff. Unsupported finite-domain or
arithmetic cases are recorded and leave native search available. A
cached witness from another node is only a proposal; passing every
current row check is required before use.

These safeguards do not constitute a certificate for the entire SCIP
solve. In particular, the cutoff comes from a numerically accepted SCIP
incumbent after a numerical original-model residual check. Its objective
is evaluated exactly for the imported binary coefficients and incumbent
coordinates, rounded upward, and given a safety margin. Exact objective
evaluation does not prove exact original-model feasibility. The
conditional validity of cutoff-derived reductions still
depends on that premise. Native presolve, propagation, branching, and
optimality claims are not replayed by an independent checker. Returned
solutions receive the declared numerical original-model validation in
the experiment, and this is reported as numerical validation.

\subsection{Cost and failure accounting}

The run deadline includes model construction and optimization. Once the
initial admission checks pass, the sidecar timer charges box inspection,
construction, witness checks, LP solves, validation, and application time.
The earlier budget, depth, and node checks are outside that timer;
their cost remains in complete process elapsed time. Expensive setup can overrun
the nominal sidecar allowance before the next admission check. An LP
time limit is likewise subject to the numerical solver's return behavior.
LP construction checks its deadline every 64 generated rows, and cached
witness screening also checks the callback deadline.
The implementation records these costs; it does not claim the exact
ledger bound in Proposition~\ref{prop:ledger} with a hard whole-callback
value of $\delta$.

The experiment measures complete worker process elapsed time separately,
including startup, parsing, validation, and output. Exceptions, unsupported
models, failed LP proposals, missing incumbents, and time-limit overshoot
remain visible. A failure to produce a bound has an unresolved status;
it is not a certificate of no benefit or infeasibility.
