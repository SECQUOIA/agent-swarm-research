The mathematical contribution remains substantial and appropriate for MPC. The exact scalar lnts characterization, dtoc5 completed-square identity, camshape envelope, hvycrash identity, rigorous finite certificates and existence-based audit provide useful reference results. The paper distinguishes continuous closures from mixed-integer closures and discretization variants from families. It credits earlier floating-point closures and limits its originality claims to a dated, bounded search. These results support the submission without a claim to a general solver or to superior performance.

I found the revised central arguments convincing on their stated assumptions. Appendix B, pp. 53–56, handles the extended-value conventions and telescoping explicitly. The moved finite-potential proof, S p. 68, retains the cap for unreachable cells. The Krawczyk proof, S p. 69, derives nonsingularity from finite positive box widths before concluding exact feasibility. The pindyck uniqueness proof, S p. 39, does not infer uniqueness on a nonconvex price set merely from strong concavity: it proves that every maximizer is interior and then uses stationarity on the enclosing convex polytope. The KAN discussion, pp. 28–29 and S pp. 64–67, distinguishes exact infeasibility of the stored model from enclosure of its relaxations and retains the guarded-path and unchecked-NaN qualifications. The eg theorem, p. 27 and S pp. 102–103, states the execution and trust assumptions rather than treating stored logs as an unconditional formal proof. I do not claim to have independently certified every long computation.

One comparison still exceeds the evidence for model correspondence. The abstract, p. 1, calls all 79 outputs “same-model”; Figure 1, p. 13, makes the same assertion for its filled triangles. But Table 8, p. 51, proves exact GAMS–OSIL identity for only 17 of the 43 instances, proves the catmix coefficient difference for four, and reports sample evaluations for the other 22. These include non-KAN families such as lnts, chain, pricing050 and etamac. P. 38 acknowledges this distinction, and S p. 104 describes it accurately, but a later caveat does not make sample agreement an exact identity proof. The issue is correspondence between the two text formats, separate from the disclosed binary64 reading of solver data. The comparison of reported numbers is valid; interpreting every one as a comparison on the identical mathematical model needs an explicit assumption for the sample-only cases. A wording correction is sufficient. Exact symbolic comparison of all remaining models would also settle it, but I do not require that additional work.

The writing is generally plain and precise. I found no pervasive generic AI-style phrasing. Terms such as “significant” occur in their numerical meaning; cited titles account for the unrelated style-scan hits. The remaining weakness is density: the abstract and C3 carry many results and conditions, and the introduction spends considerable space motivating the data semantics. Those sentences convey necessary distinctions rather than generic promotion. Section 9's broad explanations of termwise relaxations should continue to be read under its opening untested-interpretation caveat. There is no need for a separate experiment to support a claim the paper expressly does not make.

The table repairs are complete. I viewed Table 1 and Figure 1 on pp. 10 and 13, rotated Table 2 on p. 12, Tables S33 and S37 on S pp. 108 and 119, and both register pages, S pp. 120–121, at readable resolution. I found no cropped rows, overlapping cells or missing captions. Figure 1 distinguishes exact optima, tiny gaps, KAN models and exceptional solver outputs; Figure S4, S p. 109, remains a useful explanation of accumulated tolerance effects. Both can stay in their current locations. The register does retain a small time/tier ambiguity: its catmix row gives 20–46 minutes for the displayed-bound implementation and 17–77 minutes for the other implementation, yet assigns only Tier 2. P. 41 defines Tier 3 as an hour or longer without saying that a tier selects one implementation. This calls for the clarification in correction 3. The roughly 15-minute QPLIB figure on S p. 121 is a multi-instance batch, as `RUNS.md` and the S p. 20 box explain; it is not by itself evidence of a per-instance Tier 1 error.

I accept the remaining length. The relevant comparison is 42.85 to 41.07 main-text pages and 124.91 to 120.11 supplement-body pages, with another reduction of approximately 1.36 pages in Appendix B. The targets of about 38 and 105 pages were not reached. Nevertheless, my round-2 request explicitly allowed an account of indispensable material instead of enforcing a mechanical page limit, and the authors supplied it. The new structure is materially clearer: the main text gives the models, statements and proof ideas; the supplement gives derivations and arithmetic details; the artifact holds historical and operational records.

The page account is persuasive in its main allocations. About 14 pages of the main text explain the varied certificate constructions, about three explain exact points, and about six to seven cover the audit and traced/current solver claims. The opening semantics and protocol require space because decimal, binary64 and tolerance interpretations differ in ways that change the conclusions. In the supplement, S1 and S5 together occupy about 74 text-area pages; reductions, global-domain arguments, rounding constants, finite coverage and exact feasibility are the substance of the certificates, not expendable implementation narration. S2–S4 and S6 document point existence, bounded priority searches, the population audit and the SCIP mechanism. The register and reproducible setup also warrant their approximately seven pages. A 120-page supplement is long, but much of it is reference material read by family rather than a second continuous exposition.

The word “indispensable” in the editors' account is sometimes too strong. The chain completeness theorem on S pp. 23–24, the powerflow spectral explanation on S p. 42, the unused split results on S pp. 67–68, some related-work discussion on p. 39, and the per-run campaign table on S p. 106 could be relocated or shortened. The authors themselves identify several such options. They are useful explanatory material, however, and do not obstruct the central argument now that their role is clear. Moving enough further mathematics to force 105 pages would be a substantive reorganization, with limited benefit for correctness or reproducibility. I therefore do not require those cuts or splitting the submission. This is an acceptance of a long reference-oriented paper, not a claim that every retained line is essential.
