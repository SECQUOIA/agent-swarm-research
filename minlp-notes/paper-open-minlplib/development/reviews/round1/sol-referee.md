Referee report for Mathematical Programming Computation

Submission: “Rigorous certificates for open MINLPLib instances and an audit of listed dual bounds”

**Recommendation: major revision.**

I read the main PDF in full, including its references and appendices, skimmed all parts of the supplement, and examined the artifact README and its two verification entry points. Page references below use the printed PDF page numbers; supplement references carry an S prefix. The main PDF supplied for review has 62 pages, including approximately 46 pages of main text, references, and two appendices; the supplement has 138 pages. I also rendered selected tables and figures to inspect the actual page layout. This report concerns the supplied submission, rather than earlier internal reviews.

The work has substantial value for MPC. Its central mathematical approach is credible, several certificates are particularly instructive, and the distinction between an invalid dual bound and a tolerance-feasible point is handled unusually carefully. The current submission nevertheless needs significant revision. The artifact index is stale, descriptions of stored certificates conflict with the reproduction guide, the principal results table is cropped in the PDF, and the presentation combines too many overlapping summaries. The solver comparison also needs a clearer separation of comparable models and of numerical output from mathematical certificates.

The contribution is an extensive collection of certificates for selected nonconvex benchmark models. Decimal strings in the stored OSIL files are interpreted as rational data, and feasibility is required exactly. The authors provide dual bounds and exactly feasible points for 31 instances, with relative gaps at most approximately 3.1e-9, and characterize attained exact optima for nine. They improve bounds for five pump-scheduling instances and an ANN model. They prove six stored KAN formulations exactly infeasible and tightly enclose the optima of related formulations obtained by deleting inconsistent partition-of-unity rows. Separately, an audit screens 11,086 listed per-solver bounds and conditionally refutes 22 bounds on 18 instances. The paper also identifies unattainable reported values, documents a reproducible SCIP propagation defect, and reports a limited campaign with three current solvers.

The strongest scientific message is that a useful benchmark reference needs both a sound lower bound and a proved feasible point, with the model semantics specified. This matters even when floating-point computations appear extremely accurate. The exact camshape envelope, the lnts support argument, the completed-square dtoc5 identity, and the hvycrash identity are excellent examples. The other families show how useful classical techniques become when their application to a particular formulation is completed with rigorous arithmetic, domain coverage, and feasible-point constructions. The audit is potentially influential because it supplies witnesses that resolve some numerical conflicts, rather than merely comparing solver outputs.

The impact should be assessed at the family level as well as the instance level. Of the 31 closures, 28 concern continuous nonlinear programs and three concern mixed-integer models, namely the eg instances. Several instance counts arise from different discretization sizes in one family; Section 9 counts 14 closed families. MINLPLib includes continuous problems, so this scope is appropriate, but the opening should make it explicit. The work offers reference values, diagnostic examples, and certificate constructions that could help global optimization developers and benchmark maintainers. It does not establish a general MINLP algorithm, a solve rate, or a performance advantage over general-purpose solvers; the authors usually acknowledge these limits correctly.

The originality claim is largely well calibrated. The paper credits Lagrangian decomposition, verification functions, interval existence tests, safe LP/SDP bounds, Taylor models, and hidden convexity. The contribution lies in the specific constructions, their completion for the stored formulations, and the benchmark findings. This is sufficient potential originality for a computational journal. The direct antecedents cited are appropriate: [Halbig et al.](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/ijoc.2022.0099) give an algorithm for computing certificates for convex MINLP, while [Kearfott's feasible-point verification work](https://interval.louisiana.edu/preprints/constrai.pdf) addresses rigorous existence near approximate solutions of equality-constrained problems. The submission's nonconvex instance constructions and systematic bound audit extend that setting. The bounded literature search is documented conscientiously. I would retain its caveats about unread sources, particularly GLOPEQ and earlier water-network decomposition work. I would also replace the repeated assertion that every mechanism is classical with a concise account of which instance-specific step was necessary and had not previously been supplied.

At the level of the main proofs, I found no counterexample to a central certificate. Telescoping splits, weak duality, the camshape comparison argument, the dtoc5 identity, and the distinction between feasible-point existence and small residuals are logically convincing. The proof of the generalized Krawczyk test usefully explains why nonsingularity follows from the box inclusion. The supplement supplies substantial derivations, including the eg error analysis and auditor conditions. My supplement reading was a skim, not a line-by-line verification of every long computation or checking program, and the short numerical replay described below does not validate the complete collection.

The main correctness concern is the relationship between a theorem, its stored evidence, and the computation required to reproduce it. Section 2 defines “stored” as a finite certificate checked by replay. Yet catmix is labelled stored although its per-stage chord values were not saved and checking reruns the dynamic program. The emfl certificate box likewise says “hand + stored” while immediately acknowledging that the dual vectors are regenerated from a cone solver. The main text says the archived topopt certificate replays exactly, whereas Section S4.3 says its basis was not stored and its certificate is regenerated. These are substantive inconsistencies in a paper whose contribution includes checkable certificates. A rigorous rerun can still prove a bound, but it has different dependencies and reproducibility properties from replaying a preserved certificate.

A smaller logical problem occurs in Table S42. Its title promises values that no exactly feasible point attains, but its last row concerns an infeasible floating-point optcdeg2 candidate with objective above a proved primal upper bound. A value above U is not thereby unattainable at some other feasible point. Nor does that comparison alone show that constraint tolerance caused the upward objective error. The text acknowledges that this row differs from the others, but the title and qualifier still overstate its conclusion. It should be moved or described only as evidence about an infeasible numerical candidate.

The model-semantics discussion is essential and generally good. Exact OSIL decimals, exact GAMS decimals, and rounded binary64 data can define different problems. The paper makes the especially striking consequences visible in the KAN and pump examples. It also correctly avoids attributing historical listed-bound conflicts to particular solver defects. The numerical scale of an audit margin does not itself prove transfer to the binary64 model; Appendix A.4 properly treats the one-implementation transfer experiment as evidence. The display hypothesis is expressly conditional, and the distinction between invalid displayed values and invalid underlying reported values is justified. The modest absolute margins in some rows should remain visible beside the large relative examples. MINLPLib's [documentation](https://www.minlplib.org/doc.html) indeed describes a tolerance-based solved status; the submission should continue to treat its exact standard as an additional standard rather than imply that every tolerance-level result violates the library's rules.

The solver campaign is useful as a dated diagnostic experiment. Its limitations are stated, including overloaded runs, differing time measures, capability failures, missing-bound warnings, and SCIP's tightened argument bounds. However, the headline “all 109 finite final dual bounds are weaker” mixes 91 comparisons to original-model certificates with 18 comparisons to KAN relaxations. It also includes six BARON outputs with no globality guarantee and bounds for tightened SCIP models. Figure 1 visually places these observations on a common scale. Qualifying a heterogeneous comparison in a long caption is insufficient for a central claim. The numerical comparison is informative, but the same-model mathematical comparison needs its own count and presentation. Similarly, “no run closed an instance under exact feasibility” should identify what was required of the run's own returned point and bound. BARON's two camshape dual bounds are proved valid and would meet the paper's closure threshold when paired with the authors' exactly feasible points; their returned incumbents are infeasible. Reporting both facts would make the practical meaning clearer.

Claims about external runs need their conditions at the point of statement. Proposition 8.7 does this well for MINOTAUR's unavailable .nl input. Proposition 8.6 states its conclusion before introducing assumptions about CAMINO's input files and the meaning of its recorded field. The KAN published-value comparison similarly depends on correspondence with the published formulation. Those conditions belong in the proposition or immediately preceding sentence. The traced SCIP defect is much better supported: exact witnesses, solver acceptance, multiple interfaces and releases, a small reproducer, and instrumented propagation observations provide a persuasive diagnosis. The distinction between the 15 traced runs and untraced wrong runs should be retained.

The contribution is clear on the first two pages. The opening states the numerical outcomes, explains the semantic choice, and provides a concrete camshape example. An expert can follow the main argument without first reading the supplement. The introduction then becomes too long: nine contributions, three lessons, a broad literature survey, and repeated qualifications occupy much of the first six pages. Results and verification machinery delay the first certificate until page 16. The paper would be easier to read if the opening grouped its contribution into certificates, benchmark audit, and solver diagnostics, with the principal counts in one compact summary.

The main text supports understanding of the certificate ideas, but complete verification still requires the supplement and artifact. This division is acceptable if the main paper consistently explains the reduction, the validity argument, and the location of the exact data. The short full proofs of dtoc5 and hvycrash are useful. The long path framework and its six-page appendix concern mostly classical validity results and can be shortened substantially. The costate interpretation after Proposition 4.3 needs the equality-constraint caveat now left to Appendix B.4. An expert should not have to discover there that the proposition's interior-point hypothesis excludes bags containing dynamics equalities.

The writing is mostly precise, concrete, and restrained. The authors distinguish proofs from numerical evidence, acknowledge unread literature, and give useful examples. I did not find grounds to infer authorship from prose, and I would not describe this manuscript as dominated by generic AI-style writing. Its recurring weaknesses are formulaic repetition, categorical slogans, and excessive qualification inside long sentences. Examples include “These records are nevertheless used as facts” on page 2, which is broader than the cited tolerance-based uses establish; “Every printed number remains true as printed” on page 9, which would be clearer as a statement about certified displays; and “Numerical evidence ... never supports a claim” on page 10, which should distinguish mathematical certification from empirical observations. The abstract sentence beginning “The certificates combine classical devices” packs methods, arithmetic, independence, and exceptions into one sentence. The repeated phrases “within the search of Section S3,” “no instance is closed,” “cause unknown,” and “under reading (b)” often repeat qualifications already established nearby. They should be consolidated without dropping conditions needed by individual claims.

Table 2 has a serious layout defect in the supplied PDF. On page 13, the beginning of the caption lies beyond the page boundary, and the last three eg rows are clipped. The PDF text extraction also loses those rows. This prevents the central numerical summary from being read in full. Table S42 on supplement page 116 likewise extends beyond the page boundary: part of the caption and the final row are cropped. These are publication blockers and should be fixed in the table generator. Tables 1 and 7 fit but demand extensive rotated reading, and Table 1's caption is effectively a glossary. Table 6 is informative and comparatively readable, although its caption's opening sentence is awkward. Tables 3 and 4 communicate the remaining bounds well.

Figure 1 successfully conveys the enormous range of improvements, but needs separate presentation of the infeasible stored KAN models and their relaxations. Its zero-gap column should look explicitly separate from the logarithmic axis. Listing every exception in the caption and legend makes the plot difficult to absorb. Figure S4, showing the relation between camshape violation and objective deficit, is one of the most useful explanatory figures; a compact version belongs in the main text. It demonstrates a mechanism quantitatively, whereas much of Section 9 offers an interpretation that was not tested. The latter section should be shortened or renamed to describe what the certificates exploit, with its causal conjectures stated once.

The artifact is ambitious and unusually transparent about its limitations. The README separates integrity checks, short replay, long runs, and regeneration; it states the arithmetic assumptions, preserves model hashes, and documents portability edits. It acknowledges missing intermediate data and version sensitivity. These are strengths. The current packaging remains a working-tree index rather than a finished journal artifact: paths reach into a large research tree, one register checker needs an absolute-path edit, the supplement uses a shared home-directory cache, and the README contains instructions to “paper-editing agents” and an obsolete claim that the supplement has not integrated the index. Section S7.1 has in fact integrated the index. The archive DOI and license remain placeholders. The README's approximately 1.2-hour ANN verification entry also differs from the main text's approximately 2.0 CPU-hours; if these are different measurements or runs, they should be identified.

The integrity check reported **FAIL: 256 errors; 66 claims; 1841 files checked**. All errors reported SHA-256 mismatches; they involved eight distinct files: data/check.log, data/make_tables.py, data/numbers.json, sections/08-solvers.tex, sections/I-reproduction.tex, tables/tab-claims-campaign.tex, tables/tab-claims.tex, and tables/tab-solvers.tex, all under paper-open-minlplib. This is evidence of a stale index, not a mathematical refutation. The short-check runner reported **PASS**: all 15 commands exited successfully, and all five stored-result comparisons passed with the documented exclusions for time and affinity. It replayed lnts, dtoc5, the three KAN r3 searches, the eg auditor tests, and the independent eg review scripts. The scientific scripts ran in /tmp/minlp-artifact-98h1rbc4. The full audited eg suite, KAN r5 searches, water bounds, and the other long computations were not rerun. No project-wide verification or CI inspection was performed. Neither PDF nor any paper source was edited.

The split between main paper and supplement needs revision. Two hundred physical pages can be justified only if each part has a clear function. Here the main paper repeats results, independence qualifications, trust terms, literature caveats, and interpretations across the abstract, introduction, Sections 2–3, certificate boxes, Sections 9–11, and tables. The supplement then repeats many model descriptions and certificate boxes, contains detailed historical corrections, and reproduces references. The family derivations, rigorous error analysis, exact point constructions, and audit proofs deserve preservation. Repeated status descriptions, superseded displays, implementation histories, and long command/path tables can be merged or moved into the artifact documentation. I would aim for roughly 30–35 pages of main exposition before references and necessary appendices, as an editorial target rather than a strict limit. Keep representative constructions and the main audit in the paper; use the supplement for complete mathematical derivations; use the artifact for commands, historical logs, and machine-readable mappings. The paper need not be split into separate submissions if this consolidation is done well.

My recommendation is therefore major revision. The results appear strong enough to merit another review. Acceptance should depend on a fixed, consistent artifact, readable complete numerical tables, a clearer empirical comparison, and a substantially more focused exposition.
