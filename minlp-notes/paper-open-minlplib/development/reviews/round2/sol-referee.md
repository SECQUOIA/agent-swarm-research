## Fresh assessment of the revised paper

### Contribution, significance and originality

The paper is a strong fit for MPC in subject matter. Its contribution is a collection of completed, checkable nonconvex instance certificates and an audit that uses feasible-point existence to settle benchmark conflicts. The 31 closures, nine attained exact optima, improved pump bounds, exact KAN infeasibility results, 22 conditional bound refutations, and traced SCIP failure are useful reference material. The scope is now stated properly: 28 closures concern continuous models, three mixed-integer models, and several instance counts are discretization variants within 14 families. These counts do not establish a general MINLP method or a performance advantage.

The strongest results are the short certificates that expose the model: the scalar lnts support characterization, the completed-square dtoc5 identity, the camshape greatest feasible envelope and the hvycrash constant-objective identity. The audit has a different but substantial value: an exactly feasible witness settles a disagreement that agreement among approximate solver outputs cannot settle. The sizes of the margins are now prominent enough to distinguish large errors from tolerance-scale conflicts. The paper also respects the difference between an individual invalid dual bound and MINLPLib's tolerance-based solved mark.

Originality is mostly well calibrated. The paper credits the relevant Lagrangian, interval, Taylor, safe LP/SDP and feasible-point verification methods, and locates its novelty in the constructions and benchmark findings. Halbig et al.'s convex-MINLP certificate algorithm is a relevant antecedent, rather than a duplicate of the present nonconvex instance work; its stated scope is confirmed by the [authors' publication record and manuscript](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/computing-optimality-certificates-for-convex-mixed-integer-nonlin/). The unread-source and bounded-search caveats should remain. I cannot independently establish a first-in-literature claim for every instance. The new rigorous-solver paragraph requires correction: [IbexOpt's own documentation](https://ibex-team.github.io/ibex-lib/optim.html) describes a rigor mode that proves existence of a point satisfying equalities exactly. Omitting a rigorous baseline can be a stated scope choice, but cannot be justified by implying that these solvers necessarily lack equality-feasibility certification.

### Correctness and evidence

I found no refutation of a central mathematical certificate. The split-bound and value-function arguments, finite-potential construction, lnts support proof, dtoc5 identity and camshape envelope are logically convincing. The main Krawczyk statement now has the compact box its proof uses; I checked the proof on S pp. 71–72, including the width argument for nonsingularity. Exact point definitions are carefully distinguished from stored decimal approximations. The KAN infeasibility explanation accounts for interval endpoints and the scaled-copy bounds, S p. 68, and the revised guarded-path account makes the dual-enclosure evidence easier to assess.

The remaining storage-summary ambiguity is modest but important in this paper. Section 3.2, p. 14, says that for all 14 rerun closures “no search tree or stage data are stored.” The limitations on p. 43 similarly say that rerun certificates do not store their trees or per-leaf data. Neither statement tells the reader that eg retains a finite leaf partition, as p. 10 already explains. A leaf partition need not be a complete search tree, but its retention is materially different from a search whose partition was never retained. Both summaries should make that distinction. This clarification does not undermine the eg bound.

The audit now handles its display assumptions clearly. Section 7 distinguishes invalid underlying reported bounds from unsafe displays, proves validity separately from unsuccessful refutation, and explains the special role of rocket100. The external-data assumptions in the CAMINO and MINOTAUR statements are now at the point of claim. The SCIP diagnosis separates proved witnesses and optimal values from evidence obtained by instrumentation, and does not extend the traced mechanism to every untraced run. These are substantial improvements.

The one-hour comparison is now useful on its declared terms. The 79 comparable outputs and the 30 exceptional outputs are separated, BARON receives appropriate credit, and Figure 1 no longer presents the KAN models as ordinary same-model comparisons. The brief abstract should use the comparable count too. No rigorous-solver benchmark, longer campaign or causal experiment is needed to establish the certificates already proved.

### Clarity, prose, tables and length

The main argument is accessible to a global-optimization reader. The camshape example on p. 2 is concrete, and Sections 4–6 explain reductions and validity rather than merely announce numerical results. The expanded AI protocol supplies information the reader needs, although its placement and the intervening full-page floats make the route to the first certificate long.

The prose is generally plain, precise and cautious. I do not find pervasive generic AI-style language, nor would style alone establish how the paper was written. The weaknesses are repeated summaries, categorical slogans and sentences that carry too many qualifications. In particular:

- P. 2: “every such use inherits the error” does not distinguish a false exact conclusion from an application that deliberately accepts tolerance-feasible reference data.
- P. 10: “Interpretation, like numerical evidence, never supports a claim” is too broad. Numerical evidence supports empirical observations in Section 9; it does not establish rigorous bounds or exact feasibility.
- P. 5: the single list of classical mechanisms and citations is long. A shorter list and one cross-reference would preserve credit.
- Pp. 1 and 4: the methods/verification sentence in the abstract and the CAMINO/MINOTAUR passage in C3 combine method, result, exceptions and provenance. Split or shorten them without discarding the conditions.

The repaired tables are complete. Figure 1 is substantially better: KAN is separate, exact optima occupy a separate region, and extremely small gaps are annotated. Table 1 still has touching/overlapping text between the lnts arithmetic and implementation cells on p. 11; this is visible in the render, not just a text-extraction artifact. Table 2 is now usable but remains dense. Figure S4, S p. 109, explains a mechanism particularly well; keeping it in the supplement is acceptable if length is reduced elsewhere.

The length request is not a mechanical page limit. Fourteen certificate families, exact point constructions and arithmetic proofs warrant extensive supporting material. Nevertheless, a 60-page main PDF and 135-page supplement impose a large reading burden, and the revision removes only five physical pages in total. Approximately ten opening pages precede the results; Section 4.1 and Appendix B spend several pages on classical supporting arguments, including two results that no certificate uses; Sections 9–11 repeat numerical examples and recommendations already developed elsewhere. The supplement still combines derivations with implementation histories, command mappings and superseded displays. The adjudication's own targets, approximately 38 pages of main text and 100 pages of supplement body, have not been met. I would retain the detailed mathematical derivations, but move historical/operational records to the artifact and give repeated statements one home. I do not require splitting the submission into two papers.

### Reproducibility and AI disclosure

The artifact is much stronger than in round 1. The index passes, the short recipe executes successfully from a fresh copy, negative controls are included, arithmetic assumptions are named, and regeneration limits are candid. Missing trees, cone vectors and historical environment details are scientific limitations rather than evidence that the bounds are wrong. I accept the explicit single-machine limitation without requiring a second machine for this revision.

Two practical details remain. The full setup on S p. 119 and in the README populates the user's home-directory cache even though the text promises disposable work; it should use a local input root or an isolated wrapper. S p. 119 also says that prepare_inputs.py exits unsuccessfully because 26 models outside the paper are absent. A paper-only setup should not require fetching those extra models or interpreting an expected failure without a clear continuation rule. Also, the README refers to PDFs of “the version that the index validates,” but neither PDF is a hashed entry in claims.json. Record their hashes in a release manifest and say exactly what the validator covers. The present successful source/evidence integrity check should not be described as identification of the PDF bytes.

The AI-use wording is materially adequate and consistent with the records I inspected. It discloses both implementation paths and agent proof/code reviews, identifies providers without inventing missing model versions, states that sessions shared a filesystem, names common libraries and the shared reader, and acknowledges that the ANN second session read the first code. S p. 66 even records a copied saturated-neuron idiom. The declaration includes manuscript drafting and literature searching and assigns responsibility to the authors. This meets the central disclosure purpose of [MPC's submission guidelines](https://link.springer.com/journal/12532/submission-guidelines), which require substantive LLM use to be documented in an appropriate methods section and retain human accountability.

I found no documented fact that contradicts this wording. It must not be read as evidence of independent human verification: the paper expressly says there was none outside the authors. The pending statement of which proofs and codes the authors themselves read would help assess human oversight, but that author-confirmation placeholder is expected in this review and is not counted as a defect. I do not infer human inspection from author responsibility alone, and I do not require guessed session-level model identifiers. The revised disclosure does not itself certify correctness; the mathematical arguments and executable checks remain the evidence.
