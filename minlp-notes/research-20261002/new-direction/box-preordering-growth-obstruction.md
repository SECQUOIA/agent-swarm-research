# Quadratic growth does not imply a finite box-preordering certificate

Date: 2026-10-02. Status: complete elementary derivation with a
[fresh independent review](../reviews/box-preordering-growth-adversary.md).
This is a limitation of a specified certificate family,
not a lower bound for optimization or for all global optimality
certificates. No priority claim is made.

The [focused prior-art audit](../prior-art/box-quadratic-jet-certificate-prior.md)
places the Horn/SPN separation, local preordering test, and vanishing
multiplier in their established literature. The purpose here is the
explicit conditioned example and its precise certificate-family limit.

There is a rational quadratic on five box variables with a unique
minimizer, interaction treewidth four, and exact coordinate
curvature/growth ratio twelve, whose optimum has **no finite-degree
certificate in the full polynomial preordering of the box slacks**.
The obstruction also survives every finite cover by axis-aligned
sub-boxes. A connected seven-variable variant has an entire optimal
segment and the same obstruction at fixed conditioning and width.

Thus the proposed bound `f(p,kappa) poly(I)` for exact unmultiplied
sparse SOS or PSD-plus-box-product certificates fails even if dense
certificates and arbitrary degree are allowed. Higher degree alone
cannot repair it. A polynomial multiplier that vanishes at the
minimizer does repair this example; Section 6 gives its explicit
degree bound. This distinction matters for the
[unknown-growth certificate question](certificate-next-step.md).

## 1. Specify the certificate cone

For `X=[0,1]^n`, the full box preordering is

\[
 \mathcal T(X)=\left\{
  \sum_{A,B\subseteq[n]}\sigma_{A,B}(x)
       \prod_{i\in A}x_i\prod_{j\in B}(1-x_j):
       \sigma_{A,B}\text{ is a polynomial sum of squares}
                  \right\}.                                      \tag{1}
\]

Coefficients may be arbitrary real numbers; impossibility over this
larger coefficient field also excludes rational certificates. The
sets `A,B` can overlap. There is no degree bound. Repeated powers of a
generator can be absorbed into its SOS multiplier, so square-free
generator products lose nothing.

Every summand is nonnegative on `X`. An identity
`F-f*=T`, `T in mathcal T(X)`, is consequently an independently valid
optimality certificate when a feasible point with value `f*` is
provided. Sparse identities that restrict the variables in the
multipliers form a subclass of (1). The common box products
`x_i(1-x_i)` and all their products are also included in (1).

The quadratic module generated by the individual slacks is smaller:
it allows only the empty product and one slack per term. In particular,
`x_1 x_2` belongs to (1), but not to that quadratic module. The local
argument below proves this distinction directly. We use the full
preordering throughout the negative result.

## 2. A local characterization at the origin

Call a symmetric matrix **SPN** if it is the sum of a positive
semidefinite matrix and an entrywise nonnegative symmetric matrix.

**Lemma.** For a homogeneous quadratic `q(x)=x^T Q x`,

\[
               q\in\mathcal T([0,1]^n)
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Q\text{ is SPN}.              \tag{2}
\]

**Proof of necessity.** Suppose (1) equals `q`. At the origin, all
upper slacks equal one, while every lower slack vanishes. The constant
term is therefore

\[
                  0=q(0)=\sum_B\sigma_{\varnothing,B}(0).
\]

Every summand is nonnegative. Each of these SOS multipliers vanishes
at zero, so every polynomial squared in it vanishes there. Its first
nonzero homogeneous part has degree at least two, and its quadratic
part is a sum of squares of linear forms. Multiplication by upper
slacks preserves that quadratic part.

The degree-one part of the full identity is now

\[
                   \sum_{i,B}\sigma_{\{i\},B}(0)x_i=0.
\]

All displayed coefficients are nonnegative, so all are zero. Those
SOS multipliers also start at degree at least two. A term with exactly
one lower slack consequently starts at degree at least three.

Terms with exactly two lower slacks contribute to degree two only
their nonnegative multiplier constant times `x_i x_j`, `i != j`.
Terms with three or more lower slacks contribute nothing at degree
two. Thus the quadratic part of (1) is a PSD quadratic plus a
nonnegative combination of cross-products, proving the SPN assertion.

Conversely, a PSD quadratic is a sum of squares of linear forms.
An entrywise nonnegative quadratic is a nonnegative combination of
`x_i^2` and `x_i x_j`; the former are squares and the latter are
products of lower slacks. This proves sufficiency. `□`

With only individual slack generators, the two-lower-slack terms are
absent, and the same proof forces `Q` itself to be PSD. This explains
why `x_1 x_2` cannot be used as an obstruction to the full preordering.

The necessity argument is unchanged on any box
`prod_i[0,u_i]` with every `u_i>0`: the upper slacks have positive
constant terms. It also applies after restricting a purported
certificate to a face and substituting fixed coordinates.

## 3. A rational, uniformly conditioned counterexample

Let `A` be the adjacency matrix of the five-cycle, with indices read
modulo five, and `J` the all-ones matrix. Set

\[
 H=J-2A,\qquad Q=H+\tfrac15 I,\qquad F(x)=x^TQx,
 \qquad x\in[0,1]^5.                                             \tag{3}
\]

Thus `Q_ii=6/5`, its cycle-edge entries are `-1`, and its other
off-diagonal entries are `1`. This is a positive diagonal perturbation
of the standard five-dimensional Horn matrix. The proof below uses
only the displayed coefficients.

For nonnegative `x`,

\[
 x^THx=\left(\sum_i x_i\right)^2
                -4\sum_{i=1}^5x_i x_{i+1}\ge0.                  \tag{4}
\]

Here is an elementary proof of the inequality. Normalize the sum of
the coordinates to one and maximize the edge sum over the simplex.
Choose a maximizer with minimum support. If two positive coordinates
are nonadjacent, hold their sum fixed and put all their mass on the
one with the larger neighbor sum. The edge sum does not decrease,
because no edge joins those two coordinates. This contradicts support
minimality. The support is therefore a clique. A five-cycle has no
triangle, so at most two adjacent coordinates remain, and their
product is at most `1/4`. The zero vector is immediate.

Consequently

\[
 F(x)\ge\tfrac15\|x\|_2^2,\qquad S=\{0\},\qquad f^*=0.           \tag{5}
\]

Equality in the growth ratio occurs at two equal positive adjacent
coordinates with the others zero. The largest valid global growth
constant is exactly `g=1/5`. Every diagonal Hessian entry is `12/5`,
so the coordinate curvature bound `L=12/5` gives

\[
                             \kappa=L/g=12.                      \tag{6}
\]

All off-diagonal entries are nonzero. The interaction graph is `K_5`,
with treewidth four and bag size five. The eigenvalues of `Q` are
`6/5` and `6/5 +/- sqrt(5)`, the latter each twice. Thus the objective
is nonconvex. Even the full-Hessian conditioning remains bounded:

\[
       \|\nabla^2F\|_2/g=12+10\sqrt5<35.                          \tag{7}
\]

To prove `Q` is not SPN, use the rational matrix

\[
                         W=\tfrac{13}{8}I+A.                    \tag{8}
\]

It is entrywise nonnegative. Its leading principal determinants are

\[
 \frac{13}{8},\quad\frac{105}{64},\quad\frac{533}{512},\quad
 \frac{209}{4096},\quad\frac{29}{32768},
\]

so it is positive definite. Equivalently, its smallest eigenvalue is
`9/8-sqrt(5)/2>0`. But the trace inner product satisfies

\[
 \langle Q,W\rangle
       =5\cdot\frac65\cdot\frac{13}{8}-10=-\frac14.              \tag{9}
\]

If `Q=P+N` with `P` PSD and `N` entrywise nonnegative, both
`<P,W>` and `<N,W>` would be nonnegative, a contradiction. Lemma (2)
now proves that `F` has no certificate (1), at any finite degree.

This is already enough to refute an exact-degree or exact-size bound
depending on width, growth conditioning, and input length for this
certificate family: the certificate does not exist on one fixed
rational instance.

There is also a connected family of arbitrary dimension. For `m`
five-variable blocks, let

\[
 F_m(x)=\sum_{b=1}^m (x^{(b)})^TQx^{(b)}
       +\frac1{100}\sum_{b=1}^{m-1}
                    (x^{(b)}_1-x^{(b+1)}_1)^2.                 \tag{10}
\]

The graph consists of five-cliques joined by bridges, so its treewidth
is four. The growth constant is still exactly `1/5`: the lower bound
follows termwise, and equality holds when every block is the same
equal adjacent-pair vector, including coordinate one. The largest
diagonal Hessian entry is at most `2(6/5+2/100)=61/25`, hence
`kappa<=61/5`, uniformly in `m`. All coefficients are fixed rationals.

Restrict a purported preordering identity to one block, fixing the
others to zero. Its quadratic matrix becomes `Q+(d/100)e_1e_1^T`,
where `d<=2` is the block's degree in the chain. But its trace product
with `W` is at most

\[
                   -\frac14+\frac2{100}\frac{13}{8}
                             =-\frac{87}{400}<0.                \tag{11}
\]

The restriction therefore cannot be SPN, and the original identity
cannot exist. Nonconvexity persists: the direction
`(1,1,-1,-1,0)` in one block has quadratic value at most
`-16/5+2/100<0`. This connects the fixed-instance obstruction to a
growing sparse family without asserting that the family is difficult
to optimize or certify by other means.

## 4. Finite rectangular subdivision does not repair the obstruction

Suppose finitely many closed axis-aligned sub-boxes cover `[0,1]^5`,
and every cell is assigned an exact full-preordering proof of
`F>=0` using that cell's slacks. Apply the cover to the sequence
`(1/k,...,1/k)` as `k` tends to infinity. One cell contains infinitely
many of these points. Closedness puts the origin in that cell, and
one positive diagonal point ensures that all its upper bounds are
strictly positive. Since it is a sub-box of `[0,1]^5`, all its lower
bounds are zero.

The local necessity proof applies unchanged on this cell and again
forces `Q` to be SPN. This contradiction allows degenerate cells
elsewhere in the cover. Arbitrarily small cells or arbitrarily high
orders do not help if the cover is finite and every cell uses the
specified exact certificate.

This statement does not cover arbitrary polyhedral partitions,
certificates with extra generators, a special treatment of a
neighborhood of the origin, or certificates for `F+epsilon`.

## 5. A connected example with a nonisolated optimal set

The obstruction is not restricted to isolated minima. On `[0,1]^7`,
write the last two variables as `y,z` and define

\[
             \widetilde F(x,y,z)=F(x)+(x_1-y+z)^2.                \tag{12}
\]

Its optimal set is the segment
`S_tilde={(0,t,t):0<=t<=1}`. Put `b=y-z` and `r=x_1-b`. Projection
onto the segment gives

\[
 \operatorname{dist}((x,y,z),\widetilde S)^2
        =\|x\|^2+b^2/2
        \le2\|x\|^2+r^2
        \le10\widetilde F(x,y,z).                               \tag{13}
\]

The middle inequality follows from `b=x_1-r`. The largest diagonal
Hessian entry is `22/5`, so `g=1/10` is valid and `kappa<=44`.
The graph is a five-clique with a triangle attached at `x_1`.
It is connected and has treewidth four.

If (12) had a full box-preordering identity, substitute `y=x_1,z=0`.
An SOS stays SOS. Each box slack becomes zero, one, or an original
box slack, and repeated powers can be absorbed into the multipliers.
The result would be a preordering identity for `F`, contradicting
Section 3. Thus this connected example also admits no such identity.

Its Hessian remains indefinite: on directions with `y=x_1,z=0`, the
added square vanishes and the quadratic form equals that of `Q`.
The example directly permits a continuum of optimizers, without any
claim that nonisolatedness causes the obstruction.

## 6. A vanishing multiplier gives a short certificate

The preceding result must not be read as an obstruction to every
algebraic certificate. Put `s=sum_i x_i`. For any homogeneous rational
quadratic `q=x^TQx` and integer `d>=2`, the coefficient of `x^a`,
`|a|=d`, in `s^(d-2)q` is

\[
 \frac{\binom d a}{d(d-1)}
          \left(a^TQa-\sum_i Q_{ii}a_i\right).                  \tag{14}
\]

For the concrete matrix (3), (5) implies

\[
 a^TQa-\tfrac65d
       \ge\tfrac15\|a\|^2-\tfrac65d
       \ge\frac{d(d-30)}{25}.                                  \tag{15}
\]

Taking `d=30` proves that `s^28 F` has nonnegative monomial
coefficients. This is a full-preordering identity for the multiplied
polynomial: split every monomial's exponents into even parts and
zero-one parts. Since `s>0` on the nonnegative orthant except at the
origin and `F(0)=0`, the identity independently proves `F>=0`.
The multiplier vanishes at the very point where the unmultiplied
local-jet argument applies. A multiplier strictly positive at the
origin would not escape it: its quadratic jet would be a positive
multiple of `Q`.

More generally, suppose a homogeneous quadratic in `p` variables
satisfies `q(x)>=g||x||^2` on the nonnegative orthant, and let
`D=max_i Q_ii`. Then `D>=g>0`. Equation (14) and Cauchy--Schwarz give

\[
 a^TQa-\sum_iQ_{ii}a_i\ge gd^2/p-Dd\ge0
                    \quad\text{when }d\ge pD/g.                 \tag{16}
\]

Thus `d=max(2,ceil(pD/g))` suffices. A growth promise on `[0,1]^p`
implies the same orthant inequality by homogeneity. In terms of
coordinate curvature `L=2D`, this is `d=O(p kappa)`.
There are `binom(p+d-1,d)` coefficients. Exact rational coefficient
checking gives an independently valid certificate of size
`f(p,kappa) poly(I)` for this **single homogeneous corner problem**.
The verifier does not need to trust `g`; a search can try successive
degrees and stop when every coefficient is nonnegative. This is a
quantitative elementary Pólya-style argument, not a claim of a new
positivity principle.

For (3), the degree-30 expansion has at most `binom(34,4)=46,376`
monomials, a fixed constant. Applying the same multiplier globally
with `p=n` does not establish the sought sparse bound: local bag
objectives need not be individually nonnegative or inherit global
growth. Neither this calculation nor the negative result settles
whether a more general sparse multiplier or disjunctive certificate
has an `f(p,kappa) poly(I)` bound.

## 7. Consequences and verification scope

The [scalar Bellman obstruction](scalar-message-growth-obstruction.md)
still has a tiny sum-of-squares plus box-product identity. The present
example separates that observation from a general completeness claim
for the same algebraic family. Uniform quadratic growth controls
objective increase on feasible directions; it does not force the
corner quadratic into the SPN cone.

The unique-minimizer example has zero gradient at its corner, so all
active bound multipliers in its KKT system are zero. A finite SOS
convergence theorem requiring strict complementarity does not apply.
Nor does this contradict an exact algorithm that first obtains a
strict approximate lower bound and uses rational objective-value
separation: such an algorithm need not exhibit an attained exact SOS
identity. No general certificate lower bound or complexity hardness
claim follows from this note.

```
python3 -B research-20261002/new-direction/check_box_preordering_growth.py
```

passed five exact rational principal-minor checks and the negative
separator identity, eight connected block chains and their 36
single-block restrictions, 1,024 sampled exact growth identities, 9,216
connected-flat growth cases, all 46,376 degree-30 multiplier
coefficients, and 1,281 independently expanded small-degree
coefficient identities. The smallest integral sign numerator among
the degree-30 coefficients was 158. These checks support the
displayed finite algebra; they do not replace the all-degree
local-jet proof or the continuous-domain growth proof.
