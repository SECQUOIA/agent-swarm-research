# Generic stationary systems do not by themselves certify a component solver

Status: scoped independent audit of the polynomial component question in
[the strong-field QP note](strong-field-component-qp.md) and its
[source comparison](../prior-art/strong-field-component-qp-prior.md).
The genericity and finite-grid exceptional-set arguments below pass.
They leave two separate solver obligations: detecting empty systems and
certifying that a returned representation contains every solution.
This note does not assert a polynomial component algorithm.

## 1. What the source supplies

Gimenez and Matera's
[local primary text](../../literature/papers/gimenez2016-on-the-bit-complexity-of/fulltext.md),
pp. 2--4, assumes a reduced regular sequence: the equations form a
regular sequence, and the ideal generated by each prefix is radical.
The associated varieties have the expected dimensions. For a square
system in `k` variables, a successful computation gives a Kronecker
representation of its entire zero-dimensional variety.

For fixed degree, the stated bit bound can have the form
`c_d^k poly(I+b)`. This is the right dependence for an exponentially
decaying component-size tail. However, the source explicitly describes
the algorithm as Monte Carlo on p. 7. Theorem 6.8, pp. 32--33, gives a
success probability, not a deterministic acceptance certificate.
The theorem as stated also does not provide an empty-system branch.

## 2. What generic linear noise actually guarantees

Fix an integer assignment, a continuous face, and the values substituted
for coordinates outside the component. Let `f` be the resulting
polynomial in `k` free coordinates. Write

\[
 P_i(z)=\partial_i f(z),\qquad
 P_i(z)+c_i=0\quad (i=1,\ldots,k).
 \tag{1}
\]

The linear noise coordinates `c_i` are independent parameters. For a
prefix of length `s`, consider the polynomial map
`P^(s): C^k -> C^s`. Outside a proper algebraic subset of its target,
every fiber is either empty or smooth of dimension `k-s`.

Here is the relevant genericity argument. At every point of the critical
locus, the differential has rank below `s`. On each irreducible component
of that locus, the image dimension is at most this rank in characteristic
zero. Thus the closure of the set of critical values is a proper
algebraic subset of `C^s`. If the map is not dominant, its entire image
already has proper algebraic closure. If it is dominant, its image
contains a nonempty Zariski open set. Consequently a generic target has
the asserted empty or smooth fiber. A proper complex algebraic subset
cannot contain all of `R^s`, so its real part has Lebesgue measure zero.

Take the union of these exceptional sets over all prefixes. If the full
fiber is nonempty outside this union, every prefix is nonempty and
smooth of the expected dimension. Its defining ideal is radical by the
Jacobian criterion. The expected prefix codimensions in a polynomial
ring also give the regular-sequence condition. Thus the source's
reduced-regular-sequence premise holds in this nonempty case. The final
fiber is finite and smooth.

Emptiness is not a rare failure. For example, if
`f(z_1,z_2)=z_1^2`, the stationary equations are
`2z_1+c_1=0` and `c_2=0`. Almost every parameter makes the system empty.
Sending every empty face to an expensive fallback would therefore not
give a rare-fallback argument. One needs an efficient, correct emptiness
branch or a different component construction.

## 3. A uniform finite-grid bound for the singular-prefix event

The preceding exceptional event can be bounded without constructing a
discriminant polynomial. A prefix fiber is singular precisely when
there exist `z in C^k` and `lambda in C^s` such that

\[
 \begin{split}
 P^{(s)}(z)+c_{1:s}&=0,\\
 DP^{(s)}(z)^T\lambda&=0,\\
 \sum_{i=1}^s|\lambda_i|^2&=1.
 \end{split}
 \tag{2}
\]

Splitting real and imaginary parts gives one existential block of at
most `4k` real variables. It uses `O(k)` real polynomial equations of
degree at most `max(d-1,2)`, where `d` bounds the objective degree.
Normalization of a nonzero complex left-kernel vector justifies the
last equation; no positivity condition on complex coordinates is used.

Fix every noise coordinate except one. The fixed-block quantifier
elimination bound recorded in the
[section-count audit](polynomial-growth-section-audit.md) gives a uniform
bound

\[
 C\leq \exp(\operatorname{poly}_d(I))
 \tag{3}
\]

on the number of interval or point components in that one-coordinate
section. The bound depends on the number and degrees of polynomials,
not on the fixed noise values or their bit lengths. It remains valid
when output polynomials vanish identically. In particular, some
exceptional sections may be whole intervals; a conditional claim that
every section is a finite set would be false.

The singular-prefix event has probability zero under independent
continuous uniform noise. Hybridizing its coordinates one at a time to
an `M`-point uniform grid, as in the
[finite-noise tail note](polynomial-finite-noise-tails.md), therefore
gives

\[
 \Pr\{\text{this prefix is singular}\}\leq 2nC/M.
 \tag{4}
\]

This argument also covers a nondominant prefix map: every point in a
nonempty fiber is then critical, and generic parameters make the fiber
empty. Equation (4) bounds exceptional nonempty fibers in that case;
it does not supply a way to certify emptiness on the usual inputs.

For the product-box component construction, it is enough to union over
the original integer assignments and all lower/free/upper continuous
face states, with at most `n` prefixes per state. If `N_i` is the number
of native integer labels and `n_c` is the continuous dimension, the
number is bounded by

\[
 T\leq n\,3^{n_c}\prod_{i\text{ integer}}N_i.
 \tag{5}
\]

This also covers components selected after observing the noise. A
coordinate pinned by the strong-field rule is at an original endpoint.
There are no factors joining distinct surviving components, so other
unfixed components can be assigned arbitrary original endpoints when
forming the restriction relevant to a given component. The family of
restrictions in (5) is fixed before the noise is drawn. There is no need
to condition on the selected component or on its pinning event.

Because integer bounds are binary encoded, `log T=poly(I)`. Thus the
union bound is `2nTC/M`, with `log(TC)=poly_d(I)`. If a correct fallback
has an independent base bound `B=exp(poly_d(I))`, choosing
`M >= 2nTCB/rho` makes its expected contribution at most `rho` while
using polynomially many noise bits. This statement budgets only a
detectable input exception. It does not turn an unverified randomized
solver into a correct branch.

## 4. Root soundness is not root completeness

Substituting a returned representation into (1) and checking that its
univariate polynomial is squarefree verifies that the listed points are
solutions. It does not prove that no solutions were omitted. Even the
equation `z^2-1=0` admits the incomplete but squarefree representation
`T+1=0`, which passes substitution.

A component optimizer needs completeness, since an omitted stationary
point may be the minimizing point on a face. An adequate acceptance test
could establish equality with the original ideal, certify the actual
quotient dimension together with that many distinct represented roots,
or supply another exhaustive representation certificate. Merely knowing
the Bezout upper bound does not determine the actual affine root count.
Such a test must itself have `c_d^k poly(I+b)` bit work if it is run on
every ordinary component. A `2^poly(k)` verification step would leave the
original expected-work problem unresolved.

Repeated Monte Carlo runs only reduce the probability of an undetected
error. They do not give an all-draw exact algorithm. A rare-fallback
construction needs deterministic acceptance and a correct fallback on
every rejected input or internal random choice.

## 5. Separate deterministic route

The symbolic deformation in the
[exact fallback construction](polynomial-exact-fallback-construction.md)
gives a known finite quotient on every face, including degenerate and
empty original stationary systems. Its existing Cartesian product of
coordinate roots has a dimension-squared exponent, so that version
does not resolve the component bound. A proposed improvement uses
characteristic polynomials of separating linear forms to preserve the
coordinates in a single univariate representation.

The algebraic audit of that proposed improvement found no obstruction
for a good separating form. Bad forms require explicit rejection when
normalization leaves poles or the proposed common divisor fails to
divide a coordinate numerator. The quotient reductions must also be
memoized over all monomials of bounded total degree; the loose
`s^{O(k)}` reduction-tree estimate is not a constant-base bound.
This is a separate deterministic construction, not a consequence of the
probabilistic source theorem reviewed above.
