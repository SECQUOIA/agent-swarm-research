## 1. Kernel and transverse growth

After removing fixed coordinates, unit-box normalization leaves a
full-dimensional box. Convexity gives a positive semidefinite Hessian
on its interior and, by continuity, on its boundary. The affine Hessian
identity `H(x)+H(1-x)=2H(c)` is exact. It proves both the common-kernel
claim and the bound `0<=H(x)<=2H(c)` on the entire box. If the center
Hessian is zero, the restricted polynomial is affine and the separate
endpoint branch is valid.

For the positive-rank case, write `d=x-y`, where `y` is any optimizer,
and let `a=d'H(y)d`, `b=d'H(x)d`. Exact cubic Taylor expansion gives

\[
 f(x)-f(y)=\nabla f(y)^Td+(2a+b)/6.
\]

The linear term is nonnegative by constrained first-order optimality,
including when `y` is on the boundary. Both quadratic forms are
nonnegative. Full symmetry of the constant third derivative proves
the displayed center identity; Hessian affinity alone would not
justify that step for an arbitrary affine matrix function.

The constants in the subsequent estimates check. Positive
semidefiniteness and `||H(x)||<=2M` give

\[
 d^TH(c)d\le a+b+2\sqrt{nM(a+b)}.
\]

The bound `a+b<=4Mn` allows the first term to be absorbed into another
`2 sqrt(nM(a+b))`. Squaring and using `a+b<=6E` yields

\[
 [d^TH(c)d]^2\le96MnE.
\]

This proves the stated fourth-power projected-gap bound without a
positive lower bound on the Hessian at the optimizer. No distance
from that optimizer to the boundary is required.

## 2. The optimizer set and the missing linear residual

Subtracting the center gradient's affine function leaves a polynomial
whose gradient lies in the orthogonal complement of the common kernel.
It is constant along every feasible segment in a kernel direction.
The projected-gap bound puts every difference of two optimizers in
that kernel. Their equal objective values impose the additional center
gradient equality. Conversely, those two equalities make the objectives
equal along the feasible segment. This proves the claimed affine-slice
description of the entire optimizer set.

The right-hand side of this affine slice can be irrational. The proof
never computes it or an optimizer. The known coefficient matrix is
rational. Its gradient row is necessary: a nonconstant affine component
along the Hessian kernel can otherwise move a point away from optimality
while its transverse projection stays zero.

The bound on the gradient of the subtracted polynomial gives
`|g_c'd|<=E+2M sqrt(n)||Pd||`. Combining this with
`||H(c)d||<=M||Pd||` controls every equality residual in the slice.
Thus no uncontrolled affine direction remains in the final distance
estimate.

## 3. The Hoffman constant is uniform in the unknown right-hand side

The integer-matrix proof in Section 5 is valid. Project a box point
onto the nonempty affine slice. Its displacement belongs to the sum
of the equality row space and the cone generated by active outward
box normals. Conic elimination modulo that row space permits a
linearly independent collection of the latter normals. The equality
basis is explicitly selected from the original matrix rows, so all
stacked entries keep the stated integer size bound.

For a stack `R` of at most `n` independent rows, `det(RR')` is a
positive integer. Its other singular values are at most `nC`. Hence
the smallest singular value is at least `(nC)^(-(n-1))`. In the
projection inner product the active outward normals contribute
nonpositive terms, because the original point is inside the box.
The remaining equality contribution gives exactly

\[
 \operatorname{dist}(x,Z)
 \le(nC)^{n-1}\|Ax-b\|.
\]

Only the integer coefficient matrix and box normals determine this
constant. The proof does not require rational `b`, full row rank,
an interior slice, or knowledge of the active set. If the displacement
is zero, the inequality is immediate; otherwise the selected stack is
nonempty. This covers the degenerate cases omitted by an argument
that assumed an invertible full equality matrix.

## 4. Every algorithmic constant has polynomial bit length

The denominator product `D` clears the center Hessian and gradient
using only polynomially many rational entries of polynomial length.
For a positive-rank rational positive semidefinite Hessian, some
maximal-order principal minor is positive and at least `D^(-r)`.
The product of its positive eigenvalues is the sum of these minors.
Since each eigenvalue is at most `M`, the proposed
`lambda_0=D^(-n)M^(-(n-1))` is a valid positive rational lower bound.
No numerical eigenvalue computation or optimizer coordinates enter it.

The later affine-branch addition also checks: with the cleared gradient
as the sole equality row, its residual is exactly `D E`. The proved
Hoffman bound gives the stated computable constant without a positive
Hessian eigenvalue; a constant objective permits `Gamma=1`.

The stated `R_0` is deliberately larger than the fourth root actually
needed by the projected-gap bound, and it is at least one. For gaps
at most one, `E<=E^(1/4)`. Combining the equality-residual estimate
with the proved Hoffman bound therefore yields precisely the stated
`Gamma`. Integer powers with exponent at most `n`, denominator
products, row sums and their rational combinations all have polynomial
binary length and can be computed in polynomial time. Their numerical
magnitudes may be exponential; only their logarithms enter the requested
objective accuracy.

Affine box normalization also has polynomial cost and coefficient
length at degree three. The original Euclidean distance grows by at
most the largest positive side width, bounded by the stated rational
`W`. Hence using objective accuracy
`(2^(-q)/(W Gamma))^4` requires only `poly(I)+O(q)` bits. Arbitrarily
thin intervals and large translations do not invalidate this estimate.
Fixed coordinates are handled before it is used.

## 5. The value solver suffices for the point theorem

The actual value interface uses a bounded epigraph with known rational
inner and outer radii. Rational polynomial evaluation and its convex
tangent supply separation on the box. The cited weak-optimization
interface is followed by the explicit homothety and coordinate-clipping
repair, producing an exactly feasible rational point and a certified
objective interval. Strong convexity is unnecessary for this part.

The cubic error bound converts this certificate into distance to the
optimizer set with polynomial additional precision. A directly
checkable tangent lower certificate can be obtained through the stated
extra value precision, also polynomial in the input and requested
accuracy. A separately requested objective gap can be met by taking
the smaller of its tolerance and the distance-driven tolerance.

This proves the base theorem about distance to some optimizer. It does
not identify exact active bounds or expand an algebraic optimizer.
The reviewed cubic active-bound construction is consistent with this
claim, while the point-extraction reductions linked in the draft use
degree four. No complexity separation or literature-priority conclusion
follows from these comparisons.

## 6. Separate approval of the minimum-norm corollary

Read the saved Section 8 after completing the base-proof review.
Its target is the unique minimum-Euclidean-norm point `p` of the
original optimizer set in the original coordinates. This makes a
fixed Cauchy-output claim, rather than permitting the selected optimizer
to change with the requested accuracy.

For the exact minimizer `x_tau` of `f+tau||x||^2`, comparison with `p`
gives both the unregularized gap bound and `||x_tau||<=||p||`. If `s`
is nearest in the original optimizer set, regularized optimality also
gives `E_tau<=2R tau e`, where `e=||s-x_tau||`. The original error
constant applies since `E_tau<=tau R^2<=1`. Therefore

\[
 e^4/\Gamma_B^4\le2R\tau e,
 \qquad e\le(2R\tau\Gamma_B^4)^{1/3}.
\]

The zero-distance case satisfies the same conclusion directly.
Projection optimality of `p` gives `||x_tau-p||^2<=2Re`.
Substituting the stated
`tau=epsilon^6/(1024R^4 Gamma_B^4)` yields
`e<=epsilon^2/(8R)` and `||x_tau-p||<=epsilon/2`.

The regularized objective has strong-convexity modulus `2tau` on
the box. A feasible point with its objective gap at most
`eta=tau epsilon^2/4` is within the other `epsilon/2` of `x_tau`,
including at a boundary optimizer. The triangle inequality proves the
claimed approximation to `p`. Both new rational tolerances have
`poly(I)+O(q)` bits. The original error constant is used throughout;
there is no hidden recomputation with a constant that deteriorates
as `tau` tends to zero.

The penalty correctly uses the original-coordinate norm. A norm of
normalized coordinates could choose a different point and is explicitly
excluded. The affine branch instead computes its minimum-norm box-face
point directly. This establishes the canonical Cauchy corollary while
retaining the limitations on exact labels and expanded algebraic output.
