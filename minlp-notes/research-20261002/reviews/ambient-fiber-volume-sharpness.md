# Sharpness of the ambient-noise fiber-volume estimate

Date: 2026-10-02. Scope: the probability estimate in section 2 of
[ambient cell closure](../new-direction/smoothed-ambient-cell-closure.md).
This is a limitation of that estimate, not a lower bound for exact
optimization or for its cell-closure algorithm.

## 1. A rational orthonormal rank-one example

Let \(n=m^2\), where \(m\ge1\) is an integer, and take

\[
 T=\frac1m(1,\ldots,1),\qquad TT^T=1.
\]

Let the original coefficients \(\gamma_j\) be independent continuous
uniform variables on \([-\sigma,\sigma]\), with \(\sigma>0\). Write

\[
 d=T\gamma=m\bar\gamma,\qquad
 r=\gamma-\bar\gamma\mathbf1.
\]

For fixed feasible residual \(r\), the conditional law of \(d\) is
uniform on the interval

\[
 J(r)=m[-\sigma-\min_j r_j,\ \sigma-\max_j r_j].
\]

Indeed, the change of variables from \(\gamma\) to \((d,r)\) has a
constant Jacobian and the original density is constant on its cube.
Consequently the interval has center and length

\[
 c(r)=-\frac m2(\min_j r_j+\max_j r_j),\qquad
 H(r)=m(2\sigma-\operatorname{range}\gamma).
\]

The center is a continuous piecewise-linear function of the residual;
it is not an independently chosen random shift. For a prescribed length
\(L\ge0\), set \(I(r)=[c(r)-L/2,c(r)+L/2]\). The exact probability is

\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \Pr\{d\in I(r)\}
 =1-\left(1-\frac{L}{2\sigma m}\right)^n,
 \quad 0\le L\le2\sigma m.}                         \tag{1}
\]

For larger \(L\), this probability is one. To prove (1), observe that

\[
 d-c(r)=\frac m2(\min_j\gamma_j+\max_j\gamma_j).
\]

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be the minimum and maximum of \(n\) independent
uniform variables on \([0,1]\), and put \(q=L/(2\sigma m)\). The
event is \(|A+B-1|\le q\). When \(n\ge2\), the joint density of
\((A,B)\) is \(n(n-1)(B-A)^{n-2}\). With range \(s=B-A\), integration
over the possible midpoints gives

\[
 \Pr\{|A+B-1|\le q\}
 =n(n-1)\int_0^1 s^{n-2}\min\{q,1-s\}\,ds
 =1-(1-q)^n.
\]

The case \(n=1\) follows directly from a uniform interval.

In particular,

\[
 \lim_{L\downarrow0}\frac{\Pr\{d\in I(r)\}}L
 =\frac{m}{2\sigma}=\frac{\sqrt n}{2\sigma}.           \tag{2}
\]

This is exactly the coefficient in the minor-sum estimate: the sum of
absolute \(1\times1\) minors of \(T^T\) is \(n/m=m\). Its simpler
\(\sqrt n\) bound is also attained. For \(L=\sigma/m\), the
probability in (1) tends to \(1-e^{-1/2}\), while \(L/\sigma\) tends
to zero. Thus no bound \(C L/\sigma\) with a dimension-independent
constant \(C\) can hold for the permitted residual-dependent intervals.

The same example has

\[
 \mathbb E H(r)=\frac{4\sigma m}{n+1},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(d)=\frac{\sigma^2}{3}.
\]

The short conditional fibers therefore coexist with a factor coefficient
whose variance does not shrink with ambient dimension. The variance
identity alone is not a statement about its marginal density.

## 2. Independent blocks give the full dimension exponent

For \(n=k m^2\), split the coordinates into \(k\) disjoint blocks
of size \(m^2\). Row \(i\) of \(T\) equals \(1/m\) on block
\(i\) and zero elsewhere. Again \(TT^T=I_k\), and all entries are
rational. Apply the preceding centered interval construction separately
inside each block. The blocks are independent, so

\[
 \Pr\{d_i\in I_i(r)\text{ for all }i\}
 =\prod_{i=1}^k
 \left[1-\left(1-\frac{L_i}{2\sigma m}\right)^{m^2}\right]
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

for \(0\le L_i\le2\sigma m\). As all lengths decrease to zero,

\[
 \frac{\Pr\{d_i\in I_i(r)\text{ for all }i\}}
      {\prod_i L_i/(2\sigma)}
 \longrightarrow m^k=\left(\frac nk\right)^{k/2}.      \tag{4}
\]

The sum of the absolute \(k\times k\) minors is exactly \(m^k\):
a nonzero minor chooses one coordinate from each block, giving
\(m^{2k}\) minors of absolute determinant \(m^{-k}\). Thus the
full complementary-minor estimate is asymptotically attained, including
its dependence on \(n\) up to factors depending only on \(k\).
For every fixed \(k\), no finite factor depending only on \(k\)
can replace this dependence on \(n\).

## 3. What this excludes

This example excludes an FPT improvement obtained solely by replacing the
current fiber-volume estimate with a dimension-independent estimate for
arbitrary measurable residual-dependent coordinate intervals. It already
satisfies the strongest factor conditioning \(TT^T=I_k\).

It does not establish that every such interval can be generated by the
quadratic recourse function, that many globally near-optimal grid nodes
must remain, or that exact cell closure must perform many operations.
The separate [local-count construction](../new-direction/ambient-local-count-barrier.md)
addresses realizability by actual quadratic recourse problems. Its
algorithmic limits must still be distinguished from the volume estimate.

The continuous law is the probability lemma's law. A claim about a fixed
finite perturbation grid requires the additional discrete calculation or
discrepancy argument; (1) is not asserted as an exact formula for atomic
noise.

## 4. Targeted verification

The proof above is analytic. The separate exact-rational check

```sh
python research-20261002/new-direction/check_ambient_fiber_sharpness.py
```

checks the range integral against (1), the minor sums for small disjoint
blocks, and finite-grid probabilities computed independently from endpoint
counts. It also checks convergence to (1) within the direct coordinatewise
discrepancy bound. It passed 160 exact range integrals, 96 discrete
endpoint/enumeration comparisons, 36 finite-grid discrepancy checks, and
22 disjoint-block minor sums. These checks do not implement or benchmark an optimizer.
No external search, project-wide verification, or CI inspection was used.
