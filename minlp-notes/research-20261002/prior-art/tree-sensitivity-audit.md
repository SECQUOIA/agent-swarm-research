# Focused literature audit: tree sensitivity and certified grid DP

Date: 2026-10-02

This read-only audit covers (i) the relation between pointwise sensitivity decay and total influence on branching trees, (ii) computation-tree and min-sum conditions, and (iii) prior work closest to a certified, geometrically refined coordinate-grid dynamic program. I checked the project literature index and the directly relevant packages. I did not start a literature session, ingest or check records, or change the literature knowledge base.

## Main assessment

Two distinctions matter for the proposed results.

First, exponential decay of the influence of one perturbed coordinate does not by itself give a dimension-free bound on the total effect of perturbing many coordinates. The sum over each distance shell also depends on how many vertices that shell contains. On a binary tree, an explicit uniformly strongly convex quadratic has exponentially decaying individual Green-function entries but an unbounded root-row sum of its inverse. This is a useful sharp example of the gap between pointwise locality and aggregate locality. The mechanism is the standard interaction between Green-function decay and volume growth; the threshold calculation is elementary once the resolvent is written down, so it should be presented as a counterexample/diagnostic rather than as a new general decay theory.

Second, the closest treewidth algorithms discretize continuous domains uniformly or exploit special value-function structure. Adaptive gridding, dynamic programming, and quadratic-growth localization each have prior art, but I found no source combining all of the following: unknown optimizer; common coordinate grids with geometric refinement and recentering; a smoothness-based correction that gives a certified global lower bound; global quadratic growth to contract the search region; and exact tree-decomposition DP with a polylogarithmic dependence on $1/\epsilon$. That combination looks like a plausible contribution, subject to the assumptions and complexity factors stated below. This is a focused search, not an exhaustive claim of priority.

## Branching-tree sensitivity example

Let $T_N$ be the full rooted binary tree of depth $N$, $A_N$ its adjacency matrix, and
\[
H_N=I-aA_N,\qquad a\in(1/3,1/(2\sqrt 2)).
\]
The eigenvalues of $A_N$ are bounded in magnitude by $2\sqrt 2$ (indeed its largest eigenvalue is $2\sqrt 2\cos(\pi/(N+2))$). Thus
\[
\lambda_{\min}(H_N)\ge 1-2\sqrt 2a>0,
\]
uniformly in tree depth. The objective \(F_N(x)=\tfrac12x^\top H_Nx-b^\top x\) is uniformly strongly convex, has a uniformly bounded Hessian norm, maximum degree three, and treewidth one. For a local linear perturbation $b$, its solution sensitivity is $D_bx^*=H_N^{-1}$. Since $aA_N\ge0$ and \(\rho(aA_N)<1\),
\[
H_N^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}a^kA_N^k
\]
is entrywise nonnegative.

On the infinite rooted binary tree, the root-to-vertex Green function at depth $d$ is $g_d=g_0q^d$, where
\[
q=a+2aq^2=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-8a^2}}{4a}.
\]
For finite depth, let $q_+=(1+\sqrt{1-8a^2})/(4a)$ be the other recurrence root. The leaf boundary condition gives $g_d=C_Nq^d[1-(q/q_+)^{N+1-d}]$, with $C_N$ tending to a positive limit. When $a>1/3$, the depth-$N$ shell alone is $\Theta((2q)^N)$; at $a=1/3$ the shell terms stay bounded below through order $N$ levels. Thus the finite root-row sums diverge exponentially above the threshold and linearly at it. A uniform strong-convexity constant and bounded degree do not imply a dimension-free $\ell_1$ bound on the response to bounded coordinatewise perturbations: because $H_N^{-1}\ge0$, the worst response to $\|\delta b\|_\infty\le1$ is exactly this row sum, even though each fixed-distance entry decays as $q^d$.

The standard linear-algebra and graphical-model terms help keep the thresholds straight:

- The unweighted row-sum condition is \(\|aA_N\|_\infty\le3a<1\), with threshold $1/3$. This is the Gaussian analogue of a max-row-sum Dobrushin influence condition.
- For a symmetric nonnegative comparison matrix, a positive diagonal scaling exists with weighted row contraction below one exactly when its spectral radius is below one. Here \(\rho(aA_N)<1\) all the way to $a<1/(2\sqrt2)$. In Gaussian graphical-model terminology this is **walk-summability**: for unit diagonal $H=I-R$, \(\rho(|R|)<1\). It is equivalent to absolute convergence of the walk expansion and to pairwise-normalizability of the Gaussian information matrix. It is weaker than unweighted strict diagonal dominance.
- Pointwise exponential decay is sometimes called exponential decay of sensitivity, spatial mixing, or Green-function decay, depending on the field. Total influence is instead the row sum/susceptibility. Uniform total influence needs decay strong enough to dominate shell growth.

The Perron weights $w$ of $A_N$ make the scaled-diagonal-dominance ratio equal to \(\lambda_N=a\rho(A_N)<1\). Their root-to-leaf ratio is $2^{N/2}$: on radial vectors the adjacency matrix reduces to a path with off-diagonal entries \(\sqrt2\), whose Perron vector has coordinates proportional to \(\sin((d+1)\pi/(N+2))/\sqrt{2^d}\). Moallemi and Van Roy's min-sum convergence theorem includes the factor
\[
K=\frac1M\frac{\max_i w_i}{\min_iw_i}
\]
in its error bound. For this example $M=1$, so that displayed theorem bound can grow as $2^{N/2}$, even though $\lambda_N$ stays uniformly below one. This establishes that their published bound does not certify dimension-free localization here; it does not prove that no sharper message-specific bound is possible. Walk-summability supplies a decomposition into positive-definite pairwise quadratic factors. A sufficiently small positive unary quadratic can be moved out of these factors while preserving their positive definiteness, satisfying Moallemi and Van Roy's strictly convex unary-factor hypothesis. Thus the example is in their pairwise-convex framework after refactorization; the unsplit edge term \(-a x_ix_j\) being nonconvex is not an obstruction.

Primary sources:

- Shin, Anitescu, and Zavala, [“Exponential Decay of Sensitivity in Graph-Structured Nonlinear Programs”](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.03067v2), SIAM Journal on Optimization 32(2), 2022, DOI [10.1137/21M1391079](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1391079). Theorem 13 bounds a node's primal-dual response to a perturbation at another node by a coefficient exponential in graph distance, under SSOSC and LICQ; uniform regularity yields uniform constants across a graph family. It is a pairwise bound, not a volume-summed root-row bound. The paper also notes that its theoretical decay rates can be conservative.
- Malioutov, Johnson, and Willsky, [“Walk-Sums and Belief Propagation in Gaussian Graphical Models”](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/volume7/malioutov06a/malioutov06a.pdf), JMLR 7 (2006), 2031–2064. Their Proposition 1 characterizes walk-summability by \(\rho(|R|)<1\); Proposition 13 proves its equivalence to pairwise-normalizability. They show Gaussian BP means converge to the correct means on walk-summable models. For a tree, ordinary Gaussian elimination/BP is exact.
- Moallemi and Van Roy, [“Convergence of the Min-Sum Algorithm for Convex Optimization”](https://arxiv.org/html/0705.4253v1), arXiv:0705.4253 (2007; IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 56(4), 2010). The paper formulates scaled diagonal dominance, proves min-sum convergence for pairwise-separable convex objectives, unrolls updates on computation trees, and gives the $K\lambda^t/(1-\lambda)$ initialization-error bound. The bound's weight-ratio factor is the relevant caveat for dimension-free claims.

For a general graph, summing an EDS estimate over all perturbed nodes requires a graph-growth condition. If shell sizes obey $|S_d(i)|\le C\gamma^d$ and the per-node bound is at most $C'\rho^d$, the geometric-series bound is summable when $\gamma\rho<1$. Shin et al.'s theorem uses the coarser rate $\rho^{\lceil d/4-1\rceil_+}$, so the shell-growth test must be applied to that rate. The binary-tree example shows why pointwise decay is insufficient.

## Existing solver capabilities and the geometric-grid candidate

Given a finite coordinate grid with $q$ values per variable and a supplied width-$w$ tree decomposition, exact min-sum elimination has the standard $O(nq^{w+1})$ state/transition scale (with factor-evaluation cost additional). For quadratic objectives on a forest, continuous messages can sometimes be represented exactly by a compact piecewise-quadratic form. For general smooth functions, exact continuous messages need not have a finite-size representation, so ordinary treewidth alone is not a generic continuous solver.

The candidate algorithm is stronger than “run a grid DP and refine.” Its useful proof obligations are: (a) unbiased independent rounding to corners of a box and a $C^{1,1}$ smoothness bound to control interpolation error by a sum of squared interval widths (with the factor-arity multiplier for local terms); (b) a correction at each grid vertex that turns the discretized DP into a valid global lower bound; and (c) global quadratic growth $F(x)-F^*\ge c_g\|x-x^*\|^2$, which traps each corrected grid minimizer near the unknown optimizer and justifies geometric mesh reduction and recentering. If these steps hold with boundary minimizers included, each coordinate grid can have $O(\theta^{-1}\log(s_0/h))$ values instead of $O(1/h)$. The resulting tree-decomposition cost is of the proposed form $n\theta^{-(w+1)}\operatorname{polylog}(n/\epsilon)$, for fixed arity and fixed conditioning constants. The claim should state the dependence on $M/c_g$, $\theta$, grid range, factor arity, and decomposition width, and should distinguish oracle operations from bit complexity.

The latest project variant rebuilds bag-and-separator shell grids around the previous consistent point at each step, uses local slope scales $\lambda(x)$, and keeps a graded cover of the full domain so the corrected DP still supplies a global lower bound. This distinction matters against DDDP: the latter searches only a neighborhood of a trial trajectory, while the candidate keeps a globally valid shell cover. The project-side theorem, now covered by a separate adversarial proof and count review, gives certificate size $O(N C^p\log(N/\epsilon))$ and $O(N\,3^p C^p\log^3(N/\epsilon))$ exact convex-subproblem oracle calls. I have not independently checked those new bounds; the literature audit addresses priority and closest comparisons.

The nearest comparisons do not supply that full guarantee:

- Bienstock and Muñoz, [“LP Formulations for Polynomial Optimization Problems”](https://arxiv.org/html/1501.00288v15), SIAM Journal on Optimization 28(2), 2018, DOI [10.1137/15M1054079](https://doi.org/10.1137/15M1054079), already give certified bounded-treewidth LP approximations for mixed-integer polynomial optimization. Their general size scales as \(O((2\pi/\epsilon)^{w+1}n\log(\pi/\epsilon))\) (their $\pi$ denotes a degree bound), with scaled feasibility and optimality tolerances; it is not the polylogarithmic mesh dependence proposed here. Their appendix explains why such dependence on $1/\epsilon$ cannot generally be reduced to polylogarithmic for the broad feasibility/optimization model unless $P=NP$. This lower-bound discussion does not by itself rule out the more restrictive smooth, globally QG objective-only setting.
- Del Pia and Khajavirad, [“Treewidth and the complexity of box-constrained quadratic programs”](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35595v1), arXiv:2609.35595 (submitted 2026-09-28), give a strongly polynomial $O(n^2)$ exact DP for box-QP on forests by exploiting concave piecewise-quadratic univariate value functions with linearly many pieces, and prove strong NP-hardness at treewidth two for general box-QP. It is a key special-class capability and hardness boundary, not a solver for arbitrary $C^{1,1}$ objectives under QG.
- Gupte, Koster, and Kuhn, [“An Adaptive Refinement Algorithm for Discretizations of Nonconvex QCQP”](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol233-sea2022/LIPIcs.SEA.2022.24/LIPIcs.SEA.2022.24.pdf), SEA 2022, DOI [10.4230/LIPIcs.SEA.2022.24](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.SEA.2022.24), iteratively adapts a MILP discretization from a previous feasible QCQP solution. It is a primal heuristic; it does not establish a certified global lower bound or a QG-based complexity rate.
- Munos and Moore, [“Variable Resolution Discretization in Optimal Control”](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pabbeel/cs287-fa15/optreadings/MunosMoore-MLJ2001.pdf), Machine Learning 49(2–3), 2002, DOI [10.1023/A:1017992615625](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1017992615625), adaptively split kd-tree state cells using value/policy approximation and influence criteria. This is a strong adaptive-DP precedent, but concerns near-optimal control policies and value functions rather than global lower-bounding optimization of a sparse static objective.
- Luus, [“Optimal control by dynamic programming using systematic reduction in grid size”](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207179008934113), International Journal of Control 51(5), 1990, pp. 995–1013, describes starting on a coarse grid and systematically shrinking it. The publisher abstract reports convergence and says a sufficiently large initial grid gives a high likelihood of the global optimum. I could not verify a freely available full paper; the abstract does not establish the certified lower-bound/treewidth/QG complexity above.
- Heidari, Chow, Kokotović, and Meredith, [“Discrete Differential Dynamic Programing Approach to Water Resources Systems Optimization”](https://www.derongliu.org/adp/adp-cdrom/refs/heidari19710273.pdf), Water Resources Research 7(2), 1971, pp. 273–282, DOI [10.1029/WR007i002p00273](https://doi.org/10.1029/WR007i002p00273), starts from a trial trajectory and applies Bellman recursion in its neighborhood to obtain a locally improved trajectory. It is an early local iterative-DP precedent, not a global certificate.
- Bestehorn et al., [“Non-uniform Grid Refinement for the Combinatorial Integral Approximation”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12846), already has a read package in the project KB at [`literature/papers/bestehorn2023-non-uniform-grid-refinement-for/paper.md`](../../literature/papers/bestehorn2023-non-uniform-grid-refinement-for/paper.md). It proves finite termination/admissibility and weak-* approximation properties for a control-adapted refinement/rounding algorithm; it does not globally solve the resulting nonlinear objective or give the proposed treewidth/QG certificate.

The Bienstock–Muñoz LP approximation, Zhang–Sun SDDP for stochastic MS-MINLP, and Bhathena et al.'s exact indicator-QP DP already have read packages in the KB. Zhang–Sun provides global decomposition guarantees under its exact-penalty and stagewise assumptions, but its state-space sampling bounds are dimension-dependent. Bhathena et al. exploit $Q\succ0$, graph volume growth, and a margin condition in an exact indicator-QP DP. Neither is the proposed grid-refinement result.

## Adjacent disorder/correlation-decay results

These sources are relevant to the parallel high-disorder/support-screening question, but they do not establish exact continuous support optimization by screening and site percolation:

- Atamtürk and Gómez, [“Safe screening rules for L0-regression from Perspective Relaxations”](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/atamturk20a.html), ICML/PMLR 119 (2020), pp. 421–430, derive safe feature elimination rules from a convex relaxation for $\ell_0$ regression or cardinality constraints. This is the closest direct screening precedent found, but it is deterministic relaxation-based preprocessing and does not prove an expected-polynomial component algorithm under random penalties.
- The project KB also contains Atamtürk and Gómez, [“Strong Formulations for Quadratic Optimization with M-Matrices and Indicator Variables”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-018-1301-5), Mathematical Programming (2018). Under its M-matrix and sign assumptions, it uses submodularity and bivariate indicator structure to solve a deterministic support problem; it does not obtain tractability from random penalties or percolation. This is a close solver-capability precedent when $Q$ is an M-matrix, but does not cover general signed diagonally dominant $Q$.
- Gamarnik, Goldberg, and Weber, [“Correlation Decay in Random Decision Networks”](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.0338), Mathematics of Operations Research 39(2), 2014, pp. 229–261, DOI [10.1287/moor.2013.0609](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.2013.0609). Their cavity expansion unrolls a computation tree and gives high-probability near-optimal decentralized algorithms for finite-action networks with random rewards. It is an optimization precedent, but randomized finite actions and near-optimality differ from an exact continuous-spin/support solution.
- Helmuth, Lee, Perkins, Ravichandran, and Wu, [“Approximation algorithms for the random-field Ising model”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11889), prove a high-probability FPTAS for the partition function and samplers on bounded-degree graphs under sufficiently high-variance iid Gaussian random fields, using percolation on a self-avoiding-walk tree. This is an inference/sampling result, not an exact ground-state or support-optimization theorem.
- For a ferromagnetic random-field Ising model, ground-state optimization is already a minimum-cut problem for arbitrary fields; see [“Exact determination of all ground states of random field systems in polynomial time”](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/037843719400259V). The new setting must be distinguished from that class if it includes frustrated couplings, continuous spins, or indicator penalties with mediated interactions.

In the targeted searches I found no prior exact expected-polynomial theorem that combines random indicator-penalty screening, bounded-degree site percolation, and exact optimization of the induced continuous subproblems. This is a useful lead, not evidence that no such result exists. If unpenalized continuous mediators are present, their elimination can create dense interactions among screened candidates; an argument must account for those mediators rather than use candidate components in the original sparsity graph alone.
