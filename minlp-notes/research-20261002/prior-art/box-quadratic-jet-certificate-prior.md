# Prior-art audit: box quadratic jets and sparse strict-copositivity certificates

Date: 2026-10-02. This is a focused comparison of the
[box-preordering obstruction](../new-direction/box-preordering-growth-obstruction.md)
and the sparse tree-decomposition grid certificate for strict copositivity.
It is not a completeness search or a publication-priority claim. Source
availability is recorded separately from the results cited.

## Main finding

The five-variable obstruction is a concrete application of established
copositive-cone geometry and local-global Positivstellensatz theory. Its
specific matrix, rational separator, fixed growth ratio, and full box-slack
preordering jet calculation are useful explicit data, but neither the Horn
matrix nor the principle of testing a nonnegative polynomial at its zeros is
new. Scheiderer’s Corollary 3.17 gives the exact local-to-global framework:
on a compact basic closed set, a polynomial nonnegative there with finitely
many zeros belongs to the generated preordering if it belongs to the
completed local preordering at every zero. Here there is one zero, and the
quadratic initial form fails the necessary SPN decomposition at that point.

This nonmembership does not imply that no finite certificate of any kind
exists. Strict copositivity on the orthant gives a finite Pólya-type
coefficient certificate after multiplication by a power of the coordinate
sum. The project note gives an explicit multiplier degree for the example.
The distinction is between a certificate of `F` in the unmultiplied box
preordering and a certificate of `s^r F` with a multiplier that vanishes at
the optimizer.

The geometric-grid method has now been stated as an unknown-margin discovery
algorithm. If `g` is the minimum of `Q` on the nonnegative Euclidean unit
sphere, successive trials return a rational certificate with
`g/16 <= sigma < g`; the algorithm does not receive `g`. Its arithmetic
work is `f(p,L/g) poly(n+N)`, and its bit work is `f_1(p,L/g) poly(I)` with
an absolute polynomial exponent. The grid-state and trial counts depend on
the diagonal-curvature/growth ratio, not coefficient height or a requested
accuracy. Standard simplicial branch-and-bound already gives finite
termination for strictly copositive forms under exhaustive refinement. The
remaining comparison is therefore this sparse, treewidth-based complexity
and verified margin output, not the general idea of refining a finite mesh.
This is a scoped prior-art assessment, not a priority claim.

## The box-slack obstruction and its closest algebraic predecessors

For the full preordering generated by `x_i` and `1-x_i` on `[0,1]^n`, the
quadratic jet at the origin has a simple necessary form. In an identity for
a homogeneous quadratic, terms with no lower-bound slack contribute a
positive-semidefinite quadratic part; terms with one lower slack have zero
constant multiplier and start above degree two; terms with two lower slacks
contribute nonnegative multiples of `x_i x_j`. Thus the quadratic matrix
must be

`Q = P + N`, with `P` positive semidefinite and `N` entrywise nonnegative.

This is the classical SPN cone, also the level-zero inner approximation of
the copositive cone. The local project proof establishes the statement
directly for this preordering; it is not a general characterization for
arbitrary semialgebraic generators. [[baek2026-sum-of-squares-certificates-for]]
p.1-3

The standard Horn matrix `H=J-2A(C_5)` is copositive but not SPN. The
candidate uses `Q=H+I/5`, which is strictly copositive with
`x^T Q x >= ||x||^2/5` on the orthant, yet remains outside SPN by the
nonnegative positive-definite separator `W=(13/8)I+A(C_5)` with
`<Q,W>=-1/4`. This is a strict-growth perturbation of the classical
five-dimensional SPN separation, not a new exceptional ray of the
copositive cone. The same source records that SPN equals the copositive cone
through dimension four and that Horn is the standard dimension-five
counterexample. [[baek2026-sum-of-squares-certificates-for]] p.2,
p.4-5

The local calculation is consistent with, and sharpened by, Scheiderer’s
local-global theorem for preorderings. Corollary 3.17 states that if a
finitely generated preordering has compact real set, `f` is nonnegative
there, and its zero set is finite, then local membership in the completed
preordering at each zero implies global membership. Its converse is
immediate by localization. For this example, the local jet separator is
therefore enough to rule out the global full-box-preordering identity at
every degree. The theorem does not supply the SPN jet test itself, the
matrix `Q`, or the conditioning bounds; those are the explicit contribution
of the example. (Scheiderer, “Sums of squares on real algebraic curves,”
*Mathematische Zeitschrift* 245 (2003), Cor. 3.17,
[DOI 10.1007/s00209-003-0568-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00209-003-0568-1).)

I independently checked the primary article text in the local PDF and
against the public CiteSeerX copy
([direct PDF](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=6c06d73af35492041c13d19ce42e011700c6792b&repid=rep1&type=pdf)); Corollary 3.17 is printed p. 13 and appears on 1-based physical PDF page 13 (zero-based page index 12). Its exact scope is an affine finite-type real scheme `V`, a finitely generated preorder `T` in `R[V]` with compact real set `K=S(T)`, and `f>=0` on `K` with finitely many zeros `M_i`; membership in the completed local preorder at each `M_i` implies `f∈T`. The ingested local package is marked read and confirms this locator. This theorem is a local-global membership criterion, not by itself a finite-degree or complexity bound.

The standard Horn/SPN facts also delimit what this example says about
solvers. Burer’s copositive reformulation gives an exact lifted cone model
for broad nonconvex quadratic programs, but tractability is not obtained by
that reformulation; the completely-positive/copositive cone is the hard
component. [[burer2009-on-the-copositive-representation-of]] p.6,
p.13-15. Dong and Anstreicher show that PSD, RLT, and triangle constraints
can leave a gap on a three-variable box QP, while giving stronger
copositive-cut and extended-formulation alternatives for their instance.
This is adjacent evidence that familiar low-order relaxations need not be
exact, not a prior instance of the local five-variable jet obstruction.
[[dong2013-separating-doubly-nonnegative-and-completely]] p.14-15

## What existing positivity certificates do cover

For a homogeneous form strictly positive on the nonnegative orthant away
from zero, Pólya’s theorem says that `(sum_i x_i)^r q(x)` has nonnegative
coefficients for some finite `r`. Dickinson–Povh extend this type of
coefficientwise multiplier certificate to homogeneous positivity over
semialgebraic cones. [[dickinson2015-on-an-extension-of-polyas]] p.3-7.
Baek–Vargas describe the resulting copositive SOS hierarchy: the fixed
Pólya multiplier levels cover the interior of the copositive cone, while
the Horn matrix lies outside the zero-multiplier SPN level. They also give
explicit certificates for selected boundary families. This directly
precludes claiming that the strict-growth example has no finite
multiplier/SOS proof. [[baek2026-sum-of-squares-certificates-for]] p.2-3,
p.15-19

The project’s separate calculation gives an elementary quadratic-specialized
sufficient degree: if `q(x)=x^TQx >= g||x||^2` on the nonnegative orthant,
`D=max_i Q_ii`, and `p` is the number of variables, then
`(sum_i x_i)^(d-2) q(x)` has nonnegative coefficients once
`d >= pD/g` (with `d>=2`). For the five-variable example, `d=30`, so
`s^28 F` is a finite coefficientwise certificate despite nonmembership of
`F` itself in the box preordering. This is an elementary explicit bound,
not a new Pólya principle. Powers–Reznick (2001) give a general quantitative
Pólya bound in terms of the degree, normalized coefficient size, and
simplex minimum. Their exact theorem has not yet been checked against this
quadratic specialization, so no claim is made that the project’s bound is
stronger or weaker. The degree and monomial count are dimension-sensitive;
with `p=n`, the dense coefficient array is not a treewidth-FPT certificate.

Bernstein-basis certificates give a second established strict-positivity
baseline. The official publisher abstract for Boudaoud, Caruso, and Roy’s
2008 paper reports a polynomial-bit certificate bound for a univariate
integer polynomial positive on `[-1,1]`. The article text was not retrieved,
so the earlier attribution of multivariate Theorem 5.1 and Algorithm 5.9 to
that paper is withdrawn; the article remains unread in the local KB. The
publisher abstract does not supply those multivariate theorem locators, a
sparse treewidth bound, or the proposed mixed-box certificate. The
[ResearchGate entry filed under the 2008 title](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/47693516_Certificates_of_Positivity_in_the_Bernstein_Basis)
exposes a PDF whose front matter identifies it as Leroy’s separate 2009
manuscript; it is not evidence for the 2008 article’s multivariate text. ([DOI
10.1007/s00454-007-9042-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-007-9042-x).)

A distinct open manuscript by Richard Leroy, [“Certificates of positivity
in the simplicial Bernstein basis”](https://hal.science/hal-00589945),
contains the exact multivariate locators previously attributed to
Boudaoud–Caruso–Roy. Theorem 5.1 gives a degree-elevation certificate for
strict positivity on the standard simplex, with a degree bound depending on
the simplex dimension, positive minimum, and initial Bernstein-coefficient
second differences (printed p. 28; 1-based physical PDF p. 29). Theorem 5.7
gives a subdivision bound (printed p. 29; physical PDF p. 30). Algorithm 5.9
uses a coefficient-height lower bound for the minimum to return a local
Bernstein certificate when the polynomial is positive, or a point below that
threshold otherwise (printed pp. 30–31; physical PDF pp. 31–32). I inspected the
public HAL manuscript PDF at
[hal.science/hal-00589945/document](https://hal.science/hal-00589945/document).
This is a strict-positivity certificate over simplex cells, not a
treewidth-sensitive certificate for a nonnegative objective with a
minimizer at a zero. It is a separate source and must not be cited as the
2008 article’s Theorem 5.1 or Algorithm 5.9.

On a compact box, Schmüdgen’s Positivstellensatz and Putinar’s theorem give
finite preordering or quadratic-module certificates for strictly positive
polynomials under their respective assumptions. Effective results make
the dependence on the positive minimum explicit, but do not directly imply
membership when the target vanishes at its minimizer. Nie–Schweighofer give
an effective Putinar degree bound depending on the positivity margin, with
a generally large exponential expression. [[nie2007-on-the-complexity-of-putinar]]
p.2-5. Baldi–Mourrain improve the rate under their regularity and
Łojasiewicz assumptions, but their theorem still certifies `f>0` with a
positive margin; the order can diverge as that margin tends to zero.
[[baldi2023-on-the-effective-putinars-positivstellensatz]] p.1-4,
p.7-12. Handelman’s theorem is another strict-positive compact-polyhedron
certificate by nonnegative products of affine defining functions; it
likewise does not settle an exact zero at a corner (Handelman, 1988,
[DOI 10.2140/PJM.1988.132.35](https://doi.org/10.2140/PJM.1988.132.35)).

## Sparse strict-copositivity grid: closest solver comparisons

The certificate normalizes a nonzero orthant vector by `max_i x_i=1`, then
uses one shared geometric coordinate grid with a small initial step.
Independent mean-preserving rounding leaves every off-diagonal quadratic
term unbiased, so only diagonal variance contributes to the correction.
The resulting exact grid minimum gives a global lower bound after homogeneity
is restored. A one-bit OR state records whether an owned variable equals one;
tree-decomposition DP computes the minimum with one normalized coordinate
condition rather than `n` separate anchored problems.

The algorithm does not know the growth margin `g`. It tries
`delta=1/2,1/4,...`, with correction `sigma=L delta^2/8`, and stops when an
exact rational DP lower bound becomes positive. Every positive result
certifies `Q(x) >= sigma ||x||_2^2 + b ||x||_infinity^2` for all `x>=0`.
Under strict copositivity, the returned margin lies in `[g/16,g)`. A
nonpositive trial is inconclusive; the procedure need not terminate on a
boundary-copositive or noncopositive matrix. The proof uses diagonal
curvature only in the rounding error. The grid is geometric, but the exact
search grid and this output guarantee should be distinguished from general
simplex-subdivision tests.

At the successful trial the grid has `K=O(sqrt(L/g) log(n sqrt(L/g)))`
states per coordinate, and each bag table has `K^p` entries plus one
Boolean flag. The proved encoding analysis bounds exact arithmetic and
verification by `f_1(p,L/g) poly(I)` with an absolute polynomial exponent;
it also explains how logarithmic factors in `n` are absorbed into that
polynomial factor. Thus the candidate states an FPT bound in bag size and
conditioning, not merely an XP bound with exponent `p`.

The method is not an obvious corollary of the Pólya or Bernstein hierarchy:
those methods use coefficient or basis certificates and generally have
dense dimension growth. Nor is it the first finite-termination strict
copositivity test: simplicial-partition algorithms have that property. The
candidate’s potentially meaningful increment is the combination of a
diagonal-only second-order rounding correction, homogeneous max-normalization,
one-flag treewidth DP, and an unknown-margin stopping rule with an explicit
factor-sixteen verified output. A focused search found no matching
condition-sensitive sparse test, diagonal-only rounding certificate, or
geometric-grid copositivity algorithm under a different name. This is only a
scoped search observation; it does not establish novelty, and the individual
ingredients are standard.

## Solver and certificate interpretation

The obstruction rules out one precise output type: a finite exact identity
`F = T` in the full polynomial preordering generated by the original box
slacks. It does not rule out:

- the explicit vanishing-multiplier certificate `s^28 F`;
- Pólya, Bernstein, or SOS hierarchy certificates at a sufficiently high
  level for strict positivity;
- a finite-grid/tree-DP lower bound certificate;
- extended-variable, copositive-cone, or other valid inequality proofs; or
- exact global optimization that outputs a rational optimizer and value
  without outputting an SOS identity.

Therefore report the matrix as a limitation of unmultiplied box-preordering
certificates, not as hardness of minimizing the instance or of producing all
global certificates. The proposed grid method would itself be a different
certificate family, with its own proof object: decomposition, mesh,
per-factor corrected tables, recurrence checks, and a strictly positive
final rational lower bound.
