# Prior-art audit: random linear tilts and global quadratic growth

## Candidate result under review

The current candidate considers a continuous objective on a compact box and adds independent random linear coefficients. Its proposed conclusion is a unique global minimizer with high probability and an explicit inverse-polynomial lower bound on the *global* quadratic-growth constant. For a rational box QP, a finite rational perturbation grid gives polynomial-bit perturbations. The compact-domain argument and rational-grid extension passed independent adversarial mathematical review; that review does not establish literature priority. This note records the closest prior results and the boundary they leave.

## Closest prior results

Lee and Phạm, [“Stability and Genericity for Semi-Algebraic Compact Programs”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10957-016-0910-5), *Journal of Optimization Theory and Applications* 169(2), 473–495 (2016), Theorem 6.1, treat polynomial objectives of bounded degree over a fixed compact semialgebraic feasible set. They prove that an open dense set of objective parameters gives a unique global minimizer and some positive global quadratic-growth constant. The paper states that the result also applies to a fixed polynomial plus a linear perturbation. This is direct qualitative prior art for generic uniqueness and global growth under the same perturbation type. It does not give a probability tail or a lower bound for the growth modulus in terms of dimension, box scale, perturbation density, or failure probability. Its proof uses semialgebraic genericity, while the candidate’s continuous-objective statement would not require definability.

Lee and Phạm, [“Generic Properties for Semialgebraic Programs”](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1068992), *SIAM Journal on Optimization* 27(3), 2061–2084 (2017), Theorem A, fix a polynomial objective and perturb it by a linear form over a regular closed semialgebraic set. At generic perturbation parameters they establish uniqueness, local uniform quadratic growth, and a uniform global sharp-minimum inequality; on compact feasible sets, uniqueness plus local growth also gives some global quadratic-growth constant. This again makes genericity and existence of a positive modulus established ingredients. It supplies no quantitative high-probability lower bound on that modulus. The result is for semialgebraic programs and assumes regularity of the feasible set.

Drusvyatskiy and Lewis, [“Tilt Stability, Uniform Quadratic Growth, and Strong Metric Regularity of the Subdifferential”](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/3459.pdf), *SIAM Journal on Optimization* 23 (2013), 256–267, give deterministic local equivalences among tilt stability, uniform second-order growth, and strong metric regularity. This is relevant terminology and local stability theory, but it neither treats random tilts nor a global compact-domain growth modulus.

Beier and Vöcking, [“Typical Properties of Winners and Losers in Discrete Optimization”](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539705447268), *SIAM Journal on Computing* 35(4), 855–881 (2006), Lemma 5 and Theorem 1, prove a quantitative generalized isolating lemma. For an arbitrary fixed feasible set in \(\{0,1\}^n\), independent objective coefficients with density at most \(\phi\) give a winner-gap density at most \(2n\phi\); their theorem also gives a polynomial bound on the number of random coefficient bits needed to identify the discrete winner with high probability. This is strong prior art for separating discrete modes under random linear objectives. It is not a quadratic-growth result on a continuum: the winner gap controls the objective difference between distinct discrete solutions, while within-mode curvature and distances between continuous points are outside the lemma.

## Comparison and scope

The known qualitative semialgebraic results already cover “a generic linear perturbation yields a unique optimum with some quadratic growth.” The candidate contribution, if its proof survives review, must therefore be stated quantitatively: an explicit density/scale/dimension/failure-probability bound on a *global* growth modulus for continuous domains, plus a finite-bit rational perturbation construction for rational QPs. Beier–Vöcking covers discrete score isolation, not continuous distance growth. Local tilt stability and a positive Hessian on the active face are also insufficient by themselves to establish a global modulus, since they do not rule out distant near-tied regions.

The audit searched specifically for random linear perturbations with quantitative global growth, smoothed quadratic-program conditioning, optimizer-map anti-concentration, and weak-type/maximal-slope bounds for the argmin response. I did not find a prior theorem matching the candidate’s quantitative continuous-domain statement. That negative search is scoped and does not establish novelty. The rational finite-grid construction is mathematically reviewed; its value as a contribution still depends on comparison with any undiscovered bit-complexity result for this setting.
