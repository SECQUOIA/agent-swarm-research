                Sharp gaps for positive multilinear relaxations


                                                   Abstract
         Luedtke, Namazifar, and Linderoth conjectured that for multilinear functions with positive
     coefficients on nonnegative boxes, the termwise gap (the sum of the envelope widths of the
     individual terms) is at most a universal constant times the convex-hull gap. We disprove this
     conjecture with a sparse family of unit-coefficient polynomials on unit cubes. At a strictly
     interior point, the Lth member has termwise gap L and exact hull gap s + (L − s)2−s , where
     s = log2 L + O(1) is an explicit cutoff; the ratio therefore grows like L/ log2 L. A finite inequality
     bounds the hull gap and an explicit probability law attains the bound. We then determine the
     sharp growth of the worst-case ratio over all finite nonnegative boxes: R(d) ∼ ln d/ ln ln d in the
     maximum degree d and C(n) ∼ ln n/ ln ln n in the number n of variables. The upper bound uses
     one mixture of three probability laws that bounds every term at once and applies to the original
     terms on every finite nonnegative box. An accompanying standalone Lean project verifies the
     disproof, the exact hull-gap formula, the explicit finite upper bound, and both asymptotic limits.


1    Introduction
Global solvers for nonconvex problems usually relax each nonlinear term separately. For a multilinear
function, the termwise relaxation replaces each monomial by its own convex and concave envelopes;
for bilinear terms these are the McCormick inequalities. The convex hull of the whole graph is the
strongest relaxation, but its standard description uses all vertices of the box, exponentially many
[2]. This paper asks how much the termwise relaxation can lose.

both definitions.

Prior work. Luedtke et al. [2] studied this comparison. With positive coefficients on a nonnegative
box, the concave envelope of f is the sum of the concave envelopes of its terms, so all of the loss
is on the convex side. For bilinear functions with positive coefficients on the unit cube, they
proved that the McCormick gap, which equals the termwise gap for bilinear terms, is less than
twice the hull gap, with a factor that depends on the chromatic number of the graph of the terms.
Numerical experiments then led to their Conjecture 4.1 [2, p. 349]: for positive multilinear functions
on nonnegative boxes, TB /HB is bounded by a universal constant. The constant is meant to be
uniform in both degree and dimension. Without the sign condition, even bilinear functions behave
differently: Boland et al. [1] showed that on the unit cube the ratio of the McCormick gap to the

Results.
    • Disproof (Theorem 5). The family fL of (5) has unit coefficients on a unit cube and degree
      2L−1 + 1. At a strictly interior point, TL = L (Lemma 3) while HL ≤ ⌈log2 L⌉ + 1 (Lemma 4

    • Exact gap (Theorem 6). For an explicit cutoff s = log2 L + O(1), HL = s + (L − s)2−s exactly,
      and an explicit law attains it. Thus TL /HL ∼ L/ log2 L (16).
    • Sharp growth (Theorem 7). R(d) ∼ ln d/ ln ln d and C(n) ∼ ln n/ ln ln n, with leading constant
      one. The upper half is an explicit finite bound TB ≤ ZHB for every degree d (Theorem 8),
      valid for the original terms on every finite nonnegative box. The lower half comes from the
      family above.
    • Formal verification (Appendix B) of all of these results in Lean.

Key idea. By Lemma 1, vexB f (x) is the minimum of Ef (X) over probability laws on the box
vertices with mean x. In the termwise gap each term may use its own minimizing law, while the hull
gap requires one law for all terms together. The family is built so that the individual minimizing
laws conflict. For the upper bound we construct one law, depending only on the variable means and
the degree, that captures at least a fraction 1/Z of every term’s gap at once.

Scope. Our upper bounds concern the exact termwise envelopes in (3), not recursive McCormick
relaxations of higher-degree products, which can be weaker on general boxes [2]. A pointwise width
ratio also does not by itself give an approximation ratio for a constrained optimization problem or
an algorithm for evaluating a convex envelope.
    Section 2 gives the vertex-law description of graph hulls; Sections 3–5 and Appendix A prove
the results in the order listed.

A.4    Transfer to original terms on a nonnegative box
For each coordinate substitute xi = ℓi + (ui − ℓi )yi with 0 ≤ yi ≤ 1, and let fe(y) = f (x). Expanding
original monomials gives nonnegative coefficients and does not increase degree.
    First suppose all coordinates are nonfixed. The substitution is an affine bijection, so it maps
graph hulls and preserves their vertical widths:

                            HB (f ; x) = H(fe; y),        TB (f ; x) ≤ T (fe; y).                  (29)

For the second inequality, an original term becomes a sum of expanded monomials. The sum of
their convex envelopes is a convex underestimator of that term; the sum of their concave envelopes
is a concave overestimator. Thus the original term’s width is at most the sum of the expanded
widths. Sum this inequality over the original terms. If expansion produces the same support more
than once, combining its nonnegative coefficients leaves that sum of widths unchanged, because
envelopes scale linearly by a nonnegative constant.
    If a coordinate is fixed, restrict the original box to its nonfixed coordinates. In the expanded
function the fixed coordinate has zero width and its new cube coordinate is unused. Projecting
or extending laws on unused coordinates preserves every attainable expectation, as in the proof
of Theorem 7. Thus both the full-hull equality and the original-term inequality in (29) still
hold. If all coordinates are fixed, every gap is zero. Applying the cube bound to fe now proves
TB (f ; x) ≤ T (fe; y) ≤ ZH(fe; y) = ZHB (f ; x), completing Theorem 8.

proof modules are 41 multilinear modules and seven shared modules for finite laws, graph hulls,
and individual envelopes. The shared modules keep the namespace CubicGap from their original
location; they are general infrastructure, and no cubic counterexample modules are included.
    The file links below are relative to this PDF; formal/COVERAGE.md lists the same paths and
declaration names for viewers that block local links.

Supporting claims. General endpoint attainment is proved in Attainment.lean. The module
EnvelopeFunctions.lean identifies those endpoints with the greatest convex underestimator and
least concave overestimator on both cubes and boxes. MonomialEnvelope.lean proves the full indi-
vidual lower-envelope identity in Lemma 2, including an attaining law preserving all ambient means.
PaperFoundations.lean checks original-box coefficient scaling and 0 ≤ HB ≤ TB . ExactSize.lean
proves all four size identities in (6), with the coordinate count inherited from FamilySize.lean,
and the stated sparsity bounds. ExactAsymptotics.lean proves |HL − log2 L| ≤ 3 for L ≥ 2 and
the actual family ratio limit in (16). Examples.lean proves all four rows of Table 1 as equalities
about the actual graph hull.

Reproduction. With Elan, Git, and Python 3 installed and the pinned toolchain on PATH, run
from formal/:

lake exe cache get
bash scripts/verify.sh

The script checks import coverage, builds with warnings as failures, audits the transitive axiom
dependencies of all bundled declarations, and replays the project declarations through Lean’s kernel.
Only propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound are permitted, so unfinished proofs and other
axioms are rejected; no solver or sampled numerical result is a trusted step.

Trust boundary and scope. The replay uses the installed Lean kernel and compiled dependencies;
it is not a separate kernel implementation and does not replay all of Mathlib from scratch. The
mathematical interpretation also depends on matching the formal definitions to the statements
above. The verification record specifies the actual local checks and their scope. The formal scope is
the disproof, the exact family gap and attainment, the finite bound (19), and the sharp leading
limits. Optimized fixed-point or Lambert W formulas, second-order asymptotics, coefficient removal,
and homogenization are outside this paper; no theorem here needs them.
