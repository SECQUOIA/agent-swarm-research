**Findings.**

1. **Minor — use absolute coefficients in the additive scalar error bound.** Location: `thm:a-design-independent`, lines 353–355. The instruction to refine endpoints until the sum of their rational coefficients times enclosure widths is below the error is incorrect literally when the objective has negative coefficients. For example, coefficients `(1,-1)` and widths `(1,1)` give signed sum zero although the allowed errors in the two terms can add to two. The stated theorem remains true, and this is a local correction rather than a structural gap: replace the stopping condition with `sum_C |gamma_C| width_C < epsilon`, or give a common root error `epsilon/[2(1+sum_C |gamma_C|)]`. The earlier weighted-cactus theorem already uses the latter valid form. This does not affect the new measurement theorem, which separately uses absolute generator coefficients and an absolute derivative bound.

2. **Minor — explicitly discharge zero nominations before applying the positive-width root theorem in the independent-cycle proof.** Location: lines 303–309, using the bracket established at lines 203–204. If `b=0`, then `B=0` and the displayed common bracket `[-B,B]` has zero width, whereas `thm:a-design-root` assumes `a<b`. The global-capacity proof explicitly handles this case at lines 264–266, and the later optimization proofs discuss it, but the independent-cycle proof invokes the root theorem without the corresponding exception. Add an opening sentence: when `B=0`, all circulations and flows are zero, capacities are constant checks, and any rational admissible profile supplies the endpoints and objective optimizer. Alternatively supply a nontrivial bracket such as `[-1,1]` in this case. This is a missing one-line case in the proof; it does not threaten the theorem or the extension.

**Exact root optimization audit.**

The affine parameter dependence is used in the right places. At a fixed rational query, both threshold tests are rational LPs. At any root, writing the parameter as a convex combination of vertices proves that some vertex root lies on either side. Consequently both extrema are attained at original vertices, even though the root map need not be affine or convex. This also establishes its quasilinear sublevel/superlevel property without an inverse derivative bound.

The full-rank active-row argument is valid for a lower-dimensional bounded H-polytope in its ambient `R^t`. If the active normals at a vertex had rank below `t`, a sufficiently small displacement in either direction along a common null vector would preserve all inactive strict inequalities as well as the active equalities. The point would not be a vertex. Thus the stated ambient Cramer bound applies to every vertex, including a singleton and vertices supported by implicit affine-hull equalities. It does not require an independently computed affine hull or full-dimensional interior.

The coefficient bounds are uniform over all vertices and pieces. Clearing all rational polynomial denominators by one product has polynomial bit length, and multiplication by a vertex's common denominator yields the displayed `(t+1) Delta P_0` height bound. Cancellation of leading terms can lower the degree but causes no problem. On every retained positive-width piece a specialized polynomial cannot vanish identically or be constant, by strict increase. Roots at fixed breakpoints are covered by adjacent piece polynomials. Joint continuity on the parameter domain suffices; nothing requires basis functions to agree outside that domain.

I checked the separation inequality independently. The product of two vertex-piece polynomials has degree at most `2D` and height at most `(D+1)H_0^2`. Its primitive squarefree part divides it in `Z[q]`, so the factor bound applies even with shared factors or multiplicities. The displayed `H_s` safely bounds its height. In the discriminant formula the selected root pair contributes `delta^2`; every other distance is at most `2(1+H_s)`. For `n<=2D`, replacing the remaining powers by the same base shows that exponent `4D^2` is safely conservative. The resulting number of bisection bits is polynomial in the numerical degree and input coefficient bits. Dense encoding, including explicit zeros, is therefore essential and sufficient when degree grows.

The final LP and equality handling are correct. For the maximum, true threshold queries preserve a lower endpoint at or below the optimum, including equality. A vertex minimizing `H(l,theta)` then has a root inside the final bracket; uniform separation forces it to attain the exact optimum. The minimum uses the opposite LP at the upper endpoint, as required. Starting-bracket endpoint optima and a bracket that is already sufficiently short also work.

The successive-coordinate extraction produces an original vertex, not merely a vertex of an arbitrary new slice. The first equality defines an optimal face of `P`; each subsequent coordinate minimization defines a face of that face, hence a face of `P`. Each fixed coordinate is the coordinate of an original vertex. This gives a common polynomial encoding bound for every LP, avoiding cumulative uncontrolled bit growth. At the last stage all coordinates are fixed. Piece selection, rational breakpoint zeros, squarefree extraction, and the selected rational isolating interval establish the promised degree and encoding contract. For this monotone setting, refinement can also use rational sign bisection directly, so repeated roots do not introduce a condition-number requirement. The `t=0` case is correctly separated.

**Hardness and measurement maximization audit.**

The Max-Cut reduction uses distinct entry/exit vertices on each triangle and bridges between successive triangles; it is simple and has maximum degree three. Unit source/sink nominations force unit through-flow in each block. The displayed direct-edge equation gives the full interval `[1/3,2/3]` under resistances `[1,16]`, independently across triangles. Fixed physical data and polynomial-magnitude expanded objective coefficients support strong hardness. Integer optimum rounding proves the stated absolute value-error claim; it is not silently converted to a different approximate-scenario output claim. Threshold NP membership and robust coNP membership are limited to the explicitly constructed family, where endpoint scenarios have rational flows and cut certificates.

For the new continuous measurement model, local capacities preserve an independent interval product with rational endpoint profiles. Applying `R` produces rational directions `a_C`, although interval endpoints and segment lengths can have algebraic degree growing with the dense law input. Hyperplane signs depend only on those rational directions. Homogeneous strict sign feasibility is exactly equivalent to the displayed rational inequalities with right-hand side one. Incremental insertion keeps polynomially many regions for fixed `k`; lower-dimensional dependence and repeated hyperplanes do not increase their count. Rational feasible LP points have polynomial bit length.

Every zonotope vertex has an ambient full-dimensional exposing cone even when the zonotope has a smaller affine hull: the normal directions orthogonal to that affine hull are unrestricted. An exposing direction avoiding finitely many nonzero-direction hyperplanes chooses endpoints of all positive-length segments. Zero-length generators only subdivide these cones, and zero measurement directions do not affect the projected point. This proves candidate completeness, including the point zonotope. Combining independent stored profiles produces original rational admissible parameters and retains exact capacities for every candidate.

The derivative estimate for `g` on the enlarged rational box does not require convexity on that box. The bounds `T_0`, `M_0`, and `delta` correctly imply measurement error at most `M_0 delta` and value error at most `epsilon/8`. Thus the polynomial-law extension has the stated `N^{O(k)}` runtime with polynomial dependence on accuracy bits.
