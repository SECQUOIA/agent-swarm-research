## Corrections to the packet and proof details

1. **Enumerating free LP supports is insufficient for polynomial recovery.** A box LP with `k` equalities has an optimal vertex with at most `k` non-bound coordinates, but choosing those coordinates leaves exponentially many possible lower/upper assignments. The proposed enumeration does not by itself prove the claimed running time. The replacement in `lem:a-blk-boxlp` enumerates the polynomially many support chambers of the fixed-dimensional zonotope at the recovered algebraic core. Their endpoint images contain every zonotope vertex. An attainable point is a convex combination of at most `k+2` affinely independent vertices in dimension `k+1`; enumerate those subsets, solve their fixed-size affine systems, and lift the weights to their endpoint resistance vectors. This produces an optimal feasible resistance vector in the same algebraic field without an algebraic-LP black box or an exponential endpoint enumeration. The output need not be an LP vertex. The proof includes redundant equations, ties, zero columns, singleton intervals, and lower-dimensional images.
2. **Membership adds an objective equality.** Exact membership of `(z,v)` has `k+1` linking rows, including the value row. Its small-instance independent LP oracle therefore enumerates free supports of size at most `k+1`. The statement about an optimal vertex of the original objective LP still has the correct bound `k`. These are different LPs.
3. **The maxima need a polynomial-size expansion.** Simply writing a maximum for each leaf does not finish the first-order complexity argument. The proof enumerates only realizable sign conditions of `eta^T W_e(z)` in fixed dimension, then writes the corresponding guarded sum. It neither introduces a variable for each maximum nor lists all formal sign assignments. The degree stays bounded in the box specialization. The first QE call has precisely the packet's variable count and polynomial-count exponent `(k+2)(d_0+2)`.
4. **Joint-model attribution.** The 2018 Aßmann et al. paper explicitly focuses on resistance uncertainty in Section 4.1.4. It is credited for that model and Proposition 4.7's circulation sign regions. The complete product of independent resistance intervals and a balanced nomination box is verified in Aßmann's 2019 thesis, Section 3.3, equations (3.2)--(3.4), printed p.56. The thesis is added to Paper A's bibliography. It is not represented as a verification of the separate 2019 Networks article.
5. **Exact local output versus sums.** Fixed-resistance inactive blocks have isolated real physical solutions in bounded circulation dimension; projection proves algebraicity without assuming a nonsingular Jacobian or an isolated complex solution. In joint optimization each local optimizer has its own common algebraic representation. Fields from an unbounded number of blocks are never merged. Rational intervals suffice for pressure values, whereas an arc objective needs only its single block and permits exact comparison.
6. **The convex-fiber remark is an arithmetic barrier.** Quadratic singleton fibers encode independent square roots. This proves that a proposed polynomial exact extension to arbitrary such convex fibers would decide SRS; it does not establish NP-hardness, impossibility of additive approximation, or necessity of literal boxes for every possible tractable extension.
7. **Rounding and degeneracy.** Nomination rounding uses a rational box intersected with exact balance and retained face equalities; greedy interval allocation is an explicit rational LP-feasibility method here. Resistance rounding is coordinatewise within its interval. The new state may leave the original sign cell. Both Lipschitz arguments compare the physical states of the two scenarios. The zero-core-load case permits nonzero off-core flows. Bridges use signed monotonicity; zero flows, singleton bounds, fixed faces, and capacity equality are explicit.
8. **Notation.** The rank bound is `mathfrak r`, retaining A1's `r` for total rank. Block exit `c_H`, path count `p_H`, dimension `d_H`, and box right-hand-side vector `c_0` avoid reassigning A1's potential weights, support size, and flow weights. Comparison resistances are `beta'`, preserving `gamma` for thresholds. Path signs are `omega_{H,e}`, distinct from A2's affine routing coefficient. New local symbols are listed in the section's opening paragraph.

## Verification design and limits

The deterministic seed is `2026090703`. The new checker tests:

- 24 randomly subdivided theta/K4 blocks, some with terminals originally of degree two. It verifies one-block structure, the marked-vertex and path bounds, and exact edge/interior partitions.
- Exact rational smoothed physical states constructed from potentials and signed flows, with positive resistances solved from the law. Fraction Gaussian elimination solves their grounded adjoints. Every current increment, ordinary-adjoint strict block order, unit current bound, and attained/gap threshold pattern is checked. A separate zero-flow cycle realizes the two-vertex plateau exactly; an equal-endpoint path checks the flat-path obstruction directly.
- 200 parametric zonotope membership comparisons at rational core values, with degree-two input coefficients, shifted and singleton intervals, zero columns, and rank-deficient systems. The support test checks affine-span membership and every signed facet normal in the actual span. It is compared to an independently implemented exact box-LP vertex oracle on these tiny systems. Coordinate-direction false positives for a diagonal segment and a slanted facet are explicit regressions. The oracle may enumerate all tiny bound assignments; it is not the claimed polynomial recovery algorithm.
- 24 resistance finite differences for the ordinary, unsmoothed terminal objective on random cacti and subdivided blocks. Constructed nonzero exact base states ensure positive derivative weights at zero smoothing. The independent A1 whole-graph solver is used with 90-digit Decimal arithmetic. The maximum derivative discrepancy is `8.778e-16`, below the `1e-10` acceptance tolerance.
- Eight tiny parallel-path interval families, comparing nomination endpoint faces and resistance corners against 17,576 grid scenarios and the A1 solver. These families have proved coordinate monotonicity; corner sufficiency is not asserted for arbitrary joint block problems. Exact rational signed-square tests also cover capacity equality and irrational rank-one roots. The target-resistance interval `[1,4]` with unit total flow and alternate resistance one has edge-flow maximizer at one and pressure maximizer at four. The inverse estimate and rational bridge cut intervals are checked separately.

The checker does not implement certified global QE. Its exact identities and independent tiny LP tests support the encodings; its grids and finite differences are numerical regression evidence. The complexity and certification claims rest on the manuscript proofs and A1's verified algebraic algorithms.

The initial new-checker run exposed an incorrectly chosen direct-edge resistance in the explicit plateau fixture. Setting it to `5/2` gives the exact grounded adjoint `[5,6,6,5,3,0]`. The fixture now asserts that full vector and verifies its zero-flow physical base state. No theorem changed.
