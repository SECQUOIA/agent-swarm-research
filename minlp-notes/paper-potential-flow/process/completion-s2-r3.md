## Findings

### Minor R3-1: the cited polynomial-size reduction concerns fixed-design feasibility

Location: `complexity/sections/04-laws.tex:305–307`.

The sentence says Thürauf, Grübel, and Schmidt “reduce robust design to polynomially many nonlinear worst-case problems.” Their reduction establishes robust-feasibility checking, or generation of a violating scenario, for a **fixed first-stage design**. Their complete design algorithm additionally solves a mixed-integer nonlinear master and iterates the adversarial procedure. The present sentence conflates those two tasks. Its following caveat about the cost of solving each nonlinear problem does not repair that distinction.

Evidence: the local primary manuscript `literature/papers/thurauf2025-adjustable-robust-nonlinear-network-design/fulltext.md`, Section 3, manuscript pp. 7–8, first describes the finite-scenario master and iterative enrichment. It then explicitly conditions the polynomial number of nonlinear problems on given first-stage decisions. Problems (5)–(7) and the following statement on p. 8 concern those fixed decisions. The published source is [Adjustable robust nonlinear network design without controllable elements under load scenario uncertainties](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-025-02207-2).

Suggested replacement:

> Thürauf et al. reduce robust-feasibility checking for a fixed design to polynomially many nonlinear worst-case problems and use these problems in an adversarial design algorithm; that reduction is not a polynomial-time solver for each problem.

This correction affects prior-work accuracy, not the section's theorem statements or proofs. There are no other major or minor findings.
