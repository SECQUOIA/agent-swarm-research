## Structural theorem audit

Theorem `thm:a-wcac-faces` needs both reductions. Pruning an objective-free pendant subnetwork preserves the exterior state after aggregating its nomination interval at the attachment. Greedy interval disaggregation supplies rational nominations with polynomial encoding length. The zero-induced-objective contraction is stronger: the displayed decomposition of the objective removes every component's dependence on its root potential precisely when every induced sum `gamma_v` vanishes. Exterior flows depend only on the nominations away from their roots. After splitting the contracted nomination, the effective block injections balance; existence and uniqueness then permit a physical lift. No rational-state claim is required for that lift, and arbitrary allowed removed coefficients suffice.

The incidence-tree argument prevents a contraction from identifying two distinct vertices of a surviving block. Support cannot increase. Induced objective sums on surviving blocks remain unchanged, so the zero-current test and later face patterns are coefficient independent. The proof correctly limits these reductions to unfiltered nomination boxes.

The support-spanning incidence subtree has only `O(p)` branching incidences. Consequently there are `O(p)` special blocks and ordinary chains. Whole physical blocks, including their nonsupport vertices, are retained. An ordinary chain has a known nonzero adjoint current: a zero current would make all induced sources of its blocks zero and trigger contraction. Positive differential resistances give strict ordering of port values, while the strong maximum principle and biconnectivity put each internal value strictly between the ports. This includes parallel-edge blocks; bridges simply have no internal vertices.

The balanced-box normal-cone condition supplies one multiplier even with singleton intervals or a lower-dimensional balanced domain. At most one ordinary block's open adjoint range contains that multiplier. An articulation equality is covered by leaving one adjacent selected block free. Shared vertices remain free whenever either incident selected or special block needs them. Thus coarse-face enumeration is polynomial for fixed support and does not depend on the unknown coefficient values.

The two limiting stages occur in the necessary order. Uniform physical-flow and potential bounds, a spanning-tree feasibility correction, and strict convexity justify continuity and uniform smoothing convergence on the compact box. One coarse face is fixed before the positive-source perturbation. Within that face, marking ports, support, and degree-at-least-three vertices leaves `O(rp)` paths: the degree identity gives at most `2r_B-2` high-degree vertices, and Euler's formula gives `r_B+k_B-1` paths. The perturbation gives each unmarked path interior source exactly `delta`; increasing successive currents produce a unimodal adjoint profile with at most two vertices at any horizontal level, including a possible two-vertex plateau. The lower/free/upper/free/lower endpoint pattern therefore leaves at most two free interiors per path. Refining every coarse face preserves the overall `n^{O(rp)}` count. A second fixed-face subsequence gives an original global optimum. Freezing an attained joint coefficient maximizer establishes the structural joint assertion without claiming a block-objective algorithm.

## Fixed-law formulas and bit complexity

The spanning-tree objective flow has rational entries of polynomial size, and `Aw=c` gives the weighted-drop identity. Reorienting an edge requires reversing both its offset and objective weight and swapping its directional coefficients; the section does all three. Cactus cycles have disjoint edges, so their scalar cycle equations can be solved independently at a common nomination and the resulting drops integrate globally.

For Lemma `lem:a-wcac-local`, a fixed-dimensional arrangement of `q+ell_e(z)` gives polynomially many sign regions. Their closed branches cover zero flows. On a branch the cycle polynomial has constant quadratic coefficient, affine linear coefficient, and quadratic constant coefficient. At a physical solution, its derivative is a sum of nonnegative terms `2 beta_e |x_e|`. This proves the plus-root selector even when the quadratic coefficient is negative. Substitution into a quadratic drop yields a quadratic polynomial plus an affine multiple of a quadratic discriminant's square root; no variable denominator is introduced in this case.

When the quadratic coefficient vanishes, the nonsingular physical branch has positive linear coefficient and is rational. If that coefficient also vanishes, every physical cycle flow vanishes. The explicit all-zero chart is therefore sufficient, including coincidences of offsets and lower-dimensional nomination domains. The proof does not assume a positive lower bound on a variable denominator. Eligibility uses the original sign inequalities, root selector, and circulation bound before projecting only one scalar. With fixed `d`, the cited elimination bounds keep output degrees bounded independently of cycle length, and polynomial count and coefficient sizes remain polynomial.

The square-root panels are constructive. On `[4^(-j-1),4^(-j)]`, the stated rational center has rational square root and relative deviation at most `7/9`. Bounding binomial coefficients by one gives the geometric-series remainder claimed in the proof. The zero panel controls the nonsmooth endpoint. For scaling by `S`, the relevant precision is `log(S/eta)`, polynomial because `S` has polynomial bit length. Expanding a panel can produce coefficients with `O(jK)` contributions to their bit lengths, which remain polynomial in accuracy bits. Substituting a quadratic discriminant gives degree at most twice the panel degree; dense expansion remains polynomial only because nomination dimension is fixed, as explicitly assumed.

The tolerance sums over all charts or candidates, a safe polynomial overcount. Coefficient bounds on a rational box containing `P` give polynomial-bit bounds on each radical coefficient and discriminant. No condition number or radical separation bound is needed.

The common partition includes all exact eligibility tests, panel boundaries, denominator tests, and bridge sign hyperplanes. Realizable sign conditions can be represented by their sign conjunctions even when disconnected. Eligibility and the selected formulas are then constant on each part. The construction avoids an exponential product of local chart choices and explicitly retains lower-dimensional and singular parts.

For a part with `N` local rational pieces, multiplying denominators adds their degrees. The logarithm of the coefficient growth is bounded by the sum of input coefficient bit bounds plus polynomial convolution overhead; it does not grow exponentially in `N`. There are polynomially many monomials at the resulting polynomial degree in fixed dimension. Squared-denominator threshold tests preserve sign and retain all nonzero conditions. Hence the later fixed-dimensional elimination is polynomial in the now-growing numerical degree, coefficient size, and number of tests. This is a dense-encoding accuracy-bit argument, not a sparse-exponent claim.

Open parts are treated correctly. Existential strict-threshold tests optimize their suprema, and the bounded physical objective plus uniform surrogate error bounds those suprema even near a vanishing denominator. Bisection width `eps/8`, radical error `a=eps/16`, and sampling above `L-eps/16` give physical loss at most `5 eps/16`. The enclosing interval has width at most `eps/4`. Strictly lowering the sampling threshold ensures a sample exists even when a supremum is unattained or equals a bisection endpoint.

The nomination continuity proof is graph independent: nonzero difference flows follow a strictly decreasing potential difference and hence form an acyclic flow of total path weight `||b-b'||_1/2`. The quadratic law is Lipschitz on the uniformly bounded flow interval, including across zero. This yields the stated `K_b`. Rational LP in `P` intersected with the coordinate-isolation box preserves exact balance and feasibility even for lower-dimensional `P`. Rounding need not preserve a chart or denominator condition. The allocated additional loss `eps/4` leaves ample margin within the theorem's tolerance.

## Joint optimization and separate-field recovery

The one-row box LP is the correct local problem. On a fixed sign region, its reduced costs are controlled by `w_e-lambda`; nonzero untied terms choose the stated endpoints, while tied contributions fill one interval. Thus two quadratic inequalities characterize a threshold's domain, regardless of tie-group size. LP duality and the piecewise-affine dual function guarantee a threshold among the edge weights, including flat dual intervals or tails. All-zero flows, singleton intervals, and ties are expressly covered.

For a fixed nomination, the circulation domain of a sign-region/threshold pair is closed and bounded. Every optimum is at a boundary, at a stationary point of a nonflat quadratic, or on a flat objective. A boundary must activate a specialized constraint that is nonconstant in circulation; identically zero constraints do not suffice. The proof enumerates both roots of every genuine quadratic boundary, handles a vanishing linear coefficient without division, includes rational stationary points, and gives flat objectives an existential feasibility domain. The root selectors remain correct for negative leading coefficient and double roots. Artificial circulation bounds cover the compact full-domain flat case.

Candidate eligibility is projected before approximation. Comparisons added to the common partition are between rational surrogates of the same cycle. Selecting each cycle's largest eligible surrogate and then summing keeps both the number of comparisons and the parameter dimension polynomial. The maximum-of-approximants inequality supplies uniform conditional-optimum error `a`; the selected actual candidates differ from that surrogate sum by at most another `a`. The `5 eps/16` sampled-scenario accounting already includes this selection loss; it does not omit a second maximization error.

Recovery returns original coefficients. Tied contribution intervals are filled greedily and divided by `f_e` only when nonzero. At most one tied coordinate needs to be interior. All coordinates of the common nomination sample lie in one polynomial-degree real algebraic representation. Each nonflat cycle adds at most a quadratic extension; each flat cycle requires only a local fixed-dimensional algebraic sample. Reducing arithmetic in each local extension and isolating its output coordinates keeps polynomial representation size. These local operations consist of polynomially many bounded-degree expressions and interval sums, not arbitrary repeated exponentiation. There is no operation requiring the compositum of all cycle fields. Shared nominations and independent cycle closure suffice for a global physical state.

## New directional-coefficient extension

This strengthening is supported by arguments in the frozen section itself, rather than by the symmetric promoted result.

On a nonzero-flow branch, only its directional coefficient enters the physical law or objective. The opposite coefficient may take any allowed rational value. At zero, both contributions vanish, so either incident sign region and arbitrary original allowed coefficients give the same physical value. Reorientation swaps positive and negative intervals as well as changing offsets and weights. The symmetric model is handled as one original coordinate per edge; the proof correctly does not replace its diagonal constraint by two independent variables.

The all-graph coefficient continuity proof uses smoothing `g^rho=g+rho x` independent of the coefficients. This choice leaves parameter derivatives exactly `x|x|` in the symmetric case and `x_+^2`, `-x_-^2` in the directional case. The smoothed laws are jointly continuously differentiable at zero, and the positive differential Laplacian permits the adjoint identity there. Unit terminal-adjoint currents have magnitude at most one by acyclic path decomposition. Each original coefficient derivative is therefore bounded by `B_0^2`. Integrating within the original box and passing to the uniformly continuous unsmoothed limit proves the terminal estimate; normalizing one potential and summing with `|c_v|` proves the weighted estimate. Sign changes introduce no omitted derivative jump.

Rounding acts on `m` shared coordinates or `2m` independent coordinates as appropriate. Singleton intervals remain exact. Polynomial-bit coordinate widths enforce the stated objective loss, and separate local algebraic isolation suffices. A rounded state may switch the active direction; the continuity bound already covers this, so branch-preserving rounding is unnecessary.

The capacity-rounding argument also survives sign crossing. For a fixed asymmetric law, `|g(u)-g(v)| >= beta_L |u-v|^2/2` follows on opposite signs from the sum-of-squares inequality. At a fixed flow, changing the original coefficients by sup-norm `delta` changes the law by at most `B_0^2 delta`, since only one direction is active. Combining this with the terminal drop estimate gives the displayed square-root flow bound with `k+1`. The chosen widths have polynomial bit length in `eps` and `sigma`; no inverse-flow Lipschitz assumption near zero is used.

## Filters, exact profiles, examples, and implementation scope

The balanced-box corollary applies the structural theorem only to the unfiltered box, then uses fixed-dimensional optimization on polynomially many faces. Selecting the face with largest certified lower endpoint and using the smaller local tolerance gives the requested global guarantee. Rational disaggregation preserves the value after reversing reductions.

For fixed-dimensional local arc capacities, eligibility projections preserve exact feasibility, and closedness plus compact original parameter boxes gives attained extrema. Fixed-law bounded-degree polynomial filters also fit because substitution uses only the common nomination and one cycle circulation. Arbitrary joint-coefficient polynomial filters are deliberately excluded because they can create higher-degree circulation boundaries. The theorem promises algebraic exactly feasible parameters before rounding. Its rational slack result compares only with the tightened optimum and does not claim preservation of arbitrary polynomial filters or closeness to the original optimum.

The irrational-nomination example is correct: the two unit capacities force `q=1`, conservation gives the stated affine nomination, and the cycle equation becomes `t^2-2=0`. Thus strictly positive rational capacities can force a unique irrational feasible parameter.

At fixed rational nominations, all sign/capacity boundaries and nonflat stationary circulations are rational. An irrational boundary can come only from an aggregate feasibility quadratic, where equality forces all nonzero tied contributions to their interval endpoints. This gives a rational original resistance profile even with irrational circulation. The directional model recovers its inactive coordinates rationally; the symmetric model retains one shared coordinate. Flat cases choose a boundary, so they reduce to the same two classifications. Pairwise local value comparison reduces to the sign of one radical after a squared-magnitude comparison, with zero cases separated. It does not require factoring radicands or comparing a growing global sum.

The interior-optimum example has the claimed incidence, signed drops, value `-12`, and unique profile `(1,2,1,1)` in the symmetric model. The dual bound fixes `q=-1` and the strict untied reduced costs fix the other three resistances; conservation then fixes the second. The example is not presented as a unique optimizer of the independent directional model, whose unused coefficients would remain free.

The implementation paragraph accurately limits `code/potential_flow_mpd/exact_weighted_cactus.py` to supplied symmetric blocks at fixed nominations.
