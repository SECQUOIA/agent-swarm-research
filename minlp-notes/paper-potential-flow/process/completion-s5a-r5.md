**Findings.** No major findings.

1. **Minor — use absolute objective coefficients in the scalar error bound.** Location: lines 353–355, in the proof of the independent-cycle theorem. The text says to refine until the sum of each rational coefficient times its enclosure width is below the requested error. With signed coefficients this is not an error certificate: for an objective `-q`, a positive-width enclosure gives a negative such sum regardless of its accuracy. Replace the criterion by `sum_C |gamma_C| width_C <= eps`, or give each endpoint error at most `eps/[2(1+sum_C |gamma_C|)]`. This is a local correction to the described stopping rule. It preserves the polynomial bit bound and the exact optimizing-scenario result. The other error budgets in the section already use nonnegative bounds correctly.

2. **Minor — dispatch zero nominations before invoking the root theorem in the independent-cycle proof.** Location: lines 303–309; compare lines 203–204, 264–266, 442–445, and 633–636. The independent theorem allows `B=0`, but its proof directly invokes the monotone-root theorem using the preceding bracket `[-B,B]`. That theorem explicitly requires `a<b`. The later minimization proof checks `B=0` only after calling the independent theorem, and the measurement proof also starts with that theorem. Add at the beginning of the independent proof: if `B=0`, all physical flows and circulations are zero, check capacities at zero and choose any rational point of each independent nonempty parameter polytope; otherwise use the nondegenerate bracket. Alternatively explicitly inherit the zero-flow treatment from the global-capacity proof. This is a proof-order omission, not a counterexample: passivity and uniqueness already establish the required fixed zero state.

**Mathematical coverage and reasoning.**

1. **Monotone root optimization, lines 27–154.** I checked both threshold equivalences, including equality; the vertex-attainment argument; lower-dimensional polytopes; all displayed determinant, coefficient, factor, and separation bounds; both final LP recovery steps; lexicographic extraction; and the final algebraic encoding. Affinity in parameters is used in exactly the places it is needed: the LP tests and the convex combination of vertex values.    Clearing all denominators by products has polynomial logarithmic cost. The substituted polynomial height is bounded by `(t+1) Delta P_0` without enumerating vertices. The primitive squarefree part of a product of two vertex-piece polynomials is an integer factor, including when the polynomials coincide or share factors. The displayed discriminant argument bounds distances between any two distinct roots of that squarefree part. With `n<=2D`, its exponent is safely dominated by the proposed `4D^2` exponent. Thus the bound is conservative but adequate. Dense encoding is essential because the required precision is polynomial in numerical degree, not its logarithm.

   At the final lower bisection endpoint for maximization, an LP-minimizing vertex has a root between that endpoint and the global maximum. The interval is shorter than the uniform separation bound, so the two vertex roots coincide. The reversed construction for minimization works as well. Successive coordinate minimization stays on faces of the original polytope; coordinate bit lengths therefore inherit the original vertex bound rather than an uncontrolled recursive bound. Breakpoint roots and initial bracket endpoint roots cause no equality gap. The `t=0` case is explicitly separated. 7. **Fixed measurements and the new extension, lines 615–718.** The polynomial-law theorem supplies exactly the ingredients required for the extension: independent constrained circulation intervals; polynomial-size separate algebraic endpoint encodings with local degree bounds; and rational original profiles attaining every endpoint. Capacities do not alter the rational directions `a_C=R Z_C`. They only change lengths and endpoint profiles. No step assumes degree two or solves a joint algebraic system across cycles.

   The central arrangement depends only on the rational directions. The feasibility system `sigma_C h^T a_C>=1` is equivalent to strict sign feasibility by positive rescaling of a direction. Rational LP supplies bounded-bit representatives even though the cones are unbounded. Inserting hyperplanes and retaining all feasible sign patterns enumerates every full-dimensional region in fixed dimension, within `N^{O(k)}` time. Zero generators can be discarded. Repeated directions, linear dependencies, and positive or zero segment lengths cause no lost vertex. A vertex of a lower-dimensional zonotope has a full-dimensional cone of uniquely exposing directions in ambient space; choosing outside finitely many generator hyperplanes selects its positive-length segment endpoints. A point zonotope is covered as well. Combining the selected endpoint profiles remains feasible in the original independent model.

   Every measurement lies in the rational magnitude box from `T_0`. The derivative bound on its one-unit expansion follows from dense polynomial coefficients, regardless of whether the objective is convex outside the promised box. The promised convexity box contains the zonotope; in the finite case it contains the convex hull of the attainable set because a box is convex. Separate endpoint errors bounded by `delta` give coordinate error at most `M_0 delta` and value error at most `eps/8`. Choosing the largest rational estimate loses at most twice that amount. The algorithm returns the stored exact parameter scenario, not the approximate root vector, so capacity feasibility remains exact. Degree growth and the bit lengths of `T_0`, `M_0`, the derivative bound, and the required root precision are polynomial in dense input length. The candidate count multiplies a polynomial root-refinement and evaluation cost. The conclusion is additive optimization, not exact candidate ordering or an exact optimizer, and its XP dependence is stated correctly.

Independent scalar sets give a product convex hull. This justifies the finite branch only without capacity filtering, precisely as stated. It does not justify finite target realization or convex minimization.
