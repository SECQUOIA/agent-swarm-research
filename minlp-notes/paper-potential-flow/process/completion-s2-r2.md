## Mathematical audit

- **Admissibility, lines 14–88.** Taking the union of breakpoints has polynomial size and does not enumerate a Cartesian product. On a refined interval, derivative signs determine the minimizing coefficient endpoints. The product used for root isolation has polynomial degree and coefficient length, with identically zero derivatives removed. The fixed-endpoint derivative is then tested on whole intervals, not merely at sample points. Continuity covers partition roots and one-sided piece boundaries. After nondecrease is established, failure of strict increase is exactly constancy on some nontrivial interval; polynomial identity on a refined piece reduces this to the stated rational LP. This correctly accepts isolated derivative zeros and rejects flat pieces, including unbounded flat pieces. Zero bases, fixed coefficients, signed coefficient intervals, and growing dense degrees do not invalidate the argument.

- **Structural transfer, lines 91–140.** Centered convolution preserves the zero-flow normalization and monotonicity, and the added linear term makes all differentiated resistances positive. The acyclic-flow bound supplies one compact flow interval independently of the smoothing parameter. Joint continuity on a compact parameter set provides uniform constitutive bounds and uniform convergence. Bounded normalized potentials and uniqueness justify the subsequence proof of uniform state convergence. The ordering and forced-unimodality arguments in Section 3 only need positive differentiated resistances, so they transfer. The two perturbations are used for existence of an optimizer on a closed enumerated face; the algorithm does not need a computable smoothing rate. Fixing the parameters of a joint maximizer before replacing its nomination is valid.

- **Growing degrees and box elimination, lines 142–173.** In fixed dimension, the realizable support-sign list, the guarded formula, quantifier elimination, and an optimal sample all retain polynomial size when numerical degree grows polynomially. The original box lemma recovers the many interval leaves through support cells and convex combinations of at most `k+2` image vertices. Its treatment of zero columns, singleton intervals, redundant equations, and lower-dimensional zonotopes is sufficient. All local coordinates and recovered leaves remain in one algebraic field of polynomial degree; no compositum over separately encoded roots is needed. Dense encoding is essential and is explicitly required.

- **Sensitivity and polynomial-law optimization, lines 175–300.** The displayed coefficient and derivative bounds have polynomial bit length. The adjoint has absolute edge current at most one and a potential range bounded by any unit terminal path's resistance. Centering accounts for the factor two in coefficient sensitivity. The breakpoint arrangement uses closed cells with formulas agreeing at every shared boundary, including zero-dimensional feasible sets and constant affine flow forms. Independent coefficients and fixed aggregated nominations outside the selected block justify separate local optimization. Rational interval sums avoid comparing independently encoded algebraic sums. Intersecting isolating boxes with rational face constraints and exact balance gives a rational feasible nomination, and the sensitivity bound controls the new physical state after rounding. Aggregation and disaggregation are law-independent. For an arc, fixing its coefficients makes flow optimization equivalent to endpoint-drop optimization over nominations; using the affine flow objective afterward correctly allows those coefficients to vary. Reversing the ordered terminal objective for a minimum does not assume odd laws. Only one block contributes to an arc extremum, so its exact algebraic representation and equality-inclusive rational capacity comparison remain polynomial.

- **Exact signed-power obstruction, lines 314–376.** The cycle construction gives integral balanced nominations and a strictly increasing scalar circulation equation. The direction of every equivalence, including equality, is correct. Reversing the final edge converts the lower test into a rational upper-capacity test. A common positive integer clears coefficient denominators without changing the circulation root. For fixed reduced `p/q` with even `q`, `p` is odd, and the proposed data give exactly `a_i^(1/2)` at zero circulation. Fixed exponents keep all binary encodings polynomial. The claim remains SRS-hardness, without an unsupported NP-hardness claim or a claim about odd denominators.

- **Positive rational approximation, lines 378–496.** Both tail estimates are uniform for `0 <= t <= 1`. The disk used for Taylor estimation lies in the right half-plane and avoids the branch cut and all poles. Cauchy's estimate gives the stated geometric remainder; exactness and positive quadrature weights bound the panel error by twice that remainder. Summing the panel bounds yields the displayed `16 L 2^(L-2n)` bound. Linear growth of both `L` and `n` in accuracy bits suffices. Nodes, weights, and their fixed rational powers admit individual polynomial-size algebraic descriptions. Upward rational rounding preserves positivity, and its derivative estimate is uniform even as `t` approaches zero. Normalization uses an explicit positive lower bound and only exact rational arithmetic. Expanding the polynomially many positive linear denominators has polynomial degree and coefficient length.

- **Extension above exponent three, lines 498–537.** Every nonodd rational `q > 1` has the stated representation `q = 2j+1+2 alpha`, with fixed `j >= 0` and `0 < alpha < 1`. Choosing the dyadic scale exponent as a multiple of the denominator of `2 alpha` makes the scale rational with polynomial bit length. The approximation error is bounded by `T^(2 alpha) B^(2j+1) tau`. Each positive rational summand is a multiple of `x^(2j+3)/(x^2+c)`; the derivative formula is correct and strictly positive away from zero. The resulting law is smooth, strictly increasing globally, odd, and unbounded, with a globally positive denominator. Odd integers are handled exactly. No property used here requires `q < 3`.

- **Rational local optimization, lines 539–573.** Clearing the product of globally positive denominators preserves the feasible set. Total degree is a sum of input degrees, and the number of monomials remains polynomial in fixed core dimension. Introducing the objective value as one extra bounded core coordinate and one linking equation preserves fixed dimension. The supplied law bounds give the required rational objective bound. This correctly reuses the growing-degree box lemma.

- **Physical error and rational witnesses, lines 575–751.** The bounds `M`, `N_0`, and `c_*` are valid for both small and large `B`. Signed powers satisfy the displayed monotonicity inequality, including opposite signs. Pairing the two states with their common conservation circulation yields the stated identity. Selecting an edge attaining the maximum flow error gives `D^(q_e) <= eta^H`; the separate cases `D <= 1` and `D > 1` correctly handle mixed exponents and establish `D <= eta`. The path pressure bound follows. Original-law input sensitivity uses a linear regularization independent of the resistance parameter, so its derivative contains only the original signed power. The inverse estimate includes the target resistance's own rounding error. Requiring its right-hand side to be at most `xi^H` is sufficient for every exponent in the fixed family. These fixed powers require polynomially many precision bits. The final choices of `eta` and `delta` provide uniform surrogate error `a` for both objectives. The enlarged interval has width at most `3 eps/8`, and the four witness losses total at most `eps/2`. Arc recovery starts from an arc optimizer. Exact balance is retained by rational LP, and aggregate-first disaggregation makes off-core coordinate changes irrelevant to the objective.

The cases of bridges, trees, parallel edges, fixed nominations, singleton uncertainty intervals, vanishing flows, zero sensitivity terms, and zero objectives are either explicitly handled or covered by the cited constructions. The theorem appropriately distinguishes exact algebraic arc outputs for polynomial/rational surrogates from additive outputs for the original fractional laws. It makes no unsupported claim about polynomial complexity when exponents are arbitrary binary input.
