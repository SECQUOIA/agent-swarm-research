## Complete mathematical reading and assessment

I read the abstract, introduction, all six files in `complexity/narrative/`, conclusion, bibliography, macros, main inclusion structure, and every line of all eleven technical appendix files. Counting theorem, lemma, proposition, and corollary environments gives **106 formal technical results**: A 9, B 8, C 11, D 12, E 11, F 14, G 11, H 9, I 8, J 7, and K 6. The assessments below cover their proofs and surrounding examples, not merely those selected for the main text.

**A — Preliminaries (`01-preliminaries.tex`, 9 results).** The primitive energy proof really covers bounded continuous strictly increasing laws: positivity of the laws at fixed positive and negative arguments supplies a linear coercivity bound for the primitive, so surjectivity is unnecessary. Strict convexity gives the unique feasible flow; the multiplier equation gives potentials up to the stated gauges. Orientation reversal changes the directional laws correctly. Physical acyclicity and the supply bound apply to the physical state rather than arbitrary conserved trial flows. The block-incidence construction, independent local balance aggregation, potential-drop addition, and disaggregation give exact identities, with correct treatment of bridges. The adjoint/sensitivity argument is confined to the differentiable positive-derivative setting when used, and later smoothing arguments remove that restriction. The fixed-dimensional algebraic convention, rational versus algebraic outputs, and separate-field warning are explicit. The main model section correctly carries these conventions forward.

**B — Cactus pressure optimization and arithmetic (`02-cactus.tex`, 8 results).** The two-pivot and active-cycle face arguments correctly use maximum-principle structure after smoothing and pass to a compact limit. They do not accidentally freeze coefficient choices outside the active nomination block. The additive algorithm treats inactive cycle contributions separately; its local algebraic computations do not require a single field containing all cycle radicals. The nomination recovery estimate supplies rational feasible inputs without asserting rational physical states. I checked both directions of the source/sink Square-Root-Sum equivalence, including the radical encoder, simple bounded-degree cactus realization, weak equality case, positive data, and polynomial binary encoding after scaling. The upper reduction is confined to the single-source/sink family. Neither the appendix nor main section 3 labels general exact cactus MPD as settled or identifies SRS-hardness with NP-hardness.

**C — Fixed maximum block rank (`03-block-rank.tex`, 11 results).** The suppression topology and perturbed adjoint force the required unimodality along long paths. The resulting bounds of `8r-2` free nominations and `9r-3` core size have the right role; enumeration of closed faces retains boundary and limiting optima. Local nonlinear variables remain bounded in number while many coefficient variables are eliminated using the convex image of their boxes. Only realizable support signs are used, and the zonotope/Carathéodory step supplies actual preimages. Uniform joint rounding and local leaf recovery do not introduce a global algebraic compositum. The exact individual-arc corollary stays local to one block even when total rank is unbounded. The main theorem preserves the fixed-maximum-rank qualification and the distinction between pressure approximation and exact algebraic arc extrema. The bounds are parameter-dependent polynomial exponents, not an FPT claim.

**D — Polynomial and fractional laws (`04-laws.tex`, 12 results).** The dense representation is substantive: growing degree and piece count are charged to input size, and binary sparse degrees are excluded. The admissibility procedure checks continuity and uses parameter-linear derivative tests together with the exclusion of flat pieces to validate strict monotonicity; nonnegative derivative alone is not silently equated to strict increase. Denominator clearing preserves signs on the stated positive denominators. The fractional construction retains positive rational weights, controls the normalization without assuming an exact transcendental constant, and yields an increasing approximate law. The physical stability estimate covers the small and large discrepancy regimes and allows flows to reverse or vanish. Fixed exponents, including the finite-family extension, have the stated range and quantifiers. The `3/2` one-cycle SRS encoding establishes an exact-comparison barrier, and the further exponent discussion retains its parity condition. The main law summary does not extend these bit bounds to unrestricted exponent input.

**E — Finite uncertainty, individual-arc topology, and hardness (`05-boundaries.tex`, 11 results).** I checked the pressure gadget's interior maximum and its explicit Subset-Sum gap, including the dependence on `31K+18S`. The weakly conducting probe uses a quantified feedback bound, giving the asserted rank-three arc gap rather than an informal zero-resistance limit. Guessing finite resistance choices followed by the exact bounded-rank arc algorithm establishes the claimed restricted NP/coNP memberships; it does not give membership for arbitrary pressure sums. The finite secant comparison has the right orientation and target-edge sign. The positive series-parallel conclusion uses adjacent-terminal electrical signs, whose primary-source hypothesis I checked. The universal negative direction restores positive rational resistances on subdivisions and extra edges, with a controlled gap. The envelope network's conic formulation and endpoint recovery distinguish approximate physical signs from certified scenario loss. The cactus-plus-probe arithmetic family has a matching SRS upper bound, while the general SP statement is only SRS-hard. The nomination-hardness corollary correctly credits Thür­auf and does not add a general NP certificate. Finally, prescribed-flow interval realization is an LP; finite choices and existential unit capacities on one cycle give the separate NP-complete family. Main section 4 and table 2 preserve these quantifiers and rank boundaries.

**F — Weighted objectives and universal topology (`06-weighted.tex`, 14 results).** The tree objective is a piecewise rational quadratic with a polynomial-size rational maximizer certificate on a minimal optimal face. The degree-three comb reduction has the stated `K-1/2` separation; growing support is essential. Zero objective cut-flow contraction, signed orientation, and marked vertices give the correct threshold-face enumeration, including degree-two reversals. The fixed-support bound and its signed-path refinement are stated as XP guarantees. The global-rank proof handles both signs of source forcing and returning paths, and fixes total rank rather than maximum rank per block. I checked the weighted-potential triangle and weighted-flow theta interior extrema and the discrete encodings derived from them. The unit-total-flow DAG subdivision accounts for its vertices and edges and separates resistance scaling from flow-gap amplification. The universal hull hierarchy is proved with positive-resistance restoration. The full-region nonconvexity argument uses injective coordinates and deletion/projection logic rather than inferring nonconvexity from a scalar interior extremum alone. The nonlinear-power extension is qualitative for each fixed exponent; no uniform bit bound is claimed. Main table 1 and the weighted introduction correctly distinguish weighted potentials, pairwise drops, linear flows, and individual arcs.

**G — Weighted cactus algorithms (`07-weighted-cactus.tex`, 11 results).** The weighted face theorem contracts zero-source substructures and uses a two-stage perturbation with the correct order of limiting arguments. For the local cycle optimization, the quadratic branches, endpoints, stationary points, zeros, ties, and flat branches cover the full candidate set. The one-equality coefficient LP uses sign-dependent contributions and threshold ordering; equal thresholds are handled by residual allocation rather than arbitrary untied endpoint choices. Candidate generation includes specializations where coefficients vanish or the stationary equation loses degree. The compiler compares surrogates within each cycle and then sums on the same original nomination coordinate; it does not choose independent nominations across blocks or enumerate all combinations of cycle choices. Error budgets include surrogate selection and subsequent rational recovery. Directional coefficients use the physically active coefficient and do not create independent copies of a shared symmetric coefficient. The local-capacity theorem promises algebraic feasible output in general and rational output only through the stated tightened-optimum guarantee. The fixed-rational-nomination theorem separately proves exact rational optimizing coefficient profiles, even when circulation values are irrational, and the interior-optimum example prevents an unjustified endpoint interpretation. Main section 5 states these output boundaries accurately.

**H — Continuous design and scalar root optimization (`08-design.tex`, 9 results).** The monotone-root LP method handles arbitrary parameter dimension by bounding the bits of all possible rational polytope vertices and separating their polynomial roots. Bisection and a threshold LP recover an exactly optimizing rational parameter vector, not just an approximate algebraic state; repeated roots, fixed breakpoints, and equality thresholds are included. Fixed target-flow realization and capacities reduce to rational inequalities in the intended original parameters. The surrogate-box construction preserves exact feasibility by storing rational endpoint profiles, including narrow or singleton intervals whose physical circulation is irrational. The convex quadratic and dense-polynomial extensions use the explicit Lipschitz extension and exact-feasible convex optimization contract appropriately. The fixed-measurement maximization proof uses rational directions and algebraic interval lengths, requiring only additive comparison of candidates. Finite sets preserve the unfiltered convex maximum through their hull, while existential filters are explicitly outside that equivalence. The independent-cycle hypotheses are retained in the joint convex-design result; the later globally correlated arc theorem is a different result. Main section 6 gives the right min/max distinctions.

**I — Global correlations, dissipation, and rational-witness limits (`09-correlations-energy.tex`, 8 results).** The global-correlation capacity filters are affine constraints on the original shared polytope. The paired-triangle reductions encode Max-Cut with bounded data and fixed gaps; the energy construction removes parameter-dependent bridge bias rather than absorbing it into a supposedly constant threshold. Constitutive dissipation equals terminal drop in the unit-throughput setting used, while total positive arc flow is not confused with delivered throughput. The dissipation maximization dual has the correct conjugate and two-cone representation, including zero values. Compact bounds, a rational relative interior point, and a centered convex-body formulation support the accuracy-bit algorithm; a numerical SOCP status is not its proof. The ordinary upper-capacity example actually forces the irrational resistance `7-4 sqrt(2)`. Both perturbation estimates are valid at zeros and reversals by their regularization/finite-comparison arguments; the sharper estimate improves the constant rather than changing its hypotheses. Strict-margin recovery over a rational global polytope is conditional on the margin and retains the original linear constraints through the rounding LP. The pressure-filter example demonstrates nonconvexity of a physical filter and is not used to contradict the distinct concave-dissipation feasible-set result. Main section 6.3 correctly warns that closed physical filters may remove every rational parameter point.

**J — Beyond cactus blocks (`10-weighted-blocks.tex`, 7 results).** The local-value graph has the claimed fixed-dimensional semialgebraic interface. In the scalar approximation lemma, a product of value-dependent formula polynomials vanishes on the graph away from finitely many specialization points, and continuity extends the identity. Taking a primitive squarefree factor and using complex critical roots is essential; the proof includes both real and nonreal roots through their real parts. Bad-band widths and Lipschitz control cover the excluded neighborhoods. On the remaining panels, root separation, leading-coefficient control, and analytic continuation give a uniform complex bound. The interpolation proof charges coefficient growth and node approximation to degree and requested accuracy, so growing dense degree is genuinely handled. Independent local approximations are added in one common scalar coordinate with polynomially many pieces. The hybrid theorem charges the *total* noncactus rank to the nonlinear dimension and permits unbounded cactus rank. Rational recovery has the right original-domain contract. The displayed several-parameter unrestricted-higher-block sum is an external remaining question; no claimed theorem depends on an unproved resolution. The main weighted summary states the scalar and hybrid results separately and does not hide that distinction behind a general bounded-block claim.

**K — Certified computation (`11-certified-computation.tex`, 6 results).** The rational energy/conjugate inequalities imply the stated cubic flow error bound with directional coefficients selected by sign. The reverse Bregman expression puts the unknown physical state in the second argument; conservation cancels the linear term and makes the sum equal to the energy gap. The resulting root intervals are outward rational certificates. The support dual uses nonnegative multipliers, exact conservation, and the correct conjugate, with rational completeness under strict energy slack; zero-gap exact states are treated separately. Conserved-curvature bounds allow zero edges and singular gauges. The obstruction is exactly the relevant nullspace intersection, and the rational KKT system computes the finite case without an invalid inverse on a singular matrix. Sharpness is for the conserved quadratic error set, not an assertion that every physical error attains the bound. The two-path example has matching rational upper and lower support witnesses and correctly contrasts its width with the separate-edge Bregman interval. The original-instance pipeline checks the uncertainty-to-envelope mapping as well as deterministic witnesses, handles unresolved signs and the zero-adjoint/zero-load cases, and preserves scaling. Main section 7 accurately separates a certificate verifier from a generic polynomial-time numerical producer.

## Literature and bounded novelty assessment

I used actual original PDFs, not local literature summaries or abstracts as substitutes for theorem inspection. The following are the substantive comparisons I checked. Unless another path is given, local copies are `literature/papers/<key>/original.pdf`; printed page numbers below refer to those copies or the identified author version.

- **Duffin, 1965**, `duffin1965-topology-of-series-parallel-networks`: Theorem 0 and its proof on pp. 306–307, the Wheatstone characterization in Theorem 1, and Section 5/Theorem 4 on pp. 314–316. Confluence and constructive monotone-resistor series/parallel reduction are classical. The manuscript credits those mechanisms and confines its claim to the particular graph, uncertainty, and output statements it proves.
- **Eppstein, 1992**, `eppstein1992-parallel-recognition-of-series-parallel`: Lemma 9 and its induction proof, author-PDF p. 9, with Lemma 10 context. The existing-edge terminal condition in the biconnected SP lemma supports the manuscript's adjacent-terminal use. It is not a claim that every terminal pair in every SP graph has the required property.
- **Chauffoureaux and Hasler, 1990**, `hasler1990-monotonicity-in-nonlinear-resistive-circuits`: Theorems 2 and 3, proofs, and conclusion, proceedings pp. 395–398. Their differentiability/positive-slope and associated linear-circuit criterion justify the earlier nonlinear monotonicity credit. The manuscript's finite secant argument is presented with bounded novelty, not as the first nonlinear endpoint principle.
- **Groß et al., 2017**, `gro2017-algorithmic-results-for-potential-based`: model section and Lemma 4.2/Theorem 4.3 with the series/parallel proof, author-manuscript pp. 10–12. The homogeneous reduction mechanism and the source problem's capacity/potential constraints are distinct from joint independent uncertainty over many interacting blocks. The attribution is appropriate.
- **Labbé et al., cycle booking paper**, `labbe2021-deciding-feasibility-of-a-booking`: November 2020 author manuscript, Section 6, pp. 24–27, Assumption 6.1, Theorems 6.4 and 6.5, Corollary 6.6 and their proofs, and Remark 6.7. The rational-data and degree conditions underpin the polynomial Turing conclusion; the structural extension to general monotone laws does not automatically give the same unrestricted bit algorithm. Paper A respects that distinction.
- **Thür­auf, 2022**, `thurauf2022-deciding-the-feasibility-of-a`: April 6 author manuscript, Section 4, graph and equations (3) on p. 10, Lemma 4.3 and its explicit yes state on pp. 11–12, and Lemmas 4.17–4.20 with proofs on pp. 24–25. The source provides the nomination pressure gap used in Paper A's probe transfer. I checked the actual coefficients, nomination bounds, and threshold construction, not merely the hardness statement.
- **Pfetsch, Schmidt, Skutella, and Thür­auf, 2026 overview**, `pfetsch2026-potential-based-flowsan-overview`: Section 3, especially pp. 9–10, where the MPD model and cactus question are given after the switched-flow problems. The manuscript's source/sink arithmetic equivalence and additive cactus theorem do not settle the general exact multi-entry cactus hardness question or confuse it with switched-network feasibility.
- **Vigneron**, `literature/vigneron-2011-algebraic-sums-manuscript.pdf`: October 21, 2011 version, Section 2.3 and Theorem 6 with proof/Section 3.2, pp. 7–10. Its approximation scheme uses level discretization and polynomial dependence on inverse accuracy in the stated fixed-complexity setting. It does not supply Paper A's dense signed scalar Lipschitz compiler with a polynomial dependence on accuracy bits. The manuscript appropriately presents analytic approximation and common-coordinate arrangements as antecedents, not inventions.
- **Klimm, Pfetsch, Skutella, and Strubberg**, `klimm2026-approximating-the-network-design-problem`: arXiv:2604.26882v1, April 29, 2026, proof preceding Corollary 4 on p. 8 and Theorem 11/proof beginning p. 18. The optimized installation/conductance variables, fixed-cost qualifications, positive bounds, and relative FPTAS contract differ from Paper A's fixed-network resistance uncertainty and additive accuracy-bit output. I found no reason to collapse those statements into the same theorem or to infer a stronger novelty claim from their difference.
- **Onn and Rothblum**, `onn2004-convex-combinatorial-optimization`: Lemmas 2.3–2.4, Algorithm 2.5, and Theorem 2.6 with proof, author-PDF pp. 4–6. Refining normal fans through edge-direction zonotopes and reducing convex maximization to exposed candidates is established. Paper A's relevant adaptation is its rational directions, algebraic interval lengths, additive comparison, and recovery contract.
- **Bonito and Pasciak**, `bonito2015-numerical-approximation-of-fractional-powers`: Section 3.3, Lemma 3.4, and Section 3.4/Theorem 3.5 with proof, author-PDF pp. 14–17. Positive resolvent quadrature and exponential convergence are predecessors; they do not by themselves establish the manuscript's rational increasing network-law construction, bit accounting, and physical stability transfer.
- **Basu's 2014 survey**, `basu2014-algorithms-in-real-algebraic-geometry`: Section 2.5.2, Theorem 2.27 and the accompanying degree/coefficient bounds on pp. 16–17. This is the cited exposition of the classical quantifier-elimination machinery, not an independently original network theorem. The fixed-variable/dense-degree use in Paper A matches its bounds.
- **Dadush's 2012 thesis**, downloaded directly from `https://homepages.cwi.nl/~dadush/papers/dadush-thesis.pdf`: Section 2.5.3, Theorem 2.5.9, printed p. 48. Its centered-body weak-membership and approximate-value hypotheses give a rational point in the actual body with the stated optimization guarantee. This supports the manuscript's exact-feasible convex optimization invocation; the manuscript separately constructs the needed body, bounds, and oracles.

I did not inspect every cited source's complete proof, and this is not an exhaustive priority search. In particular, I did not obtain the 1993 Hasler–Wang paper in full, matching the manuscript's explicit limitation. For the scalar approximation, I audited the manuscript's construction directly and compared the principal Vigneron antecedent; I do not claim an exhaustive survey of all analytic parametrization literature. The theoretical contributions remain well motivated by the operational and output distinctions, without requiring an unrestricted firstness claim.
