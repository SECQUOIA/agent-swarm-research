## Issues and concrete repairs

### R1.1 — Minor: the constant-objective shortcut does not produce the promised face

**Location:** lines 61–65, 67–85, especially “If `c=0`, take any rational feasible nomination.”

The theorem promises an endpoint face with only `O(rp)` unfixed coordinates. An arbitrary feasible nomination is not necessarily such a face or a point on one. For example, in `[-1,1]^n` intersected with balance, the zero vector has every coordinate strictly inside its interval. Fixing those interior values is not a face under the section's definition, and leaving them all free does not give the asserted bound. Returning this vector is enough to solve a constant-objective optimization problem, but not literally enough for the face-construction statement.

**Repair:** choose a rational vertex of the balanced box, then retain its minimal endpoint face. At most one nonsingleton coordinate can be interior: two interior coordinates permit a small nonzero balanced perturbation in both directions, contradicting extremality. Such a vertex is available by rational LP or by starting at all lower endpoints and filling the balance residual in a fixed order. Its encoding is polynomial, and the associated face has at most one unfixed coordinate. One sentence replacing the present shortcut suffices.

### R1.2 — Minor: the dual may be globally constant with no actual breakpoint

**Location:** lines 512–525, especially the breakpoint-existence argument at lines 517–520 and its claim to cover singleton intervals.

Feasibility and affine tails imply a minimizing breakpoint only if the piecewise-affine function has an actual breakpoint. A feasible LP with nonzero flows and singleton intervals can have a globally constant dual. Take a two-edge coherent cycle with `f=(1,-1)`, `w=(0,1)`, and `L=U=(1,1)`. The cycle equality holds, and

`D(lambda) = (0-lambda) - (1-lambda) = -1`

for every real `lambda`. There is no breakpoint. This case is not the all-zero-`f` exception at line 524. The lemma is still true: either listed weight is a valid threshold, and the subsequent reduced-cost argument works.

**Repair:** insert “If the dual is constant on the whole real line, any listed edge weight is a minimizer; otherwise a minimum occurs at a breakpoint (possibly at the finite endpoint of a flat tail).” Equivalently, partition the line using all distinct edge weights as knots, whether or not the slope actually changes there, and use these knots instead of requiring genuine breakpoints. This also handles the case where every coefficient attached to a nonzero `f_e` is fixed while uncertain coefficients multiply zero flows.

No other mathematical gap or necessary repair was identified. Expository suggestions that merely reflect a preference are not counted as issues.

### Bounded-block-rank face theorem and full-box composition

- **Objective-free pruning, lines 85–93.** A physical subnetwork attached through a single vertex and containing no objective support away from that attachment affects its exterior only through its total nomination. - **Zero-induced-source contraction, lines 95–124.** The components `G_v` partition the original vertices. The displayed objective decomposition is exact, with each original objective coefficient counted once. When every `gamma_v=0`, translations of the components do not change the objective. With nominations outside the block fixed, each exterior component has a uniquely determined relative state, since its root supplies the balance residual. Conservation at the contracted vertex ensures that disaggregated root nominations leave balanced effective injections into the removed block. Existence supplies the block state, and translating the exterior components lifts it. This establishes preservation of attainable objective values in both directions, not just an inequality between optima. Removed coefficients can be chosen arbitrarily within their allowed sets. The lift promises rational nominations, not rational physical flows or potentials.
- **Repeated reductions.** Contracting a block cannot identify two distinct vertices of a surviving block: that would contradict the incidence tree. Surviving block ranks therefore remain unchanged. Objective coefficients are summed rather than duplicated, so support cannot increase. The induced source vector of a surviving block is unchanged under regrouping within its edge-deletion components. These observations justify the coefficient independence of the reductions. They rely on the full unfiltered nomination box.
- **Support skeleton counts, lines 126–139.** The minimal tree spanning support has at most `p` leaves, at most `p-2` branching nodes, and total branching degree `O(p)`. Marking support vertex nodes adds only `O(p)` nodes. The total degree of marked vertex nodes is `O(p)`: unbranched marked nodes contribute at most two each and branching degrees have the tree bound. Consequently there are `O(p)` special blocks and `O(p)` total special-block incidences in the minimal tree. All remaining block nodes have degree two and form `O(p)` ordinary chains. Retaining each represented block in its entirety is essential and is stated.
- **Nonzero chain currents.** An ordinary block has only two nonzero possible induced electrical sources, `I,-I`. The side sum `I` is constant along a consistently oriented chain. If it vanished, the block's full induced source vector would vanish and the contraction test would have removed it. This closes the zero-current obstruction to the ordering argument.
- **First smoothing, lines 141–185.** Adding `rho*x` gives strictly positive differential resistance even at zero and for unequal directional coefficients. The grounded weighted Laplacian is nonsingular, so the earlier adjoint derivation applies to an arbitrary balanced `c`. In a biconnected two-terminal block, the maximum principle plus a path avoiding either terminal gives strict interior potential bounds. The nonzero injected current gives distinct port levels. ### Filters, exact profiles, and implementation boundary

- Local fixed-law filters can involve many flows of one long cycle, but substituting their affine circulation expressions leaves only `(z,q)` and preserves bounded degree. Their projection therefore remains fixed dimensional. In the joint model the proof properly stops at affine arc-flow filters; arbitrary polynomial filters could introduce nonquadratic boundary circulations.
- Global feasibility is the conjunction of local eligibility and bridge conditions at a common parameter. Closed filters in the compact original scenario domain give attained extrema, but surrogate partition pieces may still be open; the strict-threshold sampling already handles this. Algebraic coefficient recovery preserves capacities because capacities depend only on `(z,q)`.
- In the irrational-nomination example, incidence differences yield the stated `b(t)`, the two capacity intervals force `q=1`, and the cycle equation becomes `t^2-2=0`. Thus rational exact feasibility cannot be demanded unconditionally.
- For coefficient rounding, the terminal estimate bounds each edge drop change by `B_0^2 k delta`. At fixed coefficients, the asymmetric quadratic law satisfies `|g(u)-g(v)| >= beta_L |u-v|^2/2`, including opposite signs. At a fixed flow, changing coefficients by at most `delta` changes the drop by at most `B_0^2 delta`, since only one direction is active. This proves the stated inverse-law flow bound and remains valid when the two physical flows have opposite signs. The proposed widths make nomination and coefficient flow errors each at most `sigma/4`. They have polynomial bit length. The resulting rational scenario is feasible for original capacities and near-optimal only relative to the tightened problem, exactly as claimed; arbitrary polynomial-filter preservation is expressly excluded.
- At fixed rational nominations, all nonaggregate boundary circulations and stationary points are rational. Irrational candidate circulations can arise only from an aggregate quadratic equality, where all nonzero tied contributions must take the same relevant endpoint extremum. Hence the coefficient profile is rational even when its physical circulation is irrational. A flat objective can choose a boundary of its compact nonempty circulation set. Directional inactive coordinates can again be chosen rationally. Thus the exact-profile extension is supported in both models.
- Pairwise local values have at most two radicals in their difference. Separating `U` and `V`, checking signs first, and then testing `U^2-V^2` when they oppose is a valid exact comparison, including equality and different stored radicands. No global-sum comparison is needed to assemble independent local winners.
- The interior-resistance example checks algebraically: `Aw=c`, physical circulation lies in `(-3,0)`, the three untied reduced costs are strictly negative, and the quadratic upper bound uniquely peaks at `q=-1`. Equality forces `(1,2,1,1)`. This rules out an endpoint-only algorithm for the symmetric problem without asserting uniqueness of inactive directional coordinates in the larger model.
