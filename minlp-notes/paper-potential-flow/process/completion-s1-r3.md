## Minor issue R3-1: state the sampling guarantee in the cited source's terms

Location: `complexity/sections/01-preliminaries.tex:663–673`, paragraph “Fixed-dimension sample points.”

The primitive currently promises sample points meeting “every nonempty sign-invariant cell of a decomposition adapted to the input polynomials,” citing BPR Chapters 12–13. The cited critical-point sampling result guarantees points meeting every semialgebraically connected component of every realizable sign condition. Such a component need not be a cell in the usual decomposition sense. The present wording suggests a cell-decomposition construction, which is a different algorithmic result. This does not invalidate any subsequent argument: every invocation in Sections 2–3 only needs a sample in a nonempty formula-defined set, and the connected-component guarantee supplies that sample and its common real univariate representation.

Fix: replace the first guarantee by “a finite set of sample points meeting every semialgebraically connected component of the realization of every sign condition of the input polynomials.” Keep the real univariate representation and polynomial fixed-dimension bit-size statements. Optionally add Basu's survey Sections 2.2.2–2.2.3 and 2.5 as an accessible description alongside the existing book citation. Alternatively cite the CAD construction explicitly if a true decomposition is intended, but no later argument needs that stronger machinery.

Primary-source check: the local Basu 2014 survey, Section 2.5, expressly describes the ingredient as producing samples in every semialgebraically connected component of every realizable sign condition; its Section 2.2.2 defines the common real univariate representation. I checked the local PDF's extracted text as well as the corpus transcription. The survey's Theorem 2.16 itself uses infinitesimal extensions, so retaining the book's actual sample-point and limit construction is preferable to replacing the book citation with Theorem 2.16 alone.

- **Block structure and aggregation.** I checked the block-incidence proof, parallel two-edge cycles, bridge treatment, and conservation at cut vertices. In a selected active block, its fixed total nomination makes the aggregated nominations in all other path blocks constant.
- **Saturation and bounded faces.** The smoothed adjoint has positive derivative resistances, and the exchange argument handles singleton bounds and lower-dimensional boxes. Strict ordering on cycle branches, and strict interior maximum/minimum principles in nonbridge blocks, justify localization. Positive internal forcing on a suppressed path makes its oriented adjoint current strictly increase, giving the claimed unimodality, at most two threshold vertices, and two-pivot patterns. - **Affine/sign-cell models.** Fundamental circulation variables are bounded chord flows when the tree routing is zero on the chords. Closed full-dimensional sign cells cover lower-dimensional feasible sets and zero-flow boundaries without adding false physical states. Cycle orthogonality plus conservation is equivalent to the passive equations, including at singular algebraic solutions.
- **Exact versus additive computation.** I checked the stated Renegar consistent-sign bound against the local primary source, Part III, Proposition 2.3. I checked the displayed QE degree, operation-count, formula-shape, and coefficient-bit bounds against Basu 2014, Theorem 2.27 in the PDF. - **Parametric box LP with arbitrarily many leaves.** “Finite rational intervals” here bounds each interval, not the number of leaves. The support-function characterization holds for degenerate and lower-dimensional zonotopes. Enumerating only realizable signs in the joint fixed-dimensional `(z, eta)` space gives a polynomial-size formula. The optimization formula includes original feasibility and excludes strictly better feasible pairs, so compactness gives an actual optimizer, not just a supremum.
- **Same-field leaf recovery.** Every zonotope vertex is exposed by a direction off the finitely many relevant central hyperplanes, including when the zonotope lies in a proper affine subspace.
