## Fixed rational exponent extension

The extension from exponents in `(1,3)` to any fixed finite rational family in `(1,infinity)` is justified, rather than merely asserted.

- **Scalar approximation, lines 394–495.** The integral identity, two tail bounds, and uniform analytic-panel estimate are valid, including at zero. On the stated complex disk, the real part of the integration variable is positive, the chosen fractional power is analytic, and the rational factor has modulus at most one. The Taylor remainder and positive quadrature weights give the displayed bound. Taking both the panel count parameter and quadrature order proportional to the accuracy bits gives the asserted quadratic term count.
- **Rational encoding, lines 451–493.** Nodes and weights admit polynomial-degree individual algebraic descriptions. The rational exponent denominators are fixed. The construction does not require a common field for all quadrature nodes. Upward rounding preserves positivity; the lower bound on each pole parameter makes the stated absolute rounding precision sufficient even for very small weights. Normalizing by the positive rational value at one preserves positive coefficients and gives the claimed uniform error. Expanding products and sums of polynomially many rational linear factors has polynomial degree and coefficient length.
- **Constitutive approximation, lines 497–537.** For every nonodd-integer exponent greater than one, `q=2j+1+2alpha` has `j>=0` and `0<alpha<1`. The fixed-family assumption fixes both `j` and the relevant denominators. Choosing the dyadic scaling exponent as specified makes `T^(2alpha)` rational with polynomial encoding length. The approximation error is bounded by `T^(2alpha) B^(2j+1) tau`. Each summand is a positive multiple of `x^(2j+3)/(x^2+c)`. Its displayed derivative is correct and positive away from zero, which proves strict increase on the entire real line despite the derivative vanishing at zero. Odd integer powers are handled exactly. Positive quadratic denominators and the fixed extra odd power preserve the encoding bound.
- **Rational local optimization, lines 539–573.** Clearing the product of positive denominators preserves the physical cycle equations and affine dependence on the independent resistance coordinates. The product's degree is additive. In fixed dimension, its dense expansion remains polynomial in the supplied encodings. The extra objective coordinate and linking equation increase the dimensions only by constants. The supplied drop bound contains every physical feasible objective.
- **Uniform physical error, lines 575–638.** The constants `H`, `c_*`, `M`, and `N_0` bound all members of the fixed family, including when `B<1`. The signed-power increment inequality is valid for equal and opposite signs. Pairing the circulation difference with the potential-gradient difference gives the displayed energy identity with the correct sign. Selecting an edge of maximum flow error yields `D^(q_e)<=eta^H`. The separate cases `D<=1` and `D>1` correctly imply `D<=eta`; this does not incorrectly replace a power on an unrestricted interval. The pathwise pressure estimate then follows.
- **Rational scenario recovery, lines 640–685 and 729–750.** Adding the linear smoothing term to the complete law makes the resistance derivative independent of that term, so the coefficient `N_0` is correct. The unit adjoint current bound and path resistance bound give the stated original-law sensitivity. Subtraction of the target edge laws yields the inverse-flow estimate, including the extra term for changing the target resistance. Requiring a fixed power of the desired flow tolerance costs only polynomially many precision bits. Intersecting isolating boxes with the rational face and balance retains a feasible point, and rounding the aggregate before disaggregation avoids an unjustified claim about closeness of arbitrary full-network disaggregations.
- **Final budgets, lines 701–750.** The chosen `eta` and `delta` control both objective types uniformly by `a=epsilon/8`. The surrogate optimum enclosure, expansion by `a` on each side, face-selection loss, two law-comparison losses, and original-law rounding loss all fit the stated guarantees. Arc recovery starts from an arc optimizer. Separate block encodings avoid an unsupported polynomial bound on a global algebraic compositum.

## Remaining S2 claims

The polynomial-law admissibility test at lines 41–88 correctly separates nonnegative derivatives from strict increase. Its linear feasibility test detects exactly those allowed laws that are constant on a nontrivial piece. The root partition and dense encoding keep the computation polynomial.

The law-independent face transfer at lines 91–140 preserves centering, the acyclic flow bound, and uniform convergence on compact input domains. Positive smoothing derivatives permit the preceding adjoint and suppressed-path arguments. The conclusion requires existence of an optimizer on one of the faces, not an effective smoothing rate.

The growing-degree box lemma and polynomial-law theorem at lines 142–312 use fixed dimension, not fixed degree. Their polynomial bit claims are consistent with the real-algebraic primitives stated in the preliminaries. Piece-boundary agreement, independent coefficient leaves, local exact arc comparisons, and pressure recovery are handled consistently.

The SRS reduction at lines 320–369 has the correct inequality direction and equality case. For fixed reduced `p/q>1` with even denominator, the chosen integer base flow and rational resistance contribute exactly `sqrt(a_i)` at zero circulation. Clearing denominators preserves the physical flow and has polynomial bit cost. Reversing the target edge converts the predicate to an upper capacity. The result is correctly limited to an exact-comparison lower bound; it is not presented as NP-hardness or an additive-approximation obstruction.
