It also applies when every point of P lies in a proper affine subspace. At an ambient-space vertex, active normals span the ambient parameter space: otherwise sufficiently small displacements in both directions preserve all active and inactive inequalities. Therefore the use of t independent active rows and Cramer's rule is valid for lower-dimensional P and singleton P.

Clearing all input coefficient denominators has polynomial bit cost because their logarithms add. Substitution at a rational vertex yields the stated height bound, independent of the number of vertices. A strictly increasing specialization cannot be constant on a positive-width piece; this excludes the zero-polynomial exception needed for the separation argument. Root-at-breakpoint cases belong to adjacent closed pieces by continuity. The proof needs continuity only on the promised parameter domain, which is sufficient for its LP queries.

For two vertex-piece polynomials, their product has the stated degree and convolution-height bounds. Its primitive squarefree part is an integer factor. The factor-height bound followed by the discriminant inequality separates distinct roots even when the original polynomials share factors or have repeated roots. Final LP extraction is sound: the selected vertex root and the extreme root occupy the same interval of width less than the uniform separation. Equality in bisection is assigned to the correct retained endpoint, including extrema at a or b. 6. Fixed measurements, including the new polynomial-law extension, lines 615–749.

The continuous case obtains exact constrained endpoints and rational original profiles from the newly proved interval theorem, for arbitrarily many cycle parameters and dense growing law degree. The finite quadratic case separately obtains the convex hull and actual endpoint realizations from the earlier cactus-region theorem. Independent finite sets are not replaced by capacity-filtered convex hulls. A box containing the attainable finite set contains its convex hull, so the stated convexity promise suffices there too.

Rational generator directions a_C=RZ_C are the key arithmetic fact. Algebraic lengths and offsets do not enter the arrangement LPs. Homogeneity makes simultaneous strict signs equivalent to signed inner products at least one. A feasible rational LP solution has polynomial bit length even when the cone contains lines. Incremental insertion retains exactly all realizable strict sign patterns; for fixed k the number of arrangement regions is polynomial in the number of generators. Repeated hyperplanes, linear dependence, and rank below k do not require algebraic arithmetic.

Every zonotope vertex has a full-dimensional set of exposing directions in the ambient space, also for a lower-dimensional zonotope. Removing finitely many nonzero-generator hyperplanes leaves an exposing direction whose signs select the appropriate endpoints. Positive-length segments select uniquely; zero-length segments only introduce harmless extra sign patterns. Zero directions can use either endpoint. These facts cover a point zonotope and establish complete candidate coverage without enumerating all circulation-box vertices.

Each candidate consists of independently combined stored rational endpoint profiles and is exactly feasible. In the finite case it belongs to the original finite domain; in the continuous polynomial case it satisfies the local capacities exactly.
