## Issues and concrete repairs

1. **Minor mathematical proof omission: the threshold dual can have no breakpoints even when some flows are nonzero.** Location: section lines 517–525, proof of `lem:a-wcac-threshold`.

   The proof asserts that a dual minimum occurs at a breakpoint, then treats only the case in which every `f_e` vanishes as exceptional. Singleton coefficient intervals are allowed. For example, take `f=(1,-1)`, `w=(0,1)`, and `L=U=(1,1)`. The cycle LP is feasible and its dual is `D(lambda)=-1` for every real `lambda`; there is no breakpoint, despite nonzero flows. This is a valid physical two-edge cycle example as well. The candidate construction and lemma conclusion remain correct: either enumerated weight supplies a valid threshold.

   Repair: after the breakpoint argument, handle the case `(U_e-L_e)|f_e|=0` for every edge. All nonzero contributions are then fixed. Feasibility makes `D` constant, and every enumerated weight works, because changing the untied endpoint cannot change any contribution. Otherwise at least one genuine breakpoint exists, and the existing convex piecewise-affine argument applies. This also subsumes the all-zero-flow case. The omission is minor because it is a one-case completion, not a failure of the elimination algorithm or its output.

2. **Minor output-contract omission: the zero-objective branch selects a nomination rather than a face.** Location: section line 85, proof of `thm:a-wcac-faces`, compared with the face definition at lines 61–65 and the theorem output at lines 72–76.

   “Take any rational feasible nomination” establishes an optimal scenario, but does not construct one of the promised endpoint-defined faces with only `O(rp)` unfixed coordinates. For instance, zero is rational and feasible in `[-1,1]^n` with balance, but no coordinate is at an endpoint, so the smallest endpoint-defined face containing it still has all `n` coordinates unfixed. The nonzero-objective argument is unaffected.

   Repair: in this branch, start at all lower bounds and fill coordinates in a fixed order until the total is zero. All but at most one coordinate are at endpoints. Fix those coordinates and retain the possible one remaining interval together with balance. This supplies the required rational face immediately. Alternatively explicitly prune the entire zero-support network to one aggregate vertex, with its recorded rational lift. This is a minor missing construction in a trivial case.

3. **Minor source-identification issue: the Vigneron locators refer to a manuscript that the bibliography does not identify.** Location: section lines 48–52 and `complexity/references.bib` lines 215–225.

   The inspected October 21, 2011 author manuscript does have Theorem 6, Sections 2.3 and 3.2, exactly as stated. The bibliography currently gives only the 2014 journal publication and its DOI, while the prose specifically distinguishes manuscript locators. A reader cannot identify the intended version from that bibliography entry alone. Unlike the Gotzes, Aßmann, and González Grandón entries, it has no manuscript note or manuscript URL.

   Repair: retain the journal citation and add a note identifying the October 21, 2011 author manuscript, with `https://antoinevigneron.github.io/manuscripts/rational.pdf` as an additional link or explicit manuscript URL. This is a retrieval/reproducibility issue, not an incorrect source comparison. The existing substantive statement about inverse-tolerance rather than accuracy-bit dependence is supported.

## Mathematical audit

**Model, hypotheses, and dependencies.** I checked the incidence convention, asymmetric-law definition and reorientation rule, existence/uniqueness, block incidence tree and block decomposition, and scenario/output definitions in `01-preliminaries.tex`. The balanced-box convention there already includes nonemptiness, so the box corollary does not silently optimize an empty set. The affine polytope is explicitly given by rational equalities and inequalities; fixed dimension means parameter dimension, not graph size. The fixed-dimensional quantifier-elimination and sample-point bounds in the preliminaries allow polynomial numerical degree and coefficient size, which is essential here. I also checked the smoothed adjoint in `02-cactus.tex` and the earlier symmetric resistance estimate in `03-block-rank.tex`. The new coefficient lemma supplies its own asymmetric argument and does not merely cite the symmetric result as if it covered the extension.

**Pruning and zero-induced-objective contraction.** Removing an objective-free pendant physical subnetwork preserves its exterior effect through the nomination sum at its attachment. Interval sums are exactly attainable, and greedy disaggregation uses only rational additions, comparisons, and subtraction. For zero-`gamma` contraction, the displayed decomposition of `W` eliminates the root-potential terms separately for each exterior component. Its internal relative potentials are determined by its nonroot nominations. Conversely, any disaggregation of the contracted root nomination gives balanced effective block injections after the exterior flows are subtracted. Solving that block and translating the exterior components reconstructs a state with the same objective. This is why arbitrarily chosen admissible removed coefficients suffice. Shared articulation nominations are counted once by the block-incidence decomposition. Contraction cannot identify two vertices of another surviving block without making an incidence-tree cycle; ranks therefore remain bounded, and support cannot increase. All tests depend on rational objective sums, not the laws. Operating filters would invalidate these arguments, as the text explicitly states.

**First face stage.** The minimal support-spanning incidence tree has at most `p` leaves. Branching degree sums and marked support/branching vertices bound the number of special blocks and ordinary chains by `O(p)`. Nonsupport vertices within retained physical blocks must be retained, and the text does so. An ordinary block has only two induced electrical sources, of values `I,-I`. If `I=0`, all induced objective coefficients would vanish and the block would already have been contracted. The strict two-terminal maximum principle follows from positive differential conductances and biconnectivity; bridges require no internal-vertex claim. Series port ordering then makes consecutive open block ranges disjoint. The balanced-box normal cone needs no interior constraint qualification. Selecting at most one block per chain, while leaving shared endpoints free whenever a selected or special block contains them, covers threshold equality at articulations and avoids incompatible endpoint prescriptions. The resulting count is `n^{O(p)}`.

**Second face stage and limits.** Smoothing with `g+rho*x` preserves strict monotonicity and gives positive differential resistance even at zero flow. Passive-flow acyclicity bounds every physical edge flow by total positive nomination. A spanning-tree feasible-flow correction, energy minimality, and strict convexity identify all limits when nominations and smoothing vary; compactness supplies uniform objective convergence. Thus it is valid to fix a closed coarse face containing an original maximizer before introducing the positive interior sources. The bounded-rank degree count gives at most `2r_B-2` high-degree vertices in each nonbridge block. Marking retained ports and support vertices as well produces `O(rp)` marked coordinates and suppressed paths. On every path, the positive source perturbation gives `j_i-j_(i-1)=delta`; signs of successive voltage differences therefore yield a unimodal sequence with at most a two-vertex plateau. A horizontal threshold has at most two interior vertices. Endpoint runs with up to two pivots give `n^{O(rp)}` faces and `O(rp)` free coordinates. The second uniform limiting argument preserves a maximum. Crucially, it does not require the perturbed objective to retain the original support size or the original chain ordering. The coefficient-independent enumeration is valid for a jointly optimal frozen coefficient profile. This remains only a structural result for higher-rank blocks.

**Fixed-law cycle charts.** A rational tree flow for `c` gives `W=sum w_e g_e(x_e)`. Cactus cycles are edge-disjoint, so their circulation equations are independent conditional on `z`, and cycle-compatible drops integrate globally. Reversing an edge reverses its offset and weight and swaps directional coefficients. Sign arrangements in fixed `d+1` variables have polynomially many realizable cells; their closures cover zero flows without exponential sign enumeration. On a cell, the cycle equation has constant quadratic coefficient, affine linear coefficient, and quadratic constant term. At a physical root its derivative is nonnegative and is zero only when all edge flows vanish. Therefore `(-B+sqrt(B^2-4AC))/(2A)` is the correct selector even for negative `A`. Substitution yields a quadratic polynomial plus affine times a square root. The `A=0,B>0` branch is an exact bounded-degree rational function, with no unjustified denominator lower bound. The explicit all-zero chart covers the remaining degeneration. Projection of one circulation variable gives bounded-degree rational eligibility formulas.

**Panels, one common partition, and accuracy bits.** The panel center satisfies `sqrt(a_j)=(3/4)2^(-j)`, and the maximum relative displacement is `7/9`. Bounding binomial coefficients by one gives the stated geometric tail bound. The small zero panel has error at most `2^(-J)`. Scaling by `S` requires precision `eta/S`, costing `log S+log(1/eta)` bits. Both panel count and polynomial degree are logarithmic in inverse accuracy; expanded coefficient sizes remain polynomial, including the `jK` term. Substituting quadratic discriminants preserves polynomial dense size in fixed dimension. All chart, denominator, and panel signs are handled in one parameter-space partition. Thus the proof does not enumerate combinations of local charts across cycles. Multiplying polynomially many rational denominators increases degree and coefficient size polynomially, and dense size remains polynomial because `d` is fixed. Squared denominators correctly preserve threshold inequality signs.

**Suprema and rational nomination recovery.** The strict existential threshold test is appropriate for open parts. Uniform surrogate error bounds every part's supremum, even near a denominator zero. Width `epsilon/8` for `[L,U]`, local error `epsilon/16`, and sampling above `L-epsilon/16` give loss at most `5epsilon/16`; the displayed enlarged interval has width at most `epsilon/4`. The difference-flow proof of nomination continuity is valid on any graph: monotonicity orients each nonzero difference along a strictly decreasing difference potential, ruling out directed cycles. Its path decomposition has total supply `||b-b'||_1/2`. The asymmetric quadratic law is Lipschitz on the common bounded flow interval, including across zero. Rational LP inside the isolating box intersected with `P` therefore returns an exactly admissible rational parameter even when `P` has lower dimension. The proof never requires preservation of the sampled chart. The additional `epsilon/4` rounding loss leaves room within the requested error.

**Coefficient elimination and circulation completeness.** Conditional on `z`, independent cycle and bridge coefficients separate the objective. On a sign cell only the active directional coefficient appears in the one-equality LP. The untied endpoint choices maximize every reduced-cost term, and tied contribution intervals fill exactly `[l_T,u_T]`. Thus the two aggregate inequalities are necessary and sufficient for a feasible threshold profile. Apart from issue 1, the dual argument proves coverage. Ties do not cause exponential enumeration. Every compact one-variable feasible set has an optimizing boundary, stationary point, or flat branch. A true boundary requires an active polynomial that is nonconstant after specializing `z`; identically zero or constant constraints cannot supply a false boundary root. Both quadratic roots are enumerated, and the selectors `2aq+b >= 0` and `<= 0` remain correct for negative `a`. Vanishing linear coefficients are explicitly split before division. Stationary candidates and existential flat eligibility complete the list. Arc bounds add only affine circulation constraints.

**Independent directional coefficients: approximate-output proof.** The extension retains the original `2m` coordinates, rather than treating symmetric uncertainty as an independent rectangle. Inactive coefficients may be assigned rational allowed values because their drops are identically zero for the selected flow direction. At zero flow both directional contributions vanish, so either adjacent closed sign cell is valid. Tied filling starts from minimum contributions, divides only by nonzero `f_e`, and uses at most one interior tied coordinate. In the symmetric model the recovered active coordinate is the single original coefficient, preserving the diagonal relation. In the directional model the selected active coordinate and an allowed inactive coordinate recover the actual asymmetric law. Processing each cycle over the common algebraic nomination field and its own degree-at-most-two extension suffices; no field containing all independent cycle roots is needed. Flat sampling likewise takes place in one local variable over the nomination field. Polynomial degrees and coefficient heights imply polynomial coordinate representations and isolation costs.

**New all-graph coefficient sensitivity proof.** The smoothing term is independent of both directional coefficients. Grounding a potential makes the positive weighted Laplacian nonsingular. The law is jointly continuously differentiable in flow and both coefficients, including at zero, so implicit differentiation is valid throughout the positive coefficient box. The terminal adjoint has one unit source and one unit sink; its nonzero currents follow strictly decreasing adjoint potential and decompose into paths of total weight one. Hence each current has magnitude at most one. Differentiating the physical equations at fixed nominations gives exactly the displayed positive-direction term `j_e (x_e)_+^2 d beta_e^+` and negative-direction term `-j_e (x_e)_-^2 d beta_e^-`; there is no missing sign. Every coordinate derivative is bounded by `B_0^2`. Integration along the original coefficient-box segment followed by uniform smoothing convergence proves the terminal bound. Normalization and summation against `|c_v|`, followed by the independent nomination estimate, prove the joint inequality. This argument includes coefficient-induced flow sign crossings. Isolating and rounding every original coefficient inside its own rational interval, with singleton coordinates fixed, gives the stated rational approximate scenario. It is unnecessary for rounding to preserve a local root selector or sign cell.

**Local filters and slack recovery.** For fixed laws, substitution of one cycle's flows into a bounded-degree polynomial filter uses only `(z,q)`; long cycles do not add independent variables. The broader polynomial-filter statement is therefore compatible with the same chart and projection proof. In the uncertain case only arc bounds are asserted, avoiding an unsupported extension of the quadratic candidate list. Feasibility is the intersection of local eligibility unions and bridge conditions. Compact parameter/coefficient boxes and closed physical filters give an attained global optimum. Exactly feasible algebraic parameters are recovered without rational rounding. For the slack result, the fixed-law inverse bound `|g(u)-g(v)| >= beta_L |u-v|^2/2` is correct for both equal and opposite flow signs. Changing coefficients at a fixed flow changes only its active branch, giving the `B_0^2 delta` term even if the other scenario has a different sign. Together with the terminal sensitivity estimate this yields the stated `2B_0^2(k+1)delta/beta_L` flow bound. The explicit choices of `h` and `delta` give total flow error at most `sigma/2` and cost only polynomial encoding length in `sigma`. The claim is explicitly relative to the tightened optimum, and does not silently compare it with the original optimum or preserve general polynomial filters.

**Examples and exact fixed-nomination output.** In the irrational-capacity example, incidence differences give the stated `b`; the two unit capacities intersect at `q=1`, and the cycle equation is `t^2-2=0`. Thus the only feasible nomination parameter in `[1,2]` is irrational despite rational positive capacities. For fixed rational nominations, sign/capacity/artificial boundaries and nonflat stationary circulations are rational. An irrational candidate can only arise at an aggregate quadratic boundary, where all nonzero tied contributions must be at their corresponding extremal endpoints. The original resistance profile is therefore rational even though the circulation and value may not be. Flat objectives can use a boundary of their nonempty compact feasible set, reducing to the same cases. Inactive directional coordinates can be rational endpoints; symmetric coordinates remain shared. This proves the directional extension of the exact-profile theorem, not merely its approximate form.

The pairwise comparison formula for quadratic values is correct, including opposing signs, exact cancellation, and differing radicands. The intermediate difference has at most two radicals; the squared-magnitude reduction has one. It requires neither integer factorization nor separation-based approximate comparisons. Local exact winners can be selected independently because nominations are fixed. Their combined value is an expression sum, not a promised polynomial-size primitive algebraic encoding or exact scalar-sum comparator. The unique interior-resistance example has `Aw=c`, physical circulation in `(-3,0)`, upper bound `-6(q+1)^2-12`, and equality only at `q=-1`, `beta=(1,2,1,1)`. It correctly rules out endpoint-profile enumeration. This uniqueness example is for the symmetric model; the text does not incorrectly claim uniqueness of all inactive coordinates in the directional model.

## Validation and limits

I inspected `code/potential_flow_mpd/exact_weighted_cactus.py`, especially the `solve_cycle` candidate/recovery logic and the `solve_cactus` input/output schema. It accepts supplied offsets, weights, symmetric intervals, local bounds, and fixed-flow bridges. It does not take an original graph, construct or verify a graph decomposition, support independent directional coefficients through its public API, or implement varying-nomination semialgebraic optimization. The implementation paragraph states those limits accurately. Separate local value encodings and their final interval are not a general exact scalar-sum comparator.

I independently ran, with bytecode writes disabled:

`PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python paper-potential-flow/verification/check_a7_weighted_asymmetric.py`

It passed 120 original directional LP cases (29 feasible), 32 exact directional cycle cases, 32 swapped-interval orientation checks, 1,280 independent physical profiles, 1,280 joint sensitivity checks, and the explicit sign-crossing case. The harness uses the symmetric block solver on restricted sign panels for one part of its checks. Its sampled physical and sensitivity checks are regression evidence, not proofs of the all-graph sensitivity theorem, global semialgebraic algorithm, structural face theorem, or universal directional candidate completeness.

I did not run a shared build or a complete public-solver test suite. The proof audit, source comparison, and narrow regression run support acceptance after the three minor repairs above. The final section hash is unchanged from the supplied frozen hash.
