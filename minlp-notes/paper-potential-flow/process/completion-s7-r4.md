## Extent of the mathematical reading

I read the abstract, introduction, all six narrative files, conclusion, bibliography, macros, and all eleven technical sources in full, including their proofs, examples and nonformal arguments. As a reading cross-check, theorem, lemma, proposition and corollary environments alone total 106 in the technical appendices: A 9; B 8; C 11; D 12; E 11; F 14; G 11; H 9; I 8; J 7; K 6. The assessments below concern the actual proofs, not only their statements or the shorter main-text summaries.

**A — `01-preliminaries.tex`, foundations and computational conventions.** The incidence signs, reversal of asymmetric laws, potential gauges, conservation subspace and block conventions are explicit. Existence does not incorrectly require a strictly increasing law to be onto: positivity away from zero makes its primitive coercive even for a bounded law. Strict convexity gives the unique flow, and the first-order condition recovers potentials. The directed support of a physical flow is acyclic, which supplies the total-positive-injection bound used later. Block aggregation preserves the exact local physical state; reconstruction uses independent translations of component potentials and rational interval disaggregation. The block-incidence tree accounts for an arc belonging to one block and a terminal difference summing over a path. The algebraic conventions distinguish a fixed-dimensional exact core from separate block fields and rational accuracy-bit output. The main model supplies enough of these conventions before the algorithm statements. I found no missing bounded-law, orientation or disconnected-gauge case in the claimed scopes.

**B — `02-cactus.tex`, cactus pressure and arithmetic.** The adjoint threshold argument yields two possible free nominations in a selected cyclic block; balance leaves one scalar parameter. Closed faces and compact limits correctly cover ties and vanishing flows after smoothing. Aggregation and enumeration do not silently retain operating filters. The additive algorithm approximates independent block contributions without forming a potentially exponential-degree compositum. I checked both directions of the source/sink Square-Root-Sum equivalence, including literal trivial outputs, equality conventions, positive rational-to-integer scaling, and off-path blocks in the reverse direction. The manuscript does not extend the restricted exact equivalence to general cactus MPD. The main narrative's triangle chain and arithmetic discussion convey this boundary accurately.

**C — `03-block-rank.tex`, localization and coefficient elimination.** The positive-source perturbation makes successive path currents strictly ordered, so a potential level meets at most two interior vertices. The mark/path count leads to the stated `8r_B-2` free-nomination and `9r_B-3` core-coordinate bounds. There is at most one active block along a terminal block path; the block-rank hypothesis is distinct from total rank. Smoothing is an existence argument over a finite family of closed faces, so the algorithm need not find a numerical perturbation size. The coefficient-box image is handled through realizable support-sign patterns in fixed image dimension, not by enumerating all coefficient-box vertices. The universal support condition, fixed-dimensional elimination and exposed-vertex lift justify both feasibility and recovery. Recovery retains polynomial algebraic degree in one local field and original-coordinate interval constraints. Exact individual-arc optimization remains local. The main proof sketch and Figure 2 accurately describe the fixed nominations outside the selected block while allowing coefficient optimization everywhere. The exponent depends on rank, so the explicitly stated XP-type bound is appropriate.

**D — `04-laws.tex`, dense laws and rational powers.** The representation requires dense rational polynomial pieces and fixed rational breakpoints; these restrictions are consistently retained. Admissibility checks partition derivative signs and treat a polynomial piece that could be identically flat through a rational LP, rather than assuming pointwise nonnegativity implies strict increase. Centered smoothing preserves zero at the origin. The dense-degree coefficient-elimination extension keeps the real dimension fixed while allowing degree and piece count to grow. Exact arc values and rational pressure scenarios are correctly distinguished. The rational-power approximation uses positive quadrature and a monotone surrogate, with the odd-power factor extending the fixed exponent family beyond the initial small interval. The state and nomination-rounding estimates supply an accuracy-bit argument after denominator clearing. The exact even-denominator obstruction is a hardness result, with equality and scaling addressed; it is not asserted as a matching general exact upper bound. Main Theorem 3.1 and the adjacent scope paragraphs match these limitations.

**E — `05-boundaries.tex`, discrete choices, secants and graph classes.** The theta pressure construction and rank-three arc probe have explicit rational gaps and correctly oriented strict/weak decision thresholds. The rank-two positive result is not contradicted by the rank-three hardness example. The finite secant identity has the target-edge sign reversal and gives an envelope comparison without assuming differentiable laws. The classical adjacent-terminal confluence mechanism is credited. The convex formulation includes bounds needed for a Turing accuracy-bit guarantee and approximate-sign endpoint recovery, rather than treating a solver tolerance as an algorithmic guarantee. The universal individual-arc hull characterization includes a K4 obstruction and finite positive restoration; zero resistance is only an intermediate limiting device. Exact probe-cactus Square-Root-Sum comparison is carefully scoped. The nomination-hardness extension credits Thürauf's source graph and pressure gap and supplies the additional arc argument. Arbitrary-graph interval realization is an LP, whereas finite realization has a separate NP-complete single-cycle construction. The main topology table and finite-choice discussion correctly separate universal hull equality, algorithmic tractability and exact comparison.

**F — `06-weighted.tex`, weighted objectives and attainable regions.** The rational quadratic-program certificate argument covers singular stationary faces; it does not assume an invertible Hessian. Tree optimization uses zero-cut contraction and support-controlled sign patterns, including the path refinement, with rational scenario recovery. The global-rank theorem includes positive and negative sources and returning paths, and keeps total rank distinct from maximum block rank. The weighted triangle and theta reductions establish the claimed gaps, with the unit-cost total-flow version handled by subdivision and the appropriate scaling. The scalar resistance-hull hierarchy—trees for all weighted potentials, cacti for pairwise differences and linear flows, series-parallel graphs for individual arcs—is supported by separate witnesses and positive restoration. For full-region convexity, the one-edge deletion argument supplies an injective scalar coordinate unless the state is constant, making the nonaffine-curve obstruction sufficient. The extension to common nonlinear powers is qualitative and does not claim uniform encoding or algorithmic bounds. The main text appropriately explains why changing the observable changes the topology boundary.

**G — `07-weighted-cactus.tex`, weighted faces and cycle optimization.** The zero-induced-objective block contraction is necessary and is proved with physical lifting, not merely an adjoint intuition. The two-stage argument first fixes a coarse face and only then perturbs the chosen blocks, preserving the chain structure that a single global perturbation could destroy. The local quadratic formula treats the sign of its leading coefficient, linear and all-zero cases. Threshold ordering includes ties and zero-flow boundaries; the candidate construction includes boundary, stationary, flat and zero branches. Directional coefficients are selected according to actual flow sign, while a shared symmetric interval remains one shared variable. Local surrogate maximization and common-parameter summation provide both a value estimate and a recoverable scenario. The rounding proof returns original rational nominations and coefficients and bounds the resulting physical objective; it does not round circulation coordinates independently. Closed local filters can force algebraic outputs, and the tightened-feasible-domain rational guarantee has the right qualification. The irrational-nomination example and the exact rational fixed-nomination coefficient-profile theorem are compatible: the latter can yield an irrational physical circulation. The interior coefficient optimum rules out an unjustified endpoint-only algorithm. The main text preserves these distinctions.

**H — `08-design.tex`, scalar roots, capacities and convex design.** The monotone-root theorem uses rational LP threshold tests and a uniform root-separation bound across possible LP vertices. It handles repeated roots and lower-dimensional parameter polytopes and returns a rational optimizing vertex without enumerating all vertices. The successive coordinate LPs remain faces of the original polytope, so the original Cramer bound controls the entire recovery sequence. Global cactus capacities become rational linear constraints in the original shared parameters. Independent cycle domains instead yield separate attainable circulation intervals, with exact rational parameter witnesses even when an endpoint circulation is irrational. The convex polynomial extension is explicitly global and Lipschitz before invoking convex optimization; it does not rely only on a cube-local convexity promise. The inner rational box/frozen-coordinate construction preserves exact final feasibility, and recovery moves back to original parameters. Few-measurement maximization enumerates rational direction arrangements of a zonotope and encloses root values separately. Its parameter-dependent exponent and finite-set maximization-only scope are stated accurately. The Max-Cut reduction concerns a separate maximization problem. The main design theorem and its explanation correctly present why one scalar circulation supports stronger exact recovery.

**I — `09-correlations-energy.tex`, correlations, energy and rational witnesses.** The two-cycle common-parameter example gives a curved feasible image and an interior optimum without falsely treating nonconvexity alone as hardness. The paired-triangle reductions establish bounded-data hardness for total flow and minimum constitutive dissipation; the prefix-bridge cancellation removes the energy bias required for the reduction. Their gap and restricted-family certificate claims do not imply general NP membership. In the opposite direction, constitutive dissipation is concave in coefficients by energy minimization; its dual and the two-cone lift support convex maximization in the stated transformed formulation. The proof supplies rational relative coordinates, an interior radius and bounds for the cited weak convex theorem. The ordinary-upper-capacity example really forces irrational resistance, and therefore blocks an unrestricted rational feasibility-witness claim. Both the elementary continuity estimate and the sharper electrical estimate cover reversals and zero flows, using regularization and passage to the physical limit. Strict-margin rational recovery is conditional on a margin; it does not assert a general margin-finding algorithm. The positive versus negative pressure-filter distinction is explicit. The main narrative's direction-of-optimization explanation matches these proofs.

**J — `10-weighted-blocks.tex`, scalar compiler, summation and hybrid recovery.** This group received additional scrutiny. The exact local-value graph first eliminates the coefficient leaves while real dimension and relevant structural parameters are fixed. **K — `11-certified-computation.tex`, rational certificates and delivery claims.** The cubic conjugate and energy gap yield the stated rational uniform-radius test.
