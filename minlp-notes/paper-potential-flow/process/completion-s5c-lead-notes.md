## Mathematical development alongside the author

The lead and author independently identified the hybrid extension: keep a fixed total rank across all noncactus blocks while allowing arbitrarily many cycle blocks. The author observed that the exceptional coefficient variables can be eliminated with the existing parametric box lemma using all exceptional cycle rows and one weighted-objective row. The lead reread that lemma in full and agrees; arbitrary objective support does not increase the image dimension. Its constructive algebraic leaf recovery works at any feasible algebraic core/value pair, not only a maximum of the box lemma alone.

The lead proposed a second extension: one shared scalar nomination parameter with arbitrarily many bounded-rank blocks. Local optimum-value graphs are univariate semialgebraic and uniformly Lipschitz. The author independently confirmed the following route. A product of all ordinate-dependent polynomials in a graph formula annihilates the continuous function: except at finitely many zeros of parameter-only polynomials, lack of any equality would make the formula locally open in two dimensions, contradicting its being a graph. Remove parameter content and take the squarefree part over the rational function field. The resulting polynomial has polynomial degree and coefficient length. The leading ordinate coefficient and discriminant describe all possible complex branch and pole locations.

Padding rational enclosures of the real parts of these exceptional points gives small real bands on which the Lipschitz bound permits constant approximation. On the complementary gaps, geometric rational panels admit a holomorphic root branch in a disk. The leading coefficient has a lower bound from its root distances, and a Cauchy polynomial-root bound gives a uniform complex magnitude with polynomial logarithm. Both lead and author then independently replaced the initially considered algebraic Taylor recurrence by rational equispaced interpolation: a contour remainder contracts geometrically, approximate node values cause a controlled interpolation perturbation, and all interpolation coefficients are rational with polynomial encoding. No large common algebraic field or exact sum comparison is needed. This is to be fully proved and independently reviewed, not treated as accepted here.

A possible extension to fixed densely encoded piecewise-polynomial laws was sent to the author. A generic modulus alternative was also noted: fixed-dimensional quantifier elimination can find a positive uniform-continuity radius for a continuous explicit semialgebraic scalar graph at a specified rational tolerance. It is optional; a supplied Lipschitz bound already suffices for the network application.
