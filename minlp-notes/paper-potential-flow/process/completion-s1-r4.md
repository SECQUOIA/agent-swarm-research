## Mathematical findings

- **Existence for bounded laws is correct.** Strict increase and normalization give positive numbers `g_e(1)` and `-g_e(-1)` and therefore a uniform linear coercivity bound for the primitive. This is sufficient on every affine conservation space; superlinear growth and surjectivity are unnecessary. The variational first-order argument correctly accommodates the redundant incidence row. The arctangent parallel example is consistent with the theorem.
- **Block and cactus reductions are sound.** The block incidence proof includes bridges and parallel edges; aggregation counts the cut-vertex nomination once in each appropriate component and reconstructs global conservation correctly. The cactus saturation argument uses a smoothed adjoint, a resistance-independent finite family, and uniform convergence, so it does not assume differentiability at zero flow. A nonconstant cactus face really varies only one cycle.
- **Approximation and outputs are correctly separated.** The active-cycle equations have two variables. Fixed block drops are refined separately. Rational recovery preserves the balanced box exactly, and the objective loss is justified by a uniform Lipschitz bound. The source-to-sink path decomposition and adjoint effective-resistance comparison justify the needed quantitative bounds.
- **Both restricted SRS reductions survive boundary cases.** The gadget's unit drop is `a+1-2 sqrt(a)`. Integer scaling preserves flows and scales the objective and threshold together with polynomial output length. The reverse reduction has positive radical coefficients after moving them to the right-hand side, handles equal branch resistances and nonpositive residual thresholds separately, and clears rational radicands without factorization. The statement is restricted to a single source/sink and does not claim NP-hardness from SRS-hardness.
- **Bounded block rank is used consistently.** The forcing perturbation makes path currents strictly increase, hence produces at most two threshold pivots per suppressed path. Marked vertices and degenerate faces are included. The resulting dimensions depend on block rank, not on the number of blocks. The stated running times are explicitly XP-type, with no FPT claim.
- **The box-elimination lemma does not hide exponential corners.** It uses realizable sign vectors in fixed dimension before quantifier elimination. For recovery, the full-dimensional support cells expose all zonotope vertices even for a lower-dimensional zonotope; a fixed-size convex combination recovers the interval variables in one common local algebraic field. The exact local output and global interval summation therefore have different, justified contracts.
- **Joint arc extrema are correctly localized.** Fixing the optimal resistances first makes an arc's flow strictly increasing in its endpoint drop, so the nomination face theorem transfers. Subsequent optimization still uses the arc objective, not a pressure-maximizing resistance scenario. The text expressly excludes capacity-filtered scenario domains.
- **The broader-parameter obstruction is correctly attributed and quantified.** I checked Thürauf's actual PDF, Figure 2, the booking/resistance data, equation (3), and the cited lemmas. The renamed rational gap formulas agree. The graph is `K_{2,j}` with pendant edges, so the claimed treewidth and feedback-vertex-number properties follow and do not conflict with the growing block rank.
