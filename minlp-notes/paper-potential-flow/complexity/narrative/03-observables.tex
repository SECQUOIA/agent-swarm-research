\section{The observable determines the topology boundary}
\label{sec:main-observables}

Replacing finite resistance sets by their interval hulls is an attractive
simplification: continuous coefficients can be eliminated through convex
images, while a product of finite sets has exponentially many choices.
Whether this replacement preserves an extremum depends on the graph
\emph{and} the objective. Throughout this section, a universal statement
quantifies over every fixed balanced nomination and every independent
compact positive resistance set with attained endpoints, under quadratic
laws and without operating filters.

\begin{theorem}[Universal resistance-hull and region classifications]
\label{thm:main-hulls}
For finite connected simple graphs, the universal interval-hull classes
for maxima and minima are exactly those in \cref{tab:main-hulls}.
The same classes characterize separate monotonicity in every resistance
coordinate, with the other coordinates fixed. The attainable flow region
is convex for every fixed nomination and positive resistance box exactly
on cacti. Normalized potential regions and joint flow--potential regions
are universally convex exactly on trees.
\end{theorem}

These claims are \cref{thm:a-weight-hierarchy,thm:a-weight-convex-regions},
with the individual-arc equivalence proved in
\cref{thm:a-bound-characterization}. Counterexamples use rational data
and one varying resistance. Restoring a cycle, theta, or $K_4$
subdivision in a larger graph requires strictly positive resistances
on all other edges; the appendix supplies quantitative restoration
bounds. No zero-resistance contraction is treated as an admissible
final instance. The full-region classifications also extend qualitatively
to every fixed common power $\sgn(x)|x|^q$ with $q>0$, $q\ne1$,
but that geometric extension asserts no uniform bit bound or algorithm
(\cref{thm:a-weight-power-regions}).

\subsection{Finite comparison and deterministic envelopes}

A finite secant argument explains the individual-arc positive result
without differentiating a law at zero. Compare two passive states for
the same nomination and two law profiles. Their difference can be
expressed as an electrical network with positive secant resistances
and explicit edge disturbances. For a target edge $a=(u,v)$, let $j$
be the unit electrical current from $u$ to $v$ in that secant network.
Its target flow difference is a linear combination of the disturbances,
with coefficients $j_e$ for $e\ne a$ and $-(1-j_a)$ on $a$, divided
by the positive target secant resistance. Classical confluence says
that the signs of these electrical currents are independent of all
positive resistances when the graph is series-parallel.

Consequently one may select, pointwise in flow, a maximum or minimum
allowed law on each edge according to the target's electrical sign.
The resulting deterministic envelope has a target state attaining the
uncertainty extremum. Compactness ensures that the selected law value
is realizable by an original parameter at that state. The construction
is uniform in the nomination, though the realizing profile can depend
on the state. \Cref{thm:a-bound-envelope-structural} proves this for
independent compact continuous law families. For quadratic interval
or finite resistance uncertainty, the envelope is directional quadratic.
Its primitive has a linear-size second-order-cone formulation.
A rational convex optimization and recovery argument gives polynomial
additive arc optimization for fixed nominations on every series-parallel
graph, without a rank bound (\cref{thm:a-bound-envelope-algorithm}).

Deterministic feasibility for rated monotone characteristics has related
series/parallel propagation methods: Zemanian's report uses endpoint
propagation and operating-point recovery
\citep[Conditions 2.1, Lemmas 4.1 and 5.1, Procedure 8.1]{zemanian1999-ratings}.
The present uncertainty-envelope and accuracy-bit output guarantees
should not be attributed to those deterministic procedures. Conversely,
our finite comparison identity does not create a new general circuit
monotonicity principle.

\subsection{What fails when the objective changes}

A triangle already separates weighted potentials from pairwise
potentials. With edges oriented $0\to1\to2\to0$, resistances
$(1,1,\theta)$, nominations $(2,-3,1)$, and
$c=(-5,12,-7)$, the circulation $q$ satisfies
$6q+3-\theta q^2=0$ on $-1/2<q<0$. Its weighted potential is
\[
 W=-\frac{105}{4}-12(q+1/4)^2.
\]
At $\theta=24$, $q=-1/4$ and $W=-105/4$. At
$\theta=16/3$ and $144$, the circulation is respectively $-3/8$ and
$-1/8$ and both values are smaller by $3/16$. Thus a two-point set
and its interval hull give different weighted-potential optima on one
cycle. The same graph remains in the universal class for every
individual potential difference. Weighted cancellation changes the
extremal geometry.

A rank-two theta block supplies the corresponding linear-flow
counterexample; a rank-three $K_4$ supplies the individual-arc
counterexample. Subdividing appropriate uncertain paths encodes
Subset Sum while preserving the small block rank. These constructions
produce the discrete boundaries in \cref{tab:main-boundaries}.
They are proved with explicit rational gaps and with positive integer
resistances where stated. For pressure objectives, multiplying all
resistances scales the gap. For flow objectives, it leaves flows
unchanged; flow-gap amplification instead scales nominations.

Two further distinctions prevent misleading interpretations. First,
the envelope theorem and hull equality do not settle exact scalar
arithmetic on unrestricted series-parallel graphs. A parallel probe
on a cactus branch transfers its radical sum to an arc comparison,
giving Square-Root-Sum hardness even with fixed unit nominations and
fixed integral resistances; a restricted probe family has the converse
reduction (\cref{thm:a-bound-arc-srs}). Second, hull preservation for
an extremum does not preserve exact realization of a target flow.
For a prescribed rational $\bar x$, conservation can be checked and
the equations $A^\top\pi=(\beta_e\bar x_e|\bar x_e|)_e$ are linear
in $\pi,\beta$. Interval realization is therefore an LP on any graph.
With two finite resistance choices per uncertain edge it is NP-complete
on one simple cycle. Existential unit-capacity design on that cycle
is also NP-complete (\cref{thm:a-bound-realization}). Its quantifier is
existential, whereas robust validation tests every scenario.
