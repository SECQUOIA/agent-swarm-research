\section{Certificates for physical outputs and original scenarios}
\label{sec:main-certificates}

The preceding algorithms specify a tolerance before computation. A
numerical solution raises a different question: what does its actual
output prove? For a fixed directional quadratic network with at least one
edge, rational nominations, and positive rational coefficients, rational
witnesses give an answer
independent of a solver's stopping rule.
They can then be attached to a verified uncertainty-envelope reduction.
The distinction between an exact verifier and a numerical producer is
central to the reproducibility claims.

Write
$E_e(t)=\beta_e^{\sgn(t)}|t|^3/3$ and $E=\sum_eE_e$,
where the positive coefficient is selected at zero by convention.
Let $x^*$ minimize $E$ on $Ax=b$, and let
$\beta_L=\min_{e,\sigma}\beta_e^\sigma>0$.
For simplicity the following statement uses a connected graph; the
appendix and deterministic certificate verifiers also permit components
with feasible nominations and separate gauges. The original-envelope
reader and numerical conservation-repair producer require a connected
graph.

\begin{theorem}[Rational energy and conserved-curvature certificates]
\label{thm:main-certificates}
Suppose $y\in\Q^m$ satisfies $Ay=b$, $p\in\Q^V$, and rational
$u_e\ge0$ satisfy
$u_e^2\beta_e^{\sgn((A^\top p)_e)}\ge|(A^\top p)_e|^3$.
Then
\[
 L=b^\top p-\tfrac23\sum_eu_e\le E(x^*)\le E(y),
 \qquad \delta=E(y)-L\ge0.
\]
Every rational $\eta\ge0$ with $\beta_L\eta^3\ge6\delta$
certifies $\|x^*-y\|_\infty\le\eta$.

More generally, suppose verified rational intervals $[l_e,u_e]$
contain both $y_e$ and $x_e^*$. Set $h_e=2\beta_e^+l_e$ when
$l_e>0$, $h_e=-2\beta_e^-u_e$ when $u_e<0$, and $h_e=0$ otherwise.
For a rational linear goal $w$, let $v$ be rational and put
$s=w-A^\top v$. If $s_e=0$ wherever $h_e=0$, define
$C(v)=\sum_{h_e>0}s_e^2/h_e$. Every rational $r\ge0$ with
$r^2\ge2\delta C(v)$ certifies
$|w^\top(x^*-y)|\le r$.
The least factor $C_*$ is attained by rational linear algebra whenever
the zero-edge condition is feasible. For $\delta>0$,
$\sqrt{2\delta C_*}$ is the exact supremum of $|w^\top d|$ over
$Ad=0$, $\tfrac12\sum_e h_ed_e^2\le\delta$. If the zero-edge
condition is infeasible, that supremum is infinite. All displayed
witness conditions are checkable by rational arithmetic in polynomial
time in their encoded lengths.
\end{theorem}

The first assertion is \cref{prop:a-cert-energy}; the goal assertions
are \cref{thm:a-cert-hessian,prop:a-cert-projection}.
The conserved trial requirement $Ay=b$ is essential in both. A small
conservation residual cannot replace it. Sharpness refers to the
\emph{conserved quadratic error set} just stated, not to every physical
error or to the additional coordinate intervals. At zero gap,
$x^*=y$ and all goals are exact without a curvature condition.

\subsection{From energy to intervals and linear goals}

The scalar conjugate is
$E_e^*(d)=\tfrac23\sqrt{|d|^3/\beta_e^{\sgn(d)}}$.
Rational upper root bounds make its dual lower energy check exact.
Uniform cubic convexity then converts the gap to a flow radius.
This is a concrete specialization of classical Fenchel duality and
primal-dual error control
\citep{boyd2004-convex,bartels2020-primal-dual}.

Separate-edge bounds can be sharpened using the reverse Bregman
expression
\[
 D_e(y_e,z)=E_e(y_e)-E_e(z)-E'_e(z)(y_e-z).
\]
For the physical state, conservation cancels the linear terms in its
sum, so $\sum_eD_e(y_e,x_e^*)=E(y)-E(x^*)\le\delta$.
Each term is nonnegative. Rational outward boundaries at which
$D_e(y_e,z)\ge\delta$ therefore enclose $x_e^*$ and can be refined
by rational bisection (\cref{prop:a-cert-bregman}). The order of the
two arguments matters. This is the convex-function divergence of
\citet{bregman1967-relaxation}, with the unknown state in the second
argument; exchanging the arguments does not preserve the asserted gap
identity. Monotone law evaluation and path sums then give deterministic
potential-difference and weighted-potential intervals.

Separate edges discard conservation information. A stronger object is
the compact conserved energy set
$S(y)=\{x:Ax=b,\ E(x)\le E(y)\}$, which contains $x^*$.
For a linear goal, its support dual uses rational multipliers
$\lambda>0,v$ and upper roots satisfying
$R_e^2\lambda\beta_e^{\sgn(s_e)}\ge|s_e|^3$,
$s=w-A^\top v$. They certify
\[
 w^\top x\le \lambda E(y)+v^\top b+\tfrac23\sum_eR_e
 \quad(x\in S(y)).
\]
Applying the same construction to $-w$ gives an interval. If
$E(y)>E(x^*)$, the infimum of rational bounds equals the exact
support value, by Slater duality and rational approximation
(\cref{thm:a-cert-support}). This is a completeness statement for
witnesses, not a polynomial-time generic producer. The curvature
certificate in \cref{thm:main-certificates} is a directly computable
quadratic alternative. Its rational KKT system remains valid with
singular gauges and zero-curvature edges; the obstruction is exactly
a goal detecting a circulation supported on the zero-curvature subgraph.
The electrical cut/cycle projection is classical
\citep[Chapter 2, Section 4]{lyons2016-probability}.

For a worked comparison, take two source-to-sink paths each of length
$L$, coefficient one, and total transfer two. The physical flow is one
on every edge. A rational conserved trial with $1+\eps$ on one path
and $1-\eps$ on the other, $0<\eps<1$, has exact gap $2L\eps^2$.
The exact support interval for a selected first-path edge has width
$2\eps$; rational upper and lower dual witnesses attain it when
$\eps$ is rational. Separate-edge Bregman intervals have width
$2\sqrt{2L}\eps+o(\eps)$, whereas the optimized curvature interval
has width $2\eps+o(\eps)$ for fixed $L$ as $\eps\downarrow0$.
\Cref{ex:a-cert-paths} supplies the exact witnesses and asymptotics.
This example shows precisely which information is recovered by
conservation, without suggesting that its elementary physical state
is computationally difficult.

\subsection{Certifying the original uncertainty question}

A correct certificate for a deterministic network does not alone prove
that this network is the envelope of a stated uncertainty instance.
The integrated verifier therefore checks the original graph, nominations,
allowed resistance intervals or finite sets, target edge, target sense,
series-parallel structure, and exact electrical signs. It reconstructs
the target-specific envelope from these data. It then verifies one
conserved-flow certificate for that envelope and a second for the
chosen original resistance scenario. Rational interval subtraction gives
a certified scenario loss; optional curvature witnesses
sharpen the target bounds.

Resolved envelope signs permit an exactly optimal original endpoint
scenario, even when its state is irrational. At an unresolved sign,
intervals quantify how much choosing the wrong directional coefficient
can matter; the loss is not silently set to zero. Exact optimality
also holds when tied coefficients or an exact zero flow make the
choice immaterial (\cref{prop:a-cert-signs}). These checks separate
original-scenario optimality from exact numerical evaluation of its
flow. Common rational scaling of nominations and resistances has an
exact covariance for all energy, interval, and goal inequalities,
proved in \cref{eq:a-cert-scaling}.

\subsection{What the implementation and saved evidence establish}

The standalone archive contains the complete manuscript source, saved
rational witnesses, exact verifiers, diagnostic scripts, and instructions
for reproduction. The core certificate replay requires only Python's
standard library and remains active with \texttt{python -S -O}.
The numerical producer uses CVXPY and Clarabel to propose flows and
potentials, then repairs conservation and constructs rational bounds.
It accepts no requested accuracy guarantee and is not an implementation
of every theoretical algorithm in this paper. The supplied-block weighted
solver likewise does not implement graph decomposition or the full
varying-nomination quantifier-elimination algorithms.

The retained six-edge witness has uniform width below
$3.405\cdot10^{-4}$, maximum Bregman flow width below
$2.894\cdot10^{-6}$, and maximum conservation-aware goal width below
$9.447\cdot10^{-7}$. These are verified enclosure widths, not observed
true errors. The original-instance benchmark contains ten certified
cases, nine with exact endpoint optimality. It includes an 80-edge graph
of rank 39, a full coefficient ratio of $3\cdot10^6$, supply
$10^{-8}$, reversed orientations, zero nominations, and unresolved signs.
The ambiguous-sign case retains a positive loss bound; goal information
improves its verified target width and loss bound without claiming exact
sign resolution. The full values, conditions, and implementation roles
are given in \cref{sec:a-cert}.

One case uses the topology and pipe geometry of the open dataset
accompanying \citet{vrachimis2018-hydraulic}, distributed at
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1185136}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.1185136}.
The packaged instance contains synthetic quadratic coefficients,
nominations, and intervals. It does not reproduce the source hydraulic
laws or leak estimates, and the archive does not redistribute the
original INP file. Numerical benchmark reproduction runs in an isolated
copy because its producer rewrites neighboring JSON records. Saved exact
checks establish the validity of the particular witnesses; the proofs
establish the universal mathematical statements.
