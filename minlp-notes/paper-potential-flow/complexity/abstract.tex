Passive potential-flow networks have a unique state at each admissible
nomination and law profile, but optimizing that state can have sharply
different complexity depending on the observable, topology, uncertainty,
and required output. We develop a unified analysis in the Turing bit
model. At fixed maximum cycle rank per biconnected block, prescribed
potential differences over balanced nomination boxes and independent
coefficient intervals admit certified additive optimization in time
polynomial in input length and accuracy bits, while individual arc
extrema have exact algebraic algorithms for dense polynomial laws.
Exact pressure comparison already contains Square-Root Sum on cacti;
the single-source/sink subclass is equivalent to that problem.
We characterize the universal resistance-hull classes: trees for weighted
potentials, cacti for pairwise potential differences and linear flows,
and series-parallel graphs for individual arcs. Weighted optimization
extends to arbitrarily many cactus blocks at fixed nomination dimension
or objective support, to bounded-rank blocks on one nomination line, and
to several parameters at fixed total noncactus rank. With fixed
nominations, cycle-local correlations admit exact capacity recovery and
additive convex design, whereas global correlations and unrestricted
convex performance yield hardness. Finally, rational energy, Bregman,
and conservation-aware support certificates connect approximate
computation to exact original-instance guarantees. Throughout, rational
parameter scenarios, generally irrational physical states, exact
thresholds, and high-precision additive outputs are kept distinct.
