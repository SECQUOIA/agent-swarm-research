\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:main-conclusion}

Passive-flow optimization has several distinct sources of difficulty.
Interacting cycles control the dimension of local algebra, objective
support controls nomination localization, and independence determines
whether local responses can be optimized separately. Exact scalar
comparison adds an arithmetic question even when the graph and the
optimizing parameter scenario are simple. The resulting classifications
explain why a rational optimizer, an exact local state, and a polynomial
additive interval are different achievements.

The positive results permit unbounded network size and many uncertain
coefficients under explicit structural hypotheses. Fixed maximum block
rank suffices for additive terminal-drop optimization and exact
polynomial-law arc validation. Weighted objectives require additional
support or nomination structure, and the completed scalar and hybrid
extensions describe how far the many-block argument currently goes.
Cycle-local correlations preserve exact capacity recovery, while global
correlations can make even total-flow performance hard. The direction
of a dissipation objective reverses the optimization boundary.

Three external questions remain particularly precise. The full exact
threshold complexity of multiple-entry cactus MPD is not classified by
the single-source/sink Square-Root-Sum equivalence. The unrestricted
several-parameter sum of bounded-rank weighted block responses still
needs an accuracy-bit algorithm or a matching obstruction. The exponents
in the fixed-rank algorithms are parameter dependent; whether sharper
FPT bounds are available is separate from their proved XP guarantees.
None of these questions is a missing step in a theorem claimed here.

The rational certificate framework provides a practical interface to the
proven model while preserving these distinctions. A numerical method
may propose a useful state, but its original-instance guarantees come
from conserved rational witnesses and exact checks. This permits useful
certified computation without converting a solver tolerance into an
unsupported universal claim.
