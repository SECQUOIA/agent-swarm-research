\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:main-introduction}

In a passive network, conservation and the potential drops across edges
determine the flow. Each scenario of nominations (injections and withdrawals) and
physical coefficients therefore has a unique state, the minimizer of a
strictly convex energy. When nominations or coefficients are uncertain,
however, finding a scenario with the largest pressure difference or arc
flow is a nonconvex global optimization problem with direct operational
meaning: a worst-case arc flow tests a capacity, a potential difference
measures a required pressure range, and a weighted potential objective
measures several locations together.

Potential-based models of this kind describe passive gas and water
transport and nonlinear resistive circuits; the survey of
\citet{pfetsch2026-potential-based-flowsan-overview} describes their common
structure and physical interpretations. In gas networks the questions
above arise in robust booking and nomination validation
\citep{labbe2020-bookings-in-the-european-gas,thurauf2022-deciding-the-feasibility-of-a,amann2018-deciding-robust-feasibility-and-infeasibility}.
Network design adds discrete or continuous investment decisions to the
same physical model
\citep{robinius2019-discrete-sizing,klimm2026-approximating-the-network-design-problem},
and robust design contains worst-case state optimization as a subproblem
\citep{thurauf2025-adjustable-robust-nonlinear-network-design}.
We work with the abstract passive equations, principally with the
quadratic law $\pi_u-\pi_v=\beta_e x_e|x_e|$. The potential may already
be a transformed physical quantity, such as squared pressure. Our results
concern this mathematical model; they do not replace a system's complete
engineering model, controls, or admissible operating conditions.

Four choices govern the complexity: how cycles interact inside blocks;
whether the objective inspects one arc, compares two potentials, or sums
measurements; whether uncertain coefficients are independent intervals,
finite sets, or correlated; and whether the output is an exact threshold
decision or an additive interval with an admissible near-optimal
scenario. The last choice is easy to overlook. Two parallel quadratic
paths already produce a square root, and a chain of independent cycles
accumulates many roots whose sum is easy to enclose but has no known
polynomial-time exact comparison. We therefore require running time
polynomial in $\log(1/\eps)$ (accuracy-bit polynomial), not in
$1/\eps$, and require returned rational parameters to satisfy the
original scenario constraints exactly, although their physical state may
be irrational.

\subsection{Main results}

Let $r_{\max}$ be the largest cycle rank of a biconnected block. Cacti
are the graphs with $r_{\max}\le1$; series-parallel graphs are those
without a $K_4$ minor. Unless stated otherwise, laws are quadratic and
scenario domains carry no additional operating constraints.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Block rank localizes nominations}
(\cref{sec:main-localization}). A law-independent face reduction
confines the free nominations of a potential-difference maximizer to
one block.
With fixed-dimensional real algebraic computation this gives, for each
fixed bound on $r_{\max}$, accuracy-bit optimization of a prescribed
potential difference over a nomination box and independent resistance
intervals, and exact algebraic arc extrema, hence exact robust capacity
validation, also for dense polynomial laws (\cref{thm:main-block}).
Block rank cannot be replaced by treewidth two,
series-parallel structure, or feedback vertex number one unless
$\mathrm P=\NP$ (\cref{prop:a-blk-limits}).

\item \emph{Exact comparison is an arithmetic problem}
(\cref{thm:main-arithmetic}). On cacti with fixed resistances, exact
single-source/sink comparison of the maximum potential difference (MPD)
is polynomial-time equivalent to Square-Root Sum; general exact
quadratic MPD lies in $\ETR\subseteq\PSPACE$. Fixed fractional power
laws with exponents above one keep the additive algorithms at fixed
block rank, but exact arc comparison is Square-Root-Sum hard on one
cycle for every reduced exponent above one with even denominator, such
as the water head-loss exponent $1.852=463/250$.

\item \emph{The observable determines the topology boundary}
(\cref{sec:main-observables}). For quadratic laws and fixed nominations,
the graphs on which independent resistance sets can always be replaced
by their interval hulls are precisely the trees for weighted potentials, the cacti for potential
differences and linear flows, and the series-parallel graphs for
individual arcs (\cref{thm:main-hulls}). The attainable flow region is
convex for every nomination and resistance box precisely on cacti;
potential regions (with one grounded vertex) and joint flow--potential
regions, precisely on trees. For fixed nominations on series-parallel
graphs, choosing an extreme allowed law on each edge by its electrical
sign gives polynomial
additive arc optimization with no rank bound
(\cref{thm:a-bound-envelope-algorithm}). With independent finite
resistance choices, weighted potentials on one cycle, potential
differences and linear flows on a rank-two block, and arc flows on a
rank-three block are already NP-hard (\cref{tab:main-boundaries}).

\item \emph{Weighted objectives need more structure}
(\cref{sec:main-weighted}). With growing support and a nomination box,
weighted-potential threshold attainment is NP-complete on trees
(\cref{thm:a-weight-tree-hard}). Accuracy-bit algorithms hold on cacti
with fixed nomination dimension or fixed support, for bounded-rank blocks
along one nomination line, and for several nomination parameters when the
total rank of noncactus blocks is fixed (\cref{thm:main-weighted}). The
remaining case, several shared parameters and many higher-rank blocks,
is isolated in \cref{eq:a-wblk-residual}.

\item \emph{Correlations and the direction of optimization decide design
complexity} (\cref{sec:main-design}). For fixed nominations on a cactus, parameter
polytopes within individual cycles give exact rational parameter
recovery for arc extrema and capacities, additive minimization of dense
convex polynomial flow objectives, and additive convex maximization
through a fixed number of linear measurements (\cref{thm:main-design}).
Capacity feasibility, robust capacity tests, and exact arc extrema
survive even a global parameter polytope. Maximizing a convex function
that couples all flows is strongly NP-hard even with independent
intervals, and global coefficient correlations make total-flow maximization and minimum
dissipation strongly NP-hard on cacti. In contrast, with fixed
nominations on any connected graph, maximum dissipation over a positive
rational resistance polytope admits a polynomial additive algorithm
(\cref{thm:main-energy}).

\item \emph{Rational certificates connect numerical and exact results}
(\cref{sec:main-certificates}). Rational energy, Bregman, support, and
curvature witnesses certify flows, potentials, linear goals, and
original uncertainty scenarios without trusting a solver's status
(\cref{thm:main-certificates}); the curvature bound is sharp for its
quadratic error set.
\end{enumerate}

The primary sources we inspected do not supply the combined
fixed-block-rank nomination and coefficient guarantees of
\cref{thm:main-block}, the weighted extensions of
\cref{thm:main-weighted}, or the objective-dependent classifications and
output boundaries; within the limits of the comparison below, these
scoped results and their network-specific proofs are the contributions
of this paper. The energy principle, electrical adjoints, circuit
confluence, parametric linear optimization, quantifier elimination,
analytic-panel approximation, and zonotope enumeration are classical
ingredients, credited where used. We claim no novelty for these
ingredients or for general principles of nonlinear network sensitivity,
circuit monotonicity, or analytic approximation, and no priority for
endpoint and envelope identities (we could not inspect
\citet{hasler1993-parameter-tolerances} in full).

\subsection{Relation to earlier work}
\label{subsec:main-literature}

\paragraph{Passive states and circuit structure.}
The primitive-energy characterization of passive states goes back to
nonlinear network theory: \citet{duffin1947-nonlinear-networks-iia},
\citet{birkhoff1956-non-linear-network-problems}, and
\citet{minty1960-monotone-networks} establish variational and monotone
foundations under their respective law hypotheses. We give a
self-contained existence proof for continuous strictly increasing laws
vanishing at zero, including bounded laws; surjectivity is not needed.
The grounded Laplacian and adjoint calculation have direct predecessors:
\citet[Section IV-A]{misra2015-maximum-throughput-problem-in-dissipative}
identify potentials and the inverse weighted Laplacian as the gradient
and Hessian of the conjugate energy. For linear two-terminal networks,
effective resistance is concave in the edge resistances
\citep{shannon1956-concavity}, and the electrical cut/cycle projection is
classical \citep[Chapter 2, Section 4]{lyons2016-probability}. Our
maximum-dissipation theorem uses the cubic analogue of the first fact,
and the conservation-aware certificates use the second.

The topology-based sign mechanism is classical circuit confluence
\citep{duffin1965-topology-of-series-parallel-networks}.
\citet[Theorem 3 and conclusion]{chauffoureaux1990-monotonicity}
use a linear-circuit criterion to study nonlinear parameter monotonicity
and extremal evaluation. The undated Wang--Hasler report studies
convexity of scalar transfer characteristics as source values vary,
including structural sufficient conditions and ladder networks
\citep[Theorems 1, 4, and 5]{wanghasler1997-convexity}. Our convexity
statements concern a different object: the vector of attainable flows
or potentials under independent resistance variation. The universal
nondegeneracy characterization of cacti by
\citet{brandenberg2025-differential} concerns linear differential-flow
polytopes, quantified over all positive elasticity vectors and all
nomination and capacity bounds; this property differs from our
resistance-attainable nonlinear state regions.

\paragraph{Robust feasibility and network complexity.}
The joint product of a balanced nomination box and positive resistance
intervals is established in \citet[Section 3.3]{amann2019-exact-methods-for-two-stage},
and the circulation sign-region method and cycle-capacity linearization
appear in \citet{amann2018-deciding-robust-feasibility-and-infeasibility}.
Gonz\'alez Grand\'on, Heitsch, and Henrion combine random loads with
robust roughness uncertainty on trees \citep{gonzalez2017-joint}; we
treat deterministic worst-case scenarios on graphs with cycles.
\citet[Section 6]{labbe2020-bookings-in-the-european-gas} leave booking
feasibility on a single nonlinear cycle open;
\citet[Section 6]{labbe2021-deciding-feasibility-on-a-cycle} later give a
polynomial Turing-time algorithm for single-cycle booking validation
under rational-data hypotheses.
Our block-rank algorithms allow unboundedly many blocks and cycles and
handle the arithmetic of their combined objective.
Th\"urauf's nomination hardness construction
\citep{thurauf2022-deciding-the-feasibility-of-a} supplies an important
boundary: its main block has unbounded rank although the graph is
series-parallel. We credit that construction and derive the required
bit-gap and arc-probe consequences.

\citet[Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 4.3]{gro2017-algorithmic-results-for-potential-based}
give homogeneous series-parallel reductions and discuss approximate
root arithmetic in the Turing model. Our exact comparison results
separate the cost of performing those reductions from the cost of
ordering the resulting radical expression. The January 2026 overview
still asks for the complexity of nonlinear cactus MPD
\citep[pp.~9--10]{pfetsch2026-potential-based-flowsan-overview}. We resolve
its additive version and classify the exact single-source/sink subclass,
but not exact MPD over arbitrary cactus nomination boxes.

\paragraph{Local formulas, approximation, and design.}
Gotzes, Heitsch, Henrion, and Schultz derive quadratic-radical circulation
formulas on affine nomination rays and discuss node-disjoint cycles
with attached trees \citep[Theorem 6, pp.~18 and 24 of the author manuscript]
{gotzes2016-quantification}. Vigneron combines algebraic functions through
common arrangements and approximate summation; his bit-model schemes
have polynomial dependence on $1/\eps$
\citep[Theorem 6 and Sections 2.3, 3.2]{vigneron2014-geometric-optimization-and-sums-of}.
Our weighted results require polynomial dependence on accuracy bits,
express all surrogates in the same original nomination coordinates, and
recover rational original parameters.
\Cref{sec:a-weighted-blocks} compares the scalar construction with
analytic enclosure, rational algebraic approximation, and semialgebraic
parametrization results
\citep{petras2002-approximation,borcea2006-rational-algebraic,yomdin2014-parametrizations,binyamini2019-complex-cells};
the difference lies in the interface.

The exact cycle-root recovery refines a classical LP-bisection mechanism
\citep{agrawal2020-dqcp,megiddo1979-rational} with dense-degree separation
and parameter output bounds. Few-measurement convex maximization uses
zonotope methods \citep{onn2004-convex,ferrez2005-fixed-rank}.
In network design, \citet[Theorem 4.7]{robinius2019-discrete-sizing}
treat hardness of minimum-cost discrete arc sizing subject to potential
feasibility, already on trees, and credit earlier water-network hardness
of Yates, Templeman, and Boffey; our finite resistance choices are
adversarial scenarios rather than investment decisions.
\citet{klimm2026-approximating-the-network-design-problem} choose
installed arcs and conductances under costs and a potential range
(convex without fixed costs, Corollary 4; an FPTAS on series-parallel
networks with strictly positive variable costs and finite conductance
bounds, Theorem 11); these investment objectives and relative-error
guarantees differ from our scenario optimization.
\citet{thurauf2025-adjustable-robust-nonlinear-network-design} reduce
fixed-design robust checking to polynomially many nonlinear worst-case
subproblems and use an adversarial design algorithm; a polynomial number
of subproblems does not by itself make each of them polynomial.

\Cref{sec:main-model} fixes the model and output conventions; each
later main-text result names the appendix statements it combines, and
Appendices \ref{sec:a-pre-preliminaries}--\ref{sec:a-cert} contain all
proofs in dependency order.
