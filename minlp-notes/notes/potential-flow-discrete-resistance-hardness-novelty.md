# Two-point resistance hardness: bounded novelty audit

Date: 2026-09-05. Candidate: [discrete resistance hardness](potential-flow-discrete-resistance-hardness.md). This is a primary-source comparison, not a proof audit.

The precise combination remains a plausible new structural hardness result: one theta subdivision, fixed small integral nominations, a common quadratic passive law, independent two-point positive resistances, and a prescribed pressure-difference objective with an explicit gap. No equivalent result was located in the open primary sources inspected. General resistor-extrema hardness and discrete pipe-sizing hardness are already known, so neither broad claim is new. The useful distinction is hardness at cycle rank two despite a polynomial high-precision algorithm for the corresponding **interval** resistance problem.

## Closest circuit complexity predecessors

Lawrence P. Huang and Randal E. Bryant, *Intractability in Linear Switch-Level Simulation*, IEEE Transactions on Computer-Aided Design of Integrated Circuits and Systems 12(6), 829–836 (1993). The [author-institution abstract](https://research.ibm.com/publications/intractability-in-linear-switch-level-simulation) states NP-completeness of computing extremal steady-state voltages in general MOS networks under unknown values in the linear switch-level model. It describes voltage-controlled linear resistors and grounded capacitors. The full 1993 proof was not retrieved; the publication link failed. This establishes a direct historical predecessor to the broad idea of hardness of resistor-voltage uncertainty analysis, but the inspected abstract does not give bounded cycle rank, a common quadratic passive law, or the candidate's independent two-point resistance model.

Randal E. Bryant, J. D. Tygar, and Lawrence P. Huang, *Geometric Characterization of Series-Parallel Variable Resistor Networks*, IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems I 41(11), 686–698 (1994), DOI 10.1109/81.331520, [open author preprint](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~tygar/papers/Geometric_characterization_of_series-parallel/Bryant_Journal_preprint.pdf). Inspected the introduction, construction, complexity discussion, and conclusion. PDF pages 2–4 explicitly discuss general voltage-range hardness and independent variable linear elements. Theorem/construction results give a polygon with at most (2k) vertices and (O(nk)) computation for series-parallel circuits; §6.2 extends this to all nodes of grounded-tree circuits. PDF page 27 describes the straight-line locus produced by changing one element in a linear circuit. These are linear-circuit results, not nonlinear theta hardness.

Our comparison: linear separate monotonicity means that replacing two-point resistance choices by their intervals preserves each pressure extremum. Thus the candidate's discrete-versus-interval separation requires its nonlinear law. General linear-circuit hardness is compatible with that fact because endpoint selection may itself be hard on unrestricted topology. Do not claim that separate monotonicity automatically yields a polynomial algorithm on every graph.

## Discrete pipe sizing is already hard on trees

Martin Robinius, Lars Schewe, Martin Schmidt, Detlef Stolten, Johannes Thürauf, and Lara Welder, *Robust Optimal Discrete Arc Sizing for Tree-Shaped Potential Networks*, Computational Optimization and Applications 73, 791–819 (2019), DOI 10.1007/s10589-019-00085-x. Inspected the [open 2018 preprint](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/6447.pdf), especially model (5), PDF pages 6–7, and Theorem 4.7, PDF page 19. The model minimizes arc-size costs subject to flow equations and potential bounds for every demand scenario. Theorem 4.7 states NP-completeness even for trees, crediting Yates, Templeman, and Boffey (1984) for hardness. The finite scenario reduction and resulting MILP do not make the discrete design problem polynomial-time solvable.

Our comparison: this is a different optimization problem. Costs and potential feasibility constraints impose tradeoffs on the sizing choices. The candidate maximizes one pressure difference over otherwise unrestricted physical states with no cost budget or scenario-feasibility filtering. On a tree with fixed nominations, that candidate objective is linear in independent resistances and optimizes coordinatewise. Consequently, the existing tree-sizing hardness neither proves the candidate's theta result nor contradicts its cactus/interval discussion. It does rule out advertising the result as the first hardness theorem for discrete resistance or diameter choices.

Historical source to retain, not independently read here: Yates, Templeman, and Boffey, *The computational complexity of the problem of determining least capital cost designs for water supply networks*, Engineering Optimization 7(2), 143–155 (1984), DOI 10.1080/03052158408960635. Its exact reduction is not needed to distinguish the objectives above, but should be inspected before any broad discrete-design priority claim.

## Robust nonlinear-flow sources

Aßmann, Liers, Stingl, and Vera, *Deciding Robust (In-)Feasibility Using Set Containment: An Application to Uncertain Gas Networks*, [arXiv:1808.10241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.10241). Section 4.1.4, PDF page 15, fixes nominations and explicitly takes independent positive **interval** pressure-loss factors. Sections 4.2 and 4.3 study trees and sign-region reductions; the latter includes a single-cycle treatment. The general framework uses polynomial set containment and SOS methods. No discrete two-point theta hardness or cycle-rank-two approximation obstruction appeared in the inspected sections or targeted full-text searches. The uncertain-resistance passive-flow model is established, not a new model introduced by this candidate.

Thürauf, *Adjustable robust nonlinear network design without controllable elements under load scenario uncertainties*, Mathematical Programming (2025), DOI 10.1007/s10107-025-02207-2, [publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-025-02207-2). Definition (2) expressly permits compact nonconvex or discrete **load** uncertainty, and the formulation separates network design from adversarial load scenarios. This is relevant context for discrete uncertainty, but the uncertain quantities are nominations rather than independent resistances. Do not infer an equivalent resistance-hardness theorem from the word “discrete.”

The earlier [cactus-hull source audit](potential-flow-cactus-hulls-novelty.md) records classical confluence and an unread Hasler–Wang NOLTA1993 paper on nonlinear parameter tolerances. The latter remains a residual search gap. Its title alone supports neither duplication nor priority of this complexity theorem. A future retrieval should check for explicit combinatorial lower bounds and restricted topology, in addition to monotonicity criteria.

## What the proposed contribution actually is

The series-path sum of binary resistance choices is a standard Subset Sum encoding. The nontrivial network-specific component is realizing a strict pressure maximum at one prescribed aggregate resistance, using positive coefficients and one common passive quadratic law on a fixed-size theta core. Subdivision preserves cycle rank while allowing arbitrarily many independent choices. The rational gap then turns the exact encoding into a barrier for algorithms polynomial in input length and **precision bits**.

Later mathematical scope correction (2026-09-05): the original displayed gap is polynomially encodable but need not be inverse-polynomial in input bit length. However, common resistance scaling multiplies all potentials and their differences while preserving flows, nominations, and topology. Writing `T=(31K+18S)^2`, the original gap is `Delta=6/T`. The integer-resistance scale `6nK`, followed by a further common scale `T`, gives gap `36nK`. Both scales have polynomial binary encoding length. Thus absolute-error-one value approximation is NP-hard on this unnormalized integer-data class; an algorithm polynomial in input length and `1/epsilon` would also contradict `P!=NP`. The earlier exclusion of a constant-error consequence was too broad. This correction does not establish strong NP-hardness, relative-error hardness, or hardness with normalized resistance coefficients or objective range. No NP-membership claim is assessed or added by this audit. The primary-source findings above are unchanged.

Recommended wording: “For common quadratic passive laws, two-point resistance uncertainty is NP-hard already on a theta subdivision with maximum degree three and fixed small nominations. The reduction gives an explicit gap and separates finite resistance choices from their interval relaxation at fixed cycle rank. General circuit uncertainty and discrete sizing hardness are known; no matching restricted theorem was found in the open primary literature inspected.”

The candidate is more plausibly distinctive as a **structural complexity separation** than as a new combinatorial reduction technique. Its cactus side remains subject to the older nonlinear tolerance priority caveat; its discrete-hardness conclusion stands independently if the proof audits pass.
