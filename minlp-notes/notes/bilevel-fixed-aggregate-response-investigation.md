# Exact bilevel optimization through a fixed aggregate response dimension

Date: 2026-09-05. Status: promoted after two independent full audits to
[the result](../results/bilevel-fixed-aggregate-response-algorithm.md).
This note preserves the candidate proof and direction selection. The
canonical result contains the same reviewed theorem and final source links.

## Direction selection

Three directions were considered after reading the current repository:

1. **Bilevel process design with many resource-allocation decisions.** A
   follower has box-bounded units, positive diagonal quadratic local costs,
   and a fixed number of resource and nonlinear aggregate coordinates.
   A few leader prices or design parameters affect its costs and capacities.
   This direction gives the complete candidate below, including a nonconvex
   aggregate follower cost.
2. **Few nonconvex directions in signomial or quadratic optimization.**
   Simply bounding a nonconvex rank does not control the complexity of a
   general high-dimensional polyhedral constraint set. This appeared too
   close to existing low-rank optimization results and their hardness limits;
   no new theorem is retained from this route.
3. **Feasibility certificates from quantitative regularity.** Margin and
   nonsingular-Jacobian conditions suggest rational certificates through
   interval or Newton estimates, but the natural versions overlap standard
   error-bound and feasibility-verification theory. No novelty claim is
   retained. The exact arithmetic obstruction for nonlinear local response
   functions is recorded separately below.

## 1. Model and candidate theorem

Fix the integers `r,s,k_eq,k_in`. All polynomials below have rational
coefficients and are supplied by explicit monomial lists. Their total
degrees may grow, provided they are polynomially bounded by the input
length; dense encodings and unary-exponent monomial encodings satisfy this
condition. The usual fixed-degree sparse encoding is a special case.
Sparse binary encodings with exponentially large degrees are outside the
polynomial-time claim. Write `delta>=2` for the maximum input degree.

Let `C subset R^r` be compact and described by a rational quantifier-free
polynomial formula. The leader chooses `x in C`. The follower has `N`
variables `z in [l(x),u(x)]`, where the endpoints are polynomial functions
satisfying `l_i(x)<=u_i(x)` on `C`. Fixed rational bounds are a special case.
The box union over compact `C` is bounded. Its feasible set is

```
P(x)={z in [l(x),u(x)]: A z=b(x), H z<=d(x)},
```

where `A` and `H` are fixed rational matrices with `k_eq` and `k_in` rows.
Only their right-hand sides depend on the leader, polynomially. Some
leader choices may have no feasible follower response.

Let `U(x)` be an `s times N` matrix of rational polynomials in the leader
coordinates; a fixed rational aggregate matrix is a special case. The
follower minimizes

```
f(x,z)=sum_i [a_i(x) z_i^2/2+c_i(x) z_i] + phi(x,U(x) z),
```

The polynomial coefficients obey `a_i(x)>0` for every `x in C`. No convexity assumption is imposed on `phi`.
Thus the follower itself can be nonconvex. The positive local quadratic
coefficients are the structural restriction used for elimination.

The upper objective `F(x,z)` and upper constraints `g_j(x,z)<=0` are
arbitrary rational polynomials supplied by explicit monomial lists. Cross
terms between follower variables are allowed.
There can be an unbounded number of upper constraints. The optimistic
bilevel problem is

```
min F(x,z)
s.t. x in C, g_j(x,z)<=0 for every j,
     z in argmin{f(x,y): y in P(x)}.
```

**Candidate theorem.** For fixed `r,s,k_eq,k_in`, under the encoding
condition above, this problem is globally
solvable in polynomial rational-input bit time. Feasibility is decidable,
and if feasible an exact real-algebraic optimizer and value of polynomial
encoding length can be returned. Neither the follower dimension `N` nor
the number of box bounds or upper constraints is fixed.

A fixed number of binary leader variables may be encoded in `C`. General
integer leader domains and follower integrality are outside the statement.
The polynomial exponent depends on the fixed dimensions. Equivalently, the
algorithm is polynomial in input length and actual input degree for fixed
dimensions, without an encoding assumption on the degrees.

## 2. Every follower global optimum has a compressed KKT representation

For a fixed leader and a follower local or global minimizer, first-order
necessity on the polyhedron `P(x)` gives multipliers `lambda in R^k_eq`
and `mu in R_+^k_in`, with resource complementarity

```
mu_j ((H z)_j-d_j(x))=0.
```

No Slater condition or independence of active rows is needed here. The
normal cone of a polyhedron is generated by its equality normals and
nonnegative combinations of its active inequality normals; this follows
from the linear alternative for its tangent cone.

Introduce `w=U(x) z` and define

```
B_i(x,w,lambda,mu)
 = c_i(x)+(U(x)^T grad_w phi(x,w))_i
          +(A^T lambda)_i+(H^T mu)_i.
```

Stationarity with the box normal cone is equivalent to

```
z_i = clip_[l_i(x),u_i(x)](-B_i/a_i(x)).
```

This equivalence uses the positivity of `a_i(x)`: the stationarity equation
and endpoint signs are exactly the optimum conditions for a scalar strictly
convex quadratic with this derivative. It does not assert convexity of the
full follower objective. A compressed point satisfying these equations,
resource feasibility, complementarity, and `w=U(x)z` is a KKT point, which may
be a nonglobal stationary point. Global optimality is imposed later by a
separate quantified comparison.

All such points are described using only

```
v=(x,w,lambda,mu) in R^(r+s+k_eq+k_in),
```

a fixed dimension. The original `N` follower coordinates are rational
functions of these coordinates within the clipping regimes.

## 3. Only polynomially many joint clipping regimes need be considered

The regime for coordinate `i` is determined by the signs of

```
L_i(v)=B_i(v)+a_i(x) l_i(x),
R_i(v)=B_i(v)+a_i(x) u_i(x).
```

Each is a polynomial of degree `O(delta)` in the fixed-dimensional vector `v`.
On `C`, `L_i<=R_i`. Use `z_i=l_i` if `L_i>=0`; otherwise use `z_i=u_i`
if `R_i<=0`; otherwise use `z_i=-B_i/a_i`. At a boundary the formulas
agree. This rule also handles `l_i=u_i`.

Enumerate all realizable sign conditions of these `2N` polynomials,
including zero signs. Standard fixed-dimensional sign determination yields
only polynomially many conditions in polynomial bit time. A condition
selects a regime simultaneously for all coordinates; no Cartesian product
of `N` independent regime choices is enumerated.

Put

```
Q(x)=prod_i a_i(x)>0 on C.
```

For each sign condition `sigma`, define polynomial numerators
`Z_i^sigma(v)=Q(x) z_i^sigma(v)`: these are `l_i Q`, `u_i Q`, or
`-B_i prod_{j!=i} a_j`. Here the bound symbols denote their polynomial
functions of `x`. Their degrees are `O((N+1) delta)`. They involve only the
fixed number of coordinates in `v`, so their
expanded encodings and coefficient bit lengths are polynomial.

Let `K_sigma(v)` be the conjunction of its sign condition and

```
U(x) Z^sigma = Q w,
A Z^sigma = Q b(x),
H Z^sigma <= Q d(x),
mu >= 0,
mu_j [(H Z^sigma)_j-Q d_j(x)]=0.
```

These are polynomial constraints of degree `O((N+1) delta)`. Their feasible points
are exactly the compressed KKT points in this clipping regime, provided
`x in C`.

The follower objective numerator is

```
R_sigma(v)=sum_i [a_i(x) (Z_i^sigma)^2/2
                 +c_i(x) Z_i^sigma Q]
           +phi(x,w) Q^2.
```

It equals `Q^2 f(x,z^sigma(v))` whenever `K_sigma` holds. As above, its
degree and encoding length are polynomial. Define a formula

```
E(x,w,lambda,mu,tau)
 = OR_sigma [K_sigma(v) AND tau Q(x)^2=R_sigma(v)].
```

On `C`, this describes every KKT point and its actual follower value.

## 4. Quantified comparison imposes global follower optimality

For any `x` with `P(x)` nonempty, the follower objective attains a global
minimum because its feasible set is compact. Every global minimizer is a
KKT point by Section 2. Consequently, an encoded KKT point has a globally
minimal follower value exactly when

```
forall w',lambda',mu',tau':
  E(x,w',lambda',mu',tau') implies tau<=tau'.
```

This step neither treats KKT conditions as sufficient for nonconvex
optimality nor assumes a unique follower minimizer. Comparing against all
KKT points is sufficient because this comparison set contains all feasible
global minimizers and contains only feasible points.

For each regime, substitute `z=Z^sigma/Q` in each upper polynomial.
Multiplication by the positive denominator `Q^delta` produces polynomial upper
inequalities and an equation expressing its objective value `eta`. Although
the number of follower variables is unbounded, every upper monomial has
degree at most `delta` and its explicit list belongs to the input. All resulting
expanded polynomials have degree `O((N+1) delta^2)` and polynomial bit length
and size in the fixed coordinate vector `(v,eta)`. There are only
polynomially many monomials of this degree in a fixed number of variables;
multiplying the explicitly encoded factors therefore remains polynomial.
Coefficient bit lengths grow polynomially under these products and powers.

Thus attainable leader objective values have a polynomial-size formula

```
exists x,w,lambda,mu,tau:
  x in C
  AND [some regime has K_sigma, tau Q^2=R_sigma,
       substituted upper constraints, substituted upper value eta]
  AND forall w',lambda',mu',tau':
        [E(x,w',lambda',mu',tau') implies tau<=tau'].
```

The total number of variables, including both quantified copies, is fixed.
Standard real quantifier elimination is polynomial in the degree, formula
length, and coefficient bit length when this total dimension is fixed.
It therefore gives an exact univariate description of attainable `eta` in
polynomial bit time.

The relevant primary real-algebraic ingredients are
[Basu, Pollack, and Roy, On the combinatorial and algebraic complexity of
quantifier elimination](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/jacm95.ps),
including sign conditions and sample-point computation. These are the same
established algorithms used in the repository's fixed-core theorem. The
new reduction here is to the compressed KKT comparison formula.

## 5. Why an optimistic optimum is attained

Feasible leader-response pairs lie in a compact product `C times [-M,M]^N`
for some finite `M`, since all bound functions are continuous on compact `C`.
Their graph is closed, including when follower feasibility fails at other
leader choices. To see this, take optimal pairs `(x_n,z_n)` converging to
`(x,z)`. Primal feasibility passes to the limit. For any `y in P(x)`, the
Hoffman error bound for the **fixed** matrix of all equality, inequality,
and box normals supplies `y_n in P(x_n)` with `y_n -> y`, since only the
right-hand side changes continuously. Comparing follower costs gives

```
f(x_n,z_n)<=f(x_n,y_n)  =>  f(x,z)<=f(x,y).
```

Thus `z` is globally optimal at `x`. The upper weak polynomial inequalities
are closed as well, so the optimistic feasible graph is compact. Its upper
objective attains a minimum whenever it is nonempty.

The error-bound source is
[Hoffman, On Approximate Solutions of Systems of Linear Inequalities (1952)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/07/On_approximate_solutions_of_systems_of_linear_inequalities_%28IA_jresv49n4p263%29.pdf).
Only existence of a uniform constant for fixed constraint normals is used;
the algorithm does not need to compute that constant.

For explicit simultaneous output recovery, let `Psi(v,tau,eta)` be the
compressed feasible leader-response formula above. Optimal pairs satisfy

```
Psi(v,tau,eta)
AND NOT exists vbar,taubar,etabar:
   [Psi(vbar,taubar,etabar) AND etabar<eta].
```

Renaming the internal follower-comparison variables in the second copy
still gives a fixed total number of variables. Quantifier elimination and
simultaneous algebraic sampling therefore return optimal
`(x,w,lambda,mu,tau,eta)` in one real-algebraic extension of polynomial
degree and encoding length.
Recovering `z_i=Z_i^sigma/Q` is rational evaluation in that same extension,
so returning all `N` follower coordinates still has polynomial total size.
No LP or nonlinear solve over independently represented algebraic fields
is being assumed. This completes the candidate proof.

## 6. Concrete application and scope boundaries

One application class is centralized process-resource allocation under a
few tariff or design parameters. Units have quadratic local operating costs,
bounded operating rates, a fixed number of shared resource constraints, and
possibly nonconvex penalties depending on a fixed number of aggregate
emissions, utility loads, or product totals. The leader can impose arbitrary
polynomial design and performance conditions under the stated encoding on the returned
unit rates. This is a structural complexity application, not a calibrated
process case study or a practical algorithm claim.

The positive diagonal quadratic local terms are essential to this proof's
rational response representation. Replacing them by general convex
univariate polynomials is not justified: independent cubic costs
`z_i^3/3-p_i z_i` on `[1,p_i+1]`, for distinct primes `p_i`, have unique
responses `sqrt(p_i)`. Even the scalar upper value `sum_i z_i` has algebraic
degree `2^N`. This is the existing root-sum output-size obstruction, not a
new arithmetic-hardness mechanism; see
[the convex-leaf arithmetic barrier](fixed-core-convex-leaf-arithmetic-barrier.md).
The corresponding decision barrier is direct: for integers `a_i>=1`, these
cubic costs with `p_i` replaced by `a_i` have unique responses `sqrt(a_i)`.
A single upper inequality `sum_i z_i<=K` is precisely the standard
sum-of-square-roots comparison problem. This already uses zero leader,
aggregate, and resource dimensions and uniformly positive local curvature
`2z_i>=2`. This embeds a well-known unresolved exact-arithmetic problem in the
cubic-cost extension; it is not a claim of NP-hardness.

Positivity of the diagonal coefficients also cannot simply be dropped.
If all `a_i=c_i=0` and `phi=0` on the unit box, every follower point is
optimal. Upper quadratic equations `z_i(1-z_i)=0` and linear Boolean-clause
inequalities then encode 3SAT. If negative local coefficients are allowed,
minimizing `sum_i z_i(1-z_i)` makes the follower optimum set exactly the
Boolean cube, and linear upper clause inequalities alone encode 3SAT.
These elementary reductions mark the theorem's curvature boundary; they
are not presented as new hardness mechanisms.

Fixed follower constraint normals are used for attainment. If one allows
them to depend on the leader, the bounded example

```
x in [0,1],  z in [0,1],  x z=0,
follower minimizes (z-1)^2,
leader minimizes x+z
```

has value `x` for every `x>0` but value one at `x=0`. Its infimum zero is
not attained. A separate infimum-and-attainment algorithm may still be
possible by the same compressed formulas; the stated optimizer theorem does
not silently include that variant.

## 7. Initial literature comparison

[Ketkov and Prokopyev, On the Complexity of Bilevel Linear and Quadratic
Programs in Fixed Dimensions, June 2026 version](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15592v2)
studies fixed follower dimensions and constraint counts. Its convex-quadratic
pessimistic hardness construction uses a large coupled quadratic objective;
its positive fixed-follower-variable theorem has a different parameter
restriction. The abstract, main statements, and Sections 2.2 and 3 were
inspected. The present follower has unbounded box constraints but only a
fixed number of nonbox resource rows, positive diagonal local curvature,
and a fixed aggregate dimension. It does not solve their general open case
with a fixed total number of follower constraints.

Clipping in continuous quadratic resource allocation and piecewise response
representations in multiparametric QP are classical. The proposed claim is
polynomial **global bilevel optimization**, including nonconvex aggregate
follower costs, through a fixed-dimensional quantified comparison. [Megiddo and Tamir, Linear time algorithms for some separable quadratic
programming problems (1993)](https://theory.stanford.edu/~megiddo/pdf/qtranrev.pdf)
already use fixed-dimensional dual arrangements for separable quadratic
resource allocation. Sections 2 and 4 of the author-hosted primary paper
were inspected. This is a direct antecedent for the clipping and
regime-compression step, which is not claimed as new. The additional
steps here are the fixed-dimensional nonconvex aggregate response,
global comparison of stationary candidates, and exact optimistic leader
optimization. Targeted searches combining bilevel, separable quadratic,
resource allocation, diagonal Hessian, fixed rank, and polynomial
complexity found no direct match. This is preliminary evidence, not proof of novelty. An independent focused source comparison is saved in
[the novelty audit](bilevel-fixed-aggregate-response-novelty.md). It also
compares recent statistical results for structured bilevel solution paths
and fixed-rank nonconvex QP algorithms. The bounded search found no direct
match for the complete theorem; this does not establish universal novelty.
