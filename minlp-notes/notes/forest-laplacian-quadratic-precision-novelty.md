# Source audit: forest-Laplacian quadratic graph precision

Date: 2026-09-05. Bounded independent source audit of
[the forest candidate](forest-laplacian-quadratic-precision.md).
Independent mathematical reviews assess the proofs and constants.

## Assessment

No matching finite minimum-integer guarantee was found for simultaneous
positive weighted squared differences on a common forest, with arbitrary
unconditional output error. The supported contribution is a polynomial
rational MILP construction within additive `6r+1` integers of every convex
lift's minimum, where `r` is the active forest edge count and common
nonlinear input rank. This includes large connected original Hessian
blocks and noncommuting weighted Laplacians.

The forest-incidence quotient and its exact cube-image volume are classical.
The result is a useful application of the repository's diagonal precision
theorem to a correlated domain whose volume loss is controlled. It does
not establish a new zonotope formula, a first tree decomposition for
quadratic optimization, or a new univariate square encoding.

## Exact primary precedent for the volume calculation

Dall and Pfeifle,
[A polyhedral proof of the matrix tree theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.3876)
(2014), Theorem 5 and Corollary 6, printed pages 5--6, state the zonotope
decomposition into generating parallelotopes and the resulting volume
formula. For a full-row-rank unimodular generator matrix, volume equals
the number of bases of its column matroid. Their Theorem 3 also identifies
this volume with the determinant of the generator Gram matrix. This is
an exact methodological predecessor, not just an analogy to graph volume.

In the candidate's convention, `B` has **edge rows and vertex columns**.
For a tree on `s` vertices, every maximal minor deletes one vertex column
and has absolute determinant one, so there are `s` column bases. Thus
`vol(B[0,1]^s)=s` follows directly from the cited formula. This is not the
usual graphical zonotope generated by edge columns; confusing these
orientations would incorrectly suggest the count is the number of spanning
trees, which is one for a tree. Multiplication over components and scaling
by `2^-r` give the candidate's `V_F`.

Gover and Krikorian,
[Determinants and the volumes of parallelotopes and zonotopes](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024379510000443)
(2010), is another primary predecessor for the cube-image, parallelotope
decomposition, and maximal-minor volume viewpoint. Its publisher abstract
was checked; Dall--Pfeifle supplies the fully checked exact statement above.

## Nearby tree quadratic optimization

Bhathena, Fattahi, Gomez and Kucukyavuz,
[A parametric approach for solving convex quadratic optimization with indicators over trees](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10107-025-02222-3)
(2025), considers positive definite quadratics whose sparsity graph is a
tree, with an indicator associated with each continuous coordinate. Its
parametric dynamic program gives quadratic time and memory complexity
for the stated optimization model. This is strong prior evidence that
tree structure can make particular mixed-integer quadratic problems
tractable. The paper studies optimization with prescribed indicator
variables, rather than the minimum number of new integers needed to
approximate an entire vector-valued graph with uniform error.

The candidate allows an unrestricted number of outputs and compares to
arbitrary convex lifts. Conversely, it does not solve general optimization
over its resulting MILP, and its positive forest-Laplacian structure is
more specific than all positive definite matrices with tree sparsity.
Neither result should be described as subsuming the other.

## Approximation and network formulation predecessors

Beach, Hildebrand and Huchette,
[Compact mixed-integer programming relaxations in quadratic optimization](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.08823),
already constructs square approximations with logarithmic binary and
continuous formulation size and studies quadratic relaxations using
separable structure. The candidate reuses this established compact-square
principle. The [diagonal source audit](diagonal-psd-quadratic-precision-novelty.md)
and [unconditional-body audit](positive-separable-unconditional-error-novelty.md)
give the previously checked sharing, allocation, and oracle precedents.

Ploussard, Usis, Iwakin and Pavicevic,
[When MILP beats QP: piecewise-linear reformulations of sequentially coupled bilinear programs](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.27312)
(August 2026), constructs and compares several approximation formulations
with exact error analysis. Section 4 treats sequentially coupled problems,
including hydropower scheduling. This is a close recent process/network
formulation precedent, but its bilinear approximation choices and
performance comparisons do not give the forest candidate's universal
minimum-integer lower bound.

Process-system relevance should be stated for models actually containing
positive squared differences on a forest, such as a path-indexed smoothing
or variation penalty. A physical network being a tree does not by itself
make its nonlinear equations weighted Laplacian quadratics. In particular,
the candidate does not automatically cover all gas or hydraulic network
equations.

## What remains potentially new

The critical bridge is that the diagonalized incidence domain has volume
at least `2^-r` in its containing unit cube. The volume-covariance parity
lower bound can therefore be compared with a compact diagonal construction
using only an additive linear loss. This controls the domain correlation
created by a non-coordinate change of variables, whereas applying the
diagonal theorem to the containing cube alone would not give a lower bound
for the original problem.

The midpoint/parity method for unrestricted integers itself is established
in Lubin--Zadik--Vielma's
[Mixed-integer convex representability](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05135).
The potentially new conclusion is the finite comparison for this forest
family, not the obstruction mechanism or the incidence geometry alone.

## Limits and search record

The proof requires positive edge coefficients and an unconditional body.
It preserves arbitrary affine output terms by an exact continuous lift.
Active cyclic edge sets introduce dependencies, so the same independent
diagonal-coordinate proof does not directly apply to them. More general
coordinate transformations need their own domain-volume control; linear
rank alone cannot justify the forest constant.

Searches combined forest/tree Laplacians, squared differences,
piecewise-linear and mixed-integer quadratic approximation, minimum
binary variables, incidence cube images, unimodular zonotope volumes,
and process-network formulations. No checked source states the combined
finite whole-formulation result. This bounded no-match assessment supports
continued development, not an unqualified claim of worldwide priority.
