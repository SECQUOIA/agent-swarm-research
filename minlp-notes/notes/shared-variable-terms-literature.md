# Shared-variable univariate terms: literature and novelty check

Date: 2026-09-21. Scope: is it known to link the auxiliary variables of
different univariate functions of the same variable (`t_s^2 + t_c^2 = 1`,
`t_b = t_a^{q/p}`, `t_b = (f_b o f_a^{-1})(t_a)`, hull of the curve
`(x, f_1(x), ..., f_m(x))`) to tighten factorable relaxations?

Sources: local base `literature/` (cited as `[[slug]] p.N`), web search, and
open full texts fetched during this check (listed in section 6). Nothing in
`literature/` was modified during the original check. The 2026-09-26 library
review later added a metadata-only Ballerstein record. It did not recover
the original thesis; the source statements below retain their original
text-extraction provenance and have not been rechecked against its PDF.

## 1. Short answer

1. The mathematical core is known. The closest prior art is Chapter 5 of
   Ballerstein's ETH thesis (2013, joint work with Michaels and Weismantel):
   simultaneous convex hull of a vector of univariate convex functions of one
   variable, closed-form relaxations for pairs and triples, SCIP separators,
   and a large computational gain. For integer powers the hull of the moment
   curve is classical (Karlin and Shapley 1953; Hankel/moment matrices), and
   Sherali and Tuncbilek (1997) already add the convex relations
   `(X_q)^p <= X_{pq}` between power variables of one variable.
2. The trigonometric identity is standard in application-specific
   relaxations (ACOPF: Jabr's SOC constraint is `sin^2 + cos^2 = 1` scaled by
   voltage magnitudes; the "lifted nonlinear cuts" are the chord of the arc),
   and substituting `s, c` with `s^2 + c^2 = 1` is folklore modelling
   practice. I found no general-purpose solver that documents doing this
   automatically when `sin(x)` and `cos(x)` sit in different expressions.
3. What I could not find anywhere: a presolve-level, solver-agnostic
   reformulation that detects different univariate functions of one variable
   across a model and adds the exact one-dimensional relations between their
   auxiliary variables (including non-convex pairs such as sin/cos, log/power,
   mixed-sign exponents), with a library-wide evaluation. Ballerstein states
   that his separators "do not automatically detect these vectors" and he
   found only one library instance (ex8_4_6) worth testing. That gap, plus a
   MINLPLib-wide study, is the defensible contribution. The hull theory is not.
4. Important correction for the pilot: the MINLPLib page for lnts50 now lists
   a Gurobi dual bound of 0.55464755 (primal 0.55466876). The value 0.5258 is
   the Couenne entry, not the best one. The pilot's 0.552 improves on SCIP's
   listed 0.5379 but does not improve the best known dual bound. See section 4.

## 2. Closest prior art, with exact statements

### 2.1 Ballerstein 2013, thesis Chapter 5 (metadata in local base; PDF not retained)

Martin Ballerstein, "Convex Relaxations for Mixed-Integer Nonlinear
Programs", Diss. ETH No. 21024, 2013, doi:10.3929/ethz-a-009959194. Chapter 5
is stated to be joint work with Dennis Michaels and Robert Weismantel
(thesis p.149). I found no journal version of the univariate part. Page
numbers below are the printed thesis pages, read from the repository's text
extraction, not from the PDF layout.

- Definition: `Q_D[f] = conv{(x, f(x)) : x in D}` for `f: R^n -> R^m` is the
  "simultaneous convex hull" (p.147). Main representation (eq. 5.1, p.148):
  `Q_D[f]` is the intersection over all `alpha in R^m` of
  `{(x,z) : vex_D[alpha^T f](x) <= alpha^T z}`. This is the Proposition 1 that
  Liers et al. attribute to him [[liers2021-solving-mixed-integer-nonlinear-optimization]] p.6.
- Motivating example (p.8, p.152-153): `f = (x^2, x^3)` on `[1,2]`. Volume of
  the term-by-term relaxation 0.1500, of the RLT-type simplex 0.0185, of the
  exact hull 0.0055 ("the gap between the two objects is 2627 %"). The hull is
  taken from Karlin and Shapley's description of the moment space by Hankel
  determinants (Theorems 5.2-5.3, p.150-151).
- Theorem 5.32 (p.174): for two three-times differentiable functions with
  `f_i'' > 0` and `(f_2''/f_1'')' > 0` on `[l,u]`, the cones of multipliers
  for which `alpha^T f` is convex, or has a vertex-polyhedral envelope, are
  generated by four explicit vectors, and `Q[f]` is described by the remaining
  `alpha`. Theorem 5.33 gives the full outer description by supporting
  hyperplanes; Lemmas 5.36-5.37 give an exact separation routine. Example 5.39
  (p.183-184): for `f_1 = x^2` and any admissible `f_2` the hull has a closed
  form, and it coincides with the moment-curve hull when `f_2 = x^3`.
- Triples: Theorems 5.41 and 5.45. Families covered (p.197):
  `(x^{k1}, x^{k2}, x^{k3})` with `k_i in R \ [0,1]` on positive domains;
  `(exp(k1 x), exp(k2 x), exp(k3 x))`; `(1/x, x^2, exp(x))`;
  `(-sqrt(x), sin(x), exp(x))` on `[3.5, 4.5]`. He stresses that, unlike the
  moment curve, the vector need not be "complete".
- Library search (p.198): "Many of the instances in these libraries are
  rather sparse, that is a lot of variables occur only once or twice so that
  it is quite unlikely that the simultaneous convexification of several
  nonlinearities has significant impact." The one instance studied is
  GLOBALLib ex8_4_6 (eight `exp(-a_i z_j)` per variable). With original
  bounds no strategy improved the trivial lower bound 0; gains appeared only
  after shrinking the `y` domains (p.199).
- SCIP integration (p.201-203): separators `2UniVarConv` and `3UniVarConv` in
  SCIP 3.0.0, called at every node. "Currently, the separators do not
  automatically detect these vectors. Instead, we need to reformulate the
  problems such that each function ... is replaced by a new variable". Test
  set: 800 random instances, constraints `sum a x_j^p <= 0` with non-integer
  exponents `p in {i+0.2,...,i+1}`, 10-50 variables, 1-50 constraints, 15 min
  limit. Result (Table 5.7): solved 542 -> 643, mean dual gap 10.48% -> 0.49%,
  mean time 58.3 s -> 53.1 s; BARON 11.1.0 and Couenne 0.4 behave like plain
  SCIP (mean gaps 13-14%).

Relation to the proposed technique. Ballerstein's cuts live in `(x, z_1, z_2)`
space. The proposed redundant constraint `t_b = g(t_a)`, `g = f_b o f_a^{-1}`,
convexified by the solver, yields only the projection of `Q[f]` onto
`(z_1, z_2)` (the planar curve's hull) intersected with the two separate
envelopes. It is therefore a weaker, but solver-agnostic and cheap, relative
of his relaxation. The cited explicit theorems assume convex components with
a monotone curvature ratio. This does not by itself exclude mixed convex/concave pairs: a
sign change of a lifted coordinate preserves convex hulls and can turn a
concave component convex. For example, `(log(x),x^2)` on a positive interval
becomes `(-log(x),x^2)`, with curvature ratio `2x^2` strictly increasing.
Coverage must therefore be checked after sign changes and reordering; for
trigonometric pairs it also depends on the interval. The general
representation 5.1 applies regardless.

### 2.2 Mertens 2019 and Liers et al. 2021

Nick Mertens, "Relaxation refinement for mixed-integer nonlinear programs
with applications in engineering", TU Dortmund 2019 (open, eldorado handle
2003/38410), p.18: Ballerstein "already exploits this result to improve the
relaxation of feasible sets given by multiple univariate convex functions".
Mertens and Liers et al. do not continue the univariate line. They build a
general separation problem over the multiplier unit ball (nonsmooth convex,
subgradient methods) and instantiate it for bivariate quadratic
absolute-value functions at gas-network junctions
[[liers2021-solving-mixed-integer-nonlinear-optimization]] p.6-9, p.12-26.
Results: two small networks, 30 scenarios each, root gap closed about 35% and
54%, 10-minute gap closure 70% vs 18% and 69% vs 17%; implementation as cut
loop around a solver, no general detection
[[liers2021-solving-mixed-integer-nonlinear-optimization]] p.26-29. So: no
univariate function pairs, no sin/cos, no presolve reformulation. A 2025 JOGO
paper applies the same machinery to planar obnoxious facility location
(doi:10.1007/s10898-025-01464-x; abstract only seen).

### 2.3 Tawarmalani 2010, inclusion certificates

Coined "simultaneous convexification". Works with extreme points of the
simultaneous hull; Corollary 3.9: a common certificate realizing each
individual envelope gives the simultaneous hull
[[tawarmalani2010-inclusion-certificates-and-simultaneous-convexification]] p.15-18.
Correction from the 2026-09-22 audit: the previous summary claimed without
adequate hypotheses that a joint epigraph hull equals the intersection of
the individual epigraph hulls. That statement is false in general and must
not be attributed to these sources as a general theorem. If all `f_a` are
convex on a convex common domain, their joint epigraph is already convex,
and separate exact epigraph descriptions suffice. For arbitrary functions,
one needs additional compatibility conditions, such as a common certificate
realizing the individual envelopes.

Counterexample: on `[0,1]`, let `f_1(x)=x^2` and `f_2(x)=sqrt(x)`. The point
`(x,t_1,t_2)=(1/2,1/4,1/2)` lies in the intersection of the individual
epigraph hulls (`t_1 >= x^2`, `t_2 >= x`). In any joint convex representation,
`1/4=t_1 >= E[X^2] >= E[X]^2=1/4` forces zero variance, so `X=1/2` and
`t_2 >= sqrt(1/2)>1/2`, a contradiction. Thus equality-defined auxiliaries or
opposite signs are useful structural indicators, not necessary conditions
for a benefit from simultaneous convexification. The paper does not treat
univariate curves specifically.

### 2.4 He and Tawarmalani (composite relaxations)

"A new framework to relax composite functions in nonlinear programs" (Math.
Program. 190, 2021), "Tractable relaxations of composite functions" (MOR 47,
2022), "MIP relaxations in factorable programming"
[[he2024-mip-relaxations-in-factorable-programming]]. They relax one outer
function `phi(f_1(x),...,f_d(x))` using several under/over-estimators of each
inner function, encoded in a polytope/simplotope. The structure is one outer
function over many inner functions. I found no statement that they exploit
several *outer* univariate functions of the *same* inner function or
variable; the abstracts of the 2021, 2022 papers and of arXiv:2205.01442 do
not mention it. Zhu, He, Tawarmalani, "Axis-aligned relaxations for MINLP"
(arXiv:2603.18458) prove a simultaneous-hull result for multilinear
compositions over axis-aligned regions and treat products of univariate
functions of *different* variables; text search of the PDF found no treatment
of several functions of one variable. Full texts of the 2021 and 2022 papers
were not read (abstracts only), so this is "not found", not "proved absent".

### 2.5 RLT for polynomial programs

Sherali and Tuncbilek (1997), univariate section: "we can generate additional
valid (linear or convex) constraints that relate the new RLT variables with
each other and with the original problem variables", and the "convex
variable-bounding" class `(X_q)^p <= X_{p q}` for primes `p`, e.g.
`x^2 <= X_2`, `X_2^2 <= X_4`, `X_2^3 <= X_6`, `X_3^2 <= X_6`
[[sherali1997-new-reformulation-linearization-convexification-relaxations]] p.3.
This is exactly the convex half of `t_b = t_a^{q/p}` for integer powers. They
add squared grid-factor and squared Lagrange-interpolation constraints and
report root bounds within 3% on seven univariate test problems (p.3-4). In
RLT each monomial has one variable, so `x * x^2 = x^3` holds by construction
and bound-factor products of degree `delta` in one variable give the simplex
relaxation of the moment curve (Ballerstein's `S^3`, 8x volume reduction in
his example, p.153). Dalkiran and Sherali's RLT-POS and the "v-semidefinite
cuts" of Sherali, Dalkiran, Desai continue this; the Lasserre hierarchy gives
the exact univariate hull via Hankel matrices. I did not re-read these three
sources in this check. None of this covers non-polynomial pairs or
non-integer exponents.

### 2.6 Trigonometric identity in applications

- ACOPF. Hijazi, Coffrin, Van Hentenryck relax `sin` and `cos` of the angle
  difference *separately* (quadratic envelope for cos, polyhedral for sin) and
  write "To the best of our knowledge, there has not been any relaxations
  directly handling the trigonometric functions"
  [[hijazi2017-convex-quadratic-relaxations-for-mixed]] p.5-6. The identity
  enters through the lifted product variables: the SOC constraint
  `w_R^2 + w_I^2 <= w_i w_j` is "easily verified using the trigonometric
  identity cos(x)^2 + sin(x)^2 = 1"
  [[hijazi2017-convex-quadratic-relaxations-for-mixed]] p.30. So
  `t_s^2 + t_c^2 <= 1`, scaled by magnitudes, is Jabr's SOC relaxation.
- Chord of the arc. Coffrin, Hijazi, Van Hentenryck, "Strengthening the SDP
  relaxation of AC power flows with convex envelopes, bound tightening, and
  lifted nonlinear cuts" (arXiv:1512.04644, eq. 36a-b): the LNCs have the form
  `v^sigma (w_R cos(phi) + w_I sin(phi)) - ... >= ...` with `phi` the midpoint
  and `delta` the half-width of the angle-difference range. With magnitudes
  fixed to 1 this reduces to `c cos(phi) + s sin(phi) >= cos(delta)`, the
  chord of the arc. Chen, Atamturk, Oren give the convex hull of the
  corresponding rank-one 2x2 Hermitian set with angle bounds and show RLT does
  not capture it [[chen2016-a-spatial-branch-and-cut]] p.1 (abstract; the
  hull statement is in their Section 2.1). Bugosen, Parker, Coffrin
  (arXiv:2404.17541, abstract only) project LNCs onto SOC-type relaxations.
- Narimani and Molzahn (arXiv:1912.05061, 2310.14463) tighten QC by rotating
  the equations so that `g cos + b sin` is enveloped as one shifted
  trigonometric function. In Ballerstein's terms these are cuts
  `vex[alpha^T f] <= alpha^T z` for one fixed `alpha` on `f = (sin, cos)`.
- Montez, Sanjeevi, Sundar: MILP relaxations for trigonometric MINLP share one
  partition (one set of binaries) among functions of the same variable, "for
  example, sin x and cos x may appear in pairs"
  [[montez2025-global-optimization-algorithm-for-mixed]] p.15-16. This couples
  the two relaxations through the interval choice. Their stated motive is
  fewer binaries; no identity constraint is added (text search for identity,
  circle, sin^2 found nothing relevant).
- Robotics/kinematics, trajectory optimization: replacing each angle by
  `(c, s)` with `c^2 + s^2 = 1` is the standard polynomialization (needed for
  BARON, which has no trig support
  [[bestuzheva2025-global-optimization-of-mixed-integer]] p.20; see also
  [[aigner2023-solving-ac-optimal-power-flow]] on MINLP solvers and trig).
  That substitution usually *drops* the angle. Keeping `u`, `sin(u)`,
  `cos(u)` and adding the identity as a redundant constraint is the variant in
  the pilot. My web searches found no paper that evaluates that variant in a
  general-purpose solver; this is a negative search result, not proof.

### 2.7 Redundant constraints as a general device

- Sahinidis and Tawarmalani, "Accelerating branch-and-bound through a
  modeling language construct for relaxation-specific constraints" (JOGO 33,
  2005): BARON's relaxation-only constraints, used for relaxation and domain
  reduction but not local search. Abstract/secondary descriptions only; their
  examples (e.g. optimality conditions) are user-supplied, not detected.
- Ruiz and Grossmann, "Using redundancy to strengthen the relaxation for the
  global optimization of MINLP problems" (CACE 35, 2011): redundant
  constraints from physical insight. Abstract only.
- Liberti's reduction constraints / reduced RLT
  [[liberti2006-an-exact-reformulation-algorithm-for]] are automatic, but for
  bilinear terms with linear equalities, not univariate terms.
- ANTIGONE (Misener and Floudas 2013/2014) lists "introducing additional
  nonconvex terms to interlink expressions" among its contributions (abstract
  of the signomial-framework paper). I could not access the full text to check
  whether any rule links powers of a common base; treat as unverified.

### 2.8 Solver presolve as documented

SCIP 8 unifies only *identical* subexpressions: "if a subexpression appears
several times ... always the same expression object is used", so that one
auxiliary variable is attached
[[bestuzheva2025-global-optimization-of-mixed-integer]] p.8. Its nonlinear
handlers (quotient, signomial, perspective, bilinear, convex/concave) work
per expression; `sin` and `cos` are separate expression handlers (p.5). I
found no documented step in SCIP, Couenne
([[belotti2009-branching-and-bounds-tightening-techniques]],
[[belotti2020-couenne-a-user-s-manual]], not re-read here), BARON, ANTIGONE,
Gurobi, Octeract, MAiNGO or EAGO that links different univariate functions of
one variable for relaxation strength. Closed-source solvers may do more than
they document; Gurobi's strong lnts bounds (section 4) are unexplained.
Reduced-space McCormick solvers (MAiNGO, EAGO) suffer the same effect as the
interval "dependency problem": `sin(x)^2 + cos(x)^2` does not relax to 1.

### 2.9 Classical mathematics behind the hull

Convex hulls of curves generated by Chebyshev (T-) systems (Karlin and
Shapley 1953; Karlin and Studden 1966; Krein and Nudelman) generalize the
moment curve; Ballerstein's curvature-ratio condition is of this type. The
trigonometric moment curve has a Toeplitz-matrix (spectrahedral) hull. My own
inference, not checked against a source: `{1, u, cos u, sin u}` is an
extended Chebyshev system on intervals shorter than `2 pi`, so the hull of
the helix segment `(u, cos u, sin u)` should follow from that theory; whether
the MINLP literature has written it down I do not know (Ranestad and
Sturmfels, "On the convex hull of a space curve", 2012, is the general
algebraic reference; not read).

## 3. Assessment

Known (do not claim):
- Hull of `(x, f_1(x), ..., f_m(x))` as a relaxation device, the
  linear-combination representation, closed forms for convex pairs/triples,
  power and exponential families, SCIP separators, order-of-magnitude gap
  reductions on synthetic instances (Ballerstein 2013).
- Moment-curve hulls; convex power-linking constraints `(X_q)^p <= X_{pq}`
  (Sherali and Tuncbilek 1997).
- `s^2 + c^2 <= 1` and arc-chord cuts in ACOPF (SOC, LNC, Chen-Atamturk-Oren).
- Redundant or relaxation-only constraints as a way to strengthen
  relaxations (Sahinidis-Tawarmalani 2005; Ruiz-Grossmann 2011).

Folklore modelling practice:
- Substituting `s = sin(u)`, `c = cos(u)`, `s^2 + c^2 = 1`.
- Writing `y = x^2`, `x^4 = y^2`, `exp(2x) = exp(x)^2` by hand so that a
  solver sees the relation; choosing one "base" term and expressing others
  through it.

Possibly new at solver level (needs the caveats below):
- Automatic detection across constraints of different univariate functions of
  one variable, and a presolve reformulation that adds the exact relations
  between their auxiliary variables, for any solver. Ballerstein explicitly
  left detection open and reformulated by hand.
- Automatic handling of additional function pairs: sin/cos (circle plus
  chord), log/power (`t_p = exp(p t_log)`), mixed-sign or fractional exponents,
  and inverse-based relations `t_b = g(t_a)` whenever `f_a` is monotone on the
  current domain. These are candidates for implementation coverage, not
  automatically new hull theory: check prior results after sign changes and
  reordering as well as in their original coordinates.
- A library-wide study. The local scan finds 189 of 1625 MINLPLib instances
  with at least one variable carrying two or more distinct univariate terms
  (`code/shared_variable_terms/scan.jsonl`), which contradicts Ballerstein's
  2013 impression that such structure is rare; his only instance, ex8_4_6, is
  in that list.

Caveats for any claim:
- The relation-only reformulation is weaker than the known hull; a paper
  must cite Ballerstein and position itself as the cheap projection of it, or
  investigate hull cuts for further pairs. New theory requires checking that
  existing results, including sign changes and reordering, do not cover them.
- When every shared function is convex and used only through its epigraph,
  separate exact epigraph descriptions suffice. There is no general
  exclusion of one-sided nonconvex or mixed-curvature uses; see the
  counterexample in section 2.3.
- Evidence so far is one instance family where a commercial solver already
  has a better bound.

- lnts50 is closed to about 4e-5 relative by Gurobi. lnts100/200/400 have

  `x^2, x^3` (local scan), so it is a fair target.
- BARON has no entries for lnts*, inscribedsquare*, truck because it does not
