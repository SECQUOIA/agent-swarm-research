# Weighted potential objectives on one cycle: bounded source audit

Date: 2026-09-05. Candidate: [weighted-potential cycle hardness](potential-flow-weighted-potential-cycle-hardness.md). This note compares primary literature and scopes the proposed contribution. It does not independently certify the reduction or the anticipated graph characterization.

No matching theorem was located for a single simple cycle, a common quadratic passive law, fixed nomination coefficients `(2,-3,1)`, fixed objective coefficients `(-5,12,-7)`, and independent two-point positive resistances. The proposed NP-completeness result, together with exact polynomial interval optimization and the tree case, is a plausible restricted complexity separation. General circuit-extremum hardness, nonlinear tolerance analysis, and arithmetic encodings by series components are established topics. Priority remains qualified, especially because the Hasler–Wang 1993 nonlinear tolerance paper is still unread.

## Closest primary complexity sources

Huang and Bryant, *Intractability in Linear Switch-Level Simulation* (1993), [IBM Research primary abstract](https://research.ibm.com/publications/intractability-in-linear-switch-level-simulation). The abstract states NP-completeness of extremal steady-state voltage computation in general MOS networks with unknown values, modeled by voltage-controlled linear resistors and grounded capacitors. The full proof was not retrieved in the earlier circuit audit. The inspected abstract establishes prior circuit uncertainty hardness; it does not specify the candidate's nonlinear passive law, independent two-point choices, fixed three-vertex data, or one-cycle topology.

Bryant, Tygar, and Huang, *Geometric Characterization of Series-Parallel Variable Resistor Networks* (1994), DOI 10.1109/81.331520, [open author preprint](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~tygar/papers/Geometric_characterization_of_series-parallel/Bryant_Journal_preprint.pdf). PDF pages 1–4 discuss general circuit objectives and prior voltage-range hardness, then obtain exact Thevenin/Norton polygons for independent variable linear resistors and sources on series-parallel circuits. The polygons have at most `2k` vertices and take `O(nk)` time to construct. Grounded-tree extensions compute individual node ranges. The paper also warns that nominal sensitivities can select the wrong parameter corner.

Our comparison: a joint weighted sum of node potentials is not determined by separately computed node ranges. Correlations between node values matter. The candidate is not contradicted by these linear two-terminal and individual-output results. It should not be presented as the first evidence that circuit objectives or worst-case parameter choices can be difficult.

Johannes Thürauf, *Deciding the feasibility of a booking in the European gas market is coNP-hard*, Annals of Operations Research 318, 591–618 (2022), DOI 10.1007/s10479-022-04732-1, [open publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10479-022-04732-1). Section 2 fixes arc pressure-loss coefficients and optimizes nominations within booking bounds; model (2) maximizes one pairwise potential difference. The paper proves hardness in general graphs by a Partition reduction and discusses the established polynomial tree and single-cycle cases. It also proves hardness when flow directions are known in advance.

Our comparison: this source varies nominations rather than resistance choices, and uses a pairwise objective rather than the candidate's weighted functional. Its one-cycle positive result therefore does not cover or contradict the candidate. The candidate's continuous-box companion also fixes nominations; it must not be advertised as a solution of arbitrary weighted nomination optimization.

## Classical monotonicity does not give the weighted conclusion

Duffin, *Topology of Series-Parallel Networks* (1965), DOI 10.1016/0022-247X(65)90125-3, [open primary PDF](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~reu/papers/current/jim/duffin.pdf). Theorems 0 and 1 on printed pages 306–307 relate resistance-independent current directions to confluence. Section 5, especially Theorem 4 on printed pages 314–316, treats monotone nonlinear resistor characteristics and series/parallel construction. These provide classical circuit topology and sign mechanisms. They do not explicitly state the inspected candidate's fixed weighted three-node objective or discrete one-cycle complexity theorem.

Our comparison: expressing a zero-sum functional as a linear combination of pairwise differences does not transfer separate monotonicity to that functional. The summands can have opposing derivatives whose magnitudes vary. The candidate's triangle gives a concrete strict interior maximum from that competition. This is compatible with a cactus hull theorem for every pairwise pressure objective considered separately. It also does not challenge the adjacent-terminal arc-flow confluence mechanism.

The proposed coefficients create a negative quadratic in the circulation with a prescribed peak. Completing that square and adding positive series resistances are elementary mechanisms; the Subset Sum encoding is familiar. A reasonable contribution claim concerns their realization within the unusually restricted passive physical model, the exact verification bound, and the interval-versus-finite separation. The numerical coefficient choice is repository-derived candidate data, not a formula attributed to an earlier circuit source.

Later mathematical scope correction (2026-09-05): the explicit gap has polynomial binary encoding length and supports hardness against algorithms polynomial in input size and precision bits. In addition, multiplying all resistances by `4nK(5K+3S)^2` gives integer resistances and amplifies `Delta=3/[4(5K+3S)^2]` to a gap of `3nK>=3`. Potentials and the objective scale while flows, nominations, and fixed objective coefficients remain unchanged. The added scale has polynomial binary encoding length, so absolute-error-one value approximation is NP-hard on this unnormalized input class. The earlier exclusion of a fixed-error consequence was too broad. No strong NP-hardness, relative-error hardness, or normalized-data hardness follows. NP membership here is unusually transparent because one cycle gives a piecewise quadratic scalar equation and hence a degree-at-most-two algebraic physical state. These exact arithmetic and scaling arguments belong to the proposed proof, not the abstract-only circuit hardness source; the primary-source findings are unchanged.

## Anticipated universal tree characterization: scope and attribution

On a tree with fixed nominations and scalar multiplicative edge resistances, conservation fixes every edge flow. Every zero-sum linear potential functional is consequently linear in those resistances. Endpoint optimization and equality with independent interval hulls follow immediately. This positive direction is elementary and should not be presented as a substantial new algorithmic idea.

A converse saying that *every* linear potential functional has the universal hull property only on trees would concern a broader family of outputs than a pairwise pressure theorem. The triangle candidate supplies a witness on one cycle, but a theorem for all non-trees still needs its own embedding argument and proof review. Equality of all linear-objective maxima is equivalently equality of convex hulls of the corresponding attainable potential sets; this is standard support-function geometry, not a new equivalence.

Two scope restrictions matter:

- Restrict the graph statement to simple graphs, or state the appropriate multigraph reduction explicitly. Two vertices connected by parallel edges form a cyclic multigraph, yet every zero-sum potential objective is a multiple of their single pairwise difference. Thus an unrestricted multigraph “tree if and only if” statement cannot follow from the simple-cycle witness.
- The converse must quantify over nonlinear laws, or specialize to the common quadratic law. For fixed linear Ohmic laws, changing one positive edge conductance gives a rank-one update of the grounded Laplacian. The Sherman–Morrison formula makes **every** fixed linear potential functional fractional-linear in that coordinate, with derivative of constant sign. Independent resistance-hull equality therefore holds on all connected graphs in that linear model. This observation is an elementary matrix calculation, not a novelty claim. It does not make simultaneous endpoint optimization polynomial on unrestricted graphs.

The function `c^T pi` must be gauge invariant (`sum c=0`) unless a reference potential is fixed. It is a weighted potential performance measure, not automatically the physical dissipation `b^T pi`; the candidate's `c` differs from `b`. This distinction should remain explicit in application claims.

## Remaining literature gap and recommended wording

Hasler and Wang, *Parameter tolerances in non-linear resistive circuits: worst case analysis based on monotonicity*, NOLTA 1993, pages 841–846, remains the principal missing older source. Its bibliographic existence is verified through Pastore's 2015 primary paper; full text has not been openly located. The [series-parallel envelope source audit](potential-flow-series-parallel-envelope-novelty.md) records this gap and other inaccessible nonlinear circuit papers. Without the text, neither duplication nor priority of a general nonlinear monotonicity characterization can be asserted.

Recommended wording: “For a common quadratic passive law, optimizing a fixed three-node weighted potential functional over independent two-point resistances is NP-complete already on a simple cycle. The corresponding interval problem is exact polynomial time at fixed cycle rank, while finite choices are elementary on trees. General circuit tolerance hardness is known; no matching restricted theorem was found in the open primary literature inspected.” Keep any universal tree characterization separate until its complete proof is reviewed.
