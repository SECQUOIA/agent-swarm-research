## Maximal scalar packing and compatibility intervals

For any continuous convex function on the compact interval, uniform
continuity makes the midpoint Jensen gap uniformly small for sufficiently
close inputs. Thus a set whose pairwise gaps exceed a fixed positive
tolerance has a finite cardinality bound. Starting from one point and
adjoining an incompatible point while one exists must terminate within
that bound. This establishes a finite inclusion-maximal packing with
`P>=1`; no algorithm for constructing it is needed.

For a fixed selected point, its compatibility set is an interval containing
that point. On either side, enlarging the interval between the two points
can only increase the midpoint Jensen gap. For the smooth polynomial
functions needed here this follows directly from the displayed derivative
formulas. Continuity closes the set and includes its extreme points.
For general continuous convex functions the same property follows from
monotone secant slopes, or from uniform smooth convex approximation on
the interval. The latter generality is not required by the theorem.

Maximality ensures that the compatibility intervals cover `[0,1]`. Splitting
each at its selected point gives at most `2P` intervals with endpoint
midpoint gap at most `tau`. A convex function's chord gap is a concave
nonnegative function vanishing at its endpoints; its maximum is at most
twice its midpoint value. Each resulting interval therefore has chord
error at most `2tau`. The finite interval cover can be trimmed to a
partition without increasing the number of pieces or the chord error.
Degenerate intervals and a singleton packing do not invalidate this step.
Consequently `N_(2tau)<=2P`.

The reviewed curvature bounds give

```
M_tau<=2M_(2tau)<=48N_(2tau)<=96P.
```

The actual compiled construction uses `U<=M_tau+1/256` and
`L=max(0,ceil(log2((5/2)U)))`. If `L>0`, then `2^L<=5U`, so
`2^L<=480P+5/256<=481P`. If `L=0`, the last inequality follows from
`P>=1`. Thus the comparison controls the number of cells actually
generated by the indexed algorithm, rather than an independently chosen
ideal partition. This distinction is essential for the construction claim.

## Independent outputs

Use the local packing tolerance `epsilon_i` in coordinate `i`. Two
distinct points in the Cartesian product of the coordinate packings differ
in at least one coordinate, where their exact output midpoint has error
strictly larger than that coordinate's tolerance. Every affine output
term cancels in this midpoint difference, including affine dependence on
other coordinates. Hence these product graph points are pairwise
incompatible with sharing an integer parity class in any convex lift.

Choosing exact graph witnesses and averaging two witnesses of equal parity
would otherwise give a feasible lifted midpoint with integral integer
coordinates, contradicting the prescribed graph tube. Thus
`2^p_conv>=product_i P_i`, regardless of continuous lift size or bounds
on the integer witnesses.

Composing the scalar compiled formulations uses `sum_i L_i` declared
binaries, contains every exact independent-output graph point, and gives
componentwise error at most `15epsilon_i/16`. The count satisfies
`sum_i L_i<=p_conv+r log2(481)<p_conv+9r`. This proves the stated weak
inequality with `9r`.

## Supporting feature comparison, including odd powers

For a single term, put `u=a^(k/2)` and `v=b^(k/2)`. Since `k/2>=1`,
convexity gives `((a+b)/2)^(k/2)<=(u+v)/2`. Squaring and subtracting
from `(u²+v²)/2` yields the lower bound `(u-v)²/4` for its Jensen gap.
For the upper bound, arithmetic-geometric mean gives
`((a+b)/2)^k>=(ab)^(k/2)=uv`, yielding `(u-v)²/2`.
Both inequalities include zero endpoints and odd integer degrees.

Multiplying by nonnegative coefficients and summing proves the two-sided
feature distance comparison. Coefficients equal to zero can be omitted.
The feature vectors need not be rational because they are used only in
the geometric proof, not in the formulation.

Along an increasing sequence of scalar inputs, every feature increment
has nonnegative components. All inner products between increments are
therefore nonnegative. Expanding the squared norm of their sum proves
the claimed superadditivity of squared secant distances.

Adjacent points of a local packing have Jensen gap greater than `tau`.
The upper feature inequality forces their squared feature distance to
exceed `2tau`. Superadditivity then implies squared feature distance
greater than `2tau h` across `h>0` consecutive index steps. Applying the
lower feature inequality gives a Jensen gap greater than `tau h/2`.
For identical coordinate inputs both quantities are zero. Consequently,
for distinct product points, summing over the coordinates gives the
strict bound

```
J_f(x(u),x(v))>(tau/2)sum_i |u_i-v_i|.
```

This proves the earlier sufficient condition using index distance greater
than `2r`. The stronger direct argument below removes the factor two.

## Direct Jensen-gap superadditivity

For a twice continuously differentiable convex function,

```
J_phi(a,b)=(1/2) integral_a^b min(t-a,b-t) phi''(t) dt.
```

Given any ordered subdivision of `[a,b]`, its child intervals have disjoint
interiors. On a child `[a_h,b_h]`, both `t-a_h<=t-a` and `b_h-t<=b-t`.
Thus its tent kernel is bounded by the parent kernel at each point.
Multiplying by the nonnegative curvature and summing the integrals gives

```
J_phi(a,b)>=sum_h J_phi(a_h,b_h).
```

This requires only convexity and the stated smoothness; the positive
polynomials satisfy both. It does not use monotone curvature. The final
candidate's continuous-convex extension is also valid: a convex piecewise
linear function is affine plus a nonnegative sum of hinges, each having
the same tent formula for its midpoint gap. Convex interpolants on
successively finer uniform grids converge uniformly to a continuous convex
function. Passing to the limit preserves the finite superadditivity
inequality. Thus no endpoint differentiability or finite endpoint slope
is required by that extension.

Since each adjacent pair in a packing has gap greater than `tau`, a
separation of `h` ordered indices has gap greater than `tau h`. For two
distinct product points, summing this inequality yields

```
J_f(x(u),x(v))>tau sum_i |u_i-v_i|.
```

At `tau=epsilon/r`, index distance greater than `r` is therefore sufficient
for midpoint incompatibility. Strictness also covers equality, although
deleting the whole closed radius-`r` ball is convenient and valid.

## Product code and improved lattice constant

Greedily select an index point and delete the radius-`r` integer lattice
ball around it, intersected with the finite product box. Any two selected
points have distance greater than `r`, so they are pairwise midpoint
incompatible. If a ball has at most `B_r` points, the resulting code has
at least `product_i P_i/B_r` points. A nonintegral right-hand side causes
no difficulty because this is a lower bound on an integer cardinality.

For this radius the exponential weight base 2 gives

```
B_r <= 2^r sum_(z in Z^r) 2^(-sum_i |z_i|)
    = 2^r [1+2sum_(k>=1)2^(-k)]^r
    = 6^r.
```

Indeed each point of the radius-`r` ball contributes at least one to
the weighted upper sum. Product-box boundaries only reduce the deletion
count. The improved parity bound is therefore
`2^p_conv>=product_i P_i/6^r`. Neither the maximal packings nor the
greedy code is enumerated by the formulation algorithm.

## Scalar-sum construction and final count

Apply the scalar compiled construction in each coordinate at tolerance
`epsilon/r`, and sum their outputs with the exact affine term. For every
input, choose the independent exact-graph witnesses of its coordinates;
their product is feasible and restores the exact scalar sum. Conversely,
every feasible tuple has absolute summed error at most
`r*(15/16)*(epsilon/r)=15epsilon/16`. No cancellation assumption is used.

Combining the actual cell-count estimate with the improved parity packing
gives

```
p_out=sum_i L_i
 <=sum_i log2 P_i+r log2(481)
 <=p_conv+r log2(6*481)
 <p_conv+12r,
```

because `6*481=2886<4096`. Hence `p_out<=p_conv+12r`; the earlier
fourteen- and thirteen-per-coordinate assertions also follow.

## Rational computation and precise scope

Each coordinate construction is polynomial in that polynomial's dense
coefficient encoding and its rational tolerance. Dividing the scalar
tolerance by `r` adds only `O(log r)` bits. Summing polynomially many
coordinate circuits remains polynomial in the full input size. All
coefficients of the MILP are rational; the only integer declarations are
the coordinate index bits. Polynomial coefficients, affine terms, and
the final output summation are imposed by exact rational linear equations.

Affine-only input coordinates can remain continuous. For a lower-bound
packing they can be fixed at any common point in their box, while affine
terms still cancel. If there are no nonlinear coordinates at all, the
exact affine graph requires zero integers; the displayed `epsilon/r`
construction is used for `r>=1` only.

The product input domain and the two specified output structures are
substantive assumptions. The scalar-sum lower bound adds nonnegative
Jensen gaps. The independent-output lower bound identifies a violating
coordinate output. Neither proof establishes the same result for arbitrary
coupled output combinations or a general coupled error body. Sparse
binary-encoded huge degrees remain outside the inherited certified
integration complexity bound.

No mathematical defect or unresolved computational dependency was found.

## Final finite corollaries for continuous convex summands

The final revised candidate also includes the finite comparisons suggested
during this audit. The already reviewed three-way chord refinement gives
`N_tau<=3N_(2tau)<=6P` for an arbitrary continuous convex scalar function.
Its finite chord-band formulation therefore uses an integer index length
`L=ceil(log2 N_tau)` with `2^L<=12P`, including `N_tau=1`.

For independent outputs, use the full product packing to obtain
`p_bin<=p_conv+r log2(12)<p_conv+4r`. For scalar sums use local tolerance
`epsilon/r` and the direct-Jensen radius-`r` product code to obtain
`p_bin<=p_conv+r log2(72)<p_conv+7r`. The summed absolute local errors
are at most the required scalar tolerance. Both constructions contain
the whole exact graph. The continuous-convex superadditivity argument
above verifies the only additional regularity issue.

These are finite real-coefficient, unrestricted-size formulation results.
They supply no algorithm for computing the functions or the partition
knots. Their scope is correctly separated from the polynomial rational
construction for dense positive polynomials. The final rewritten proof
and its constants were reread and passed.
