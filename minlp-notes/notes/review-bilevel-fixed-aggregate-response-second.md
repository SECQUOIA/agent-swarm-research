## First-order necessity and clipping

For a differentiable objective minimized locally on a polyhedron, its negative gradient lies in the polyhedral normal cone. The cone is generated by equality normals with unrestricted coefficients and active inequality normals with nonnegative coefficients. This conclusion follows directly from the linear alternative applied to feasible tangent directions. It does not require Slater's condition, a full-dimensional feasible set, or independent active constraints. Singleton feasible sets and redundant resource rows are included.

Splitting off the box normals leaves the stated scalar derivative `a_i z_i+B_i`. Since `a_i>0`, its box stationarity is exactly the clipping formula. At a lower endpoint the derivative is nonnegative, at an upper endpoint it is nonpositive, and in the interior it vanishes. The signs in both threshold polynomials are therefore correct. Fixed coordinates with equal lower and upper endpoints also satisfy the formula. Box endpoint ordering should be explicit or inconsistent boxes should be treated as infeasible.

The aggregate variable and resource multipliers have fixed dimension even as the number of box constraints grows. Positivity of the local quadratic coefficient, rather than convexity of the complete follower objective, makes elimination of each follower coordinate rational. A compressed point represents a feasible KKT point; it is not automatically a global minimizer.

## Regime enumeration and polynomial encodings

The clipping thresholds are polynomial functions of the fixed-dimensional compressed vector. Realizable joint sign conditions, including zero signs, have polynomial count and can be enumerated in polynomial bit time when degrees are bounded or polynomially encoded. The construction never enumerates the exponential Cartesian product of independent coordinate regimes. Sign conditions realized outside the leader set may be kept harmlessly because the final formula also requires membership in that set.

On the leader set every `a_i` and hence their product `Q` is strictly positive. Each clipped coordinate has a polynomial numerator over `Q`; the stated formulas for lower, upper, and interior regimes are correct. Clearing resource inequalities preserves their direction. The complementarity equations require precisely one factor `Q`. The displayed follower objective numerator equals `Q^2 f` on the aggregate-consistency equations.

Although `Q` multiplies an unbounded number of factors, its expanded degree is only linear in that number for fixed initial degree. In a fixed number of compressed variables, the number of possible monomials of polynomial degree is polynomial. Coefficient bit growth under the products and sums is polynomial as well. Substituting explicit bounded-degree upper monomials and clearing by `Q^D` therefore has polynomial encoding, including arbitrary cross terms between follower coordinates.

## Global comparison and exact algebraic optimization

For each feasible leader, the follower feasible set is compact, so it has a global minimizer. Every global minimizer has a compressed KKT representation, and every encoded KKT point is primal feasible. Therefore an encoded KKT point is globally minimizing exactly when its objective is no greater than that of every encoded KKT point. The universal second copy has the correct direction and quantifies every aggregate coordinate, multiplier, and comparison value. It imposes no upper-level restrictions on competing follower responses, as required by the original bilevel model.

A simple diagnostic is the follower `f(z)=z^4-z^2` on `[-1,1]`, represented by a positive local quadratic and an aggregate quartic. The stationary point zero is encoded but is not globally optimal; the two points with `z^2=1/2` have lower value. The universal comparison excludes zero. Thus the proof does not commit the usual error of treating nonconvex KKT conditions as sufficient.

Both quantified copies and all remaining free coordinates have fixed total dimension. The formula length, degree, and coefficient bit lengths are polynomial, so general real quantifier elimination gives polynomial bit complexity in this setting. I checked the sign-sampling discussion, Theorem 2.15, and the explicit quantifier-elimination degree and intermediate bit-size bounds of Theorem 2.18 in [Basu's primary-author survey](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011_final.pdf). These support the imported algorithmic steps. The candidate's older PostScript link did not parse in the web tool; the accessible survey gives the necessary statements directly.

The univariate set of attainable upper values can be represented exactly. Once attainment is established, its smallest value can be isolated and a compressed optimizer sampled over that value. Fixed-dimensional algebraic sampling provides a common extension of polynomial degree and encoding length for the compressed coordinates. Evaluating all follower rational functions in that same extension has polynomial total cost and output length; it does not create an independent field extension for every follower coordinate. Unbounded multipliers do not prevent algebraic sampling or invalidate the compactness argument, which concerns visible leader-response pairs.

## Attainment with changing right-hand sides

I read the main theorem and its proof in [Hoffman's 1952 paper](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/07/On_approximate_solutions_of_systems_of_linear_inequalities_%28IA_jresv49n4p263%29.pdf). The proof takes constants over subsets of a fixed row matrix, so the constant is independent of a changing right-hand side when the corresponding systems remain feasible. Equalities may be represented by opposite inequality rows.

For a convergent sequence of optimal leader-response pairs, primal feasibility passes to the limit. Given any feasible comparison response at the limit leader, its constraint residuals for the nearby leaders converge to zero. Hoffman's bound supplies nearby feasible comparisons converging to that response. Continuity of the follower objective then passes global optimality to the limit. This proves that the response graph is closed, including near leaders where feasibility elsewhere may fail.

Intersecting this graph with closed upper inequalities inside the compact leader-box product gives a compact optimistic feasible set. The continuous upper objective therefore attains a minimum. The example with the leader-dependent normal in `xz=0` correctly shows why continuity of the right-hand sides alone cannot justify attainment after allowing arbitrary changing normals.

## Proposed extensions checked independently

Polynomial box endpoints `l_i(x)<=u_i(x)` on a compact leader set are valid. Their ranges are uniformly bounded by continuity, and the box normals remain the fixed vectors `+e_i` and `-e_i`. Clipping remains rational, while the Hoffman argument includes these changing endpoints among the right-hand sides. Thus neither dimension reduction nor attainment fails.

Degrees can grow if the actual degree bound is polynomially bounded by the explicit encoding, as for dense or unary-exponent monomial input. Let that bound be `delta`. Clipping polynomials have degree `O(delta)`, the common denominator has degree `N delta`, coordinate and follower-value numerators have degree `O(N delta+delta)`, and substituted upper polynomials have degree `O(N delta^2)`. Expansions in fixed compressed dimension, sign determination, quantifier elimination, and algebraic recovery remain polynomial. Exponentially large degrees supplied only in binary are outside this argument.

A further optional extension is polynomial aggregate coefficients `U(x)` with a fixed row count: the derivative with respect to the follower is still `U(x)^T grad_w phi`, and aggregate consistency is still polynomial after clearing `Q`. This does not change the feasible follower constraint normals used for attainment. It therefore preserves the same proof, with polynomially increased degrees, if explicitly included in the model.

This audit checks mathematical correctness and exact encoding. The candidate's novelty relative to structured bilevel and parametric quadratic optimization remains a separate literature question.
