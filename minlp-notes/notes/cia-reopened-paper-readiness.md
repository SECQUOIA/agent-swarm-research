## Evidence and reproducibility

The [code guide](../code/cia_reopened/README.md) gives the public API, exact input conventions, and reproduction commands. The public research routines use standard-library rational arithmetic; SciPy is needed only by the optional LP/MILP comparisons. Independent reviewers used separate original-objective enumeration or independently derived formulations, in addition to reading proofs. Review records report their completed runs; the final integration check is recorded separately in [final-validation.json](../code/cia_reopened/final-validation.json).

- Finite-grid review compared the explicit formula with independently formulated allocation LPs, certified compressed LPs, direct schedule evaluation, and small original-disjunction MILPs. Further checks covered extreme rational grids and mode counts above `10^100`. Public input validation also passed with Python assertions disabled. Numerical MILPs corroborate the analytic proof; they do not replace it.
- The fixed-budget review checked 3,836 budget instances and 3,139 prescribed partitions against direct integration and exhaustive schedules. Another 608 dwell comparisons included 52 infeasible cases. Independent one-switch and prefix-completion checks cover ties, allowed boundaries, fixed initial modes, and residuals of either sign.
- The transfer review checked 8,819 exhaustive controls with 22,065 supported witnesses, 500 random rational schedules, 56 sharpness examples, and 200 binary nonuniform examples. Its proof establishes the universal result.
- The seeded-bound review checked 3,900 coefficients and 97 formal series, audited the unclipped refinement, and reran the existing four-block seed certificates. The seed retains its computer-assisted proof dependency.
- The small-grid proof has two independent integer enumerations. One recursively constructs feasible chambers; the other tests all 14,400 candidate floor histories. Both obtain 396 realizable strict histories, all admitting two-switch schedules. Exact feasibility and boundary-continuity arguments establish coverage.

The public Lotka–Volterra profile provides a modest application check. For the documented normalized and rationally quantized 12,000-cell input, the exact one-switch error is `1.889`, with a recorded solve of about `0.27` seconds. Permitting switches only on the documented 24-cell grid gives exact two-switch error `1.5`; the 12-cell grid gives exact three-switch error `0.8540056` with a repeated mode. Independent enumeration and evaluation on all original fine-grid prefixes agree. Coarse-grid results are optima for their permitted boundaries. These experiments do not establish full fine-grid multi-switch optimality, superior physical-state performance, or a general speed advantage over other solvers. Source URL, data hash, quantization bounds, and reproduction instructions are in the [benchmark note](cia-reopened-practical-algorithm.md).

## Literature and corrections

The [completed source audit](cia-reopened-literature.md) includes openly accessible primary papers, the full Zeile dissertation, the final Sager–Zeile article, matching-based CIA methods, SCARP, and a 2025 dwell-constrained method. It found no matching theorem for the retained specialized results within its bounded search. This wording is the supported novelty assessment; absence from the search is not proof of priority.

Credit support-preserving matching rounding, network integrality, standard subset dynamic programming, and existing state-error bounds. The new claims concern the particular CIA reductions, sharp constants, exact formulas, and stated complexity dependence. Do not claim invention of these general ingredients or of dwell-constrained rounding.

Two source issues are distinct. The five-cell theorem contradicts Corollary 5 as printed on p.611 of the final article. The sharp-transfer example invalidates the instance-wise half-grid reasoning on p.615 and dissertation p.139; that passage is an argument preceding a conjecture, not an established theorem. No replacement for Corollary 5 across its full parameter range is proved here. The old helper `cor5_lower` was renamed to `cor5_claim` in [cia_tv.py](../code/cia_tv_conjecture/cia_tv.py), and [the historical investigation](cia-investigation.md) now points to the correction.

## Retained negative findings and limits

- An initially incomplete one-switch minimax formula fails on a retained nine-mode nonuniform example. The final formula includes the missing family, and its exact extremizers and upper bounds passed review. The [investigation](cia-reopened-finite-grid.md) records the counterexample so that the discarded simplification is not reused.
- A proposed event LP for five-block reach admits a rational point with decreasing allocations between chronologically ordered events. The [exact witness](../code/cia_reopened/general_reach_relaxation_witness.json) and independent check are retained. It exposes missing chronological constraints in the relaxation; it is not a realizable control or a counterexample to the reach conjecture.
- The general exact finite-mode formula for five and more blocks remains open outside the established cases. The improved bound is usable without resolving it. The present event-relaxation route supplies no sound additional theorem.
- The fixed-budget algorithm still enumerates grid partitions; its exponent in `N` grows with the budget. It is not a demonstrated solution for dense grids with large switch budgets. Minimum dwell times are checked on every maximal run, including both horizon ends. Arbitrary transition graphs couple modes and are outside the subset separation theorem.
- Grid transfer does not preserve arbitrary minimum dwell times or transition restrictions. Its multicomponent theorem uses a uniform grid; only the binary refinement here covers arbitrary nonuniform grids.

## Manuscript scope and stopping decision

A paper can lead with exact finite-grid minimax and exact optimization for small switch budgets, supported by sharp continuous-to-grid transfer and the corrected literature boundary. The seeded bound is a supporting theorem; the exact five-cell result is a separate correction with an explicit computer-assisted proof. The existing continuous results can provide context or be incorporated with their full certificate dependencies, depending on manuscript length.

The current ideas have been developed through explicit formulas, constructive witnesses, exact code, dwell constraints, benchmark checks, sharper bounds, and independent review. The retained claims have no outstanding verification gate. Further progress on the unresolved general reach problem or a broad application study would require a substantial additional investigation. Neither is needed to state and prove the results retained here. Research is therefore closed at this boundary, with unresolved questions and useful negative evidence preserved.
