# Source comparison for weighted cactus optimization with a fixed number of load factors

Date: 2026-09-06. Candidate: [weighted cactus investigation](potential-flow-reopened-weighted-investigation.md). This is an independent bounded literature comparison, not a proof audit. The proposed theorem fixes the dimension of an affine family of nominations, allows arbitrarily many cycles and arbitrary weighted potential support, and asks for additive approximation in polynomial input and accuracy-bit time.

No inspected primary source states this combined network result. The closest generic predecessor is Vigneron's optimization of sums of algebraic functions. It substantially overlaps the architecture but does not supply the claimed precision dependence. The fixed-dimensional correlated-load model also has a concrete connection to point-to-point demand uncertainty in potential-flow literature. These findings support treating the theorem as a candidate computational contribution, subject to proof review; they do not establish priority by absence.

## Closest generic algorithm

Antoine Vigneron, *Geometric optimization and sums of algebraic functions*, ACM Transactions on Algorithms 10(1), 2014, DOI [10.1145/2532647](https://doi.org/10.1145/2532647); [open author manuscript](https://antoinevigneron.github.io/manuscripts/rational.pdf).

Vigneron optimizes sums of bounded nonnegative algebraic functions in fixed dimension, with constant-complexity supports. Theorem 6 uses an arrangement of geometrically spaced level sets; Section 3.2 approximates summand values to avoid exact comparison of algebraic sums. Section 2.3 explicitly discusses a bit model, so it would be inaccurate to dismiss this result as real-RAM only. Its running time is polynomial in `1/epsilon`, including in that bit model. [[vigneron2014-geometric-optimization-and-sums-of]] p.5-9

That is a direct predecessor for a common partition and approximate summation. It does not imply polynomial dependence on `log(1/epsilon)`. Shifting bounded signed terms to make them nonnegative can transfer a relative guarantee to an additive one, but may require relative tolerance proportional to `epsilon/M`, where `M` is the aggregate magnitude bound. On rational inputs, `M` can be exponentially large in its encoding length. This argument therefore supplies no shortcut to the candidate's accuracy-bit guarantee. Its decisive additional work is the polynomial-size uniformly accurate surrogate, with explicit rational coefficients, before fixed-dimensional optimization.

The candidate should not claim a new general principle of replacing exact algebraic-sum comparisons by approximation. It should credit Vigneron even though the approximation representation and precision dependence differ.

## Network comparison

Klimm, Pfetsch, Raber, and Skutella, *On the robustness of potential-based flow networks*, Mathematical Programming 197 (2023), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-021-01760-w), study point-to-point demands. Their primary formulation explains that pairwise transmission-right bounds induce a lower-dimensional set of nodal nomination scenarios. The main results characterize when reduced demands cannot worsen maximum normalized stress; they do not optimize an arbitrary weighted potential sum over a fixed-dimensional affine family to additive accuracy. This is a useful application reference for correlated nominations, not a competing algorithm for the candidate objective. [[klimm2023-on-the-robustness-of-potential]] p.2-4 [[klimm2023-on-the-robustness-of-potential]] p.9-10

The [Pfetsch–Schmidt–Skutella–Thürauf overview](https://optimization-online.org/2026/01/potential-based-flows-an-overview/) treats maximum potential difference over nomination bounds, records tractability of trees and one cycle, and identifies nonlinear cactus complexity as open. That setting has a pairwise objective and does not fix affine-load dimension. Its open statement is therefore useful context but does not mean the candidate settles the full open problem. In particular, an exact threshold question remains different from additive approximation. [[pfetsch2026-potential-based-flowsan-overview]] p.9-10

The [Wang–Hasler structural convexity report](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/d2fb4e43-bb7e-42b7-977c-39560d9e7d46/content) supplies sufficient topology and curvature conditions for convex or concave source-to-resistor transfer characteristics, including ladder networks. It is not a general weighted-global-optimization theorem. The candidate instead accommodates signed weighted objectives whose convexity is not assumed. [[hasler1997-convexity-of-resistive-circuit-characteristics]] p.12-13 [[hasler1997-convexity-of-resistive-circuit-characteristics]] p.17-18

An additional 2026 search hit, Pardo-Guerra, Thapa, and Washburn, [*The Cactus Criterion: When Nonlinear Hodge Theory Reduces to Linear on Graphs*](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17775v1), concerns minimization over an affine gradient class and a nonlinear coclosed selector for one common even convex edge potential. Its cactus criterion explains when that selector equals the linear Hodge projector. The inspected setup and main statements do not provide optimization over affine nominations with heterogeneous asymmetric quadratic laws and a weighted potential objective. This is a structural neighbor, not evidence that the candidate is already proved.

## What the contribution statement can safely say

“For cactus networks with fixed asymmetric quadratic laws, a fixed number of correlated affine load factors permits additive optimization of any gauge-invariant weighted potential objective in polynomial input and accuracy-bit time, with rational feasible load recovery. The proof combines local cycle formulas with a uniformly accurate rational polynomial representation and fixed-dimensional real-algebraic optimization.”

This statement is conditional on the candidate proof passing review. The representation, branch-domain construction, denominator handling, and rational feasible recovery are essential parts of the combined result. Generic real quantifier elimination with one variable per cycle would not have fixed dimension.

Do not claim novelty for Taylor approximation, geometric refinement near a square-root singularity, fixed-dimensional quantifier elimination, or rational-function denominator clearing. Nor should an observation that a sum has growing algebraic degree be called a hardness proof. The [existing inverse-approximation source audit](monotone-polynomial-inverse-approximation-novelty.md) records older algebraic-series and inverse-approximation predecessors; the present square-root construction is a simpler supporting specialization.

The follow-up searched cactus weighted potential optimization, nomination uncertainty, fixed-dimensional correlated loads, algebraic-sum optimization, rational/piecewise-polynomial approximation, and accuracy-bit complexity. The unrestricted nomination-box/fixed-objective-support extension requires its own face-reduction proof. The follow-up below records the proposed joint-resistance extension and its exact scope.


## Follow-up: independent interval resistance uncertainty

The [joint-resistance draft](potential-flow-reopened-joint-weighted.md) now proposes the stronger theorem with symmetric quadratic laws `beta_e x_e|x_e|`, independent positive rational intervals for every `beta_e`, a fixed-dimensional compact rational affine nomination family, and arbitrary gauge-invariant weighted potential support. The proposed output is a certified additive optimum interval and rational epsilon-optimal original nomination and resistance parameters, with polynomial dependence on input length and `log(1/epsilon)`. The number of cactus cycles is unrestricted. This is the precise claim compared here; the fixed asymmetric-law theorem above remains a separate result.

The meaningful extra step is elimination of all local resistance variables by one-equality box LP duality, followed by polynomially many circulation candidates. A common partition chooses the best local surrogate before summing over all cycles. Neither elementary LP duality nor the existence of a threshold solution is a standalone novelty claim. The existing internal common-factor/fixed-linking-constraint work must also be credited; the proposed change is that the number of independent cactus blocks is unbounded while the final optimization dimension remains fixed.

### Closest network precedents

**Independent resistance intervals and cycle reductions are established models.** Aßmann, Liers, Stingl, and Vera, *Deciding Robust Feasibility and Infeasibility Using a Set Containment Approach: An Application to Stationary Passive Gas Network Operations*, SIAM Journal on Optimization 28 (2018), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/17M112470X), [open manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.10241), explicitly fixes nominations and lets positive pipe pressure-loss coefficients vary independently in intervals. Its robust question is whether every coefficient realization admits pressures within prescribed bounds. Tree instances reduce exactly to LP; on one cycle, monotonicity of the circulation equation converts circulation intervals to polyhedral coefficient regions. General certificates use polynomial optimization and the Lasserre hierarchy. These are direct predecessors for resistance uncertainty, circulation sign partitions, and certified robust feasibility. The inspected statements do not give arbitrary weighted potential optimization on unbounded cacti in polynomial accuracy-bit time. [[amann2018-deciding-robust-feasibility-and-infeasibility]] p.15-18 [[amann2018-deciding-robust-feasibility-and-infeasibility]] p.20-21 [[amann2018-deciding-robust-feasibility-and-infeasibility]] p.28-29

**Joint load and friction uncertainty is also established.** González Grandón, Heitsch, and Henrion, *A joint model of probabilistic/robust constraints for gas transport management in stationary networks*, Computational Management Science 14 (2017), [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10287-017-0284-7), [open WIAS preprint](https://www.wias-berlin.de/people/heitsch/GHH17_Preprint.pdf), use Gaussian exit loads and robust roughness uncertainty on a passive tree with one entry. They optimize the size of the admissible roughness uncertainty set subject to a probability guarantee. Their inner resistance minimization is explicit for rectangles and ellipsoids because the relevant tree expression is linear in resistance. Probability is then computed through sampled spherical-radial integration. The proposed cactus result does not invent joint uncertainty or endpoint resistance elimination; its difference is deterministic weighted extrema, cycles, and a certified accuracy-bit algorithm. [[henrion2017-a-joint-model-of-probabilistic]] p.6-10 [[henrion2017-a-joint-model-of-probabilistic]] p.11-12

**Do not conflate two probust models.** Adelhütte and coauthors, *Joint Model of Probabilistic-Robust (Probust) Constraints Applied to Gas Network Optimization*, Vietnam Journal of Mathematics 49 (2021), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10013-020-00434-y), explicitly excludes roughness uncertainty from its scope. It instead combines random exit loads and robust entry-load allocation while studying booked capacity. Section 3.4 uses affine rays in nomination space, cycle variables, and polynomial systems to identify feasible-ray intervals. Thus low-dimensional load slices and algebraic cycle computation are direct methodological predecessors. The paper does not state polynomial accuracy-bit complexity independent of cycle count. [[wintergerst2021-joint-model-of-probabilistic-robust]] p.3 [[wintergerst2021-joint-model-of-probabilistic-robust]] p.12-13 [[wintergerst2021-joint-model-of-probabilistic-robust]] p.17-19

**Direct 2016 source check completed.** Gotzes, Heitsch, Henrion, and Schultz, *On the quantification of nomination feasibility in stationary gas networks with random load*, [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00186-016-0564-y), has an [open author manuscript](https://www.wias-berlin.de/people/heitsch/GHHS16_Preprint.pdf), now retrieved locally. Theorem 6 gives a continuous piecewise quadratic-radical or rational circulation function along an affine nomination ray. Its proof partitions the ray using roots of quadratic sign-boundary equations; pressure-feasibility boundaries then reduce to quartics. The final section explicitly extends the methodology to node-disjoint cycles with attached trees. Thus local radical formulas, affine load slices, and disjoint-cycle extension are direct prior work. The source keeps resistance parameters fixed in its analysis and does not give weighted extrema over interval resistances or the proposed unbounded-cactus accuracy-bit theorem. [[schultz2016-on-the-quantification-of-nomination]] p.18-22 [[schultz2016-on-the-quantification-of-nomination]] p.25-26

The locally available Gotzes–Nitsche–Schultz 2017 paper treats fixed coefficients, sampled affine rays and up to three interacting cycles with Gröbner systems, rather than the joint weighted theorem. [[gotzes2017-probability-of-feasible-loads-in]] p.3-6 [[gotzes2017-probability-of-feasible-loads-in]] p.10-15

The more general adaptive probust algorithm of Berthold, Heitsch, Henrion, and Schwientek, *On the algorithmic solution of optimization problems subject to probabilistic/robust (probust) constraints*, Mathematical Methods of Operations Research 96 (2022), [open publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00186-021-00764-8), proves convergence of an adaptive inequality-discretization scheme. Its conceptual iterations solve two optimization problems; its gas-load/friction discussion motivates the uncertainty model rather than supplies the proposed polynomial oracle. The inspected abstract, introduction, algorithm discussion, and conclusion contain no matching accuracy-bit claim.

### Practical use and limitations

The proposed oracle could certify adverse weighted pressure responses for a correlated load model and uncertain pipe parameters. It could also serve inside scenario-generation or design methods, but that is an application opportunity rather than a proved end-to-end design guarantee. Thürauf, Grübel, and Schmidt's *Adjustable Robust Nonlinear Network Design* (2025), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-025-02207-2), provides a concrete finite-scenario framework under load uncertainty; its general inner potential and flow extrema remain optimization subproblems. A new oracle must match its precise uncertainty and operating constraints before being substituted. [[thurauf2025-adjustable-robust-nonlinear-network-design]] p.3-5 [[thurauf2025-adjustable-robust-nonlinear-network-design]] p.9-10

Hydraulic interval state estimation and constrained-zonotope methods cited in the [reopened audit](potential-flow-reopened-literature-audit.md) also treat combinations of load and model uncertainty. Those give useful practical comparison points, but do not by themselves assert the exact weighted hull or accuracy-bit guarantee at issue here.

The independently reviewed joint draft now has two further, distinct corollaries. First, arbitrary balanced nomination boxes are allowed when weighted objective support is fixed, using the separately reviewed face-contraction theorem; this retains rational epsilon-optimal original parameters but excludes operating filters. Second, fixed-dimensional affine nominations permit exact local arc-flow capacities, exact feasibility decision, and algebraic epsilon-optimal original parameters. Rational output under capacities needs a supplied positive tightening margin and is compared with the tightened optimum, not the unrestricted constrained optimum. These distinctions are essential. The inspected sources do not state either precise combined corollary. Resistance correlations across cycles and cross-cycle potential filters remain outside the proved scope.

Vigneron's predecessor remains relevant after resistance elimination. Taking local maxima and common arrangements does not remove the difference between polynomial `1/epsilon` and polynomial `log(1/epsilon)`. Conversely, no source search establishes novelty on its own. A defensible current description is a **candidate combined network algorithm**, distinguished by arbitrary weighted support, fixed load-factor dimension, independent continuous resistance intervals, unbounded cactus cycle count, and certified accuracy-bit complexity with original-parameter recovery.

## Final addendum: exact rational resistance design at fixed nominations

[Section 8 of the joint result](../results/potential-flow-joint-weighted-cactus-accuracy-bits.md) strengthens the fixed-nomination specialization: rational fixed balanced nominations, an arbitrary rational weighted potential objective, independent positive rational resistance intervals, and optional rational signed arc capacities on a cactus admit a polynomial-bit algorithm returning an **exact optimizing rational resistance profile**, whenever feasible. Objective support and cycle count are unrestricted. The objective value can remain a sum of independent quadratic-algebraic local values; the theorem does not decide an arbitrary rational threshold for that sum.

The focused final search combined cactus/potential-based flow, fixed demands, weighted pressure or potential objectives, and continuous resistance/roughness optimization. It found no primary statement of this precise guarantee. This negative search result is bounded evidence only. The main inspected predecessor remains Aßmann et al. (2018): fixed-demand interval-resistance robust feasibility and one-cycle coefficient-region reductions, not arbitrary weighted exact optimization or a rational optimizing resistance profile. The tree resistance minimizations in González Grandón et al. (2017) are linear and explicitly solvable; their result does not supply the cycle stationary-point and interior-resistance cases. Gotzes et al. (2016) supplies local exact radical formulas with fixed resistance, not the resistance design result. Their precise source locators are recorded above.

A practical neighboring primary source is *Bi-objective design-for-control of water distribution networks with global bounds*, [open publisher article, DOI 10.1007/s11081-021-09598-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11081-021-09598-z). Section 3.2 uses tailored spatial branch-and-bound to obtain feasible solutions and global bounds for pressure/resilience objectives with valve and pipe design variables. Appendix F uses head loss `q(a|q|+b)`. This supports the practical role of pressure optimization and certified bounds, but differs from continuous independent coefficient intervals on a fixed cactus and gives no matching polynomial exact-rational-profile guarantee. The inspected scope is Sections 2–3.2 and Appendix F; this source was read online and not added as a new local package.

The new four-edge example gives an exact reason not to identify this corollary with endpoint-profile methods: its unique optimizer has an interior resistance. That is an obstruction to a particular shortcut, not by itself a priority claim. The defensible contribution statement is the combined exact algorithm and rational-witness theorem, including its stationary and aggregate-boundary recovery argument. Exact local comparison is sufficient because the blocks separate; no exact comparison of growing sums of radicals is needed to choose the optimal resistance profile.
