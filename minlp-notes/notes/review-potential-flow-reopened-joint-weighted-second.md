## Local physical model and resistance elimination

A spanning-tree particular flow depends affinely on the nominations, and on a cactus each chord circulation affects only its own cycle. After consistent cycle orientation, all cycle flows have the form q+ell_e(z). Expressing c^T pi through tree paths supplies rational fixed edge weights; chords have weight zero. Reversing an edge reverses its weight as required by the odd law. Because the fundamental cycles are edge-disjoint, their compatibility equations suffice to integrate all edge drops into one global potential. Thus the local objectives and resistance choices separate at a fixed z. A global rational bound on total positive nomination gives a polynomial-bit bound on every physical flow and hence on every q.

The one-equality box LP is exact at fixed (z,q). For a multiplier lambda, every non-tied coordinate maximizes its reduced-cost term at the specified endpoint. Tied coordinates have a continuous interval of attainable total contributions, including when individual f_e are negative or zero. The two inequalities S+lo_T≤0≤S+hi_T are necessary and sufficient for completing these coordinates. On their domain the equality constraint removes the lambda term, proving the displayed quadratic objective Q_lambda.

Conversely, the finite piecewise-linear convex dual has a minimum at an edge weight. Feasibility bounds it below, so it cannot decrease indefinitely along an affine tail. A flat minimizing tail reaches its endpoint; a constant dual permits every threshold. When all f_e vanish, every threshold is feasible and the objective is zero. Zero-width resistance intervals and tied weights do not invalidate this argument. LP strong duality applies to the feasible compact box slice even if its relative dimension is zero. The argument therefore covers all LP optima without enumerating arbitrary endpoint subsets.

The sign hyperplane arrangement lies in d+1 variables, with d fixed. Its number of cells is polynomial in the cycle length. Closed cells cover zero flows without changing f_e=0, so using closures neither loses physical points nor introduces a wrong sign at zero. Multiplying this polynomial count by the number of distinct weights still gives polynomially many threshold domains.

## Circulation candidates, degeneracies, and encodings

At a fixed z, each nonempty local domain is a compact subset of a line defined by finitely many polynomial inequalities. A nonconstant quadratic objective has a maximum at an interval endpoint, an isolated feasible point, or a stationary point in an interval interior. A constant objective is handled by the flat branch. The artificial q bounds ensure compactness and supply boundaries even if all other inequalities are independent of q.

All boundary polynomials have constant rational q² coefficient, affine q coefficient, and quadratic constant term in z. For a nonzero leading coefficient, both quadratic roots must be considered; the conditions 2aq+b≥0 and ≤0 select the plus and minus radicals independently of the sign of a. Double roots are harmless. Substitution into the quadratic objective yields P(z)+L(z)sqrt(Delta(z)), with quadratic P, affine L, and nonzero constant rational denominators. This constant-denominator property is the decisive advantage over optimizing variable resistances inside an unreduced physical-root formula.

When a=0 and b(z)≠0, the root is a rational function and the objective has denominator b(z)². When a=b(z)=0, the constraint is independent of q at that z: it either excludes the whole fiber or imposes no q boundary. Other boundaries, stationarity, or the flat case still supply a candidate. The affine q coefficient of Q can similarly vanish on a lower-dimensional locus; the explicit flat branch covers it. No proof step divides by a coefficient that might vanish on its domain.

Projecting one q and d fixed parameters uses bounded-degree real quantifier elimination in fixed dimension. Both the number and degree of resulting domain polynomials, and their rational coefficient bit lengths, are polynomially bounded. Candidate formulas may overlap or have disconnected domains; the algorithm never requires a single connected projection. Importantly, it eliminates each q separately, rather than introducing one optimization variable for every cycle.

## Addendum: fixed-nomination exact rational optimizer

The author subsequently added Section 8, and the coordinating agent requested a separate delta audit. **This strengthening passes**, including optional rational signed arc capacities. This conclusion follows from a fresh check of the boundary classification, not merely from setting d=0 in the approximate theorem.

With fixed rational nominations every ell_e, sign breakpoint, capacity endpoint after subtracting ell_e, and artificial q bound is rational. All coefficients of Q_lambda are rational. A nonflat quadratic's stationary point is rational; a linear nonconstant objective has no stationary point. Hence a rational candidate q yields rational f_e and a rational one-row box LP. Its greedy tied-coordinate fill uses polynomially many arithmetic operations on polynomial-bit rational coefficients. Each recovered resistance has polynomial bit length.

If a candidate q is irrational, it cannot arise from a sign, capacity, or artificial-bound equality. A nonconstant quadratic domain polynomial must instead be one of S+lo_T or S+hi_T. At S+lo_T=0 the required tied total is exactly the sum of all tied intervals' lowest contributions. No contribution can exceed its own minimum without raising the total, so every nonzero-f tied coordinate must be at its corresponding resistance endpoint. The argument for S+hi_T=0 is identical with maxima. Zero-f coordinates contribute zero at either endpoint. Non-tied coordinates are already endpoints. Thus a rational endpoint profile realizes the exact irrational circulation and its objective value. In fact, with fixed rational offsets an irrational q cannot make any f_e zero, but the proof's separate zero case is harmless and also handles rational boundary points.

If both aggregate endpoints coincide, their difference is sum_{e in T}|f_e|(U_e−L_e)=0. Every summand is nonnegative, so each nonzero-f tied interval has zero width. The two endpoint constructions then agree on all relevant coordinates and are both feasible. Identically-zero domain polynomials provide no boundary and are correctly excluded.

For a flat objective, a nonempty closed bounded feasible q-set has a boundary point. If all nonconstant domain inequalities were strict there, a neighborhood would remain feasible, contradicting that it is a boundary. A nonconstant active polynomial therefore supplies one of the classified candidates; artificial q bounds cover the case with no other effective restriction. An isolated feasible point obeys the same argument. Selecting such a boundary preserves the flat objective and gives a rational profile by the preceding cases.

The number of sign intervals, thresholds, and boundary/stationary candidates per cycle is polynomial. All candidate q values and objective values have algebraic degree at most two with polynomial coefficient heights. Exact sign testing of the local inequalities and comparing two candidate values can use constant-degree algebraic arithmetic or root isolation; their compositum has degree at most four. Polynomial bit complexity follows without an accuracy parameter. Empty local feasible sets and violating bridge capacities certify infeasibility. Feasible bridges have fixed rational flow and choose an objective-maximizing rational resistance endpoint.

Finally, capacities remain local to their cycles, so the exact cycle winners can be combined independently. The assembled profile is rational and exactly optimizes the original global weighted objective. A symbolic sum of the local quadratic-algebraic optimum values is a compact representation of the optimum; this does not imply polynomial-time comparison of that sum with a rational threshold. The distinction between an exact optimizing *scenario* and an exact global scalar-threshold algorithm is maintained in Section 8.

The independent exact LP checks already cover tied extreme contributions, zero coefficients, singleton intervals, and rational recovery. The existing root-identity checks cover both quadratic branches and repeated roots. They support this addendum's ingredients; they are not an implementation of the complete new exact optimizing-profile algorithm. No additional theorem premise or correction is needed. As with the main theorem, this audit establishes internal mathematical confidence, not literature priority.
