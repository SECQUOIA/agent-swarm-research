# Independent audit of fixed-support weighted optimization on trees

Date: 2026-09-05. Reviewer: `noncommutative_rank_review`, independent of
the author and the other reviewer.

Reviewed: [the fixed-support weighted-tree candidate](potential-flow-fixed-support-weighted-tree.md).
The final section also audits the separately requested arbitrary-support

**Verdict: PASS.** The zero-weight contraction, resistance-independent

and `N^{O(p)}` bit bound are correct under the stated assumptions. No
required mathematical correction was found. This review does not promote
the candidate or establish novelty.

## Cut representation and zero-weight contraction

For the stated incidence convention, a connected tree has full column
rank and its incidence range is exactly the zero-sum vectors. Therefore
`Aw=c` and `Ax=b` each have a unique solution. The coordinates are signed
cut sums, and

```
c^T*pi=w^T*A^T*pi=sum_e w_e*beta_e*x_e*|x_e|.
```

The tree flow depends only on nominations. Every balanced nomination
admits its physical state: conservation supplies the flow, and summing
edge drops supplies potentials up to a constant.

If `w_e=0`, that edge contributes nothing to the objective. Contracting
all such edges preserves every retained cut flow when the original
nominations are replaced by their cluster sums. It also preserves the
retained cut weights when the objective coefficients are summed within
clusters. This argument does not require original potentials to be equal
inside a contracted cluster, which would generally be false.

The sum of independent real nomination intervals is their exact summed
interval. Each balanced aggregate nomination can therefore be disaggregated
independently inside each cluster while keeping the total sum zero.
There is no other coupling constraint to preserve. All resistance choices
on zero-weight edges are irrelevant to the objective and can be recovered
arbitrarily from their allowed sets.

There remain at most `p` nonzero aggregate objective vertices, since each
one contains an original nonzero coefficient and clusters are disjoint.
A retained-tree leaf must have a nonzero coefficient: its single cut
weight would otherwise vanish. Hence there are at most `p` leaves and
at most `p-2` branching vertices. Marking objective and branching vertices
gives at most `2p-2` marked vertices and `2p-3` intervening paths.

All unmarked internal vertices have degree two and zero objective
coefficient, so consistently oriented path cut weights are constant and
nonzero. The case with no remaining edge is handled separately; it includes
all zero objectives and avoids applying the displayed path counts when
`p<2`.

## Gradient and threshold completeness

With fixed positive resistances, the smoothed objective is

```
F_rho(b)=sum_e w_e*beta_e*(x_e*|x_e|+rho*x_e).
```

It is continuously differentiable because flows are linear in nominations.
For a balanced direction `db=A*dx`, its differential is
`sum_e w_e*beta_e*(2|x_e|+rho)*dx_e`. There is a potential vector `h`,
unique modulo constants, with

```
A^T*h=(w_e*beta_e*(2|x_e|+rho))_e.
```

Thus the differential equals `h^T*db`, establishing the claimed gradient
formula without invoking an unproved inverse derivative. Along each
suppressed path, every gradient difference has the same nonzero sign.
The gradient is therefore strictly ordered along that path for every
nomination, every positive resistance vector, and every `rho>0`.

At a constrained maximum, the gradient belongs to the normal cone of the
box intersected with the balance hyperplane. For a polyhedron its normal
cone is generated by the active inequality normals and the equality
normal, without an interior-point qualification. Consequently a scalar
`lambda` exists with the stated lower/upper/free conditions. A coordinate
whose two bounds coincide causes no defect: both endpoint identities are
true and its box normal is unrestricted.

Strict gradient order implies that at most one internal path coordinate
can be strictly between its bounds. Coordinates on either side must have
the prescribed endpoint status. If a gradient equals `lambda` at an
endpoint-valued coordinate, the enumeration may simply declare it free;
the corresponding closed face still contains that nomination. Fixed
coordinates can be assigned either endpoint status. Thus the free-vertex
placements and cuts between vertices cover every required threshold
configuration, including degeneracies.

Each path with `k` internal vertices has at most `2k+1` choices, and each
marked vertex has three statuses. The product count is `N^{O(p)}`.
Every enumerated face has at most one free coordinate per path and one
per marked vertex, hence at most `4p-5` before applying balance. These
faces form a sufficient optimizer family; they need not contain every
feasible nomination.

## Zero-flow limit and joint resistances

On the compact nomination polytope, all tree flows are uniformly bounded.
For the fixed resistance vector,

```
F_rho-F_0=rho*sum_e w_e*beta_e*x_e
```

converges uniformly to zero. The finite face family is independent of
`rho`. Along a sequence of smoothed maximizers, choose a subsequence on
one face and then a convergent subsequence. That face is closed, so its
limit belongs to it. Uniform convergence shows the limit is an original
maximizer. This proves completeness even when an optimum has many zero
flows or the balanced nomination polytope has lower dimension.

For joint optimization, the nomination polytope and the independent
resistance sets are compact, and the objective is continuous. Hold the
resistances of a joint optimizer fixed and apply the preceding result.
The same graph-and-cut-weight face family supplies a nomination attaining
that joint value. This applies to both finite sets and closed intervals;
no resistance derivative or monotonicity assumption beyond positivity is
used in the face argument.

## Resistance elimination and sign cells

For fixed nominations the objective is linear in each independent
resistance. Choosing its upper or lower endpoint according to the sign
of `w_e*x_e*|x_e|` gives the exact maximum. Both endpoints are allowed
even when the input is a finite set. At zero contribution either choice
is optimal. Thus intermediate discrete values cannot improve the result.

On a nominated face, balance and any further forced equalities can be
eliminated using rational linear algebra. The affine dimension is at
most `4p-5`. Every original or retained edge flow is rational affine
in these variables; using retained edges alone would also suffice for
the objective. Identically zero flow forms can be removed.

The full-dimensional flow-sign cells relative to the feasible affine hull
have polynomial number for fixed dimension. Their closures cover the
feasible polytope: perturb any feasible point toward a relative-interior
point outside the finitely many nontrivial hyperplanes and select a
subsequence in one cell. On shared boundaries, a zero flow contributes
zero regardless of the resistance endpoint assigned there.

An implementation should test full-dimensional cell interiors, not retain
every closed sign pattern merely because its boundary is feasible. The
candidate explicitly makes the required relative-hull and full-dimensional
qualification. Rational linear feasibility with a common positive slack
can implement these tests. The resulting objective on each closed cell
is one rational quadratic, with polynomial coefficient bit length.

## Exact rational quadratic optimization

Let `z*` be a global maximizer on a bounded rational polyhedron `P`.
It lies in the relative interior of its minimal face. Choose an independent
basis `I` of its active inequality normals. All active affine equalities
follow from this basis because they hold consistently at `z*`.
Stationarity along that face gives

```
Q*z*+g=A_I^T*mu,
A_I*z*=d_I.
```

No multiplier sign is required for this argument. Enumerating all
independent row sets of size at most the ambient dimension therefore
includes a stationarity system containing a global maximizer. The empty
set covers an interior stationary point, while a full-rank set covers a
vertex.

For two feasible solutions `(z,mu)` and `(z',mu')` of one selected system,
put `v=z'-z`. Then

```
A_I*v=0,
Q*v=A_I^T*(mu'-mu),
v^T*Q*v=0,
v^T*(Q*z+g)=0.
```

The exact quadratic expansion gives `q(z')=q(z)`. Hence the linear
feasibility solver may return any point of that system, including when
the Hessian or stationarity equations are singular. If the system
contains a global maximizer, its returned point has the same optimal
value. Other systems can only return feasible points and so cannot
overestimate the true maximum.

Every nonempty rational linear system with inequalities admits a rational
feasible point of polynomial encoding length. This remains true with
unbounded free multipliers; they can be treated as ordinary unrestricted
LP variables. The returned nomination point and its evaluated quadratic
objective are therefore rational of polynomial bit length. This is an
exact rational algorithm, not a numerical KKT heuristic or an algebraic
root approximation.

## Complexity and recovery

The face enumeration, sign-cell enumeration in dimension `O(p)`, and
active-normal subset enumeration each contribute an `N^{O(p)}` factor.
Their exponents add under multiplication. Rational LP and linear algebra
are polynomial in their encoded inputs, so the total remains `N^{O(p)}`.
Cut summation, affine-hull parameterization, and coefficient construction
have polynomial bit growth. This is an XP-type bound, not an FPT claim.

A rational aggregate nomination can be disaggregated by starting at
original lower bounds and allocating the remaining rational total up to
each available upper-bound slack. This preserves every retained cut flow.
Recover resistance endpoints, compute original flows by cut sums, and
integrate rational quadratic drops on the original tree. Every output
vector has polynomial binary length for fixed `p`. Negating `c` proves
the minimum version with unchanged support size.

I reran the existing mechanism checker. All 3,119 stationary candidates,
30 exact contraction cases, and four degenerate quadratic controls passed.
Its stationary computations use floating-point linear algebra, so these
checks supplement rather than replace the rational proof above.

## Additional audit: arbitrary-support NP/coNP membership

The following consequence is also sound when support is not fixed. It
is a membership statement, not a polynomial optimization algorithm.
Keep the same finite rational nomination box, zero-sum rational objective,
and independent positive resistance intervals or explicitly listed finite
sets on an arbitrary connected tree.

Eliminate resistance variables by their exact endpoint rule. Choose a
closed flow-sign cell containing a global maximizer. In the original
nomination variables this is a bounded rational polyhedron described by
polynomially many linear inequalities, and its objective is a rational
quadratic with polynomial encoding length. No threshold-face reduction
or bounded support is needed to describe this particular cell.

The stationary-face argument above still supplies a nonempty rational
linear system containing an optimal point. Its dimension may now grow,
but its number of variables, coefficients, and bit lengths are polynomial
in the original input. A polynomial-bit rational feasible point exists,
and the constant-objective argument makes it an optimal nomination for
that cell. Thus the whole problem has a polynomial-bit rational global
optimizer, together with rational resistance endpoints. It is unnecessary
to enumerate all sign cells or active faces to establish this certificate
existence.

This also has a direct primary citation:
[Del Pia, Dey, and Molinaro, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.4798),
printed page 3, states the small rational linear-system representation
of a continuous quadratic optimum and credits Vavasis for QP membership
in NP. I read that theorem directly. The explicit stationarity proof here
verifies its application to the present bounded quadratic cell.

A verifier reads a rational nomination and resistance vector, checks the
nomination box and balance, checks membership in each resistance set,
computes the unique tree flows and potential differences rationally, and
evaluates the objective. Every operation has polynomial bit complexity.
Thus the threshold question `max F>=T` belongs to NP. If `max F>J`, an
optimal rational witness also certifies that strict violation; universal
satisfaction `F<=J` belongs to coNP.

The reviewed growing-support comb-tree reduction supplies NP-hardness.
Its gap additionally supplies coNP-hardness of robust upper-bound
satisfaction, for example at `J=K-1/4`. Therefore the unrestricted-support
tree class is NP-complete for existential thresholds and coNP-complete for
robust weighted upper bounds, already on the fixed-resistance subclass.
This does not establish such memberships for arbitrary cyclic networks
or for models with additional nonlinear operating constraints.

The fixed-support tractability theorem and the arbitrary-support complete
classification are compatible. The obstruction is the growing objective
support, while the certificate-size argument remains polynomial regardless
of that support.
