# Series-parallel arc envelopes and the discrete rank boundary: source audit

Date: 2026-09-05. This is a bounded primary-literature comparison, not a proof audit. Candidates: [single-envelope optimization](potential-flow-series-parallel-envelope-optimization.md), [reviewed series-parallel arc hull theorem](../results/potential-flow-series-parallel-arc-validation.md), and [reviewed rank-three discrete arc hardness](../results/potential-flow-discrete-arc-capacity-hardness.md).

No matching theorem was located for polynomial bit-time additive optimization of one signed arc flow over independent quadratic-resistance uncertainty on every undirected series-parallel graph, with fixed nominations, unbounded cycle rank, and recovery of a near-optimal allowed endpoint scenario. Nor was a matching exact rank-two polynomial / rank-three coNP-complete boundary for finite resistance uncertainty found. These remain plausible computational contributions, subject to the proof reviews and the search limitations below. The circuit sign mechanism is classical. Priority of the single-envelope identity itself is **not cleared**, because a directly relevant 1993 nonlinear tolerance paper remains unread.

## What is already established in circuit theory

R. J. Duffin, *Topology of Series-Parallel Networks*, Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications 10, 303–318 (1965), DOI 10.1016/0022-247X(65)90125-3, [open primary PDF](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~reu/papers/current/jim/duffin.pdf). Theorems 0 and 1, printed pages 306–307, connect resistance-independent current directions with confluence and the absence of an embedded Wheatstone configuration. Section 5, especially Theorem 4 on printed pages 314–316, extends series/parallel characteristic construction to strictly monotone nonlinear resistors. The source covers classical topology, current directions, and nonlinear equivalent characteristics. The inspected statements do not explicitly formulate the candidate's arbitrary fixed nominations, independently uncertain constitutive laws, and target-arc envelope optimization problem.

David Eppstein, *Parallel Recognition of Series-Parallel Graphs*, Information and Computation (1992), [author preprint](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~eppstein/pubs/Epp-IC-92.pdf). Lemma 9, printed page 9, establishes that a biconnected series-parallel graph is two-terminal series-parallel when any existing edge's endpoints are selected as terminals. This directly supports the candidate's electrical adjoint sign lemma after restricting to the target edge's block. Eppstein's whole-graph convention is not automatically the same as an arbitrary graph whose blocks are series-parallel; the block restriction matters.

Our comparison: the candidate's graph-fixed adjoint signs, obtained by series/parallel current splitting, should be attributed to these sources. They are not a new graph characterization. Adjacency of the objective terminals explains why target arc flows admit the series-parallel class, while arbitrary pressure differences need the narrower cactus condition in the accompanying investigation.

Stefano Pastore, *DC tolerance analysis of electronic circuits by polyhedral circuits*, International Journal of Circuit Theory and Applications, DOI 10.1002/cta.2098, [open accepted manuscript](https://arts.units.it/retrieve/e2913fde-d2e2-f688-e053-3705fe0a67e0/2869823_10.1002-cta.2098-PostPrint.pdf). Sections 2–4, PDF pages 3–7, model uncertain nonlinear characteristics by strips between extremal curves and compute sets of circuit solutions through polyhedral regions, LP, and clustering. The model also permits controlled sources and nonmonotone devices. Section 6, PDF pages 15–16, explains why boundary circuits alone do not reconstruct its general equilibrium sets. No single series-parallel envelope identity or polynomial binary-precision guarantee appeared in the inspected text.

Our comparison: upper and lower constitutive envelopes and nonlinear tolerance strips are established modeling devices. Pastore's general counterexamples do not contradict the proposed theorem, whose passive strictly increasing laws and topology are substantive restrictions. The candidate's potentially distinct step is an exact choice of one complete asymmetric envelope network for one arc extremum, followed by a quantitative algorithm and endpoint recovery.

Randal E. Bryant, J. D. Tygar, and Lawrence P. Huang, *Geometric Characterization of Series-Parallel Variable Resistor Networks*, IEEE Transactions on Circuits and Systems I 41(11), 686–698 (1994), DOI 10.1109/81.331520, [open author preprint](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~tygar/papers/Geometric_characterization_of_series-parallel/Bryant_Journal_preprint.pdf). The introduction and constructions study independent variable linear elements and represent possible Thevenin/Norton equivalents by polygons with at most 2k vertices, computed in O(nk) time. Section 6.2 treats all nodes in grounded-tree circuits; PDF page 27 describes the straight-line locus associated with one varying linear element.

Our comparison: tractable series-parallel tolerance analysis is not new in general. This predecessor uses linear circuit geometry; it does not supply the inspected candidate's common quadratic-law envelope, energy error bound, or discrete rank-three arc-capacity reduction. Neither its linear result nor separate monotonicity alone should be advertised as a polynomial algorithm for arbitrary nonlinear uncertain networks.

## Important unresolved primary sources

Hasler and Wang, *Parameter tolerances in non-linear resistive circuits: worst case analysis based on monotonicity*, NOLTA 1993, Hawaii, pages 841–846. The bibliographic entry is confirmed by reference 2 of Pastore's primary manuscript, PDF page 20. Exact-title, author, proceedings, and institutional-repository searches did not locate an openly readable full copy. Pastore's introduction describes the earlier method as producing rigorous lower bounds for worst-case tolerances, but that secondary description within a primary paper does not establish the earlier theorem's precise scope. In particular, it cannot exclude an exact result for a special graph class. This is the most important outstanding source for the single-envelope identity and positive hull theorem.

Related missing sources are Hasler–Wang's *Monotonic dependence on sources in nonlinear resistive circuits* (1992), and Zemanian–Chan's *Does a series-parallel network of monotone resistors make sense? Yes or no?*, DOI 10.1109/81.558441, [IEEE record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/558441/). The IEEE full text was unavailable in this audit. Primary SUNY repository records also list A. H. Zemanian's 1997 reports [*Does a network of monotone resistors have an operating point?*](https://dspace.sunyconnect.suny.edu/items/66440270-9ec2-4808-a8fe-ad02d0e56871) and [*Does the operating point of a series-parallel network of monotone resistors satisfy all resistor ratings?*](https://dspace.sunyconnect.suny.edu/items/44bcf33f-6542-4f5e-a279-816fba2421e6). Repository listings were read; a linked PDF returned 403. No access restriction was bypassed. Titles alone do not establish overlap or non-overlap with uncertainty envelopes.

## Which computational claims remain distinct

The new approximation candidate uses one asymmetric cubic-energy minimization to determine a target-flow extremum. Its precise claim includes rational input, polynomial dependence on log(1/epsilon), no cycle-rank bound within the series-parallel class, a certified value interval, and an allowed endpoint resistance vector whose objective is within epsilon of optimal. The energy model and convex-optimization machinery are standard ingredients. The work needed to substantiate the combined claim is the exact envelope identity, energy-to-flow error control, and stability of endpoint selection when approximate flows can have uncertain signs. Merely exhibiting an SOCP formulation would not prove all these statements.

This result fixes nominations. The reviewed exact rank-two result allows a shifted nomination box because its hull reduction is combined with the separate fixed-block-rank exact algorithm. Those are different scopes: the envelope construction does not by itself prove polynomial high-precision optimization over arbitrary nomination uncertainty on series-parallel graphs of unbounded rank. Likewise, a high-precision additive algorithm does not decide an exact algebraic threshold without a separation guarantee.

The reviewed discrete arc reduction adds a weakly conducting probe edge to the pressure construction. It produces a simple graph of maximum degree three with a single rank-three block, common quadratic laws, fixed nominations, and independent two-point positive resistances. An explicit rational threshold and gap encode whether some choice violates the arc capacity. Existential violation is NP-complete and universal validation is coNP-complete; membership uses the reviewed exact fixed-rank algebraic algorithm, not an assumed rational physical-state certificate. Rank at most two per block gives exact polynomial validation by series-parallel hull equality and the fixed-rank interval algorithm.

No corresponding restricted boundary was found in the inspected circuit or robust-flow literature. General circuit-voltage uncertainty hardness and discrete pipe-sizing hardness are older results; the [discrete pressure source audit](potential-flow-discrete-resistance-hardness-novelty.md) gives their primary-source comparison. Cost-constrained sizing on trees does not imply hardness of one unconstrained worst-case physical arc flow with fixed nominations. The proposed boundary is specifically about the maximum cycle rank of a biconnected block, not global cycle count, and does not say that every rank-three graph is hard. Series-parallel blocks may have arbitrarily large cycle rank and still admit the proposed additive envelope algorithm.

The rank-three arc-flow reduction with fixed small nominations starts from Subset Sum and has a polynomially encodable gap. This supports an obstruction to polynomial dependence on precision bits; this arc-flow construction does not establish strong hardness, a constant-error barrier, or exclusion of running time polynomial in 1/epsilon. Common resistance scaling leaves arc flows unchanged, so it cannot amplify this gap. This differs from pressure and weighted-potential objectives, for which common resistance scaling does give fixed absolute-error hardness on unnormalized data; see the later corrections in the [pressure audit](potential-flow-discrete-resistance-hardness-novelty.md) and [weighted-potential audit](potential-flow-weighted-potential-cycle-novelty.md). The probe transfer is a network-specific construction built on a familiar encoding mechanism.

## Recommended attribution and next source check

Use qualified wording: “Building on classical confluence and adjacent-terminal series-parallel structure, we obtain a single-envelope reduction for fixed-nomination quadratic passive networks and derive a polynomial bit-time additive algorithm with endpoint scenario recovery. Separately, exact finite-resistance arc validation is polynomial at block cycle rank two and coNP-complete at rank three. No matching computational results were located in the open primary sources inspected.” Keep the Hasler–Wang retrieval gap explicit whenever claiming priority for the positive nonlinear tolerance theorem.

The highest-value next literature action is obtaining the actual 1993 Hasler–Wang paper and checking whether its monotonicity assumptions imply this complete envelope construction. If it does, attribute that structural step and assess the computational guarantees and sharp rank boundary separately. The present bounded search does not justify calling the circuit-level envelope idea unprecedented.
