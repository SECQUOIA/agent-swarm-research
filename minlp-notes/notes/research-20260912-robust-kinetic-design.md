# Robust kinetic sampling across three parameter scenarios

Date: 2026-09-12. The [separate robust-design prototype](../code/research_20260912/robust_kinetic_design.py)
improves the worst scenario performance of a central nominal design in two
small chemical examples. Including uncertainty in each scenario's optimal
reference, the numerical worst D-efficiency lower bound improves from 93.69%
to 96.94% at 48 candidate times and from 96.12% to 96.95% at 96 times.
Greedy/exchange supplies the better discrete robust schedule in the first
case; paths generated by the shared relaxation supply it in the second.
The relaxation also bounds possible further improvement.

These results support using a robust sampling objective when the specified
parameter scenarios lead to different preferred times. They do not establish
an advantage over all robust-design algorithms, a calibrated chemical
uncertainty set, or coverage of parameters between the three scenarios.
The tables below retain the original floating-point bounds. The
[separate exact certificates](research-20260912-robust-design-certificates.md)
and [implementation review](research-20260912-robust-solver-independent-review.md)
are complete. The later [dense comparison and completed exchange polishing](research-20260912-robust-dense-comparison.md)
also passed independent review and include exact recertification of the improved
96-candidate schedule.
No accepted solver core was edited.

## Chemical scenario set and common covariance

The observed mean is the intermediate species in consecutive first-order
kinetics:

```text
B(t) = A0 k1/(k2-k1) [exp(-k1 t)-exp(-k2 t)].
```

The local mean-parameter coordinates are `log(A0),log(k1),log(k2)`. The
three stipulated nominal scenarios are:

| Scenario | A0 | k1 | k2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Early peak | 1 | 0.7 | 0.2 |
| Central nominal | 1 | 0.4 | 0.1 |
| Late peak | 1 | 0.18 | 0.045 |

Analytic sensitivities are checked by independent complex-step evaluations of
the mean formula. The early and late values match the
[previously reviewed kinetic probe](research-20260912-noisy-markov-kinetics-probe.md);
the central scenario uses the same equations. These rate values span distinct
response regimes; they are not confidence limits or a posterior fitted to a
particular experiment. The uncertainty set is finite and specified before
optimization.

Every scenario uses the same known, mean-parameter-independent covariance

```text
R_ij = 0.00125 (0.4^|i-j| + 1[i=j]),
J0 = 0.01 I_3.
```

This is stipulated stationary latent AR(1) plus independent observation
noise, with total pointwise noise standard deviation `0.05`. It is not an
estimated chemical residual model. There are `n=48` or `96` candidate times
`t_j=12(j+1)/n`, over a horizon of 12 model time units, and exactly `k=n/3`
samples. Holding per-grid correlation `0.4` fixed while changing `n` changes
the physical correlation scale. All three scenarios at a given `n` share one
schedule and one covariance.

The local-design scope matters. Observing `B(t)` with unknown `A0` admits a
global rate-swap ambiguity after adjusting `A0`. The Fisher criterion here
assesses local precision at stipulated nominal parameters and does not resolve
that ambiguity. Saved decimal sensitivity matrices specify the numerical
instances; no exact-real sensitivity or measured-data claim is made.

## Fixed offsets and genuine scenario standardization

The solver accepts arbitrary finite offsets `c_s` and maximizes

```text
f_c(S) = min_s [logdet J_s(S)-c_s],
J_s(S) = J0_s + F_(s,S)^T R_SS^(-1) F_(s,S).
```

Using raw logdet would compare very different intrinsic information scales:
at `n=48`, the three individual optimum reference scores are approximately
`14.95,14.29,9.96`. The practical probe therefore constructs an individual
reference for each scenario with the accepted single-scenario finite-memory
solver at `L=8`. It retains the full, unmodified reference hull result.
Its original-covariance feasible logdet is `L_s`, and its numerical true
upper bound is `U_s`. The robust solver uses the fixed offsets `c_s=L_s`.

This fixed-offset optimization must be distinguished from normalization by
the unknown individual true optima `phi_s=max_S logdet J_s(S)`. Since
`L_s<=phi_s<=U_s`, a selected schedule has the standardized score enclosure

```text
min_s [logdet J_s(S)-U_s]
 <= min_s [logdet J_s(S)-phi_s]
 <= min_s [logdet J_s(S)-L_s].
```

An upper bound for the fixed-offset robust optimum is also an upper bound
for the true standardized optimum. The latter is at most zero. The tables
below use the conservative lower expression for feasible standardized
performance. Its exponential divided by `p=3` gives worst-scenario
D-efficiency relative to each scenario's own unknown optimal design.
Reference uncertainty is therefore included explicitly rather than treating
a feasible reference as a proven individual optimum.

All six individual reference relaxations close to their requested hull
tolerance. Their remaining numerical true logdet gaps are 0.00347–0.00372.
These are not claims of exact individual optimality. The full reference
records and their matrices are saved so the separate exact certifier can
recompute the required reference enclosures.

## Shared path mixture and numerical upper bounds

Every feasible path is one size-`k` schedule. For a path `a` and scenario `s`,
let `J_(s,a)^L` be its finite-history information. A common mixture `alpha`
gives `M_s=sum_a alpha_a J_(s,a)^L` for every scenario. A fully corrective
SLSQP problem maximizes `t` subject to the simplex constraint on `alpha` and
`t<=logdet M_s-c_s` for all scenarios. Thus scenarios share the same mixture;
they cannot separately choose favorable schedules.

The unchanged `CalendarOracle` is constructed from horizontally concatenated
scenario sensitivity arrays. Only the resulting diagonal scenario information
blocks and block-diagonal linear gradients are used. Cross-scenario blocks
are a computational byproduct and are never interpreted as a correlated
joint statistical model.

At the current scenario matrices, nonnegative weights `lambda_s` summing to
one combine the scenario logdet tangents. SLSQP's scenario-constraint
multipliers are clipped and normalized; an active-scenario fallback is
available. Any resulting probability vector gives a valid tangent bound,
irrespective of multiplier optimality. One shared dynamic-programming price
maximizes the combined linear increment, giving

```text
upper_L = sum_s lambda_s [logdet M_s-c_s-p
                         +tr(M_s^(-1) J0_s)]
          +max_path sum_s lambda_s tr(M_s^(-1) A_s(path)),
```

where `A_s(path)` is the sum of local scenario information increments.
Generated paths are evaluated under every original covariance, and feasible
mixture weights are retained even if correction stops early. The best
completed upper witness remains available after a cap.

The reviewed scalar memory correction transfers the surrogate upper bound to
the true fixed-offset objective. A final price also retains the unscaled prior:

```text
N_s = J0_s+(M_s-J0_s)/(1-delta_s),
H_s = lambda_s N_s^(-1)/(1-delta_s).
```

Applying the same weighted tangent construction at `N_s` with gradients
`H_s` gives a true upper bound. The smaller available bound is saved. Each
witness includes the scenario matrices, dual weights, gradient blocks,
constants, priced path, and linear price. All comparisons and matrix
operations are numerical, without directed rounding.

Closing this common-mixture relaxation does not close the discrete robust
problem. Both examples have a visible remaining gap between the hull bound
and the best generated or greedy schedule.

## Comparison with nominal and completed greedy designs

The nominal comparator is the central scenario's individual reference
schedule. Robust greedy selection starts from the empty schedule, repeatedly
adds the time with the highest robust fixed-offset score, and then repeatedly
takes the best improving single exchange. It completes the full final
exchange neighborhood in both cases; it receives no robust-hull path as an
initial schedule. Both robust methods use the same offsets, prior, count,
scenario set, and original-covariance objective.

| n | Method | Fixed-offset worst logdet difference | Numerical worst D-efficiency lower bound |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 48 | Central nominal reference | -0.1918353 | 93.6938% |
| 48 | Shared-hull generated schedule | -0.0942782 | 96.7943% |
| 48 | Completed robust greedy/exchange | -0.0897938 | **96.9355%** |
| 96 | Central nominal reference | -0.1152569 | 96.1153% |
| 96 | Shared-hull generated schedule | -0.0892262 | **96.9529%** |
| 96 | Completed robust greedy/exchange | -0.1039055 | 96.4797% |

The per-scenario sacrifice relative to the corresponding feasible individual
reference is explicit. For the best `n=48` schedule, logdet losses are
`0.08979,0.02760,0.08926` in early/central/late order. For the best `n=96`
schedule, they are `0.08923,0.01790,0.06711`. The central nominal designs
instead lose `0.19184` and `0.11526` in the early scenario. Robust sampling
accepts a small central-scenario loss to reduce that imbalance. Full raw
scenario logdet values and all schedules are in the artifacts.

Combining the better feasible schedule with the shared upper bound gives:

| n | Standardized robust score enclosure | Optimal worst D-efficiency enclosure |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 48 | [-0.0933735, -0.0815247] | [96.9355%, 97.3191%] |
| 96 | [-0.0928345, -0.0728581] | [96.9529%, 97.6007%] |

These are numerical enclosures, not exact certificates. The `n=48` lower
bound uses the completed greedy schedule with the shared-hull upper witness;
the saved method records remain separate. The nonzero gaps prevent a claim
that either feasible schedule is globally robust-optimal.

The final scenario dual weights are approximately `(0.3786,0,0.6214)` and
`(0.4338,0,0.5662)`. The early and late scenarios constrain the relaxed
worst-case score; the central scenario has slack. This is a property of these
instances, not a reason to remove scenarios before solving.

## Timing, validation, and reproducibility

| n | Individual references | Shared solver alone | Greedy/exchange alone | References + shared solver | References + greedy |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 48 | 2.076 s | 1.072 s | 0.500 s | 3.149 s | 2.576 s |
| 96 | 9.980 s | 3.655 s | 5.807 s | 13.635 s | 15.787 s |

The standalone solver columns exclude reference and incumbent generation.
The pipeline columns include all three reference runs, which generate both
the normalization constants and the reference schedules supplied to the shared
solver. Greedy needs the same constants. Its own discrete search starts
independently. A user needing only the nominal schedule would require only
the central reference run, not all three. Individual timings are local
measurements, not controlled asymptotic comparisons.

The shared solvers use 11 and 9 main prices and close their surrogate hull
gaps to `2.16e-9` and `3.36e-12`. Their estimated array workspaces are 9.92
and 21.27 MiB. Greedy performs 4,233 and 39,441 robust objective evaluations,
with 6 and 17 accepted exchanges. Each robust objective evaluation includes
all three scenarios. All runs finish without a time or iteration cap.
The final `n=48` mixture correction reported a positive-directional-derivative
line-search failure. Its retained feasible mixture and independently evaluated
simplex tangent still closed the reported hull gap; optimizer success was
not required for that bound.

Each individual reference has a five-second cap; shared and greedy solvers
have separate 30-second caps. Memory is limited by a conservative 256-MiB
array estimate, not process RSS. Deadline checks occur between numerical
operations. The driver records exceptions and atomically checkpoints after
each completed stage. A failed required reference prevents dependent
normalization and is recorded as a reference failure.

The tiny validation enumerates all 120 size-three schedules at `n=10`, with
three seeded rational-decimal sensitivity scenarios, two mean parameters,
and windows `L=0,2,6,9`. Separate per-scenario oracles agree with the diagonal
blocks of the concatenated pricing container within `5.56e-17`. Weighted
prices agree with enumeration, every tested tangent contains the surrogate
discrete optimum, and true bounds contain the original discrete optimum.
At full history, a separate optimization over all 120 schedules agrees with
the generated-path hull.

An analytic two-scenario scalar example has path information pairs `(2,1)`
and `(1,2)`; the exact optimal mixture and scenario weights are both
`(1/2,1/2)`, with value `log(1.5)`. The implementation reproduces this result.
Finite-difference mixture derivatives agree within `3.00e-10`. Immediate
deadline and memory refusals do not fabricate feasible or pricing bounds.
Fresh review found that summing extreme finite dual entries could overflow
before normalization. The normalizer now scales by the largest positive
entry first. A regression with dual entries `1e308` reproduces the correct
`log(3)` upper bound for two identical scalar scenarios. Tiny validation was
rerun after the fix; the chemical artifacts retain their original generating
hashes and ordinary-magnitude witnesses and were not rerun.
Review also found a generic greedy status issue: a retained seed could be
better than the completed greedy branch without having undergone exchanges
itself. Exchanges now start from the better retained incumbent, and a
local-optimum status returns the path whose neighborhood was completed.
The reviewer's seven-time regression now returns `(0,2,3)` and passes all
of its exchange-neighbor checks. The saved chemical results already returned
locally optimal exchange paths and remain unchanged.
