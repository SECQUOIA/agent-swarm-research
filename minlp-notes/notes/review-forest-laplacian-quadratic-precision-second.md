## Incidence quotient and nonlinear rank

For an oriented forest incidence matrix, the change
`u=(Bx+1)/2` maps the cube onto the stated compact convex zonotope inside
`[0,1]^r`. Each nonlinear output becomes

```
g_j(u)=(1/2)sum_e a_je(2u_e-1)^2,
```

whose Hessian is exactly `diag(4a_je)`. The factor four in `C` is correct.
The affine terms generated by expanding this expression cause no change
in the Jensen or square-prefix arguments.

Both formulation minima are preserved. In the forward map, imposing the
original cube first prevents projections of irrelevant outside inputs;
subtracting the original affine output term gives exactly `g(u)` at graph
points and preserves every error vector. Each point of the image domain
has a cube preimage, so no exact graph point is lost. The reverse map keeps
the original cube variables and the rational incidence equation and restores
the affine output. It adds no integer. This reasoning is valid for arbitrary
convex lifts and for binary linear lifts, including when the affine output
varies along a fiber of `B`.

After inactive edges are removed, the sum of all output Hessians is
`B^T diag(s_e) B` with `s_e>0` on every retained edge. Its kernel is
exactly `ker B`, the vectors constant on each connected component.
Positive semidefiniteness implies this is also the intersection of the
individual Hessian kernels. Forest incidence rows are independent, hence
the common nonlinear input rank is precisely `r`, including when the
active forest has isolated vertices or multiple components. The `r=0`
case is correctly separated as an exact affine graph.

## The face tiling proves the exact volume

For a tree on `s` vertices, all cube points in one incidence fiber differ
by a constant vector. Subtracting their minimum produces the same cube
point with minimum zero. This gives a unique such representative for each
image point. Consequently the image is the union of the images of the
`s` faces `x_v=0`.

Deleting column `v` from the tree incidence matrix yields a square matrix
with determinant of absolute value one. An induction may always delete a
leaf different from `v`, expand along its column, and continue; the empty
matrix at the end has determinant one. Thus every face image is a
full-dimensional parallelotope of volume one in the standard edge
coordinates.

If two distinct face images intersect, their preimages with minimum zero
coincide by uniqueness. This common preimage has both distinguished
coordinates zero, so the intersection is the image of a face of dimension
at most `s-2`. It has zero `(s-1)`-dimensional volume. The union therefore
has volume exactly `s`; overlap of positive measure is excluded.

The forest images form a Cartesian product because their component vertex
sets are disjoint. Isolated vertices contribute dimension zero and factor
one. Scaling all edge coordinates by one half proves

```
vol_r(Omega)=2^(-r)product_c n_c=V_F>=2^(-r).
```

This is volume in the standard independent edge coordinates, which are
the coordinates used by the subsequent covariance argument. There is no
missing Euclidean subspace-volume factor or unproved measure-preservation
claim.

## Lower bound on the actual correlated domain

For two exact graph points in one parity support, their integer-lift
midpoint is feasible. The input midpoint stays in `Omega` by convexity.
The admitted error is

```
J_j(u,v)=(1/8)sum_e C_je(u_e-v_e)^2>=0,
J(u,v) in K.
```

Compact closures preserve this condition. For independent uniform points
in a positive-volume support, the expectation belongs to the closed convex
body `K` and equals `(1/4)C diag(Sigma)`. Hence `p_e=Sigma_ee/4` gives
`Cp in K`, with `0<=p_e<=1/16` because the domain lies in the unit cube.

Hadamard's inequality supplies `det Sigma<=4^r product_e p_e<=4^r D`.
Together with the reviewed volume-covariance inequality this bounds each
support's volume by `2^(A_r) sqrt(D)`. The compact parity supports cover
the actual domain volume `V_F`, not the containing cube's volume. Summing
over their at most `2^p` parity classes gives the displayed lower bound
`p_conv>=Phi-A_r+log2 V_F`. This argument does not require `Omega` to be
a product, or the original lift to be closed or measurable.

## Upper construction, constants, and bit complexity

On the containing cube, the shared square-prefix construction with
`L_e=ceil[(1/2)log2(1/p_e)]` has total output error bounded componentwise
by `Cp/8`. Since `Cp in K` and `K` is unconditional and convex, every
admitted error belongs to `K`. The exact graph remains contained. An empty
prefix when `p_e=1` is allowed by the residual-square model. Restricting
this construction to `Omega` through the original continuous cube variables
adds no integers and gives a valid original formulation.

An optimal positive allocation yields `sum_e L_e<=Phi+r`, proving the
finite upper bound. The reviewed rational log-product oracle applies with
the rational matrix `C` and the stated strong separation and inner-radius
assumptions. It gives an exactly feasible positive rational allocation
whose product is at least `exp(-1)D`, adding at most `1/(2 ln 2)` to that
count. Its explicit positive coordinate lower bound has polynomial bit
length. All prefix depths can be chosen by exact rational comparisons;
the depths, rational coefficients, and generated model size are polynomial
in the full input and oracle encoding. The incidence equations require
only the rational coefficients `0`, `1`, `-1`, and `1/2`.

Combining the lower and upper bounds gives

```
p_out<=p_conv+A_r+r-log2 V_F+1/(2 ln 2).
```

The previously reviewed bound `A_r<4r`, the exact-volume consequence
`-log2 V_F<=r`, and `1/(2 ln 2)<1` yield `p_out<=p_conv+6r+1`.
The finite bounds themselves need neither rationality nor a body oracle;
these enter only the polynomial rational construction. Full input sizes
and body-radius encodings remain in the running time, as stated. No
polynomial MILP solution algorithm is inferred.

## Verification and scope

Jensen, and canonical-representative checks passed. The cases include

graph. No unresolved mathematical or encoding defect was found.
