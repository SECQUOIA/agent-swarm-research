# Reopened potential-flow literature audit

Date: 2026-09-06. This is an independent, bounded primary-source comparison of the [series-parallel envelope theorem](../results/potential-flow-series-parallel-envelope-optimization.md), its [finite secant proof](../results/potential-flow-secant-envelope-comparison.md), and the [rational](../results/potential-flow-envelope-rational-certificates.md) and [Bregman](../results/potential-flow-envelope-bregman-certificates.md) certificates. It is not a proof review or exhaustive priority clearance.

The practical computational package remains a defensible development target. Its component ideas need explicit attribution: topology-based nonlinear monotonicity, endpoint sensitivity analysis, convex primal-dual error bounds, and hydraulic uncertainty enclosures all have substantial predecessors. The exact complete envelope identity still has an unresolved priority gap.

## Circuit overlap: what should be attributed

Chauffoureaux–Hasler explicitly motivate worst-case parameter sensitivity by replacing a parameter sweep with its endpoints when monotonicity is known. Their theorem differentiates a source-to-resistor transfer and tests a linear circuit structure for universal well-posedness; their conclusion sketches extension to a monotonic parameter in a resistor law. These are direct predecessors of the general strategy, not just distant circuit background. The inspected theorem does not construct the repo's whole asymmetric law profile for independent uncertainty with arbitrary fixed balanced nominations. [[hasler1990-monotonicity-in-nonlinear-resistive-circuits]] p.1 [[hasler1990-monotonicity-in-nonlinear-resistive-circuits]] p.3-4

Hasler's earlier nonlinear-to-affine transfer of structural solvability further establishes that replacing nonlinear networks by suitably chosen linear networks is classical. The finite secant identity should be presented as a useful elementary proof device, without a standalone priority claim. [[hasler1986-non-linear-non-reciprocal-resistive]] p.6-8

The [open Wang–Hasler report](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/d2fb4e43-bb7e-42b7-977c-39560d9e7d46/content) derives a second-derivative circuit with positive resistances and signed independent sources, then uses structural sign tests to obtain convexity/concavity of transfer functions. Its ladder conditions are explicit. This is a material predecessor for any future claim about convexity of nomination-to-flow value functions. Its inspected statements concern source variation and do not give the complete uncertain-law envelope. [[hasler1997-convexity-of-resistive-circuit-characteristics]] p.4-6 [[hasler1997-convexity-of-resistive-circuit-characteristics]] p.12-13 [[hasler1997-convexity-of-resistive-circuit-characteristics]] p.17-18

Zemanian's rated-segment report propagates monotone characteristic segments through series/parallel reductions and recovers a designated branch by reversing reductions. This establishes an important practical predecessor for branch recovery and operation within ratings. It is a fixed-law rated-segment feasibility problem, whereas the repo's attainment argument selects one uncertain member law independently at each edge after solving the envelope. The report does not establish that uncertain coefficient boxes and their finite endpoint subsets have identical target-flow extrema. [[zemanian1999-does-the-operating-point-of]] p.4-8 [[zemanian1999-does-the-operating-point-of]] p.11-13

The precise distinction to retain is the complete profile, rather than the word “endpoint”: quadratic coefficient laws reverse their pointwise order at zero. The repo solves one asymmetric network first, then chooses allowable coefficients from its flow signs. Ordinary endpoint enumeration and a statement of separate monotonicity do not by themselves supply that reduction or its precision guarantee. Whether the unread 1993 paper already supplies it remains unresolved.

## Certification overlap and practical counterparts

The [Bartels–Milicevic paper](https://numdam.org/item/10.1051/m2an/2019074.pdf) gives the abstract primal-dual gap error estimate in Proposition 3.1 and specializes it to power-growth nonlinear Laplace energies in Section 4. It includes nonnegative local gap indicators and attributes earlier duality-based error estimation to Repin. Thus the repo's energy-gap-to-error logic belongs to established convex error analysis. A useful narrower contribution would be its explicit rational realization for asymmetric quadratic network laws, including the original uncertain-instance mapping and independently checked endpoint scenario. [[milicevic2020-primal-dual-gap-estimators-for]] p.7 [[milicevic2020-primal-dual-gap-estimators-for]] p.9-12

The [Vrachimis–Eliades–Polycarpou case study](https://dwes.copernicus.org/articles/11/19/2018/dwes-11-19-2018.pdf) uses interval pipe coefficients and measurement bounds, then iterative linear enclosures and LPs to bound hydraulic states. Its modified Cyprus network has 12 pipes and three loops; the published model uses the Hazen–Williams exponent 1.852. The [public dataset](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.1185136) supplies an EPANET file with realistic demands. This is a useful application-motivated benchmark lead, not evidence that all original sensor traces are public. Using quadratic laws would be an explicit model adaptation. [[polycarpou2018-real-time-hydraulic-interval-state]] p.2-3 [[polycarpou2018-real-time-hydraulic-interval-state]] p.5

The [open Rego et al. manuscript](https://arts.units.it/bitstream/11368/3073208/3/State_Estimation_and_Leakage_Detection_in_Water_Distribution_Networks_Using_Constrained_Zonotopes-Post_print.pdf) develops faster interval enclosures and constrained-zonotope enclosures for hydraulic state estimation and leakage detection. Its Section III explicitly produces outer approximations; Remark 2 does not promise an exact interval hull for its rescaling method. This is relevant practical competition on general networks. It does not provide the repo's exact structural identity or standalone rational verifier. A comparison should distinguish physical uncertainty width, conservative set-enclosure error, and numerical certification error. [[raimondo2022-state-estimation-and-leakage-detection]] p.1 [[raimondo2022-state-estimation-and-leakage-detection]] p.5-7

## Consequences for the paper

1. Attribute graph signs to Duffin/Eppstein, source and parameter monotonicity to the circuit literature, and posterior convex error analysis to its established literature. Do not frame any of these broad ideas as new.
2. State the candidate contribution at its full precision: one target, independent allowed coefficient choices, arbitrary fixed balanced nominations, series-parallel graph with unbounded cycle rank, exact envelope value identity, polynomial dependence on accuracy bits, and a checkable allowed scenario.
3. Complete certification of the original input-to-envelope mapping. Existing deterministic certificates alone do not justify an uncertainty-extremum claim.
4. Benchmark against meaningful enclosure methods and report exactly what is certified. Point-solver residuals, actual scenario performance, physical uncertainty, and certificate width answer different questions.
5. Keep the 1993 and thesis access gaps visible. No matching combined computational theorem was located in this bounded search, but that is not proof of novelty or absence of prior work.

The search used exact titles and author combinations, the public EPFL DSpace title index, nonlinear circuit tolerance/monotonicity, hydraulic interval state estimation, and primal-dual error certification for power-growth energies. Primary text, rather than search snippets or a source's title, supports the comparisons above.
