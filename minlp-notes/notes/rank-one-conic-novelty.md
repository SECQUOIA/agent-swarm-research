# Equivalent-object novelty screening for the rank-one conic results

Date: 2026-09-04. Scope: the three results on the unit-capacity rank-one hull, its correlation-polytope face, and exact/approximate LP/SOCP/SDP lift sizes.

## Assessment

No matching theorem for the **continuous rank-one doubly substochastic hull with all lower bounds zero and all row/column upper bounds one** was found in the primary open sources inspected here. The broader mechanisms are established: correlation polytopes encode binary quadratic optimization; complemented variables appear in completely positive lifts; norm-bounded rank-one convex hulls are standard atomic objects; and cone lift lower bounds pass through sections and projections.

The proposed new contribution should therefore be stated narrowly: the particular trace-exposed Boolean face inside this simple continuous flow hull, its explicit exposing functional and quantitative stability, and the resulting exact and positive-error conic size barriers. Neither the use of correlation polytopes nor the general idea of atomic convexification should be presented as new.

The approximate SDP result was strengthened during this screen: the final theorem uses sign correlation coordinates and proves a stretched-exponential PSD-order lower bound already for error `epsilon<=1/[2m(184m+6)]`. This stronger version passed independent review. It supersedes the earlier `o(m^-2)` and smaller-error formulations as the main claim.

## Exact equivalent formulations of the local object

For nonnegative matrices, define

```
N(W)=max{ max_i sum_j W_ij, max_j sum_i W_ij }.
```

The local object is precisely

```
K_n=conv{W>=0:rank(W)<=1,N(W)<=1}.
```

Equivalently, `N` is the maximum of the induced matrix 1-norm and infinity-norm. For `W=uv^T` with nonnegative factors, its atomic normalization is

```
N(uv^T)=max{||u||_infinity ||v||_1,
            ||u||_1 ||v||_infinity}.
```

This is useful terminology for searching atomic norm and tensor literature. It does not make `K_n` a standard one-sided projective tensor ball: its normalization couples two distinct factor-norm products through their maximum.

The trace face identified locally is

```
conv{aa^T/|A|:empty!=A subset[n],a=1_A}
 = cone{aa^T:a in {0,1}^n} intersect {trace(W)=1}.
```

The equality with the correlation-cone section follows directly by rescaling nonnegative generator weights by `|A|`. This section is an established type of correlation object. The nontrivial embedding step is showing it is the trace face of `K_n`, whose generators initially need not be symmetric or binary.

## Source comparisons

### 1. Atomic norm framework: the ambient construction is known

Fawzi and Parrilo's *Self-scaled bounds for atomic cone ranks: applications to nonnegative rank and cp-rank* defines, for a monotone positively homogeneous function `N`, the atoms `A_N={X>=0:rank(X)<=1,N(X)<=1}` and the gauge of `conv(A_N)`. It compares the associated lower bound on nonnegative rank with their self-scaled rank bound. The definitions are on PDF p.16, equations (31)–(32), and the comparison begins on p.17. [Open primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.3240).

Our `N=max(row-sum maximum,column-sum maximum)` satisfies these hypotheses, so the construction of `K_n` itself fits their general framework exactly. The inspected section does not single out this `N`, identify its correlation face, or establish the conic extension-size results proved locally. Its optimization target is atomic decomposition rank and its lower bounds, rather than the extension complexity of this fixed atomic hull. This is adjacent prior art that belongs in a paper's framing.

### 2. Correlation cone and the misleading word “normalized”

Caprara, Furini, Gentile, Liberti, and Lodi's March 19, 2026 manuscript *Hardness of some optimization problems over correlation polyhedra* studies membership and decomposition-rank questions for the cone generated by Boolean symmetric rank-one matrices and its convex-hull variants. Their “normalized correlation polytope,” defined on PDF p.3, removes the zero vertex; it does **not** divide every atom by its trace. Section 4.1, PDF p.7, uses a bordered matrix to embed correlation-polytope membership into correlation-cone membership. [Open primary manuscript](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/complexity-v2.pdf).

Thus the search hit on “normalized correlation polytope” does not identify our trace-normalized face. The unnormalized correlation cone is the same cone underlying that face, but the paper does not establish its realization within the continuous doubly substochastic rank-one hull, its local stability, or the relevant conic lift barriers. The difference is mathematical, not merely terminology.

### 3. Complemented-variable CP lifts: the binary embedding device is known

Fiorini, Massar, Patra, and Tiwary's *Generalised probabilistic theories and conic extensions of polytopes* uses Burer's completely positive reformulation to give a completely positive extension of `COR(n)` in order `2n+1`. Its construction on PDF pp.12–13 uses complemented variables and moment equations; on p.16 it explicitly uses the atom `(1,a,1-a)(1,a,1-a)^T`. The paper also proves a broad reduction from efficiently recognizable binary polytopes to correlation polytopes. [Open primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.4125).

Consequently the local block inverse involving `(x,1-x)(x,1-x)^T` is an established binary-lifting device. The local restriction is different: it obtains that structure from three affine conditions on `K_(2m)`, using row/column unit capacities and trace equality rather than introducing a CP cone and binary moment identities. A compact lift over the completely positive cone does not contradict large SDP or SOCP lifts; the cone families differ.

### 4. Bipartite Boolean quadric and multiple-choice polytopes

Sripratak, Punnen, and Stephen's *The Bipartite Boolean Quadric Polytope* studies the linearization of products of two binary blocks, with facet classes and relaxations. [Open author manuscript](https://www.sfu.ca/~tstephen/Papers/bbqp.pdf).

Bärmann, Martin, and Schneider's *The Bipartite Boolean Quadric Polytope with Multiple-Choice Constraints* adds an at-most-one partition structure on one binary block. Its introduction and PDF p.2 explicitly distinguish that polytope from a face of the unrestricted bipartite BQP; Appendix B discusses the equality variant as a face of the multiple-choice polytope. Its application includes pooling with fixed input proportions. [Open primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.11674).

These objects explain why paired selection and bilinear products are familiar in pooling convexification. They start with discrete factors and finite polytopes. Our ambient `K_n` permits arbitrary nonnegative rank-one factors and is nonpolyhedral already for `n=2`. Neither the binary-polytope identity nor its facet theory automatically yields the trace face or a quantitative error transfer from that nonpolyhedral hull.

### 5. Biclique polyhedral literature

Bondarenko, Nikolaev, and Shovgenov's *Polyhedral characteristics of balanced and unbalanced bipartite subgraph problems* defines balanced biclique incidence-vector polytopes and analyzes their adjacency and cone decompositions. PDF pp.1–3 state the combinatorial models and the restricted linear-comparison complexity interpretation. [Open primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.00359).

The balanced support matrices occurring in our final face are related to biclique incidence matrices, after normalization. The inspected biclique work concerns finite incidence sets and bounds for a particular comparison model. It does not supply the continuous rank-one embedding, an arbitrary conic-lift lower bound, or the positive-error SDP result.

## Searches performed and access limits

The searches included these equivalent terms and combinations:

- rank-one substochastic / doubly substochastic convex hull;
- normalized correlation polytope / correlation cone / trace-normalized Boolean matrices;
- nonnegative projective tensor ball / nonnegative nuclear norm / row-sum nuclear norm;
- biclique polytope / bipartite Boolean quadric polytope / multiple-choice face;
- correlation polytope / completely positive extension / complemented variables;
- rank-one matrices / extension complexity / pooling / approximate semidefinite lifts.

Primary PDFs were opened for the atomic framework, correlation-cone manuscript, CP lift construction, both bipartite BQP sources, and the biclique paper. Relevant definitions and constructions were read, not merely inferred from titles. An MIT repository PDF returned a 403 and a ULB repository PDF was not retrievable through the browser; their open arXiv versions were used instead. No paywall or access restriction was bypassed.

The current screen is bounded, not exhaustive. In particular, it does not establish that every older paper on matrix gauges, nonnegative factorization, or correlation-cone sections lacks the embedding. The strongest remaining check is citation tracing around the specific atomic normalization `max(||u||_infinity||v||_1,||u||_1||v||_infinity)` and any papers on convex hulls of rank-one contractions for mixed operator norms.

## Recommended claim language

“The unit-capacity two-sided rank-one flow hull has an exposed face affinely isomorphic to a correlation polytope. Quantitative stability of this face transfers established polyhedral and semidefinite extension-complexity lower bounds, including a positive-error SDP barrier. We did not find this face or its quantitative transfer in the primary open literature reviewed.”

Avoid claims that correlation-polytope lower bounds, complemented-variable encodings, nonnegative atomic hulls, or robust pseudo-density methods are themselves new. The mathematical proofs and independent audits remain intact; this screen narrows the novelty claim rather than weakening their conclusions.
