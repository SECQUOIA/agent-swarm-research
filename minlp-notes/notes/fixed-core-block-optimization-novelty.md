# Novelty audit: fixed nonlinear core and polyhedral blocks

Date: 2026-09-05. Scope: independent literature audit of [the fixed-core theorem and pooling corollaries](../results/fixed-core-block-polyhedral-optimization.md). Mathematical correctness is assessed in the separate independent reviews.

**Assessment:** no matching general theorem or matching multiple-pool tractability results were found in the inspected sources. The strongest application claims are exact polynomial bit complexity for fixed inputs plus fixed pools, with arbitrary outputs and qualities and bypasses; and fixed pools plus fixed qualities, with arbitrary inputs and outputs without bypasses. Both extend explicitly stated older open questions. The general proof combines established geometric and real-algebraic methods; it should be presented as a parameter-dependent extension and synthesis, rather than a new support-function principle or a new quantifier-elimination algorithm. Novelty remains provisional after this bounded search.

## The 2014/2016 source discrepancy is now clarified

The final Haugland article is now available as a **user-supplied full text** in [the repository](../literature/papers/haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the/fulltext.md). Its exact title, author, DOI, journal pages, and theorem statements were checked. This supersedes the earlier access limitation recorded in [the source-discrepancy note](pooling-fixed-pools-qualities-source-audit.md).

Haugland, *The computational complexity of the pooling problem*, Journal of Global Optimization 64 (2016), 199–215, DOI 10.1007/s10898-015-0335-y, does **not** prove fixed-two-pool/one-quality NP-hardness. Theorem 3 concerns one quality and two sources and two terminals, with a growing number of pools. Theorems 4–5 concern one quality and, respectively, two sources or two terminals. Section 6, printed page **214**, explicitly asks about a bounded number of pools together with a bound on inputs, outputs, or qualities. Section 2.1, printed page **201**, defines arcs only from sources to pools and pools to terminals. Thus its precise open model has no bypass arcs. [User-supplied primary text](../literature/papers/haugland2016-the-computational-complexity-of-the/fulltext.md), [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10898-015-0335-y).

The MAGO 2014 abstract really did state two-pool/one-quality hardness, but supplied no proof and deferred proofs to a full version. The later full article instead states the relevant parameter combination as open. The proper conclusion is that the abstract's claim is **not a verified obstruction in the final article**. There is no need to assume a formal correction or withdrawal that has not been located. Cite the final proved theorems and final open question; acknowledge the abstract discrepancy if needed in a detailed literature discussion. [MAGO proceedings, printed page 31](https://www.hpca.ual.es/~MAGO14/MAGO14-Proceedings.pdf).

## Pooling predecessors and the exact extension

Boland, Kalinowski, and Rigterink, *A polynomially solvable case of the pooling problem*, JGO 67 (2017), 621–630, prove in Theorem 1 that one pool and a fixed number of inputs suffice, allowing unbounded outputs and qualities. Their argument partitions the input-fraction simplex by output-quality comparisons and solves polynomially many LPs. Section 4, Open Problem 4, asks about two pools combined with bounds on inputs, outputs, and qualities. The fixed-input/fixed-pool corollary answers the input-bounded branch for every fixed pool count; the no-bypass fixed-quality corollary answers the quality-bounded branch. These corollaries do not settle the remaining case where only pools and outputs are fixed. [Primary preprint](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/5059.pdf), [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.03181).

Haugland and Hendrix, *Pooling problems with polynomial-time algorithms*, JOTA 170 (2016), 591–615, independently establish a fixed-source single-pool algorithm in Theorem 4.1. Their analysis also gives a pseudopolynomial algorithm for a different small-source/small-terminal class; that does not imply the present exact bit-polynomial statements for multiple fixed pools. The repository includes the supplied full article. [Primary text](../literature/papers/haugland2016-pooling-problems-with-polynomial-time/fulltext.md).

Baltean-Lugojan and Misener, *Piecewise parametric structure in the pooling problem: from sparse strongly-polynomial solutions to NP-hardness*, use pool concentrations as parameters and exploit additive decomposition over outputs. Assumption 2.2 restricts their analysis to one quality, removes feed and pool capacities, and fixes output demands. Their exact analytical subclasses therefore are relevant methodological prior but do not supply the proposed capacitated, arbitrary-quality, multiple-pool result. Remark 4.6 uses broad hardness language when relaxing assumptions; it does not provide a matching fixed-pool/fixed-quality hardness reduction for the no-bypass model. In particular, the occurrence of a bivariate polynomial by itself is not a proof of NP-hardness under dense rational encoding and fixed dimension. [Open published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6417401/).

A recent check of Jalilian and Kocuk, *Improved Rank-One-Based Relaxations and Bound Tightening Techniques for the Pooling Problem* (2025), found new convexification and bound-tightening results, not the candidate parameterized tractability theorem. Its introductory complexity discussion cites the preceding older results. This is a check of one recent paper, not evidence that every later publication has been excluded. [Open published paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11081-025-09997-6).

## The support construction has clear established antecedents

For fixed core data, the theorem replaces the aggregate contribution of the leaf blocks by a Minkowski sum in fixed dimension. Polynomial-time construction of sums of many polytopes in fixed dimension is established by Gritzmann and Sturmfels, *Minkowski Addition of Polytopes: Computational Complexity and Applications to Gröbner Bases*, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 6 (1993), 246–269. Their published abstract identifies both vertex and facet input presentations; a primary conference report explicitly states the fixed-dimensional polynomial bound. [Published source](https://doi.org/10.1137/0406019), [primary conference report, printed page 8](https://oda.mfo.de/bitstream/handle/mfo/647/full-text.pdf?sequence=).

Fukuda, *From the zonotope construction to the Minkowski addition of convex polytopes*, J. Symbolic Computation 38 (2004), 1261–1272, makes the mechanism particularly clear. Section 2 defines support-maximizing faces and normal fans; Proposition 2.1 describes compatible summand faces by a common support direction. The paper credits the fixed-dimensional polynomial complexity to Gritzmann–Sturmfels and develops output-sensitive reverse search. Its introduction gives the bound \(O(k^{d-1}n^{2(d-1)})\) for a sum of \(k\) polytopes with at most \(n\) vertices each. [Author manuscript](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~fukuda/download/paper/minksum030111.pdf), [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2003.08.007).

Consequently, avoiding the exponential Cartesian product by enumerating compatible support choices is established geometric reasoning. The candidate's additional work is **uniformity over a polynomially varying nonlinear core**: local vertex validity and ordering become sign conditions in the joint core/support space; denominator clearing retains polynomial encoding because this dimension is fixed; the resulting fixed-variable quantified formula allows exact global optimization. No source inspected stated this full rational-input model with varying local constraint matrices, a growing number of local rows and blocks, fixed aggregate dimension, and exact algebraic optimizer recovery.

## Relation to block LP and parametric search

Cslovjecsek, Eisenbrand, Hunkenschröder, Rohwedder, and Weismantel, *Block-Structured Integer and Linear Programming in Strongly Polynomial and Near Linear Time*, SODA 2021, use a small number of linking rows and independent local LPs. Section 3, Theorem 1, adapts Norton–Plotkin–Tardos parametric search; the Lagrange dual has a low-dimensional multiplier and an additive collection of local optimization problems. This is directly related to the theorem's decomposition geometry. Their data are linear and fixed, however; they do not optimize over a nonlinear parameter changing all local matrices and right-hand sides. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.07745).

The candidate should not imply that a fixed number of nonlinear parameters alone makes an arbitrary LP family globally tractable. Its small local blocks and fixed aggregate dimension are doing essential work. General parametric LP methods can have many critical regions; the proposed polynomial bound comes from the explicit fixed-dimensional sign construction and the block structure.

Realizable-sign enumeration, quantifier elimination in fixed total dimension, real-algebraic sampling, and algebraic-coefficient LP over a common extension are established methods, already cited correctly in the result to Basu–Pollack–Roy and Adler–Beling. These supply the exact bit model and recovery guarantees. None should be relabeled a new algorithmic primitive.

## Recommended positioning and limitations

The result can be described as an exact elimination theorem for polynomially parametrized sums of small polytopes, with new pooling tractability consequences. Its useful combination of parameters is more informative than merely observing that the problem is bilinear or has a few pool variables.

Keep the following distinctions explicit:

- The runtime is polynomial for each fixed parameter tuple; the proof does not establish fixed-parameter tractability or strong polynomiality.
- Arbitrarily many continuous leaves are allowed, but arbitrary integer leaves are not: one linking equation already accommodates subset sum.
- General convex nonlinear leaves are excluded; their exact algebraic optima can accumulate independent radicals of exponential degree.
- The fixed-pool/fixed-quality corollary excludes an unbounded number of bypass arcs. The fixed-input/fixed-pool corollary can include them because it also bounds the number of shared source constraints.
- Exact algebraic output is a theoretical guarantee. No practical implementation of the full elimination algorithm is claimed.

Searches combined fixed/bounded pools, inputs, outputs, qualities, pooling tractability, parametric LP, block-angular programming, fixed linking constraints, Minkowski sums, normal fans, semialgebraic parameters, bilinear rank, and recent pooling papers. Primary sources were preferred; publisher barriers were respected, and the final Haugland audit used the legitimately supplied repository copy. No equivalent result was located, but publication-level novelty should remain qualified pending broader citation checking and possible differently named antecedents.
