# Many constraints in a three-dimensional quadratic span

Date: 2026-09-22. Status: a proved elementary extension and an explicit
lower-bound family checked by a fresh
[independent review](review-20260922-span-three-many.md). The general improved upper
bound remains open in this investigation. These observations are not being
promoted as a substantial new contribution.

## Question and conclusion

Let `n>=3`, let `Q_1,...,Q_m` be homogeneous matrices of affine quadratics,
and assume their linear span `L` has dimension three and contains a positive
definite matrix. Let the strict feasible set `S` be nonempty with proper
ordinary convex hull. Write `K=cone(Q_1,...,Q_m)` and let `k` be its number
of extreme rays after redundant generators are removed. Strict feasibility
makes `K` pointed; a pointed three-dimensional polyhedral cone with `k`
extreme rays has `k` facets.

Blekherman–Dey–Sun (BDS), Proposition 2.21 in the inspected author PDF
(arXiv v1, October 2022), gives at most `2k` good
aggregations. The following refinement of their proof is valid:

> If `D` is positive definite in `L`, at most twice the number of facets of
> `K` whose inward normal is negative on `D` are needed.

In particular, if the PSD cone in `L` is not contained in `-K`, at most
`2k-2` aggregations suffice. This is the direct polyhedral-cone version of
Blekherman–Dunbar (BD), Proposition 8.6. It is an extension of an existing
argument, not a new topological theorem.

The complementary case, in which the PSD cone is contained in `-K`, is not
resolved here. BD Proposition 8.10 gives two aggregations when `K` is
simplicial and its closed-set hypotheses hold. A corresponding general
polyhedral-cone extension, together with a valid transfer from its closed
hypotheses to the strict setting here, would imply the unconditional bound
`2k-2`. Merely replacing
the orthant by `K` in its statement does not verify that extension.

A bound independent of `k` is impossible, even for an already convex set
defined by positive-definite centered ellipsoids. The explicit family below
requires all `k` original aggregation rays. Thus any general result must
retain linear dependence on the number of extreme generators. This does
not establish sharpness of `2k-2` or `2k`.

## Directional facet bound

The stated hypotheses supply HHC, as required by the strict aggregation
theorems used below. Choose three basis matrices spanning `L`. On every
homogenized hyperplane, their positive definite combination remains positive
definite. Its dimension is `n>=3`, so Calabi's three-form convexity result
applies. The full `m`-form image is a linear image of this convex image.
Thus the original family has hidden hyperplane convexity.

Use irredundant facet inequalities

```
K={M in L : ell_j(M)>=0, j=1,...,k}.
```

Fix `D` positive definite in `L` and let
`J_-(D)={j:ell_j(D)<0}`. This set is nonempty: otherwise `D` would belong
to `K`, whereas evaluation at any lifted point of `S` is strictly negative
on every nonzero matrix in `K` and positive on `D`.

Take any good aggregation matrix `M` whose strict sublevel is not all of
`R^n`. It has exactly one negative eigenvalue. Set

```
t_* = min_{j in J_-(D)} ell_j(M)/(-ell_j(D)),
M_* = M+t_* D.
```

Then `t_*>=0`, `M_*` belongs to `K`, and it lies on a facet indexed by
`J_-(D)`. It is nonzero: if `M_*` were zero, then either `M=0` or `M` would
be negative definite, both contradicting its inertia. The PSD-improvement
lemma, BDS Proposition 9.1, applies to matrices in `K`: choose any
nonnegative representation of `M_*` in the original generators and express
`t_*D` as a signed combination in their span. It gives

```
M_* is good, and {x:q_{M_*}(x)<0} subset {x:q_M(x)<0}.
```

Consequently all good cuts on the facets indexed by `J_-(D)` already give
the full hull. Every such facet is generated by two extreme rays. BDS
Proposition 9.6 reduces all good aggregations on that facet to at most two
endpoint aggregations, where goodness is always with respect to the full
set `S`. Hence `2|J_-(D)|` cuts suffice.

If a PSD matrix `E` lies outside `-K`, some `ell_j(E)>0`. Choose any positive
definite `D_0` in `L`. For sufficiently small positive `delta`, the matrix
`D=E+delta D_0` is positive definite and still has `ell_j(D)>0`. It follows
that `|J_-(D)|<=k-1`. The argument needs no regularity, boundedness, or
projective perturbation, since it uses the strict BDS theorem directly.

The count can be strengthened by minimizing `|J_-(D)|` over positive
definite directions in `L`. This is an existence bound, not an algorithmic
claim about finding an optimal direction or the good endpoints.

## Explicit linear lower bound

Fix `m>=3`, `n>=2`, and distinct numbers `0<a_1<...<a_m<1`. Set

```
f_i(x)=a_i x_1^2+(1-a_i^2) sum_{j=2}^n x_j^2-1,
S={x:f_i(x)<0 for every i}.
```

Each `f_i` has positive-definite Hessian. Thus `S` is nonempty, bounded,
open, and convex. Its homogeneous matrices have coordinate vectors
`(a_i,1-a_i^2,-1)` in the basis

```
x_1^2, sum_{j=2}^n x_j^2, t^2.
```

Any three distinct parameters give linearly independent vectors: their
determinant is a nonzero Vandermonde determinant up to a nonzero sign.
Therefore the span is exactly three-dimensional and contains the positive
definite form `sum_{j=1}^n x_j^2+t^2`.

For each `i`, choose a point `p_i` whose squared coordinates satisfy

```
(p_i)_1^2=2a_i/(1+a_i^2),
sum_{j=2}^n (p_i)_j^2=1/(1+a_i^2).
```

Direct calculation gives

```
f_j(p_i)=-(a_j-a_i)^2/(1+a_i^2).
```

Thus `f_i(p_i)=0` and every other constraint is strictly negative. For any
nonzero nonnegative multiplier `lambda`,

```
f_lambda(p_i)>=0  iff  lambda is a positive multiple of e_i.
```

The point `p_i` is outside the open set `S=conv(S)`. Any exact intersection
of strict aggregation inequalities must exclude it. Therefore that family
must contain a positive multiple of `e_i`. This holds for every `i`, and
even for infinite exact strict families. In particular, the minimum finite
cardinality is exactly `m`. Each original inequality is good, and its
homogeneous matrix has precisely one negative eigenvalue.

For a finite exact description of the closed set `cl(S)` by nonstrict
aggregations, the same necessity follows by continuity. If no selected
multiplier uses only ray `i`, every selected inequality is strict at `p_i`.
They then all hold in a neighborhood of `p_i`, including points just outside
the ellipsoid `f_i<=0`, a contradiction. An infinite closed family can
recover a missing ray by a limit, so that stronger assertion is not made.

The witness also proves that each `Q_i` is an extreme ray of `K`: a
nonnegative combination of other matrices would evaluate strictly negatively
at `p_i`, whereas `Q_i` evaluates to zero. Hence `k=m` here.

## Why the remaining case needs more work

Three shortcuts do not supply the missing general upper bound.

1. A triangulation of `K` does not permit replacing the full hull by an
   intersection of hulls of three-constraint subsystems. Convex hull does
   not commute with intersection. Goodness for the full feasible set also
   does not imply goodness for a larger subsystem feasible set.
2. Adding or removing a negative-definite aggregation does not turn a
   general polyhedral coefficient cone into a simplicial one while keeping
   all other inequalities equivalent.
3. Good matrices do not in general form a convex cone. For example, the
   one-negative-eigenvalue matrices `diag(-4,1,1)` and `diag(1,-4,1)` are
   negative at the common vector `(1,1,1)`, but their sum has two negative
   eigenvalues. Taking a sufficiently small convex affine neighborhood of
   a point on the corresponding common negative components makes each
   inequality valid there; validity does not repair the sum's inertia.

BD's topology is formulated in part for general polyhedral cones, which
makes an extension plausible. However, its Proposition 8.10 also uses
connectedness of a particular set of good aggregations, a separation
argument in the spectral curve, and interlacing after restriction to a
hyperplane. Each part needs a proof with the original full set and general
`K`. No such complete proof was obtained in this investigation.

The positive-definite centered family is deliberately easy. It identifies
an unavoidable dependence on input cone complexity, but does not capture
the additional cost of convexifying nonconvex quadrics. A worthwhile next
target is either a nonconvex family requiring more than `k` aggregations,
or a proof that `k+1` or `2k-2` always suffices. The sharp four-necessary
three-generator example shows that `k` is not a universal bound.

## Literature examined and verification

- Blekherman–Dey–Sun, *Aggregations of Quadratic Inequalities and Hidden
  Hyperplane Convexity*, SIAM J. Optim. 34(1), 2024. Read the local full
  text, especially Proposition 2.21 and Propositions 9.1 and 9.6; checked
  the openly accessible [author PDF](https://www2.isye.gatech.edu/~sdey30/HHC.pdf).
  Their exact `2m` statement is the direct prior result. The proof already
  uses the three-dimensional matrix cone and its facets.
- Blekherman–Dunbar, *A Topological Approach to Simple Descriptions of
  Convex Hulls of Sets Defined by Three Quadrics*. Read
  [arXiv v1, Section 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18282v1), especially
  Propositions 8.6 and 8.10 and their proofs. The directional bound above
  generalizes the elementary geometry of Proposition 8.6; the hard
  general-cone analogue of Proposition 8.10 is not asserted.
- Examined the [Dunbar dissertation record](https://etd.library.emory.edu/concern/etds/vq27zq10w)
  and searched for combinations of “quadratic aggregations”, “2m-2”,
  “three dimensional”, and “polyhedral cone”. No stronger many-constraint
  bound was identified. The full dissertation was not inspected here, and
  unsuccessful searches do not establish novelty.

Targeted exact arithmetic checked all boundary identities for
`a_i=i/(m+1)`, `m=3,...,30`, using Python `fractions.Fraction`:
`9,450` pair evaluations passed. The computation verifies those rational
instances only; the displayed algebraic identity proves the general
family. No Lean proof, repository-wide verification, or CI inspection was
performed. The fresh independent review also passed 9,450 exact boundary
checks and 31,465 Vandermonde determinant checks. It confirmed the stated
proofs and source assumptions; the unresolved general-cone case remains
open, and the proved observations remain supporting work.
