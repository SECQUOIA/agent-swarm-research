**Verdict: PASS.** I independently checked the complete revised statement in

## Compression preserves the global problem

At a local minimum of a differentiable function over a polyhedron, the negative
gradient belongs to the polyhedral normal cone. The normal cone is generated by
active inequality normals and equality normals, with the usual multiplier signs.
This remains true for redundant constraints, empty interiors, and singleton
feasible sets. No nonlinear constraint qualification is being imported.

For fixed leader coordinates, differentiating the aggregate term with respect to
the follower gives `U(x)^T grad_w phi`; there is no derivative of `U(x)` in this
derivative. After introducing the aggregate and the fixed number of resource
multipliers, coordinate stationarity is the box inclusion for `a_i z_i+B_i`.
Since `a_i>0`, its solution is exactly the stated clipping formula. The lower
endpoint requires a nonnegative derivative, the upper endpoint a nonpositive
derivative, and an interior point a zero derivative. The threshold signs are
correct. If both endpoints coincide, clipping returns that endpoint for every
derivative, as required by its unrestricted box normal cone.

Every compressed point satisfying aggregate consistency, resources, and
complementarity therefore recovers a feasible stationary follower point. Every
global follower minimizer has such a representation. At each feasible leader,
compactness of the follower polyhedron guarantees that at least one global
minimizer exists. A candidate is globally minimizing if and only if its value is
no greater than all values in the compressed stationary set: that set contains a
global minimizer and contains only feasible points. This proves the universal
comparison without treating nonconvex stationarity as sufficient.

As a diagnostic example, take one follower variable on `[-2,2]`, positive local
cost `z^2/2`, and aggregate term `(w^2-1)^2-w^2/2`, with `w=z`. The follower
objective is `(z^2-1)^2`. Its stationary point zero is represented but is rejected
by comparison with the stationary global minima `z=+1` and `z=-1`. Competing
responses correctly carry no upper-level feasibility restrictions.

## Fixed dimension and polynomial encoding

Only `r,s,k_eq,k_in` are fixed. The number of follower coordinates, upper
constraints, and input polynomial monomials may grow. Both copies of the
compressed coordinates, the follower values, and the upper value still give a
fixed total number of real variables.

The signs of the `2N` threshold polynomials determine each clipping formula.
Enumerating realizable sign conditions, including zero signs, has polynomial
complexity in a fixed number of variables and polynomially bounded degree.
Disconnected regions with the same sign condition need no different recovery
formula. Sign conditions outside the leader set can be retained, because the
outer formula requires `x in C`. This is essential: there is no enumeration of
all formal strings in `{-,0,+}^{2N}`.

On `C`, `Q=prod_i a_i` is positive. The coordinate numerators, resource
inequalities, and complementarity equations consequently have the stated signs
and denominators. The follower numerator uses `Q^2` correctly: its nonlinear
aggregate term is evaluated directly at the compressed coordinate `w`, rather
than by substituting a new rational aggregate expression.

Let `delta` be the actual maximum input degree. Threshold degree is `O(delta)`;
`Q` has degree at most `N delta`; coordinate, resource, and follower-value
numerators have degree `O(N delta+delta)`. Upper substitution and multiplication
by the positive `Q^delta` give degree `O(N delta^2)`. These estimates include
polynomial endpoints and polynomial `U(x)`.

Expansion is polynomial because all resulting polynomials have a fixed number
of variables. A polynomial of degree `d` in that dimension has only
`binomial(d+c,c)` possible monomials. Products of the explicitly supplied upper
monomials can therefore be formed in polynomial time, and coefficient bit
lengths grow polynomially under the polynomially many products and sums. The
argument applies to the stated degree-controlled encodings, including dense and
unary-exponent lists. It does not imply polynomial time in the length of sparse
binary exponents when those exponents encode exponentially large degree.

## Primary real algebraic imports and algebraic recovery

I read the original [Basu–Pollack–Roy paper](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/jacm95.ps)
by converting the repository's PostScript copy to PDF in `/tmp`. Section 1.3,
printed pages 1004–1005, states the arithmetic complexity, control of intermediate
and output integer bit lengths, and dependence on Boolean formula length.
Theorem 1.3.1 gives quantifier elimination. Section 3.1.3, pages 1028–1029,
computes sample points meeting every sign cell as common univariate
representations, with the required degree and arithmetic bounds. Thus the
imports cover rational bit complexity, zero sign conditions, and exact samples;
they are not merely real arithmetic operation counts.

Clearing rational coefficient denominators before using these algorithms costs
polynomial bit time. With fixed total dimension, their exponents are fixed.
The final draft directly samples the formula for a feasible leader-response
tuple with no feasible tuple of smaller upper value. Duplicating the compressed
formula and renaming its internal quantified variables keeps the total number
of variables fixed. This gives all selected coordinates and the optimal value
in one algebraic extension of polynomial degree and coefficient height, using
only rational input. Recovering every `z_i=Z_i/Q` then uses rational
evaluation in that same extension. Since `Q` is nonzero at the selected leader,
field division is valid. The `N` output coordinates do not introduce `N`
independent algebraic extensions.

An algebraic sample also supplies resource multipliers even if the multiplier
set is unbounded. Compactness is required for the original response graph and
upper optimization, not for the auxiliary multiplier space.

## Attainment and changing box endpoints

Write all follower constraints as `M z<=h(x)`, where

```
M = [A; -A; H; I; -I],
h(x) = [b(x); -b(x); d(x); u(x); -l(x)].
```

The matrix is fixed for the instance and the right-hand side is continuous.
For any sequence of optimal pairs converging to `(x,z)`, primal feasibility
passes to the limit. Given any `y in P(x)`, its positive residual for `h(x_n)`
tends to zero. The nearby systems are nonempty because they contain `z_n`.
Hoffman's bound supplies `y_n in P(x_n)` tending to `y`. Comparing follower
values and taking limits proves optimality of `z` at `x`.

I read the theorem and proof on printed pages 263–264 of
[Hoffman (1952)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/07/On_approximate_solutions_of_systems_of_linear_inequalities_%28IA_jresv49n4p263%29.pdf).
The proof constructs constants from subsets of the row matrix; they do not
depend on the changing right-hand side. Only existence of this constant is
needed here, so no quantitative algorithm for computing it is assumed.

Polynomial box bounds are uniformly bounded on compact `C`. Thus the response
graph lies in a compact product, is closed by the preceding argument, and remains
closed after imposing the upper weak inequalities. A continuous upper objective
attains its minimum whenever this set is nonempty. Polynomial aggregate weights
affect the objective and compressed consistency but do not change `M`, so they
do not interfere with this proof.

The changing-normal counterexample in the investigation is correct: `xz=0`
forces `z=0` for `x>0`, but at `x=0` the follower minimizes `(z-1)^2` at one.
The upper infimum of `x+z` is zero and is not attained.

## Scope boundaries

The cubic-cost construction has response `sqrt(a_i)` on `[1,a_i+1]`, including
the endpoint case `a_i=1`. Its positive second derivative does not make the
response rational. The upper sum comparison is the sum-of-square-roots problem;
for distinct primes the sum has degree `2^N`. This supports the stated
arithmetic boundary without proving NP-hardness of that comparison problem.

The zero-curvature and negative-curvature Boolean reductions also check out.
With zero follower objective all box points are optimal, and upper equations
can impose Boolean coordinates. With follower objective `sum_i z_i(1-z_i)`,
the global optima over the cube are exactly its Boolean vertices. Linear clause
constraints then express Boolean satisfiability. These are elementary scope
counterexamples, appropriately not presented as new hardness mechanisms.

The theorem is therefore a valid exact complexity result under its stated
parameters and input representation. Priority and impact are separate questions;
the independent literature comparison is recorded in
[the novelty note](bilevel-fixed-aggregate-response-novelty.md).
