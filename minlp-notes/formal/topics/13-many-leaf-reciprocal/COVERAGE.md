## Cost model and software boundaries

The operation counts refer to the terminating Lean procedures and their
explicit charge functions. Bit-work uses the `BitCost` schoolbook model:
digit scans for addition/comparison, shifted-row multiplication, binary long
division, and Euclidean normalization of rational fractions. Primitive costs
are combined with proved bounds on the actual rational operand families and
charged operation counts. The evaluator, oracle, and witness theorems give
explicit polynomials in the leaf count and input bit bound.

These are high-level algorithmic complexity proofs. They do not verify a
machine-level digit implementation, refine the cost model to Lean's compiled
`Rat` implementation, or predict wall-clock runtime. The polynomial bit-work
claims in the table have this same scope throughout.

The new Lean algorithms and constructions are verified as Lean mathematical
objects. There is no refinement theorem identifying their execution with
`code/common-factor-anchor-verify.py`, its parser, or floating-point HiGHS.
The historical seeded experiments, review history, and novelty claims remain
outside mathematical proof coverage, as listed in the inventory.

No product bounds or extra linking constraints are added to the original
graph. Generalizing the number of leaves does not assert a hull description
for those stronger graph sets.
