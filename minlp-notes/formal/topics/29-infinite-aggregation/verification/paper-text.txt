1    Formal verification of the infinite-aggregation example
The third Lean package concerns the explicit family
               Sr = {(u, v) ∈ Rr × Rr : ∥u∥2 < 1, ∥v∥2 < 1, uT v > 1/2},                r ≥ 2.
Squared lengths and inner products in the formalization are Euclidean coordinate sums. The
package treats the actual quadratic map and ordinary convex hull, including the four-variable case
r = 2.

Geometry and good multipliers. The set Sr is nonempty and bounded, and its ordinary convex
hull is proper. Its homogeneous map is
                           (u, v, t) 7−→ (∥u∥2 − t2 , ∥v∥2 − t2 , t2 /2 − uT v).
The formal HHC assertion requires convexity of this image on the kernel of every nonzero real linear
functional. It is proved for all r ≥ 2; HHC is not supplied as an additional premise for this example.
    The proof uses an explicit orthonormal frame and an algebraic unit-circle construction to
obtain the full range of a linear functional on a fixed two-column Gram fiber. For a hyperplane
tr(P T U ) + st = 0, put G = U T U , H = P T P , and T = t2 . Its exact feasible Gram domain is
                                                                   √
                       G ⪰ 0,       T ≥ 0,      s2 T ≤ tr(HG) + 2 det H det G.
Both necessity and realization are proved, including singular Gram matrices and zero coefficients.
Concavity of the square root of the two-by-two determinant proves convexity of this domain. The
homogeneous quadratic image is its linear image, which gives HHC.
   A good multiplier is defined by the original conditions: it is nonzero and nonnegative, its
homogeneous aggregate has at most one negative eigenvalue counted with multiplicity, and its strict
aggregation contains conv Sr . The proved classification is
                          λ is good    ⇐⇒      λ ≥ 0,    λ ̸= 0,       λ23 ≤ 4λ1 λ2 .
The homogeneous matrix consists of r copies of
                                                                   !
                                                 λ1    −λ3 /2
                                       Bλ =
                                                −λ3 /2  λ2
and the scalar λ3 /2 − λ1 − λ2 , up to coordinate order. For nonnegative multipliers violating the
discriminant inequality, the repeated block gives at least r negative eigenvalues. For nonzero
nonnegative multipliers satisfying it, the block is PSD and the scalar is strictly negative, so there is
exactly one negative eigenvalue. Convexity of the original aggregate and its strict negativity on Sr
establish strict negativity throughout the ordinary convex hull. Thus the classification does not
assume a general aggregation hull-description theorem.

    Consequently, if a family of strict good aggregations describes conv Sr exactly, it contains a
positive multiple of ρ(τ ) for every τ ∈ [1, 2]. The formal cardinality argument normalizes weights to
third coordinate two and proves that the set of normalized weights is uncountable. This counts
distinct rays, not repeated indices in a family. In particular, no countable family of strict good
aggregations describes the ordinary hull. This statement is conditional on an exact description; the
package does not separately prove that the intersection of all good strict aggregations equals the
hull.

The closed hull requires a different witness. For any finite family of good multipliers, choose
an omitted ray and decrease the Gram off-diagonal 2/5 by a sufficiently small positive amount. The
perturbed Gram matrix remains positive definite and is realized by actual vectors. Every selected
aggregate is strictly negative there, while the omitted aggregate is strictly positive. Every good
aggregate is nonpositive on cl(conv Sr ) by continuity. Hence the perturbed point is outside this
closed hull, although it satisfies every selected weak aggregation. No finite intersection of weak good
aggregations can therefore equal cl(conv Sr ).

Scope and records. The Lean sources are in Formal.InfiniteAggregation; the frozen claims,
declaration coverage, independent reviews, and targeted verification records are in formal/topics/
29-infinite-aggregation/. This package does not establish an exact hull formula, countable
weak aggregation sufficiency, equality with the hull of the original weak system, a finite SDP lift,
an obstruction to arbitrary quadratic descriptions, approximation bounds, or the general sharp
Gram-map theorem. It also makes no literature-priority claim.
