# Independent review of corrections and scalar/quadratic curvature

This is a source-level semantic review of CM09–CM18 and CM35–CM41 against
Sections 3 and 4 of the manuscript. It also checks the reused `Coordinates`
and `SafeCuts` interfaces. No project-wide verification or CI inspection was
performed for this review. Module build results belong in `VERIFICATION.md`.

No mathematical gap or incorrect conclusion was found in the reviewed scope.
The review checked the statements and their connection to the source claims,
not merely that the modules elaborate.

| Claims | Reviewed declarations and result |
|---|---|

## Independent review of monomials, fractions, and range/domain rules

A second source-level review covered CM42–CM49 in `MonomialConcave`,
`Monomial`, `MonomialHessian`, `MonomialMatrix`, `LinearFraction`,
`IntervalArithmetic`, and `DomainExamples`. No incorrect theorem or missing
sign condition was found in these mathematical results. The representation
and definedness limits below should remain explicit in the coverage map.

| Claims | Reviewed declarations and result |
|---|---|
| CM42 | `realMonomial_line_hasDerivAt` differentiates the actual finite product of real powers. `realMonomial_line_second_deriv` establishes eventual positivity along the line before replacing the derivative function locally and differentiating again. Thus the second derivative is not obtained by assuming the displayed Hessian. `realMonomial_directional_hessian_matrix` identifies its value for every direction with the displayed symmetric matrix quadratic form. Its formal representation is the actual iterated scalar derivative along every affine line, rather than a separate equality involving an iterated Fréchet derivative or an array of mixed partial derivatives. |
| CM43–CM45 | The curvature conclusions concern `realMonomial` itself. Concavity follows from weighted AM–GM with unused weight assigned to one, then global upper supports and Jensen's inequality. Convexity uses a proved geometric-mean lift with positive logarithm arguments. The nonpositive-exponent case normalizes by `1 - sum a`, whose positivity is proved. The single-positive case requires a strictly positive distinguished exponent, every other exponent nonpositive, and total exponent at least one. Its lifted weights are nonnegative and total at most one; there is no missing reciprocal-sign condition. The separate middle-form results establish weighted Cauchy–Schwarz, both definite-sign cases, and the single-positive case with zero weights and empty blocks allowed. The inverse-block identity requires strictly positive weights, exactly where invertibility is used. The general curvature result retains zero exponents directly and therefore does not rely on applying that inverse to a singular block. |
| CM46 | The nonnegative-boundary results use actual continuity, perturb every coordinate by a positive scalar, and pass a closed Jensen inequality to zero. They impose nonnegative exponents, so negative-power singularities are excluded. The convex boundary result additionally retains the recognizer's sufficient conditions. Zero exponents explicitly contribute one, including at zero; this convention is stated in the definition and does not claim to change the original expression's checked domain. Empty products are admitted by the finite-type statements. |
| CM47 | Both first and second derivatives are derivatives of the actual quotient on a nonzero-denominator neighborhood. The decomposition additionally requires a nonzero denominator coefficient. All four residual/denominator sign combinations have the correct convexity direction, including a negative denominator cubed. The zero residual gives the stated constant on the valid domain. The constant-denominator identities and affine-curvature result also hold for Lean's totalized division when the constant is zero; applying them to a source quotient still requires the separately checked nonzero denominator. They are algebraic/curvature lemmas, not a claim that division by zero is an admitted source expression. |
| CM48 | Finite interval multiplication uses all four endpoint products and proves containment by actual sign cases. Signed scaling inherits this result without requiring a positive scalar. Reciprocal bounds require an interval strictly on one side of zero; negative integer powers invoke that requirement before inversion. Natural powers and finite products have inductive containment proofs, including zero powers and empty products. Sharper even-power bounds and each stated sign-certified half-line product endpoint are separately proved. These theorems give bounds only under their stated interval/sign premises; no total half-line propagation dispatcher or unsupported-case behavior of Python is claimed here. Rational-input and dyadic-endpoint lemmas prove exact real interpretation, while the outward enclosure itself remains a premise. |
| CM49 | `quotientDefined` separates source-domain validity from Lean's totalized division. Cancellation is allowed only under that predicate, and a box containing zero fails it. Square-root simplification proves the exact absolute-value identity and the necessary and sufficient nonnegative-sign condition. Nonnegativity of the bilinear product on the quadrant is proved separately from a midpoint counterexample to concavity. |

For CM42, the directional matrix formula is an accurate description of what
Lean checks and should be preserved in documentation. For CM47–CM49, the
original-domain checks remain necessary even when an algebraic identity or a
curvature theorem happens to extend to a totalized value at an otherwise
undefined point. Neither issue is an error in the proved statements.
