1    Quadratic systems and the certificate question

where Ai ∈ Sn , bi ∈ Rn , and ci ∈ R. Here Sn denotes the real symmetric n × n matrices; A ⪰ 0 and

is whether a nonempty quadratic system has a proper hull, and how this can be certified by its
defining inequalities.

Definition 1.1 (Hidden hyperplane convexity). A homogeneous quadratic map ϕ : Rd → Rm has
hidden hyperplane convexity (HHC) if ϕ(H) is convex for every linear hyperplane H ⊂ Rd .

    This is the definition of Blekherman, Dey, and Sun [3, Definition 2.1]; their work appeared as
[4]. Ordinary hidden convexity means only that ϕ(Rd ) is convex. The two properties differ. All
homogeneous quadratic images contain zero and are invariant under nonnegative scaling, so a convex
such image is a convex cone; it need not be closed. HHC implies ordinary hidden convexity when
d ≥ 3: any two points of Rd lie in some linear hyperplane, whose image contains the segment joining
their images.
Question 1.2 (Blekherman–Dey–Sun, Conjecture 3.3). Suppose S ̸= ∅ and f h has HHC. Is

                    conv S ̸= Rn    ⇐⇒      ∃λ ∈ Rm
                                                  + : Aλ ⪰ 0,     (Aλ , bλ ) ̸= (0, 0)?            (1.5)

    The formulation in Blekherman et al. [3, Conjecture 3.3] says that the aggregated function is
not a negative constant. Under nonemptiness this is equivalent to the displayed condition. Their
standing setting has n ≥ 3 and m ≥ 2; the formulation here also includes the smaller positive
dimensions and numbers of inequalities. Throughout, numbered result locators for that work refer
to the explicitly cited May 29, 2023 arXiv version; the earlier author manuscript has different
numbering for several background results.
    The reverse implication in (1.5) is elementary: a nonconstant convex quadratic aggregation
bounds S by a proper convex sublevel set. The unresolved case in the cited conjecture concerns the
opposite implication when K contains nonzero vectors but all of them belong to T . Under convexity
of (g1 , . . . , gm )(Rn ), Blekherman, Dey, and Sun already prove that K = {0} implies conv S = Rn
[3, Proposition 2.14]. For n ≥ 2, their separable case, with all Qi diagonal, also already gives the
certificate characterization [3, Theorem 2.12].
    It is essential to distinguish this certificate question from a complete description of the hull.
In the aggregation literature, a good aggregation is a nonnegative nonzero multiplier λ such that
conv S ⊆ {x : fλ (x) < 0} and Qλ has at most one negative eigenvalue. The function fλ need not be
globally convex. Blekherman, Dey, and Sun prove that these good aggregations describe conv S
under HHC, n ≥ 3, and ∅ ̸= S with proper hull [3, Theorem 2.9]. Thus an existence certificate and
a family of hull-defining aggregations address different questions.
    The two-constraint case belongs to established theory. Dines’ theorem gives convexity of the joint
image of two homogeneous quadratic forms [6], and therefore HHC for a pair of homogeneous forms
and their restrictions. Yildiran characterizes the hull of a nonempty set defined by two strict quadratic
inequalities using at most two aggregations [7]. For three quadratic inequalities in dimension n ≥ 3
with a nonempty proper hull, Dey, Muñoz, and Serrano obtain an aggregation description when the
homogenized matrices Q1 , Q2 , Q3 admit a positive definite real linear combination [5]. Here “positive
definite linear combination” allows real coefficients of either sign; it does not mean a nonnegative
aggregation. More recently, Blekherman and Dunbar use the spectral curve and algebraic topology
to obtain further three-quadratic hull descriptions [2]. Their available preprint treats finiteness
and related spectral conditions [1]. These results set the context for the certificate question, while
neither the classical two-form convexity theorem nor an elementary separation lemma is itself a new
contribution of the present work.


2    Formal verification of the certificate theorem
The Lean development verifies the following statement for finite families of real symmetric matrices
and real vectors and constants. Suppose S is nonempty and, for every nonzero α, the image f h (Hα,s )
is convex at arbitrarily large positive levels s. Then

                 conv S ̸= Rn      ⇐⇒    ∃λ ≥ 0, λ ̸= 0 : Aλ ⪰ 0,    (Aλ , bλ ) ̸= (0, 0).

The development proves that the unbounded-level assumption is equivalent to the existence of a
strictly increasing sequence of such levels tending to infinity. It also proves the implication from
HHC, so the HHC specialization has exactly the assumptions of Question 1.2. The convex hull is
the ordinary hull. There is no positive-definiteness assumption, and the certificate-to-proper-hull
implication requires neither HHC nor nonemptiness.

Scope and checking. The package also verifies the negative-recession-direction lemma, supporting-
hyperplane exclusion, normalized separation certificates, closedness of finitely generated coefficient
cones, and uniform separation of closed cones meeting only at zero. The last separation lemma
remains available independently, although the shorter proof above does not need it. The imple-
mentation uses an entrywise maximum norm for matrices and the product maximum norm for
coefficient pairs; the compactness argument works with these norms as well as with Euclidean and
Frobenius norms.
     The proof sources are in formal/Formal/QuadraticAggregation/. The main declarations are
proper_hull_iff_certificate and proper_hull_iff_certificate_of_hhc, in the namespace
QuadraticAggregation.System. The coverage, independent statement reviews, and reproducible
topic-specific checks are recorded in formal/topics/27-quadratic-aggregation/. These checks
comprise warning-free compilation, a transitive axiom audit of every declaration owned by the topic,
and kernel replay of each module. Only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice, and
Quot.sound are permitted. Kernel replay uses the pinned Lean kernel and imported dependencies;
it is not an independently implemented checker.
     The package proves existence of a nontrivial globally convex aggregation. It does not assert
that globally convex aggregations describe the whole hull. The closed-system, Shor-relaxation,
SDP-decision, and checkable-hypothesis corollaries, counterexamples, and literature-priority claims
are outside this formal package. Formal completion of the stated theorem does not certify the
remaining manuscript or its numerical computations.
