# Review: right-hand-side smoothing and exact norm penalties

Date: 2026-09-25. Status: independent literature and significance review of a proposed result, with supporting counterexamples. This is not a proof of novelty or a complete review of the accompanying theorem.

The proposed theorem is a useful quantitative synthesis of established convex sensitivity, finite-disjunction exact penalization, and convex-boundary anti-concentration. Its strongest feature is that perturbing only the equality right-hand side can control exact penalty **encoding length** without controlling the algebraic complexity of the compact convex slices. The present argument does not support a claim to a new general smoothed-conditioning principle. On its own, it looks more like a concise complement to the repository's worst-case penalty obstruction than a substantial new theory.

## Statement being assessed

There are at most `K` nonempty compact convex slices `X_z`, a linear map `A`, and continuous convex slice objectives `f_z`. Write `C_z=A X_z`. All objective values lie in an interval `[L,U]`, with `M=U-L`. The equality-constrained problem at right-hand side `b` minimizes `f_z(x)` over all slices with `Ax=b`. Assume this problem is feasible. Distances and the penalty use the same fixed norm, and boundaries are ambient boundaries.

If

```
d <= min_z dist(b, boundary C_z),   d>0,
```

then a zero-multiplier norm penalty `rho=M/d` suffices for equality of optimal values. Every penalty minimizer is feasible if `rho>M/d`. Feasible slices contain the norm ball of radius `d` about `b`; infeasible slices have residual norm at least `d`. The convex value function of a feasible slice has a supporting subgradient at `b` of dual norm at most `M/d`. These observations give the proposed deterministic estimate.

This margin keeps the feasible/infeasible status of **every slice** stable under small RHS changes. It is generally smaller than the distance to the boundary of the union of all slice images. The latter distance alone does not control the penalty.

For the Gaussian statement, specialize both distance and penalty to the **Euclidean norm**. With noise `b=b_bar+sigma*g`, `g~N(0,I_m)`, the already established convex-boundary estimate gives

```
P[min_z dist(b,boundary C_z) <= d] <= 8 K m^(1/4) d/sigma.
```

Consequently, outside an event of probability at most `epsilon`, every feasible sampled problem has an exact zero-multiplier penalty at most `8 M K m^(1/4)/(sigma epsilon)`. Lower-dimensional slice images require the usual approximation argument when applying a theorem stated for convex bodies; under full-dimensional noise they are themselves hit with probability zero. This review verifies the cited full-dimensional Gaussian tube estimate, not the complete cube or rational-grid proof proposed elsewhere.

## Closest primary results

**Finite slices and exact penalties.** Lefebvre and Schmidt, *Exact Augmented Lagrangian Duality for Nonconvex Mixed-Integer Nonlinear Optimization*, [December 15, 2025 preprint](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/exact-penalty-for-minlp-1.pdf), Theorem 14, already proves finite exactness by splitting integer assignments into feasible convex slices and infeasible slices, then maximizing finite thresholds over bounded integer assignments. Feasible slices use Slater and dual multipliers; infeasible slices are raised above a primal upper bound. The proposed margin bound replaces existential slice certificates with a uniform geometric estimate and then studies its random RHS behavior. Neither the finite-slice decomposition nor zero-multiplier exactness is new.

**Penalty parameters and multipliers.** Friedlander and Tseng, *Exact Regularization of Convex Programs*, SIAM J. Optim. 18 (2007), 1326–1350; [open preprint](https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2006/11/1531.pdf), Section 4, Theorem 4.2, relates convex exact-penalty thresholds to a polar-gauge size of Lagrange multipliers. Their discussion identifies earlier results by Bertsekas and by Bertsekas–Nedić–Ozdaglar. The supporting-subgradient argument is standard convex sensitivity and exact-penalty theory. Bounding that subgradient by objective range divided by interior radius is elementary and should be presented as a lemma or direct derivation, not the main novelty claim.

**Convex error bounds and disjunctive sensitivity.** Boț and Csetnek, [*Error Bound Results for Convex Inequality Systems via Conjugate Duality*](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~rabot/publications/jour12-07.pdf), Theorem 7 and its preceding discussion, give a diameter divided by Slater-distance error bound and compare it with Robinson's 1975 bound. This is not an identical equality-image result, but the range/radius structure is classical. Gugat and Hante, [*Lipschitz Continuity of the Value Function in Mixed-Integer Optimal Control Problems*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.04639), Assumption 4 and Theorem 3, establish value-function Lipschitz continuity with uniform Slater and boundedness assumptions across discrete controls. They build on Gugat's [*Parametric Disjunctive Programming: One-Sided Differentiability of the Value Function*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1022603112856), JOTA 92 (1997), 285–310. Only the older paper's primary abstract and its use in Gugat–Hante were inspected. These references further narrow any novelty claim: stable sensitivity across convex disjunctions predates the proposed theorem.

**Smoothed conditioning and precisely the needed tube bound.** Dunagan, Spielman, and Teng, *Smoothed Analysis of Condition Numbers and Complexity Implications for Linear Programming*, [March 30, 2009 manuscript](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/spielman/Research/lpcond.pdf), Theorem 2.3.3 and Lemma 2.3.4, derive inside and outside Gaussian boundary-tube probabilities, each at most `4 m^(1/4) d/sigma`, from Ball's Gaussian surface-area theorem. Their Section 2 treats fixed convex cones, while the full result studies Renegar conditioning under perturbations of linear data. Their definition of ill-posedness records feasibility changes. The proposed RHS result is not identical: it fixes nonlinear convex slices, tracks every slice, and controls an exact-penalty threshold. Its Gaussian probability step is nevertheless a direct application of their lemma followed by a union bound. Section 1.3 also explicitly notes adaptability of parts of their method to sphere/cube distributions.

Ball's dimension order is not a new geometric estimate. Nadimpalli and Pascale, [*On the Maximal Gaussian Perimeter of Convex Sets, Revisited*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.20079), Theorems 2–3, restates Ball's upper bound and Nazarov's matching order, and gives a new treatment of the latter construction. This recent source was inspected to check whether the standard `m^(1/4)` comparison remained appropriate; no sharp constant is needed here.

**Perturbed exact penalties and stability.** Yang and Ralph, *Characterizations for Perturbed Exact Penalty Functions*, Nonlinear Analysis 62 (2005), 101–106, DOI [10.1016/j.na.2005.02.112](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.na.2005.02.112), relates exact `l1` penalties for perturbed inequality problems to continuity of their value functions. The accessible indexed primary text includes Remark 2.2: convex value functions finite near the reference perturbation are locally Lipschitz. Its inspected statements are qualitative, with no finite-disjunction smoothed tail or encoding conclusion. A direct PDF retrieval failed, so this comparison has less documentary coverage than the downloaded papers. Zaslavski's [*Existence of Exact Penalty and its Stability for Inequality-Constrained Optimization Problems*](https://www.heldermann-verlag.de/jca/jca16/jca0768_b.pdf), J. Convex Anal. 16 (2009), 261–276, studies stability under perturbations of objectives, constraints, and RHS, including approximate solutions. Its model and claims are stability results, not the proposed random boundary-distance bound.

**Mixed-integer robust feasibility has different quantifiers.** Liers, Schewe, and Thürauf, [*Radius of Robust Feasibility for Mixed-Integer Problems*](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/166588346/final_radius_robust_feasibility_IJOC_style.pdf), INFORMS J. Computing 34 (2022), 243–261, Section 2, studies existence of one point satisfying every allowed data perturbation. By contrast, `B(b,d) subset C_z` permits a different continuous point for each perturbed RHS. A nontrivially perturbed equality cannot have a positive static robust radius. The paper's Theorem 2(i) concerns equality of mixed-integer and continuous-relaxation robust radii when the latter is unattained; Lemma 14 gives attainment under compactness. These are not the proposed penalty or smoothing statements. An independent subreview checked this comparison and also inspected Goberna–Jeyakumar–Li–Vicente-Pérez's [2014 author revision](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~gyli/papers/updated_gjlp2_14.03.20.pdf), Theorem 2.3, which connects that static robust radius with an epigraphical distance.

**Adjacent randomized exactness work.** Friedlander, Kubal, Plan, and Scott, [*Average-case Thresholds for Exact Regularization of Linear Programs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13083), studies Gaussian random objective vectors for a fixed polytope and probabilities of preserving an LP optimum under added regularization. Its geometric objects are shifted normal cones. This is relevant evidence that probabilistic exactness thresholds are an active established formulation, but it is not an equivalent finite-union RHS penalty theorem. Likewise, Bhojanapalli, Boumal, Jain, and Netrapalli, [*Smoothed Analysis for Factored, Penalized SDPs*](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v75/bhojanapalli18a/bhojanapalli18a.pdf), studies perturbed cost matrices and approximate second-order points of a quadratic-penalty factorization, not exact norm-penalty encoding. Only the latter paper's abstract and main-result discussion were inspected.

## Limitations and counterexamples

1. **Convexity of each objective is necessary for the stated argument.** Bounded objective range by itself is insufficient. On the single slice `X=[-1,1]`, `A=1`, `b=0`, take the continuous nonconvex objective `f(x)=-sqrt(abs(x))`. The image-boundary distance is one and the range is one, but no finite linear norm penalty is exact: sufficiently small nonzero `x` gives `-sqrt(abs(x))+rho*abs(x)<0`.

2. **Equality at the threshold need not exclude infeasible minimizers.** Let a feasible slice have image `[-1,1]` and constant cost one; let a second slice have image `{1}` and cost zero; take `b=0`. Then `M=d=1`. At `rho=1`, the infeasible singleton ties with the feasible optimum. Strictly larger penalties rule out these ties.

3. **Conditioning on feasibility changes the probability claim.** If `p=P[feasible]>0`, the conditional bad-event bound obtained from an unconditional bound `epsilon` is at most `epsilon/p`. No uniform positive lower bound on `p` follows from compactness and convexity. If every slice image is lower-dimensional, `p=0` under full-dimensional continuous noise. Wording such as “with probability at least `1-epsilon`, the perturbed problem is infeasible or has the stated penalty bound” is accurate.

4. **Expected numerical penalties can be infinite.** Consider two slices in residual coordinate `y`: `[-1,1]` at cost zero, and `{0}` at cost minus one. For every `b` strictly between minus one and one with `b!=0`, the primal value is zero. The zero-multiplier threshold is exactly `1/abs(b)`. The threshold after optimizing over all multipliers is exactly `1/(2*abs(b))`: exactness on the baseline slice forces `abs(lambda)<=rho`, while the singleton requires `-1-lambda*b+rho*abs(b)>=0`. The choice `lambda=-rho*sign(b)` attains the bound. Thus uniform `b` on `[-1,1]` gives infinite expected numerical thresholds, even with two polyhedral slices. Their logarithms are integrable. The native model can be written with one binary `q`, `-(1-q)<=y<=1-q`, and objective `-q`.

5. **Linear numerical dependence on the slice count can be necessary.** Put low-cost singleton images at `j/N`, `j=0,...,N`, all with cost minus one, and add the full interval `[0,1]` with cost zero. There are `K=N+2` slices. For any non-grid `b` between two neighboring grid points, let their residual magnitudes be `u,v>0`, with `u+v=1/N`. A balanced mixture of the two singleton points has average cost minus one and average absolute residual `2uv/(u+v)<=1/(2N)`. Therefore every exact optimized-multiplier penalty satisfies `rho>=2N`. This holds for almost every uniform sample in `[0,1]`. The result can support polynomial **bit length** in `log K`; it cannot generally support polynomial numerical size in the number of binary variables.

6. **Rational grids need a quantitative rounding term.** A fixed finite grid has atoms that may lie exactly on slice boundaries. A valid transfer from continuous sampling must account for a rounding displacement `eta`, replacing the boundary tube width `d` by at least `d+eta`. For a cube grid, coupling each sampled cell center to a uniform point of its cell is a natural proof. “Almost surely off the boundary” is not a grid proof. Bit claims also require explicit bounds on `log K`, `log M`, `log(1/sigma)`, the failure probability, and grid resolution.

7. **The perturbation changes the optimization problem.** Exactness is for the perturbed equality, not the original equality. The theorem provides no guarantee that the original optimal integer assignment survives. Computing the common margin may require exploring all slices. Solving the penalized mixed-integer problem may remain hard even when a short sufficient penalty is available.

## Assessment and next useful questions

A defensible contribution claim is a representation-independent smoothed encoding bound for exact norm penalization of bounded finite unions of convex slices, with a stated RHS perturbation model and explicit feasibility qualification. The deterministic margin estimate, Gaussian tube theorem, and finite union step should be attributed or described as elementary. The completed search did not reveal the identical combined theorem, but that does not establish novelty.

The result becomes more useful if connected to a solver or modeling task that needs a short penalty certificate and naturally tolerates RHS uncertainty. A stronger direction would replace all-slice boundary avoidance by objective-relevant geometry, or prove a feasible-instance conditional guarantee under meaningful recourse assumptions. Those improvements would need new arguments; counting every slice and perturbing through infeasibility may be too pessimistic or too permissive for some applications.
