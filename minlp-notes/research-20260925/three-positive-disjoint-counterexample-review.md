# Independent adversarial review of the three-positive disjoint-SDP counterexample

Date: 25 September 2026.

The candidate passes this review. The quadratic is nonnegative on the cube,
and the supplied rational moments satisfy all 27 disjoint-multiplier LMIs
strictly while giving objective value `-1/40`. Thus this is an exact
counterexample, rather than a numerical indication of a gap. The separate
argument excluding a disjoint affine-square certificate is also correct.

This review addresses correctness and the precise relaxation being refuted.
It does not establish that the result is new in the literature.

## Quadratic and nonnegativity

Consider

\[
p(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+9z^2+6xy-12xz-12yz-x-y+9z+\tfrac14.
\]

Its quadratic matrix is

\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&3&-6\\3&1&-6\\-6&-6&9\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Every two-coordinate principal restriction is indefinite: its determinant is
`-8`, `-27`, or `-27`. A cube minimizer exists by compactness. If a minimizer
were in the relative interior of a face of dimension at least two, the
second-order necessary condition in that face would require its quadratic
restriction to be positive semidefinite. Any two free coordinates contradict
that requirement. Consequently, every cube minimizer lies on an edge.

The exact edge minima are as follows. In the middle column, the fixed values
are listed in the order of the other two coordinates.

| Free coordinate | Fixed coordinates | Minimum on the edge |
|---|---|---:|
| `x` | `(y,z)=(0,0)` | `0` |
| `x` | `(y,z)=(0,1)` | `25/4` |
| `x` | `(y,z)=(1,0)` | `1/4` |
| `x` | `(y,z)=(1,1)` | `1/4` |
| `y` | `(x,z)=(0,0)` | `0` |
| `y` | `(x,z)=(0,1)` | `25/4` |
| `y` | `(x,z)=(1,0)` | `1/4` |
| `y` | `(x,z)=(1,1)` | `1/4` |
| `z` | `(x,y)=(0,0)` | `1/4` |
| `z` | `(x,y)=(0,1)` | `0` |
| `z` | `(x,y)=(1,0)` | `0` |
| `z` | `(x,y)=(1,1)` | `0` |

This proves `min p = 0` on `[0,1]^3`. The five reported zeros are

\[
A=(1/2,0,0),\quad B=(0,1/2,0),\quad
C=(1,0,1/6),\quad D=(0,1,1/6),\quad E=(1,1,5/6).
\]

All were checked by exact substitution. The second-order argument is needed:
checking only the edges would not prove nonnegativity for an arbitrary
quadratic.

## Exact meaning of the relaxation

For a partition `R,I,J` of `{1,2,3}`, define

\[
f_{I,J}(x)=\prod_{i\in I}x_i\prod_{j\in J}(1-x_j),
\qquad v_R(x)=(1,(x_i)_{i\in R}).
\]

The LMI is `L(f_{I,J} v_R v_R^T) >= 0`, where `L` is the moment
functional. There are `3^3=27` such matrices. Their entries use exactly the
20 monomials consisting of all squarefree monomials and all monomials with
one squared coordinate and the other coordinates of degree at most one.
The constant moment is fixed at one, leaving 19 free moment coordinates.

I checked this definition against Theorem 3, equation (17), of
[Khajavirad, *Tight semidefinite programming relaxations for sparse
box-constrained quadratic programs*, v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18545v2#S3).
For `P=V={1,2,3}` and `M` empty, it is the full displayed relaxation.
The quadratic has three positive diagonal coefficients and every pair is
an edge, so its graph is the complete graph with three plus loops.

The claim does not concern every possible SDP relaxation or every possible
moment multiplier. Multipliers overlapping the affine-square variables are
outside the definition above.

## Independent exclusion of disjoint affine-square certificates

Let `C` be the cone generated by

\[
g(x)=f_{I,J}(x)\ell(x_R)^2,
\]

where `R,I,J` are disjoint and `ell` is affine in the free coordinates.
Allowing unused coordinates does not affect the argument.

Suppose `p` were a finite sum of these generators. Every generator is
nonnegative on the cube. Since `p` vanishes at `A,B,C,E`, every generator
in the sum must vanish at those four points. Since `p(0,0,0)=1/4`, at least
one generator is positive at the origin. Fix that generator and write
`c=ell(0)`, so `c` is nonzero.

Positivity at the origin excludes every factor `x_i`. Thus all its factors
are of the form `1-x_i`. These factors are positive at `A` and `B`, so
`ell(A)=ell(B)=0`. If `x` were not free, `ell(A)=ell(0)` would be nonzero.
Hence `x` is free, and its affine coefficient is `-2c`. The same reasoning
forces `y` to be free with coefficient `-2c`.

At `(1,0,0)`, the affine form has value `-c`; its complementary factors
are also positive because neither `x` nor `y` can occur in them. If `z`
were not free, then at `C=(1,0,1/6)` the affine form would still equal
`-c`, and any factor `1-z` would equal `5/6`. That would make the generator
positive at a zero of `p`. Therefore `z` is free. Vanishing at `C` then
forces its coefficient to be `6c`.

All coordinates are now free, so there are no factors and

\[
\ell(x,y,z)=c(1-2x-2y+6z).
\]

But `ell(E)=2c`, a contradiction. Therefore `p` does not belong to `C`.
The zero `D` is unnecessary for this exclusion. Equivalently, the four
points `A,B,C,E` affinely span three-dimensional space; the determinant of
their augmented coordinate matrix is `1/12`.

This proof uses disjointness at two essential steps: a variable appearing
in a factor cannot also appear in the affine form, and forcing every
coordinate to be free removes all factors. Dropping disjointness requires
a separate analysis.

## Closure issue and its resolution

Nonmembership in an arbitrary cone of certificates need not by itself yield
a strict separating moment functional: the cone could be nonclosed. Here
the cone is closed, although the explicit rational witness makes this
fact unnecessary for establishing the gap.

There are finitely many multiplier/free-variable choices. Write a
certificate using positive semidefinite Gram matrices `A_r`:

\[
\sum_r f_r v_r^T A_r v_r.
\]

For each choice,

\[
K_r=\int_{[0,1]^3} f_r(x)v_r(x)v_r(x)^T\,dx
\]

is positive definite: the multiplier is positive in the cube interior,
and a nonzero affine polynomial cannot vanish throughout that interior.
If a sequence of certificate polynomials converges in coefficient space,
their integrals are bounded. Each summand contributes the nonnegative
quantity `tr(A_r K_r)`, which bounds `tr(A_r)` below by a positive constant
times that trace. Thus every Gram matrix is bounded. A subsequence of the
finite tuple of Gram matrices converges, preserving positive
semidefiniteness and representing the limiting polynomial. Hence the cone
is closed.

Separation would consequently yield a feasible functional with negative
value on `p`. If its constant moment were zero, adding a sufficiently
small positive multiple of the uniform-distribution functional would make
the constant moment positive while preserving the negative objective;
normalization would finish the construction. The rational table avoids
relying on this existence argument.

## Assessment

The exact gap is the decisive result. The zero-propagation argument explains
why the disjoint construction fails and can guide searches for other
instances. Neither result proves that the three-positive hull has no
semidefinite representation. Literature priority and the relationship to
other known three-variable quadratic inequalities require their own audit.
