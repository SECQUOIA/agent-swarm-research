\section{Changing the certificate representation}
\label{sec:decomposition}

A spatial tree describes its final certificate by boxes in all the original
variables. A decomposition certificate can reuse local boxes across many
combinations of those variables. The distinction matters even when both
certificates use exactly the same factor relaxations. We show this on a
nonconvex path problem with a unique, nondegenerate interior minimum: every
spatial certificate is exponentially large in the dimension, whereas a
decomposition certificate has size $O(n\log(n/\epsilon))$.
The comparison concerns certificate size. It does not charge the work of
finding the minimizer or identify a local convex program with a spatial
branch-and-bound node.

Decomposition and the reuse of independent subproblems are established
ideas in AND/OR search and branch-and-bound
\citep{marinescuDechter2009AndOrBranchAndBound}.
Width also controls approximate formulations for polynomial optimization
\citep{bienstockMunoz2018LpFormulations}.
Our purpose here is to isolate what changes when the local relaxation is
fixed and the representation of its certificate changes.

\subsection{Local boxes and separator minorants}

In this section there are no constraints beyond a compact, nondegenerate
box $D=\prod_{i=1}^n[L_i,U_i]$. Write
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:factorization}
 f(x)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}f_c(x_c),\qquad
 f_* =\min_D f,
\end{equation}
where $c\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ is a factor's coordinate set and
$f_c$ is continuous on $D_c$. The
factorization is fixed. For each factor and box $B_c$, the local oracle
supplies a continuous convex function $f_{c,B_c}\le f_c$ on $B_c$.
The spatial oracle is
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:spatial-oracle}
 L(B)=\min_{x\in B}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}f_{c,B_c}(x_c).
\end{equation}

Let $T$ be a rooted tree with root $r$ and bags $V_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$.
Every factor is assigned to one bag containing its coordinates. The bags
cover all variables, and the bags containing any one variable form a
connected subtree. Put
\[
 a_t=\sum_{c\text{ assigned to }t}f_c,\qquad
 S_t=V_t\cap V_{p(t)}\quad(t\ne r),\qquad S_r=\varnothing,
\]
where $p(t)$ is the parent. The separator $S_t$ contains the coordinates
shared with the parent. We take $|V_t|\le w+1$ and $|S_t|\le w$.
Let $M=|T|$, let $k$ be the maximum number of bags containing one variable,
and let $\Delta$ be the maximum number of children of a bag. Width alone
does not bound $k$.

For the subtree rooted at $t$, let $\phi_t(s)$ be its exact minimum with
$x_{S_t}=s$. Connectedness of the bags containing each variable gives the
recursion
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:exact-recursion}
 \phi_t(s)=\min_{z\in D_{V_t}:z_{S_t}=s}
 \left\{a_t(z)+\sum_{u\in\operatorname{ch}(t)}\phi_u(z_{S_u})\right\},
 \qquad \phi_r=f_*.
\end{equation}
All these minima exist. The value functions are continuous, since each is
the minimum of a continuous function over a fixed compact product box.

\begin{definition}[Decomposition certificate]\label{decomp:certificate}
For each nonroot bag, choose a rectangular partition $\mathcal P_t$ of
$D_{S_t}$ and an affine function
$\ell_{t,E}(s)=\lambda_{t,E}^{\mathsf T}s+\beta_{t,E}$ for each cell
$E\in\mathcal P_t$. For each bag choose a rectangular partition
$\mathcal L_t$ of $D_{V_t}$ into local leaves. Boxes are closed and their
interiors are disjoint; their shared boundaries are retained in every
applicable test.

For every local leaf $B\in\mathcal L_t$ and child $u$, choose a continuous
convex function $\psi_{u,B}$ on $B_{S_u}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:child-test}
 \psi_{u,B}(s)\le\ell_{u,E}(s)
 \quad(s\in B_{S_u}\cap E,\ E\in\mathcal P_u).
\end{equation}
Define
\[
 R_{t,B}(z)=\sum_{c\text{ assigned to }t}f_{c,B_c}(z_c)
            +\sum_{u\in\operatorname{ch}(t)}\psi_{u,B}(z_{S_u}).
\]
Require
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:leaf-test}
 R_{t,B}(z)\ge\ell_{t,E}(z_{S_t})
 \quad(z\in B,\ z_{S_t}\in E)
\end{equation}
for every nonempty leaf--cell intersection. At the root require
$R_{r,B}\ge\ell_r$ on every leaf, for a scalar $\ell_r$.
The size is
\[
 N_{\mathrm{dec}}=\sum_t|\mathcal L_t|+
                         \sum_{t\ne r}|\mathcal P_t|.
\]
The certificate proves tolerance $\epsilon$ if $\ell_r\ge f_*-\epsilon$.
With a feasible incumbent $U$, the operational test is
$\ell_r\ge U-\epsilon$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Validity and reuse]\label{decomp:validity}
Every certificate satisfies $\ell_{t,E}\le\phi_t$ on $E$ and
$\ell_r\le f_*$. Moreover, for each $x\in D$ there are local leaves
$B_t\ni x_{V_t}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:chain}
 \ell_r\le\sum_t\sum_{c\text{ assigned to }t}f_{c,(B_t)_c}(x_c).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct upward from the terminal bags. For $s\in E$, choose a minimizing
$z$ in \eqref{decomp:exact-recursion} and a local leaf containing it.
The child tests and the induction hypothesis give
$\psi_{u,B}(z_{S_u})\le\phi_u(z_{S_u})$. Since factor relaxations are
underestimators, the leaf test gives
$\ell_{t,E}(s)\le R_{t,B}(z)\le\phi_t(s)$. The root is identical.

For \eqref{decomp:chain}, start at the root with a leaf containing
$x_{V_r}$. Replace each child bound by the minorant of a cell containing
$x_{S_u}$, using \eqref{decomp:child-test}, and then use the leaf test in a
child leaf containing $x_{V_u}$. Continue down the tree. Every cell
minorant cancels with the corresponding child term.
\end{proof}

There can be exponentially many compatible combinations of local leaves.
The certificate stores their local members and the separator inequalities
that justify their reuse, rather than listing every combination.

\subsection{A small certificate under quadratic growth}

The existence result uses one slope $\lambda_t$ on every cell of separator
$S_t$. Given the intercepts of its children, set
\begin{align}
 \psi_{u,B}(s)&=\lambda_u^{\mathsf T}s+
       \min_{E\in\mathcal P_u:E\cap B_{S_u}\ne\varnothing}\beta_{u,E},
       \label{decomp:common-slope-child}\\
 \beta_{t,E}&=\min_{B\in\mathcal L_t}\;
       \min_{z\in B:z_{S_t}\in E}
       \{R_{t,B}(z)-\lambda_t^{\mathsf T}z_{S_t}\},
       \label{decomp:intercepts}
\end{align}
omitting empty intersections. At the root take
$\ell_r=\min_{B\in\mathcal L_r}\min_B R_{r,B}$.
These choices satisfy the certificate tests.

\begin{theorem}[Certificate existence]\label{decomp:small-certificate}
Assume $w\ge1$, $k\ge2$, and the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $f(x)-f_*\ge c_g\|x-x^*\|_2^2$ on $D$, for $c_g>0$ and $x^*\in D$;
\item each $a_t$ is $C^1$ on a neighborhood of $D_{V_t}$ and has an
      $H_a$-Lipschitz gradient on that box, for a positive constant $H_a$;
\item $0\le f_c(z)-f_{c,B_c}(z)\le\alpha' q_{B_c}(z)$, where
      $q_{B_c}(z)=\sum_{i\in c}(z_i-l_i)(u_i-z_i)$.
      Let $A$ bound the sum of the coordinate-set sizes of the relaxed
      factors assigned to any one bag.
\end{enumerate}
Put
\begin{align*}
 D_k&=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Delta^j,&
 K_1&=\max\left\{1,(k-1)\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}\Delta^j\right\},\\
 Q&=\frac{H_a^2wk}{c_g}+\frac{H_aw}{2}+\alpha'A,&
 H_0&=768K_1Q+3\alpha'A.
\end{align*}
Choose $\theta=2^{-\mu}$ for an integer $\mu\ge1$ so that
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:theta-conditions}
 4\theta\sqrt{(k-1)D_k}\le\frac12,
 \qquad
 \theta^2k\{48K_1(1+\Delta)Q+\alpha'A\}\le\frac{c_g}{2}.
\end{equation}
For any $h>0$ with $h^2\le\epsilon/(4MH_0)$, there is a certificate of size
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:size-bound}
 N_{\mathrm{dec}}\le
 2M(4/\theta)^{w+1}(J+1),\qquad
 J=\max\{0,\lceil\log_2(s_0/h)\rceil\},
 \quad s_0=\max_i(U_i-L_i).
\end{equation}
It uses partitions centered at $x^*$ and slopes
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:gradient-slopes}
 \lambda_{t,i}=
 \sum_{u\in\operatorname{sub}(t):i\in V_u}
              \partial_i a_u(x^*_{V_u})\qquad(i\in S_t).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:decomp:existence}. The construction gives
each local box $B$ width at most $h+\theta\dist_\infty(B,x^*)$.
Separator copies can disagree, but bounded variable occurrence controls
the accumulated disagreement. The slopes in
\eqref{decomp:gradient-slopes} cancel its first-order cost. The remaining
error is quadratic and can be paid by the growth margin. No smoothness of
the exact value functions is required, and $x^*$ may lie on the boundary.

For fixed $k,\Delta,c_g,H_a,\alpha'A$ and $w$, choosing
$h^2=\epsilon/(4MH_0)$ gives $O(M\log(M/\epsilon))$ local members as
$\epsilon\downarrow0$. Formula \eqref{decomp:size-bound} displays the
exponential dependence on width and the conditioning constants. It is an
existence result with a known center and slopes. Locating this center and
constructing the certificate without it are separate algorithmic questions.

\subsection{An exponential separation with the same oracle}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed termwise path oracle]\label{decomp:path-separation}
For $n\ge3$, let $D=[-1,1]^n$ and
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:path-objective}
 f_n(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i^2-\tfrac1{10}x_i^4)
            +\tfrac45\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_ix_{i+1}.
\end{equation}
Keep each unary factor exact. Relax each bilinear factor $\tfrac45xy$ by
the termwise alphaBB function
\[
 \tfrac45xy-\tfrac25\{(x-l_x)(u_x-x)+(y-l_y)(u_y-y)\}.
\]
This is a convex underestimator. The objective is nonconvex, but has the
unique, nondegenerate interior minimizer $x^*=0$, with $f_*=0$.

Every spatial cover whose boxes pass \eqref{decomp:spatial-oracle} has size
at least
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:path-lower}
 \max\left\{
 0.068\sqrt n(2e/\pi)^{n/2}\log\frac{0.2}{n\epsilon},\quad
 (5/3)^n\exp\left[-\frac59(1+\tfrac54\epsilon)\right]
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The first expression is useful when $\epsilon<0.2/n$.
Consequently this bound applies to $N_{\mathrm{cover}}$, and hence to
$N_{\mathrm{rect}}$ and $N_{\mathrm{tree}}$.

With path bags $V_t=\{t,t+1\}$, the same factor relaxations admit a
decomposition certificate of size at most
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:path-upper}
 U_n(\epsilon)=3.36\,10^7(n-1)
 \left\{\frac12\log_2\frac{1.96\,10^6(n-1)}{\epsilon}+2\right\}
 \qquad(0<\epsilon\le10^{-4}).
\end{equation}
There is an absolute $c>0$ such that, throughout this accuracy range,
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:uniform-ratio}
 \frac{N_{\mathrm{cover}}(\epsilon)}{U_n(\epsilon)}
       \ge c\,\frac{(2e/\pi)^{n/2}}{\sqrt n}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Appendix~\ref{app:decomp:path} proves the assertions and both spatial lower
bounds. Each valid spatial box must absorb the summed gaps of all the
separately relaxed bilinear terms. A local path certificate absorbs those
gaps in two-dimensional leaves and reuses the separator information.
Even an optimal choice of spatial cuts cannot remove the lower bound.
For a binary spatial tree with no additional reductions, $N$ terminal
boxes correspond to $2N-1$ nodes. With tightening, the appropriate bound
is on the owned certification events described in the certificate
framework, including the certified removed regions; it is not a bound on
leaves alone.

The same size distinction holds for termwise McCormick envelopes in both
models. The second expression in \eqref{decomp:path-lower} and the upper
bound \eqref{decomp:path-upper} still apply, as shown in the appendix.
Thus at each fixed tolerance in the stated range the ratio is exponential
in $n$.
The alphaBB integral bound, and hence the uniform ratio
\eqref{decomp:uniform-ratio}, are not claimed for McCormick envelopes.

\subsection{Size, queries, and checking work}

The number of stored local members differs from the work needed to check
them. For one nonroot leaf--cell pair, \eqref{decomp:leaf-test} is a convex
minimization problem of dimension at most $w+1$; a root leaf needs one such
problem. For the common-slope construction, the child tests reduce to
comparisons of intercepts. If $l_t=|\mathcal L_t|$ and
$p_t=|\mathcal P_t|$, the number of local convex checks is at most
\begin{equation}\label{decomp:checking-count}
 l_r+\sum_{t\ne r}l_tp_t
 \le M(4/\theta)^{2w+1}(J+1)^2.
\end{equation}
Only intersecting pairs need checking. If the leaves are aligned so that
each separator projection meets at most a fixed number of cells, the
number of checks is instead proportional to the number of leaves. The
shell construction need not have that alignment: requiring it may refine
the leaf partitions. Its unconditional checking bound therefore contains
an additional logarithm.

The oracle in \eqref{decomp:spatial-oracle} supplies a factor relaxation
on a specified box. One such query for each assigned factor and local
leaf suffices: its returned function is reused on all cell intersections.
If at most $R$ factors are assigned to a bag, there are at most
$R\sum_tl_t$ such queries. This is $O(M\log(M/\epsilon))$ for fixed
parameters. If an oracle instead supplies only the result of a local
minimization on each requested leaf--cell intersection, unaligned queries
are counted by \eqref{decomp:checking-count}, giving the safe bound
$O(M\log^2(M/\epsilon))$. Neither count specifies the arithmetic or bit
cost of solving the convex programs.

The factorization and oracle restrictions are substantive. Aggregating
terms, adding stronger cuts, or choosing different functional factors can
change the gap and the certificate size. The theorem is not a lower bound
for every representation of \eqref{decomp:path-objective}, nor a prediction
of a general solver's runtime. It compares two representations of a
certificate under one fixed local relaxation.
