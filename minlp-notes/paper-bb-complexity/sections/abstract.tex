\begin{abstract}
We study the size of an optimality certificate for a fixed relaxation and
specified branch-and-bound operations. For continuous problems with a
quadratic relaxation gap that vanishes at box vertices, we compare minimum
cover, partition, and tree certificates with a running supremum of
near-optimal covering numbers, up to a logarithmic factor. For unconstrained
objectives with Lipschitz gradients and matching vertex-vanishing upper
error, integrals over all positive-dimensional root-box faces characterize
these sizes within constant factors. Analytic sublevel asymptotics then give
polynomial and logarithmic rates, including rates governed by boundary
faces. We also analyze McCormick exact faces, relaxed constraints,
objective-cutoff propagation, and branching decisions. A fixed path family
separates spatial and decomposition certificate sizes exponentially in
dimension while preserving the local relaxation; one-dimensional
relaxation-minimizer branching is 4-competitive under an exact-gap oracle.
For integer problems, a convex-class covering number gives a
relaxation-dependent lower bound and an exact semantic certificate size.
Gaussian sparse regression exhibits linear certificates below root
exactness. Gaussian binary least squares has matching certificate-size laws
at divergent sublinear signal-to-noise ratio and distinct thresholds for a
sufficient linear-tree criterion and equality of box and orthant relaxations. A finite
nonnegative least-squares coefficient bound proves the simultaneous
criterion for square and tall systems. We distinguish certificate size,
search nodes, relaxation evaluations, and propagation rounds throughout;
archived computations illustrate the mechanisms and their practical limits.
\end{abstract}
