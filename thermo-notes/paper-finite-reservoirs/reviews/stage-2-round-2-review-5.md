2. **Positive matching-label derivative proof.** The displayed summand logarithm follows from BCT (6.13)–(6.14). Its label volumes, contour intersections, component multiplicity, and admissible-family set do not depend on temperature. Compatibility and the fixed lattice geometry bound the total number of internal contour intersections by a constant times interior volume plus boundary-contour size. The volume inequality in BCT Lemma 5.7 then gives `V_gamma = O(m_gamma^2)`, including torus interiors. Differentiating the positive sum yields an expectation for its first logarithmic derivative and expectation plus variance for its second. Consequently the two stated polynomial activity derivative bounds follow. No identification with a simply connected infinite-lattice random-cluster partition function is needed.

3. **Stable branches on both sides.** BCT Lemma A.3 identifies whether the ordered truncated free energy minimizes through the spontaneous magnetization. Below coexistence this forces the disordered branch to minimize; above coexistence the ordered branch minimizes. Continuity gives equality at coexistence. This reasoning is internal to the original construction and does not require derivatives of a minimum cutoff.

4. **Identification of the physical limiting pressure.** I checked BCT (6.25)–(6.27) and Appendix A. On a full torus, the boundary factor in Lemma A.1(ii) is absent and its maximum is at most one. The lower bound (A.9) applies to a minimizing phase. The interface-network estimate uses the common minimum free energy, the Appendix A upper bounds, contour counting, and the stated bound on `q`; it does not use ordered-phase minimization. Its derivation therefore applies in the sufficiently small two-sided neighborhood specified in the revision. The exponentially small finite-volume error can be absorbed in the interface suppression for fixed dimension and large size. These upper and lower bounds identify `min_i f_i^tr` with the physical random-cluster free energy. The explicit normalization `Z_RC = exp(-d beta N) Z_spin` then gives the displayed stable-side identification with the smooth spin branches.

5. **Cluster summability and cutoff removal.** The BCT truncated activities retain enough exponential decay to allow a fixed exponential factor in total cluster size. The anchored cluster bounds sum to order `N`. Polynomial first-derivative factors are absorbed by this margin, establishing uniform Lipschitz bounds through almost-everywhere differentiation of the minimum. This step precedes the cutoff removal and uses no second derivative of a cutoff. Lipschitz continuity and equality of the branches at coexistence make the inactive-cutoff criterion hold for every torus contour in a window of order `1/L`. Only on this window does the proof differentiate twice, using the original smooth activities and the polynomial fourth-order cluster bound. I find no circularity.

6. **Restricted energy center and moment transfer.** The stable-side finite difference of width proportional to `1/sqrt(N)` lies inside the cutoff-free window for all fixed `d >= 2`. The second-derivative bound yields the stated `O(sqrt(N))` centering error; dividing the exponentially small partition error by this displacement remains negligible. The conditional binomial noise bound, divided by the positive phase probability, and Cauchy–Schwarz transfer this to the physical spin energy. The transfer does not assume that the binomial law survives conditioning on the bond-phase event.
