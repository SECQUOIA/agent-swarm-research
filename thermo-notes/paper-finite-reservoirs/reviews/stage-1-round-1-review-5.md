## Mathematical checks

1. **Exact marginal and accuracy criterion.** Direct division of the microcanonical marginal density by the canonical density gives the stated energy-only likelihood. Its boundedness for every finite total energy follows from exponential decay at negative infinite subsystem energy and the positive power at the upper cutoff. The admissibility criterion is exact. Taking the pushforward preserves this likelihood, so the full-state and energy-law total variation distances are equal, rather than merely bounded by data processing. The normalization lemma has the correct normalization factor.

2. **Heat-capacity convention.** Differentiation of the specified surface entropy gives the stated microcanonical temperature and heat capacity. The canonical Gamma distribution gives the separate `c+1` convention. The text distinguishes these quantities explicitly and does not use their finite difference as an asymptotic approximation inside a proof.

3. **Two-center calibration and interpolation.** Solving equality of the endpoint likelihoods gives the displayed composite energy. Both endpoint residual bath energies are positive. Writing `t = beta Delta/c`, their inverse-temperature factors are `(1-exp(-t))/t` and `(exp(t)-1)/t`, which yield endpoint slopes of order `Delta/c`. On the center interval the negative second derivative is bounded by a constant times `1/c`. In the interpolation argument, subtracting the positive quadratic upper bound from `h` indeed produces a convex function with zero endpoint values, hence a nonpositive function throughout the interval. The direction of this comparison is correct. Concavity also supplies the exterior bound and both tangent bounds. The phase-window Taylor estimate includes both the linear `Delta s/c` term and the quadratic `s^2/c` term and establishes cutoff feasibility under the stated hypotheses.

4. **Chord inequality.** I differentiated the displayed chord function independently. Its second derivative is `theta(1-theta) exp(q)/((1-theta) exp(q)+theta)^2`. For `0 <= q <= 1`, this is at least `theta(1-theta)/e`. Twice integrating yields the displayed quadratic lower bound. Convexity with zero value at the origin extends it to the linear lower bound for `q >= 1`. The constant is uniform even as `theta` approaches zero or one, after retaining the explicit factor `theta(1-theta)`. The endpoint and chord identities have the correct signs and interpolation weights.

5. **Weak-support equivalence.** For sufficiency, tightness, `c >> b^2`, and the global bound `0 <= W <= 1` suffice; no hidden tail or moment assumption is needed. For necessity, the selected good likelihood energies can be taken from nonempty positive-probability events without assuming atoms or densities. Three separated fixed support neighborhoods bound the interpolation fraction away from both endpoints. The identities imply `cq ~ beta D -> infinity` and `c f_theta(q) -> 0`. The chord bound first forces `q -> 0` and then `c q^2 -> 0`, giving `D^2/c -> 0`. Thus the necessity covers arbitrary admissible total-energy tuning, including tunings whose cutoffs would otherwise remove appreciable canonical probability. The two-atom exception follows exactly from equality of the two likelihood values and does not incorrectly extend to a two-atom scaling limit.

6. **Two-scale necessity.** Positive component weights transfer small unconditional bad-set probability to each phase. The two within-phase selections and the other-phase selection give outer gap of order `Delta` and interpolation factor of order `s/Delta`. Substituting `q ~ Delta/c` into the uniform chord bound gives a positive constant times `min(Delta s/c, s)`. Because `s` diverges, convergence of this expression to zero implies `Delta s/c -> 0`. This argument remains valid before one knows whether `q` is small and does not silently use a Taylor expansion at an uncontrolled scale. Interchanging the two phases gives the same conclusion.

7. **Positive-decomposition sufficiency and equivalence.** The exponential moment yields tightness and an eventually uniform second-moment bound for the reweighting factor, hence the necessary uniform integrability. The exceptional-component estimate is valid for any positive exceptional measure, regardless of its energy support. The surface-scale exceptional-mass assumption implies the required amplification bound when `c >> Delta s`; the moment condition also supplies the other-phase concentration needed for necessity. The generalized exceptional-mass rate is presented only as sufficient, as warranted by its use of the global amplification maximum.

8. **Density-envelope sufficiency.** I checked both gain-to-cost comparisons. With `lambda = kappa N^(3/2)`, the Gaussian comparison is bounded by a constant times `lambda/R`, and the interfacial comparison by a constant times `lambda N^(1/2-alpha)`. Both vanish for the entire stated range `alpha in [1/2,1]`, including the endpoint `alpha=1/2`. The bound involving the minimum of the two costs is handled correctly by summing two exponentials. The Gaussian integral leaves a tail tending to zero as `R` increases. The other integral has finite integral over the positive half-line and carries the prefactor `N^(-1/2)`, so it vanishes already at fixed positive `R`. Exterior energies cannot gain weight under the concavity and zero-endpoint conditions. The two successive limits, first in `N` and then in `R`, prove the claimed `L^1` convergence.

9. **Local densities to conditional weak limits.** The final physical equivalence does not infer global tightness from one local peak alone. Both local limits have positive weights summing to one, and their bounded phase windows become disjoint. Consequently the probability outside the union of these windows vanishes in the iterated limit. Splitting at the midpoint then gives limiting phase probabilities `w_i`; division by these probabilities turns the local Gaussian mass limits into the stated conditional weak Gaussian limits. This supplies exactly the hypotheses of the two-scale necessity theorem.
