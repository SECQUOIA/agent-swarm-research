## Issue

### R4.1 — Total variation does not by itself control an unbounded mean

- **Severity:** minor; local correction, with no change to a theorem or proof.
- **Location:** `sections/framework.tex`, lines 96–100, especially “Agreement of a mean energy, a pressure, or a fixed local observable is a weaker statement.” The opening abstract has a related comparison but does not itself make an implication claim.
- **Problem:** Describing agreement of a mean energy as a weaker statement can suggest that full-law total-variation convergence always implies agreement of the means. This is false for unbounded energies, which the framework expressly allows. Total variation controls uniformly bounded observables; control of unbounded observables additionally requires a tail condition such as uniform integrability under the relevant laws. The intended converse—that matching a few observables need not determine the full law—is correct.
- **Adversarial example within this reservoir framework:** Fix beta equal to one. Put canonical probabilities `(1-1/N)/3` at energies `0, N, 2N`, and `1/N` at energy `N^4`. This is a legitimate canonical law by choosing the counting reference weights proportional to `p(e) exp(e)`. With center `a_N=N`, scale `b_N=N`, and bath exponent `c_N=N^3`, the scaled law converges to three equally weighted atoms. The centered bath eliminates the rare `N^4` atom and the theorem correctly gives total variation tending to zero. Nevertheless, the difference of the mean energies is asymptotic to `N^3`. Thus even the theorem's hypotheses do not supply mean control.
- **Proposed correction:** Say that matching a mean or a few observables does not generally determine full-state total variation, and add one sentence: “Conversely, total variation controls uniformly bounded observables; convergence of unbounded observables such as the energy requires additional uniform-integrability assumptions.” If the intended quantity is an energy density, the same qualification remains necessary in this general framework.

## Mathematical checks performed

1. **Exact physical law and heat capacity:** Re-derived the subsystem likelihood from the power-law density of states. For each fixed finite total energy its likelihood is bounded, even on an unbounded negative-energy half-line. Checked surface heat capacity `k_B c_N` against canonical Gamma heat capacity `k_B(c_N+1)`. The distinction is correct, and the specification that only total energy is tuned is clear.
2. **Full-state versus energy-law TV:** Checked the pushforward Radon–Nikodym calculation directly. It requires no regular conditional probability or density assumption. It correctly exploits an energy-only likelihood, rather than asserting equivalence of arbitrary state and energy laws.
3. **Centered and secant calibration:** Re-derived both exact calibrations and all derivative signs. The centered weight is globally at most one. The secant weight is nonnegative in log between centers and nonpositive outside, and the quadratic interpolation and tangent bounds have the correct signs. Fixed phase neighborhoods lie below the cutoff in the asserted regimes.
4. **Three-support necessity:** Verified the chord identity and its uniform lower bound, including theta approaching zero or one. Checked the good-energy selection from positive-probability open intervals: atoms at selected energies are unnecessary. The argument rules out arbitrary cutoff placements and arbitrary total-energy tunings. In particular, it does not silently assume the total energy lies far above all canonical energies before proving necessity.
5. **Three-support sufficiency:** Tightness together with the global bounded weight suffices; moments and tail bounds really are unnecessary. The proof still works for moving target inverse temperatures converging to a positive finite limit and for heavy-tailed scaled limits. Exactly two energy atoms are a valid exception, not a counterexample to the theorem.
6. **Two-scale necessity:** Checked the asymmetric three-point configuration, where the chord fraction is of order `s_N/Delta_N`. The lower bound reduces to a constant times `min(Delta_N s_N/c_N, s_N)`, yielding the claimed divergence condition. Positive mixture components let unconditional good-set control pass to each phase even with overlapping supports. Vanishing exceptional mass is not needed for this necessary result, correctly.
7. **Positive-phase sufficiency:** The tangent bound and fixed exponential moment give a uniform second-moment bound on the reservoir weights. This justifies uniform integrability under varying phase laws. The exceptional-component estimate is valid and intentionally only sufficient. A signed partition-function remainder cannot replace the positive measure, as the text states.
8. **Density-envelope route:** Checked both gain-to-cost ratios, including the limiting exponent alpha equal to one half. The first gives `O(kappa_N N^(3/2)/R)` and the second `O(kappa_N N^(3/2) N^(1/2-alpha))`; both vanish as used. The exterior needs no envelope because the weight cannot amplify it. Local Gaussian masses summing to one imply tightness around the two centers and justify the conditional phase limits used for necessity.
9. **Units and quantifiers:** The fixed-energy-unit convention and bounded positive limit of beta make the abbreviated asymptotic conditions consistent. The target beta sequence is specified before bath tuning. The theorem's existential tuning and its necessity for every tuning are consistent throughout. The phase sufficient theorem correctly drops the positive-limiting-weight condition used by necessity.

I also evaluated the finite four-atom adversarial example above at N equal to 10, 30, 100, and 300 using direct likelihood normalization. Total variation was respectively 0.1, approximately 0.03333, 0.01, and approximately 0.003333, while the mean-energy differences grew as N cubed. This numerical check supplements the explicit asymptotic calculation; no numerical calculation was used as a substitute for a theorem proof.
