\begin{abstract}
We determine when a finite thermal reservoir reproduces the complete
canonical law of a coexisting subsystem, allowing optimization of the
composite total energy. For a reservoir density of states proportional
to $U^{c_N}$, a weak energy limit on scale $b_N$ with at least three
support points requires and permits total-variation convergence
exactly when $c_N\gg b_N^2$. Two energy phases are exceptional:
a gap $\Delta_N$ and nondegenerate fluctuation scale $s_N$ require
$c_N\gg\Delta_Ns_N$, with matching sufficiency under explicit positive
phase-tail bounds. We verify the resulting $N^{3/2}$ threshold for
mean-field and sufficiently-large-$q$ short-range Potts systems.
Positive contour estimates provide the needed short-range energy
moments, including for plain spins. Under stronger phase-tail and
exceptional-mass assumptions, at the $N^{3/2}$ boundary we derive
the full-state error and optimize it over every composite
energy; an optimum can discard a phase. For two independent copies
sharing a bath, $N\ll c_N\ll N^2$ can preserve both balanced canonical
marginals while enforcing opposite phases, joint total-variation
error $1/2$, and one bit of microscopic mutual information.
Joint canonical accuracy instead requires $c_N\gg N^2$.
Exact Gaussian geometry, smooth-reservoir extensions, and a stated
capillarity model clarify which conclusions depend on phase support,
reservoir curvature, or interfacial tails. Exact finite-system
occupation calculations illustrate the physical thresholds.
\end{abstract}
