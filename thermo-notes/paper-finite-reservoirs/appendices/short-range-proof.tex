\section{Positive contour measures and the short-range theorem}
\label{app:short-range-proof}

This appendix proves Theorem~\ref{thm:sr-bath}.
Section~\ref{app:sr-inputs} collects the published inputs and fixes
the energy normalization. Section~\ref{app:sr-weak} proves weak
Gaussian limits with positive variances for the midpoint energy events,
which gives necessity. Section~\ref{subsubsec:sr-moments} bounds the
second derivatives of the logarithms of the positive restricted contour
partition functions in a window of width $O(1/L)$ around $\beta_c$, and
Section~\ref{app:sr-transfer} transfers the resulting occupied-bond
moment bounds to the spin energy and completes the proof.

The color threshold $q_0(d)$ is fixed here as the largest of:
the thresholds required by the cited finite-size scaling and contour
results \cite{BorgsKoteckyMiracleSole1991,BorgsChayesTetali2012} on
a fixed temperature interval around coexistence; the value $2d+1$,
used for the variance lower bound in
\eqref{eq:sr-variance-lower}; and the value needed for the
cluster-expansion criterion of \cite{BorgsChayesTetali2012} to hold
with the extra factor $e^{\rho m_\gamma}$ in
\eqref{eq:sr-cluster-majorant} for a fixed $\rho>0$. Every
``sufficiently large $q$'' below refers to this threshold.

\subsection{Published finite-volume inputs and normalization}
\label{app:sr-inputs}

Theorem 1 of Borgs, Koteck\'y, and
Miracle-Sol\'e~\cite{BorgsKoteckyMiracleSole1991} (BKMS) gives
six-times differentiable dimensionless free energies $\psi_o,\psi_d$
and a fixed real interval containing $\beta_c$ on which
\begin{equation}
 Z_N^{\mathrm{spin}}(\beta)
 =q e^{-N\psi_o(\beta)}+e^{-N\psi_d(\beta)}
   +O(e^{-bL})e^{-N\min_i\psi_i(\beta)}.
 \label{eq:sr-partition-input}
\end{equation}
The constants are uniform on a sufficiently small fixed compact
interval; the source's error $O(q^{-bL})$ is $O(e^{-bL})$ for fixed $q$,
with a different $b$. At coexistence the free energies are equal and
\begin{equation}
 u_i=\psi_i'(\beta_c),\qquad
 u_o<u_d,\qquad \sigma_i^2=-\psi_i''(\beta_c).
 \label{eq:sr-phase-parameters}
\end{equation}
The ordered branch is stable above $\beta_c$, and the disordered branch
below. The source Hamiltonian, its equation (6) with $J=1$, is
\eqref{eq:sr-hamiltonian}, and our $\psi_i$ is its $\beta f_i$. No
complex-temperature continuation of \eqref{eq:sr-partition-input} is
assumed.

The positive decomposition lives on bond sets
$A\subseteq\mathcal B_L$. With $p=1-e^{-\beta}$, their random-cluster
weights are
\begin{equation}
 w_\beta(A)=p^{|A|}(1-p)^{dN-|A|}q^{k(A)},
 \qquad
 Z_N^{\mathrm{RC}}(\beta)=\sum_Aw_\beta(A)
 =e^{-\beta dN}Z_N^{\mathrm{spin}}(\beta).
 \label{eq:sr-rc-normalization}
\end{equation}
This normalization, obtained by expanding
$\prod_{\{x,y\}}[1+(e^\beta-1)\ind_{\{\sigma_x=\sigma_y\}}]$,
fixes the additive energy convention independently of the contour
references.

Throughout, contours, interiors and exteriors,
compatibility, phase events, restricted partition functions, and activities
are those of Borgs, Chayes, and Tetali~\cite{BorgsChayesTetali2012}
(BCT), Definitions 4.1--4.4 and 5.4, equations (6.1)--(6.5) and
(6.13)--(6.22). BCT's exterior of a contour is the component
containing a compatible interface if one exists, and otherwise the
larger component, with a fixed tie rule; this matters for large
contours on the torus. Following BCT, $\gamma$ denotes a contour in
this appendix; it is unrelated to the capacity coefficient $\gamma$
of Section~\ref{sec:boundary-smooth}.

The construction thickens the occupied bonds and separates ordered and
disordered regions by their boundary components: components with zero
winding vector are contours, and the others are interfaces. Without
interfaces, the common exterior of the contours has one label, ordered
or disordered, which defines the positive event $\mathcal O_L$ or
$\mathcal D_L$; configurations with an interface form the tunneling
event $\mathcal T_L$. These events partition the bond configurations
and do not depend on $\beta$. With $Z_{o,L}$ the ordered contribution
(color multiplicity removed), $Z_{d,L}$ the disordered one, and
$Z_{t,L}$ the tunneling one, BCT (6.1)--(6.5) give exactly
\begin{equation}
 Z_N^{\mathrm{RC}}=q Z_{o,L}+Z_{d,L}+Z_{t,L}.
 \label{eq:sr-positive-contour-decomposition}
\end{equation}
At $\beta_c$, BCT Lemma 6.1 bounds the tunneling probability by
$C e^{-bL^{d-1}}$. The later construction
of~\cite{BorgsChayesHelmuthPerkinsTetali2022}, Definition 5, uses a
modified exterior convention; we import nothing from it.

Write $m_\gamma=\|\gamma\|$ for the size of a contour. Its diameter
and interior obey
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{diam}\gamma\le L,\qquad
 \operatorname{diam}\gamma\le m_\gamma/2,\qquad
 |\operatorname{Int}\gamma\cap\Lambda_L|
       \le\tfrac12m_\gamma\operatorname{diam}\gamma.
 \label{eq:sr-contour-geometry}
\end{equation}
These follow from the definition of diameter and Lemma 5.7
of~\cite{BorgsChayesTetali2012}, for all $d\ge2$.
The positive restricted partition functions have the polymer form
\begin{equation}
 Z_{i,L}=e^{-e_i(\beta)N}
       \sum_{\Gamma\ \mathrm{compatible}}
       \prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}K_i(\gamma;\beta).
 \label{eq:sr-polymer-form}
\end{equation}
The ground coefficients are $e_o=-d\log(1-e^{-\beta})$ and
$e_d=d\beta-\log q$. With $\kappa=\tfrac12\log(e^\beta-1)$, the
activities are $e^{-\kappa(\beta)m_\gamma}$ times a ratio of positive
interior partition functions, with a factor $q$ in the disordered case
(BCT (6.18)--(6.22)). The coefficients $e_i$ and $\kappa$ have bounded
derivatives on the fixed temperature interval.

BCT Appendix A defines truncated activities
\begin{equation}
 K_i'(\gamma;\beta)=\min\{K_i(\gamma;\beta),
                                  e^{-\tau(\beta)m_\gamma}\},
 \label{eq:sr-truncation}
\end{equation}
where BCT (A.3) uses $\tau(\beta)=\beta/8-\beta/20+1$; this cap
ensures uniform convergence of the cluster expansion for large $q$.
Write $Z_{i,L}'$ for the truncated partition functions and
$f_i^{\mathrm{tr}}$ for their infinite-volume free energies (distinct
from the $\psi_i$ in~\eqref{eq:sr-partition-input}). If
$a_i=f_i^{\mathrm{tr}}-\min_j f_j^{\mathrm{tr}}$, BCT Lemma A.1(i) states
\begin{equation}
 a_i(\beta)\operatorname{diam}\gamma\le c_0\beta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad K_i(\gamma;\beta)=K_i'(\gamma;\beta).
 \label{eq:sr-inactive-cutoff-input}
\end{equation}
Moreover, BCT (A.8) on the full torus gives
\begin{equation}
 |\log Z_{i,L}'(\beta)+Nf_i^{\mathrm{tr}}(\beta)|
       \le CNe^{-bL}.
 \label{eq:sr-truncated-volume-input}
\end{equation}
The assumptions of BCT Appendix A cover a fixed neighborhood on both
sides of $\beta_c$, although BCT's Lemma 6.3 is stated on the ordered
side.

It remains to identify the stable sides and the truncated free
energies with the data in~\eqref{eq:sr-partition-input}. BCT
Lemma A.3 says that the spontaneous
magnetization is positive exactly when $a_o=0$. BCT define their
transition point $\beta_0$ as the onset of positive magnetization,
whereas $\beta_c$ above is the crossing $\psi_o=\psi_d$ of the BKMS
free energies. The two coincide: for
$q\ge q_0(d)$ the model has a unique, disordered Gibbs state below the
large-$q$ transition point and $q$ ordered extremal states above it
\cite{KoteckyShlosman1982,LaanaitEtAl1991}, and the BKMS expansion
\eqref{eq:sr-partition-input} is derived from the same Pirogov--Sinai
construction, in which the phase with smaller $\psi_i$ is stable at
each $\beta$. Hence the magnetization vanishes below
$\beta_c$ and is positive above it, so $a_d=0$ below and $a_o=0$
above. Continuity gives $f_o^{\mathrm{tr}}=f_d^{\mathrm{tr}}$ at
coexistence.

The minimum $f^{\mathrm{tr}}=\min_i f_i^{\mathrm{tr}}$ is the physical
thermodynamic random-cluster free energy. For the untruncated
partition functions on the full torus, BCT Lemma A.1(ii) gives
$Z_{i,L}\le\exp[-Nf^{\mathrm{tr}}+O(Ne^{-bL})]$, while BCT (A.9)
supplies the corresponding lower bound for any minimizing phase. The
interface-network sum BCT (6.26)--(6.27) bounds $Z_{t,L}$ by
$\exp[-Nf^{\mathrm{tr}}-bL^{d-1}]$.
That sum uses only the bounds of BCT Appendix A and the large-$q$
contour counting inequalities, valid throughout the fixed neighborhood
(shrunk if necessary so that $q\le e^{2d\kappa+d}$ there, as BCT (6.27)
requires), and does not require the ordered phase to minimize.
With~\eqref{eq:sr-positive-contour-decomposition} these estimates
identify the limiting pressure, and~\eqref{eq:sr-rc-normalization}
with the stable branch of~\eqref{eq:sr-partition-input} gives
\begin{equation}
 f_i^{\mathrm{tr}}(\beta)=\psi_i(\beta)+d\beta
 \quad\text{on the stable side of phase }i.
 \label{eq:sr-stable-branch-identification}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Weak phase energy limits and nonzero variances}
\label{app:sr-weak}

Applying~\eqref{eq:sr-partition-input} at $\beta_c+t/N$ and using the
boundedness of $U_N/N$ gives
\begin{equation}
 U_N/N\ \Rightarrow\ \frac q{q+1}\delta_{u_o}
                           +\frac1{q+1}\delta_{u_d}.
 \label{eq:sr-macro-limit}
\end{equation}
The partition-function ratios are Laplace transforms that converge
for every fixed real $t$ to those of this bounded law. The
midpoint energy events $B_{o,N}=\{U_N/N<(u_o+u_d)/2\}$ and
$B_{d,N}=B_{o,N}^c$ therefore have the limiting probabilities
in~\eqref{eq:sr-macro-limit}.

For $s>0$, use~\eqref{eq:sr-partition-input} at
$\beta_c+s/\sqrt N$. The ordered branch dominates, and its quadratic
Taylor expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 \E_{\beta_c}\exp[-s(U_N-Nu_o)/\sqrt N]
       \longrightarrow\frac q{q+1}e^{\sigma_o^2s^2/2}.
 \label{eq:sr-one-sided-laplace}
\end{equation}
The part on $B_{d,N}$ is at most
$e^{-s(u_d-u_o)\sqrt N/2}$ and vanishes, so this is also the transform
limit of the positive subprobability measure restricted to $B_{o,N}$.
The disordered phase uses the opposite sign and $\beta_c-s/\sqrt N$.

This one-sided limit suffices for weak convergence. Tilt the ordered
subprobability law of $x=(U_N-Nu_o)/\sqrt N$ by
$e^{-s_0x}$ for any fixed $s_0>0$ and normalize it. Ratios of
\eqref{eq:sr-one-sided-laplace} at $s_0-t$ and $s_0$, for $|t|<s_0$,
give convergence of its moment-generating function to that of
$\Normal(-\sigma_o^2s_0,\sigma_o^2)$, so the tilted probabilities
converge weakly by the moment-generating-function convergence theorem.
Untilting against compactly supported continuous functions gives vague
convergence of the original measures; since their total masses converge
to the recovered Gaussian mass, the convergence is weak. Hence
\begin{equation}
 (U_N-Nu_i)/\sqrt N\mid B_{i,N}
       \Rightarrow\Normal(0,\sigma_i^2),\qquad i=o,d.
 \label{eq:sr-conditional-clt}
\end{equation}
A priori $\sigma_i^2\ge0$, by convexity of the limiting log transforms.

In fact both variances are strictly positive. Choose an
independent vertex set $S$ of size at least $N/(2d+1)$ and condition on all spins
outside it, which makes the spins in $S$ independent. For $x\in S$,
let
$n_{x,a}$ count the neighbors of color $a$. Then
$\sum_a n_{x,a}=2d$, and the conditional color probabilities on a
compact inverse-temperature interval with upper endpoint $\beta_{\max}$
obey $p_{x,a}\ge q^{-1}e^{-2d\beta_{\max}}$. If $q>2d$, two neighbor
counts differ by at least one. Since
$\Var_p(n)=\sum_{a<b}p_ap_b(n_a-n_b)^2$, conditional variance gives
\begin{equation}
 \Var_\beta U_N\ge v_*N,\qquad
 v_*:=\frac{e^{-4d\beta_{\max}}}{(2d+1)q^2}>0.
 \label{eq:sr-variance-lower}
\end{equation}
Thus $N^{-1}\log Z_N^{\mathrm{spin}}-v_*\beta^2/2$ is convex, and so is
its pointwise thermodynamic limit. On each open stable
side that limit equals $-\psi_i(\beta)-v_*\beta^2/2$, so
$-\psi_i''\ge v_*$. Continuity of $\psi_i''$ extends the bound to
$\beta_c$, proving $\sigma_i^2\ge v_*>0$. The argument uses the
stable branches, not the much larger total variance at coexistence.

\subsection{Uniform moments from positive contour measures}
\label{subsubsec:sr-moments}

Since the minimum in~\eqref{eq:sr-truncation} need not be twice
differentiable, we first prove a Lipschitz estimate for the truncated
free energies, use it to remove every cutoff in a window of width
proportional to $1/L$ around $\beta_c$, and only then differentiate
twice.

\begin{lemma}[Restricted contour partition derivatives]
\label{lem:sr-restricted-derivatives}
For the fixed sufficiently large $q$ above, there are constants
$\eta,C>0$ such that, for $i=o,d$ and all sufficiently large $L$,
\begin{equation}
 \left|\frac{\dd^2}{\dd\beta^2}\log Z_{i,L}(\beta)\right|\le CN,
 \qquad |\beta-\beta_c|\le\eta/L.
 \label{eq:sr-restricted-second}
\end{equation}
On this interval $Z_{i,L}=Z_{i,L}'$, and
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\dd}{\dd\beta}
 \log[e^{d\beta N}Z_{i,L}(\beta)]\right|_{\beta=\beta_c}
       =-Nu_i+O(\sqrt N).
 \label{eq:sr-restricted-center}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first bound the derivatives of the activities. For a general
contour interior $\Lambda=\operatorname{Int}\gamma$, use the
positive matching-label sums in BCT (6.13)--(6.14). A summand
indexed by an admissible family $\Gamma$ has logarithm
\begin{equation}
 F_\Gamma(\beta)
 =-e_o(\beta)n_o(\Gamma)-e_d(\beta)n_d(\Gamma)
   -\kappa(\beta)M(\Gamma)+c_\Gamma\log q,
 \label{eq:sr-matching-summand}
\end{equation}
where the nonnegative integers $n_o,n_d$, with
$n_o+n_d=|\Lambda\cap\Lambda_L|$, count vertices with each label,
$M(\Gamma)=\sum_{\gamma'\in\Gamma}m_{\gamma'}$ counts internal contour
intersections, and the integer $c_\Gamma$ counts ordered components,
minus one when the ordered color multiplicity is removed. These
quantities and the set of admissible families do not depend on
$\beta$.

To bound $M(\Gamma)$, note that BCT build contours from cubes of side
$1/2$ and define contour size as the number of intersections with
lattice edges (Section 4 and equation (4.5)). A contour surface is a
union of faces of the half-integer cube lattice, so it meets a given
lattice edge in at most a fixed number $C_d'$ of points; compatible
contours are disjoint, so the intersections counted by $M(\Gamma)$ are
distinct points on distinct edges or distinct contours. A contour of
$\Gamma$ lies in the closure of $\Lambda$, so each edge it meets
either has an endpoint in $\Lambda\cap\Lambda_L$ (at most
$2d|\Lambda\cap\Lambda_L|$ edges) or crosses the bounding contour
$\gamma$ (at most $m_\gamma$ edges, by the definition of contour
size). Consequently
\[
 M(\Gamma)\le C_d'\bigl(2d|\Lambda\cap\Lambda_L|+m_\gamma\bigr)
 \le C_d\bigl(|\Lambda\cap\Lambda_L|+m_\gamma\bigr)
 =:C_d V_\gamma.
\]
The bounded derivatives of $e_o,e_d,\kappa$ now give
$|F_\Gamma'|+|F_\Gamma''|\le C V_\gamma$, even when the interior wraps
around the torus.

For a positive sum $S=\sum_\Gamma e^{F_\Gamma}$, direct differentiation gives
\[
 (\log S)'=\E_S F_\Gamma',\qquad
 (\log S)''=\E_S F_\Gamma''+\Var_S F_\Gamma'.
\]
Hence $|(\log S)'|\le CV_\gamma$ and
$|(\log S)''|\le C(V_\gamma+V_\gamma^2)$.
Applied to the numerator and denominator of BCT's activity ratios
(6.18) and (6.22), with $V_\gamma=O(m_\gamma^2)$
by~\eqref{eq:sr-contour-geometry}, these give the uniform bounds
\begin{equation}
 |\partial_\beta\log K_i(\gamma)|\le Cm_\gamma^2,
 \qquad
 |\partial_\beta^2K_i(\gamma)|
                \le Cm_\gamma^4K_i(\gamma).
 \label{eq:sr-activity-derivatives}
\end{equation}
These inequalities do not require the interior phase to be stable.

For a cluster $\mathcal C$, write
$m(\mathcal C)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal C}m_\gamma$ and let
$\Phi(\mathcal C)$ denote its Ursell coefficient, including the
factor for repeated polymers. The contour counting and incompatibility
estimates (A.5)--(A.6) of~\cite{BorgsChayesTetali2012} give, for some fixed $\rho>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\mathcal C}|\Phi(\mathcal C)|
       \prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal C}K_i'(\gamma;\beta)
       e^{\rho m(\mathcal C)}\le CN.
 \label{eq:sr-cluster-majorant}
\end{equation}
The factor $e^{\rho m_\gamma}$ is absorbed by the choice of
$q_0(d)$: the criterion BCT (A.5)--(A.6) holds with a slack
$e^{(c\beta+1)m_\gamma}$ that grows with $q$, so it survives a fixed
$\rho<c\beta+1$ on the temperature interval. To sum over clusters, use
the device of BCT's proof of Lemma A.2 (their Appendix A.3): every
contour in $\Lambda_L$ is incompatible with at least one of the $N$
contours of size two obtained from configurations with a single
disordered edge in a fixed direction, so every cluster is incompatible
with one of these $N$ fixed contours. Summing the incompatibility bound
BCT (A.6) over them, with the polynomial factor absorbed in the
exponential slack, proves~\eqref{eq:sr-cluster-majorant}; overcounting
only increases the bound.

For the Lipschitz estimate, note that on any compact real temperature
interval the minimum in~\eqref{eq:sr-truncation} is absolutely
continuous, and almost everywhere its logarithmic derivative is that of
one of its two positive branches; both obey
$|\partial_\beta K_i'|\le Cm_\gamma^2K_i'$.
Termwise first differentiation is therefore justified by
\eqref{eq:sr-cluster-majorant}, since a differentiated cluster is bounded
by $C m(\mathcal C)^2$ times its absolute weight (equivalently,
integrate the almost-everywhere derivatives and use the same majorant
to interchange sum and integral). With the ground term
in~\eqref{eq:sr-polymer-form}, $|\partial_\beta\log Z_{i,L}'|\le CN$
almost everywhere, so the finite-volume free energies, and hence their
pointwise limits $f_i^{\mathrm{tr}}$, are Lipschitz with a common
constant.

Since $a_i(\beta_c)=0$ and $a_i$ is a difference of two $C$-Lipschitz
functions,
$a_i(\beta)\le 2C|\beta-\beta_c|$. Choose $\eta>0$ small enough that
$2C\eta<c_0\inf\beta$ on the fixed interval. Every torus contour has
diameter at most $L$, so~\eqref{eq:sr-inactive-cutoff-input} now removes
\emph{all} cutoffs on $|\beta-\beta_c|\le\eta/L$, where the activities
are therefore the original smooth positive functions. A second
derivative of a cluster product is bounded by $C m(\mathcal C)^4$ times
its absolute weight, by~\eqref{eq:sr-activity-derivatives}, and the
exponential factor in~\eqref{eq:sr-cluster-majorant} absorbs this
polynomial uniformly. Twice differentiating the uniformly convergent
cluster expansion, and adding the ground term,
proves~\eqref{eq:sr-restricted-second}.

For the center, set
$F_{i,L}(\beta)=\log[e^{d\beta N}Z_{i,L}(\beta)]$ and take
$t_N=\eta/(2\sqrt N)$ with the sign pointing to the stable side of
phase $i$; $|t_N|\le\eta/L$ since $d\ge2$. By
\eqref{eq:sr-truncated-volume-input}
and~\eqref{eq:sr-stable-branch-identification}, at both $\beta_c$ and
$\beta_c+t_N$,
$F_{i,L}(\beta)=-N\psi_i(\beta)+O(Ne^{-bL})$, and the
second-derivative bound and a stable-side finite difference imply
\[
 F_{i,L}'(\beta_c)
 =-N\frac{\psi_i(\beta_c+t_N)-\psi_i(\beta_c)}{t_N}
     +O(N|t_N|)+O(Ne^{-bL}/|t_N|)
 =-Nu_i+O(\sqrt N).
\]
The exponentially small error is negligible, proving
\eqref{eq:sr-restricted-center}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Transfer to the spin energy and proof of the theorem}
\label{app:sr-transfer}

Lemma~\ref{lem:sr-restricted-derivatives} controls the occupied-bond
count; the next lemma transfers its bounds to the spin energy exchanged
with the reservoir.

\begin{lemma}[Transfer from bond fluctuations to spin energy]
\label{lem:sr-energy-transfer}
In the Edwards--Sokal joint law at $\beta_c$, the conditional spin
energy on each of the positive bond phase events obeys
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{L\ge L_0,i\in\{o,d\}}
 \E\left[\exp\left(\eta_1\frac{|U_N-Nu_i|}{\sqrt N}\right)
                 \,\middle|\,\mathcal I_{i,L}\right]<\infty
 \label{eq:sr-spin-phase-moment}
\end{equation}
for some fixed $\eta_1>0$ and sufficiently large fixed $L_0$, where
$\mathcal I_{o,L}=\mathcal O_L$ and
$\mathcal I_{d,L}=\mathcal D_L$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\lambda=\log(e^\beta-1)$ be the bond fugacity parameter, so
$\dd\beta/\dd\lambda=p$, and put $B=|A|$ and $M_\sigma=-U_N$, the
number of monochromatic spin bonds. Up to the constant ordered multiplicity,
$e^{d\beta N}Z_{i,L}(\beta)$ is a positive sum of
$e^{\lambda B}q^{k(A)}$ over the fixed phase event, so the first and
second $\lambda$-derivatives of its \emph{logarithm} $F_{i,L}$ are the
conditional mean and variance of $B$.

Lemma~\ref{lem:sr-restricted-derivatives}, the bounded derivatives of
$\beta(\lambda)$, and the elementary $|F_{i,L}'|\le CN$ give a second
$\lambda$ derivative bounded by $CN$ throughout a window of width
$\eta_2/\sqrt N$. At coexistence the first derivative is
$-pNu_i+O(\sqrt N)$, by~\eqref{eq:sr-restricted-center}.
Taylor's theorem for log partition ratios at
$\lambda_c\pm\eta_3/\sqrt N$ therefore proves
\begin{equation}
 \E\left[e^{\eta_3|B+pNu_i|/\sqrt N}
                     \,\middle|\,\mathcal I_{i,L}\right]\le C
 \label{eq:sr-bond-moment}
\end{equation}
for a sufficiently small fixed $\eta_3>0$.

Conditional on the spin configuration, $B$ is Binomial$(M_\sigma,p)$,
since each monochromatic bond is retained independently with probability $p$.
For a centered Bernoulli variable, the second derivative of its log
moment-generating function is at most $1/4$; integrating twice bounds
that function by $t^2/8$. Conditional independence therefore gives,
for $D=B-pM_\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
 \E[e^{tD}\mid\sigma]\le e^{dNt^2/8}
 \quad(t\in\R),\qquad
 \E e^{t|D|/\sqrt N}\le2e^{dt^2/8}.
 \label{eq:sr-binomial-noise}
\end{equation}
Conditioning on an event multiplies these bounds by at most its
inverse probability. At coexistence all cutoffs are inactive, and
\eqref{eq:sr-truncated-volume-input} with equal free energies and the
tunneling bound gives
\begin{equation}
 P(\mathcal O_L)\to\frac q{q+1},\qquad
 P(\mathcal D_L)\to\frac1{q+1},\qquad
 P(\mathcal T_L)\le Ce^{-bL^{d-1}}.
 \label{eq:sr-positive-phase-probabilities}
\end{equation}
Thus the conditional noise moments are uniformly bounded. Finally
\[
 |U_N-Nu_i|=|M_\sigma+Nu_i|
 \le p^{-1}\{|B+pNu_i|+|D|\}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz,~\eqref{eq:sr-bond-moment}, and
\eqref{eq:sr-binomial-noise} prove~\eqref{eq:sr-spin-phase-moment}
with $2\eta_1/p\le\eta_3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sr-bath}]
For necessity, the positive midpoint phase weights,
\eqref{eq:sr-conditional-clt}, and $\sigma_i^2\ge v_*>0$ satisfy
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-scale-necessity}, with gap $N(u_d-u_o)$ and
$s_N=\sqrt N$.

For sufficiency, use the positive decomposition by
$\mathcal O_L,\mathcal D_L,\mathcal T_L$ on the Edwards--Sokal space.
Lemma~\ref{lem:sr-energy-transfer} supplies the physical-energy moment
bound. The exceptional mass is at most $Ce^{-bL^{d-1}}$, while the
largest reservoir amplification is $\exp[O(N^2/c_N)]$. If
$c_N\gg N^{3/2}$, its logarithm is $o(\sqrt N)=o(L^{d/2})$.
Since $d-1\ge d/2$ for $d\ge2$, the exceptional contribution vanishes.
Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-sufficiency} therefore gives total-variation
convergence on the joint spin--bond space, where the likelihood is the
exact function of $U_N(\sigma)$ in~\eqref{eq:exact-marginal}. Summing
over bonds gives the physical subsystem law, so projection proves the
spin statement.
\end{proof}

Optional independent quadratic momenta preserve the threshold: their
centered Gamma transform adds the moment bound from
Lemma~\ref{lem:mf-exponential}, their mean shifts both centers by
$aN/\beta_c$, and their variance adds $a/\beta_c^2$ to the weak phase
limits. For $a>0$, convolution also gives continuous local Gaussian
energy limits; Theorem~\ref{thm:sr-bath} needs no pointwise density
envelope.
