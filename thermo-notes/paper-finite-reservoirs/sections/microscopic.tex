\section{Microscopic realizations}
\label{sec:microscopic}

We now verify the probabilistic hypotheses for two microscopic Hamiltonians.
The mean-field example permits exact finite-system calculations. The
short-range example requires positive contour measures and a transfer
estimate between bond and spin energies. In both models the reservoir
threshold is a consequence of their established phase structure, rather
than a new claim about the location or order of their transitions.

\subsection{The three-state Curie--Weiss--Potts model}
\label{subsec:meanfield}

Let $n_1+n_2+n_3=N$ be the occupation numbers of $N$ three-state spins and
take
\begin{equation}
 U_N=-\frac{J}{2N}\sum_{j=1}^3n_j^2,\qquad J>0,
 \qquad \beta J=b:=4\log2.
 \label{eq:mf-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
Optionally add $2aN$ independent quadratic momentum coordinates, with
$a>0$ fixed and $2aN$ integral on the size sequence under consideration.
Their canonical energy $K_N$ has the Gamma distribution of shape $aN$
and rate $\beta$. Set $E_N=U_N+K_N$. We also allow $a=0$, meaning no
momenta and $K_N=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Mean-field microscopic reservoir threshold]
\label{thm:mf-bath}
For every fixed $J>0$ and $a\ge0$ in the model just defined, an admissible
total-energy tuning for which $\TV(Q_N,P_N)\to0$ exists if and only if
$c_N\gg N^{3/2}$. The two-center calibration uses
\begin{equation}
 e_{-,N}=N\left(-\frac J4+\frac a\beta\right),\qquad
 e_{+,N}=N\left(-\frac J6+\frac a\beta\right).
 \label{eq:mf-centers}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

We give the phase analysis and uniform bounds needed for this statement.
The known mean-field phase structure and fluctuation theory are treated
more generally in~\cite{CosteniucEllisTouchette2005,GandolfoRuizWouts2010}.
The calculation here makes the normalization and the degenerate
disordered spin-energy fluctuation explicit.

Write $p_j=n_j/N$ and use $(p_1,p_2)$ as coordinates on the probability
simplex, with $p_3=1-p_1-p_2$. The rate potential, up to an additive
constant, is
\begin{equation}
 I(p)=\sum_{j=1}^3p_j\log p_j-\frac b2\sum_{j=1}^3p_j^2.
 \label{eq:mf-rate}
\end{equation}
Its minima are
\begin{equation}
 p^{(d)}=(1/3,1/3,1/3),\qquad
 p^{(o)}\in\{(2/3,1/6,1/6),(1/6,2/3,1/6),(1/6,1/6,2/3)\}.
 \label{eq:mf-minima}
\end{equation}
There are three ordered minima and one disordered minimum.
Here is a direct completeness check. A boundary point cannot minimize
$I$, because transferring a small positive mass to a zero coordinate
lowers the entropy term by order $\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|$ and
changes the quadratic term by only $O(\varepsilon)$. At an interior
stationary point, all $\log p_j-bp_j$ are equal. Strict concavity of
$\log x-bx$ permits at most two coordinate values. If two coordinates
share the larger value $x$ and the remaining value is $y<x$, stationarity
gives $b=\log(x/y)/(x-y)>1/x$. The second variation exchanging the two
large coordinates is then negative. A nonsymmetric minimum must
therefore have one large coordinate $r$ and two equal small coordinates
$(1-r)/2$, with $r>1/3$. Its stationary equation is
\begin{equation}
 \log\frac{2r}{1-r}-\frac b2(3r-1)=0.
 \label{eq:mf-scalar-stationarity}
\end{equation}
The derivative of the left side is $1/[r(1-r)]-3b/2$, which has at most
two zeros. Rolle's theorem therefore allows at most three roots of
\eqref{eq:mf-scalar-stationarity}; the three roots are $1/3,1/2,2/3$.
At $r=1/2$ the second variation along this curve is
$4-6\log2<0$. The other candidates have the same value
$I_{\min}=-\log3-(2/3)\log2$. Their Hessians are positive definite:
\begin{align}
 H_d&=(3-b)\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},
 &\det H_d&=3(3-b)^2,
 \label{eq:mf-hessian-d}\\
 H_o&=\begin{pmatrix}15/2-2b&6-b\\6-b&12-2b\end{pmatrix},
 &\det H_o&=3(3-b)(6-b).
 \label{eq:mf-hessian-o}
\end{align}
This proves the complete minimum classification used below.

Partition the simplex into the four Voronoi cells of these minima,
resolving ties deterministically. All four cells have positive limiting
probabilities. Uniform Stirling expansion on compact interior sets gives
the unnormalized occupation weight
\begin{equation}
 \frac{N!}{\prod_j n_j!}e^{-\beta U_N(n)}
 =\frac{1+o(1)}{2\pi N\sqrt{p_1p_2p_3}}\,e^{-NI(p)}.
 \label{eq:mf-stirling}
\end{equation}
In a fixed $N^{-1/2}$ neighborhood of a minimum, the two-dimensional
lattice has mesh $N^{-1/2}$ after rescaling and hence density $N$.
Taylor expansion of $I$ in~\eqref{eq:mf-stirling} and Gaussian Riemann
sums give a phase contribution proportional to
$(p_1p_2p_3)^{-1/2}(\det H_i)^{-1/2}$ and a conditional occupation
limit $\Normal(0,H_i^{-1})$. The uniform bound proved below justifies
discarding the tails of these Riemann sums. Thus, with minus denoting
the combined ordered phases,
\begin{equation}
 r_*:=\sqrt{\frac{2(3-b)}{6-b}},\qquad
 w_-:=\frac{3r_*}{1+3r_*},\qquad w_+:=\frac1{1+3r_*}.
 \label{eq:mf-phase-weights}
\end{equation}
The tangent gradient of $u(p)=-J\sum_jp_j^2/2$ is
$(-J/2,0)$ at the displayed ordered minimum and zero at the disordered
minimum. The delta method and independence of the kinetic energy give
the conditional weak energy limits
\begin{equation}
 \frac{E_N-e_{i,N}}{\sqrt N}\ \Rightarrow\ \Normal(0,v_i),
 \qquad
 v_-:=\frac a{\beta^2}+\frac{J^2}{6(3-b)},\quad
 v_+:=\frac a{\beta^2}.
 \label{eq:mf-variances}
\end{equation}
For $a=0$, $\Normal(0,0)$ means the point mass at zero. Indeed the
disordered potential energy fluctuates only on scale one, because its
first derivative on the simplex vanishes. The ordered phase has a
strictly positive $\sqrt N$ variance even without momenta.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform positive-phase energy moments]
\label{lem:mf-exponential}
For each fixed $a\ge0$, there are $\eta>0$ and $M<\infty$ such that
the two positive energy phase laws defined by the Voronoi partition
satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{N\ge N_0,i\in\{-,+\}}
 \E\left[\exp\left(\eta\frac{|E_N-e_{i,N}|}{\sqrt N}\right)
       \,\middle|\,i\right]\le M.
 \label{eq:mf-moment-bound}
\end{equation}
Here $N_0$ is fixed and large enough that every conditioning phase has
positive probability. Only these sufficiently large sizes are needed
for the asymptotic theorem.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal M$ be the four minima. Compactness, the complete minimum
classification, and their positive Hessians give
$I(p)-I_{\min}\ge c_0\operatorname{dist}(p,\mathcal M)^2$ globally.
The Gaussian neighborhoods in~\eqref{eq:mf-stirling} give a partition
function lower bound $c e^{-NI_{\min}}$. On a fixed interior subset,
Stirling bounds then give
\[
 P_N(n)\le\frac CN e^{-cN\operatorname{dist}(n/N,\mathcal M)^2}.
\]
The same bound holds globally with smaller $c$. To verify the boundary,
choose a boundary strip disjoint from $\mathcal M$, where the rate gap
is at least $\zeta>0$. The multinomial type bound
$N!/\prod n_j!\le e^{-N\sum p_j\log p_j}$ gives
$P_N(n)\le C e^{-N[I(p)-I_{\min}]}$ there. Splitting the exponent
between the gap and distance bounds recovers the missing $1/N$, since
$N e^{-N\zeta/2}$ is bounded.

In the cell of $p^{(i)}$, the distance to $\mathcal M$ is
$|p-p^{(i)}|$ and the Lipschitz property of $u$ gives
$|U_N-Nu(p^{(i)})|/\sqrt N\le C\sqrt N|p-p^{(i)}|$.
Completing the square in the resulting lattice Gaussian sum shows
\[
 \frac CN\sum_{n_1,n_2}
 e^{-cN|n/N-p^{(i)}|^2+\eta C\sqrt N|n/N-p^{(i)}|}\le C_\eta.
\]
The phase probabilities are bounded below for large $N$, so conditioning
preserves the bound. For $a>0$, the centered Gamma transform is
\[
 \E e^{t(K_N-aN/\beta)/\sqrt N}
 =e^{-ta\sqrt N/\beta}
   \left(1-\frac{t}{\beta\sqrt N}\right)^{-aN}.
\]
It is uniformly bounded for fixed $t$ and all sufficiently large $N$.
Using $e^{t|x|}\le e^{tx}+e^{-tx}$ and independence completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:mf-bath}]
The gap of the two energy centers is $NJ/12$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:mf-exponential} supplies a positive decomposition with
no exceptional component and the required $\sqrt N$ moment bounds.
Equation~\eqref{eq:mf-variances} supplies a nondegenerate weak limit
in the ordered phase. Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:two-phase-iff}.
\end{proof}

The kinetic variables therefore have a useful but limited role: they
make both energy peaks continuous with positive Gaussian variance and
permit the exact integral formulas below. They are not required for the
reservoir exponent itself.

\subsection{Continuous energy limits and exact finite-system formulas}
\label{subsec:mf-exact}

For $a>0$, the standardized Gamma density converges uniformly to the
Gaussian density of variance $a/\beta^2$. One way to check uniformity
is to apply Stirling's formula on compact standardized intervals and
then use the Gamma density's unimodality: outside such an interval its
supremum is attained at the nearest endpoint once the mode is inside.
Increasing the interval bounds both limiting tails uniformly.
Convolution with the conditional spin-energy weak limits (the $a=0$
case of~\eqref{eq:mf-variances}) therefore gives
the local $L^1$ density limits~\eqref{eq:local-density-assumption} with
weights~\eqref{eq:mf-phase-weights}. There is also the stronger interior
bound
\begin{equation}
 p_N(E)\le\frac C{\sqrt N}\sum_{i\in\{-,+\}}
          \exp\left[-c\frac{(E-e_{i,N})^2}{N}\right]
 \quad(e_{-,N}\le E\le e_{+,N}).
 \label{eq:mf-density-envelope}
\end{equation}
To see this without a Gaussian-mixture assumption, write $g_N$ for the
kinetic density. The allowed kinetic energies in this interval obey
$0<K\le C_0N$. Its logarithmic curvature is
$-(aN-1)/K^2\le-c_1/N$ for all large $N$, and its maximum is
$O(N^{-1/2})$. Expanding at its mode, which differs from $aN/\beta$
by a constant, gives
$g_N(K)\le C N^{-1/2}e^{-c(K-aN/\beta)^2/N}$ on this whole interval.
For a nearest minimum $p^{(i)}$, the Lipschitz property of $u$ yields
\[
 |p-p^{(i)}|^2+|E/N-u(p)-a/\beta|^2
 \ge c\{|p-p^{(i)}|^2+|E/N-u(p^{(i)})-a/\beta|^2\}.
\]
Multiply the occupation bound from Lemma~\ref{lem:mf-exponential} by
the Gamma bound, and sum over each cell. The lattice Gaussian sum is
$O(N)$ and cancels the occupation prefactor $N^{-1}$, proving
\eqref{eq:mf-density-envelope}. This also controls contamination from
the other phase in the local density limit.

For finite-system evaluation set $A=aN>0$ and
$m(n)=N!/(n_1!n_2!n_3!)$. The canonical occupation law and its physical
reservoir counterpart are exactly
\begin{align}
 P_N(n)&=\frac{m(n)e^{-\beta U_N(n)}}
                  {\sum_{n'}m(n')e^{-\beta U_N(n')}},
 \label{eq:mf-canonical-occupancy}\\
 Q_N(n)&=\frac{m(n)[\mathcal E_N-U_N(n)]_+^{A+c_N}}
                  {\sum_{n'}m(n')[\mathcal E_N-U_N(n')]_+^{A+c_N}}.
 \label{eq:mf-bath-occupancy}
\end{align}
Conditional on $n$, $K_N$ is canonical Gamma$(A,\beta)$, whereas
\begin{equation}
 \frac{K_N}{\mathcal E_N-U_N(n)}\ \bigg|\ n
 \sim\operatorname{Beta}(A,c_N+1)
 \quad\hbox{under }Q_N.
 \label{eq:mf-beta-conditional}
\end{equation}
Indeed integration of the product of subsystem and reservoir densities
gives
\[
 \int_0^{\mathcal E_N-U_N(n)}
 K^{A-1}[\mathcal E_N-U_N(n)-K]^{c_N}\dd K
 =\mathrm B(A,c_N+1)[\mathcal E_N-U_N(n)]^{A+c_N}.
\]
The exponent is $A+c_N$, and the second Beta parameter is $c_N+1$.
For $a=0$, the occupation formulas remain valid with $A=0$, but the
conditional energy is the point mass $K_N=0$; no shape-zero Gamma or
Beta distribution is used.

Let $G_A$ be the CDF of a unit-rate Gamma law and $I_x(A,c_N+1)$ the
regularized incomplete Beta function, extended by zero for $x\le0$
and one for $x\ge1$. Then, for $a>0$,
\begin{align}
 P_N(E_N\le t)&=\sum_nP_N(n)G_A\bigl(\beta[t-U_N(n)]\bigr),
 \label{eq:mf-gamma-cdf}\\
 Q_N(E_N\le t)&=\sum_{n:Q_N(n)>0}Q_N(n)
 I_{[t-U_N(n)]/[\mathcal E_N-U_N(n)]}(A,c_N+1),
 \label{eq:mf-beta-cdf}
\end{align}
where $G_A(x)=0$ for $x\le0$. These are finite sums, with no histogram
approximation. The log likelihood in~\eqref{eq:exact-marginal} is
strictly concave below its cutoff and tends to $-\infty$ at both ends
of that half-line. For $a>0$ it is not constant on the canonical support,
so its normalized maximum exceeds zero. Its positive set is an interval
$(t_L,t_R)$, determined by two scalar roots. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \TV(Q_N,P_N)=Q_N(t_L<E_N<t_R)-P_N(t_L<E_N<t_R).
 \label{eq:mf-tv-cdf}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:mf-gamma-cdf}--\eqref{eq:mf-beta-cdf} evaluate
this expression directly. An interval endpoint may lie outside the
subsystem support; the CDF formula already handles that case. For the
pure-spin model one instead sums the positive likelihood differences
over the discrete occupation law.

\subsection{A short-range Potts theorem}
\label{subsec:short-range}

Fix a dimension $d\ge2$ and an integer $q\ge q_0(d)$, independent of
system size. The threshold $q_0(d)$ is fixed once at the start of
Appendix~\ref{app:short-range-proof}: it exceeds $2d$, and it is large
enough for the cited contour results and for one additional exponential
slack in their cluster-expansion criterion. On the periodic nearest-neighbor torus
$\Lambda_L=(\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z)^d$, let $N=L^d$ and
\begin{equation}
 U_N(\sigma)=-\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\mathcal B_L}
                   \ind_{\{\sigma_x=\sigma_y\}},
 \qquad \sigma\in\{1,\ldots,q\}^{\Lambda_L}.
 \label{eq:sr-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
There are $dN$ bonds. The unit coupling fixes the energy units. The
coexistence inverse temperature is denoted $\beta_c$.

\begin{theorem}[Short-range microscopic reservoir threshold]
\label{thm:sr-bath}
For every fixed $d\ge2$ and fixed $q\ge q_0(d)$, the canonical
law of~\eqref{eq:sr-hamiltonian} at $\beta_c$ can be reproduced in
total variation by an exact reservoir~\eqref{eq:bath-dos}, with some
total-energy tuning, if and only if $c_N\gg N^{3/2}$. The sufficient
choice is~\eqref{eq:secant-calibration} with centers $Nu_o$ and $Nu_d$,
where $u_o<u_d$ are the two coexistence energy densities.
\end{theorem}

In particular, this statement applies to the plain two-dimensional
spin model, with no auxiliary kinetic energy. The proof uses two
different positive phase partitions. Midpoint energy events provide
the weak fluctuations needed for necessity. Contour phase events in
the Edwards--Sokal joint space provide the uniform moments needed for
sufficiency. There is no requirement that these partitions coincide.


The detailed proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:short-range-proof}.
The published finite-volume free energies provide weak Gaussian energy
limits with positive phase weights. A uniform conditional-variance
bound proves that each stable-phase variance is positive. These inputs
give necessity through Theorem~\ref{thm:two-scale-necessity}.
For sufficiency, the logarithms of positive restricted contour partition
functions have second derivatives bounded in absolute value by $CN$
in the stated finite-size temperature window. Their moment
bounds transfer from occupied-bond count to the exchanged spin energy
using the conditional binomial law of the Edwards--Sokal coupling.
The exceptional contour mass decays on the surface scale, which
dominates the reservoir gain when $c_N\gg N^{3/2}$.
The distinction between bond count and physical energy is essential;
differentiating a bond-conditioned spin law would not justify this step.
