\section{Smooth reservoirs and the boundary law}
\label{sec:boundary-smooth}

This section extends the two-phase criteria of
Section~\ref{sec:thresholds} to smooth reservoir entropies and computes
the exact-reservoir error at $c_N\sim\gamma N^{3/2}$, where each phase
acquires a finite Gaussian tilt.

\subsection{Smooth reservoir entropies}

Let a reservoir induce a log likelihood $h_N(E)$ relative to a
specified canonical law. For reservoir entropy $s_{B,N}=S_{B,N}/k_B$,
$h_N(E)=\beta_NE+s_{B,N}(\mathcal E_N-E)$ up to a constant. Where
temperature and positive heat capacity are defined by this entropy
convention, the curvature of $h_N$ is a heat-capacity ratio:
\begin{equation}
 -h_N''(E)=-s_{B,N}''(\mathcal E_N-E)
 =\frac{[\beta_B(\mathcal E_N-E)]^2}{C_B(\mathcal E_N-E)/k_B}.
 \label{eq:smooth-capacity}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Curvature necessity]
\label{prop:smooth-necessity}
Suppose a positive decomposition \eqref{eq:positive-decomposition}
has two phases with positive limiting weights, centers
$e_{-,N}<e_{+,N}$ separated by $\Delta_N\asymp N$, and conditional
limits $(E-e_{i,N})/\sqrt N\Rightarrow\Normal(0,v_i)$ with $v_i>0$.
Let $Q_N$ have likelihood proportional to $e^{h_N}$, with $h_N=-\infty$
outside an interval. On its interior assume $h_N$ is twice continuously
differentiable and $-h_N''\ge k\kappa_N$ between the phase centers,
for some $k>0$ and a positive sequence $\kappa_N>0$.
Then $\TV(P_N,Q_N)\to0$ requires $\kappa_NN^{3/2}\to0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We adapt the good-point chord argument for
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-scale-necessity}.
TV convergence gives $h_N(E)-\log Z_N\to0$ in canonical probability
on the feasible set. Choose good energies with standardized positions
in $[1/2,1]$ and $[3/2,2]$ above the lower center, and $[-1,-1/2]$
below the upper center, each with positive limiting probability.
The selected feasible energies $x_N<y_N<z_N$ have log likelihoods
within $o(1)$ of $\log Z_N$, $y_N-x_N\asymp\sqrt N$, $z_N-y_N\asymp N$,
and a feasible convex hull between the centers.
Concavity of $h_N(E)+k\kappa_NE^2/2$ gives
\[
 h_N(y_N)-\frac{z_N-y_N}{z_N-x_N}h_N(x_N)
 -\frac{y_N-x_N}{z_N-x_N}h_N(z_N)
 \ge \frac{k\kappa_N}{2}(y_N-x_N)(z_N-y_N).
\]
The left side tends to zero; the right side is of order
$\kappa_NN^{3/2}$.
\end{proof}

Under the density hypotheses of Section~\ref{subsec:density-tails},
Proposition~\ref{prop:density-sufficiency} gives the converse when
$h_N(e_{-,N})=h_N(e_{+,N})=0$, $h_N$ is globally concave on its
feasible interval, and its curvature satisfies
\eqref{eq:concave-curvature-upper}. Hence $\kappa_NN^{3/2}\to0$ is sharp for
curvature comparable to $\kappa_N$, provided a composite energy
equalizes $h_N$ at the two centers, which for an arbitrary entropy
need not exist.
Exactly Gaussian separated phases need no interphase density
hypothesis: the chord and curvature upper bound make the standardized
endpoint slopes vanish, and Gaussian exponential moments make the
tangent bound uniformly integrable, giving the analogous criterion
$\kappa_N\Delta_Ns_N\to0$ for gap $\Delta_N$ and Gaussian fluctuation
scale $s_N$.

\subsection{Finite tilts at capacity proportional to \texorpdfstring{$N^{3/2}$}{N to the power 3/2}}

For the exact reservoir, use the positive decomposition
\eqref{eq:positive-decomposition} with $w_{i,N}\to w_i>0$,
$w_-+w_+=1$, and $\Delta_N/N\to\ell>0$. Write
$\mathcal E_N^{\mathrm{sec}}$ and $h_N^{\mathrm{sec}}$ for the secant
calibration \eqref{eq:secant-calibration} and its log
likelihood~\eqref{eq:secant-h}. A Gaussian $G_i=\Normal(0,v_i)$ with
$v_i=0$ is a point mass.

\begin{theorem}[Boundary law and finite calibration corrections]
\label{thm:physical-boundary}
Suppose $c_N/N^{3/2}\to\gamma\in(0,\infty)$ and
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 X_{i,N}:=(E_N-e_{i,N})/\sqrt N\Rightarrow G_i
 \ \text{under }P_{i,N},\\
 \sup_{N\ge N_0}\E_{P_{i,N}}e^{\eta|X_{i,N}|}<\infty
 \quad\text{for some }N_0\text{ and }\eta>b:=\frac{\beta^2\ell}{2\gamma}.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:boundary-moment}
\end{equation}
Assume also
\begin{equation}
 \delta_N\exp\!\left[\max_E h_N^{\mathrm{sec}}(E)\right]\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:boundary-exception}
\end{equation}
For $\mathcal E_N=\mathcal E_N^{\mathrm{sec}}+\xi_N\sqrt N$, $\xi_N\to\xi$,
put $b_-=b$, $b_+=-b$, $D=\beta^2\ell\xi/\gamma$, and
\begin{equation}
 A_-=0,\quad A_+=D,\qquad
 Z(\xi)=\sum_iw_i e^{A_i+b^2v_i/2},\qquad
 r_i(\xi)=\frac{w_i e^{A_i+b^2v_i/2}}{Z(\xi)}.
 \label{eq:boundary-phase-weights}
\end{equation}
The reservoir phase weights, defined through the positive
decomposition~\eqref{eq:positive-decomposition} (possibly on an
enlarged probability space), tend to $r_i(\xi)$; their standardized
conditional fluctuations converge weakly to
$G_i^b:=\Normal(b_iv_i,v_i)$. Because the phases are separated on
scale $N$, the same limits hold for the reservoir probabilities of the
energy events below and above the midpoint of the centers. On the full
microscopic state space,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_N\TV(P_N,Q_N)
 =\frac12\sum_iw_i\int_{\R}
 \left|\frac{e^{A_i+b_i z}}{Z(\xi)}-1\right|\dd G_i(z).
 \label{eq:physical-boundary-tv}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Normalize $h_N$ at $e_{-,N}$. Uniformly on bounded standardized
windows, the exact secant formula gives
\begin{equation}
 h_N(e_{i,N}+\sqrt Nz)=A_i+b_i z+o(1).
 \label{eq:physical-local-tilt}
\end{equation}
Indeed, with $q_N=\beta_N\Delta_N/c_N\to0$, the secant endpoint
derivatives are $\beta_Nq_N/2+O(q_N^2)$ and
$-\beta_Nq_N/2+O(q_N^2)$; their products with $\sqrt N$ tend to
$b$ and $-b$, and $N\sup|h_N''|=O(N/c_N)\to0$ on these windows.
Changing $\mathcal E_N$ by $\xi_N\sqrt N$ changes the upper-minus-lower
endpoint value by
\[
 \xi_N\sqrt N\,c_N
 \left[(\mathcal E_N^{\mathrm{sec}}-e_{+,N})^{-1}
 -(\mathcal E_N^{\mathrm{sec}}-e_{-,N})^{-1}\right]+o(1)
 \longrightarrow D,
\]
and each standardized endpoint slope by $o(1)$.

For the tails, concavity gives
$h_N(E)\le h_N(e_{i,N})+h_N'(e_{i,N})(E-e_{i,N})$
on the feasible set, so
$e^{h_N(E_N)}\le C\exp[(b+\epsilon)|X_{i,N}|]$ for small fixed
$\epsilon>0$ and large $N$. Choosing $p>1$ with $p(b+\epsilon)<\eta$,
\eqref{eq:boundary-moment} gives uniform integrability, also for the
absolute difference in \eqref{eq:physical-boundary-tv}; energies at or
above the cutoff have zero weight.
For bounded $\xi_N$, the maximum of $h_N$, at
$E=\mathcal E_N-c_N/\beta_N$, differs from the secant maximum by
$O(1)$: when $h_N(e_{-,N})=0$, the derivative of its value with
respect to $\mathcal E_N$ is
$\beta_N-c_N/(\mathcal E_N-e_{-,N})=O(N^{-1/2})$ in this range.
Thus \eqref{eq:boundary-exception} removes the exceptional
contribution. Weak convergence, \eqref{eq:physical-local-tilt}, and
uniform integrability give $Z_N\to Z(\xi)$ and the Gaussian tilts and,
with the exact identity $\TV(P_N,Q_N)=\frac12\E_{P_N}|e^{h_N}/Z_N-1|$,
also \eqref{eq:physical-boundary-tv}. Finally, the same uniform
integrability and tightness on scale $\sqrt N$ show that each
reweighted phase puts vanishing reservoir mass across the midpoint of
the centers, and the exceptional contribution vanishes; this gives the
limits for the energy events below and above the midpoint.
\end{proof}

The secant calibration $\xi=0$ equalizes the likelihoods at the centers;
it preserves the phase weights ($r_i(0)=w_i$) exactly when
$v_-=v_+$. For $v_-=v_+=v$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_N\TV(P_N,Q_N)=2\Phi(b\sqrt v/2)-1,
 \label{eq:physical-balanced-boundary}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi$ is the standard normal distribution function.

\begin{corollary}[Compensating the phase weights]
\label{cor:physical-compensation}
Under these hypotheses, the calibration
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_N=\mathcal E_N^{\mathrm{sec}}
 +\frac{\beta^2\ell(v_--v_+)}{8\gamma}\sqrt N+o(\sqrt N)
 \label{eq:physical-compensation}
\end{equation}
restores the original phase weights asymptotically, and
\begin{equation}
 \lim_N\TV(P_N,Q_N)
 =\sum_iw_i\{2\Phi(b\sqrt{v_i}/2)-1\}.
 \label{eq:compensated-tv}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The correction makes $D=b^2(v_--v_+)/2$, so the integrated factors
in \eqref{eq:boundary-phase-weights} are equal. Within each phase
the remaining change is the Gaussian translation by $b_iv_i$, with
TV $2\Phi(b\sqrt{v_i}/2)-1$. This is zero when $v_i=0$.
\end{proof}
Restoring the phase weights thus leaves a finite distortion in each
phase with $v_i>0$.

\subsection{Optimization over all calibrations}

To optimize over all calibrations, not only shifts of order $\sqrt N$,
let $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{var}}$ be the total variation norm of finite
signed measures and define the continuous convex function
\begin{equation}
 F(r)=\frac12\left\{
 \|w_-G_- -rG_-^b\|_{\mathrm{var}}
 +\|w_+G_+ -(1-r)G_+^b\|_{\mathrm{var}}\right\},
 \qquad 0\le r\le1.
 \label{eq:physical-boundary-objective}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Optimized boundary]
\label{thm:physical-boundary-optimum}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:physical-boundary}'s hypotheses,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_N\inf_{\mathcal E:\,Z_N(\mathcal E)>0}\TV(P_N,Q_{N,\mathcal E})
 =\min\left\{\min_{0\le r\le1}F(r),\,w_-,\,w_+\right\}.
 \label{eq:physical-optimal-boundary}
\end{equation}
For $w_-=w_+=1/2$ and $v_-=v_+=v$, this is
$\min\{2\Phi(b\sqrt v/2)-1,1/2\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the upper bound, as $\xi$ varies through $\R$,
\eqref{eq:boundary-phase-weights} realizes every strictly positive
pair of phase weights, so the boundary theorem realizes $F(r)$ for
every $r\in(0,1)$; continuity extends this to the infimum on $[0,1]$.
Centering the reservoir maximum at either phase, as in
\eqref{eq:centered-calibration}, gives the upper bounds $w_-$ and
$w_+$: its weight lies in $[0,1]$, tends to one in the selected phase
since $N/c_N\to0$, and to zero in the other since
$\Delta_N^2/c_N\to\infty$, so positive decomposition and macroscopic
separation give TV tending to the discarded phase weight.

For the lower bound consider a subsequence of arbitrary calibrations
with TV limit strictly below $\min(w_-,w_+)$, and let $R_N$ be their
normalized likelihood. Since $\TV(P_N,Q_N)=\E_{P_N}(1-R_N)_+$, each
phase loss is at most TV, so the overlap
$O_{i,N}=w_{i,N}\E_{P_{i,N}}\min(R_N,1)\ge w_{i,N}-\TV(P_N,Q_N)$
stays positive in both phases.
Tightness, the overlap bound from $R_N<\epsilon$, and
$P_N(R_N>M)\le1/M$ allow selection of feasible points
$x_N=e_{-,N}+O(\sqrt N)$ and $y_N=e_{+,N}+O(\sqrt N)$ with
$R_N(x_N),R_N(y_N)\in[\epsilon,M]$, for fixed positive $\epsilon,M$.
Set $\zeta_N=\log R_N(y_N)-\log R_N(x_N)=O(1)$ and
$D_N=y_N-x_N\asymp N$. Solving the exact likelihood for the composite
energy gives
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal E_N=x_N+
 \frac{D_N}{1-\exp[-(\beta_ND_N-\zeta_N)/c_N]}.
 \label{eq:boundary-overlap-calibration}
\end{equation}
A bounded $\zeta_N$ changes this by $O(c_N/D_N)=O(\sqrt N)$ from
the secant calibration for $x_N,y_N$, which uniformly on these windows
is
\[
 \frac{c_N}{\beta_N}+\frac{x_N+y_N}{2}
 +\frac{\beta_N(y_N-x_N)^2}{12c_N}
 +O\!\left(\frac{(y_N-x_N)^4}{c_N^3}\right).
\]
Moving its endpoints by $O(\sqrt N)$ changes it by $O(\sqrt N)$, so
$(\mathcal E_N-\mathcal E_N^{\mathrm{sec}})/\sqrt N$ is bounded.
On a convergent subsequence, Theorem~\ref{thm:physical-boundary}
gives the limit $F(r)\ge\min F$. Sequences with limiting error at
least $\min(w_-,w_+)$ already obey the bound. Applied to calibrations
within $1/N$ of the infimum (no minimizer need exist), this makes the
upper and lower bounds coincide, so the limit in
\eqref{eq:physical-optimal-boundary} exists and has the stated value.
In the balanced equal-variance case reflection sends $r$ to $1-r$,
so convexity minimizes $F$ at $1/2$, giving the normal formula.
\end{proof}

To compute $\min F$, set $r_-=r$, $r_+=1-r$, and $\lambda_i=b\sqrt{v_i}$.
For $\lambda_i>0$ the contribution of phase $i$ to the positive part of
reservoir minus reference, $r_iG_i^b-w_iG_i$, is
\begin{equation}
 r_i\Phi\!\left(\frac{\log(r_i/w_i)}{\lambda_i}+\frac{\lambda_i}{2}\right)
 -w_i\Phi\!\left(\frac{\log(r_i/w_i)}{\lambda_i}-\frac{\lambda_i}{2}\right).
 \label{eq:physical-optimum-cdf}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:physical-optimum-cdf} follows by integrating over
the half-line where the tilted Gaussian density exceeds the weighted
original density. Use zero for $r_i=0$, by continuity, and
$(r_i-w_i)_+$ when $\lambda_i=0$. The two contributions sum to $F(r)$
because the positive and negative masses agree. If both $\lambda_i>0$,
differentiation gives
\[
 F'(r)=
 \Phi\!\left(\frac{\log(r/w_-)}{\lambda_-}+\frac{\lambda_-}{2}\right)
 -\Phi\!\left(\frac{\log((1-r)/w_+)}{\lambda_+}+\frac{\lambda_+}{2}\right).
\]
This is strictly increasing from a negative to a positive value, so
its unique zero, where the two CDF arguments are equal, is the unique
minimizer. For equal positive variances this is $r=w_-$, even with
unequal target weights, and the optimized limit is
$\min\{2\Phi(b\sqrt v/2)-1,w_-,w_+\}$.

\subsection{The exact interior gain of the secant calibration}

Hypothesis~\eqref{eq:boundary-exception} involves the maximum of
$h_N^{\mathrm{sec}}$, which is explicit for the exact reservoir: at a
fraction $\theta$ of the gap,
\begin{equation}
 h_N(e_{-,N}+\theta\Delta_N)
 =c_N\left[\theta q_N+
 \log(1-\theta+\theta e^{-q_N})\right],
 \qquad q_N=\frac{\beta_N\Delta_N}{c_N}.
 \label{eq:physical-interior-exact}
\end{equation}
For $q_N\to0$, expanding the logarithm of the Bernoulli transform in
\eqref{eq:physical-interior-exact} gives, uniformly on the bounded
range $0\le\theta\le1$,
\begin{align}
 h_N(e_{-,N}+\theta\Delta_N)
 &=\frac{\beta_N^2\Delta_N^2}{2c_N}\theta(1-\theta)
 \notag\\
 &\quad+\frac{\beta_N^3\Delta_N^3}{6c_N^2}
       \theta(1-\theta)(2\theta-1)
 +O(\Delta_N^4/c_N^3).
 \label{eq:physical-interior-expansion}
\end{align}
The maximum lies between the centers since the endpoint slopes have
opposite signs. When $c_N/N^{3/2}\to\gamma$ and $\Delta_N/N\to\ell$,
the quadratic term is maximized at $\theta=1/2$, and the other terms,
including the cubic one, are $O(1)$. They do not change the $\sqrt N$
limit, but the quadratic term alone does not determine the $O(1)$
weights. Hence \eqref{eq:physical-interior-expansion} gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\max_Eh_N^{\mathrm{sec}}(E)}{\sqrt N}
 \longrightarrow\frac{\beta^2\ell^2}{8\gamma}.
 \label{eq:boundary-maximal-gain}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Microscopic applications of the boundary law}
\label{subsec:boundary-applications}

We verify the hypotheses of Theorems~\ref{thm:physical-boundary}
and~\ref{thm:physical-boundary-optimum} for the models of
Section~\ref{sec:microscopic}.

For the mean-field model of Theorem~\ref{thm:mf-bath}, the boundary law
holds for every $\gamma>0$ and kinetic parameter $a\ge0$. The
occupation bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:mf-exponential} is Gaussian on the
$\sqrt N$ scale, so it bounds $\E e^{t|X_{i,N}|}$ for every fixed $t$
and large $N$; so does the independent Gamma contribution, by its
exact transform, and there is no exceptional component. The variances
are \eqref{eq:mf-variances}; pure spins ($v_+=0$) are allowed, so no
kinetic energy is needed.

For the short-range model of Theorem~\ref{thm:sr-bath}, the boundary
law and its optimization hold for every $\gamma>0$ in fixed spatial
dimension $d\ge3$, and for all sufficiently large fixed $\gamma$ in
$d=2$. Since Lemma~\ref{lem:sr-energy-transfer} gives only a small
fixed exponential moment, we use the cutoff-free derivative bounds in
the window of width $\eta_0/L$, where $\eta_0$ is the constant $\eta$
of Lemma~\ref{lem:sr-restricted-derivatives}. For $d>2$ every fixed
multiple of $N^{-1/2}=L^{-d/2}$ eventually lies in that window, so the
second-derivative bound \eqref{eq:sr-restricted-second} and center
estimate \eqref{eq:sr-restricted-center} bound exponential moments of
the standardized bond count at every fixed parameter. The change from
inverse temperature to bond fugacity has bounded derivatives, and the
conditional binomial estimate and Cauchy--Schwarz argument of
Lemma~\ref{lem:sr-energy-transfer} transfer these bounds to the spin
energy. The weak limits for the contour phases agree with
\eqref{eq:sr-conditional-clt}, proved for the midpoint phases: the
small exponential moment bounds the conditional probability of the
opposite macroscopic midpoint phase by $Ce^{-c\sqrt N}$, the
exceptional contour probability vanishes, and positive limiting phase
weights identify the conditional laws.

The exceptional contour probability is at most $Ce^{-\tau_* L^{d-1}}$.
For $d>2$ this surface exponent dominates the gain
\eqref{eq:boundary-maximal-gain} of order $\sqrt N=L^{d/2}$, giving
\eqref{eq:boundary-exception} for every $\gamma$. For $d=2$, with
$\eta_*>0$ a valid spin-phase moment coefficient and $\tau_*>0$ a valid
exceptional surface constant, it suffices that
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\beta^2\ell}{2\gamma}<\eta_*,
 \qquad \frac{\beta^2\ell^2}{8\gamma}<\tau_*.
 \label{eq:two-dimensional-boundary-range}
\end{equation}
Both conditions hold for all sufficiently large $\gamma$; for smaller
$\gamma$ in $d=2$ these estimates assert nothing.

Instead of \eqref{eq:boundary-moment} and
\eqref{eq:boundary-exception}, one may assume the continuous density
envelope \eqref{eq:interior-density-envelope} with $\alpha>1/2$ and
\eqref{eq:local-density-assumption}; these also give the boundary law
and its finite corrections for every fixed $\gamma$. Put
$x=\min_i|E-e_{i,N}|$ on the interior. At the scaling
$c_N\sim\gamma N^{3/2}$, concavity gives
$h_N^{\mathrm{sec}}(E)\le Cx/\sqrt N$; with a finite calibration
correction the endpoint values are bounded but need not both vanish,
so $h_N(E)\le C_0+C_1x/\sqrt N$ with constants independent of $N$.
For $x\ge R\sqrt N$ the ratio of the linear term to $x^2/N$ is at
most $C_1/R$, and its ratio to $x^\alpha$ is at most
$C_1N^{1/2-\alpha}$, so choosing $R$ and then $N$ large absorbs this
term in the envelope; the offset adds only the bounded prefactor
$e^{C_0}$ to the tail integral, whose limit as $R\to\infty$ still
vanishes. Outside the centers the secant weight is at most one, and
with finite corrections concavity and the endpoint slope signs bound
the exterior weights by a constant, which suffices. Weighted tail
control and local density convergence then give the same integral
formula. At $\alpha=1/2$ the second ratio need not vanish.

Both $\alpha=1/2$ and the restricted range
\eqref{eq:two-dimensional-boundary-range} have a scale explanation: an
interface with surface cost of order $L^{d-1}=N^{(d-1)/d}$ competes
with a maximal reservoir gain of order $N^2/c_N$, and they balance at
$c_N\asymp N^{1+1/d}$, below the $N^{3/2}$ fluctuation threshold for
$d>2$ and equal to it for $d=2$. A surface bound alone does not
identify a favored droplet or slab;
Appendix~\ref{sec:capillarity-diagnostics} analyzes a stated
capillarity model for this comparison.
