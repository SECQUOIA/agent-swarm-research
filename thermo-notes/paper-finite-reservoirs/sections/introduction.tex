\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A subsystem in weak contact with a finite reservoir does not have
exactly its canonical distribution, because the reservoir entropy is a
concave rather than linear function of energy. Away from phase
transitions this matters little: nearly all canonical probability of a
subsystem of size $N$ lies in an energy window of width of order
$\sqrt N$, and a reservoir whose heat capacity grows faster than $N$
reproduces the canonical law. At first-order coexistence the canonical
law has two or more such windows, separated by energies of order $N$.
A reservoir that is accurate inside one window can then change the
relative weights of the phases, the fluctuations inside them, or both.
This paper determines how large a reservoir must be to reproduce the
complete canonical law of an interacting subsystem at coexistence, when
the total energy of the isolated composite is chosen as favorably as
possible.

The question arises whenever a system exchanges energy with an
environment not much larger than itself. Finite-bath and
Gaussian-ensemble Monte Carlo methods use such reservoirs deliberately
to sample first-order transitions
\cite{ChallaHetherington1988PRA,GriffinMattySwendsen2017}, and a
subsystem of a larger closed system thermalizes only as accurately as
the remainder allows \cite{RieraGogolinEisert2012}. In such settings
the reservoir heat capacity is expected to control the error, and the
practical question is how fast it must grow with subsystem size at
coexistence.

\paragraph{Setting.}
The subsystem has energy $E_N$ and canonical law $P_N$ at a fixed
inverse temperature. The reservoir has density of states
$\omega_B(U)\propto U^{c_N}$ for $U>0$, the form produced by
$2(c_N+1)$ classical quadratic degrees of freedom. We call $c_N$ the
reservoir capacity: it is the reservoir heat capacity in units of $k_B$
(Section~\ref{sec:framework} fixes the entropy convention). The coupling
is weak, and the only adjustable quantity is the total energy
$\mathcal E_N$ of the isolated composite; we call its choice the
calibration. We measure accuracy by the total variation distance
$\TV(P,Q)=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|$ between the subsystem's reservoir law $Q_N$
and $P_N$ on the full microscopic state space, and call it the
full-state TV. When it is small, every bounded observable, including the
phase label and the energy fluctuations within a phase, has nearly its
canonical distribution; matching thermodynamic potentials or a few
averages does not guarantee this. Because the reservoir reweights states
only through their energy, the full-state TV equals the TV between the
two energy distributions (Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-tv}). It can therefore
be computed from energy laws without replacing the microscopic system by
an energy-only model. We write $x_N\gg y_N$ when $x_N/y_N\to\infty$.

\subsection{The mechanism}

Up to a constant, the reservoir multiplies the canonical weight of a
subsystem state of energy $e$ by
$\exp[\beta e+c_N\log(\mathcal E_N-e)]$. The exponent is a concave
function of $e$ with curvature of order $1/c_N$ at the relevant
energies. Suppose first that three energies spread over a common scale
$b_N$ must all keep their canonical relative weights. A concave function
with curvature of order $1/c_N$ cannot take nearly equal values at three
such points unless $b_N^2/c_N\to0$, and no choice of $\mathcal E_N$
changes this. Two phases behave differently. Choosing $\mathcal E_N$ so
that the exponent takes equal values at two phase centers separated by
$\Delta_N$, which we call the secant calibration, leaves slopes of order
$\Delta_N/c_N$ at the centers. Across a phase of energy width $s_N$,
such a slope changes the log weight by order $\Delta_Ns_N/c_N$, so the
phase is preserved only if $c_N\gg\Delta_Ns_N$. For an extensive latent
heat and $\sqrt N$ fluctuations this is $c_N\gg N^{3/2}$, well below the
$N^2$ required for three distinct phase energies.

\subsection{Main results}

We prove two-sided capacity thresholds for the complete state law,
verify their hypotheses for interacting Potts Hamiltonians, compute the
exact error at the threshold scale and optimize it over all
calibrations, and determine the marginal, joint, and information limits
for two systems sharing a reservoir.

\paragraph{Sharp criteria (Section~\ref{sec:thresholds}).}
Suppose that on some scale $b_N$ the energy concentrates on at least
three distinct values, in the sense that $(E_N-a_N)/b_N$ converges
weakly to a law with at least three support points. Then some
calibration makes the full-state TV vanish if and only if
$c_N\gg b_N^2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:three-support}); no density or moment
bound is needed. A single phase with a nondegenerate Gaussian limit on
the $\sqrt N$ scale thus requires $c_N\gg N$, and three or more
distinct extensive phase energies require $c_N\gg N^2$. For two phases
with gap $\Delta_N$ and fluctuation scale $s_N$, the condition
$c_N\gg\Delta_Ns_N$ is necessary for every calibration as soon as one
phase has a nondegenerate fluctuation limit
(Theorem~\ref{thm:two-scale-necessity}). It is sufficient, with the
secant calibration, under exponential moment bounds within each phase
and a bound on the exceptional mass, the canonical probability of
configurations assigned to neither phase, such as those with
macroscopic interfaces (Theorem~\ref{thm:positive-sufficiency} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:two-phase-iff}). What matters is the number of
distinct phase \emph{energies}: symmetry-related ordered phases with the
same energy do not create a three-energy obstruction.

\paragraph{Microscopic models (Section~\ref{sec:microscopic} and
Appendix~\ref{app:short-range-proof}).}
The threshold is exactly $c_N\gg N^{3/2}$ for the three-state
Curie--Weiss--Potts model and for the nearest-neighbor Potts model with
a fixed, sufficiently large number of colors in every dimension
$d\ge2$ (Theorems~\ref{thm:mf-bath} and~\ref{thm:sr-bath}), with or
without independent kinetic energy (for the short-range model, see the
end of Appendix~\ref{app:short-range-proof}). For the short-range
model the difficulty is sufficiency: the secant calibration can raise
the weight of rare interfacial configurations by up to
$\exp[O(N^2/c_N)]$. We therefore need a bound on the canonical
probability of these configurations themselves; a signed error bound
on the partition function alone does not give this control. We
obtain it from positive restricted contour partition functions, which
also give exponential moments of the phase energies, and we transfer
these moments from the number of occupied bonds to the spin energy
through the Edwards--Sokal coupling.

\paragraph{A reservoir smaller than the subsystem's heat capacity.}
In both models, with limiting phase weights $w_-,w_+$ (the canonical
probabilities of the two phases), the subsystem's own canonical heat
capacity is of order $N^2$ at coexistence:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{C_{S,\mathrm{can}}}{k_BN^2}
 =\frac{\beta_N^2\Var_{P_N}(E_N)}{N^2}
 \longrightarrow\beta^2w_-w_+\ell^2,
 \qquad \ell=\lim_N\Delta_N/N.
 \label{eq:canonical-coexistence-capacity}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the bounded spin energy per particle converges in law to a
two-atom law, and optional independent kinetic energy with Gamma shape
$aN$ ($a=0$ meaning none) adds only $aN/\beta_N^2$ to the variance. The
$N^2$ term comes from the latent heat; the within-phase variances are at
most of order $N$. Yet a reservoir with $N^{3/2}\ll c_N\ll N^2$, which
is still much larger than the subsystem, reproduces the coexisting law.
Absorbing the latent heat changes the reservoir temperature enough to
bias the phase odds by a factor of order $\exp(N^2/c_N)$, but the
secant calibration cancels this bias. What remains is a change of order
$N^{3/2}/c_N$ in the log weight across each phase, and this sets the
threshold.

\paragraph{The threshold regime (Section~\ref{sec:boundary-smooth}).}
At the boundary scale $c_N\sim\gamma N^{3/2}$, the energy distribution
of each phase is exponentially tilted, which shifts it by a finite
number of standard deviations. We compute the limiting full-state TV
exactly (Theorem~\ref{thm:physical-boundary}). A further calibration
shift of order $\sqrt N$ restores the phase weights but not the
within-phase distributions of phases with nondegenerate fluctuations
(Corollary~\ref{cor:physical-compensation}).
Optimizing over every calibration gives the smallest achievable
limiting error (Theorem~\ref{thm:physical-boundary-optimum}), which can
be attained by discarding one phase entirely; an optimized distance
should therefore be reported together with the resulting phase weights.
These results use stronger phase-tail and exceptional-mass hypotheses,
which hold for every $\gamma>0$ in the mean-field model and in the
short-range model for $d\ge3$, and in two dimensions for sufficiently
large $\gamma$.

\paragraph{Shared reservoirs (Section~\ref{sec:shared-baths} and
Appendix~\ref{app:shared-extensions}).}
Consider two otherwise independent copies of a two-phase system coupled
to one reservoir with $N\ll c_N\ll N^2$, and place the reservoir maximum
at the opposite-phase pair energy $e_{-,N}+e_{+,N}$, midway between the
two same-phase energies. Same-phase pairs are then suppressed, while
the ordinary fluctuations inside each phase survive
(Theorem~\ref{thm:shared-selection}). The mutual information between the
two full microscopic states tends to one bit for any phase weights
(Proposition~\ref{prop:shared-one-bit}). With balanced phase weights,
each copy's full-state law also converges to its canonical law, whereas
the joint TV error tends to $1/2$. In the spin models, balance is
obtained by shifting the reference temperature by order $1/N$ from
coexistence; the reservoir uses the same shifted temperature, and the
target is the balanced canonical law at that temperature. Joint
canonical accuracy requires $c_N\gg N^2$, because the combined energy of
two two-phase copies has three macroscopic support points
(Corollary~\ref{cor:shared-joint-threshold}).
Appendix~\ref{app:shared-extensions} treats a prescribed number of
copies in each phase among $m$ copies, and the correlated fluctuations
that appear when $c_N$ is proportional to $N$.

\paragraph{Exactly solvable models and diagnostics
(Appendices~\ref{sec:gaussian-geometry}
and~\ref{sec:capillarity-diagnostics}).}
For Gaussian phases that exchange several quantities with a reservoir
of isotropic quadratic entropy, the threshold is on the $N^{3/2}$ scale
if the phase points lie on a common sphere and on the $N^2$ scale
otherwise (Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-geometry}); between these scales
we compute the smallest achievable error, which is the weight of the
phases that must be discarded (Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-phase-loss}).
The reservoir can raise the weight of an interfacial configuration by
at most $\exp[O(N^2/c_N)]$, while its canonical weight is suppressed by
a surface cost of order $N^{(d-1)/d}$. These balance at
$c_N\asymp N^{1+1/d}$, which is below $N^{3/2}$ for $d\ge3$ and equal to
it for $d=2$; a stated two-dimensional capillarity model makes this
competition precise and motivates the large-$\gamma$ restriction above.
Finally, two examples show that an unweighted Euclidean histogram norm
can vanish while the full-state TV does not, and that, for an extensive
gap under the secant calibration, absolute accuracy of the log-density
barrier between a phase center and the interphase midpoint requires
$c_N\gg N^2$.

\paragraph{Calibration precision.}
The secant calibration must fix the reservoir energy, or equivalently
its temperature at the phase centers, to relative accuracy $o(1/N)$ to
preserve the phase weights; a relative error of order $1/N$ changes the
phase odds by a factor of order one. This is no stricter than the
canonical target itself, whose phase odds change by a factor of order
one when $\beta$ changes by order $1/N$. Section~\ref{sec:boundary-smooth}
quantifies calibration errors of order $\sqrt N$ in $\mathcal E_N$ at
the threshold scale.

\subsection{Relation to earlier work}

Finite-reservoir ensembles and their fluctuation corrections are well
established. Challa and Hetherington introduced Gaussian ensembles as a
controllable interpolation between familiar ensembles and showed how
they change first-order energy histograms
\cite{ChallaHetherington1988PRL,ChallaHetherington1988PRA}. A Gaussian
ensemble is the quadratic approximation of the reservoir used here,
with curvature about $\beta^2/c_N$. The two-Gaussian coexistence
description of Challa, Landau, and Binder \cite{ChallaLandauBinder1986}
already separates an extensive latent energy gap from ordinary
within-phase widths, and their later review covers finite-size and
finite-bath approaches \cite{ChallaLandauBinder1990}. Within such a
two-Gaussian picture, the mechanism above gives the $N^{3/2}$ scale
heuristically; our contribution is the two-sided theorem, its precise
tail conditions, and its proof for interacting Hamiltonians.
Power-law subsystem laws from finite reservoirs of constant heat capacity are standard
\cite{Campisi2007}, and the surface and volume entropy conventions for
the reservoir are compared in
\cite{HilbertHanggiDunkel2014,DunkelHilbert2014}.

For a single phase, the condition $c_N\gg N$ is the expected
equivalence-of-ensembles scale. It parallels the sharp growing-block
conditional limit theorems of Diaconis and Freedman
\cite{DiaconisFreedman1988}, which treat a regular exponential-family
conditioning problem rather than the phase mixture of an interacting
subsystem coupled to a separate reservoir. Riera, Gogolin, and Eisert
\cite{RieraGogolinEisert2012} control a reduced microcanonical-shell
state in trace distance by the nonlinear remainder of the reservoir
entropy. In their reservoir example a sufficient size is proportional
to the square of the subsystem energy range; in a commuting setting
this is a TV bound of the $N^2$ type. A bound based on the full energy
range is not sharp for a single phase, where $c_N\gg N$ suffices. We
show that two coexisting phases need only $N^{3/2}$ after calibration, and that $N^2$
is sharp when three distinct phase energies must be preserved. Our
results concern classical systems, described by probability measures on
the state space.

The closest numerical precedent is the finite-reservoir Potts study of
Griffin, Matty, and Swendsen \cite{GriffinMattySwendsen2017}. They
measure agreement by an unweighted Euclidean norm of energy-histogram
differences and optimize a comparison temperature;
Appendix~\ref{sec:capillarity-diagnostics} gives an exact example in
which that norm vanishes while the full-state TV does not. Generalized
Gaussian ensembles recover nonconcave entropies and macrostate
equivalence at a fixed penalty
\cite{CosteniucEllisTouchette2006,CosteniucEllisTouchetteTurkington2005},
and squeezed ensembles select first-order states with finite-size
conversion formulas \cite{YonetaShimizu2019}. Both address
thermodynamic-limit questions at a fixed penalty, whereas we study the
complete law as the reservoir curvature shrinks with $N$.

For shared reservoirs, the ensemble dependence of fluctuations and
correlations is classical \cite{LebowitzPercusVerlet1967,Mishin2015}.
Ram\'irez-Hern\'andez, Larralde, and Leyvraz found opposite-phase
assignments in two weakly coupled identical systems with negative
specific heat \cite{RamirezHernandezLarraldeLeyvraz2008}, and Cohen,
Rittenberg, and Sadhu studied ensemble-dependent shared information,
including order-one contributions from degenerate phases
\cite{CohenRittenbergSadhu2015}. We add the capacity window in which a
separate reservoir selects opposite phases, and we prove the limits of
the full marginal error, the joint error, and the microscopic mutual
information in that window.
