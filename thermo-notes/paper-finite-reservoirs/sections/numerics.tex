\section{Deterministic numerical illustrations}
\label{sec:numerics}

Figure~\ref{fig:potts-scaling} shows exact finite-$N$ calculations for
the mean-field model with $J=1$, $\beta=4\log2$, and $a=1/2$.
Occupation triples are enumerated modulo color permutations, and the
Gamma and Beta conditional energy integrals are evaluated through
their distribution functions (Section~\ref{sec:microscopic}), so the
plotted quantity is the full-state TV of the spin--momentum law.
No Monte Carlo sampling or Gaussian approximation enters the finite-$N$ points.

\begin{figure}[t]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=.94\linewidth]{figures/potts-scaling.pdf}
 \caption{Exact finite-system mean-field Potts calculations with the
 exact reservoir at the secant calibration. Points are occupation sums
 and Gamma/Beta CDF evaluations, connected to guide the eye. Dotted
 lines are the proved secant boundary limits for $c_N=\gamma N^{3/2}$,
 with $\gamma=0.5$ and $2$. Only these two sequences extend to
 $N=12000$; the others stop at $N=6000$.
 These are mean-field data and do not test a short-range
 interfacial law.}
 \label{fig:potts-scaling}
\end{figure}

At $c_N=0.5N^{3/2}$ the predicted limit is approximately $0.157392$
and the exact $N=12000$ value is $0.148341$; at $c_N=2N^{3/2}$ they
are $0.037986$ and $0.037304$. These are errors at the secant
calibration, not finite-$N$ optima. The figure also shows
$c_N=N^{7/4}$, above the threshold, where the error decays, and
$c_N=N^{5/4}$, below it, where it persists. Such finite sizes cannot
establish an asymptotic exponent; the theorems do.

These sizes are also far from the asymptotic regime of the phase
decomposition. The saddle of the rate function \eqref{eq:mf-rate} at
$(1/2,1/4,1/4)$ lies on the Voronoi boundary between the ordered and
disordered cells, with rate excess
$I(1/2,1/4,1/4)-I_{\min}=\log3-\tfrac{19}{12}\log2\simeq1.1\times10^{-3}$,
so the interphase valley is suppressed only by $\exp(-0.0011N)$, of
order one below $N\simeq10^3$. Exact pure-spin occupation sums
show the slow approach. With Euclidean Voronoi
distance in the three occupation fractions and ties assigned to the
ordered phase, so that the combined ordered event is
$\max_j n_j\ge N/2$, the Voronoi-conditional ordered variance divided
by $N$ is $1.02$ at $N=3000$ and $0.78$ at $N=12000$, against the
pure-spin limit $v_-=J^2/[6(3-\beta J)]\simeq0.733$ (the $a=0$ case
of \eqref{eq:mf-variances}), while the ordered
weight is $0.528$ and $0.529$ against $w_-\simeq0.530$ from
\eqref{eq:mf-phase-weights}. The dotted lines use the limiting
constants.

Figure~\ref{fig:boundary-optimization} shows the proved limiting
formula for balanced phases of equal variance, not finite-size data.
Writing $\lambda=b\sqrt v$, preserving the phase weights gives
$2\Phi(\lambda/2)-1$, whereas optimizing the calibration gives
$\min\{2\Phi(\lambda/2)-1,1/2\}$, whose second branch discards one
phase.

The accompanying source bundle contains the finite-system data,
figure code, and independent checks. Small systems are checked against
explicit enumeration of all $3^N$ labeled spin states for
$1\le N\le8$; at $N=12$, direct absolute-density integration verifies
the Gamma/Beta crossing formula for two kinetic shapes; and separate
normal-density quadratures verify the weighted CDF expression
\eqref{eq:physical-optimum-cdf} and its optimizer. These
floating-point calculations are numerical checks, not interval
certificates or substitutes for the proofs. The reproducibility
instructions separate quick checks and figure regeneration from the
expensive full occupation enumeration through $N=12000$.
