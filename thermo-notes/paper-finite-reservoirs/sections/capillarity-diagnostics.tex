\section{Interfacial tails and accuracy diagnostics}
\label{sec:capillarity-diagnostics}

This appendix supplies the capillarity model behind the two-dimensional
boundary range \eqref{eq:two-dimensional-boundary-range} and the exponent
$\alpha=1/2$ in the density criterion, and it records two diagnostic
distinctions: histogram norms versus total variation, and barrier
accuracy versus full-law accuracy.

\subsection{A stated capillarity model and what it proves}

The competition between background fluctuations and droplet surface
costs is established in the work of Biskup, Chayes, and
Koteck\'y~\cite{BiskupChayesKotecky2002}. The droplet-to-strip shape
transition on a periodic square, at fixed excess volume fraction, was
located by Leung and Zia~\cite{LeungZia1990} and analyzed for
first-order simulations by Neuhaus and Hager~\cite{NeuhausHager2003};
droplet and strip energy regions in a toroidal two-dimensional Potts
system are exhibited by Kim, Keyes, and
Straub~\cite{KimKeyesStraub2011}. These results motivate the following
capillarity model; none of them supplies this paper's assumed full
density envelope or derives the square-torus rate used below for our
microscopic systems.

To make the interfacial competition precise without importing an
unproved microscopic rate function, consider the following model.
Assume $Y_N=(E-e_{-,N})/\Delta_N$ is supported in a fixed compact
interval $K$ containing $[0,1]$ and satisfies a large-deviation
principle with speed $\sqrt N$ and good rate $I$. The speed $\sqrt N$
is the two-dimensional surface scale $N^{(d-1)/d}$ at $d=2$; the
model is stated for that case, where the surface and fluctuation
scales coincide. Assume $I$ is
continuous and finite on $[0,1]$, infinite on $K\setminus[0,1]$,
zero exactly at $0,1$, and positive on $(0,1)$.
The compact support is a convenient explicit hypothesis for this
variational model, not an assertion about systems with unbounded
kinetic energy.

\begin{proposition}[Capillarity variational law]
\label{prop:capillarity-ldp}
For $\Delta_N/N\to\ell>0$ and $c_N/N^{3/2}\to\gamma>0$, physical
secant reweighting gives a large-deviation principle at speed
$\sqrt N$ with rate
\begin{equation}
 I_\gamma(\theta)=I(\theta)-J_\gamma(\theta)
 -\min_{0\le u\le1}\{I(u)-J_\gamma(u)\},
 \qquad
 J_\gamma(\theta)=\frac{\beta^2\ell^2}{2\gamma}\theta(1-\theta).
 \label{eq:capillarity-rate}
\end{equation}
If
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_*=\frac{\beta^2\ell^2}{2}
 \sup_{0<\theta<1}\frac{\theta(1-\theta)}{I(\theta)}<\infty,
 \label{eq:capillarity-threshold}
\end{equation}
then $\gamma>\gamma_*$ leaves only the endpoints as rate minimizers.
For $\gamma<\gamma_*$ all minimizing energies lie away from the
endpoints and $\TV(P_N,Q_N)\to1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:physical-interior-exact}, expanded on the fixed
compact $K$, gives $h_N/\sqrt N\to J_\gamma$ uniformly.
The cutoff lies beyond $K$ for large $N$ because $c_N/\Delta_N\to\infty$.
For a continuous bounded tilt on a compact space, the large-deviation
upper bound applied to a finite cover of sets where the tilt varies
by at most $\epsilon$, and the lower bound on an open neighborhood
of each point, give
\[
 \lim_N\frac1{\sqrt N}\log\E e^{h_N}
 =\max_{\theta\in K}\{J_\gamma(\theta)-I(\theta)\}.
\]
The same bounds for restricted integrals prove
\eqref{eq:capillarity-rate}. If $\gamma>\gamma_*$,
$I(\theta)>J_\gamma(\theta)$ throughout the interior.
If $\gamma<\gamma_*$, the minimum of $I-J_\gamma$ is strictly
negative, whereas its endpoint values are zero. Compactness and
continuity place its minimizing set a positive distance from the
endpoints. A neighborhood of that set has reservoir probability
tending to one and canonical probability tending to zero, proving TV.
\end{proof}

For $\gamma>\gamma_*$ the proposition specifies macroscopic energy
concentration, not full-state TV or the fluctuation law within a
phase. At equality, a rate function alone cannot determine weights
among tied minimizers. Multiplication by arbitrary positive constants,
or factors with logarithm $o(\sqrt N)$, near tied minima leaves the
same large-deviation rate and can change their weights or select a
single minimum. This is nonidentifiability from the stated model
input, rather than a missing consequence of the rate principle.

For an explicit square-torus capillarity model, set
\begin{equation}
 I(\theta)=\tau\min\{2\sqrt{\pi\theta},\,2,\,
                         2\sqrt{\pi(1-\theta)}\},
 \qquad 0\le\theta\le1,\quad \tau>0.
 \label{eq:square-torus-model}
\end{equation}
The three prescribed branches represent an isotropic circular
droplet, a slab with two interfaces, and the complementary bubble.
This is the isotropic-tension form of the standard droplet, strip, and
bubble construction on a square torus
\cite{LeungZia1990,NeuhausHager2003}; its droplet-to-strip crossover
is at $\theta=1/\pi$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:square-torus-model} defines the variational model;
it is not a derived Wulff law for the lattice Potts model.

\begin{corollary}[Square-torus minimizers]
\label{cor:square-capillarity}
For \eqref{eq:square-torus-model},
$\gamma_*=\beta^2\ell^2/(16\tau)$.
Above $\gamma_*$ the only minimizers are $0,1$; below it the unique
minimizer is $1/2$; at equality the minimizers are $0,1/2,1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For all $\theta$,
\begin{equation}
 I(\theta)\ge8\tau\theta(1-\theta),
 \label{eq:square-perimeter-bound}
\end{equation}
with equality exactly at $0,1/2,1$.
The slab branch obeys this because $\theta(1-\theta)\le1/4$.
For the droplet branch, away from $\theta=0$ the desired bound follows
from
$8\sqrt\theta(1-\theta)\le16/(3\sqrt3)<2\sqrt\pi$;
the bubble branch is symmetric.
Put $A=\beta^2\ell^2/(2\gamma)$.
If $A<8\tau$, only endpoints minimize $I-A\theta(1-\theta)$.
If $A=8\tau$, equality in \eqref{eq:square-perimeter-bound} identifies
the three minimizers. If $A>8\tau$, using $I(1/2)=2\tau$ gives
\[
 [I(\theta)-A\theta(1-\theta)]-[I(1/2)-A/4]
 \ge(A-8\tau)(\theta-1/2)^2,
\]
which is strictly positive off $1/2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Histogram norms can hide a persistent error}

The distinction between TV and an unscaled density norm is visible
even in one phase. Let $p_\sigma$ be the density of $\Normal(0,\sigma^2)$
and $q_\sigma$ that of $\Normal(0,r\sigma^2)$, for fixed $0<r<1$.
Direct Gaussian integration gives
\begin{equation}
 \|p_\sigma-q_\sigma\|_2^2
 =\frac1{2\sqrt\pi\,\sigma}
 \left[1+r^{-1/2}-2\sqrt{\frac2{1+r}}\right]\longrightarrow0
 \quad(\sigma\to\infty),
 \label{eq:histogram-l2}
\end{equation}
although
\begin{equation}
 \TV(p_\sigma,q_\sigma)
 =2\left[
 \Phi\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log r}{r-1}}\right)
 -\Phi\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{r\log r}{r-1}}\right)\right]>0.
 \label{eq:histogram-tv}
\end{equation}
For the latter formula, the two densities cross at
$|E|/\sigma=\sqrt{r\log r/(r-1)}$; integrating their difference
inside the crossings gives \eqref{eq:histogram-tv}.
For histogram probabilities in bins of fixed width $a>0$,
a rescaled Riemann sum gives
$\sum_j(P_j-Q_j)^2\sim a\|p_\sigma-q_\sigma\|_2^2$.
Consequently its Euclidean norm decays as $\sigma^{-1/2}$, or
$N^{-1/4}$ when $\sigma\asymp\sqrt N$. The normal laws still have
a fixed variance ratio and fixed TV error.
This is a normalization effect of the chosen norm. It is the
distinction promised in Section~\ref{sec:introduction}: the
finite-bath Potts comparison of Griffin, Matty, and Swendsen measures
agreement by the unweighted Euclidean norm of energy-probability
differences, their equation~(23), and reports its decay with size
\cite{GriffinMattySwendsen2017}. The example shows that such decay is
compatible with a fixed within-phase variance ratio and a fixed
full-state error. Gaussian reservoir fluctuation formulas themselves
are established \cite{ChallaHetherington1988PRL}.

\subsection{Barrier accuracy requires a separate scale}

Suppose canonical and reservoir energy densities are positive at
specified energies $e_-$ and $E^\dagger$, and define their fixed-energy
log barriers by
$B_P=\log[p(e_-)/p(E^\dagger)]$ and
$B_Q=\log[q(e_-)/q(E^\dagger)]$.
With $h(e_-)=0$, normalization cancels exactly:
\begin{equation}
 B_Q-B_P=-h(E^\dagger).
 \label{eq:fixed-barrier-shift}
\end{equation}
The same identity holds for ratios of point probabilities in a
discrete energy law.
For a quadratic endpoint-balanced weight with gap $\Delta=2m$,
the midpoint gain is $\kappa m^2/2=\kappa\Delta^2/8$.
For the exact physical secant bath,
\begin{equation}
 h\!\left(\frac{e_-+e_+}{2}\right)
 =c\log\cosh\!\left(\frac{\beta\Delta}{2c}\right).
 \label{eq:physical-exact-barrier}
\end{equation}
It follows directly from \eqref{eq:physical-interior-exact} at
$\theta=1/2$, and is positive.

If $c\gg\Delta$, the lowering is
$\beta^2\Delta^2/(8c)\,[1+O((\Delta/c)^2)]$.
More generally, for $\Delta\to\infty$ and inverse temperature bounded
away from zero and infinity, its vanishing is equivalent to
$c\gg\Delta^2$. Indeed, $\log\cosh x$ is bounded below by a positive
constant times $\min(x^2,|x|)$ and above by $x^2/2$. The lower bound
follows from continuity of the corresponding ratios on $0<|x|\le1$
and $|x|\ge1$, with positive limits at zero and infinity.
Thus a subsequence with $\Delta/c$ bounded below has barrier error
of order at least $\Delta$, while in the other regime it is
comparable to $\Delta^2/c$.

For an extensive gap, absolute midpoint-barrier accuracy therefore
requires $c\gg N^2$, even though the full-state two-phase TV error
can vanish for $c\gg N^{3/2}$. If a specified canonical barrier has
size $B_P\asymp N^{(d-1)/d}$ and $c\gg N$, relative accuracy of this
fixed-energy barrier instead requires
$c\gg N^2/B_P\asymp N^{1+1/d}$.
These statements concern density ratios at specified energies.
They do not locate shifted peaks or saddle points, nor do they
constitute a theorem about a dynamical transition rate.
