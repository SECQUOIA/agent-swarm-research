\section{Exact Gaussian models and calibration geometry}
\label{sec:gaussian-geometry}

This appendix makes calibration geometry exactly solvable by
reweighting Gaussian phases with a quadratic log-weight whose
curvature $\kappa_N\ge0$ stands for the entropy
curvature~\eqref{eq:smooth-capacity}. Gaussian reservoirs and
coexistence peaks are
classical~\cite{ChallaHetherington1988PRL,ChallaLandauBinder1986};
here they are exact models only, and their interphase tails are not
substituted for the microscopic laws of Section~\ref{sec:microscopic}.
The identification $\kappa=\beta^2/(C_B/k_B)$ holds only locally,
because an exactly quadratic entropy has constant curvature, not
constant heat capacity.

\subsection{Two scalar phases and the optimized error crossover}

With $\varphi_{s^2}$ the centered normal density of standard
deviation $s>0$, consider
\begin{equation}
 p_N(E)=\tfrac12\varphi_{s_N^2}(E-m_N)+\tfrac12\varphi_{s_N^2}(E+m_N),
 \quad m_N/s_N\to\infty,
 \qquad
 q_{N,t}\propto p_N e^{tE-\kappa_NE^2/2},\quad\kappa_N\ge0.
 \label{eq:scalar-gaussian-model}
\end{equation}
The unrestricted field $t\in\R$ acts as the calibration;
completing the square gives the transformed variance, means, and
upper-phase weight
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde s_N^2=\frac{s_N^2}{1+\kappa_Ns_N^2},\qquad
 \widetilde m_{\pm,N}=\frac{\pm m_N+t s_N^2}{1+\kappa_Ns_N^2},\qquad
 r_{+,N}(t)=\frac1{1+e^{-2tm_N/(1+\kappa_Ns_N^2)}}.
 \label{eq:scalar-gaussian-completion}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Scalar convergence and optimized crossover]
\label{prop:scalar-gaussian}
There exists a field sequence giving $\TV(p_N,q_{N,t_N})\to0$ if and
only if $\kappa_Nm_Ns_N\to0$. If instead
$\kappa_Nm_Ns_N\to a\in[0,\infty)$, then
\begin{align}
 \TV(p_N,q_{N,0})&\longrightarrow2\Phi(a/2)-1,
 \label{eq:scalar-balanced-crossover}\\
 \inf_{t\in\R}\TV(p_N,q_{N,t})
       &\longrightarrow\min\{2\Phi(a/2)-1,1/2\}.
 \label{eq:scalar-optimal-crossover}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $\rho_N=m_N/s_N$, $k_N=\kappa_Ns_N^2$, and $b_N=t_Ns_N$.
In units of $s_N$, the transformed means are
$(\pm\rho_N+b_N)/(1+k_N)$, with variance $(1+k_N)^{-1}$.
If $\kappa_Nm_Ns_N\to0$, then $k_N\to0$, and $t_N=0$ preserves the
weights and makes both standardized displacements vanish, so
each component converges in TV.

Necessity mirrors the label matching for
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-geometry}. For fixed $R>0$, the windows
$|E\mp m_N|\le Rs_N$ are disjoint for large $N$, each with target mass
tending to $\tfrac12[2\Phi(R)-1]$. Since $\widetilde s_N\le s_N$ and
$m_N/s_N\to\infty$, each transformed component has vanishing
probability in one of them, so TV convergence makes each component
supply one window, with weight of lower limit at least
$\tfrac12[2\Phi(R)-1]$. As $R\to\infty$, the weights tend to $1/2$
and the centers are $\widetilde m_{\pm,N}=\pm m_N+O(s_N)$, in natural
order. Subtracting these estimates and using the exact means gives
\[
 \frac{2\rho_N}{1+k_N}=2\rho_N+O(1),\qquad
 \frac{k_N\rho_N}{1+k_N}=O(1).
\]
Since $\rho_N\to\infty$, $k_N\to0$ and the variance ratio tends to one.
Restricting to the matched windows and letting $R\to\infty$
gives TV convergence of each component to its target normal, so
its probability above the target mean,
$\Phi(\delta_{\pm,N}\sqrt{1+k_N})$ with displacement
$\delta_{\pm,N}=(b_N\mp k_N\rho_N)/(1+k_N)$, tends to $1/2$. Hence
both displacements vanish; subtracting them gives $k_N\rho_N\to0$.

For the finite crossover, $k_N\to0$ since $k_N\rho_N\to a$ and
$\rho_N\to\infty$. At $t_N=0$ the limiting standardized components are
unit-variance normals displaced by $\mp a$; separating their windows
gives~\eqref{eq:scalar-balanced-crossover}, using
$\TV(\Normal(0,1),\Normal(a,1))=2\Phi(|a|/2)-1$.

To optimize, take any fields and a subsequence on which the
transformed upper weight converges. If its limit lies in $(0,1)$,
the logistic formula in \eqref{eq:scalar-gaussian-completion} gives
$b_N=O(1/\rho_N)$, so the displacements still tend to $\mp a$; the
limiting TV, a convex function of the limiting upper weight that is
symmetric about $1/2$ by reflection, is minimized there, giving the
first term in~\eqref{eq:scalar-optimal-crossover}. If one weight
tends to zero, a single bounded-width Gaussian cannot cover both
target windows, so the lower bound is $1/2$, also when the means
escape all windows. These bounds hold for arbitrary approximate
minimizers. The choice $t_N=0$ attains the first bound; for $a>0$,
$t_N=\kappa_Nm_N$ centers the upper component at $m_N$ with
weight tending to one and attains $1/2$; for $a=0$ the first bound
already vanishes.
\end{proof}

With $\kappa_N=\beta^2/c_N$, $m_N=\Delta_N/2$, $s_N=\sqrt{vN}$,
$\Delta_N/N\to\ell$, and $c_N/N^{3/2}\to\gamma$, one has $a=b\sqrt v$
with $b$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:physical-boundary}, and the limits
above are the balanced equal-variance cases of
\eqref{eq:physical-balanced-boundary} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:physical-boundary-optimum}. Above
$a_*=2\Phi^{-1}(3/4)\simeq1.34898$, near-optimal fields keep one
phase accurately and discard the other.

A fixed tangent changes the required scale. Give the upper
reference phase weight $w\ne1/2$, $0<w<1$, and hold the tangent of
the quadratic weight at the target canonical mean
$\bar E_N=(2w-1)m_N$, not at a self-consistent mean:
$q_N\propto p_Ne^{-\kappa_N(E-\bar E_N)^2/2}$, or
$t_N=\kappa_N\bar E_N$. The exact phase odds are
\begin{equation}
 \log\frac{r_{+,N}}{1-r_{+,N}}
 =\log\frac w{1-w}
   +\frac{2\kappa_N(2w-1)m_N^2}{1+\kappa_Ns_N^2}.
 \label{eq:gaussian-untuned-odds}
\end{equation}
The separated windows again force $\kappa_Ns_N^2\to0$; the weights
then converge if and only if $\kappa_Nm_N^2\to0$, which also removes
the shape errors. For $m_N\asymp N$ and $s_N\asymp\sqrt N$, this
fixed tangent needs $\kappa_NN^2\to0$, whereas
$t_N=0$, which like the secant calibration equalizes the reservoir
weight at the two phase centers, needs only $\kappa_NN^{3/2}\to0$.

\subsection{Several exchanged quantities and phase geometry}

Exact invariants limit which weights a field can restore. Let
$Y\in\R^d$, where $d$ counts exchanged quantities (not spatial
dimensions), take distinct phase points $e_1,\ldots,e_k$ with
positive weights $w_i$ summing to one, and set
\begin{equation}
 p_N=\sum_{i=1}^k w_i\Normal(Ne_i,NI_d),\qquad
 q_{N,t}\propto p_N\exp(t\cdot Y-\kappa_N|Y|^2/2),
 \quad t\in\R^d,\quad\kappa_N\ge0.
 \label{eq:multivariate-gaussian-model}
\end{equation}
With
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_N=(1+\kappa_NN)^{-1},\qquad
 \alpha_N=\frac{\kappa_NN^2}{1+\kappa_NN},\qquad
 z_N=\frac{Nt}{1+\kappa_NN},
 \label{eq:multivariate-parameters}
\end{equation}
the transformed covariance is $N\lambda_NI_d$, the means are
$N\lambda_N(e_i+t)$, and the weights are
\begin{equation}
 r_{i,N}=\frac{w_i\exp(z_N\cdot e_i-\alpha_N|e_i|^2/2)}
                 {\sum_jw_j\exp(z_N\cdot e_j-\alpha_N|e_j|^2/2)}.
 \label{eq:multivariate-weights}
\end{equation}
For any affine dependence $(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$, meaning
$\sum_i a_i=0$ and $\sum_i a_ie_i=0$, the field cancels, giving the
exact invariant
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i a_i\log(r_{i,N}/w_i)
       =-\frac{\alpha_N}{2}\sum_i a_i|e_i|^2.
 \label{eq:multivariate-invariant}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Geometry of Gaussian reservoir convergence]
\label{thm:gaussian-geometry}
For $k\ge2$, some field sequence in
\eqref{eq:multivariate-gaussian-model} gives TV convergence if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \begin{cases}
 \kappa_NN^{3/2}\to0,&\text{if the phase points are cospherical},\\
 \kappa_NN^2\to0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:gaussian-geometry-classification}
\end{equation}
For $k=1$, the condition is $\kappa_NN\to0$. Cospherical means
that the points lie on a common sphere of positive radius in $\R^d$;
in $d=1$ a sphere is a pair of points, so three distinct scalar
phase points are never cospherical.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
To match component labels, note that on $Y/N$ every transformed
covariance is at most $I_d/N$, so if TV tends to zero, each target
phase neighborhood of positive mass receives a distinct transformed
component of nonvanishing weight. With $k$ components this is a
bijection, and the matched centers $\lambda_N(e_i+t_N)$ lie in
bounded neighborhoods of the phase points. Along any subsequence, pass
to a further one on which $\lambda_N$, $\lambda_Nt_N$, and the
matching permutation converge. The limiting map $e\mapsto\lambda e+a$
maps the finite phase set onto itself; its positive diameter forces
$\lambda=1$, the arithmetic centroid forces $a=0$, and distinct phase
points make the permutation the identity. Hence $\lambda_N\to1$,
$\lambda_Nt_N\to0$, and $r_{i,N}\to w_i$ with the original labels.

Isolating each matched component in expanding fluctuation windows
gives its Gaussian TV convergence; in particular,
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt N\{(\lambda_N-1)e_i+\lambda_Nt_N\}\longrightarrow0
 \quad\hbox{for every }i.
 \label{eq:gaussian-shape-necessity}
\end{equation}
Subtracting two equations in~\eqref{eq:gaussian-shape-necessity}
for distinct phase points proves $\kappa_NN^{3/2}\to0$.

When $\alpha_N>0$, the phase-weight equations $r_i=w_i$ are exactly
solvable if and only if
\begin{equation}
 |e_i|^2=2c\cdot e_i+b\quad\hbox{for every }i
 \label{eq:cosphere-equation}
\end{equation}
for some $c\in\R^d,b\in\R$. Then
$t_N=\kappa_NNc$ restores every weight exactly and makes the
standardized mean changes $\kappa_NN^{3/2}(c-e_i)/(1+\kappa_NN)$
vanish, proving sufficiency in the spherical case.

If~\eqref{eq:cosphere-equation} has no solution, linear algebra
gives an affine dependence with $\sum_i a_i|e_i|^2\ne0$. Since
$r_{i,N}/w_i\to1$, \eqref{eq:multivariate-invariant} forces
$\alpha_N\to0$, equivalently $\kappa_NN^2\to0$, and $t_N=0$ then
proves sufficiency, including the component shapes. For a single
phase, $t_N=\kappa_NNe_1$ aligns the mean exactly; matching the
covariance is equivalent to $\kappa_NN\to0$ and is necessary for any
mean.
\end{proof}

Any affinely independent set of at most $d+1$ points is cospherical,
as are some larger sets, so the number of phases alone does not fix
the threshold. For scalar phase points $-1,0,1$, the invariant reads
\begin{equation}
 \frac{r_{+,N}r_{-,N}}{r_{0,N}^2}
       =\frac{w_+w_-}{w_0^2}e^{-\alpha_N}.
 \label{eq:three-gaussian-invariant}
\end{equation}
As for three support points in Theorem~\ref{thm:three-support}, the
threshold is $\kappa_NN^2\to0$.

\subsection{The exact optimum when some phases must disappear}

For non-cospherical points with $\kappa_NN^2\to\infty$ some phase
weight is lost; the next theorem gives the optimal loss in the regime
$\kappa_NN^{3/2}\to0$, where component shapes are still preserved.
An empty-sphere contact set is the set of phase indices nearest to a
center $c$ (its points lie on a sphere about $c$ with no phase point
inside),
\begin{equation}
 F(c)=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{1\le i\le k}|e_i-c|^2,
 \qquad c\in\R^d.
 \label{eq:empty-sphere-contact}
\end{equation}
These are the contact sets of the lower-paraboloid, or Delaunay,
construction \cite{EdelsbrunnerSeidel1986}. Concentration
of exponential families on faces has a general
theory \cite{CsiszarMatus2005}; the theorem here determines the exact
optimized TV loss under the specified diverging quadratic parameter. As only finitely many subsets occur,
$W_*:=\max_c\sum_{i\in F(c)}w_i$ is attained.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal Gaussian phase loss]
\label{thm:gaussian-phase-loss}
For the model~\eqref{eq:multivariate-gaussian-model}, if
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_NN^{3/2}\to0,\qquad \kappa_NN^2\to\infty,
 \label{eq:gaussian-phase-loss-regime}
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\inf_{t\in\R^d}\TV(p_N,q_{N,t})=1-W_*.
 \label{eq:gaussian-phase-loss-optimum}
\end{equation}
The optimization includes field sequences of unbounded norm.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the upper bound, fix $c$ and take $t_N=\kappa_NNc$. Up to a
common constant, the phase score in \eqref{eq:multivariate-weights}
is $-\alpha_N|e_i-c|^2/2$, so as $\alpha_N\to\infty$ the weights
converge to the reference weights conditioned on $F(c)$. With shape
changes vanishing by the first condition
in~\eqref{eq:gaussian-phase-loss-regime}, the limiting TV is
$1-\sum_{F(c)}w_i$; maximize over $c$.

For the lower bound, take any fields. On a subsequence with
$|t_N|\ge\varepsilon>0$, put $u_N=t_N/|t_N|$ and
$H_N=\max_i u_N\cdot e_i$. Indices with
$u_N\cdot e_i<H_N-N^{-1/4}$ carry vanishing weight: relative to a
maximizer, their linear score is lower by at least $c_1N^{3/4}$ for a
fixed $c_1>0$, while their quadratic score differs only by
$O(\alpha_N)=o(\sqrt N)$.
For the remaining indices the projected transformed mean per particle
is at least
\[
 \lambda_N(H_N-N^{-1/4}+|t_N|)\ge H_N+\varepsilon/2
\]
for all large $N$, uniformly also when $|t_N|\to\infty$.
The target mixture has negligible mass where
$u_N\cdot Y/N>H_N+\varepsilon/4$, whereas the transformed law has
mass tending to one there, so TV tends to one.

It remains to treat $t_N\to0$. On a subsequence where all weights
converge, let $I$ be the nonempty set of indices with positive
limiting weight. As $\lambda_N\to1$, the transformed means divided by $N$
approach their own $e_i$, and fixed small disjoint neighborhoods of
the phase points give
\begin{equation}
 \liminf\TV(p_N,q_{N,t_N})\ge1-\sum_{i\in I}w_i.
 \label{eq:phase-loss-missing-mass}
\end{equation}
To constrain $I$, fix $i_0\in I$ and put $\hat c_N=z_N/\alpha_N$;
the exact score ratios imply
\begin{align*}
 \hat c_N\cdot(e_i-e_{i_0})
       -\tfrac12(|e_i|^2-|e_{i_0}|^2)&\to0&& (i\in I),\\
 \hat c_N\cdot(e_j-e_{i_0})
       -\tfrac12(|e_j|^2-|e_{i_0}|^2)&\le o(1)&& (1\le j\le k).
\end{align*}
For the inequalities, $r_{i_0,N}$ is bounded below and
$r_{j,N}\le1$, so the log-ratio bound divided by $\alpha_N$ vanishes.
A fixed finite linear system satisfied up to vanishing errors cannot
be infeasible: Farkas' alternative would supply fixed multipliers
annihilating the unknown vector and yielding a strictly negative
constant, contradicting the vanishing errors. So some finite $c$
satisfies the limiting system exactly, even if $\hat c_N$ has no
bounded subsequence. Then $F(c)\supseteq I$, so
$\sum_{i\in I}w_i\le W_*$, and \eqref{eq:phase-loss-missing-mass}
gives the lower bound along every subsequence of approximate
minimizers.
\end{proof}

For three scalar points $-1,0,1$, the largest retained sets in
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-phase-loss} are the two adjacent pairs, so
the limiting optimal error is $\min(w_-,w_+)$, or $1/3$ for equal
weights.

\subsection{Anisotropic curvature: the effective metric}

Keep the common covariance $NI_d$ but replace $\kappa_NI_d$ by a
symmetric positive-semidefinite curvature matrix $B_N$. Completing
squares gives
\begin{equation}
 C_N=N(I_d+NB_N)^{-1},\qquad
 M_N=B_N(I_d+NB_N)^{-1},\qquad
 z_N=N(I_d+NB_N)^{-1}t_N,
 \label{eq:anisotropic-effective-metric}
\end{equation}
as the transformed covariance, effective metric, and effective field.
The means are $N(I_d+NB_N)^{-1}(e_i+t_N)$, and
$r_i\propto w_i\exp[z_N\cdot e_i-N^2e_i^{\mathsf T}M_Ne_i/2]$.
The affine invariants and exact weight-restoration equations
therefore use $M_N$, not $B_N$. The sphere becomes an ellipsoid, or a
point at zero radius, for positive-definite $M_N$. For singular
$M_N\ne0$ it is an elliptic cylinder, or an affine subspace at zero
radius, if the linear coefficient lies in the range, and a
paraboloid, possibly with flat directions, if the coefficient has a
nonzero nullspace component. For $M_N=0$ it is a hyperplane if the
coefficient is nonzero, or the whole space if the coefficient and
compatible constant vanish. Inconsistent level constants give an
empty locus in the completed-square or constant cases. These
finite-$N$ algebraic statements do not assert the exponent
classification~\eqref{eq:gaussian-geometry-classification} for
arbitrary anisotropic sequences or unequal phase covariance matrices.
