\section{Discussion and conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

A reservoir requirement becomes precise only after specifying what
must be reproduced. The complete canonical probability law imposes
constraints on both phase populations and phase fluctuations.
For the physical power-law reservoir, a weak energy limit with at
least three support points already forces capacity growing faster than the
square of its scale, even after arbitrary total-energy tuning.
Two separated energy phases permit an endpoint calibration that
reduces the obstruction to the product of latent-energy gap and
fluctuation width. Positive phase tails are then needed for
sufficiency, because a small canonical interfacial probability can
be amplified by the bath.

These statements have microscopic content. The mean-field and
short-range Potts results verify the relevant hypotheses for
interacting Hamiltonians, including plain spin models.
The short-range proof uses positive contour measures and an explicit
bond-to-energy transfer; it does not replace a signed partition
remainder by a probability error. At the $N^{3/2}$ boundary, the
phase measures acquire finite exponential tilts. Their probabilities
can be compensated by an additional physical calibration, but this
does not remove the fluctuation distortion. The optimization theorem
also shows that the smallest TV error can come from discarding a
phase. Phase populations remain an essential diagnostic even when
the metric itself has been optimized.

Shared reservoirs require a further distinction between separate
and joint tests. Two independent copies can each have a canonical
full marginal while their phase assignments remain anticorrelated
and their microscopic mutual information approaches one bit.
This is an equilibrium effect of the stated common reservoir.
It does not require direct subsystem interaction, and it does not
extend without modification to arbitrary interacting spatial
subregions. The full information limit follows from a separate
relative-entropy argument; it cannot be inferred from TV continuity
on growing microscopic spaces.

For finite-system thermodynamics, these results separate capacity,
calibration, and the measured observable. The heat-capacity
convention matters at finite size, as finite ideal-gas contact
calculations also emphasize \cite{CortiEtAl2023}.
Smooth positive-capacity entropies share the curvature mechanism
when an appropriate calibration and tail control are available.
Barrier accuracy is stronger in a different sense: at specified
midpoint and endpoint energies the exact logarithmic barrier
lowering can remain large even while full TV tends to zero.
Conversely, macroscopic energy concentration or a small histogram
Euclidean norm does not establish full-law accuracy.

The proved scope is an additive equilibrium coupling to the stated
bath, fixed positive limiting phase weights, and the explicit
fluctuation and tail hypotheses of each theorem.
The short-range result requires a fixed number of colors
$q\ge q_0(d)$.
Its boundary law holds for every fixed positive capacity coefficient
in dimensions above two and for sufficiently large coefficients in
two dimensions. The square-torus interfacial calculation is a stated
capillarity model, not a derivation of microscopic morphology.
The appendices keep this separation explicit: the contour proof
verifies a microscopic hypothesis, whereas the Gaussian, capillarity,
and linear-capacity appendices analyze stated models exactly.
